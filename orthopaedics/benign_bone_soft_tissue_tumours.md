# Benign Bone & Soft Tissue Tumours (良性骨與軟組織腫瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Benign Tumours
* **Neoplastic but Non-Metastasizing:** Localized overgrowth of bone, cartilage, fibrous tissue, or soft tissue.
* **Locally Aggressive Potential:** Certain tumours [e.g., **Giant Cell Tumour (骨巨細胞瘤)**] display aggressive local bone destruction despite benign histology.
* **Incidentalomas:** Often discovered asymptomatically during X-rays for unrelated trauma.

### Epidemiology of Benign Bone Tumours
* **Age-Specific Prevalence:**
* **< 20 years:** **Simple Bone Cyst (SBC)**, **Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC)**, **Osteochondroma (骨軟骨瘤)**.
* **20-40 years:** **Giant Cell Tumour (GCT)**, **Enchondroma (內生軟骨瘤)**.
* **Any Age:** **Osteoid Osteoma (類骨質骨瘤)** (mostly young males), **Lipoma (脂肪瘤)**.

### Pathogenesis & Genetics
* **Multiple Hereditary Exostoses (MHE):** Autosomal dominant (EXT1/EXT2 gene mutations) → multiple osteochondromas.
* **Ollier Disease:** Multiple enchondromas (non-hereditary).
* **Maffucci Syndrome:** Multiple enchondromas + soft tissue haemangiomas (high malignancy risk).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Topographical Anatomy of Bone Tumours (The "Where" Rule)
* **Epiphysis (骨骺):**
* **GCT:** Unfused epiphysis (rare), fused epiphysis (common, extends to subchondral bone).
* **Chondroblastoma:** Rare, strictly epiphyseal.
* **Metaphysis (乾骺端):**
* **Osteochondroma:** Grows away from the joint (cartilage cap).
* **SBC / ABC:** Central (SBC) or eccentric (ABC) expansile lesions.
* **Diaphysis (骨幹):**
* **Osteoid Osteoma:** Intracortical nidus.
* **Fibrous Dysplasia (FD):** Medullary canal expansion.

### Biomechanical Implications of Bone Cysts
* **Cortical Thinning:** Expanding lesions (ABC/SBC) ↓ cortical thickness → ↑ stress risers.
* **Pathological Fracture Risk:** ↑ risk if lesion involves > 50% cortical diameter or > 2.5cm size (Mirels' Criteria concept).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Presenting Symptoms
* **Pain Pattern:**
* **Osteoid Osteoma:** Severe, unrelenting deep pain, characteristically **worse at night**, dramatically relieved by **NSAIDs/Aspirin**.
* **GCT:** Dull ache, mechanically worse with weight-bearing (due to subchondral micro-fractures).
* **Mass/Swelling:**
* **Osteochondroma:** Painless, hard, fixed bony lump (pain implies bursitis, stalk #, or malignant transformation).
* **Soft Tissue:** Lipoma (soft, painless), Ganglion (transilluminates, cystic), Schwannoma (Tinel's +ve along nerve).
* **Deformity / Pathological #:** Sudden onset severe pain with minor trauma.

### Physical Examination Findings (Look, Feel, Move)
* **Look:**
* Overlying skin changes (erythema/venous engorgement seen in aggressive ABC/GCT).
* Muscle atrophy 2° to disuse.
* Asymmetry or gross deformity.
* **Feel:**
* Lump characteristics: Size, shape, consistency, mobility, tethering to deep fascia/bone.
* **Tender Palpation:** Localized point tenderness (e.g., over OO nidus or impending #).
* **Transillumination:** +ve for ganglion cysts.
* **Move:**
* Joint Range of Motion (ROM) restriction (if tumour is juxta-articular like GCT or mechanically blocks motion like large OC).
* **Neurovascular (NV):**
* Peripheral pulse volume.
* Distal neurological deficit (e.g., OC compressing Common Peroneal Nerve at fibular neck).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### WHO Histological Classification of Benign Bone Tumours
| Cell of Origin | Benign Tumour Example (English & 中文) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bone-Forming** | Osteoid Osteoma (類骨質骨瘤), Osteoblastoma |
| **Cartilage-Forming** | Osteochondroma (骨軟骨瘤), Enchondroma (內生軟骨瘤) |
| **Fibrous/Cystic** | Fibrous Dysplasia (骨纖維發育不良), Non-Ossifying Fibroma (NOF) |
| **Unknown Origin** | Giant Cell Tumour (骨巨細胞瘤), ABC, SBC |

### Enneking Classification for Benign Bone Tumours
| Stage | Biological Behavior | Clinical/Radiological Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1 (Latent)** | Inactive, static | Asymptomatic, well-defined thick sclerotic margin (e.g., NOF) |
| **Stage 2 (Active)** | Growing slowly | Mild symptoms, well-defined margin, thin expanded cortex (e.g., ABC, SBC) |
| **Stage 3 (Aggressive)** | Locally destructive | Painful, indistinct margins, cortical breach, soft tissue mass (e.g., GCT) |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiograph (X-ray) Principles
* **Zone of Transition:**
* **Narrow:** Sharp, well-defined border (sclerotic) → signifies slow growth (Benign).
* **Wide:** Ill-defined, permeative border → signifies rapid growth (Aggressive/Malignant).
* **Periosteal Reaction:**
* **Solid/Smooth:** Benign, slow chronic irritation (e.g., OO).
* **Absent:** Uncomplicated benign cysts.
* (Note: Sunburst, Codman's triangle, onion-skin imply malignancy).

### Specific Pathognomonic X-ray Findings
| Tumour | Classic Radiological Appearance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Osteoid Osteoma** | Dense cortical sclerosis surrounding a small (< 1.5cm) radiolucent **Nidus** |
| **Osteochondroma** | Exostosis projecting away from joint; medullary cavity continuous with parent bone |
| **Enchondroma** | Intramedullary lytic lesion with punctate **"Popcorn" calcification** (O-ring) |
| **SBC** | Central, purely lytic, **"Fallen Leaf" sign** (+ve if pathological # occurs) |
| **ABC** | Eccentric, expansile, "Soap-bubble" appearance with thin cortical shell |
| **GCT** | Epiphyseal, purely lytic, eccentric, "Soap-bubble", no sclerotic rim |
| **Fibrous Dysplasia** | **"Ground-glass"** appearance, Shepherd's crook deformity (proximal femur) |

### Advanced Imaging (MRI, CT, Bone Scan)
* **MRI with Gadolinium:**
* Gold standard for evaluating soft tissue extension, neurovascular involvement, and intramedullary skip lesions.
* **Fluid-fluid levels:** Pathognomonic for **ABC**.
* **CT Scan:**
* Superior for assessing cortical integrity and pinpointing the **nidus** in OO.
* **Isotope Bone Scan (Tc-99m):**
* Identifies polyostotic disease (MHE, Ollier's, Fibrous Dysplasia).
* Hot in OO, OC; cold in SBC.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Management for Pathological Fractures
* **Primary Survey:** Rule out other injuries.
* **Immobilisation:**
* Apply strict non-weight bearing (NWB) status to affected limb.
* Plaster splinting / Backslab (e.g., above-knee slab for distal femur #).
* **Analgesia:** IV/PO pain relief; specific use of NSAIDs for suspected OO.
* **Do NOT empirically fix:** Pathological # of unknown origin MUST undergo full workup (staging, biopsy) prior to internal fixation to avoid disseminating a potential primary sarcoma.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Non-Operative Management Indications
* **Observation (Watch & Wait):**
* Asymptomatic latent lesions (Enneking Stage 1) → NOF, asymptomatic OC, small enchondromas.
* Serial X-rays (e.g., every 6-12 months) to monitor growth.
* **Medical Therapy:**
* **Denosumab (RANKL Inhibitor):** Indicated for locally advanced, unresectable **GCT** (e.g., sacral/spinal GCT) to consolidate tumour prior to surgery or as definitive palliation (HA protocol).

### Operative Management & Surgical Techniques
| Tumour | Definitive Surgical Procedure | Key Surgical Principles / Adjuvants |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Osteochondroma** | Excision (at base of stalk) | Ensure entire cartilage cap is removed to prevent recurrence. |
| **Osteoid Osteoma** | **Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)** | CT-guided percutaneous thermal destruction of nidus. (Alternative: En bloc resection). |
| **Enchondroma** | Intralesional Curettage | Bone grafting (auto/allo) or bone substitute filling post-curettage. |
| **SBC** | Aspiration + Injection | Corticosteroid or Bone Marrow aspirate injection. Curettage if refractory. |
| **GCT** | **Extended Curettage + Adjuvants** | High-speed burring + **Phenol / Liquid Nitrogen / H2O2** + **PMMA Bone Cement**. |
| **ABC** | Curettage + Bone Grafting | Pre-op embolization may be needed due to high vascularity. |

### The Principle of Extended Curettage for GCT
* **Goal:** Eliminate microscopic disease in the reactive zone.
* **Steps:**
* Create a large cortical window.
* Aggressive intralesional curettage.
* **High-speed burr:** Extends margins by 1-2mm into healthy bone.
* **Chemical/Thermal Adjuvants:** Phenol (chemical necrosis) or Liquid Nitrogen (cryonecrosis) to kill residual cells.
* **Reconstruction:** Pack with **PMMA (Polymerizing Methyl Methacrylate) Cement**. The exothermic reaction of PMMA provides further thermal necrosis and immediate structural support.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative Mobility Guidelines
* **Following Excisional Biopsy/RFA:** Immediate weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT); rapid return to baseline.
* **Following Curettage + Cement (GCT):**
* PMMA cement hardens within 15 minutes → provides immediate compressive strength.
* Typically allows early partial to full WB depending on cortical thickness remaining.
* **Following Curettage + Bone Grafting:**
* Graft requires physiological incorporation (creeping substitution).
* Strict non-weight bearing (NWB) for 6-12 weeks until radiographic union/consolidation.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Tumour-Specific Complications
* **Malignant Transformation:**
* **Osteochondroma → Chondrosarcoma:** Risk < 1% for solitary, ~5% for Multiple Hereditary Exostoses (MHE). Look for sudden growth or cartilage cap > 2cm in adults.
* **Enchondroma → Chondrosarcoma:** ↑ risk in Ollier's and Maffucci syndrome.
* **Local Recurrence:**
* Extremely high in GCT (~15-20%) if inadequately burred/cemented. Requires regular X-ray surveillance for 5 years.
* **Pathological Fracture:**
* Common in SBC, ABC, and GCT.
* **Osteoarthritis (2°):**
* Due to subchondral bone collapse in GCT or altered joint biomechanics from a large juxta-articular mass.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic clinical presentation of an Osteoid Osteoma? Answer: Deep, unrelenting bone pain that is classically worse at night and dramatically relieved by NSAIDs or aspirin, caused by high prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) production within the nidus.
* Trap: Fixing a pathological fracture immediately in the ED without a biopsy. Pearl: Never blindly fix a pathological fracture of unknown origin. Doing so can contaminate the entire medullary canal with malignant cells if the lesion turns out to be a sarcoma. Immobilise, stage, and biopsy first.
* Pitfall: Mistaking a Fallen Leaf Sign for an aggressive fracture fragment. A piece of fractured cortex falling into a cystic cavity (Fallen Leaf/Fragment sign) is pathognomonic for a Simple Bone Cyst (SBC) because the cyst is fluid-filled, unlike solid tumours.
* Viva Question: Why do we use PMMA cement instead of bone graft for Giant Cell Tumour (GCT) cavities? Answer: PMMA provides immediate structural stability, its exothermic reaction provides adjuvant thermal necrosis to residual tumour cells, and its radiopacity makes detecting local recurrences on follow-up X-rays much easier (radiolucency appearing around cement = recurrence).
* Trap: Assuming an Osteochondroma is growing in a 40-year-old. Pearl: Osteochondromas stop growing when the physis closes. Any new growth or pain in an adult osteochondroma strongly suggests malignant transformation to a secondary Chondrosarcoma.
