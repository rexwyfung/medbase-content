# Distal Radius Fracture (橈骨遠端骨折)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### DRF Clinical Definition
* **Distal Radius Fracture (DRF)**: # occurring within **2.5 cm** of the radiocarpal joint.
* Most common upper limb # in ED.

### DRF Mechanism of Injury (MOI)
* **Low-energy MOI**: **FOOSH** (Fall On OutStretched Hand) from standing height (osteoporotic bone).
* **High-energy MOI**: Motor vehicle accidents (MVA), falls from height (yields intra-articular comminution, soft tissue trauma).
* Position of wrist at impact dictates # pattern:
  * Wrist extension → **Dorsal** displacement.
  * Wrist flexion → **Volar** displacement.

### DRF Epidemiology
* **Bimodal distribution**:
  * **Peak 1**: Young males (18-25 yrs) → High-energy trauma.
  * **Peak 2**: Elderly females (>60 yrs) → Low-energy falls (post-menopausal osteoporosis).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Normal Distal Radius Radiographic Parameters
* **Radial Inclination**: **22°** (Normal range 21-25°).
* **Volar Tilt (Palmar Tilt)**: **11°** (Normal range 10-12°).
* **Radial Height**: **11-12 mm**.
* **Ulnar Variance**: **Neutral** (0 mm difference between articular surfaces of distal radius and ulna).

### DRF Deforming Muscle Forces
* **Brachioradialis**: Attaches to radial styloid → Pulls distal fragment proximally (causing **shortening** & **radial deviation**).
* **Pronator Quadratus**: Attaches to volar distal radius → Pulls distal fragment volarly & into **pronation**.
* **Extensor Carpi Radialis Longus/Brevis (ECRL/B)**: Pulls wrist into dorsal angulation.

### Distal Radius Vascular Supply
* Volar supply: **Radial artery** & **Anterior Interosseous Artery (AIA)**.
* Dorsal supply: **Posterior Interosseous Artery (PIA)**.
* **Lister’s Tubercle (李斯特結節)**: Bony prominence on dorsal distal radius; acts as pulley for **Extensor Pollicis Longus (EPL)** tendon (watershed area for ischemia post-#).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### DRF Visual Inspection (Look)
* **Colles' # Deformity**: **Dinner fork deformity (餐叉樣畸形)** (Dorsal prominence).
* **Smith's # Deformity**: **Garden spade deformity (園丁鏟畸形)** (Volar prominence).
* Gross swelling, ecchymosis around wrist/hand.
* Open wounds (check volar crease).

### DRF Palpation (Feel)
* +ve Bony tenderness over distal radius.
* +ve Tenderness at **Distal Radioulnar Joint (DRUJ)** → Suggests concomitant DRUJ instability/TFCC tear.
* **Anatomical Snuffbox (解剖鼻煙壺)** tenderness → MUST rule out concurrent **Scaphoid # (舟骨骨折)**.

### DRF Neurovascular (NV) Status Evaluation
| Structure | Assessment Method | Pathological Finding in DRF |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Median Nerve** | Sensation over volar thumb/index/middle finger tip. OK sign (AIN motor). | **Acute Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)** (+ve paresthesia). |
| **Ulnar Nerve** | Sensation over volar little finger tip. Finger abduction/adduction. | Ulnar nerve neurapraxia (less common). |
| **Radial Artery** | Palpate radial pulse at volar wrist. Capillary refill time (CRT). | Diminished pulse / CRT > 2s (vascular compromise). |
| **Compartments** | Passive finger extension/flexion. | Extreme pain on passive stretch (Compartment Syndrome). |

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### DRF Eponymous Classifications
| Eponym | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Colles' # (柯雷氏骨折)** | Extra-articular #, distal fragment displaced **dorsally**, dorsally angulated, radially shifted, foreshortened. |
| **Smith's # (史密斯骨折)** | "Reverse Colles". Extra-articular #, distal fragment displaced **volarly** (palmar). |
| **Barton's # (巴頓骨折)** | Intra-articular lip # with radiocarpal **subluxation/dislocation**. Can be Volar (more common) or Dorsal. |
| **Chauffeur's # (司機骨折)** | Intra-articular # of the **Radial Styloid** (Hutchinson #). |
| **Die-Punch #** | Intra-articular depressed # of the **Lunate Fossa**. |

### DRF Frykman Classification System
* Based on articular involvement (Radiocarpal/DRUJ) & presence of **Ulnar Styloid #**.
* Odd numbers = Isolated radius #; Even numbers = Associated ulnar styloid #.
* **Type I / II**: Extra-articular (- / + ulnar styloid #).
* **Type III / IV**: Radiocarpal joint involved (- / + ulnar styloid #).
* **Type V / VI**: DRUJ involved (- / + ulnar styloid #).
* **Type VII / VIII**: BOTH Radiocarpal & DRUJ involved (- / + ulnar styloid #).

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### DRF Standard Radiographic Protocol
* **AP / PA Wrist**: Assess **Radial Height** (loss >3mm), **Radial Inclination** (loss >5°), **Ulnar variance**, intra-articular step-off/gap.
* **Lateral Wrist**: Assess **Volar Tilt** (dorsal angulation indicates instability).
* **Oblique Wrist**: Better visualization of radial styloid/scaphoid.

### DRF Advanced Imaging (CT)
* **CT Wrist (Non-contrast)**:
  * Gold standard for pre-operative planning.
  * Quantifies intra-articular step-off/gap (threshold: >2 mm).
  * Evaluates die-punch fragments, comminution severity, and distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) congruity.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### DRF Acute ED Interventions
* **1° Survey**: ATLS principles if poly-trauma.
* **Analgesia**: IV paracetamol, weak opioids.
* **Open # Protocol (Gustilo-Anderson)**: Immediate IV antibiotics (e.g., Cefazolin/Augmentin), tetanus toxoid, sterile saline dressing.
* Remove rings/jewelry immediately to prevent digit ischemia from swelling.

### DRF Closed Reduction Technique
* **Anesthesia**: **Hematoma block** (10-20mL 1% Lignocaine injected directly into # hematoma) OR **Bier's block** (IV regional anesthesia) OR Procedural sedation (Propofol).
* **Reduction Maneuver (for Colles')**:
  1. **Traction**: Exaggerate deformity (dorsal angulation) to unlock fragments, apply longitudinal traction.
  2. **Manipulation**: Push distal fragment volarly and ulnarly.
  3. **Molding**: Place wrist in slight flexion, ulnar deviation, and pronation.
* **Immobilization**: Apply well-padded **Below-Elbow Dorsal Backslab** (allows for volar swelling).
* **Post-Reduction**: Immediate repeat X-ray + documented post-reduction NV check.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### DRF Management Algorithm: Non-Op vs Operative
| Modality | Indications |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative (Conservative)** | Undisplaced #, stable # post-reduction. Low-demand/elderly pt with high surgical risk. |
| **Operative (Surgical)** | Instability criteria met (see below). Open #. Associated neurovascular compromise (Acute CTS). Bilateral #. Floating elbow. |

### DRF Radiographic Instability Criteria (Lafontaine's Criteria)
* Loss of Volar Tilt > 20° (dorsal tilt >10°).
* Initial shortening > 5 mm.
* Intra-articular comminution / step-off > 2 mm.
* Severe dorsal comminution.
* Associated ulnar fracture.
* Age > 60 years.
* *(Presence of $\ge$ 3 indicates high risk of secondary displacement in cast).*

### DRF Surgical Techniques
* **Volar Locking Plate (VLP) ORIF (掌側鎖定鋼板內固定)**: Standard of care in HK HA.
  * **Approach**: Modified Henry’s approach (between Flexor Carpi Radialis & Radial Artery).
  * **Pros**: Direct visualization, strong construct, avoids EPL irritation (compared to dorsal plates), allows early ROM.
* **Percutaneous K-wire Fixation**: For simple extra-articular # with no comminution. Requires 6 weeks cast.
* **External Fixator (Ex-Fix)**: For open # (highly contaminated), damage control, or severe intra-articular comminution relying on ligamentotaxis.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### DRF Phase 1 Rehab (0-6 Weeks)
* **Non-Op/K-wires/Ex-Fix**: Immobilized in POP cast.
* **VLP ORIF**: Resting splint. Early active ROM of wrist at 2 weeks post-op.
* **CRITICAL**: Immediate and aggressive Active ROM of fingers, thumb, elbow, and shoulder to prevent stiffness and CRPS. Strict limb elevation to ↓ edema.

### DRF Phase 2 Rehab (>6 Weeks)
* Discontinue cast/splints.
* Physiotherapy: Passive ROM, grip strengthening, functional occupational therapy (OT).
* Progress to weight-bearing and resistance training.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### DRF Early Complications
* **Acute Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)**: Swelling/fracture fragment compressing median nerve. (Requires urgent release if progressive).
* **Compartment Syndrome**: Especially high-energy injuries.
* **Loss of Reduction**: Secondary displacement in cast (usually within first 2 weeks).

### DRF Late Complications
* **Extensor Pollicis Longus (EPL) Tendon Rupture**: 
  * Occurs 3-8 weeks post-injury.
  * MOI: Mechanical attrition over unreduced dorsal bony spike (Lister's tubercle) OR ischemia from hematoma/swelling.
* **Malunion**: Leads to cosmetic deformity, DRUJ dysfunction, limited supination.
* **Post-Traumatic Osteoarthritis (OA)**: Radiocarpal joint (if articular step >2mm).
* **Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS) / Sudeck's Atrophy**: Abnormal autonomic response. Prev: Early finger movement & Vitamin C.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the anatomical rationale for the Modified Henry's approach in DRF ORIF? Answer: It utilizes the internervous plane between the Flexor Carpi Radialis (FCR - Median nerve) and the Brachioradialis (Radial nerve), providing safe access to the volar radius while protecting the median nerve and radial artery.
* Viva Question: A patient comes to the clinic 4 weeks post-Colles' fracture treated conservatively, suddenly unable to extend their thumb. What is the diagnosis and treatment? Answer: EPL tendon rupture. Treatment is usually an Extensor Indicis Proprius (EIP) to EPL tendon transfer, as the frayed ends of the EPL preclude primary repair.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with DRF cannot have a concurrent scaphoid fracture if the initial X-ray shows no scaphoid radiolucency. Pearl: Scaphoid fractures are notoriously occult on initial X-rays. If there is anatomical snuffbox tenderness, treat as a concurrent scaphoid fracture and obtain follow-up films or CT/MRI.
* Trap: Reassuring a patient that their severe dorsal wrist pain post-VLP ORIF is normal surgical pain. Pearl: Severe dorsal pain with active finger extension post-VLP strongly suggests protruding volar plate screws irritating the dorsal extensor tendons. Evaluate for potential screw back-out.
* Pitfall: Discharging an ED patient post-closed reduction without a clear follow-up plan. X-rays must be repeated at 1 and 2 weeks post-reduction to catch early secondary displacement before the fracture consolidates into malunion.
