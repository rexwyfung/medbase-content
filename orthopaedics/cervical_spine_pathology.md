# Cervical Spine Pathology (頸椎病變)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition & Degenerative Cascade
* **Cervical Spondylosis (頸椎骨質增生/退化)**: Age-related degenerative changes of **cervical spine** starting at **intervertebral disc**.
* **Pathophysiology**: Disc desiccation/height loss → ↑ load transfer to **facet joints** & **uncinate processes** → osteophyte formation, **ligamentum flavum** hypertrophy → canal/foraminal stenosis.
* **Cervical Radiculopathy (頸椎神經根病變)**: Unilateral spinal nerve root compression (most common: osteophytes, **disc herniation**).
* **Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy (CSM, 頸椎脊髓病變)**: Multi-segmental spinal cord compression; most common cause of spinal cord dysfunction in adults >55 yrs.

### Demographics & Risk Factors
* **Epidemiology**: Prevalent in >50% of population >50 yrs, >90% >70 yrs.
* **Most common levels**: **C5/C6** (most mobile segment), **C6/C7**.
* **Risk factors**:
	* Repetitive neck axial loading.
	* Heavy manual labor.
	* Genetic predisposition.
	* Smoking.

### Mechanism of Compression
* **Static factors**:
	* Canal stenosis (developmental sagittal diameter <13 mm).
	* Disc protrusion.
	* Osteophytes.
	* Ossification of posterior longitudinal ligament (**OPLL**).
* **Dynamic factors**: Cervical extension → **ligamentum flavum** buckles anteriorly + spinal cord shortens & thickens → compression ↑.
* **Vascular factors**: Ischemia of spinal cord via compression of anterior spinal artery or intramedullary microcirculation.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Uncinate Processes & Neuroforamina
* **Uncinate processes (鉤突)**: Posterolateral projections of C3-C7 superior vertebral bodies; form **Uncovertebral joints (Joints of Luschka)**.
* **Hypertrophy effect**: Uncinate osteophytes project posterolaterally into anterior wall of **neural foramen** → direct anterior compression on exit nerve root.
* **Nerve root numbering**: Cervical nerve roots exit ABOVE corresponding numbered pedicle (e.g., C6 nerve root exits C5/C6 foramen).
* **Disc herniation impact**: C5/C6 central/posterolateral herniation affects **C6 nerve root**.
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/fdd554a60fa545c29e246cef334e2a8e.png)![image|300](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/fd7cff295fea5c8e38d18a6a2c37ce53.png)

### Vascular Supply of Cervical Cord
* **Anterior Spinal Artery (ASA)**: Supplies anterior 2/3 of spinal cord (corticospinal tracts, spinothalamic tracts).
* **Posterior Spinal Arteries (PSA)**: Pair, supply posterior 1/3 of spinal cord (dorsal columns).
* **Radicular arteries**: Branches of **vertebral artery** entering via neural foramina; vulnerable to dynamic osteophytic compression.
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/7602acd702e0d90cea994385d64b64dc.png)

### Deforming Vectors & Dynamic Mechanics
* **Cervical Flexion**: Stretches spinal cord over anterior osteophytes; increases intramedullary pressure.
* **Cervical Extension**: Decreases sagittal canal diameter by 2-3 mm; causes posterior pincer effect (**ligamentum flavum** vs. anterior osteophyte).
* **Subaxial alignment**: Loss of cervical lordosis → shift of axial loading vector anteriorly → accelerated degeneration.


## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Physical Assessment: Look & Feel
* **Look**:
	* Neck posture (forward head, loss of cervical lordosis).
	* Muscle wasting (**deltoid**, **thenar**, **hypothenar**, **interossei**).
	* Gait observation (broad-based, spastic, ataxia).
* **Feel**:
	* Midline spinous process tenderness.
	* Paraspinous muscle spasm.
	* Trapezius trigger points.
![image|300](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/565e391872116743e7cd180ed711b7c1.png)

### Physical Assessment: Move & Provocative Signs
* **Range of Motion (ROM)**: Flexion, extension, lateral flexion, rotation (record restrictions & pain reproduction).
* **Spurling's Test (頸椎壓迫試驗)**: Neck extension + lateral flexion + axial compression → positive if radiates electric pain down arm (high specificity for radiculopathy).
* **Shoulder Abduction Relief Sign (Bakody's Sign)**: Resting hand on top of head relieves radicular pain (reduces tension on nerve root, typical for C5/C6).
* **Lhermitte's Sign**: Active cervical flexion → shock-like sensation down spine into limbs (indicates posterior column/cord irritation).
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/27f756ade53ad7161e08ee512942b6c5.png)

### Neurovascular Examination: Radiculopathy (Lower Motor Neuron)
| Nerve Root | Primary Disc Level | Key Motor Test               | Key Reflex                 | Sensory Landmark     |
| ---------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **C5**     | C4/C5              | **Deltoid** / Biceps flexion | Biceps reflex              | Lateral shoulder     |
| **C6**     | C5/C6              | Wrist extension / Biceps     | **Brachioradialis** reflex | Thumb & index finger |
| **C7**     | C6/C7              | Triceps / Wrist flexion      | **Triceps** reflex         | Middle finger        |
| **C8**     | C7/T1              | Finger flexors (**FDP**)     | Finger flexor reflex       | Ring & little finger |
| **T1**     | T1/T2              | Finger abductors (**DAP**)   | None                       | Medial forearm       |

![image|650](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/c7aa28ffd58e5db283723568dd55e0c6.png)

### Neurovascular Examination: Myelopathy (Upper Motor Neuron)
* **Hoffmann's Sign**: Flicking nail of middle finger → involuntary flexion of thumb and index finger (+ve UMN sign).
* **Inverted Brachioradialis Reflex**: Tapping brachioradialis tendon → absent brachioradialis reflex + involuntary finger flexion (indicates cord lesion at C5/C6 level).
* **Clonus**: Rapid ankle dorsiflexion / wrist extension → >3 sustained rhythmic beats.
* **Babinski Sign**: Stroking plantar lateral border → extensor plantar response (great toe dorsiflexion + fanning of toes).
* **Myelopathy Hand Signs**:
  * **Finger Escape Sign**: Inability to keep 4th and 5th digits adducted/extended for 30 seconds.
  * **Grip & Release Test**: Inability to make and open a fist 20 times in 10 seconds.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Nurick Classification System for Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy
| Grade         | Clinical Description                                                        |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Grade 0**   | Signs/symptoms of root involvement without evidence of spinal cord disease. |
| **Grade I**   | Signs of cord involvement; normal gait; employment unhindered.              |
| **Grade II**  | Slight gait abnormality; employment maintained.                             |
| **Grade III** | Severe gait abnormality; prevents employment; able to walk unassisted.      |
| **Grade IV**  | Able to ambulate only with assistance or frame.                             |
| **Grade V**   | Chair-bound or bedridden.                                                   |

### Modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) Score for Myelopathy
| Assessment Domain | Maximum Score | Key Scoring Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Motor (Upper Extremity)** | 5 | 0 = Inability to feed self; 5 = Normal. |
| **Motor (Lower Extremity)** | 7 | 0 = Inability to walk; 7 = Normal. |
| **Sensory (Upper Extremity)** | 3 | 0 = Severe sensory loss/pain; 3 = Normal. |
| **Sphincter Dysfunction** | 3 | 0 = Inability to urinate / severe incontinence; 3 = Normal. |

### Severity Grading via mJOA Score
| Severity Category | Total mJOA Score Range |
|---|---|
| **Mild CSM** | 15 - 17 |
| **Moderate CSM** | 12 - 14 |
| **Severe CSM** | < 12 |


## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiography Protocols & Views
* **AP View**: Evaluates subaxial alignment, uncinate process hypertrophy, vertebral body height.
* **Lateral View**: Evaluates cervical lordosis, disc space narrowing, anterior/posterior osteophytes, sagittal canal diameter (**Torg-Pavlov ratio** = canal diameter / vertebral body width; <0.8 = stenosis).
* **Open-Mouth Odontoid View**: Assesses C1/C2 alignment, Atlanto-axial interval (**ADI**).
* **Flexion-Extension Lateral Views**: Evaluates dynamic instability (>3.5 mm translation or >11° angular motion between adjacent segments).
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/d725ad428e0e7a161f81ec7e4b8265a0.png)

### Advanced Imaging: MRI & CT
* **MRI Cervical Spine (Gold Standard)**:
  * Sagittal & Axial T2-weighted images show cord compression, loss of CSF signal shadow.
  * **Myelomalacia**: High T2 signal intensity within spinal cord (indicates cord edema, gliosis, or soft softening; poor prognostic indicator if seen on T1 as low signal).
  * Evaluates soft disc herniation vs. ligamentum flavum hypertrophy.
* **CT Cervical Spine**:
  * Superior evaluation of bony architecture, ossification of posterior longitudinal ligament (**OPLL**), and hard osteophytes.
  * Essential for surgical planning (e.g., screw trajectory, bone stock assessment).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Symptom Control & Immobilization
* **Soft Cervical Collar**: Short-term immobilization (<1-2 weeks) for acute flare-up of radiculopathy; avoid prolonged use to prevent neck muscle atrophy.
* **Hard Collar (Philadelphia/Miami J)**: Reserved for acute trauma or gross dynamic instability.
* **Analgesic Ladder**: Non-opioid (Paracetamol) + NSAIDs (COX-2 inhibitors) + Muscle relaxants (Eperisone/Baclofen) for acute muscle spasm.
* **Neuropathic Pain Agents**: First-line for radicular pain: **Pregabalin** or **Gabapentin**.

### Emergency Triage & Tongs/Traction
* **Emergency Surgical Indications**:
  * Progressive or severe neurological deficit (e.g., acute quadriparesis).
  * Cauda equina / Conus medullaris / Cervical cord compression syndrome with sphincter dysfunction.
* **Gardner-Wells Tongs / Axial Traction**: Contraindicated in degenerative cervical myelopathy/radiculopathy without acute dislocation/fracture-subluxation.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Non-Operative vs. Operative Decision Matrix
| Clinical Presentation         | Primary Indication                                                    | Preferred Treatment Strategy                                               |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cervical Radiculopathy**    | Mild/moderate pain, NO progressive motor deficit                      | **Non-operative** (Physiotherapy, medications, epidural steroid injection) |
| **Cervical Radiculopathy**    | Failed non-operative therapy (>6-12 wks) OR progressive motor deficit | **Operative intervention**                                                 |
| **Cervical Myelopathy (CSM)** | Mild (mJOA ≥15) without progression                                   | **Trial of close observation** vs. early surgery                           |
| **Cervical Myelopathy (CSM)** | Moderate to severe (mJOA <15) OR progressive neurological decline     | **Surgical Decompression** (Primary treatment)                             |

### Anterior Surgical Techniques
* **Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF)**:
  * **Indications**: 1-3 level disease anterior to spinal cord (disc herniation, focal osteophyte).
  * **Technique**: Anterior approach along sternocleidomastoid internervous plane → discectomy → interbody cage insertion + autograft/allograft → anterior plate fixation.
* **Anterior Cervical Corpectomy and Fusion (ACCF)**:
  * **Indications**: Multi-level disease behind vertebral body, OPLL.
  * **Technique**: Removal of vertebral body + adjacent discs → expandable cage / strut graft + anterior plate.
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/80b9b3ac19902186fcfc6bb34b70beff.png)

### Posterior Surgical Techniques
* **Cervical Laminoplasty (開門式頸椎後路成形術)**:
  * **Indications**: Multi-level (≥3 levels) cord compression with preserved cervical lordosis.
  * **Technique**: Hinge created on one side of lamina, open cut on opposite side → expanded canal held open with mini-plates/micro-screws. Preserves motion.
* **Posterior Laminectomy and Fusion (後路椎板切除加融合術)**:
  * **Indications**: Multi-level compression, cervical kyphosis, rigid deformity.
  * **Technique**: Complete resection of lamina + ligamentum flavum + lateral mass screw / pedicle screw fixation.
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/6753b24c836dd3819b406791a9a3a7da.png)

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative Immobilization & Early Phase
* **Cervical Spine Protection**:
  * ACDF/ACCF: Soft or hard collar for comfort (2-6 weeks depending on construct stability).
  * Laminoplasty: Soft collar for 1-2 weeks to encourage early range-of-motion.
* **Early Mobilization**: Out of bed on Post-Op Day 1; deep vein thrombosis (**DVT**) prophylaxis.
* **Dysphagia Precautions**: Soft diet initially; speech therapy / swallowing assessment if symptomatic.

### Physical Therapy Progression
* **Phase 1 (Weeks 0-6)**: Deep cervical flexor isometric training, gentle active shoulder/elbow ROM, nerve gliding exercises, posture correction. Avoid neck extension & rotation.
* **Phase 2 (Weeks 6-12)**: Progressive isometric neck resistance exercises, upper back extensor strengthening, dynamic balance & gait retraining for myelopathy patients.
* **Phase 3 (Week 12+)**: Full active ROM exercises, functional task training, gradual return to full activity/work.


## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Anterior Approach Complications
* **Dysphagia (吞嚥困難)**: Most common early complication (up to 30%); caused by retraction of esophagus/pharynx or local hematoma.
* **Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve (RLN) Palsy**: Causes hoarseness & vocal cord paralysis; direct retraction injury. Right side at higher risk due to variable course.
* **Horner's Syndrome**: Injury to **sympathetic chain** located on anterior surface of **longus colli** muscle (Ptosis, Miosis, Anhidrosis).
* **Esophageal Perforation**: Rare (<1%), high mortality due to mediastinitis.

### Posterior Approach & Neurological Complications
* **C5 Nerve Root Palsy**: Deltoid weakness / biceps weakness (3-10% post-op); caused by tethering effect/posterior drift of spinal cord after decompression.
* **Axial Neck Pain**: Common after posterior laminectomy/laminoplasty due to detachment of posterior cervical musculature (**semispinalis cervicis**).
* **Adjacent Segment Disease (ASD)**: Accelerated degeneration at segments above or below fusion site due to hypermobility & altered stress vectors.
* **Pseudoarthrosis**: Failure of solid bony fusion after ACDF/ACCF (↑ risk in smokers, multi-level fusion).


## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the single most sensitive clinical test for detecting cervical spondylotic myelopathy early? Answer: The Hoffmann's sign and inverted brachioradialis reflex are highly sensitive screening physical signs for upper motor neuron lesion in CSM.
* Viva Question: Why is anterior decompression (ACDF) preferred over posterior decompression (Laminoplasty) in a patient with cervical kyphosis? Answer: In a kyphotic spine, the spinal cord is draped over anterior osteophytes; posterior decompression will not allow the cord to drift backward away from the compression (posterior drift mechanism fails).
* Trap: Assuming a patient with leg spasticity and hyperreflexia has a thoracic or lumbar pathology. Pearl: Always examine and image the cervical spine first, as Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy is the most common cause of painless spastic quadriparesis/gait ataxia in the elderly.
* Trap: Attributing thenar atrophy and reduced thumb abduction solely to Carpal Tunnel Syndrome. Pearl: Always check C8/T1 motor function, neck provocative maneuvers, and triceps reflex to rule out concomitant double crush syndrome or cervical radiculopathy.
* Pitfall: Performing a posterior laminectomy alone in a patient with dynamic cervical instability or pre-existing kyphosis. Avoid this because laminectomy disrupts posterior muscle-ligamentous complex, resulting in post-laminectomy kyphotic deformity and progressive neurological compromise.
