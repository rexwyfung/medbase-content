# Paediatric Musculoskeletal Injuries (兒童骨骼肌肉創傷)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Unique Paediatric Bone Characteristics
* **Thick Periosteum**: Acts as hinge, limits displacement, speeds healing.
* **Increased Porosity**: Bones fail in compression/bending before tension.
* **Presence of Physis (Growth Plate)**: Cartilaginous, structurally weak point.
* **Rapid Remodelling**: High capacity to correct angular deformities (especially in plane of joint motion), but poor rotational correction.

### Paediatric Incomplete Fracture Patterns
* **Plastic Deformation (塑性變形)**: Micro-fractures on tension side, bone bends but does not break visibly.
* **Torus / Buckle Fracture (扣壓性骨折)**: Compression failure of metaphysis. 
* **Greenstick Fracture (青枝骨折)**: Tension failure on convex side, intact periosteum on concave side.

### Supracondylar Humerus Fracture (髁上肱骨骨折)
* **Definition**: # of distal humerus just above epicondyles.
* **Epidemiology**: Most common paediatric elbow #, peak age 5-7 yrs.
* **MOI (Extension type - 95%)**: Fall On Outstretched Hand (FOOSH) with elbow hyperextended.
* **MOI (Flexion type - 5%)**: Direct trauma to flexed elbow.

### Non-Accidental Injury (NAI) (非意外損傷)
* **Definition**: Injuries inflicted via abuse/neglect.
* **Epidemiology**: Highest risk in infants < 1 yr.
* **MOI**: Shaking, twisting, direct blunt force, burning.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Physis (Growth Plate) Anatomy
* **Zone of Resting Cartilage**: Germinal cells, nearest epiphysis.
* **Zone of Proliferation**: Chondrocyte columns, active growth.
* **Zone of Hypertrophy**: Structurally weakest zone (primary site of physeal #).
* **Zone of Calcification**: Nearest metaphysis, cell death & matrix calcification.

### Distal Humerus Biomechanics
* **Coronoid & Olecranon Fossae**: Extremely thin bone connecting medial/lateral columns.
* **Remodelling Potential**: Very limited at distal humerus (contributes only 10-20% of longitudinal arm growth).

### Supracondylar Deforming Muscle Forces
* **Triceps**: Pulls distal fragment proximally and posteriorly.
* **Brachialis**: Proximal fragment buttonholes through it anteriorly.

### Regional Neurovascular Anatomy at the Elbow
* **Brachial Artery**: Bowstrings across anterior elbow; highly susceptible to kinking/spasm/laceration by proximal fragment.
* **Anterior Interosseous Nerve (AIN)**: Branch of Median nerve; tethered anteriorly; most commonly injured in extension-type # (posteromedial displacement).
* **Radial Nerve**: Injured in posterolateral displacement.
* **Ulnar Nerve**: Injured in flexion-type # or iatrogenically via medial K-wire.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Visual Inspection (Look)
* **Elbow Contour**: Gross swelling, S-shaped deformity.
* **Skin Integrity**: Pucker sign (dimpling of anterior skin → proximal fragment piercing brachialis).
* **NAI Indicators**: Multiple bruises at different healing stages, patterned burns/bruises (cigarette, handprint), torn frenulum.

### Palpation (Feel)
* **Tenderness**: Pinpoint over supracondylar ridges.
* **Compartment Tension**: Tense, woody forearm (rule out evolving compartment syndrome).

### Range of Motion (Move)
* **Pseudoparalysis**: Child refuses to move limb due to pain (classic for paediatric # or septic arthritis).
* **Elbow ROM**: Strictly avoid passive testing in acute setting to prevent NV injury.

### Distal Neurovascular (NV) Assessment (Critical)
| Structure | Motor Test | Sensory Test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AIN (前骨間神經)** | "OK" sign (FPL & FDP to index finger) | None (Pure motor) |
| **Median Nerve (正中神經)** | Thumb opposition (AbPB) | Palmar tip of index finger |
| **Radial Nerve (橈神經)** | Wrist/Thumb extension (ECRL/B, EPL) | Dorsal 1st web space |
| **Ulnar Nerve (尺神經)** | Finger spread (Dorsal interossei) | Palmar tip of little finger |
| **Vascular** | Capillary refill < 2s | Radial & Ulnar pulses |

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Salter-Harris Classification of Physeal Injuries (骨骺損傷分類)
| Type | Description (Mnemonic: SALTR) | Prognosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | **S**lipped: # through physis only | Excellent |
| **II** | **A**bove: # through physis + metaphysis | Excellent (Most common) |
| **III** | **L**ower: # through physis + epiphysis | Fair (Intra-articular) |
| **IV** | **T**hrough: # through metaphysis, physis + epiphysis | Poor (High risk of growth arrest) |
| **V** | **R**ammed: Crush injury to physis | Worst (Premature closure guaranteed) |

### Gartland Classification for Extension-Type Supracondylar #
| Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Undisplaced (< 2mm). |
| **II** | Displaced, but posterior periosteal hinge intact. |
| **IIA** | Posterior angulation only. |
| **IIB** | Posterior angulation + Malrotation. |
| **III** | Completely displaced (no cortical contact). |
| **IIIA** | Posteromedial displacement. |
| **IIIB** | Posterolateral displacement. |
| **IV** | Multidirectional instability (periosteum circumferentially torn) - diagnosed intra-op. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Standard Paediatric Trauma X-ray Principles
* **Views**: True AP and Lateral of affected limb.
* **Rule of 2s**: 2 views, 2 joints (above & below), 2 limbs (compare with contralateral normal side if ossification centers confusing).

### Key Radiological Lines for Paediatric Elbow (Lateral View)
* **Anterior Humeral Line (AHL)**: Drawn down anterior cortex of humerus; should intersect middle 1/3 of capitellum. (In Gartland II/III, it passes anterior to capitellum).
* **Radiocapitellar Line (RCL)**: Drawn through center of radial neck; must intersect center of capitellum in ALL views (rules out radial head dislocation/Monteggia).

### Pathognomonic X-ray Signs
* **Fat Pad Sign / Sail Sign (脂肪墊徵)**: Visible posterior fat pad on lateral view → indicates joint effusion/occult #.
* **Metaphyseal Corner / Bucket-Handle #**: Micro-avulsions from forceful pulling/twisting (highly specific for NAI).
* **Multiple Healing Stages**: Callus formation of varying ages (pathognomonic for NAI).

### Advanced Imaging Protocols
* **Skeletal Survey**: Mandatory total-body X-rays for suspected NAI (< 2 yrs old).
* **CT Scan**: Rarely used in acute supracondylar #; reserved for complex intra-articular # (e.g., triplane # of ankle, complex Salter-Harris III/IV).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Triage & Resuscitation
* **ATLS Protocol**: Prioritize ABCDE for poly-trauma.
* **Pain Management**: IV/IN Fentanyl or Morphine; avoid oral analgesia if requiring emergency theatre.

### Acute Immobilization
* **Splinting**: Above-elbow backslab in ~30° flexion (NEVER flex to 90° acutely → ↑ compartment pressure and vascular occlusion).
* **Elevation**: High arm sling to ↓ edema.

### Pulseless Pink Limb Protocol
* **Action**: Immediate gentle longitudinal traction and slight flexion in ED.
* **Outcome**: If pulse returns → splint. If limb remains pink but pulseless → urgent orthopaedic consult for theatre.

### Pulseless Pale / White Limb Protocol
* **Action**: True surgical emergency. Indicates brachial artery occlusion.
* **Intervention**: Urgent ED reduction ± immediate transfer to OT for exploration.

### NAI Safeguarding Protocol
* **Admission**: Mandatory admission for safety (Place of Safety order in HK).
* **Referral**: Immediate notification to Medical Social Worker (MSW) and Child Protection Registry.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Paediatric Incomplete Fracture Management
| # Type | Management |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Torus/Buckle** | Removable splint or soft cast x 2-3 weeks (non-operative). |
| **Greenstick (< 15° angulation)** | Above-elbow/knee cast immobilization x 4 weeks. |
| **Greenstick (> 15° angulation)** | Closed reduction (must complete the # on tension side to prevent recoil) → Cast. |

### Supracondylar Humerus Fracture Treatment Algorithm
| Gartland Type | Management Strategy |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Type I** | Non-operative. Collar & cuff or above-elbow cast x 3-4 weeks. |
| **Type II** | Closed Reduction and Percutaneous Pinning (CRPP). |
| **Type III** | Urgent CRPP. (Open reduction if closed fails, pulseless pale hand, or open #). |

### Surgical Principles of CRPP (閉合復位及經皮穿針固定術)
* **Setup**: Supine, C-arm image intensifier, general anaesthesia.
* **Reduction Sequence**: Traction → Correct rotation → Correct translation → Hyperflexion with olecranon push.
* **Pin Configuration**: 2 or 3 divergent/parallel **lateral** K-wires (minimizes Ulnar nerve injury risk). Medial cross-pin only if biomechanically unstable (mini-open technique to protect ulnar nerve).

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative & Cast Care
* **Immobilization Duration**: 3-4 weeks (faster union in kids). K-wires left protruding and removed in clinic at 3-4 weeks.
* **Wound Care**: Keep pin sites clean and dry to prevent pin-tract infection.

### Paediatric Physiotherapy (PT) Principles
* **Active ROM**: Let child resume movement naturally through play after cast removal.
* **Passive Stretching**: STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED at the elbow (high risk of myositis ossificans / heterotopic ossification).
* **Recovery Timeline**: Normal ROM generally returns within 6-12 weeks spontaneously.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Acute & Early Complications
* **Volkmann's Ischaemic Contracture (福克曼缺血性攣縮)**: End-stage of untreated compartment syndrome. Necrosis of volar forearm muscles → fibrosis → wrist flexion, MCP hyperextension, IP flexion.
* **Neurological Injury**: AIN (most common, usually neurapraxia recovering in 3-6 months), Ulnar nerve (iatrogenic from medial wire).
* **Pin-Tract Infection**: Superficial erythema (treat with oral Abx), deep osteomyelitis (requires early pin removal).

### Late Complications
* **Cubitus Varus ("Gunstock" Deformity) (肘內翻)**: Most common late complication. Due to malunion (uncorrected medial rotation/collapse). Mainly a cosmetic issue; does not restrict ROM. Treat with late corrective closing-wedge osteotomy if severe.
* **Physeal Arrest / Premature Closure**: Leads to limb length discrepancy (LLD) or angular deformity (high risk in Salter-Harris IV/V).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically test the Anterior Interosseous Nerve (AIN) in a 5-year-old? Answer: Ask the child to make an "OK" sign; if unable to flex the IP joint of the thumb and DIP of the index finger, it indicates AIN palsy.
* Trap: Flexing the elbow to 90 degrees to splint a Gartland III supracondylar fracture in the ED. Pearl: Strictly immobilize at 20-30 degrees of flexion acutely. 90 degrees of flexion compresses the brachial artery against the anterior fracture fragment and increases compartment pressures.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing a "pulseless" supracondylar fracture without checking capillary refill. A "pulseless pink" hand means collateral circulation is intact; it requires urgent reduction but is not as critically ischemic as a "pulseless pale" hand.
* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of Cubitus Varus following a supracondylar fracture? Answer: Malunion due to inadequate correction of internal rotation and medial comminution.
* Trap: Prescribing aggressive passive physiotherapy to a child to regain elbow extension after cast removal. Pearl: Passive stretching traumatizes the healing capsule and soft tissues, leading to myositis ossificans. Encourage active ROM through play only.
* Pitfall: Missing a Non-Accidental Injury (NAI). Always examine the whole child and look for pathognomonic fractures (metaphyseal corner fractures, posterior rib fractures, varying stages of callus) in infants presenting with vague trauma histories.
