# High Energy Trauma & Open Fractures (高能量創傷與開放性骨折)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Open Fractures & High Energy Trauma
* **Open Fracture (開放性骨折):** # with direct communication between fracture hematoma and external environment via soft tissue breach.
* **High Energy Trauma:** Injury transferring large kinetic energy to skeletal/soft tissue envelope → ↑ risk of comminution, devascularization, and neurovascular (NV) compromise.
* **Polytrauma:** Injury to ≥ 2 body systems + ISS (Injury Severity Score) > 16.

### Mechanism of Injury (MOI) for High Energy Trauma
* **Direct Trauma:**
	* **Crush Injury:** High mass, low velocity → severe soft tissue degloving (Morel-Lavallée lesion) & ischemia.
	* **High-Velocity Penetrating:** Gunshot wounds (kinetic energy cavitation).
	* **Motor Vehicle Collision (MVC):** Pedestrian vs. car, motorcycle crash.
	* **Fall From Height:** > 3 meters → axial loading forces.

### Epidemiology of High Energy Trauma
* **Bimodal Distribution:**
	* **Peak 1:** Young males (20-30s) → RTAs, sports, industrial accidents.
	* **Peak 2:** Elderly (osteoporotic #) → lower energy but catastrophic tissue envelope failure.
* **Most Common Open # Site:** Tibial diaphysis (subcutaneous anteromedial border).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Soft Tissue Envelope & Vascular Supply Biomechanics
* **Periosteal Blood Supply:** Disrupted in high-energy # → devitalized cortical bone → ↑ risk of non-union/infection.
* **Endosteal Blood Supply:** Disrupted by medullary nailing or medullary canal comminution.
* **Zone of Injury:** Microvascular thrombosis extends far beyond visible wound margin.

### Compartment Anatomy of the Lower Leg
* **Four Rigid Fascial Compartments:**
	* **Anterior:** Tibialis anterior, EHL, EDL, peroneus tertius, **Deep Peroneal Nerve**, **Anterior Tibial Artery**.
	* **Lateral:** Peroneus longus, peroneus brevis, **Superficial Peroneal Nerve**.
	* **Superficial Posterior:** Gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris, **Sural Nerve**.
	* **Deep Posterior:** Tibialis posterior, FHL, FDL, **Tibial Nerve**, **Posterior Tibial & Peroneal Arteries**.

### Compartment Anatomy of the Forearm
* **Three Rigid Fascial Compartments:**
	* **Volar (Flexor):** Superficial/deep flexors, **Median Nerve**, **Ulnar Nerve**, **Radial/Ulnar Arteries**.
	* **Dorsal (Extensor):** Extensors, brachioradialis, **PIN (Posterior Interosseous Nerve)**.
	* **Mobile Wad:** Brachioradialis, ECRL, ECRB.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Soft Tissue & Wound Assessment (Look, Feel, Move)
* **Look:**
	* Visible bone ends, bleeding (pulsatile vs. venous oozing).
	* Soft tissue degloving, contamination (dirt, grease, farm soil).
	* Skin viability (blanching, necrosis, tension blisters).
* **Feel:**
	* Palpable gaps in bone/tendon.
	* Swelling & tenseness (woody/hard compartments).
* **Move:**
	* Pain out of proportion to injury on passive stretch.
	* Inability to actively move digits.

### Compartment Syndrome (腔室症候群) Assessment (6 Ps)
* **Pain:** Out of proportion, exacerbated by passive stretch (earliest/most sensitive sign).
* **Paresthesia:** ↓ two-point discrimination (early nerve ischemia).
* **Pallor:** Late sign.
* **Paralysis:** Late sign (irreversible muscle necrosis).
* **Pulselessness:** Very late/unreliable sign (capillary pressure rises before arterial pressure falls).
* **Poikilothermia:** Cool limb.

### Hard vs. Soft Signs of Vascular Injury
| Vascular Sign Category | Clinical Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hard Signs** | Pulsatile bleeding, expanding hematoma, absent distal pulses, palpable thrill/bruit, visible ischemia (pallor/cyanosis). |
| **Soft Signs** | Hx of moderate hemorrhage, diminished but palpable pulse, peripheral nerve deficit, proximity of # to major vessel. |

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Gustilo-Anderson Classification System
| Grade         | Wound Size | Soft Tissue Damage                                             | Contamination     | Fracture Pattern               |
| :------------ | :--------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------- | :----------------------------- |
| **Type I**    | < 1 cm     | Clean, minimal muscle damage.                                  | Minimal           | Simple, minimal comminution.   |
| **Type II**   | 1 - 10 cm  | Moderate muscle damage, no crush.                              | Moderate          | Moderate comminution.          |
| **Type IIIA** | > 10 cm    | Severe crush, extensive stripping. **Adequate bone coverage.** | High (farm/crush) | Highly comminuted / Segmental. |
| **Type IIIB** | > 10 cm    | Severe loss. **Requires local/free flap coverage.**            | Massive           | Highly comminuted.             |
| **Type IIIC** | Any size   | **Vascular injury requiring repair.**                          | Massive           | Highly comminuted.             |

### Tscherne Classification for Closed Tissue Injury
| Grade       | Soft Tissue Assessment                                                                        |
| :---------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Grade 0** | Minimal soft tissue damage, simple #.                                                         |
| **Grade 1** | Superficial abrasion/contusion, mild-moderate #.                                              |
| **Grade 2** | Deep abrasion, skin/muscle contusion, impending compartment syndrome.                         |
| **Grade 3** | Extensive skin contusion/crush, severe muscle damage, subcutaneous avulsion (Morel-Lavallée). |

### Mangled Extremity Severity Score (MESS)
* **Skeletal/Soft Tissue Injury:** Low (1) to Massive/Crush (4).
* **Limb Ischemia:** None (0) to > 6 hours (3).
* **Shock:** Normotensive (0) to Persistent hypotension (2).
* **Age:** < 30 (0), 30-50 (1), > 50 (2).
* **Scoring threshold:** Score ≥ 7 predicts high likelihood of amputation.

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Standard Trauma Series Radiographs
* **C-Spine:** AP, Lateral, Peg (Odontoid) views.
* **CXR:** AP view (evaluate hemothorax/pneumothorax, widened mediastinum).
* **Pelvis:** AP view (evaluate pelvic ring stability).
* **Affected Limb:** AP & Lateral views (must include joint above & below #).

### Advanced Vascular & Polytrauma Imaging
* **Trauma CT Scan (Pan-Scan):** Brain, C-spine, Thorax, Abdomen, Pelvis.
* **CT Angiography (CTA):** Indications: hard signs of vascular injury, ABI $< 0.9$, Type IIIC #, knee dislocation (popliteal artery risk).
* **Duplex US:** If CTA unavailable or bedside assessment needed.

### Intracompartmental Pressure Measurement
* **Indication:** Obtunded/intubated polytrauma patient, equivocal clinical signs, pediatric patients.
* **Delta Pressure Formula:** $DP = \text{Diastolic BP} - \text{Compartment Pressure} < 30\text{ mmHg}$ indicates compartment syndrome.
* **Absolute Pressure:** $> 30\text{ mmHg}$ requires fasciotomy.

## 6. Initial ED Management (Reduction, Splinting/Casting, Traction)

### Acute ATLS & Polytrauma Protocol
* **C-Spine:** Immobilize (rigid collar, blocks, tape).
* **Primary Survey:** A (Airway), B (Breathing), C (Circulation/Hemorrhage control), D (Disability/GCS), E (Exposure).
* **Hemorrhage Control:** Direct pressure, pelvic binder, combat tourniquet (if life-threatening exanguination).
* **Fluid Resuscitation:** Warm crystalloids, 1:1:1 massive transfusion protocol if shock +ve.

### HK HA Emergency Open Fracture Protocol
* **Documentation:** Clinical photo of wound (avoids repeated un-dressing).
* **Wound Care:** Remove gross debris, cover with sterile saline-soaked dressing. Do NOT probe bone or perform mini-debridements in ED.
* **Medication (< 1 hour):** 
	* IV Antibiotics: 1st Gen Cephalosporin (e.g., Cefazolin) + Gentamicin for Type III. Add Penicillin if farm soil (Clostridium).
	* Tetanus toxoid/immunoglobulin booster.
* **Reduction & Splinting:** 
	* Gentle axial traction → correct gross deformity → Backslab splint (e.g., above-knee/below-knee).
	* Re-check NV status post-splinting.

### Emergency Compartment Syndrome Management
* **Immediate Decompression:** Remove all constrictive dressings, cut casts/padding down to skin (split completely).
* **Positioning:** Keep limb at level of heart (DO NOT elevate → ↓ arterial perfusion).
* **Oxygen & Resuscitation:** Maximize systemic perfusion to ischemic tissues.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative Indications & Techniques)

### Operative Debridement Principles (BOAST/HK HA Guidelines)
* **Timing:** Within 6 hours (high contamination/marine/agriculture) to 24 hours (low energy Type I).
* **Tourniquet:** Avoid inflation unless severe bleeding (blurs assessment of tissue viability).
* **Wound Extension:** Extend along extensile surgical planes (Z-plasty across joints).
* **Excision Rule (The 4 Cs of Muscle Viability):** Color, Consistency, Contractility (upon pinching/diathermy), Capacity to bleed.
* **Irrigation:** Copious low-pressure normal saline (3L for Type I, 6L for Type II, 9L for Type III). Do NOT use high-pressure lavage (drives debris into bone).

### Skeletal Stabilization Algorithm
| # Pattern / Condition | Preferred Definitive Fixation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Type I & II Open #** | Intramedullary (IM) Nailing (Tibia/Femur) / ORIF (Upper limb). |
| **Type III (Severe Contamination)** | Temporary External Fixator (Damage Control Orthopaedics). |
| **Polytrauma (Unstable pt)** | Damage Control Ex-Fix → Convert to definitive IM Nail in 1-2 weeks. |
| **Massive Bone Defect** | Bone transport (Ilizarov frame), Masquelet technique. |

### Soft Tissue Reconstruction & Flap Coverage Protocol
* **Timing of Coverage:** Ideally within 72 hours (Fix and Flap protocol).
* **Type IIIA:** Delayed primary closure, split-thickness skin graft (STSG), or Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (VAC).
* **Type IIIB (Local Flaps):** Proximal third tibia (Gastrocnemius flap), Middle third (Soleus flap).
* **Type IIIB (Free Flaps):** Distal third tibia requires high-energy tissue transfer (e.g., Latissimus Dorsi, Anterolateral Thigh - ALT flap) with microvascular anastomosis.

### Emergency Double-Incision 4-Compartment Fasciotomy (筋膜切開術)
* **Indication:** Compartment syndrome ($DP < 30\text{ mmHg}$ or clinical signs).
* **Incision 1 (Anterolateral):** 2 cm lateral to tibial crest. Decompresses Anterior and Lateral compartments. Preserve superficial peroneal nerve.
* **Incision 2 (Posteromedial):** 2 cm posterior to medial tibial border. Decompresses Superficial and Deep Posterior compartments. Preserve saphenous nerve and vein.
* **Wound Management:** Leave open → apply VAC dressing → delayed closure or STSG at 48-72 hours.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Early Inpatient Phase (0-2 Weeks)
* **Elevation:** Post-fasciotomy or flap surgery to ↓ edema.
* **Mobilization:** Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) or Touch Weight Bearing (TWB) depending on fracture stability & Ex-Fix rigidity.
* **DVT Prophylaxis:** LMWH (e.g., Enoxaparin) or DOACs per HK HA protocol, mechanical calf pumps (unaffected leg).
* **Joint ROM:** Passive/Active-assisted ROM of adjacent uninjured joints to prevent stiffness.

### Late Phase Rehabilitation Protocol
* **Weight Bearing Progression:** Gradual progression to Partial (PWB) → Full Weight Bearing (FWB) based on callus formation on X-ray.
* **Ex-Fix Pin Site Care:** Daily cleaning with chlorhexidine/saline to prevent pin tract infection.
* **Muscle Strengthening:** Isometric exercises progressing to concentric/eccentric loading.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Acute Complications of High Energy Trauma
* **Compartment Syndrome (腔室症候群):** Requires immediate fasciotomy.
* **Vascular Injury / Limb Ischemia:** Amputation if reperfusion $> 6$ hours.
* **Fat Embolism Syndrome (FES):** Triad of hypoxia, confusion, petechial rash (prevented by early # fixation).
* **Wound / Flap Failure:** Venous congestion or arterial thrombosis of microvascular free flap.
* **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) / Pulmonary Embolism (PE).**

### Chronic Complications of Open Fractures
* **Infection:** Superficial, deep space, or Chronic Osteomyelitis (骨髓炎).
* **Non-union / Delayed Union:** Avulsed periosteum limits healing potential.
* **Volkmann's Ischemic Contracture:** Late sequela of untreated compartment syndrome → muscle replaced by fibrous tissue → fixed deformities (e.g., claw hand/toes).
* **Post-Traumatic Osteoarthritis:** Secondary to intra-articular extension of #.
* **Chronic Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS).**

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most reliable clinical sign of impending compartment syndrome? Answer: Pain out of proportion to the apparent injury, specifically elicited by passive stretching of the muscles in the affected compartment.
* Trap: Assuming palpable distal pulses rule out compartment syndrome. Pearl: Pulses remain palpable until late stages because compartment pressure ($> 30\text{ mmHg}$) exceeds capillary/venous pressure but rarely exceeds systolic arterial pressure.
* Pitfall: Removing an impaled object at the accident scene or ED. Do not remove penetrating objects until in the operating theatre with vascular control ready, as the object may be tamponading a major vessel.
* Viva Question: What antibiotic coverage is required for a Gustilo Type III farm injury? Answer: IV 1st generation cephalosporin + aminoglycoside + Penicillin G (for Clostridium/gas gangrene).
* Trap: Elevating the leg to reduce swelling in suspected compartment syndrome. Pearl: Never elevate a limb with suspected compartment syndrome above heart level; this decreases arterial perfusion pressure to the ischemic tissues and exacerbates the condition.
* Pitfall: Performing primary closure on a contaminated, high-energy wound in the ED. Leave high-energy wounds open after theatre debridement; perform delayed primary closure or flap coverage to prevent necrotizing fasciitis or gas gangrene.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate between arterial and venous compromise in a free tissue flap post-op? Answer: Arterial failure appears pale, cool, and flat with delayed capillary refill; venous congestion appears swollen, dark blue/purple, with brisk/immediate capillary refill and rapid dark bleeding on pinprick.
