# Tendinopathies & Ankle Instability (肌腱病變與足踝不穩)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Achilles Tendon Rupture Definition & MOI (跟腱斷裂)
* **Definition**: Disruption of the triceps surae (gastrocnemius + soleus) conjoined tendon. 
* **Epidemiology**: Typically affects middle-aged males (weekend warriors, 30-50 y/o). 
* **MOI**: 
  * Sudden forced dorsiflexion on a plantarflexed foot (e.g., landing off an edge).
  * Pushing off a weight-bearing foot with the knee extended (e.g., badminton, tennis, basketball sprint).
  * Direct trauma (rare).
* **Risk Factors**: Fluoroquinolone antibiotics (ciprofloxacin), local corticosteroid injections, inflammatory arthropathies, gout, aging (degeneration).

### Patellar Tendinopathy Definition & MOI (髕骨肌腱病變)
* **Definition**: Overuse injury resulting in microtearing and focal degeneration at the inferior pole of the **Patella (髕骨)** (Jumper’s Knee).
* **Epidemiology**: Jumping athletes (volleyball, basketball).
* **MOI**: Repetitive eccentric loading of the knee extensor mechanism during jump landings.

### Lateral Ankle Sprain & Chronic Instability Definition & MOI (外踝韌帶扭傷及慢性不穩)
* **Definition**: Stretching or tearing of the lateral ligamentous complex of the ankle. Chronic Lateral Ankle Instability (CLAI) occurs when mechanical/functional instability persists >6 months post-injury.
* **Epidemiology**: Most common athletic injury presenting to ED.
* **MOI**: Inversion + internal rotation + plantarflexion force on a loaded foot (e.g., landing on another player's foot).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Achilles Tendon Anatomy & Watershed Zone
* **Structure**: Largest and strongest tendon in the human body. Fibers rotate 90° internally as they descend to insert on the posterior calcaneal tuberosity.
* **Vascular Supply**: 
  * Supplied proximally by musculotendinous junction, distally by osseous insertion, and segmentally by the paratenon (no true synovial sheath).
  * **Watershed Zone**: Area of critical hypovascularity located **$2-6\text{ cm}$ proximal to the calcaneal insertion**. Most common site of spontaneous rupture.

### Patellar Tendon Anatomy & Biomechanics
* **Structure**: Originates from the inferior pole of the patella, inserts into the **Tibial Tuberosity (脛骨粗隆)**. 
* **Biomechanics**: Transmits force from quadriceps to tibia. Force transmitted can reach up to 6-8x body weight during deep squatting/landing.

### Lateral Ankle Ligament Complex Anatomy
* **Anterior Talofibular Ligament (ATFL)**: 
  * Origin: Anterior distal fibula → Insertion: Talar neck.
  * Biomechanics: Primary restraint to anterior translation of talus in plantarflexion. Weakest ligament; 1° torn in sprains.
* **Calcaneofibular Ligament (CFL)**: 
  * Origin: Tip of lateral malleolus → Insertion: Lateral calcaneus.
  * Biomechanics: Primary restraint to inversion in neutral/dorsiflexion. Extracapsular. 2° torn.
* **Posterior Talofibular Ligament (PTFL)**: 
  * Origin: Posterior fibula → Insertion: Posterior talus.
  * Biomechanics: Strongest ligament. Rarely torn except in severe ankle dislocations.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Achilles Rupture Physical Examination
* **Look**: 
  * Swelling and bruising around the posterior ankle.
  * Loss of resting plantarflexion cascade (foot rests in more dorsiflexion compared to contralateral side).
* **Feel**: 
  * Palpable gap in the tendon ($2-6\text{ cm}$ proximal to insertion).
* **Move & Special Tests**: 
  * **Simmonds-Thompson Test (+ve)**: Squeezing the calf bulk fails to elicit passive plantarflexion. Pathognomonic for complete tear.
  * **Matles Test (+ve)**: Patient prone with knees flexed to 90°. The affected foot falls into neutral/dorsiflexion (normal side stays in resting plantarflexion).
  * **O’Brien Needle Test**: Needle inserted $10\text{ cm}$ proximal to insertion. If needle hub tilts proximally during passive dorsiflexion = tendon intact. (Rarely done acutely now due to USG availability).
* **Neurological**: Check **Sural nerve (腓腸神經)** sensation (lateral border of foot).

### Patellar Tendinopathy Physical Examination
* **Look**: Mild localized swelling at the inferior patellar pole.
* **Feel**: 
  * **Basset’s Sign**: Tenderness at the inferior pole of the patella is most prominent in full extension and significantly ↓ or disappears in 90° flexion (as the tendon becomes taut and covers the deeper inflamed fibers).
* **Move**: Pain exacerbated by resisted knee extension.

### Lateral Ankle Instability Physical Examination
* **Look**: Swelling over the anterolateral ankle, ecchymosis (acute). 
* **Feel**: Tenderness over ATFL/CFL. Rule out tenderness over base of 5th metatarsal and proximal fibula to exclude associated fractures.
* **Move & Special Tests**: 
  * **Anterior Drawer Test (+ve)**: Assesses ATFL. >$5\text{ mm}$ translation or soft end-point vs contralateral side.
  * **Talar Tilt Test (+ve)**: Assesses CFL. Inversion force applied to the hindfoot. Asymmetry indicates laxity.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Kuwada Classification for Achilles Tendon Defect
| Type | Defect Size |
|---|---|
| **I** | Partial tear |
| **II** | Complete tear with defect <$3\text{ cm}$ |
| **III** | Complete tear with defect $3-6\text{ cm}$ |
| **IV** | Complete tear with defect >$6\text{ cm}$ |

### Blazina Classification for Patellar Tendinopathy (Jumper's Knee)
| Phase | Clinical Symptoms |
|---|---|
| **I** | Pain ONLY after activity |
| **II** | Pain during and after activity (but no performance impairment) |
| **III** | Pain during and after activity (with performance impairment) |
| **IV** | Complete tendon rupture |

### West Point Ankle Sprain Grading System
| Grade | Ligament Damage | Clinical Features |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Microscopic tear of ATFL | Mild swelling, no instability, weight-bearing possible |
| **II** | Partial tear of ATFL (+/- CFL) | Moderate swelling, mild instability, painful weight-bearing |
| **III** | Complete tear of ATFL & CFL | Severe swelling/bruising, gross instability, NWB |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Achilles Tendon Rupture Imaging
* **X-ray**: 
  * Usually normal. 
  * Used to rule out avulsion fractures of the calcaneal tuberosity.
  * **Kager's triangle**: Obliteration of the normal radiolucent pre-Achilles fat pad indicates edema/hemorrhage.
* **Ultrasound (USG)**: 
  * First-line dynamic imaging. 
  * Assesses tendon gap size during passive dorsiflexion/plantarflexion (helps decide conservative vs op).
* **MRI**: 
  * Gold standard for chronic, delayed, or equivocal cases. 
  * Demonstrates tendon retraction and fatty infiltration of the calf muscles.

### Patellar Tendinopathy Imaging
* **USG**: Hypoechoic thickening of the proximal patellar tendon, focal neovascularization on color Doppler.
* **MRI**: Sagittal T2/STIR hyperintensity and thickening at the proximal insertion.

### Lateral Ankle Instability Imaging
* **X-ray (AP, Lateral, Mortise)**: 
  * Directed by **Ottawa Ankle Rules** (Image if: bone tenderness at posterior edge of lateral/medial malleolus, base of 5th MT, navicular, OR inability to bear weight for 4 steps).
  * Excludes malleolar fractures, talar dome osteochondral lesions (OCL).
* **Stress Radiographs**: 
  * Talar tilt >$10^{\circ}$ (or >$5^{\circ}$ compared to normal side) = CFL insufficiency.
  * Anterior translation >$10\text{ mm}$ = ATFL insufficiency.
* **MRI**: Pre-operative planning for chronic instability to assess ATFL/CFL integrity, exclude peroneal tendon pathology or OCL.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Achilles Rupture ED Management
* **Splinting**: Plaster of Paris (POP) backslab in resting **Equinus (Plantarflexion)** to approximate tendon ends. Do NOT splint in neutral (widens gap).
* **Weight-bearing**: Strictly Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) with crutches.
* **DVT Prophylaxis**: LMWH (e.g., Clexane) may be indicated based on local HA protocols for casted lower limbs.
* **Analgesia**: NSAIDs / Paracetamol.
* **Referral**: Urgent O&T outpatient clinic (within 1 week) for definitive decision.

### Acute Ankle Sprain ED Management
* **First Aid**: RICE protocol (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation).
* **Support**: Figure-of-8 tubular bandage, Tubigrip, or semi-rigid ankle brace (e.g., Air-Stirrup).
* **Weight-bearing**: Weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT). Early mobilization promotes better collagen alignment than strict immobilization.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Achilles Tendon Rupture: Conservative vs Operative Management
| Modality | Indications | Surgical / Treatment Principles |
|---|---|---|
| **Conservative (Functional Bracing)** | Acute closed rupture (<2 weeks), gap <$1\text{ cm}$ in equinus on USG, older/sedentary patients, high surgical risk (DM, PVD). HA trend favors this. | **VACOped / ROM boot protocol**: NWB in equinus (30°) for 2 weeks → progressive weight bearing with sequential removal of heel wedges to allow gradual dorsiflexion over 8 weeks. |
| **Operative Repair** | Athletes, delayed presentation (>2 weeks), gap >$1\text{ cm}$ in equinus, avulsion fractures, patient preference. | **Open Repair**: Krackow or Kessler locking suture technique (non-absorbable). <br> **Percutaneous Repair**: Less wound breakdown, but higher risk of **Sural nerve** entrapment. |

### Patellar Tendinopathy Management Algorithm
| Phase | Management Modalities |
|---|---|
| **Non-Operative (1st Line)** | Activity modification, NSAIDs. Eccentric quadriceps strengthening (decline squats). Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (ESWT). Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) injection (Avoid corticosteroid injections due to rupture risk). |
| **Operative (Refractory >6 mos)** | Open or arthroscopic debridement of necrotic tendon tissue, excision of inferior patellar pole osteophytes. |

### Lateral Ankle Instability Management Algorithm
| Condition | Management Modalities |
|---|---|
| **Acute Sprain** | Functional rehabilitation. Early ROM. Proprioceptive training (wobble board). Peroneal strengthening. |
| **Chronic Instability (CLAI)** | **Broström-Gould Procedure**: Anatomical repair and imbrication of the ATFL & CFL, reinforced with the extensor retinaculum. <br> **Tenodesis (e.g., Chrisman-Snook)**: Non-anatomical reconstruction using split peroneus brevis (for failed Broström or hyperlaxity/Ehlers-Danlos). |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Achilles Tendon Post-Management Rehab (HA Guidelines)
* **0-2 weeks**: NWB in equinus cast/boot (30°).
* **2-4 weeks**: Boot with 20° equinus wedge. Partial WBAT. Start active plantarflexion, NO active dorsiflexion past neutral.
* **4-6 weeks**: Boot with 10° equinus wedge. FWB. 
* **6-8 weeks**: Boot in neutral (0°).
* **>8 weeks**: Transition to normal shoe with small heel raise. Aggressive calf strengthening (concentric → eccentric).
* **Return to sports**: Typically 6-9 months minimum.

### Chronic Ankle Instability Post-Op Rehab
* **0-2 weeks**: NWB in POP backslab/boot.
* **2-6 weeks**: WBAT in walking boot. Start active straight-plane sagittal ROM (dorsiflexion/plantarflexion). Strictly avoid inversion/eversion.
* **>6 weeks**: Wean boot to ankle brace. Begin proprioception (wobble board) and peroneal muscle strengthening.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Achilles Rupture Complications
* **Re-rupture**: Historically higher in conservative management (10-12%) vs operative (2-3%), but modern functional bracing protocols have reduced conservative re-rupture rates to <5%.
* **Sural Nerve Injury**: Higher risk in percutaneous repair. Causes numbness over lateral foot.
* **Wound Breakdown/Infection**: 5-10% in open repairs (poor skin envelope posteriorly).
* **Heel Cord Lengthening**: Results in decreased push-off strength and limp.

### Lateral Ankle Instability Complications
* **Osteochondral Lesion (OCL) of Talus**: Chronic repeated impaction of talus against mortise.
* **Peroneal Tendinopathy / Subluxation**: Due to altered ankle biomechanics.
* **Ankle Osteoarthritis (OA)**: Chronic abnormal wear pattern.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most sensitive clinical test for diagnosing an Achilles tendon rupture? Answer: The Simmonds-Thompson test (calf squeeze). A positive test shows no passive plantarflexion.
* Viva Question: Why do we avoid corticosteroid injections in the Achilles and Patellar tendons? Answer: Corticosteroids inhibit collagen synthesis and promote tendon degeneration, significantly increasing the risk of iatrogenic rupture.
* Trap: Relying on the patient's ability to actively plantarflex to rule out an Achilles rupture. Pearl: Patients with a completely ruptured Achilles tendon can still actively plantarflex using intact secondary flexors (tibialis posterior, flexor hallucis longus, flexor digitorum longus, peroneals). Always perform the Simmonds-Thompson test.
* Trap: Splinting an acute Achilles rupture in a neutral (90 degree) position in the ED. Pearl: A neutral position distracts the torn tendon ends, preventing healing. You must apply the backslab in resting equinus (plantarflexion).
* Pitfall: Missing a proximal fibula fracture in a patient presenting with a severe lateral ankle sprain. Always palpate the entire length of the fibula to rule out a Maisonneuve fracture (proximal fibula fracture with syndesmotic disruption).
* Pitfall: Recommending strict immobilization for acute lateral ankle sprains. Early functional rehabilitation and weight-bearing as tolerated yields superior outcomes regarding joint stiffness and return to sport compared to prolonged cast immobilization.
