# Medical Legal Issues in Orthopaedics (骨科醫療法律問題)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Medical Negligence (醫療疏忽)
* **Duty of Care**: Doctor-patient relationship established → legal obligation to provide standard care.
* **Breach of Duty**: Care provided falls below acceptable standard (judged by peers/law).
* **Causation**: The breach directly → patient's injury (Foreseeability & "But-for" test).
* **Damages**: Actual physical, psychological, or financial harm suffered.

### "Mechanism of Injury" (Root Causes of Medico-Legal Claims)
* **Missed Diagnoses**: Failure to diagnose # (esp. scaphoid, NOF, subtle Lisfranc) or compartment syndrome.
* **Consent Failures**: Inadequate explanation of risks/alternatives → breach of Montgomery standard.
* **Intra-operative Errors**: Wrong-site surgery, iatrogenic NV injury, retained foreign bodies.
* **Post-operative Neglect**: Ignoring ↑ pain (compartment syndrome) or failing to document NV status post-casting.

### Epidemiology of O&T Claims in Hong Kong
* **High-Risk Specialty**: O&T consistently ranks in top 3 specialties for litigation globally & in HK HA.
* **Most Sued Sub-specialties**: Spine (paralysis risk) > Trauma (missed #, compartment syndrome) > Arthroplasty (infection, nerve injury).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics (Vascular supply, deforming muscle forces)

### "Anatomy" of Valid Informed Consent (知情同意)
* **Capacity**: Pt must be of sound mind to understand, retain, weigh up, & communicate decisions.
* **Voluntary**: Free from coercion (by family or medical staff).
* **Informed**: Must understand nature of condition, proposed Tx, material risks, & alternatives (including conservative Tx).

### "Biomechanics" of Medico-Legal Standards
* **Bolam Test (1957)**: Doctor is not negligent if acting in accordance with a responsible body of medical opinion.
* **Bolitho Modification (1997)**: The body of medical opinion must withstand logical analysis by the court.
* **Montgomery Standard (2015)**: Shift from doctor-centric to patient-centric. Must disclose risks that a *reasonable person in the patient's position* would attach significance to (Material Risk).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Critical Pre- & Post-Intervention Neurovascular (NV) Assessment
* **Look**: Skin color (pallor, cyanosis), capillary refill time (<2 secs), swelling.
* **Feel**: Peripheral pulses (grading 0 to 2+), temperature (coldness), sensory mapping of specific nerve dermatomes (e.g., 1st dorsal webspace for deep peroneal nerve).
* **Move**: Active ROM of distal digits. 
* **Crucial Legal Defense**: Documenting normal NV status *before* and *after* any reduction, casting, or surgical intervention.

### Assessment of Compartment Syndrome (The "Legal Landmine")
* **Look**: Tense, woody, swollen limb.
* **Feel**: Pain out of proportion to injury, firmness of compartments.
* **Move**: Extreme pain on passive stretch of muscles in the affected compartment (earliest & most reliable sign).
* **NV Status**: Pulselessness and paralysis are *late* signs (documenting these as primary assessment is legally indefensible).

### Assessment of Mental Capacity (精神行為能力評估)
* **Look**: Alertness, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), eye contact.
* **Feel**: (Not applicable physically, but assess emotional/affective state).
* **Move**: Can the patient communicate a consistent decision verbally, via writing, or via gestures?

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Classification of Legal Duties (Standard of Care)
| Legal Test | Core Principle | Medico-Legal Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bolam** | Peer-approved standard | Used for technical surgical competence. |
| **Bolitho** | Logical basis required | Court can reject "peer opinion" if illogical. |
| **Montgomery** | Material risk disclosure | Used exclusively for the Informed Consent process. |

### Classification of Consent Modalities
| Type of Consent | Description | Clinical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Implied** | Patient cooperates via action | Rolling up sleeve for blood test. |
| **Verbal** | Spoken agreement | Minor ward procedures (e.g., joint aspiration). |
| **Written** | Formal signed documentation | All elective & emergency operative procedures. |

### Grading of Mental Capacity (Mental Health Ordinance Cap 136)
* **Part IVC**: Legal framework in HK for treating adults lacking capacity.
* **Form 1**: Certificate of Necessity for emergency life-saving or limb-saving Tx (Requires 1 doctor, ideally 2).
* **Guardianship Board**: For non-emergency/elective decisions in incapacitated adults (NOK cannot legally consent for adults in HK).

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### The "Rules of Two" for Legal Defensibility in X-rays
* **2 Views**: AP & Lateral mandatory (missed #s usually occur on single views).
* **2 Joints**: Include joints above & below the suspected # (prevents missing associated injuries e.g., Monteggia/Galeazzi).
* **2 Limbs**: Compare with uninjured side in pediatric cases (growth plates vs #s).
* **2 Occasions**: Repeat X-ray in 10-14 days if clinical suspicion remains high but initial X-ray -ve (e.g., scaphoid).

### Medico-Legal Documentation of Radiology
* **Time & Date Stamp**: Ensure correct patient, correct side, correct date.
* **Post-Operative Imaging**: Mandatory documentation of implant position and fracture reduction prior to discharge.

## 6. Initial ED Management (Reduction, Splinting/Casting, Traction)

### Medico-Legal Prerequisites for ED Interventions
* **Document Baseline**: Pre-reduction NV status must be recorded.
* **Analgesia/Sedation**: Document specific drugs, doses, and patient monitoring parameters.
* **Post-Intervention**: Mandatory repeat NV status check immediately post-reduction/casting.

### Immediate Management of an Adverse Event (醫療事故)
* **Open Disclosure**: Immediate, honest explanation to patient/family. Empathy/apology ≠ admission of legal liability.
* **Documentation**: Objective, factual entry in patient notes. Do NOT alter previous notes.
* **AIRS Reporting**: File an Advance Incident Reporting System (AIRS) form immediately within HK HA.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative Indications & Techniques)

### Algorithm for Valid Consent in Elective Orthopaedics
| Step | Action Required to Satisfy Law |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Explain Diagnosis** | Clear, jargon-free explanation of pathology. |
| **2. Offer Alternatives** | MUST discuss non-operative options (e.g., conservative Tx for OA knee vs TKR). |
| **3. Detail Procedure** | Explain surgical steps, type of anesthesia, implant used. |
| **4. Disclose Risks** | Discuss common risks (>1%) and serious risks (death, amputation, paralysis, infection) specific to *this* patient (Montgomery). |
| **5. Cooling-off Period** | Allow time for patient to think (never consent on the corridor outside OT). |

### Management Algorithm for Incapacitated Patients (HK Context)
* **Emergency (Life/Limb Threatening)** → E.g., Open #, Compartment Syndrome, septic arthritis → 2 doctors sign Cap 136 Form 1 → Proceed to OT.
* **Elective (Non-Urgent)** → E.g., THR for OA in severe dementia → Cannot proceed on NOK signature → Apply to Guardianship Board.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Medico-Legal "Rehabilitation" (Risk Management)
* **Audit & M&M**: Mandatory presentation of complications at Morbidity & Mortality meetings.
* **Clinical Governance**: Adherence to local HK HA guidelines (e.g., prophylactic antibiotics for open #s, DVT prophylaxis).
* **Continuous Medical Education (CME)**: Maintaining surgical logbooks and attending courses to prove up-to-date competence.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Legal & Professional Complications
* **Civil Litigation**: Lawsuit for damages/financial compensation (Requires proving Duty, Breach, Causation, Damages).
* **Medical Council of Hong Kong (MCHK)**: Disciplinary hearings for professional misconduct (can → suspension or striking off register).
* **Coroner's Court**: Mandatory reporting for deaths within 24 hours of operation/anesthesia, or unexpected intra-operative deaths.
* **Criminal Negligence**: Extremely rare; gross negligence manslaughter.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the legal standard required for informed consent today? Answer: The Montgomery standard, which requires disclosing any material risk that a reasonable person in the patient's position would consider significant.
* Trap: Having a patient's son sign the consent form for his 80-year-old father's elective total knee replacement because the father has dementia. Pearl: In Hong Kong, next-of-kin cannot legally consent for an adult. You must refer to the Guardianship Board for elective procedures if the patient lacks capacity.
* Pitfall: Delegating the consent process for a complex spine surgery to a junior house officer. This breaches the Montgomery standard, as a junior doctor cannot adequately explain all material risks or answer detailed questions about alternatives, invalidating the consent.
* Viva Question: A patient develops a foot drop after a fibular head fracture. How do you defend yourself against a claim of iatrogenic injury? Answer: By presenting clear clinical notes documenting a detailed pre-intervention and post-intervention neurovascular assessment proving the common peroneal nerve was assessed.
* Trap: Only checking for the absence of pulses when assessing for compartment syndrome. Pearl: Pulselessness is a late sign. Legally and clinically, you must assess and document pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch of the digits.
* Pitfall: Altering clinical notes after receiving a patient complaint or legal letter. Modifying notes retroactively is professional misconduct and will destroy your credibility in court or at the MCHK; always write a separate, dated, and signed addendum if retrospective clarification is needed.
