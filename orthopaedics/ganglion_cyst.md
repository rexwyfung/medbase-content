# Ganglion Cyst (腱鞘囊腫)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Ganglion Cyst
* **Benign** mucin-filled soft tissue cyst
* **Originates** from adjacent joint capsule, ligament, or tendon sheath
* **Lacks** true epithelial lining (pseudocyst)
* **Fluid contents**: highly viscous mucopolysaccharide (hyaluronic acid)

### Pathogenesis & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)
* **Idiopathic** in most cases
* **Microtrauma**: repetitive stress → joint capsule/ligament degeneration
* **Mucinous degeneration** theory: extra-articular mucin production from degenerated connective tissue
* **One-way valve** mechanism: synovial fluid pumps from joint into cyst but cannot return

### Epidemiology of Ganglion Cyst
* **Most common** soft tissue mass of the hand & wrist (60-70%)
* **Gender**: F > M (3:1 ratio)
* **Peak Age**: 20-40 years old (except mucous cysts: 50-70 years old)
* **Spontaneous resolution**: occurs in ~50% of cases

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Dorsal Wrist Ganglion Anatomy
* **Most common** site (60-70% of wrist ganglions)
* **Origin**: **Scapholunate (SL) ligament** (dorsal aspect)
* **Location**: typically between **EPL** (3rd compartment) and **EDC** (4th compartment)
* **Nerve relation**: **Posterior Interosseous Nerve (PIN)** provides articular branching here (source of deep ache)

### Volar Wrist Ganglion Anatomy
* **2nd most common** site (15-20%)
* **Origin**: **Radiocarpal joint** or **Scaphotrapezial (STT) joint**
* **Location**: usually between **FCR** and **Abductor Pollicis Longus (APL)**
* **Vascular relation**: deeply intertwined with or enveloping the **Radial Artery** (橈動脈)

### Retinacular & Mucous Cyst Anatomy
* **Volar Retinacular Cyst**: arises from **A1 or A2 pulley** of flexor tendon sheath
* **Digital Mucous Cyst**: arises from **DIP Joint** capsule
* **DIPJ biomechanics**: intimately associated with underlying **Heberden's nodes** (osteoarthritis osteophytes) and **Extensor tendon insertion**

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Ganglion Cyst Inspection (Look)
* **Mass**: solitary, well-circumscribed, smooth, spherical/lobulated swelling
* **Size**: fluctuates over time (↑ with activity, ↓ with rest)
* **Transillumination test**: brightly **+ve** (distinguishes from solid tumor/giant cell tumor of tendon sheath)
* **Nail changes**: longitudinal nail grooving (if mucous cyst compresses germinal matrix)

### Ganglion Cyst Palpation (Feel)
* **Consistency**: firm, rubbery, fluctuant
* **Tenderness**: typically painless or mildly tender
* **Mobility**: tethered to underlying joint/tendon, strictly NOT tethered to overlying skin
* **Provocation**: dorsal cyst becomes more prominent and tense with **active wrist flexion**

### Range of Motion & Neurovascular Exam for Ganglion Cyst
* **ROM**: mostly full; terminal flexion/extension may elicit mechanical block or mild pain
* **Vascular (Crucial for Volar)**: **Allen’s Test** must be performed to assess radial/ulnar collateral flow
* **Neurological**: rarely causes compression neuropathy
* **Ulnar nerve**: can be compressed by volar ulnar cyst in **Guyon's canal**
* **Median nerve**: can be compressed in **Carpal Tunnel**

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Anatomical Classification of Ganglion Cysts
* **System**: Not formally graded; classified strictly by anatomic origin/location

| Ganglion Type | Primary Anatomical Location | Articular/Tendon Origin | Key Clinical Association |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dorsal Wrist** | Dorsum of wrist (over SL interval) | Dorsal SL ligament | Most common type |
| **Volar Wrist** | Volar aspect of wrist | Radiocarpal / STT joint | Radial artery proximity |
| **Volar Retinacular**| Base of digit (volar aspect) | A1 / A2 pulley | Painful gripping |
| **Mucous Cyst** | Dorsum of DIP joint | DIP joint capsule | DIPJ Osteoarthritis |
| **Occult** | Intra-articular / Intra-osseous | Intercarpal ligaments | Unexplained wrist pain |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Radiographic (X-ray) Findings for Ganglion Cyst
* **Standard views**: AP, Lateral, Oblique of wrist/hand
* **Bone/Joint**: typically normal (soft tissue shadows only)
* **Specific exclusions**: 
* **SL dissociation**: look for Terry Thomas sign / widened SL interval >3mm
* **Osteoarthritis**: essential for mucous cysts (joint space narrowing, osteophytes at DIPJ)
* **Trauma**: rule out old **Scaphoid #**

### Ultrasound (USG) Features of Ganglion Cyst
* **1° imaging modality** in HK HA outpatient setting for confirmation
* **Echogenicity**: anechoic or hypoechoic well-defined cystic lesion
* **Structure**: multiloculated or unilocular
* **Sign**: **Posterior acoustic enhancement** +ve
* **Pedicle**: often identifies the stalk connecting to the joint capsule

### MRI Indications & Features for Ganglion Cyst
* **Indications**: suspected occult ganglion, atypical presentation, solid mass rule-out
* **T1-weighted**: uniformly low signal intensity
* **T2-weighted**: uniformly high signal intensity (bright fluid)
* **Contrast (Gadolinium)**: no central enhancement (peripheral rim enhancement only)

## 6. Initial ED & Outpatient Management

### Acute/Conservative Management of Ganglion Cyst
* **Reassurance**: primary treatment (50% spontaneous resolution)
* **Activity modification**: avoid repetitive stress to affected joint
* **Immobilization**: short-term resting wrist splint if acutely painful
* **Traditional remedies**: "Bible bump" smashing is strictly **contraindicated** in modern practice (high risk of capsular/ligament injury)

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Definitive Management Indications for Ganglion Cyst
* **System**: Non-operative vs. Operative Excision

| Modality | Primary Indications | Recurrence Rate | Key HK Local Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Observation** | Asymptomatic, cosmetically acceptable | N/A | 1° line in HA SOPC |
| **Aspiration** | Dorsal cysts, symptomatic, pt refuses surgery | 50-60% | Often combined with steroid |
| **Surgical Excision**| Severe pain, NV compromise, recurrent, aesthetic | 10-15% | Requires stalk + capsular excision |

### Ganglion Cyst Aspiration Principles
* **Dorsal cysts**: aspirate with large-bore needle (18G) due to thick jelly-like mucin
* **Adjunct**: +/- injection of corticosteroid and local anesthetic (minimally improves cure rate)
* **Volar cysts**: Aspiration generally **avoided** (or done strictly with USG guidance) due to extreme risk of **Radial Artery** puncture

### Surgical Excision Principles for Ganglion Cyst
* **Anesthesia**: GA, Regional block, or Wide Awake Local Anesthesia No Tourniquet (WALANT)
* **Dorsal approach**: transverse incision across Langer's lines over cyst
* **Key Surgical Step 1**: careful dissection of surrounding structures (protect **Superficial Radial Nerve** branches)
* **Key Surgical Step 2 (Crucial)**: trace stalk to the origin and excise a 1x1cm window of the **joint capsule** (SL ligament) to prevent recurrence
* **Mucous cyst specific**: MUST excise underlying osteophyte (Heberden's node) or recurrence is near 100%

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Excision Rehabilitation for Ganglion Cyst
* **0-2 weeks**: Volar resting splint (wrist in neutral), elevate to ↓ edema
* **Digital ROM**: immediate full active ROM of fingers to prevent adhesions
* **2-4 weeks**: remove sutures (Day 10-14), commence active/active-assisted wrist ROM
* **4-6 weeks**: strengthening exercises, aggressive scar massage to prevent hypertrophic scarring and contractures

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Complications of Ganglion Cyst Aspiration
* **Recurrence**: very high (up to 60% for dorsal, 80% for volar)
* **Infection**: septic arthritis if needle tracks into joint capsule
* **Vascular injury**: inadvertent radial artery puncture (volar cysts)

### Complications of Ganglion Cyst Excision
* **Recurrence**: 10-15% (usually due to failure to excise capsular root/stalk)
* **Stiffness**: most common postoperative morbidity (decreased terminal wrist flexion)
* **Neuroma**: injury to **Superficial Radial Nerve** (SRN) or **Palmar Cutaneous Branch of Median Nerve** (PCBMN)
* **Vascular**: **Radial Artery** laceration or thrombosis (volar excision)
* **Scar**: Hypertrophic scarring / keloid formation (common over dorsal wrist crease)

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical surgical step in excising a dorsal wrist ganglion to prevent recurrence? Answer: Excising the stalk along with a generous window of the underlying joint capsule (usually the dorsal SL ligament capsule).
* Trap: Recommending blind aspiration for a volar wrist ganglion in the clinic. Pearl: Volar cysts are intimately related to the radial artery. Blind aspiration carries a high risk of arterial puncture; they should either be observed or surgically excised/aspirated under direct USG guidance.
* Pitfall: Excising a digital mucous cyst without addressing the underlying joint. Recurrence is guaranteed if you do not simultaneously debride the underlying DIP joint osteophyte (Heberden's node) during cyst excision.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate a ganglion cyst from a solid soft tissue tumor like a giant cell tumor of the tendon sheath? Answer: A ganglion cyst will brightly transilluminate when a penlight is applied, whereas a solid tumor will not.
* Trap: Assuming restricted terminal wrist flexion post-excision is a surgical failure. Pearl: Mild loss of terminal flexion is a common, expected outcome after excision of the dorsal capsule, and patients must be counselled on this pre-operatively.
