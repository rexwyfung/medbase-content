# Common Foot and Ankle Conditions (常見足踝疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Hallux Valgus (拇趾外翻) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Complex triplanar deformity characterized by lateral deviation of the great toe (hallux) and medial deviation of the 1st metatarsal (varus).
* **Epidemiology:** ♀ > ♂ (ratio 15:1). Strong genetic predisposition (FHx +ve in 70%).
* **Risk Factors:** Tight, narrow-toe box footwear (extrinsic); pes planus, ligamentous laxity, Achilles contracture (intrinsic).

### Pes Planus (扁平足) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Loss or collapse of the **medial longitudinal arch (內側縱弓)**.
* **Flexible vs. Rigid:** Flexible = arch reconstitutes on toe-stand; Rigid = arch remains flat (often 2° to tarsal coalition in youths).
* **Adult-Acquired Flatfoot Deformity (AAFD):** Primarily caused by **Posterior Tibial Tendon Dysfunction (PTTD)**. ♀ > ♂, > 40 years old, obesity, diabetes.

### Plantar Fasciitis (足底筋膜炎) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Degenerative microtears (fasciosis, not purely inflammatory) of the plantar fascia at its origin on the **medial calcaneal tubercle**.
* **Epidemiology:** Most common cause of heel pain. Peaks in 40-60 y/o.
* **Risk Factors:** Obesity, prolonged standing, tight gastrocnemius-soleus complex, sudden ↑ in running distance.

### Ankle Fractures (足踝骨折) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Fractures involving lateral malleolus (fibula), medial malleolus (tibia), and/or posterior malleolus (tibia).
* **MOI:** Typically indirect twisting forces. MOI dictates fracture pattern (e.g., Supination-External Rotation [SER] is most common, → spiral fibular #).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### 1st Metatarsophalangeal (MTP) Joint Biomechanics
* **Deforming Forces:** As 1st MT drifts medially, **Adductor hallucis** and lateral capsule contract, pulling proximal phalanx laterally.
* **Sesamoid Subluxation:** 1st MT head slips off the sesamoid apparatus medially; sesamoids appear "subluxated" laterally into the intermetatarsal space.
* **Bowstringing:** Extensor Hallucis Longus (EHL) and Flexor Hallucis Longus (FHL) drift laterally, acting as bowstrings that exacerbate the valgus deformity.

### Medial Longitudinal Arch Biomechanics
* **Dynamic Stabilizer:** **Tibialis Posterior (脛後肌)** tendon (inverts and plantarflexes; locks transverse tarsal joint during toe-off).
* **Static Stabilizers:** **Spring ligament (Calcaneonavicular ligament)**, plantar fascia, short/long plantar ligaments.
* **Windlass Mechanism:** Dorsiflexion of toes tenses plantar fascia → elevates medial arch → creates rigid lever for propulsion.

### Ankle Syndesmosis (脛腓韌帶聯合) Anatomy
* **Function:** Maintains integrity of ankle mortise.
* **Components:** 
  * Anterior Inferior Tibiofibular Ligament (AITFL)
  * Posterior Inferior Tibiofibular Ligament (PITFL)
  * Interosseous Membrane/Ligament (IOM/IOL)

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Hallux Valgus Physical Assessment
* **Look:** Medial eminence prominence (bunion), pronation of great toe, overriding/crowding of lesser toes, hammer toes.
* **Feel:** Tenderness/bursa over medial 1st MT head.
* **Move:** Assess 1st MTPJ ROM (crepitus suggests osteoarthritis). Assess Silfverskiöld test for Achilles tightness.

### Pes Planus (PTTD) Physical Assessment
* **Look:** Valgus hindfoot, abducted forefoot. **"Too many toes" sign (多趾徵)** (viewed from behind, >1.5 toes visible lateral to heel).
* **Feel:** Tenderness along the course of the Tibialis Posterior tendon (posterior to medial malleolus).
* **Move:** **Single-leg heel raise test (單腳踮起測試)** (Normal: heel inverts; PTTD: inability to perform or heel stays in valgus).

### Plantar Fasciitis Physical Assessment
* **Look:** Usually no gross deformity.
* **Feel:** Pinpoint tenderness over the **medial calcaneal tubercle (內側跟骨結節)**.
* **Move:** Pain exacerbated by passive dorsiflexion of toes (Windlass test). 

### Ankle Fracture & Trauma Assessment
* **Look:** Gross deformity, rapid swelling, bruising, skin integrity (blisters/open wounds).
* **Feel:** Bony point tenderness over medial/lateral malleoli, proximal fibula (Maisonneuve).
* **Neurovascular (NV) Status:** 
  * Pulses: **Dorsalis Pedis (DP)**, **Posterior Tibial (PT)**.
  * Nerve: Check **Deep Peroneal Nerve** (sensation in 1st dorsal web space) and **Superficial Peroneal Nerve** (dorsum of foot).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Hallux Valgus Severity Classification
| Grade | Hallux Valgus Angle (HVA) | Intermetatarsal Angle (IMA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | $< 15^\circ$ | $< 9^\circ$ |
| **Mild** | $15^\circ - 20^\circ$ | $9^\circ - 11^\circ$ |
| **Moderate** | $20^\circ - 40^\circ$ | $11^\circ - 16^\circ$ |
| **Severe** | $> 40^\circ$ | $> 16^\circ$ |

### PTTD (Adult-Acquired Flatfoot) Johnson & Strom Classification
| Stage | Clinical Features | Joint Deformity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Pain/swelling along tendon, normal heel raise | No deformity |
| **II** | Weak heel raise, +ve "too many toes" sign | Flexible flatfoot deformity |
| **III** | Severe pain, cannot perform heel raise | Rigid, fixed flatfoot deformity |
| **IV** | Lateral ankle pain (fibular impingement) | Rigid flatfoot + Ankle joint arthritis |

### Danis-Weber Classification for Ankle Fractures
| Type | Fibular Fracture Level | Syndesmosis | Stability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Below syndesmosis | Intact | Usually stable |
| **B** | At level of syndesmosis | Intact or torn (variable) | Variable (depends on medial side) |
| **C** | Above syndesmosis | Torn (IOM disrupted) | Always unstable |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Foot/Ankle X-ray Standard Views
* **Foot:** Weight-bearing (WB) AP, WB Lateral, Oblique.
* **Ankle:** AP, Lateral, **Mortise view** (AP taken with leg internally rotated $15^\circ-20^\circ$ to assess articular space).

### Hallux Valgus Radiographic Parameters
* **HVA & IMA:** Measured on WB AP view.
* **Distal Metatarsal Articular Angle (DMAA):** Evaluates joint congruency.
* **Sesamoid Station:** Grades degree of lateral sesamoid subluxation (Grades 1-3).

### Pes Planus Radiographic Parameters
* **Lateral View:** ↑ Talo-1st Metatarsal Angle (**Meary's angle**; normal is $0^\circ$). ↓ Calcaneal pitch (normal $>20^\circ$).
* **AP View:** ↑ Talo-navicular uncoverage ($>30\%$).

### Ankle Fracture Radiographic Signs
* **Medial Clear Space (MCS):** $>4$ mm on mortise view indicates deltoid ligament rupture/lateral talar shift.
* **Tibiofibular Overlap:** $<1$ mm on mortise view indicates syndesmotic injury.
* **Proximal Fibula:** Always check for high fibula # (Maisonneuve fracture).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Ankle Fracture ED Protocol
* **Resuscitation:** ATLS protocol if high-energy trauma.
* **Reduction:** Immediate closed reduction under conscious sedation/ENTONOX if ankle is clinically dislocated/subluxed.
* **Immobilization:** Apply well-padded **Below-Knee (BK) Backslab** (U-slab + posterior slab).
* **Post-reduction X-ray:** Mandatory to confirm restoration of joint congruency.
* **Symptom Control:** Elevation on Braun frame (strict ICE + elevation to ↓ swelling), NBM if awaiting OT, analgesia.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Hallux Valgus Management Algorithm
| Treatment | Indications | Specific Techniques/Devices |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | Mild symptoms, poor surgical candidates | Wide toe-box shoes, toe spacers, orthotics, NSAIDs. |
| **Operative: Distal Osteotomy** | Mild-Moderate (#, IMA $<13^\circ$) | **Chevron osteotomy** (V-shaped cut at MT neck). |
| **Operative: Diaphyseal Osteotomy** | Moderate-Severe (IMA $13^\circ-20^\circ$) | **Scarf osteotomy** (Z-step cut along MT shaft). |
| **Operative: Arthrodesis** | Severe (IMA $>20^\circ$) or hypermobile 1st TMTJ | **Lapidus procedure** (1st TMT joint fusion). |
| **Operative: MTPJ Fusion** | Severe OA of 1st MTPJ + severe deformity | 1st MTPJ Arthrodesis. |

### Pes Planus (PTTD) Management Algorithm
| Treatment | Indications | Specific Interventions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | Stage I, poor surgical candidates | Medial arch supports, rigid UCBL orthoses, NSAIDs, PT. |
| **Operative: Soft Tissue/Osteotomy**| Stage II (Flexible) | FDL tendon transfer + Medial displacement calcaneal osteotomy (MDCO). |
| **Operative: Arthrodesis** | Stage III (Rigid) | **Triple Arthrodesis** (Subtalar, Talo-navicular, Calcaneo-cuboid joints). |
| **Operative: Pan-talar Fusion** | Stage IV (Rigid + Ankle OA) | Triple arthrodesis + Ankle joint fusion (Tibiotalar). |

### Plantar Fasciitis Management Algorithm
* **First-line (Conservative - $>90\%$ success):** Calf/plantar fascia stretching exercises, heel cups/cushions, NSAIDs.
* **Second-line:** Night splints (keeps foot in dorsiflexion), **Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (ESWT)**.
* **Third-line:** Corticosteroid injections (risk of fat pad atrophy and fascia rupture).
* **Operative (Rare, $<5\%$):** Plantar fascia release (if conservative fails $>6-12$ months).

### Ankle Fracture Management Algorithm
| Treatment | Indications | Technique |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | Undisplaced, isolated lateral malleolus (Weber A, stable Weber B), intact mortise. | CAM boot or short leg cast for 4-6 weeks (WBAT). |
| **Operative** | Bimalleolar, Trimalleolar, Unstable Weber B (talar shift), All Weber C, Open #. | **ORIF**: Fibular plate & screws, medial malleolus tension band wire/cannulated screws. Syndesmotic screw if needed. |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Op Ankle Fracture (ORIF) Rehab
* **0-2 Weeks:** Non-weight bearing (NWB) in backslab/boot. Strict elevation. Suture removal at 14 days.
* **2-6 Weeks:** NWB or protected WB (depending on fixation rigidity). Begin active ROM out of boot to prevent stiffness.
* **6-12 Weeks:** Advance to full weight-bearing (FWB) as tolerated. Proprioceptive training (wobble board) to prevent chronic instability.

### Post-Op Hallux Valgus Rehab
* **0-6 Weeks:** Heel-weight bearing only using specialized stiff-soled shoe (Darco shoe) to protect osteotomy site.
* **6+ Weeks:** Transition to wide soft shoes. FWB. Toe ROM exercises.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Hallux Valgus Surgery Complications
* **Hallux Varus:** Overcorrection (excessive lateral release or overtightening of medial capsule).
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN):** Of 1st MT head, especially if extensive lateral soft tissue release combined with distal osteotomy.
* **Transfer Metatarsalgia:** Altered biomechanics causing ↑ pressure on 2nd/3rd MT heads.
* **Recurrence:** High if underlying hypermobility is missed.

### Ankle Fracture Complications
* **Post-Traumatic Osteoarthritis:** Highly probable if articular step-off $>1$ mm remains.
* **Skin Necrosis / Wound Breakdown:** Ankle has thin soft tissue envelope. Surgical incisions over swollen tissues risk dehiscence (wait for "wrinkle sign" before surgery).
* **Chronic Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS):** Disproportionate pain, swelling, skin changes post-injury.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What constitutes the "Ottawa Ankle Rules" to justify an X-ray? Answer: Bone tenderness at posterior edge/tip of lateral or medial malleolus, base of 5th metatarsal, or navicular; OR inability to weight bear (4 steps) immediately and in the ED.
* Viva Question: Why do we order a weight-bearing X-ray for Hallux Valgus? Answer: Non-weight bearing films underestimate the true intermetatarsal and hallux valgus angles due to lack of gravity and physiological loading.
* Trap: Treating Plantar Fasciitis aggressively with multiple steroid injections. Pearl: Repeated corticosteroid injections significantly increase the risk of plantar fascia rupture and irreversible heel fat pad atrophy.
* Trap: Assuming an isolated medial malleolus fracture is a simple injury. Pearl: Always palpate the proximal fibula and order full-length tib-fib X-rays to rule out a Maisonneuve fracture (high fibular # with torn syndesmosis).
* Pitfall: Rushing an ankle ORIF when the ankle is severely swollen. Waiting for soft tissue swelling to subside is mandatory to prevent devastating wound dehiscence. Look for the "wrinkle sign" before operating.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing heel spurs on X-ray as the primary cause of Plantar Fasciitis. Heel spurs are a radiographic finding present in 50% of asymptomatic adults and are not the direct source of pain; the pain is from fascial microtears.
