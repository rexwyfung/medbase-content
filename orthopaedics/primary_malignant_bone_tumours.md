# Primary Malignant Bone Tumours (原發性惡性骨腫瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Osteosarcoma (骨肉瘤) Epidemiology & Pathogenesis
* **Definition:** Most common 1° malignant bone tumour producing malignant osteoid.
* **Epidemiology:** Bimodal age distribution. 
* **1° Peak:** 10-25 yrs (associated with rapid bone growth during puberty).
* **2° Peak:** >60 yrs (secondary to **Paget's disease**, prior irradiation, bone infarcts).
* **Location:** Metaphysis of long bones (Distal femur > Proximal tibia > Proximal humerus).

### Ewing's Sarcoma (尤文氏肉瘤) Epidemiology & Pathogenesis
* **Definition:** Highly malignant round cell tumour of neural crest origin.
* **Genetics:** Pathognomonic chromosomal translocation **$t(11;22)(q24;q12)$** → EWS-FLI1 fusion protein.
* **Epidemiology:** 2nd most common 1° pediatric bone tumour (Peak 5-20 yrs). Rare in adults.
* **Location:** Diaphysis of long bones (Femur, tibia) & flat bones (Pelvis).

### Chondrosarcoma (軟骨肉瘤) Epidemiology & Pathogenesis
* **Definition:** Malignant tumour of cartilage-forming cells.
* **Epidemiology:** Older adults (40-75 yrs).
* **Location:** Pelvis, proximal femur, proximal humerus.
* **Pathogenesis:** Can be 1° or 2° (malignant transformation of osteochondroma or enchondroma).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Anatomical Distribution of Bone Tumours
* **Metaphysis (Osteosarcoma):** High vascularity & rapid osteoblastic activity near the **physis (骺板)** during growth spurts ↑ risk of malignant transformation.
* **Diaphysis (Ewing's):** Marrow-rich cavity; allows rapid intramedullary spread before cortical breach.
* **Epiphysis:** Rarely involved by 1° malignancies (except Giant Cell Tumour/Chondroblastoma).

### Compartmental Anatomy & Tumour Spread
* **Natural Barriers:** Articular cartilage, joint capsule, and thick fascial septa act as natural barriers to local invasion.
* **Path of Least Resistance:** Tumours spread longitudinally within the medullary canal (**skip lesions**) and via venous channels.
* **Biopsy Tract Contamination:** Any fascial compartment breached during biopsy becomes contaminated with tumour cells and MUST be excised entirely during definitive surgery.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### General Inspection (Look)
* Swelling / rapidly enlarging mass.
* Overlying skin changes: Erythema, shiny skin, prominent engorged superficial veins.
* Cachexia or weight loss (late sign).
* Obvious deformity if presented with **Pathological fracture (病理性骨折)**.

### Palpation (Feel)
* Fixed, hard, irregular, and tender bony mass.
* Local warmth over the tumour site (↑ vascularity).
* Assessment of regional lymph nodes (rarely +ve in sarcomas unless advanced).

### Range of Motion & Neurovascular Assessment (Move & NV)
* **Move:** ↓ ROM of adjacent joints due to pain, mechanical block, or joint effusion.
* **NV:** Distal pulses may be ↓ if tumour compresses major vessels (e.g., popliteal artery in distal femur lesions).
* Distal neurological deficit if nerve compression exists (e.g., common peroneal nerve stretching).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Enneking Surgical Staging System for Musculoskeletal Sarcomas
* Used to stage mesenchymal tumours based on Grade (G), Site (T), and Metastasis (M).

| Stage | Grade (G) | Site (T) | Metastasis (M) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **IA** | G1 (Low grade) | T1 (Intracompartmental) | M0 (None) |
| **IB** | G1 (Low grade) | T2 (Extracompartmental) | M0 (None) |
| **IIA** | G2 (High grade) | T1 (Intracompartmental) | M0 (None) |
| **IIB** | G2 (High grade) | T2 (Extracompartmental) | M0 (None) |
| **III** | Any G | Any T | M1 (Regional/Distant) |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiograph (X-ray) Tumor Characteristics
* **Zone of Transition:** Wide/ill-defined (indicates aggressive, rapid growth).
* **Bone Destruction:** Permeative or moth-eaten osteolysis.
* **Periosteal Reactions:**
  * **Codman's triangle (科德曼三角):** Elevation of periosteum at the tumour edge.
  * **Sunburst appearance (日光放射狀外觀):** Spiculated, perpendicular new bone formation (**Osteosarcoma**).
  * **Onion-skinning (洋蔥皮樣外觀):** Multi-lamellated periosteal reaction (**Ewing's**).
* **Matrix:** Osteoid matrix (cloudy/fluffy in Osteosarcoma), Chondroid matrix (popcorn/stippled calcification in Chondrosarcoma).

### Advanced Local Staging MRI Imaging
* **MRI entire affected bone (with Gadolinium):** Gold standard for local staging.
* Determines exact intramedullary extent.
* Identifies **skip lesions** (metastasis within the same bone).
* Assesses extra-osseous soft tissue mass and Neurovascular (NV) bundle involvement.

### Systemic Metastatic Workup
* **CT Thorax:** Essential to rule out pulmonary metastases (most common site of sarcoma spread).
* **Whole Body Bone Scan (Tc-99m) / PET-CT:** To detect distant bony metastases.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Presentation Protocol
* **Resuscitation:** ABCDE approach if systemic toxicity (more common in Ewing's).
* **Analgesia:** IV/PO Multimodal analgesia (often requires strong opioids for severe rest/night pain).
* **Immobilisation:** Backslab or long leg splint to provide comfort and prevent pathological fracture.
* **Bloods:** Routine + **Serum ALP & LDH** (independent prognostic factors for tumour burden).

### ED Referral & Triage
* Strict **NBM** (Nil by Mouth) in anticipation of urgent biopsy or stabilization.
* **DO NOT** attempt empirical incision & drainage if Ewing's mimics osteomyelitis (fever, erythema, ↑ ESR/CRP).
* Urgent referral to HA tertiary musculoskeletal oncology centre (e.g., PWH, QMH).

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Diagnostic Core Needle Biopsy Principles
* MUST be performed at the definitive tumour treatment centre by the operating surgeon.
* **Incision:** Strictly **longitudinal** (never transverse).
* **Placement:** Direct approach through single muscle compartment, avoiding major NV bundles.
* **Haemostasis:** Meticulous; plug the bone hole with bone wax to prevent hematoma tracking (hematoma = tumour spread).
* **Definitive Excision:** The entire biopsy tract MUST be excised en bloc during definitive surgery.

### Osteosarcoma (骨肉瘤) Definitive Algorithm
* **1. Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy:** 8-12 weeks (Methotrexate, Doxorubicin, Cisplatin - MAP regimen) to shrink tumour and treat micrometastases.
* **2. Surgical Resection:** 
  * **Limb Salvage Surgery (保肢手術):** Wide local excision + reconstruction (Megaprosthesis/allograft). Indicated if NV bundle is free and adequate margins achievable.
  * **Amputation:** Indicated if NV bundle encased, massive soft tissue contamination, or non-functional limb post-resection.
* **3. Adjuvant Chemotherapy:** Based on histologic necrosis response to neoadjuvant chemo (>90% necrosis = good prognosis).

### Ewing's Sarcoma (尤文氏肉瘤) Definitive Algorithm
* **1. Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy:** VDC-IE regimen (Vincristine, Doxorubicin, Cyclophosphamide alternating with Ifosfamide, Etoposide).
* **2. Local Control:** 
  * **Surgery:** Wide resection (preferred if expendable bone like fibula/rib or if margins clear).
  * **Radiotherapy:** Ewing's is highly radiosensitive. Used if surgical morbidity too high (e.g., sacrum/pelvis) or margins +ve.
* **3. Adjuvant Chemotherapy:** To eradicate systemic disease.

### Chondrosarcoma (軟骨肉瘤) Definitive Algorithm
* Chemotherapy and Radiotherapy are generally **ineffective** (highly resistant).
* **Definitive Surgery:** Wide en bloc surgical resection is the ONLY curative treatment.
* **Low-grade limb/pelvis:** Excision with wide margins. Intralesional curettage + adjuvant (cryotherapy/phenol) ONLY strictly for select benign/Grade 1 atypical cartilaginous tumours.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Limb Salvage Reconstruction Rehabilitation
* **Implant Precautions:** Restricted weight-bearing initially if osteoarticular allograft or massive endoprosthesis used.
* **ROM Exercises:** CPM (Continuous Passive Motion) machine to prevent joint stiffness (especially post-distal femur replacement).
* **Muscle Strengthening:** Focus on quads/hamstrings. Avoid active resisted extension if extensor mechanism was reconstructed (e.g., patellar tendon attached to prosthesis).

### Amputee Rehabilitation Protocol
* **Stump Care:** Figure-of-8 compression bandaging to shape the stump (conical shape desired).
* **Desensitization:** Massage, tapping to ↓ hypersensitivity.
* **Prosthetic Fitting:** Temporary pylon fit at 3-6 weeks once edema resolves; definitive prosthesis at 3-6 months.
* **Gait Training:** Supervised physical therapy to minimize gait deviations and ↑ energy efficiency.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Disease Progression & Systemic Complications
* **Pulmonary Metastases:** Most common cause of mortality.
* **Local Recurrence:** Drastically ↓ survival rate; requires radical amputation.
* **Chemotherapy Toxicity:** Cardiotoxicity (Doxorubicin), Nephrotoxicity/Ototoxicity (Cisplatin), Neutropenic sepsis.

### Surgical & Reconstructive Complications
* **Prosthetic Joint Infection (PJI):** High risk (up to 15%) due to massive implants and immunosuppression (chemo). Often requires 2-stage revision or amputation.
* **Aseptic Loosening / Implant Failure:** Major late complication of megaprostheses in young, active survivors.
* **Wound Breakdown / Flap Necrosis:** Due to extensive soft tissue stripping and prior radiotherapy.
* **Neurological:** Phantom limb pain, painful amputation neuroma.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important imaging modality before performing a biopsy on a suspected bone tumour? Answer: An MRI of the entire affected bone MUST be done before biopsy to stage the local disease, identify skip lesions, and plan the biopsy tract without artifact interference.
* Trap: Ewing's sarcoma presenting with fever, raised white cell count, CRP, and a warm swollen limb is often misdiagnosed as osteomyelitis. Pearl: Always review plain radiographs for permeative bone destruction or onion-skinning, and if in doubt, avoid blind incision and drainage.
* Pitfall: Using a transverse incision for a bone tumour biopsy. Any transverse incision contaminates multiple longitudinal muscle compartments and prevents successful limb-salvage surgery. Always use a longitudinal incision in line with the planned definitive excision.
