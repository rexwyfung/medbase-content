# Orthopaedic Rehabilitation & Amputation (骨科復康與截肢)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Orthopaedic Amputation Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Surgical or traumatic removal of a limb extremity, requiring extensive **Multidisciplinary Team (MDT)** rehabilitation.
* **Epidemiology (Hong Kong HA Context):**
* **Lower Limb Amputation (LLA):** 80-90% dysvascular 2° to **Diabetes Mellitus (DM)** & **Peripheral Vascular Disease (PVD)**.
* **Upper Limb Amputation (ULA):** Majorly traumatic (industrial/crush injuries) in young males.
* **Tumor:** Osteosarcoma (骨肉瘤) in adolescents.

### The Gait Cycle (步態週期) Basics
* **Definition:** Sequence of events from heel strike of one foot to the next heel strike of the *same* foot.
* **Stance Phase (站立期):** 60% of cycle; foot in contact with ground (weight-bearing).
* **Swing Phase (擺動期):** 40% of cycle; foot airborne (limb advancement).
* **Double Support:** 20% of cycle; both feet on ground (↓ as walking speed ↑; disappears in running).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Normal Gait Cycle Biomechanics
| Phase | Sub-phase | Muscle Activation | Joint Biomechanics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stance (60%)** | Heel Strike (Initial Contact) | Tibialis Anterior (eccentric) | Ankle neutral; Knee extended |
| | Foot Flat (Loading) | Quadriceps (eccentric) | Knee flexes 15° (shock absorption) |
| | Midstance | Gluteus Medius | Pelvis stabilized (prevents Trendelenburg) |
| | Heel Off (Terminal Stance) | Gastrocnemius-Soleus | Ankle plantarflexes (propulsion) |
| | Toe Off (Pre-Swing) | Hip flexors (Iliopsoas) | Hip/Knee begin flexion |
| **Swing (40%)** | Acceleration (Initial Swing) | Iliopsoas & Rectus Femoris | Hip flexes to advance limb |
| | Midswing | Tibialis Anterior (concentric) | Ankle dorsiflexes (clears ground) |
| | Deceleration (Terminal Swing)| Hamstrings (eccentric) | Decelerates knee extension |

### Biomechanics of Amputation Stumps & Deforming Forces
* **Transfemoral / Above-Knee Amputation (AKA) (膝上截肢):**
* **Deforming Force:** Adductor magnus lost → Hip abductors (Gluteus Medius) & flexors (Iliopsoas) overpower.
* **Result:** Stump tends to **abduct & flex**.
* **Transtibial / Below-Knee Amputation (BKA) (膝下截肢):**
* **Deforming Force:** Hamstrings overpower weakened quadriceps (if patellar tendon compromised).
* **Result:** Stump tends to **flex** (knee flexion contracture).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Post-Amputation Stump Assessment (殘肢評估)
* **Look:**
* Shape: Ideal is cylindrical (BKA) or conical (AKA). Bulbous ends delay prosthetic fitting.
* Skin: Scars (adherent/mobile), skin grafts, ulcerations, erythema.
* **Feel:**
* Temperature (perfusion status).
* Tender points (bone spurs, **Neuroma (神經瘤)**).
* **Move:**
* Range of Motion (ROM) of proximal joints to rule out contractures.
* **Neurovascular (NV):**
* Sensibility of stump end (protective sensation).
* Pulses proximal to stump.

### Pathological Gait Assessment
* **Trendelenburg Gait (特倫德倫伯格步態):** Pelvic drop on contralateral side during stance phase (indicates weak Gluteus Medius / Superior Gluteal Nerve palsy).
* **Antalgic Gait (痛性步態):** Shortened stance phase on the affected limb to avoid pain.
* **Drop Foot Gait (垂足步態) / Steppage Gait:** Exaggerated hip/knee flexion during swing phase to clear toes (indicates Tibialis Anterior weakness / Common Peroneal Nerve palsy).
* **Circumduction Gait:** Swinging leg outward in an arc during swing phase (compensation for stiff knee, leg length discrepancy, or poorly fitted prosthesis).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Lower Limb Amputation Levels
| Level | Anatomical Description | Pros/Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Toe / Ray** | Phalanges or metatarsal resection | Preserves weight-bearing heel |
| **Syme's (賽姆氏截肢)**| Ankle disarticulation with heel pad flap | End-bearing stump; bulky prosthesis |
| **BKA (Transtibial)** | Ideal length: 12-15cm below joint line | Preserves knee joint; ↑ walking efficiency |
| **Gritti-Stokes** | Knee disarticulation + patella to femur | End-bearing; poor cosmetic prosthetic knee |
| **AKA (Transfemoral)** | Mid-shaft femur | ↑ Energy expenditure (up to 65% ↑); no knee |

### Spinal Cord Injury (SCI) Rehabilitation Levels (ASIA Impairment Scale)
| Grade | Description (Clinical Findings) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A (Complete)** | No sensory or motor function preserved in sacral segments S4-S5. |
| **B (Sensory Incomplete)** | Sensory but NO motor function preserved below neurological level + includes S4-S5. |
| **C (Motor Incomplete)** | Motor function preserved below level; >50% key muscles have grade < 3. |
| **D (Motor Incomplete)** | Motor function preserved below level; ≥50% key muscles have grade ≥ 3. |
| **E (Normal)** | Normal sensory and motor function. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Stump Imaging & Vascular Assessment
* **Plain Radiography (X-ray):**
* Evaluates bone cut angles.
* Checks for heterotopic ossification or bone spurs.
* **Duplex Ultrasound (Doppler):**
* Assesses vascular patency of proximal vessels for healing potential.

### Gait & Functional Analysis
* **Gait Laboratory Analysis (步態實驗室分析):**
* **Kinematics:** Joint angles/motion tracking via reflective markers.
* **Kinetics:** Force plates measure ground reaction forces (GRF).
* **Dynamic EMG:** Muscle firing patterns during specific gait phases.

## 6. Initial ED Management (Reduction, Splinting/Casting, Traction)

### Acute Post-Op Stump Care
* **Rigid Dressing / Plaster Cast:**
* Applied immediately post-op.
* ↓ Edema, protects wound, prevents early knee flexion contractures.
* **Elevation:**
* Stump elevated on a stump board (NOT pillows) to ↓ swelling.

### Acute Phantom & Residual Limb Pain Management
* **Pharmacotherapy:**
* Initiate Gabapentin (加巴噴丁) or Pregabalin early for neuropathic pain.
* Amitriptyline (TCA) for nocturnal pain.
* **Physical Modalities:**
* TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation).

### Acute Contracture Prevention Protocol
* **BKA (Transtibial):**
* Strictly avoid pillows under the knee.
* Keep knee extended using posterior splint.
* **AKA (Transfemoral):**
* Encourage prone lying (俯臥) 2-3 times daily to stretch hip flexors.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative Indications & Techniques)

### Prosthetic Prescription Algorithm (義肢處方)
| Patient Profile | Functional Level | Prosthesis Choice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Elderly / Low Demand**| Household ambulator | **SACH** (Solid Ankle Cushion Heel) foot + manual locking knee |
| **Active Adult** | Community ambulator | **Dynamic Response Foot** + Polycentric pneumatic knee |
| **Athlete** | High-impact activities | **Carbon Fiber Blade** + Microprocessor knee (C-Leg) |

### Prosthetic Socket Design Principles
* **BKA Socket:**
* **Patellar Tendon Bearing (PTB):** Loads pressure-tolerant areas (patellar tendon, medial tibial flare).
* Relieves pressure-sensitive areas (fibular head, tibial crest).
* **AKA Socket:**
* **Ischial Containment:** Encloses ischial tuberosity to lock pelvis and control rotation.

### Neuroma & Soft Tissue Management
* **Non-Operative:**
* Socket modification (relief pockets).
* Corticosteroid/local anesthetic injections.
* **Operative:**
* Neuroma excision and embedding nerve end into deep muscle belly.
* **Targeted Muscle Reinnervation (TMR):** Splicing amputated nerves into nearby intact motor nerves (advanced HA protocol for pain ↓ and myoelectric prosthetic control).

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Phase 1: Pre-Prosthetic Phase (0-6 Weeks)
* **Goal:** Wound healing, edema control, and stump shaping.
* **Interventions:**
* Figure-of-8 elastic bandaging (shrinker socks).
* Desensitization therapy (tapping, massage).
* Upper limb and sound leg strengthening (crucial for transfer skills).

### Phase 2: Temporary Prosthesis / Pylon Phase (6-12 Weeks)
* **Goal:** Early ambulation and gait training.
* **Interventions:**
* Fitting with temporary plaster/plastic socket and pylon.
* Parallel bar gait training → Zimmer frame → Elbow crutches.

### Phase 3: Permanent Prosthesis & ADL Integration (>3 Months)
* **Goal:** Community reintegration.
* **Interventions:**
* Definitive custom socket fabrication once stump volume stabilizes (no shrinkage for 3-4 weeks).
* **Occupational Therapy (OT):** ADL retraining (dressing, bathing, cooking).
* Return to work / vocational rehabilitation.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Biological Stump Complications
* **Skin Breakdown / Ulcers:** Due to prosthetic piston-ing or poorly fitted sockets.
* **Stump Neuroma:** Painful enlargement of severed nerve ending (common in sural or saphenous nerves).
* **Heterotopic Ossification:** Bone formation in soft tissue, causing pressure points against socket.

### Prosthetic Mismatch Complications
* **Chocking Syndrome:** Socket too tight proximally → venous congestion → edema and verrucous hyperplasia at distal stump.
* **Piston Effect:** Socket too loose → up/down movement during gait → sheer friction blisters.

### Phantom Limb Complications (幻肢併發症)
* **Phantom Limb Sensation:** Non-painful perception that the amputated limb is still present (almost 100% of patients).
* **Phantom Limb Pain (PLP) (幻肢痛):** Severe, burning/shooting neuropathic pain in the missing limb. Requires MDT pain clinic referral and **Mirror Visual Feedback Therapy (鏡面治療)**.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical joint to preserve during lower limb amputation for energy efficiency? Answer: The knee joint. An AKA requires up to 65% more energy to walk compared to baseline, whereas a BKA requires only 10-25% more.
* Trap: Confusing phantom limb pain with residual stump pain. Pearl: Residual pain is localized to the actual physical stump (e.g., neuroma, ischemia, infection), whereas phantom pain is felt in the anatomically missing part (e.g., pain in the missing toes).
* Pitfall: Placing a pillow under the knee of a BKA patient post-operatively for comfort. This inevitably → severe flexion contracture, rendering the patient unable to wear a prosthesis.
* Viva Question: What are the pressure-tolerant areas in a BKA Patellar Tendon Bearing (PTB) socket? Answer: Patellar tendon, medial tibial flare, medial tibial shaft, and posterior calf musculature.
* Trap: Assuming SACH (Solid Ankle Cushion Heel) feet are strictly for the elderly. Pearl: While common in low-demand patients, they are extremely durable and require low maintenance, making them ideal for patients in remote areas or those with poor follow-up compliance.
