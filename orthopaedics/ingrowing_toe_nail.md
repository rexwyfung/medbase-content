# Ingrowing Toe Nail (嵌甲)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Onychocryptosis
* **Definition:** Pathological condition where the lateral or medial edge of the nail plate pierces the paronychium (lateral nail fold) → foreign body reaction → secondary inflammation/infection.

### Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Risk Factors
* **Improper Nail Trimming:** Cutting corners too short or curved (most common).
* **Footwear:** Tight, narrow toe-box shoes (crowding).
* **Trauma:** Direct micro-trauma or crush injury to toe.
* **Anatomical/Physiological:** Pincer nails (excessive transverse curvature), hyperhidrosis (maceration of skin), fleshy toes.

### Epidemiology
* **Demographics:** Peaks in adolescents & young adults (active, sweaty feet).
* **Location:** >95% affect the **1st toe (Hallux)** (拇趾). Lateral border more common than medial.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Nail Apparatus Anatomy
* **Nail Plate:** Hard keratinized structure.
* **Germinal Matrix (甲基):** Proximal portion under proximal nail fold; responsible for 90% of nail growth. **Must be destroyed** to prevent recurrence.
* **Sterile Matrix (甲床):** Distal to lunula; anchors nail plate to distal phalanx.
* **Paronychium:** Soft tissue folds surrounding lateral & proximal margins.
* **Hyponychium:** Junction between sterile matrix & fingertip/toetip skin.

### Pathomechanics of Onychocryptosis
* **Spicule Formation:** Improper cutting leaves a sharp keratin spicule laterally.
* **Foreign Body Cascade:** Spicule penetrates paronychial dermis → foreign body inflammatory response → epithelial breakdown → colonization by skin flora (usually *Staph. aureus*) → chronic hypervascular hypertrophic **granulation tissue** (肉芽組織) overgrowth.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Visual Inspection (Look)
* **Paronychial Signs:** Erythema, edema, hypertrophy of lateral/medial nail fold.
* **Discharge:** Serous, purulent, or frankly bloody exudate.
* **Granulation Tissue:** Friable, red, hypertrophic tissue overlapping nail plate.

### Palpation (Feel)
* **Tenderness:** Exquisite point tenderness over affected paronychial fold.
* **Fluctuance:** +ve if paronychial abscess is present.

### Range of Motion (Move)
* **Functional Limitation:** Antalgic gait; pain ↑ on toe-off phase of walking or wearing closed-toe shoes.

### Neurovascular (NV) Status
* **Vascular:** Palpate **Dorsalis Pedis (DP)** & **Posterior Tibial (PT)** pulses. Assess capillary refill time (CRT). CRITICAL in diabetics.
* **Neurological:** Check light touch/pinprick to rule out peripheral neuropathy.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Mozena Classification System
* **Purpose:** Stages severity to guide management.

| Stage | Name | Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Inflammatory | Erythema, mild edema, pain with pressure. NO discharge/granulation. |
| **II** | Abscess | ↑ Pain/swelling, seropurulent discharge, localized infection. NO granulation. |
| **III** | Granulation | Chronic inflammation, hypertrophic granulation tissue overlapping nail plate. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiographs (X-ray) Indications
* **Routine:** NOT indicated for acute/uncomplicated cases (clinical diagnosis).
* **Indications for X-ray:** Chronic recurrent cases, persistent sinus tract, suspected deep infection.

### Standard X-ray Views
* **Views:** AP & Lateral of affected toe.

### Key Radiological Pathologies to Exclude
* **Osteomyelitis (OM):** Lytic lesions, cortical destruction of **distal phalanx**.
* **Subungual Exostosis:** Benign bony outgrowth from dorsal distal phalanx elevating the nail bed.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Conservative Interventions (Stage I)
* **Soaks:** Warm normal saline soaks 15-20 mins, 2-3x/day.
* **Mechanical Elevation:** Pack cotton wisp or dental floss under the embedded nail corner to lift it off the dermis.
* **Taping:** Slant taping (pulling lateral paronychium away from nail).
* **Footwear Modification:** Open-toe sandals or wide toe-box shoes.
* **Patient Education:** Cut nails straight across; do NOT round corners.

### Pharmacological Therapy
* **Antibiotics:** PO Augmentin or Flucloxacillin ONLY if localized cellulitis +ve. Not required for sterile inflammation.
* **Analgesia:** PO Paracetamol/NSAIDs.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Operative Indications
* **Stage II & III Disease:** Abscess formation or hypertrophic granulation.
* **Failure of Conservative Tx:** Persistent symptoms >2-4 weeks.
* **Recurrence:** Multiple episodes of ingrowing nail.

### Digital Ring Block Technique
* **Agent:** 1% or 2% Lignocaine **WITHOUT Adrenaline** (strictly avoid to prevent terminal digital ischemia).
* **Technique:** Inject at base of proximal phalanx (medial & lateral) to block dorsal and plantar digital nerves. Wait 5-10 mins.

### Wedge Resection & Phenolization (Gold Standard in HA)
* **Preparation:** Tourniquet at base of toe to exsanguinate & ensure bloodless field (phenol is neutralized by blood).
* **Resection:** Excise longitudinal lateral 1/4 of nail plate + offending spicule.
* **Debridement:** Curette away hypertrophic granulation tissue.
* **Chemical Matrixectomy:** Apply **88% Phenol** solution via cotton bud specifically to the lateral **germinal matrix** for 1-3 minutes.
* **Neutralization:** Wash thoroughly with 70% Alcohol or Normal Saline to neutralize phenol and prevent collateral chemical burns.

### Zadek's Procedure (Radical Matrixectomy)
* **Indications:** Severe, refractory, or multiply recurrent cases; severe pincer nail deformity.
* **Technique:** Complete excision of nail plate, nail bed, and surgical excision (not chemical) of entire germinal matrix. Primary closure or left to heal by secondary intention.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative Wound Care
* **Immediate:** Bulky compressive dressing applied. Release toe tourniquet BEFORE dressing to check hemostasis.
* **Elevation:** Strict strict leg elevation for first 24-48 hrs to ↓ edema & throbbing pain.

### Dressing Changes & Hygiene
* **First Change:** Remove bulky dressing at 48 hrs.
* **Daily Care:** Daily wound cleansing with normal saline + non-adherent dressing (e.g., Melolin) until exudate stops.
* **Avoid:** Prolonged submersion in water (swimming/baths) until fully epithelialized.

### Return to Activity Guidelines
* **Footwear:** Open-toe footwear for 1-2 weeks.
* **Sports:** Return to impact sports (running/football) in 3-4 weeks once pain-free and wound dry.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Recurrence (Nail Spicule Formation)
* **Pathology:** Most common complication (5-10%). Due to incomplete ablation of the lateral germinal matrix.
* **Cause:** Insufficient phenol application time, low phenol concentration, or blood in the field neutralizing the phenol.

### Infectious Complications
* **Superficial:** Post-op cellulitis or paronychia.
* **Deep:** **Distal Phalanx Osteomyelitis** (requires IV Abx ± surgical bone debridement/amputation).

### Iatrogenic Soft Tissue Complications
* **Chemical Burn:** Overspill of phenol causing necrosis of surrounding healthy skin/paronychium.
* **Epidermoid Cyst:** Inclusion cyst from implanted nail fragments during surgery.
* **Nail Deformity:** Asymmetrical, narrowed, or dystrophic nail growth.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why must you use plain Lignocaine WITHOUT Adrenaline for a digital ring block? Answer: End-artery anatomy of the digits; Adrenaline causes intense vasoconstriction leading to irreversible digital gangrene/ischemia.
* Viva Question: What is the purpose of applying phenol during a wedge resection? Answer: To perform a chemical matrixectomy, selectively destroying the lateral germinal matrix to prevent longitudinal growth of a new nail spicule.
* Trap: Prescribing antibiotics as the primary definitive treatment for Stage 3 ingrowing toenail. Pearl: Antibiotics will not resolve the issue as the primary pathology is a mechanical foreign body (nail spicule); mechanical removal (wedge resection) is mandatory.
* Pitfall: Applying phenol to a bloody surgical field. Blood neutralizes phenol, leading to incomplete matrix destruction and high recurrence rates. Always use a digital tourniquet for a bloodless field before phenol application.
* Trap: Performing a total nail avulsion for a first-time lateral ingrowing toe nail. Pearl: Total avulsion without matrixectomy has a high recurrence rate (~70%) and causes unnecessary nail dystrophy; partial wedge resection + phenolization is the standard of care.
