# Lumbar Spine Pathology (腰椎病變)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Prolapsed Intervertebral Disc (PID) (椎間盤突出)
* **Definition:** Extrusion of **nucleus pulposus** through annular defect compressing neural elements.
* **Epidemiology:** Most common in 30-50 yrs.
* **Common Sites:** 95% occur at **L4/L5** or **L5/S1**.
* **MOI:** Axial loading + flexion + rotation → microtrauma to **annulus fibrosus**.

### Lumbar Spinal Stenosis (LSS) (腰椎管狹窄症)
* **Definition:** Narrowing of spinal canal, lateral recess, or intervertebral foramen.
* **Epidemiology:** >60 yrs; most common reason for spinal surgery in elderly.
* **Pathophysiology:** Degenerative cascade → disc desiccation → loss of disc height → facet hypertrophy + **ligamentum flavum (黃韌帶)** buckling.

### Spondylolisthesis (脊椎滑脫)
* **Definition:** Forward translation of one vertebra over another.
* **MOI (Degenerative):** Long-standing instability + facet arthropathy (most common L4/L5).
* **MOI (Isthmic):** Fatigue # of **pars interarticularis (峽部)** from repetitive hyperextension (most common L5/S1).

### Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES) (馬尾神經綜合症)
* **Definition:** Surgical emergency; acute compression of multiple lumbosacral nerve roots below L1/L2 (conus medullaris).
* **MOI:** Massive central disc herniation, tumor, trauma, epidural hematoma/abscess.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Intervertebral Disc Biomechanics
* **Nucleus Pulposus:** Type II collagen + proteoglycans; resists compressive loads.
* **Annulus Fibrosus:** Type I collagen; resists tensile/torsional forces.
* **Posterior Longitudinal Ligament (PLL):** Weakest laterally → ↑ susceptibility to **posterolateral** disc herniations.
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/2eb138a8ee9d497e6319b4327ae07aaf.png)

### Spinal Canal & Nerve Root Exit Zones
* **Central Canal:** Houses thecal sac and traversing nerve roots.
* **Lateral Recess:** Bounded by superior articular process, pedicle, and posterior vertebral body. 
* **Intervertebral Foramen:** Exit pathway for nerve root.
* **Traversing vs. Exiting Root Principle:** 
	* **Posterolateral Disc Herniation:** Compresses the **traversing** root (e.g., L4/L5 disc hits L5 root).
	* **Far Lateral (Foraminal) Disc Herniation:** Compresses the **exiting** root (e.g., L4/L5 disc hits L4 root).
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/6e2b5ce9f5e35184ca79f4eb3c9ed967.png)

### Pars Interarticularis Anatomy
* **Definition:** Bone segment between superior and inferior articular processes.
* **Biomechanics:** Acts as a bony block to anterior translation; failure → Isthmic Spondylolisthesis.
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/b7046d04d814c6560520192e4e62167f.png)

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Lumbar Spine Inspection & Palpation
* **Look:**
	* **Gait:** Antalgic gait, foot drop (steppage gait), wide-based gait.
	* **Alignment:** Loss of normal lumbar lordosis, scoliosis (sciatic list).
	* **Skin:** Scars, café-au-lait spots, hairy tufts (spina bifida occulta).
* **Feel:**
	* **Bony tenderness:** Spinous processes, paraspinal muscle spasm.
	* **Step-off sign:** Palpable dip indicating spondylolisthesis.
* **Move:**
	* **ROM:** Flexion, extension, lateral flexion, rotation.
	* **Pain on extension:** Suggests facet arthropathy or spinal stenosis.

### Lumbar Neurological Examination (L4-S1)
| Nerve Root | Motor Test | Sensory Dermatome | Reflex Arc |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **L4** | Ankle dorsiflexion (Tibialis Anterior) | Medial malleolus / medial leg | Patellar tendon |
| **L5** | Great toe extension (EHL) | Dorsal first web space | None |
| **S1** | Ankle plantarflexion (Gastrocnemius) | Lateral border of foot | Achilles tendon |

![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/e0c8535c918ad0ffbd1aad6135cdb6d6.png)

### Lumbar Special Tests
* **Straight Leg Raise (SLR) (直腿抬高試驗):**
	* **Technique:** Passive elevation of symptomatic leg.
	* **+ve Sign:** Radicular pain < 60° (indicates L5/S1 root tension).
	* **Crossed SLR:** Elevation of asymptomatic leg produces pain in symptomatic leg (highly specific for large PID).
* **Femoral Nerve Stretch Test:**
	* **Technique:** Prone, passive knee flexion + hip extension.
	* **+ve Sign:** Anterior thigh pain (indicates L3/L4 root tension).
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/ca502ef6348f9a946e580d9d8375b423.png)

### Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES) Red Flag Assessment
* **Perineal Sensation:** Saddle anesthesia (S2-S4 dermatomes).
* **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE):** ↓ or absent anal sphincter tone.
* **Motor/Sensory:** Bilateral lower limb neurological deficits.
* **Autonomic:** Painless urinary retention (palpable bladder).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Morphological Classification of Disc Herniation
* **Bulge:** Annulus intact, >25% of disc circumference involved.
* **Protrusion:** Focal asymmetric extension, base > apex.
* **Extrusion:** Focal extension, apex > base, annulus disrupted.
* **Sequestration:** Free disc fragment separated from parent disc.
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/166da4788ebea0c6c7184e384c1e28f0.png)

### Wiltse Classification of Spondylolisthesis
| Type    | Name         | Description                                                 |
| :------ | :----------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **I**   | Dysplastic   | Congenital malformation of facet joints                     |
| **II**  | Isthmic      | Pars interarticularis defect (lytic, elongated, or acute #) |
| **III** | Degenerative | Segmental instability without pars defect                   |
| **IV**  | Traumatic    | Acute # in posterior arch (excluding pars)                  |
| **V**   | Pathologic   | Secondary to bone disease (e.g., Paget's, tumor)            |
| **VI**  | Iatrogenic   | Post-surgical instability                                   |

![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/2940a5da3308722ef23d5e646d2d1336.png)

### Meyerding Grading System for Spondylolisthesis
| Grade | % Translation over inferior vertebral body |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Grade I** | 0 - 25% |
| **Grade II** | 26 - 50% |
| **Grade III** | 51 - 75% |
| **Grade IV** | 76 - 100% |
| **Grade V** | >100% (Spondyloptosis) |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Lumbar Radiographs
* **AP View:** Assess coronal alignment, scoliosis, spina bifida.
* **Lateral View:** Assess sagittal balance, disc height, osteophytes, spondylolisthesis.
* **Dynamic Flexion-Extension Views:**
	* **Purpose:** Assess for radiographic instability (translation >3mm or angulation >10°).
* **Oblique View:**
	* **Purpose:** Visualize the "Scottie Dog" (Pars interarticularis is the neck).
	* **Sign:** Collared dog indicates pars defect (spondylolysis).
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/052499114f57bb08c0061320b7f22511.png)

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) Lumbar Spine
* **Gold Standard:** Defines soft tissue anatomy, disc morphology, nerve root compression.
* **T1-Weighted:** Excellent for bony anatomy, fat, marrow replacing lesions.
* **T2-Weighted:** Excellent for CSF (appears bright), discs, spinal cord edema ("myelographic effect").
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/3ab73fa9097a010e5a1b6bb6777b2d43.png)

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Radiculopathy & Back Pain Management (Non-CES)
* **Analgesia:**
	* **Step 1:** Paracetamol + NSAIDs.
	* **Step 2:** Weak opioids (Tramadol).
* **Neuropathic Agents:** Pregabalin or Gabapentin for radicular pain.
* **Mobility:** Short period of rest (max 1-2 days) → encourage early mobilization.

### Suspected Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES) ED Protocol
* **Strict NPO:** Prepare for emergency theatre.
* **Urinary Catheterization:** Monitor post-void residual volume (PVR >200ml highly suspicious).
* **Urgent MRI:** Entire neuroaxis (spine) within hours.
* **Orthopaedic/Spine Consult:** Immediate referral; do not delay for imaging if clinically obvious and MRI unavailable.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Definitive Management Algorithm for Lumbar Pathologies
| Condition             | Non-Operative Indications                           | Operative Indications                                                            |
| :-------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PID**               | Mild/moderate pain, no progressive neuro deficit.   | - Cauda Equina.<br>- Progressive motor deficit.<br>- Intractable pain > 6 weeks. |
| **LSS**               | Tolerable claudication distance, intact ADLs.       | - Intractable neurogenic claudication.<br>- Progressive neuro deficit.           |
| **Spondylolisthesis** | - Grade I-II.<br>- Asymptomatic or controlled pain. | - Grade III-V.<br>- Refractory pain.<br>- Progressive neuro deficit.             |

### Non-Operative Protocol Details
* **Physiotherapy:** McKenzie exercises (extension bias for PID), flexion bias for LSS.
* **Injections:** Epidural steroid injection (ESI) or selective nerve root block (SNRB) for diagnostic/therapeutic pain relief.
* **Orthotics:** Lumbosacral corset (short-term only to prevent core deconditioning).
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/48c7619da884e8163cc9d600cf7391cb.png)

### Definitive Surgical Procedures
* **Emergency Decompression (Lamniectomy/Discectomy):**
	* **Indication:** Cauda Equina Syndrome (must perform within 24-48 hrs).
* **Microdiscectomy (微創椎間盤切除術):**
	* **Indication:** Isolated PID.
	* **Technique:** Excision of herniated disc fragment via minimal access.
* **Decompressive Laminectomy (椎板切除術):**
	* **Indication:** Multilevel Lumbar Spinal Stenosis.
	* **Technique:** Removal of lamina, spinous processes, and ligamentum flavum to decompress thecal sac.
* **Lumbar Interbody Fusion (TLIF/PLIF):**
	* **Indication:** Spondylolisthesis, iatrogenic instability post-laminectomy, recurrent disc herniations.
	* **Technique:** Transforaminal (TLIF) or Posterior (PLIF) approach → disc removal → interbody cage + pedicle screws/rods.
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/73d97c0c9dde672e924f05b4ea872c90.png)

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Lumbar Surgery Inpatient Timeline
* **Day 0:** Bed rest, log-rolling only, monitor lower limb neurology & wound.
* **Day 1:** Sit out of bed, mobilize with walking frame + physiotherapist.
* **Day 2-3:** Stair climbing, independence in ADLs, prepare for discharge.

### Outpatient Physiotherapy & Ergonomics
* **Core Strengthening:** Stabilize multifidus and transverse abdominis.
* **Lifting Ergonomics:** Keep load close to body, bend at knees, avoid simultaneous flexion + rotation.
* **Return to Work:** Sedentary work (2-4 weeks), manual labor (3-6 months post-fusion).

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Intraoperative Lumbar Complications
* **Incidental Durotomy (Dural Tear):**
	* **Consequence:** CSF leak → postural headache.
	* **Management:** Primary watertight suture repair + bed rest for 48-72h.
* **Nerve Root Injury:** Direct surgical trauma or excessive traction.
* **Vascular Injury:** Anterior penetration of instruments (e.g., pituitary rongeur) injuring aorta or iliac vessels → catastrophic hemorrhage.

### Postoperative & Long-Term Complications
* **Failed Back Surgery Syndrome (FBSS):** Persistent/recurrent pain post-op (fibrosis, recurrence, missed pathology).
* **Adjacent Segment Disease (ASD):** Accelerated degeneration at levels above/below a rigid fusion construct due to ↑ biomechanical stress.
* **Recurrent Disc Herniation:** 5-10% risk following microdiscectomy.
* **Surgical Site Infection (SSI):** Deep infection requiring surgical debridement (Washout).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate Neurogenic Claudication from Vascular Claudication? Answer: Neurogenic (Spinal Stenosis) improves with lumbar flexion (sitting, walking uphill) and pain radiates proximal to distal. Vascular improves simply with standing still and pain radiates distal to proximal.
* Viva Question: What is the "Shopping Cart Sign"? Answer: A classic presentation of lumbar spinal stenosis where the patient experiences relief of leg pain by leaning forward over a shopping cart, which flexes the spine, buckling the ligamentum flavum posteriorly and opening up the spinal canal. ![image|300](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/7f3cfd8ff5d5e67913e13329e780bb65.png)
* Trap: Assuming an L4/L5 disc herniation compresses the L4 nerve root. Pearl: A typical posterolateral L4/L5 disc herniation spares the exiting L4 root and compresses the TRAVERSING L5 root.
* Pitfall: Discharging a patient with severe back pain and bilateral sciatica without doing a DRE. Always perform a PR examination to assess anal tone and perineal sensation to rule out Cauda Equina Syndrome; failure to document this is a major medicolegal disaster.
* Trap: Relying solely on a positive Straight Leg Raise (SLR) to diagnose a disc herniation. Pearl: The Crossed-SLR test (pain in the affected leg when raising the asymptomatic leg) is much more SPECIFIC for a large, extruded disc herniation than a standard SLR.
