# Hand Osteoarthritis (手部骨關節炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology
### Definition of Hand Osteoarthritis
* Degenerative loss of articular cartilage → joint space narrowing & osteophyte formation.
* Predominantly affects **DIPJ (遠端指間關節)**, **PIPJ (近端指間關節)**, & **1st CMCJ (第一腕掌關節)**.

### Aetiology & MOI of Hand OA
* **1° OA**: Idiopathic, genetic predisposition, wear & tear.
* **2° OA**: Post-traumatic (e.g., intra-articular #, Bennett's #), inflammatory arthritis sequelae, chronic infection.

### Epidemiology of Hand OA
* F > M (strongly associated with post-menopausal status).
* Age > 50 yrs.
* High prevalence in manual laborers & occupations requiring heavy grip/pinch tasks.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics
### Anatomy of 1st CMCJ (Trapeziometacarpal Joint)
* Biconcave saddle joint (articulation between trapezium & 1st metacarpal base).
* **Anterior Oblique Ligament (Beak Ligament)**: Primary palmar stabilizer preventing dorsal/radial subluxation of 1st metacarpal.

### Biomechanics of Thumb Pinch
* Tip pinch / key pinch → force magnified **10-12x** at **1st CMCJ**.
* Palmar beak ligament attenuation → dorsal subluxation of 1st metacarpal base under repetitive shear stress.

### Pathoanatomy of Finger OA
* Capsular laxity + asymmetric cartilage loss → collateral ligament insufficiency → angular deformities (ulnar/radial deviation).
* Mucoid degeneration of joint capsule → mucous cysts at dorsal DIPJ base.

## 3. Clinical Assessment
### Look (Visual Inspection)
* **Heberden's nodes**: Palpable osteophytes at **DIPJ**.
* **Bouchard's nodes**: Palpable osteophytes at **PIPJ**.
* Mucous cysts (dorsal DIPJ, often indenting nail bed → nail ridging).
* **Thumb Z-deformity**: 1st CMCJ adduction contracture + MCPJ hyperextension + IPJ flexion.
* Squaring of thumb base (prominence from 1st CMCJ radial subluxation).

### Feel (Palpation)
* Joint line tenderness over **1st CMCJ**, **DIPJ**, **PIPJ**.
* Palpable bony hard lumps (osteophytes, differentiating from soft boggy synovitis in Rheumatoid Arthritis).

### Move & Special Tests
* ↓ ROM (global stiffness, especially terminal flexion/extension).
* Fixed flexion deformities (FFD) of digits.
* **Grind test (+ve)**: Axial compression + rotation of 1st metacarpal base → pain/crepitus at CMCJ.

### Neurovascular (NV) Status & Nerve Testing
* Check for concurrent **Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)** (median nerve entrapment highly associated with hand OA).

| Nerve | Motor Test | Sensory Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Median Nerve (正中神經)** | APB (Abductor Pollicis Brevis) - Palmar abduction | Volar tip of index finger |
| **Ulnar Nerve (尺神經)** | FDI (First Dorsal Interosseous) - Index abduction | Volar tip of little finger |
| **Radial Nerve (橈神經)** | EPL (Extensor Pollicis Longus) - Thumb retropulsion | Dorsal first web space |

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)
### Eaton-Littler Classification for 1st CMCJ OA
| Stage | Radiological Description |
|---|---|
| **Stage I** | Normal joint space contour, possible mild widening (synovitis). <1/3 subluxation. |
| **Stage II** | Mild joint space narrowing. Osteophytes <2mm. Up to 1/3 subluxation. |
| **Stage III** | Severe joint space narrowing. Osteophytes >2mm. >1/3 subluxation. |
| **Stage IV** | Stage III features + concurrent **Scaphotrapezial (STT) joint** OA. |

### Kellgren-Lawrence Classification for Generalized Hand OA
| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| **Grade 1** | Doubtful joint space narrowing (JSN), possible osteophytes. |
| **Grade 2** | Definite osteophytes, absent/questionable JSN. |
| **Grade 3** | Moderate osteophytes, definite JSN, some subchondral sclerosis. |
| **Grade 4** | Large osteophytes, severe JSN, severe sclerosis, definite bone deformity. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations
### Standard Radiographic Views
* X-ray Hand AP & Lateral views.
* **Robert's view**: True AP of thumb (full pronation of forearm) → optimal profiling of 1st CMCJ.
* **Bett's view**: Evaluates STT joint & trapezium articulations.

### Classic X-ray Findings (LOSS)
* **L**: Loss of joint space (asymmetric).
* **O**: Osteophytes (marginal).
* **S**: Subchondral sclerosis.
* **S**: Subchondral cysts.
* Joint subluxation (1st metacarpal translating radially/dorsally off trapezium).

## 6. Initial ED Management (Reduction, Splinting/Casting, Traction)
### Acute Flare Management in ED/GOPC
* Acute immobilization: **Thumb spica splint** (rigid thermoplastic vs soft neoprene) to offload 1st CMCJ pain.
* Acute immobilization: **Aluminium finger splint** for acute DIPJ/PIPJ inflammatory flare.

### Pharmacological & Injection Therapies (Acute/Subacute)
* Oral: Paracetamol + NSAIDs (PO/Topical) + PPI cover.
* Intra-articular injection: Corticosteroid + Lignocaine (Diagnostic & Therapeutic) into 1st CMCJ or STT joint under USG/fluoroscopic guidance.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative Indications & Techniques)
### Management Decision Matrix
| Treatment Tier | Indications | Options in HK HA Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-Operative** | Early stage (Eaton I/II), minimal ADL impact, poor surgical candidate. | Activity modification, Orthotics, Analgesia, HA/Steroid injections. |
| **Operative** | Eaton III/IV, failed conservative tx (>3-6 months), severe ADL impairment/pain at rest. | Arthrodesis, Arthroplasty, Trapeziectomy +/- LRTI. |

### Definitive Surgical Options for 1st CMCJ OA
* **Trapeziectomy**: Excision of trapezium. Gold standard in older, lower-demand patients. Rapid recovery, excellent pain relief.
* **Trapeziectomy + LRTI (Ligament Reconstruction & Tendon Interposition)**: Uses **FCR (橈側腕屈肌)** tendon to suspend 1st metacarpal base (prevent subsidence) & fill void. Indicated for younger/higher-demand patients.
* **CMCJ Arthrodesis (Fusion)**: Reserved for young, heavy manual laborers. Fused at 30° palmar abduction, 15° radial extension, slight pronation. Preserves raw grip strength, sacrifices flat hand span.

### Definitive Surgical Options for DIPJ/PIPJ OA
* **DIPJ Arthrodesis**: Gold standard for end-stage DIPJ OA. Fused in 0-10° flexion using Headless Compression Screw (HCS) or K-wires + tension band.
* **PIPJ Arthroplasty (Silicone Spacer)**: Preserves motion. Strictly indicated for **ulnar digits (ring/little)** to maintain hand wrap/grasp function.
* **PIPJ Arthrodesis**: Indicated for **radial digits (index/middle)** to provide a rigid, stable post for key pinch grip. Fused in cascading flexion (Index 40° → Little 70°).

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol
### Post-Trapeziectomy/LRTI Protocol
* **0-4 weeks**: Thumb spica cast/thermoplastic splint (immobilize wrist & thumb to allow capsular healing).
* **4-6 weeks**: Wean to removable splint. Gentle AROM of CMCJ.
* **6-8 weeks+**: Pinch grip strengthening & ADL retraining (Occupational Therapy).

### Joint Protection Strategies
* Avoid sustained pinch grips.
* Ergonomic modification: Use built-up handles/adaptive equipment for utensils, keys, and door knobs.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications
### Disease Progression Complications
* **Thumb Z-deformity**: Severe mechanical impairment of pinch grip.
* Tendon rupture: FPL (Flexor Pollicis Longus) or extensor tendons wearing over prominent osteophytes/STT spurs.
* Mucous cyst rupture → high risk of superimposed septic arthritis of DIPJ.

### Surgical Complications (CMCJ Procedures)
* **Superficial Radial Nerve (SRN) Neuroma**: High risk during dorsal/radial skin incisions over thumb base.
* First metacarpal subsidence (post-trapeziectomy) → loss of thumb length & secondary impingement on scaphoid.
* Non-union (post-arthrodesis, ↑ risk in smokers).
* Implant loosening/breakage (silicone arthroplasty at PIPJ).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the classic pathoanatomical cascade of the thumb Z-deformity in CMCJ OA? Answer: CMCJ cartilage loss → anterior oblique (beak) ligament laxity → dorsal/radial subluxation of 1st metacarpal base → adductor pollicis contracture → compensatory MCPJ hyperextension to grasp objects.
* Trap: Assuming PIPJ OA is strictly treated with arthroplasty across all digits. Pearl: PIPJ arthroplasty (silicone) is reserved for ulnar digits (ring/little) to allow hand wrap; radial digits (index/middle) require arthrodesis to act as a stable post for key pinch.
* Pitfall: Recommending CMCJ arthrodesis for an elderly, low-demand patient. The lack of mobility will severely impair fine ADLs (e.g., picking up coins). Trapeziectomy alone is the gold standard for elderly patients in the HA system.
* Viva Question: Which radiographic view is mandatory to accurately stage 1st CMCJ OA? Answer: Robert's view, which is a true AP of the thumb requiring full forearm pronation, preventing overlap of the trapezium and 1st metacarpal base.
