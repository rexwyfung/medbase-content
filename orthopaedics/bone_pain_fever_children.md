# Pediatric Bone Pain and Fever (兒童骨痛與發燒)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Acute Hematogenous Osteomyelitis (AHO) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Infection of bone marrow/cortex, typically spread via hematogenous route in children.
* **Epidemiology:** Bimodal peak (neonates & children <5 yrs). Boys > Girls.
* **Common Pathogens:** 
  * *S. aureus* (Most common overall)
  * Group A *Streptococcus* 
  * *Kingella kingae* (Children <4 yrs)
  * *Salmonella* (Sickle cell disease patients)
  * *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (Puncture wounds through sneakers)

### Septic Arthritis (SA) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Purulent infection of joint space → rapid destruction of **articular cartilage (關節軟骨)**.
* **Epidemiology:** Most common <3 yrs. Hip and knee most frequently affected.
* **MOI:** Hematogenous spread (most common) OR direct contiguous spread from metaphyseal AHO (if metaphysis is intracapsular).

### Transient Synovitis (TS) Definition
* **Definition:** Self-limiting, sterile joint inflammation (usually hip).
* **Epidemiology:** Most common cause of acute hip pain in children 3-8 yrs.
* **MOI:** Often post-viral URI or minor trauma.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Metaphyseal Vascularity in Children
* **Anatomy:** Nutrient artery branches into fine hairpin loops at the **metaphysis (乾骺端)** adjacent to the **physis (生長板)**.
* **Biomechanics/Pathophysiology:** Sluggish blood flow in these loops ↓ phagocytosis → ideal nidus for bacterial seeding.

### Transphyseal Vessels & Joint Anatomy
* **Infants (<18 months):** **Transphyseal blood vessels** cross the growth plate → AHO can easily spread to the epiphysis and joint space causing concomitant SA.
* **Intracapsular Metaphyses:** In specific joints, the joint capsule attaches distal to the physis, meaning the metaphysis is intra-articular. Metaphyseal AHO here easily ruptures into the joint → SA.
* **Key Intracapsular Joints:** Hip (proximal femur), Shoulder (proximal humerus), Ankle (distal fibula), Elbow (proximal radius).

### Joint Capsule Biomechanics in Effusion
* **Position of Maximum Volume:** A joint with purulent effusion assumes a position that maximizes capsular volume to ↓ intra-articular pressure and ↓ pain.
* **Hip:** Flexion, Abduction, External Rotation (**FABER position**).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Look (Observation) Assessment
* **Pseudoparalysis (假性癱瘓):** Child refuses to move affected limb.
* **Gait:** Limping (antalgic gait) or absolute refusal to weight-bear.
* **Skin:** Overlying erythema, edema (late signs in deep joints like the hip).
* **Posture:** Joint held rigidly in position of maximum volume (e.g., FABER for hip).

### Feel (Palpation) Assessment
* **Temperature:** Localized warmth.
* **Tenderness:** 
  * **AHO:** Exquisite, localized **point bone tenderness** over the metaphysis.
  * **SA:** Diffuse tenderness over the joint line.

### Move & Neurovascular Assessment
* **Passive Range of Motion (ROM):**
  * **SA:** Severe pain on minimal passive micromotion (hallmark).
  * **AHO:** Preserved ROM if joint not involved, though extreme ROM may cause pain.
* **Neurological:** Normal (unless epidural abscess in spinal osteomyelitis).
* **Vascular:** Pulses typically intact; check for capillary refill <2s.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Kocher Criteria for Septic Arthritis of the Hip
| Criterion | Description / Threshold |
| :--- | :--- |
| **History** | Non-weight bearing |
| **Temperature** | Fever > 38.5°C |
| **Hematology** | ESR > 40 mm/hr |
| **Hematology** | WBC count > 12,000 cells/mm³ |

* **Probability of SA:**
  * 1/4 predictors = 3%
  * 2/4 predictors = 40%
  * 3/4 predictors = 93%
  * 4/4 predictors = 99%
* **Note:** CRP >20 mg/L is often added as a 5th independent predictor in modified criteria.

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Blood & Microbiological Workup
* **Bloods:** ↑ WBC (left shift), ↑ ESR, ↑ CRP (CRP rises fast in 6-8 hrs, peaks 2 days; ESR rises slow, peaks 3-5 days).
* **Cultures:** Blood cultures (+ve in 30-50% cases).

### Plain Radiography (X-rays)
* **Early (0-10 days):** Often normal. May show soft tissue swelling or blurred fascial planes.
* **Late (10-14 days):** **Periosteal reaction (骨膜反應)**, regional osteopenia, lytic lesions, **sequestrum** (dead bone), **involucrum** (new bone).

### Ultrasonography (US)
* **Indications:** Highly sensitive for detecting joint effusions (especially hip).
* **Limitations:** Cannot definitively distinguish between purulent (SA) and sterile (TS) effusions.

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)
* **Indications:** Gold standard for AHO diagnosis.
* **Findings:** Bone marrow edema (↓ T1, ↑ T2/STIR), subperiosteal fluid collections, soft tissue abscess. 

### Joint Aspiration (Arthrocentesis)
* **Technique:** US-guided aspiration of the hip joint.
* **SA Diagnostic Criteria:**
  * Appearance: Turbid/purulent.
  * WBC Count: **>50,000 cells/mm³** (often >100k).
  * Polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs): **>75-90%**.
  * Gram stain & Culture: Definitive identification.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Resuscitation & Immobilization
* **NBM (Nil By Mouth):** Keep fasting for potential emergency theatre.
* **Analgesia & Fluids:** IV fluids (resuscitation if septic), adequate pain relief.
* **Immobilization:** Splint limb in resting position to ↓ pain and prevent pathological fracture.

### Antibiotic Protocol Strict Rule
* **HOLD Antibiotics:** Do NOT give empiric IV antibiotics in ED until blood cultures and joint aspirate/bone biopsy are obtained (unless patient is in septic shock). 

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Septic Arthritis (SA) Management Indications
| Management Type | Indications | Key Techniques |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operative (Emergency)** | ALL confirmed/highly suspected SA cases | **Emergency Arthrotomy & Washout (關節切開灌洗術)**. Open or arthroscopic. Leave drain in situ. |
| **Non-Operative** | NEVER a primary definitive treatment for SA | IV antibiotics ONLY after washout. |

### Acute Hematogenous Osteomyelitis (AHO) Management Indications
| Management Type | Indications | Key Techniques |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | Uncomplicated AHO, early presentation | **IV Antibiotics** (e.g., Cloxacillin/Cefazolin) for 1-2 weeks → switch to oral for 3-4 weeks total. Based on clinical improvement & CRP ↓. |
| **Operative** | Subperiosteal/intramedullary abscess, failure to improve after 48-72h of IV Abx, concurrent SA | **Cortical Window / Drilling (骨皮質開窗術)** to decompress marrow, debridement of necrotic tissue. |

### Transient Synovitis Management
| Management Type | Interventions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | Self-limiting. Strict bed rest, NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen), observation. Discharge when pain/limp resolves. |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative / Post-Acute Phase 
* **Weight Bearing:** Strictly Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) initially to prevent pathological fractures → progress to Partial Weight Bearing (PWB) as symptoms and CRP settle.
* **Physiotherapy:** Gentle passive ROM exercises once acute inflammation subsides to prevent joint contractures.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Joint & Bone Complications
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN) of Femoral Head (股骨頭缺血性壞死):** Due to ↑ intra-articular pressure occluding retinacular vessels (SA hip complication).
* **Cartilage Destruction:** Proteolytic enzymes from bacteria/WBCs destroy articular cartilage → early secondary osteoarthritis.
* **Physeal Arrest:** Infection crossing/damaging the growth plate → limb length discrepancy or angular deformity.
* **Pathological Dislocation:** Due to capsular distension and joint destruction.
* **Chronic Osteomyelitis:** Untreated AHO leading to persistent infection, discharging sinuses, and recurrent flares.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important initial step when a child presents with suspected septic arthritis of the hip? Answer: Secure blood cultures and perform an urgent US-guided joint aspiration before starting any antibiotics.
* Trap: Assuming a normal X-ray rules out acute osteomyelitis. Pearl: X-rays are usually normal for the first 10-14 days; MRI is the gold standard for early detection of marrow edema.
* Pitfall: Giving empiric antibiotics in the ED for an irritable hip before aspiration. This will sterilize the joint fluid, making it impossible to isolate the organism and target ongoing antibiotic therapy.
* Viva Question: Why are neonates more prone to concurrent osteomyelitis and septic arthritis? Answer: Transphyseal blood vessels remain patent until 18 months of age, allowing infection to cross the growth plate directly into the epiphysis and joint.
* Trap: Confusing the clinical signs of osteomyelitis and septic arthritis. Pearl: Osteomyelitis causes focal, point bony tenderness (metaphysis) with preserved joint ROM, whereas septic arthritis causes severe pain on any passive micromotion of the joint.
