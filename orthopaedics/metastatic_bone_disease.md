# Metastatic Bone Disease & Pathological Fractures (骨轉移及病理性骨折)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Metastatic Bone Disease (MBD) & Pathological Fractures
* **Metastatic Bone Disease (骨轉移)**: 2° malignant lesions in bone arising from hematogenous spread of a 1° distant solid tumor.
* **Pathological Fracture (病理性骨折)**: # occurring through bone weakened by pre-existing disease (e.g., MBD, osteoporosis, infection) under normal physiological load or minimal trauma.

### Common Primary Tumors (BLT-KP)
* **Prostate (前列腺)**
* **Breast (乳腺)**
* **Lung (肺)**
* **Kidney (腎)**
* **Thyroid (甲狀腺)**
* **Note**: In patients >40yo with a destructive bone lesion, consider MBD or Multiple Myeloma until proven otherwise.

### Epidemiology & Predilection Sites
* Bone = 3rd most common site of metastasis (after Lung, Liver).
* **Location frequency**: Spine (Thoracic > Lumbar) → Pelvis → Ribs → Proximal Femur → Proximal Humerus.
* Lesions distal to knee/elbow (acrometastasis) are rare (strongly suspect Lung or Renal cell carcinoma).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Pathophysiology of Bone Metastasis
* **Hematogenous spread**: Often via **Batson's venous plexus** (valveless paraspinal veins) → frequent spinal/pelvic metastases.
* Tumor cells disrupt normal osteoblast/osteoclast coupling cycle.

### Osteolytic vs. Osteoblastic Lesions
* **Osteolytic (溶骨性)**: Tumors (Breast, Lung, Thyroid, Kidney) secrete **PTHrP** → ↑ RANKL → ↑ Osteoclast activity → massive bone resorption.
* **Osteoblastic (成骨性)**: Tumors (Prostate, Hodgkin's) secrete Endothelin-1/BMPs → stimulate Osteoblasts → disordered, weak woven bone formation.
* Mixed lesions frequently occur (e.g., Breast cancer).

### Biomechanics of Pathological Fractures
* Cortical defects act as **stress risers**.
* A defect involving >50% of cortical diameter ↓ bone torsional strength by ~60-70%.
* Avulsion of normal physiological forces (e.g., Iliopsoas on lesser trochanter) causes mechanical failure.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### History & Symptoms of MBD
* Insidious onset of deep, unrelenting localized bone pain.
* **Red Flags**: Night pain, rest pain, pain not relieved by standard analgesia.
* Mechanical pain: Worsens with weight-bearing (indicates impending #).
* Constitutional symptoms: Unexplained weight loss, night sweats, fatigue.

### Physical Examination (Look, Feel, Move)
* **Look**:
  * Cachexia, pallor.
  * Visible mass, swelling, or clinical deformity (if actual #).
  * Mastectomy scars, thyroidectomy scars (clues to 1°).
* **Feel**:
  * Localized bony tenderness (often prominent over spinous processes in spinal mets).
  * Palpable firm, fixed soft tissue mass.
  * Prostate exam (DRE) / Breast exam (mandatory if 1° unknown).
* **Move**:
  * Pain exacerbated by loading or ROM.
  * Inability to weight-bear (acute pathological #).

### Neurological & Vascular Assessment
* **Neuro**:
  * Mandatory complete spinal examination (tone, power, sensation, reflexes).
  * Look for signs of **Spinal Cord Compression** (UMN signs: Hyperreflexia, clonus, +ve Babinski).
  * Cauda Equina signs (LMN signs: Saddle anesthesia, ↓ sphincter tone).
* **Systemic/Metabolic**:
  * Signs of **Hypercalcemia of Malignancy**: Confusion, lethargy, dehydration (polyuria), hyporeflexia, muscle weakness.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Mirels' Scoring System for Impending Fractures
* Determines risk of pathological # in long bones and guides prophylactic fixation.
* Score is calculated by adding points from 4 categories (Min = 4, Max = 12).

| Variable | 1 Point | 2 Points | 3 Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Site** | Upper limb | Lower limb | Peri-trochanteric |
| **Pain** | Mild | Moderate | Functional / Severe |
| **Lesion** | Blastic | Mixed | Lytic |
| **Size** (Cortical width) | $<1/3$ | $1/3 - 2/3$ | $>2/3$ |

* **Mirels' Score Interpretation**:
  * $\le 7$: Low risk (Radiotherapy & Observation).
  * $8$: Equivocal (Borderline, clinical judgment).
  * $\ge 9$: High risk (Prophylactic internal fixation indicated).

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiographic Findings of MBD
* Requires $30-50\%$ bone mineral loss to be visible on X-ray.
* **Lytic lesions**: Moth-eaten or permeative destruction, lack of sclerotic rim, cortical thinning/breakout.
* **Blastic lesions**: Ivory vertebra, localized sclerotic zones.
* **Spine**: "Winking Owl" sign (loss of pedicle on AP view).

### Advanced Imaging (Bone Scan, CT, MRI, PET)
* **Technetium-99m Bone Scan**: Sensitive for osteoblastic activity. Good for screening whole skeleton. (Note: May be false -ve in purely lytic lesions like Myeloma or Renal/Thyroid).
* **PET-CT**: High sensitivity for both lytic and blastic lesions. Excellent for staging and locating unknown 1°.
* **MRI**: Modality of choice for assessing soft tissue extension, spinal cord compression, and marrow infiltration.

### Laboratory & Pathological Investigations
* **Blood**: ↑ Ca2+ (Hypercalcemia), ↑ ALP (bone turnover).
* **Renal/Liver**: Cr, Urea, LFTs.
* **Tumor Markers**: PSA (Prostate), CEA, CA 125, Thyroglobulin.
* **Myeloma Screen**: Serum/Urine Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP/UPEP), Bence-Jones proteins.
* **Biopsy**: Essential for definitive tissue diagnosis if 1° is unknown.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management
* **ABCDEs**: Stabilize hemodynamics, IV access.
* **Hypercalcemia Management**:
  * Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation (Normal Saline).
  * Loop diuretics (post-rehydration).
  * IV Bisphosphonates (e.g., Zoledronic acid) or Denosumab.
* **Pain Control**: IV opioids, paracetamol (avoid NSAIDs if renal impairment).
* NPO (keep fasted) and prepare for urgent OT if actual # or impending cord compression.

### Immobilization & Orthopaedic Splinting
* **Extremity #**: Apply immediate stabilization (e.g., Thomas splint for femur, Plaster of Paris backslab) to ↓ pain and prevent further soft tissue damage.
* **Spinal Lesions**: Strict bed rest, flat positioning, log-rolling if spinal instability or cord compression is suspected.
* **Steroids**: Stat IV Dexamethasone (16mg) if suspected malignant spinal cord compression.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Goals of Definitive Surgical Management
* Relieve pain.
* Restore function and facilitate immediate mobilization.
* Construct MUST outlive the patient's expected lifespan.
* Facilitate nursing care.

### Non-Operative Management Indications
* Estimated life expectancy $< 6$ weeks.
* Severe comorbidities precluding anesthesia (unfit for surgery).
* Mirels' Score $\le 7$ (Low risk of impending #).
* Highly radiosensitive tumors (e.g., Lymphoma, Myeloma) without structural instability.
* **Management**: Local external beam radiation therapy (EBRT), Bisphosphonates, bracing, systemic chemo/hormonal therapy.

### Operative Management & Surgical Techniques
* **Indications**: Mirels' Score $\ge 8$, completed pathological #, intractable pain unresponsive to radiotherapy, avulsion of lesser trochanter.

| Anatomical Site | Preferred Surgical Technique |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Diaphysis (Femur/Tibia/Humerus)** | Cephalomedullary / Intramedullary Nailing (IMN). Load-sharing, bypasses the entire bone to protect against skip lesions. |
| **Proximal Femur (Neck / Head)** | Cemented Hemiarthroplasty or Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA). Excision of tumor burden. Cement provides immediate stability. |
| **Metaphysis / Epiphysis (Extensive loss)** | Endoprosthetic reconstruction (Tumor prosthesis) + PMMA bone cement. |
| **Spine (Cord compression/Instability)** | Posterior decompression (Laminectomy) + pedicle screw stabilization. Palliative radiotherapy post-op. |

* **Important Principle**: Always use **PMMA bone cement** (polymethylmethacrylate) to augment fixation in massive lytic defects. Do NOT rely on bone healing/union (bone will not heal due to tumor and subsequent radiotherapy).

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative Mobilization & Weight-Bearing
* **Weight-Bearing**: Immediate Weight-Bearing As Tolerated (WBAT) is the standard goal. Implants and cement are designed for immediate stability.
* **Physiotherapy**: Early mobilization, chest PT to prevent pneumonia, DVT prophylaxis via mechanical pumps and early ambulation.
* **Occupational Therapy**: Assessment for home modifications, walking aids, ADL training.

### Adjuvant Therapy Coordination
* **Radiotherapy Timing**: Must delay post-operative EBRT for at least **2-3 weeks** to allow surgical wound healing. Early radiation → severe wound dehiscence and infection.
* Multi-Disciplinary Team (MDT) approach (Oncology, Orthopaedics, Palliative Care).

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Surgical Complications in MBD
* **Implant Failure / Construct Loosening**: Disease progression outlasting the implant.
* **Non-union**: Pathological fractures rarely achieve bony union, especially post-radiotherapy.
* **Infection**: ↑ risk due to immunosuppression (chemo/radiotherapy), malnutrition, cachexia.
* **Intra-operative Bleeding**: Especially high risk with Renal Cell and Thyroid carcinomas (highly vascularized).

### Disease-Related Complications
* **Malignant Spinal Cord Compression (MSCC)**: Irreversible paralysis, bowel/bladder incontinence.
* **Hypercalcemic Crisis**: Arrhythmias, cardiac arrest, coma.
* **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE)**: Deep vein thrombosis/Pulmonary embolism due to hypercoagulable state of malignancy + immobilization.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of a destructive bone lesion in a patient over 40 years old? Answer: Metastatic bone disease or Multiple Myeloma.
* Viva Question: Why do we prefer Intramedullary Nailing (IMN) over plates for diaphyseal pathological fractures? Answer: IMN is a load-sharing device that protects the entire length of the bone, mitigating the risk of subsequent fractures from skip lesions.
* Viva Question: What specific pre-operative intervention is mandatory before operating on a bone metastasis from Renal Cell or Thyroid Carcinoma? Answer: Pre-operative angiographic embolization to minimize massive intra-operative hemorrhage.
* Trap: Assuming a purely lytic lesion will show up on a Technetium-99m bone scan. Pearl: Bone scans detect osteoblastic activity (bone formation). Purely lytic lesions (e.g., Myeloma, Renal) may result in a false-negative "cold" scan. Request a PET-CT or skeletal survey instead.
* Trap: Depending on physiological bone healing (callus formation) for a pathological fracture. Pearl: Always assume the bone will NOT heal. You must use load-bearing implants and augment with PMMA bone cement to provide immediate, lifetime stability.
* Pitfall: Performing an excisional biopsy or open reduction through standard anatomical planes without consulting oncology. You risk contaminating whole compartments, destroying future limb-salvage options.
* Pitfall: Starting post-operative radiotherapy immediately after discharge. Delayed wound healing, necrosis, and deep infection will occur; always wait 2-3 weeks for the surgical incision to heal completely.
