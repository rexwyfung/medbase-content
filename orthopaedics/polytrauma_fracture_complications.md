# Polytrauma & Fracture Complications (多發性創傷與骨折併發症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Polytrauma Clinical Definition
* **Berlin Definition**: Abbreviated Injury Scale (**AIS**) $\ge$ 3 in $\ge$ 2 body regions + $\ge$ 1 physiological derangement (e.g., hypotension, coagulopathy)
* **Classic Definition**: Injury Severity Score (**ISS**) > 16 

### Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS) Pathophysiology
* **1° Hit**: Initial traumatic insult → triggers massive innate immune response → systemic release of cytokines (**IL-6**, **TNF-$\alpha$**) → **SIRS (全身炎症反應綜合症)**
* **2° Hit**: Subsequent physiological insults (surgery, intramedullary nailing, hypoxia) → exacerbates SIRS → **ARDS** & **MODS (多器官功能障礙綜合症)**
* **Compensatory Anti-inflammatory Response Syndrome (CARS)**: Late phase immunosuppression → ↑ sepsis risk

### Fracture Complication Definitions
* **Delayed Union**: # not united within expected timeframe (e.g., 3-6 months) but ongoing signs of healing
* **Non-Union (骨折不癒合)**: Cessation of all osteogenic activity at # site $\ge$ 6-9 months + no clinical/radiological progression for 3 consecutive months
* **Malunion (骨折畸形癒合)**: # heals in unacceptable anatomical position (angulation, rotation, shortening)

### Epidemiology & MOI
* **Bimodal Distribution**: Young males (high-energy RTAs, falls from height) vs Elderly (low-energy falls)
* **Blunt Trauma**: Most common MOI in Hong Kong (pedestrian vs vehicle, industrial accidents)

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### The Lethal Triad (死亡三角)
* **Hypothermia**: Core temp < 35°C → ↓ enzymatic activity of clotting cascade
* **Acidosis**: pH < 7.35 2° to hypoperfusion/anaerobic metabolism/↑ lactate → ↓ myocardial contractility & ↓ coagulation
* **Coagulopathy**: Consumptive coagulopathy/dilution from crystalloids → uncontrolled bleeding
* **Vicious Cycle**: Bleeding → ↓ perfusion → Acidosis + Hypothermia → Worsens Coagulopathy → More bleeding

### Biomechanics of Fracture Healing
* **1° (Direct) Bone Healing**: Requires absolute stability (rigid **ORIF**) + anatomical reduction. No callus formation. Cutting cones bridge gap.
* **2° (Indirect) Bone Healing**: Requires relative stability (**IMN**, **Ex-Fix**, cast). Endochondral ossification → hematoma → soft callus → hard callus → remodeling.
* **Perren’s Strain Theory**: Tissues tolerate specific strain limits (Granulation tissue = 100%, Cartilage = 10%, Bone = 2%). High strain → non-union.

### Vascular Supply & Fracture Healing
* **Dual Blood Supply**: 
  * Endosteal (nutrient a.) → supplies inner 2/3 cortex
  * Periosteal → supplies outer 1/3 cortex
* **Trauma Impact**: High-energy trauma/periosteal stripping destroys vascularity → Atrophic non-union risk

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### ATLS Primary Survey Findings (ABCDE)
* **Airway/Breathing**: Tracheal deviation, absent breath sounds (tension pneumothorax), flail chest
* **Circulation**: Tachycardia, hypotension, cool peripheries, delayed capillary refill (+ve shock signs)
* **Disability**: ↓ GCS, pupillary asymmetry, focal neurological deficits
* **Exposure**: Hypothermia, active bleeding sites, obvious deformities

### Orthopaedic Secondary Survey (Head-to-Toe)
* **Look**: Severe limb deformity, open wounds (bone exposed/bleeding), degloving, ecchymosis, compartment swelling
* **Feel**: Subcutaneous emphysema, pelvic instability on gentle AP compression (perform ONCE only), crepitus, step-off deformities
* **Move**: Paradoxical limb movement, inability to weight-bear/actively move joints

### Local Assessment of Non-Union
* **Look**: Persistent swelling, angular deformity
* **Feel**: Tenderness at # site $> 6$ months post-injury, palpable gap
* **Move**: Pain on weight-bearing, palpable/visible motion at # site (pseudoarthrosis)

### Systemic Complication Assessment (Fat Embolism Syndrome - FES)
* **Look**: Petechial rash (axilla, conjunctiva, palate) - pathognomonic
* **Vital Signs**: Tachypnea (hypoxia), Tachycardia
* **CNS**: Altered mental status, confusion, agitation

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) & Injury Severity Score (ISS)
| ISS Region | AIS Score (0-6) | ISS Calculation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Head & Neck | 1 = Minor | ISS = Sum of squares of top 3 AIS scores |
| Face | 2 = Moderate | Max Score = 75 |
| Chest | 3 = Serious | ISS $> 15$ = Major Trauma |
| Abdomen | 4 = Severe | |
| Extremities/Pelvis| 5 = Critical | |
| External | 6 = Unsurvivable | AIS 6 automatically assigns ISS 75 |

### Weber & Cech Non-Union Classification
| Type | Subtype | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypertrophic (Elephant foot)** | Hypertrophic | Abundant callus, mechanically unstable, biologically active |
| | Mildly hypertrophic (Horse hoof)| Moderate callus |
| | Oligotrophic | Minimal callus |
| **Atrophic** | Torsion wedge | Intermediate fragment with ↓ blood supply |
| | Comminuted | Necrotic fragments |
| | Defect | Gap at # site |
| | Atrophic | No callus, biologically inert, narrow/rounded bone ends |

### Gurd's Criteria for Fat Embolism Syndrome (FES)
| Criteria Type | Clinical Features (Requires 1 Major + 4 Minor OR 2 Major) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Major** | Petechial rash, Respiratory insufficiency, Cerebral involvement |
| **Minor** | Tachycardia, Pyrexia, Retinal changes, Jaundice, Renal signs, ↑ ESR, Fat macroglobulinemia, Sudden ↓ Hb/Plt |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Polytrauma Imaging Protocol
* **Trauma Pan-Scan CT**: Non-contrast CT Head/C-Spine + Contrast CT Thorax/Abdomen/Pelvis (standard in HA EDs for major trauma)
* **Plain X-rays (Trauma Series)**: AP Chest, AP Pelvis (often superseded by pan-scan in modern trauma bays)
* **Extremity X-rays**: AP & Lateral views of affected limbs + joint above & below

### Non-Union & Malunion Imaging
* **Serial Plain X-rays**: Assess progressive callus formation, obliteration of # line, implant loosening/breakage
* **CT Scan Extremity**: Gold standard to confirm non-union (assesses bridging trabeculae across # gap)
* **Bone Scan (Tc-99m)**: Hot = hypertrophic; Cold = atrophic (rarely used now, replaced by CT)

### Complication Diagnostics
* **Bloods**: Serial lactate & ABG (perfusion markers), Coagulation profile (fibrinogen, PT, aPTT for DIC)
* **CXR**: Bilateral fluffy infiltrates (ARDS/FES presentation)

## 6. Initial ED Management (Reduction, Splinting/Casting, Traction)

### Acute Resuscitation (ATLS Protocol)
* **Airway**: Rapid Sequence Induction (RSI) if GCS $\le$ 8
* **Circulation**: Massive Transfusion Protocol (1:1:1 PRBC:FFP:Platelets), Tranexamic Acid (TXA) 1g IV within 3 hrs
* **Pelvic Stabilization**: Apply pelvic binder centered over **greater trochanters** (NOT iliac crests)

### Acute Orthopaedic Extremity Interventions
* **Gross Deformity**: Immediate closed reduction to restore axial alignment & relieve skin tension
* **Splinting**: Plaster of Paris (POP) backslab (e.g., above-knee/below-knee) for splintage and pain control
* **Traction**: Thomas splint + Skin traction / Skeletal traction for femur fractures to control bleeding and spasm
* **Open Fractures**: 
  * Remove gross debris, photograph, cover with betadine-soaked sterile dressing
  * IV Antibiotics (Cefazolin $\pm$ Gentamicin) & Tetanus toxoid IMMEDIATELY

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative Indications & Techniques)

### Decision Matrix: Early Total Care (ETC) vs. Damage Control Orthopaedics (DCO)
| Parameter | Early Total Care (ETC) | Damage Control Orthopaedics (DCO) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Concept** | Definitive internal fixation $< 24$ hrs | Rapid temporary Ex-Fix $\rightarrow$ definitive fixation later |
| **Hemodynamics**| Stable (Responds to fluids) | Unstable / In extremis |
| **Lactate** | $< 2.5$ mmol/L | $> 2.5$ mmol/L or rising |
| **Coagulation** | Normal Platelets & PT/aPTT | Coagulopathic (Plt $< 90$, INR $> 1.5$) |
| **Temperature** | $> 35^\circ$C | $< 35^\circ$C |
| **Thoracic Injury**| None or mild | Severe (Bilateral contusions, ARDS) |

### DCO Surgical Principles
* **Goal**: Minimize surgical time, limit 2° hit, control hemorrhage
* **Technique**: Rapid application of external fixator (Ex-Fix) spanning the fracture zone
* **Conversion**: Convert Ex-Fix to IMN/ORIF once patient physiology normalizes (usually day 5-10)

### ETC Surgical Principles
* **Goal**: Immediate definitive stabilization to facilitate upright nursing and pulmonary toilet
* **Technique**: Reamed Intramedullary Nailing (IMN) for long bones, Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF) for articular fractures

### Non-Union Surgical Management
* **Hypertrophic Non-Union**: 
  * Biologically active but mechanically unstable
  * **Intervention**: Improve mechanical stability (Exchange nailing to larger IMN, or revise to compression plating). Bone graft NOT usually required.
* **Atrophic Non-Union**: 
  * Biologically inert 
  * **Intervention**: Debridement of fibrous tissue + Decortication (Petalling) + **Bone Grafting** (Autologous iliac crest bone graft - ICBG) + Rigid fixation (ORIF).
* **Infected Non-Union**:
  * **Intervention**: Radical debridement (Masquelet technique: cement spacer with Abx $\rightarrow$ induced membrane $\rightarrow$ bone graft later) or Ilizarov frame (bone transport).

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Trauma Intensive Care
* **Pulmonary Toilet**: Early mobilization / upright sitting (facilitated by ETC) to prevent VAP and atelectasis
* **DVT Prophylaxis**: LMWH (Clexane) started once hemorrhagic control achieved + mechanical calf pumps

### Functional Rehabilitation
* **Weight-Bearing Strategy**: 
  * IMN (Femur/Tibia): Usually early weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT)
  * ORIF (Articular): Strict non-weight-bearing (NWB) for 6-8 weeks
* **Range of Motion**: Early active ROM of adjacent joints to prevent stiffness/contractures

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Acute Systemic Complications
* **Fat Embolism Syndrome (FES)**: Marrow fat enters venous system $\rightarrow$ pulmonary/cerebral microvasculature occlusion. Peaks at 24-72 hrs.
* **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)**: Severe inflammatory lung injury 2° to systemic SIRS.
* **Compartment Syndrome**: Surgical emergency. Tissue pressure $>$ capillary perfusion pressure $\rightarrow$ muscle/nerve ischemia. Requires emergency 4-compartment fasciotomy.

### Late Local Complications
* **Implant Failure**: Fatigue breakage of screws/plates due to non-union (race between bone healing and metal fatigue).
* **Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS)**: Disproportionate pain, autonomic dysfunction (sweating, color changes), trophic changes.
* **Post-Traumatic Osteoarthritis**: Common following intra-articular fractures (malunion/step-off $> 2$mm).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What constitutes the lethal triad of trauma? Answer: Hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy.
* Viva Question: What is the primary difference in managing an atrophic versus a hypertrophic non-union? Answer: Hypertrophic needs mechanical stability (fixation); atrophic needs biological stimulation (bone graft) plus stability.
* Viva Question: What is the timeline and classic triad for Fat Embolism Syndrome? Answer: 24-72 hours post-injury; respiratory distress, neurological changes, and a petechial rash.
* Trap: Treating a hypertrophic non-union with bone grafting. Pearl: Hypertrophic non-union already has excellent biology (plentiful callus); it only needs improved mechanical stability (e.g., exchange nailing).
* Trap: Applying a pelvic binder over the iliac crests. Pearl: Pelvic binders must be centered directly over the greater trochanters to effectively close the pelvic volume.
* Pitfall: Delaying external fixation in a hemodynamically unstable polytrauma patient to attempt definitive IMN. Definitive fixation acts as a massive "second hit" (SIRS response) which can precipitate ARDS or death; always opt for Damage Control Orthopaedics (DCO) with Ex-Fix first.
* Pitfall: Ruling out compartment syndrome just because palpable pulses are present. Pulses remain intact until very late stages of compartment syndrome; diagnosis relies on pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch.
