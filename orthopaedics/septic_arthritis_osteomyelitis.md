# Septic Arthritis & Osteomyelitis (化膿性關節炎與骨髓炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Septic Arthritis (SA) Clinical Definition
* **Definition:** Purulent infection of joint space (關節腔).
* **Pathogenesis:** Rapid cartilage destruction via bacterial toxins & host inflammatory enzymes (metalloproteinases).
* **MOI:** 
* Hematogenous spread (most common).
* Direct inoculation (trauma, intra-articular injection, surgery).
* Contiguous spread (from adjacent OM).

### Osteomyelitis (OM) Clinical Definition
* **Definition:** Infection of bone & bone marrow (骨髓).
* **Pathogenesis:** Suppuration → ↑ intramedullary pressure → vascular thrombosis → bone necrosis (**sequestrum** 死骨) → reactive new bone formation (**involucrum** 包殼).
* **MOI:** 
* Hematogenous (mostly children).
* Contiguous (e.g., diabetic foot ulcer).
* Direct inoculation (e.g., open #).

### Epidemiology & Demographics
* **SA:** Bimodal (children <3 yrs, adults >65 yrs). Most common in knee (adults) & hip (children).
* **OM:** Bimodal. Children (long bone metaphysis). Adults (vertebrae).
* **Risk Factors:** DM, RA, IVDU, immunosuppression, prosthetic joints, end-stage renal failure (HK HA prevalent).

### Pathogen Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* ***S. aureus*:** Most common overall (both SA & OM).
* **Group B *Streptococcus*:** Neonates.
* ***N. gonorrhoeae*:** Young sexually active adults (polyarticular, migratory).
* ***Salmonella* spp.:** Sickle cell disease / Thalassaemia major.
* ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa*:** IVDU, puncture wounds through rubber sole shoes.
* ***M. tuberculosis* (TB):** Endemic in HK. Gradual onset, spinal involvement (Pott's disease).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Metaphyseal Vascular Anatomy (Children)
* **Vascular Loops:** Nutrient artery ends in acute hairpin loops in metaphysis.
* **Sluggish Flow:** ↓ blood velocity at loops → ideal nidus for bacterial deposition.
* **Lack of Macrophages:** Metaphyseal capillaries lack active phagocytes.

### The Physis (Growth Plate) Barrier Effect
* **Neonates (<1 yr):** Transphyseal blood vessels exist. OM rapidly spreads → SA.
* **Children (1-15 yrs):** Physis is avascular & acts as mechanical barrier. OM contained in metaphysis. Breakout occurs laterally into subperiosteal space.
* **Adults (>15 yrs):** Physis fused. Vascular connections re-establish. OM can spread directly into joint space → SA.

### Intracapsular Metaphyses (High-Risk Zones)
* **Anatomy:** Certain metaphyses lie completely within the joint capsule.
* **Clinical Consequence:** Metaphyseal OM here erupts directly into joint → primary SA.
* **High-Risk Joints:** 
* **Hip** (Proximal femur).
* **Shoulder** (Proximal humerus).
* **Ankle** (Distal fibula/tibia).
* **Elbow** (Proximal radius).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Septic Arthritis (SA) Clinical Findings
* **Look:** Gross swelling, severe erythema, joint held in position of maximal capacity (e.g., hip in flexion, abduction, external rotation). Pseudoparalysis in neonates.
* **Feel:** Exquisite warmth (calor), palpable effusion, diffuse tenderness over entire joint capsule.
* **Move:** **Pathognomonic:** Severe, excruciating pain with *any* passive micromotion. True ROM is zero.

### Osteomyelitis (OM) Clinical Findings
* **Look:** Erythema, swelling. Chronic OM: draining sinus tracts, thickened deformed skin.
* **Feel:** Exquisite, highly localized **point tenderness** over affected metaphysis. Calor +ve.
* **Move:** Adjacent joint ROM usually preserved initially (differentiates from SA). Pain on weight-bearing.

### Neurovascular (NV) Status Examination
* **Vascular:** Assess distal pulses (dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial) & capillary refill. Rule out concurrent septic thromboembolism or compartment syndrome from profound swelling.
* **Neurological:** Light touch & motor function. Crucial in vertebral OM / epidural abscess (assess for myelopathy, radiculopathy, sphincter tone).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Waldvogel Classification for Osteomyelitis (Etiology/Duration)
| Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hematogenous** | Bloodborne seeding (mostly kids/vertebrae). |
| **Contiguous** | Spread from adjacent soft tissue (e.g., diabetic ulcer). |
| **Direct** | Introduced via trauma or surgery (e.g., open #). |
| **Duration** | Acute (days/weeks), Subacute (weeks/months, Brodie's abscess), Chronic (necrotic bone present). |

### Cierny-Mader Classification for Osteomyelitis (Anatomic)
| Stage | Anatomic Extent |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1** | Medullary (Endosteal). |
| **Stage 2** | Superficial (Cortical surface). |
| **Stage 3** | Localized (Full thickness cortical, stable). |
| **Stage 4** | Diffuse (Circumferential, mechanically unstable). |

### Cierny-Mader Classification for Osteomyelitis (Physiologic)
| Host Grade | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Normal host (good immunity/vascularity). |
| **B** | Compromised host (Systemic e.g., DM / Local e.g., poor vessels). |
| **C** | Treatment worse than disease (Severe morbidity). |

### Gächter Classification for Septic Arthritis (Arthroscopic)
| Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Opacity of fluid, redness, petechiae, normal cartilage. |
| **II** | Fibrinous deposits, pus, normal cartilage. |
| **III** | Thickening of synovial membrane, cartilage damage. |
| **IV** | Extensive cartilage destruction, subchondral bone exposed. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations 

### Blood Laboratory Investigations
* **WBC:** ↑ (often left shift). Can be normal.
* **ESR / CRP:** ↑↑. CRP rises rapidly (6-8h), falls rapidly with successful Tx. ESR rises slowly, falls slowly.
* **Blood Cultures:** Mandatory (x2 sets) *before* Abx. +ve in 50% of hematogenous cases.

### Synovial Fluid Analysis (Arthrocentesis)
* **Gross:** Turbid, purulent, yellow/green.
* **Cell Count:** WBC >50,000/mm³ (often >100,000), >90% PMNs (Polymorphonuclear leukocytes).
* **Gram Stain & Culture:** Pathognomonic if +ve.
* **Crystal Analysis:** Rule out gout/pseudogout (can coexist).
* **Glucose:** ↓ (compared to serum).

### Plain Radiography (X-ray)
* **Early (0-2 weeks):** Often completely normal. Soft tissue swelling, fat pad displacement.
* **Late (>2 weeks):** Regional osteopenia, periosteal reaction, joint space narrowing (cartilage loss), **sequestrum** (dense dead bone), **involucrum** (new bone shell).

### Advanced Imaging Modalities
* **Ultrasound (US):** Highly sensitive for detecting joint effusion (especially deep joints like hip). Used for US-guided aspiration.
* **MRI with Gadolinium:** Gold standard for OM. Shows bone marrow edema (↓ T1, ↑ T2/STIR). Delineates epidural abscesses and soft tissue extension.
* **Bone Scan (Tc-99m):** High sensitivity, low specificity. Three-phase scan +ve in all phases for OM.
* **WBC Scan (Indium-111):** Useful for distinguishing infection from aseptic loosening in arthroplasty.

## 6. Initial ED Management (Reduction, Splinting/Casting, Traction)

### Acute Resuscitation & Sepsis Protocol
* **Airway/Breathing/Circulation:** Stabilize hemodynamics. IV fluids for septic shock.
* **Sepsis 6 (HK HA Protocol):** O2, Blood C/x, IV Abx, IV Fluids, Lactate, Urine output monitoring.

### Urgent Joint Aspiration
* **Action:** Arthrocentesis must be performed immediately in ED for suspected SA.
* **Rule:** Must be done *before* administering IV Abx to avoid sterile cultures.
* **Exception:** If patient is in fulminant septic shock, give Abx first.

### Empirical Antibiotic Therapy (HK HA Guidelines)
* **General (Adult):** IV Cloxacillin (covers *MSSA*) +/- IV Ceftriaxone (covers Gram -ve).
* **High MRSA Risk (Nursing home, prior colonization):** IV Vancomycin (requires trough level monitoring).
* **Gonococcal Suspicion:** IV Ceftriaxone.
* **Children (<5 yrs):** IV Cefuroxime or Cefotaxime (covers *H. influenzae* & *S. aureus*).

### Immobilization & Splinting
* **Action:** Apply backslab or skin traction (Thomas splint for knee/femur).
* **Rationale:** Relieves severe muscle spasm, provides pain relief, prevents joint contracture in non-functional position.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative)

### Septic Arthritis: Operative Management (Surgical Emergency)
| Indication | Management Algorithm |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Native Joint SA** | Urgent **Irrigation & Debridement (I&D)**. Arthroscopic (knee/shoulder) vs Arthrotomy (hip/open washout). Copious saline (9L+). Synovectomy if severe. |
| **Prosthetic Joint Infection (Acute <4 wks)** | **DAIR** (Debridement, Antibiotics, Implant Retention) + polyethylene liner exchange. |
| **Prosthetic Joint Infection (Chronic >4 wks)** | **2-Stage Revision:** 1st stage (Remove implant, insert antibiotic PMMA cement spacer). 6 wks IV Abx. 2nd stage (Re-implantation). |

### Acute Osteomyelitis Management
| Approach | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative (Primary)** | IV Abx for 4-6 weeks (PICC line). Switch to PO Abx when CRP normalizes & clinical improvement. |
| **Operative Indications** | 1. Failure of IV Abx after 48-72h. <br> 2. Subperiosteal abscess formation. <br> 3. Intra-articular extension (associated SA). |
| **Surgical Technique** | Cortical windowing (drilling holes in cortex) to decompress medullary cavity and drain pus. |

### Chronic Osteomyelitis Management (Operative)
* **Principle 1: Radical Debridement:** Excise all necrotic bone/tissue until bleeding bone ("paprika sign").
* **Principle 2: Dead Space Management:** Fill resultant void with antibiotic-loaded PMMA beads, muscle flaps, or bone transport (Ilizarov technique).
* **Principle 3: Soft Tissue Coverage:** Local/Free muscle flaps (e.g., gastrocnemius, latissimus dorsi) to bring vascularity to the avascular bed.
* **Principle 4: Bone Stabilization:** External fixation if extensive bone resection causes instability.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Surgical Septic Arthritis Rehab
* **Immediate Post-op (Days 0-3):** **Continuous Passive Motion (CPM)**. Essential to prevent intra-articular adhesions/ankylosis and to provide nutritional diffusion to surviving chondrocytes.
* **Weight Bearing:** Protected weight-bearing with crutches. FWB delayed until inflammation subsides to protect weakened cartilage.
* **Physiotherapy:** Aggressive active-assisted ROM exercises once acute pain settles.

### Post-Surgical Osteomyelitis Rehab
* **Weight Bearing:** Strict Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) if massive cortical resection (high risk of pathological #).
* **Orthosis:** Cast or functional brace to protect the bone window.
* **Progression:** Advance to Partial Weight Bearing (PWB) once x-ray shows consolidation of the bone defect/graft.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Joint-Specific Complications (Septic Arthritis)
* **Cartilage Destruction:** Rapid onset osteoarthritis (2° OA).
* **Ankylosis:** Fibrous or bony fusion of the joint → permanent stiffness.
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN):** Especially in pediatric hip SA due to tamponade of retinacular vessels by intracapsular pus.
* **Joint Dislocation:** Destruction of ligaments/capsule (Charcot-like joint destruction).

### Bone-Specific Complications (Osteomyelitis)
* **Pathological Fracture:** Weakened bone from cortical osteolysis/debridement.
* **Growth Arrest / Deformity:** Physis destruction in children → limb length discrepancy or angular deformity.
* **Chronic Suppuration:** Sinus tract formation with lifelong intermittent discharge.
* **Marjolin's Ulcer:** Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) arising in chronic OM sinus tract (usually >10-20 years post-infection).

### Systemic Complications
* Septic shock, multi-organ failure, death.
* Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) / Pulmonary Embolism (PE) due to prolonged immobility.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most reliable clinical sign to differentiate Septic Arthritis from Osteomyelitis in a child? Answer: Exquisite pain with passive micromotion of the joint indicates Septic Arthritis. In isolated Osteomyelitis, joint ROM is usually preserved and painless.
* Trap: Waiting for X-ray changes to diagnose acute osteomyelitis. Pearl: Plain X-rays are typically normal for the first 10-14 days. MRI is the gold standard for early diagnosis, showing bone marrow edema.
* Pitfall: Administering empirical IV antibiotics in the ED before attempting joint aspiration. This sterilizes the fluid and ruins diagnostic cultures. Always aspirate first unless the patient is in fulminant septic shock.
* Viva Question: Why is a septic hip in an infant considered an extreme surgical emergency? Answer: The purulent effusion causes elevated intracapsular pressure, which occludes the delicate retinacular vessels, leading to irreversible avascular necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head within 24 hours.
* Trap: Assuming a normal WBC count rules out septic arthritis. Pearl: Peripheral WBC can be normal. The most sensitive serum marker is CRP, and the definitive test is a synovial fluid WBC >50,000/mm³.
* Pitfall: Leaving a massive bone void empty after chronic osteomyelitis debridement. Dead space fills with hematoma and provides a nidus for recurrent infection; always manage dead space with antibiotic beads, cement spacers, or vascularized flaps.
* Viva Question: What is the "paprika sign" in chronic osteomyelitis surgery? Answer: It is the presence of punctate bleeding from cortical bone during debridement, indicating that all necrotic, avascular bone has been successfully removed and healthy bone has been reached.
