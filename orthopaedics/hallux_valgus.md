# Hallux Valgus (拇趾外翻)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Hallux Valgus Clinical Definition
* **Hallux Valgus (拇趾外翻)**: Complex 3D deformity characterized by lateral deviation of the great toe (proximal phalanx) at the **1st metatarsophalangeal joint (1st MTPJ)** + medial deviation of the **1st metatarsal (1st MT)**.

### Hallux Valgus Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Risk Factors
* **Intrinsic Factors**:
  * **Genetics**: +ve family history (autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance).
  * **Anatomy**: **Pes planus** (flatfoot), hypermobility of **1st tarsometatarsal joint (TMTJ)**, tight Achilles tendon.
  * **Systemic**: **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)**, connective tissue disorders (Marfan's, Ehlers-Danlos).
* **Extrinsic Factors**:
  * **Footwear**: Narrow toe-box, high heels (forces toes into cramped space).

### Hallux Valgus Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: F > M (approx. 15:1 ratio in surgical cohorts).
* **Age**: ↑ incidence with ↑ age (common in 5th-7th decades); juvenile variant exists but less common.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### 1st MTP Joint Anatomy & Sesamoids
* **Sesamoid Bones**: **Tibial (medial)** and **Fibular (lateral)** sesamoids sit within the tendons of **flexor hallucis brevis (FHB)** beneath the 1st MT head.
* **Crista**: Bony ridge on plantar 1st MT head separating the sesamoids; erodes in chronic valgus.
* **Vascular Supply**: **1st MT head** blood supply relies heavily on the **capsular network** (via 1st dorsal metatarsal artery). 

### Hallux Valgus Deforming Muscle Forces (Biomechanics)
* **Adductor Hallucis**: Pulls proximal phalanx laterally + pulls fibular sesamoid laterally.
* **Abductor Hallucis**: Slips plantar-ward; loses abducting power, acts as a plantarflexor → pronates the great toe.
* **EHL & FHL Bowstringing**: With lateral deviation, **Extensor Hallucis Longus (EHL)** & **Flexor Hallucis Longus (FHL)** tendons bowstring laterally, exacerbating the valgus force.
* **Medial Capsule**: Attenuates and stretches, allowing medial subluxation of the 1st MT head.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Hallux Valgus Look (Observation)
* **Great Toe**: Lateral deviation, pronation (nail faces medially).
* **Medial Eminence**: Prominent **bunion (滑液囊腫)** over medial 1st MT head + erythema/bursa.
* **Lesser Toes**: Overriding 2nd toe (crossover toe), hammer toe deformities (due to load transfer).
* **Plantar Aspect**: **Transfer metatarsalgia** (callosities under 2nd/3rd MT heads).
* **Hindfoot/Midfoot**: Look for associated **pes planus** or Achilles tightness.

### Hallux Valgus Feel (Palpation)
* **Bunion**: Tenderness over medial eminence bursa.
* **Lesser MT Heads**: Tenderness indicative of transfer metatarsalgia.

### Hallux Valgus Move (Range of Motion)
* **1st MTPJ ROM**: Assess for restriction/crepitus → rules out concurrent **Hallux Rigidus (OA)**.
* **Reducibility**: Passively correct valgus to differentiate flexible vs. fixed deformity.
* **Silfverskiöld Test**: Assess gastrocnemius tightness.
* **1st TMTJ Mobility**: Assess for hypermobility in sagittal plane (indicates need for Lapidus procedure).

### Hallux Valgus Neurovascular (NV) Status
* **Nerves**: Check sensation over medial aspect of great toe (compression of **dorsomedial cutaneous nerve**).
* **Pulses**: Palpate **dorsalis pedis** & **posterior tibial** arteries (crucial before planning surgery/tourniqet use).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Hallux Valgus Radiographic Angles
* **Hallux Valgus Angle (HVA)**: Angle between longitudinal axes of 1st MT and proximal phalanx.
* **Intermetatarsal Angle (IMA)**: Angle between longitudinal axes of 1st MT and 2nd MT.
* **Distal Metatarsal Articular Angle (DMAA)**: Angle between 1st MT axis and its distal articular surface (evaluates joint congruency).

### Hallux Valgus Severity Classification Table
| Severity | HVA (Normal <15°) | IMA (Normal <9°) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | 15° - 30° | 9° - 13° |
| **Moderate** | 30° - 40° | 13° - 20° |
| **Severe** | > 40° | > 20° |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Hallux Valgus X-Ray Views
* **Weight-bearing (WB) AP Foot**: Essential to assess true deformity under physiological load.
* **Weight-bearing (WB) Lateral Foot**: Assess medial arch (pes planus), 1st TMTJ, and dorsal osteophytes.
* **Sesamoid Skyline View**: Evaluates sesamoid subluxation and crista erosion.

### Hallux Valgus Radiological Findings
* **Angles**: ↑ HVA, ↑ IMA, ↑ DMAA.
* **Joint Congruency**: 1st MTPJ subluxation (lateral shift of phalanx base).
* **Sesamoids**: Lateral subluxation of sesamoid complex relative to 1st MT head (Hardy & Clapham grading).
* **OA Changes**: Joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, osteophytes (rules out isolated valgus, dictates fusion).

## 6. Initial ED / Outpatient Management

### Hallux Valgus Acute Presentation Principles
* **Setting**: Rarely an ED presentation unless acute bursitis/infection of the bunion.
* **Infection**: If ulcerated/infected (common in diabetic foot) → IV antibiotics, wound swab, offloading footwear.

### Hallux Valgus Non-Operative Management (1° Line)
* **Footwear Modification**: Wide, deep toe-box shoes, low heels (mainstay of conservative Rx in HK HA clinics).
* **Orthoses**: Silicone toe spacers, bunion pads, medial arch supports (for pes planus). 
* **Analgesia**: Oral NSAIDs or topical gels for bursitis flares.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative)

### Hallux Valgus Operative Indications
* **Primary Indication**: Intractable pain failing conservative treatment.
* **Secondary Indication**: Difficulty fitting into standard footwear, recurrent skin breakdown.
* **Contraindications**: **Purely cosmetic reasons** (absolute contraindication in HK HA), active infection, severe PVD, Charcot arthropathy.

### Hallux Valgus Soft Tissue Procedure
* **Modified McBride Procedure**: Lateral release (release of adductor hallucis + lateral MTP capsule + deep transverse metatarsal ligament) + medial capsular plication. *Rarely done in isolation, usually combined with osteotomy.*

### Hallux Valgus Surgical Algorithm Table
| Deformity Grade | Pathoanatomy | Preferred Surgical Procedure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** (IMA <13°) | Congruent/Subluxed MTPJ | **Chevron Osteotomy** (Distal 1st MT V-shaped cut) |
| **Moderate** (IMA 13-20°) | Subluxed MTPJ | **Scarf Osteotomy** (Diaphyseal Z-shaped cut) |
| **Severe** (IMA >20°) | Fixed Deformity | **Ludloff** (Proximal osteotomy) or **Lapidus** |
| **Hypermobile 1st TMTJ** | Instability at base | **Lapidus Procedure** (1st TMTJ Arthrodesis) |
| **Severe + 1st MTPJ OA** | Arthritis / RA | **1st MTPJ Arthrodesis** (Fusion) |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Hallux Valgus Post-Op Weight Bearing Protocol
* **0-6 Weeks**: Heel-weight bearing ONLY using a **Darco shoe** (stiff-soled post-op shoe). No forefoot loading.
* **6 Weeks**: X-ray check for bony union of osteotomy.
* **6-12 Weeks**: Transition to wide toe-box trainers/sneakers. Full weight-bearing permitted based on radiological union.

### Hallux Valgus Post-Op Physiotherapy
* **Early ROM**: Gentle active/passive 1st MTPJ ROM starts @ 1-2 weeks post-op to prevent severe stiffness.
* **Edema Control**: Strict leg elevation (toes above heart level) for the first 2 weeks to promote wound healing and ↓ swelling.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Hallux Valgus Early Surgical Complications
* **Wound Breakdown / Infection**: 1-2% risk, ↑ in diabetics/smokers.
* **Nerve Injury**: Neuropraxia of the **dorsomedial cutaneous nerve** (numbness over medial great toe).
* **DVT/PE**: Rare, chemical prophylaxis usually not required for isolated forefoot surgery in Asian populations unless high risk.

### Hallux Valgus Late Surgical Complications
* **Recurrence**: Most common complication (up to 15%), often due to under-correction or failure to address pes planus.
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN) of 1st MT Head**: ↑ risk with distal osteotomy (Chevron) combined with aggressive lateral capsular release.
* **Hallux Varus**: Over-correction (iatrogenic), leading to a medially deviated toe (harder to treat than valgus).
* **Non-union / Malunion**: Esp. with proximal osteotomies or Lapidus procedure.
* **Stiffness**: Loss of MTPJ dorsiflexion (common after Scarf/Chevron).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important radiological investigation for assessing Hallux Valgus? Answer: Weight-bearing AP foot X-ray, because non-weight-bearing views underestimate the HVA and IMA angles.
* Trap: Recommending surgery for a painless bunion because the patient dislikes how it looks. Pearl: Surgery is strictly reserved for painful deformities or footwear intolerance; cosmetic surgery has a high dissatisfaction rate due to complications like stiffness or recurrence.
* Pitfall: Performing a distal osteotomy (Chevron) with a very aggressive lateral release. Increases the risk of 1st metatarsal head Avascular Necrosis (AVN) by destroying the delicate extracapsular arterial supply.
* Trap: Telling patients that silicone toe spacers will correct their deformity. Pearl: Orthoses and toe spacers only provide symptomatic relief by offloading pressure; they do not alter the natural history or correct the bony structural deformity.
* Viva Question: Why must you check the range of motion of the 1st MTPJ prior to deciding on an osteotomy? Answer: If the MTPJ is stiff and crepitant (Hallux Rigidus/OA), an osteotomy will fail to relieve pain, and a 1st MTPJ arthrodesis (fusion) should be performed instead.
