# Orthopaedic Deep Infections (骨科深層感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Septic Arthritis (化膿性關節炎) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** 1° pyogenic infection of joint space → catastrophic articular cartilage destruction within 24 hours via bacterial/leukocyte proteolytic enzymes.
* **Epidemiology:** Bimodal (children <3 yo, elderly >65 yo). Medical emergency.
* **Pathogens:** *Staphylococcus aureus* (most common), *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* (young, sexually active), *Salmonella* (Sickle cell anemia).

### Osteomyelitis (骨髓炎) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Infection of bone and bone marrow space.
* **MOI 1 (Hematogenous):** Bacteremia seeding bone. Most common in children.
* **MOI 2 (Direct Inoculation):** Post-trauma (open #), surgery, or contiguous spread (diabetic foot ulcer).
* **Pathogens:** *S. aureus* (>80%), *Pseudomonas* (puncture wounds through sneakers).

### Spinal Infections (脊柱感染) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Spectrum encompassing pyogenic **Spondylodiscitis (脊椎椎間盤炎)**, epidural abscess, and **Tuberculous Spondylitis / Pott's Disease (結核性脊椎炎)**.
* **Epidemiology:** High incidence of TB in Hong Kong (endemic). Pyogenic often targets lumbar spine; TB targets lower thoracic/thoracolumbar junction.

### Periprosthetic Joint Infection (PJI) (人工關節感染) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Deep infection involving a joint replacement implant and adjacent tissue.
* **MOI:** Intraoperative contamination or delayed hematogenous seeding (e.g., from UTI/dental work).
* **Biofilm:** Bacteria secrete extracellular polymeric substance (glycocalyx) → highly resistant to systemic Abx.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Pediatric Bone Vascularity & Osteomyelitis
* **Metaphyseal Blood Supply:** Nutrient artery forms hairpin loops in metaphysis near physis → sluggish venous flow, lack of phagocytes → ideal environment for bacterial seeding in children.
* **Transphyseal Vessels:** Present in neonates (<18 months) → infection easily crosses physis to joint (osteomyelitis → septic arthritis).

### Synovial Joint Vulnerability
* **Synovial Membrane:** Lacks true limiting basement membrane → highly vascularized → easy entry for blood-borne bacteria.
* **Cartilage Destruction Biomechanics:** Increased intra-articular pressure → compresses retinacular vessels → avascular necrosis of epiphysis (esp. femoral head in pediatric hip).

### Spinal Vascular Anatomy & Infection Spread
* **Batson's Venous Plexus:** Valveless paraspinal venous network → allows retrograde flow of septic emboli from pelvic/abdominal infections to spine.
* **Disc Avascularity:** Adult intervertebral disc is avascular → acts as a sanctuary site. Infection typically starts at endplates (vascularized) → secondary invasion of disc space.

### Biofilm Biomechanics in Implants
* **Glycocalyx Matrix:** Polysaccharide layer protects sessile bacteria from phagocytosis and blocks Abx penetration.
* **Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC):** Planktonic (free-floating) bacteria require normal MIC; sessile (biofilm) bacteria require up to 1000x MIC → systemic Abx ineffective without physical debridement.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Septic Arthritis Physical Assessment
* **Look:** Erythema, swelling, joint effusion. Child holding limb in resting position (max joint capacity: e.g., hip in flexion, abduction, external rotation).
* **Feel:** Hot joint, severe diffuse tenderness.
* **Move:** **Pseudoparalysis** (refusal to move). Agonizing pain with *any* micromotion (pathognomonic).
* **NV:** Usually intact, assess distal pulses.

### Osteomyelitis Physical Assessment
* **Look:** Soft tissue erythema, swelling, potentially chronic discharging **sinus tract (竇道)**.
* **Feel:** Exquisite **point tenderness (局部壓痛)** over affected metaphysis. +ve percussion tenderness.
* **Move:** Adjacent joints may have sympathetic effusion but usually allow some pain-free ROM (unlike septic arthritis).

### Spinal Infections Physical Assessment
* **Look:** Localized kyphotic deformity (Gibbus deformity in TB).
* **Feel:** Paraspinal muscle spasm, spinous process percussion tenderness.
* **Move:** Markedly restricted spinal ROM, splinting posture.
* **NV:** Strict dermatome/myotome/reflex testing. Check perianal sensation and rectal tone (rule out Cauda Equina Syndrome / epidural abscess).

### Periprosthetic Joint Infection (PJI) Assessment
* **Look:** Sinus tract communicating with prosthesis (definitive sign of PJI), wound breakdown.
* **Feel:** Warmth, swelling over replaced joint.
* **Move:** Pain with WB or end-range ROM (often chronic, worsening).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Cierny-Mader Classification for Osteomyelitis
* **Purpose:** Grades osteomyelitis based on anatomy and host physiology.

| Anatomic Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1** | Medullary (endosteal infection only) |
| **Stage 2** | Superficial (surface of bone, deep to soft tissue defect) |
| **Stage 3** | Localized (full-thickness cortical sequestration, stable bone) |
| **Stage 4** | Diffuse (circumferential/through-and-through, mechanically unstable) |

| Physiologic Host | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A Host** | Normal immune/metabolic function |
| **B Host** | Compromised locally (Bs), systemically (Bc), or both (Bsc) |
| **C Host** | Treatment is worse than the disease |

### Waldvogel Classification for Osteomyelitis
* **Purpose:** Based on pathogenesis/chronicity.
* **Types:** 
  * Hematogenous
  * Contiguous focus
  * Vascular insufficiency (e.g., diabetic foot)
  * Chronic

### Tsukayama/Coventry Classification for Periprosthetic Joint Infection
* **Purpose:** Classifies PJI based on timing of onset to guide surgical strategy.

| Type | Timing | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I (Early)** | < 1 month post-op | Wound drainage, erythema, highly virulent |
| **II (Late)** | > 1 month post-op | Chronic low-grade pain, loosening, low virulence |
| **III (Hematogenous)** | Any time | Acute onset of symptoms in previously well-functioning joint |
| **IV (+ve Intra-op Cultures)**| Discovered at revision | Clinically silent, +ve cultures from revision surgery |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Early vs Late Plain Radiographs (XR) in Infections
* **Early (< 10-14 days):** Often completely normal or shows only soft tissue swelling/loss of fat planes. DO NOT rule out infection based on early XR.
* **Late (> 14 days):** Periosteal reaction (new bone formation), osteopenia, focal osteolysis.
* **Osteomyelitis Signs:** **Involucrum (包殼)** (new living bone surrounding dead bone), **Sequestrum (死骨)** (necrotic bone fragment).
* **Spine XR:** Loss of disc height, endplate erosions.

### MRI Findings in Deep Infections
* **Role:** Gold standard (highest sensitivity/specificity).
* **T1 Weighted:** ↓ signal (hypointense) in marrow (edema/exudate replacing fat).
* **T2 Weighted:** ↑ signal (hyperintense) indicating fluid/edema/pus.
* **Contrast (Gadolinium):** Ring enhancement of abscesses (e.g., intraosseous Brodie's abscess, epidural abscess).

### USG & CT Scan Roles
* **Ultrasound:** Excellent for detecting joint effusions (especially deep joints like hip). Guides diagnostic arthrocentesis.
* **CT Scan:** Useful for mapping bony destruction, preoperative planning for sequestrectomy, and assessing spinal stability.

### Nuclear Medicine (Bone Scans)
* **Technetium-99m MDP:** High sensitivity, low specificity (cannot differentiate infection from # or tumor).
* **Indium-111 Labeled WBC Scan:** Higher specificity for acute infection.
* **Role:** Useful if MRI is contraindicated (e.g., pacemaker).

## 6. Initial ED Management (Reduction, Splinting/Casting, Traction)

### Acute Sepsis Protocol
* **Sepsis 6 (within 1 hour):** High-flow O2, take blood cultures, give IV fluids, give IV Abx (AFTER arthrocentesis/cultures if joint involved), measure lactate, measure urine output.

### Diagnostic Arthrocentesis (Joint Aspiration)
* **Rule:** MUST be performed BEFORE administering empiric antibiotics.
* **Procedure:** Strict aseptic technique. Send fluid for: Cell count/differential, Gram stain, Aerobic/Anaerobic Culture & Sensitivity (C&S), Crystals (rule out gout).
* **Septic Synovial Fluid Profile:** Turbid/pus, WBC >50,000/mm³ (>75% PMNs), +ve Gram stain.

### Initial Antibiotic Strategy (Hong Kong HA Context)
* **Do NOT start Abx** for chronic/subacute infections (e.g., spinal, PJI) until tissue biopsies are obtained, unless patient is systemically septic.
* **Septic Arthritis Empiric Abx (Post-aspiration):** IV Cloxacillin + IV Ceftriaxone (covers S. aureus and Gram -ve). Adjust to Vancomycin if MRSA suspected or high local prevalence.

### Acute Immobilization & Pain Control
* **Splinting:** Apply resting splint (e.g., backslab) to immobilize affected limb → ↓ pain, ↓ muscle spasm, prevents contracture.
* **Skin Traction:** May be used in pediatric septic hip to relieve capsular pressure and prevent hip dislocation.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative)

### Septic Arthritis Definitive Management
* **Indication for Surgery:** ALMOST ALWAYS OPERATIVE (Emergency).
* **Operative Technique (Arthrotomy & Washout - 關節切開引流術):** 
  * Open arthrotomy (hip/shoulder) vs. Arthroscopic lavage (knee/shoulder).
  * Copious irrigation (6-9 liters normal saline).
  * Synovectomy if required. Leave drain in situ.
* **Post-op:** Tailored IV Abx for 2 weeks → PO Abx for 4 weeks (total 6 weeks).

### Osteomyelitis Definitive Management
* **Acute Pediatric (Hematogenous):** Often Non-operative (IV Abx) IF caught early (<48h) and no abscess on MRI.
* **Operative Indications:** Failure of Abx after 48h, presence of abscess, chronic osteomyelitis with sequestrum.
* **Operative Technique:** 
  * I&D, cortical windowing (drill holes) to decompress marrow.
  * **Sequestrectomy:** Excision of dead bone.
  * **Dead Space Management:** PMMA Abx-loaded cement beads (local high-dose Abx delivery), muscle flap coverage (rich blood supply promotes healing).

### Spinal Infections Definitive Management
* **Non-Operative:** Mainstay for uncomplicated cases (no neuro deficit, structurally stable). Prolonged Abx/Anti-TB drugs (6-12 months) + rigid bracing (TLSO).
* **Operative Indications:** Progressive neurological deficit, spinal instability/deformity, failure of medical mgmt, large epidural abscess.
* **Operative Technique:** Anterior debridement (corpectomy) + strut graft + posterior instrumented stabilization.

### Periprosthetic Joint Infection (PJI) Algorithm

| Clinical Scenario | Surgical Intervention | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute (<4 weeks) or Acute Hematogenous** | **DAIR** | **D**ebridement, **A**ntibiotics, **I**mplant **R**etention. Exchange modular polyethylene liner. |
| **Chronic (>4 weeks), implant loose** | **2-Stage Revision** (Gold Standard) | Stage 1: Explant components, insert Abx-loaded cement spacer, IV Abx x 6 weeks. <br> Stage 2: Reimplant new prosthesis once infection eradicated. |
| **Healthy host, sensitive bug (Europe/selective cases)** | **1-Stage Revision** | Explant, radical debridement, and reimplant new prosthesis in single setting. |
| **Poor host (Cierny C), intractable infection** | **Salvage Procedures** | Resection arthroplasty (Girdlestone), Arthrodesis, or Amputation. |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Washout Septic Arthritis Rehab
* **Early Mobilization:** Continuous Passive Motion (CPM) machine initiated immediately post-op (day 1) to prevent intra-articular adhesions/stiffness and promote cartilage nutrition.
* **Weight Bearing:** Typically PWB initially, progressing as tolerated based on pain and cartilage status.

### Osteomyelitis & Bone Void Rehab
* **Weight Bearing Restrictions:** Strict NWB if massive sequestrectomy or cortical windowing performed (high risk of pathological #).
* **Flap Protocol:** If muscle flap used for dead space, adhere to strict plastic surgery flap protocols (avoid pressure, optimize perfusion).

### Spinal Infection Rehab
* **Orthosis:** Log-rolling strictly. Mobilize in custom rigid orthosis (e.g., TLSO) to prevent kyphotic collapse during bony healing phase (can take 3-6 months).

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Articular & Skeletal Complications
* **Secondary Osteoarthritis (2° OA):** Rapid chondrolysis in septic arthritis → premature end-stage arthritis.
* **Pathological Fracture:** Weakened bone from osteolytic infection or surgical debridement.
* **Physeal Arrest:** Infection crossing pediatric growth plate → limb length discrepancy or angular deformity.

### Systemic & Chronic Complications
* **Chronic Osteomyelitis:** Intractable infection, lifelong risk of recurrence.
* **Marjolin's Ulcer:** Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) degenerating from the chronic draining sinus tract of long-standing osteomyelitis.
* **Neurological Deficit:** Permanent paraplegia/tetraplegia secondary to epidural abscess or vertebral collapse.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are the 4 parameters of Kocher's Criteria for predicting pediatric septic arthritis of the hip? Answer: 1) Non-weight bearing, 2) ESR >40 mm/hr, 3) Fever >38.5°C, 4) WBC >12,000/mm³. (Probability is 99% if 4/4 are met).
* Trap: Assuming a normal X-ray rules out acute osteomyelitis in a febrile child. Pearl: X-ray lags behind clinical infection by 10-14 days; normal X-ray does NOT rule out early bone infection. Order an MRI or act clinically.
* Pitfall: Giving a dose of IV antibiotics in the ED before drawing synovial fluid from a hot swollen joint. This sterilizes the culture, making targeted therapy impossible; ALWAYS aspirate before giving antibiotics.
* Viva Question: What is the classic MRI sign distinguishing pyogenic spondylodiscitis from a spinal tumor? Answer: Infection characteristically crosses the disc space and involves adjacent vertebral endplates, whereas tumors typically respect the disc boundary and destroy the vertebral body alone.
* Trap: Thinking a superficial swab of a discharging sinus provides the causative organism for osteomyelitis. Pearl: Superficial swabs only grow skin flora colonizers. Deep tissue biopsy or intra-operative sampling is strictly required for accurate culture.
* Pitfall: Delaying arthrotomy in a pediatric septic hip. Cartilage destruction occurs within 24 hours due to proteolytic enzymes. It is a surgical emergency demanding immediate washout.
