# Soft Tissue Infections & Surgical Emergencies (軟組織感染及外科急症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Necrotizing Fasciitis (壞死性筋膜炎) Definition & MOI
* **Definition**: Rapidly progressive, life-threatening bacterial infection of **deep fascia** with 2° necrosis of subcutaneous tissues.
* **MOI/Epidemiology**: Minor trauma, lacerations, insect bites, or post-surgical. High risk in immunocompromised (DM, ESRF, liver cirrhosis). 
* **HK Context**: High incidence of *Vibrio vulnificus* due to handling raw seafood/fish market injuries (街市魚刺傷) or seawater exposure.

### Gas Gangrene (氣性壞疽) Definition & MOI
* **Definition**: Clostridial myonecrosis (muscle necrosis) with local gas production. Highly lethal.
* **MOI/Epidemiology**: Contaminated traumatic wounds (e.g., open # with soil exposure), deep penetrating injuries causing anaerobic environment.
* **Pathogen**: *Clostridium perfringens* (anaerobic, spore-forming, gram +ve bacillus). Alpha-toxin mediates profound tissue destruction.

### Infective Tenosynovitis (感染性腱鞘炎) Definition & MOI
* **Definition**: Closed-space bacterial infection of the **flexor tendon sheath** in the hand.
* **MOI/Epidemiology**: Direct penetrating trauma to palmar aspect of hand/digits (e.g., animal bite, needle prick). 
* **Pathogen**: *Staphylococcus aureus* (most common), *Streptococcus*, *Pasteurella multocida* (cat/dog bites).

### Soft Tissue Abscess (軟組織膿腫) & Paronychia (甲溝炎) Definition
* **Abscess**: Localized collection of pus within dermis/subcutaneous space.
* **Paronychia**: Infection of the soft tissue folds around the nail plate (eponychium/perionychium).
* **MOI**: Nail biting, manicure trauma, hangnails.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Fascial Planes & Infection Spread Anatomy
* **Deep Fascia**: Relatively avascular fibrous layer. Bacteria spread rapidly horizontally along this plane.
* **Skin/Subcutaneous Tissues**: Blood supply via vertical perforating vessels traversing the deep fascia.
* **Pathomechanics**: Fascial necrosis → thrombosis of vertical perforators → superficial skin ischemia & full-thickness necrosis (late sign).

### Hand Flexor Tendon Sheath Anatomy
* **Digital Synovial Sheaths**: Enclose flexor tendons from neck of metacarpals to distal phalanges (Index, Middle, Ring).
* **Radial Bursa**: Extension of flexor pollicis longus (FPL) sheath into wrist/distal forearm.
* **Ulnar Bursa**: Extension of 5th digit (little finger) sheath.
* **Horseshoe Abscess**: Communication between Radial & Ulnar bursae proximal to the flexor retinaculum (Parona's space) in ~80% of patients.

### Fingertip & Nail Anatomy
* **Germinal Matrix**: Proximal portion of nail bed, responsible for nail growth.
* **Sterile Matrix**: Distal portion, for nail adherence.
* **Paronychium**: Lateral soft tissue borders.
* **Eponychium**: Proximal soft tissue border covering the nail base.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Necrotizing Fasciitis (壞死性筋膜炎) Assessment
* **Look**: Rapidly spreading erythema, swelling, violaceous skin discoloration, hemorrhagic bullae/blisters, skin necrosis.
* **Feel**: **Pain out of proportion** to visible skin changes (early), woody induration, subcutaneous crepitus (advanced), loss of sensation over affected skin (nerve destruction).
* **Systemic**: Septic shock (↑ HR, ↓ BP, ↑ RR, altered mental state).

### Gas Gangrene (氣性壞疽) Assessment
* **Look**: Sudden onset profound edema, bronze/brownish skin discoloration, foul-smelling "dishwater" exudate, blebs.
* **Feel**: Exquisite pain, massive soft tissue crepitus (crunching sensation).
* **Systemic**: Rapid toxemia, overwhelming sepsis.

### Kanavel's Signs for Infective Tenosynovitis (感染性腱鞘炎)
* **Look**: 
  * 1. Fusiform (sausage-like) swelling of the entire digit.
  * 2. Digit held in slight flexion (resting posture).
* **Feel**: 
  * 3. Exquisite tenderness localized strictly along the course of the flexor tendon sheath.
* **Move**: 
  * 4. Severe pain on passive extension of the digit (most sensitive early sign).

### Soft Tissue Abscess & Paronychia Assessment
* **Look**: Localized erythema, swelling, visible pus collection.
* **Feel**: Fluctuance (fluid thrill), localized warmth, marked tenderness.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Necrotizing Fasciitis Microbiological Classification
| Type | Characteristics | Common Pathogens |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type I** | Polymicrobial (Aerobic + Anaerobic) | *E. coli, Bacteroides, Klebsiella*. Common in elderly, DM, perineal (Fournier's gangrene). |
| **Type II** | Monomicrobial | *Group A Strep (GAS)*, *MRSA*. Can occur in healthy, young adults (minor trauma). |
| **Type III** | Marine Organisms | *Vibrio vulnificus*. Rapid fulminant course. Strong HK clinical relevance (seafood handlers). |
| **Type IV** | Fungal | *Mucor, Rhizopus*. Rare, severe immunocompromise or severe burns. |

### LRINEC Score (Laboratory Risk Indicator for Necrotizing Fasciitis)
* **Purpose**: Differentiates Necrotizing Fasciitis from severe cellulitis based on admission bloods. Score ≥6 indicates high suspicion (>90% PPV).

| Parameter | Value & Score |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CRP (mg/L)** | <150 (0), ≥150 (4) |
| **WBC (x10^9/L)** | <15 (0), 15-25 (1), >25 (2) |
| **Hb (g/dL)** | >13.5 (0), 11-13.5 (1), <11 (2) |
| **Sodium (mmol/L)** | ≥135 (0), <135 (2) |
| **Creatinine (umol/L)**| ≤120 (0), >120 (2) |
| **Glucose (mmol/L)** | ≤10 (0), >10 (1) |

### Michon Classification for Infective Tenosynovitis
| Stage | Pathophysiology | Fluid Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage I** | Early inflammation | Serous exudate. Tendon viable. |
| **Stage II** | Purulent collection | Purulent fluid. Sheath distended, tendon still viable. |
| **Stage III** | Necrosis | Septic necrosis of tendon, sheath, and pulleys. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Necrotizing Fasciitis & Gas Gangrene Radiology
* **Plain Radiograph (X-ray)**: 
  * Finding: Subcutaneous gas / tracking along fascial planes (highly specific, low sensitivity - absence of gas does NOT rule out condition). 
  * Rules out underlying osteomyelitis or radiopaque foreign bodies.
* **Ultrasound (USG)**: 
  * Finding: Thickened fascial planes with fluid accumulation ("cobblestone" appearance).
* **MRI (Gold Standard but time-consuming)**: 
  * T2/STIR: Fascial edema & hyperintensity. 
  * T1+C (Gadolinium): Non-enhancing fascial planes indicate necrosis.

### Hand Infections Radiology
* **Plain Radiograph (X-ray)**: Essential to rule out foreign bodies (e.g., needles, glass) and underlying osteomyelitis/septic arthritis in tenosynovitis or paronychia.
* **USG**: Useful to differentiate simple cellulitis from a drainable localized abscess/fluid collection in the tendon sheath.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Resuscitation for Necrotizing Sepsis
* **Hemodynamic Support**: Aggressive fluid resuscitation (crystalloids), vasopressors if refractory shock.
* **Urgent Referrals**: Immediate O&T and ICU consultation. "Time is tissue". Do not delay surgery for imaging.
* **Tetanus Prophylaxis**: Administer toxoid/immunoglobulin as per local protocol.

### HK HA Empiric Antibiotic Protocols for Sepsis
* **Necrotizing Fasciitis/Gas Gangrene (IV)**: 
  * Meropenem/Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Broad gram -ve/anaerobe) + Vancomycin (MRSA) + Clindamycin (Inhibits bacterial toxin production in GAS/Clostridium).
  * If marine exposure (*Vibrio*): Add IV Ceftriaxone + Doxycycline.
* **Infective Tenosynovitis/Hand Infections (IV)**: 
  * Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) or Cefazolin. Add Vancomycin if MRSA suspected.

### Acute Splinting & Elevation
* **Splinting**: Plaster of Paris (POP) resting splint in safe position (Wrist 30° ext, MCP 70° flex, IP fully extended) to prevent contractures and minimize pain.
* **Strict Elevation**: High arm sling or Bradford sling to ↓ edema and improve venous return.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Necrotizing Fasciitis & Gas Gangrene Surgical Management
* **Indication**: Absolute emergency. Clinical diagnosis alone is sufficient for surgery.
* **Surgical Technique**: 
  * Radical excisional debridement of all necrotic skin, fat, fascia, and muscle until healthy, bleeding tissue is reached.
  * Fasciotomy to decompress compartments.
  * "Dishwater pus" and lack of fascial resistance ("finger test" - fascia separates easily from underlying muscle) confirms diagnosis intra-op.
* **Algorithm**: 
  * Primary debridement → Leave wound open (pack with saline/betadine gauze) → Mandatory planned re-look debridement in 24-48 hours → Repeat until infection is cleared.
  * **Amputation**: Considered if massive necrosis prevents limb salvage or profound septic shock requires rapid source control.

### Infective Tenosynovitis Surgical Algorithm
* **Non-Operative (Very Early Stage I only)**: IV antibiotics, elevation, splinting. Monitor closely for 12-24 hours. If no improvement → surgery.
* **Operative (Stage II/III or Failed Conservative)**: 
  * **Catheter Irrigation (Early/Stage II)**: Proximal (A1 pulley) and distal (A5 pulley) incisions. Insert infant feeding tube/catheter proximally, continuous irrigation with normal saline for 24-48 hours.
  * **Open Washout & Debridement (Late/Stage III)**: Mid-axial or Bruner zig-zag incisions. Full exposure of flexor sheath. Excision of necrotic tendon if necessary. Leave wound open or loosely closed over a drain.

### Paronychia & Abscess Drainage Techniques
* **Paronychia (Acute)**: 
  * Elevate eponychial fold with a blunt probe/elevator. 
  * If deep/subungual extension: Partial nail plate removal (longitudinal wedge resection) to allow drainage.
* **Soft Tissue Abscess**: 
  * Formal Incision & Drainage (I&D). Break all loculations. Pack wound with alginate/gauze to heal by secondary intention.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Debridement Wound Rehabilitation
* **Wound Coverage**: Once infection clears and granulation tissue forms → Vacuum-Assisted Closure (VAC) therapy, Split-Thickness Skin Graft (STSG), or local/free muscle flaps (Plastic Surgery input).
* **Joint Mobilization**: Early passive/active ROM of adjacent unaffected joints to prevent stiffness.
* **Amputee Protocol**: Stump conditioning, early prosthetic fitting, gait training.

### Post-Tenosynovitis Hand Therapy Protocol
* **Goal**: Prevent severe tendon adhesions and joint stiffness.
* **Timeline**: Remove catheter/drains at 48 hours → immediate active/passive ROM exercises in warm water baths. 
* **Splinting**: Dynamic extension splints to prevent flexion contractures if severe.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Systemic & Limb Complications of Necrotizing Infections
* **Systemic**: Septic shock, Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), Multi-Organ Failure (MOF), Death (Mortality >20-30%).
* **Limb**: Major amputation, chronic osteomyelitis, massive soft tissue defects requiring complex free flaps.

### Functional Complications of Hand Infections
* **Tendon Rupture**: Septic necrosis of flexor tendon.
* **Adhesions**: Scarring of tendon to sheath causing fixed stiffness and loss of ROM.
* **Spread**: Proximal spread to deep fascial spaces of the hand (Parona's space) or systemic bacteremia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most sensitive early physical sign of infective tenosynovitis? Answer: Severe pain on passive extension of the affected digit.
* Trap: Waiting for MRI or blood results (LRINEC score) before taking a patient with suspected necrotizing fasciitis to the operating theatre. Pearl: Necrotizing fasciitis is a clinical diagnosis; definitive management is immediate surgical exploration. Imaging should never delay life-saving surgery.
* Viva Question: What specific antibiotic must be added for a necrotizing infection caused by a fish bone prick in a Hong Kong wet market? Answer: Doxycycline and Ceftriaxone to cover *Vibrio vulnificus*.
* Pitfall: Prescribing only antibiotics for a fluctuant soft tissue abscess. Pus under pressure cannot be adequately treated with antibiotics alone; it mandates surgical Incision & Drainage (I&D) for source control.
* Trap: Assuming the absence of subcutaneous gas on X-ray rules out necrotizing fasciitis. Pearl: Gas is only seen in a minority of cases (typically clostridial or polymicrobial). Its absence does not exclude the diagnosis.
* Viva Question: What intra-operative finding during exploration confirms necrotizing fasciitis? Answer: Positive "finger sweep test" (fascia separates easily from muscle without resistance), lack of tissue bleeding, and gray "dishwater" exudate.
