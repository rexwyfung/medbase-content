# The Limping Child: DDH, Perthes, SCFE (跛行兒童：發育性髖關節發育不良、潘塞氏症、股骨頭骺滑脫)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH) Definition & Epidemiology
* Abnormal development of hip joint → shallow acetabulum, capsular laxity, subluxation, or frank dislocation.
* Epidemiology: ~1/1000 live births (HK). Left hip > Right hip (60%). Bilateral (20%).
* Risk Factors (The 6 Fs): 
  * **Firstborn**
  * **Female** (F:M = 4:1 to 8:1)
  * **Family History**
  * **Frank Breech** presentation
  * **Fluid ↓ (Oligohydramnios)**
  * **Foot Deformities** (e.g., congenital talipes equinovarus)

### Perthes Disease (潘塞氏症) Definition & Epidemiology
* Idiopathic **Avascular Necrosis (AVN) (缺血性壞死)** of the capital femoral epiphysis in a growing child.
* Epidemiology: Peak age 4-8 yrs. M:F = 4:1.
* 10-15% bilateral (classically asymmetrical/sequential onset).
* Associated with delayed skeletal maturity, lower socioeconomic status, passive smoking, ADHD.

### Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE) (股骨頭骺滑脫) Definition & Epidemiology
* Displacement of the proximal femoral epiphysis off the metaphysis through the weakened growth plate (Salter-Harris Type I equivalent).
* Epidemiology: Peak age 10-15 yrs (pubertal growth spurt). M:F = 2:1.
* Phenotype: Overweight/obese adolescent boys.
* 20-40% bilateral.
* Endocrine link: If child <10 yrs or weight <50th percentile, rule out **Hypothyroidism** or **Renal Osteodystrophy**.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Paediatric Hip Vascular Supply Anatomy
* Neonates: Significant supply from metaphyseal vessels crossing physis.
* 4 months to adult: Physis forms barrier. Dominant supply becomes **Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery (MCFA)** → lateral epiphyseal vessels. 
* **Ligamentum Teres** artery (branch of obturator artery): Negligible contribution until age 8; does not prevent AVN.
* SCFE complication: Stretching/tearing of MCFA retinacular branches → high risk of AVN.

### Biomechanics of Acetabular Development in DDH
* Concentric reduction of femoral head provides essential mechanical stimulus for normal acetabular cartilage growth.
* Eccentric loading / subluxation → dysplastic, shallow acetabulum, hypertrophied pulvinar, inverted limbus, and contracted **Psoas** tendon.

### Biomechanics of Physis Failure in SCFE
* Pubertal growth spurt → relative thinning of perichondrial ring & widening of the **Zone of Hypertrophy** in the physis.
* Obesity ↑ axial mechanical shear stress.
* Epiphysis remains anchored in acetabulum (via ligamentum teres). Femoral neck displaces **Anteriorly and Superiorly** relative to the head.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Neonatal DDH Physical Assessment (<6 Months)
* Look: Asymmetrical thigh/gluteal skin folds.
* Feel: Palpate for clunks (not clicks) during provocative maneuvers.
* Move: Limited hip abduction in flexion (<60° is abnormal).
* **Barlow Test** (Provocative): Hip flexed & adducted → posterior force applied → palpable clunk as hip dislocates posteriorly.
* **Ortolani Test** (Reductive): Hip flexed & abducted → anterior lifting force applied → palpable clunk as hip reduces into acetabulum.

### Older Child DDH Physical Assessment (>6 Months)
* Look: Waddling gait (bilateral), apparent short leg.
* Feel: Normal, no acute tenderness.
* Move: Asymmetrical limited hip abduction.
* **Galeazzi Sign**: Supine with hips/knees flexed → unequal knee heights indicating unilateral shortened femur.
* **Trendelenburg Sign**: +ve due to biomechanical disadvantage of hip abductors (**Gluteus Medius**) from shortened lever arm.

### Perthes Disease Physical Assessment
* Look: Antalgic gait, limb shortening, thigh/calf muscle wasting.
* Feel: Mild anterior hip/groin tenderness.
* Move: Painless limp initially. Classical ↓ ROM in **Abduction** and **Internal Rotation (IR)**.
* NV: Intact distal pulses and sensation.

### SCFE Physical Assessment
* Look: Limping, out-toeing gait. Classic affected limb resting posture is shortened and externally rotated.
* Feel: Pain localized to groin, anteromedial thigh, or referred to knee (via **Obturator Nerve**).
* Move: Loss of IR, flexion, and abduction.
* **Drehmann Sign**: Pathognomonic. Obligatory **External Rotation (ER)** of the hip during passive hip flexion.
* NV: Intact. Check for neurological deficits if endocrinopathy suspected.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### DDH Graf Ultrasound Classification System
| Graf Type | Alpha Angle (Bony Roof) | Beta Angle (Cartilage Roof) | Morphological Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type I** | >60° | <55° | Normal mature hip |
| **Type II** | 43°-60° | 55°-77° | Immature / Delayed ossification |
| **Type III** | <43° | >77° | Subluxated, shallow acetabulum |
| **Type IV** | Unmeasurable | Unmeasurable | Dislocated hip |

### Perthes Herring Lateral Pillar Classification System
| Herring Group | Lateral Pillar Height (AP Radiograph) | Prognosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group A** | 100% (No lateral pillar involvement) | Excellent |
| **Group B** | >50% lateral pillar height maintained | Good (age <8), Fair (age >8) |
| **Group C** | <50% lateral pillar height maintained | Poor |

### SCFE Loder Stability Classification System
| Loder Classification | Definition | AVN Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stable** | Patient can bear weight (with or without crutches) | Low (<10%) |
| **Unstable** | Patient CANNOT bear weight even with crutches | High (up to 50%) |

### SCFE Southwick Slip Angle Classification System
| Southwick Grade | Epiphyseal-Diaphyseal Angle Difference (Frog Lateral) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | <30° |
| **Moderate** | 30° - 50° |
| **Severe** | >50° |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### DDH Imaging Modalities & Radiographic Lines
* <6 months: **Ultrasound (US)**. Standard of care (femoral head unossified). Assesses morphology (Graf) and dynamic stability.
* >6 months: **AP Pelvis X-ray**. 
* **Hilgenreiner's Line**: Horizontal line through triradiate cartilages.
* **Perkin's Line**: Vertical line through lateral margin of acetabular roof.
* Normal Head Position: Inner lower quadrant formed by intersecting lines.
* **Shenton's Line**: Continuous arc from medial femoral neck to inferior border of superior pubic ramus. Broken in DDH.
* Acetabular Index: Normal is <25° in children >6 months.

### Perthes Radiographic Waldenström Stages
* Required views: **AP and Frog-leg Lateral Pelvis**.
* Stage 1 (Initial): Increased joint space, medial epiphyseal sclerosis.
* Stage 2 (Fragmentation): Epiphyseal fragmentation, **Crescent Sign** (subchondral radiolucency / microfracture).
* Stage 3 (Reossification): New bone formation, healing of subchondral bone.
* Stage 4 (Remodelling): Residual deformity (coxa magna, coxa plana).

### SCFE Radiographic Signs
* Required views: **AP and Frog-leg Lateral Pelvis** (Must image bilateral hips).
* **Klein's Line**: Drawn along superior border of femoral neck on AP view. Normal = intersects lateral portion of femoral epiphysis. 
* **Trethowan's Sign**: +ve when Klein's line passes completely superior to the epiphysis (indicates slip).
* "Ice cream slipping off cone": Classic appearance on Frog-leg lateral.
* **Blanch Sign of Steel**: Crescent-shaped density at metaphysis (superimposition of displaced head over neck).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute DDH & Perthes ED Management
* Reassurance and education to parents.
* Analgesia (Paracetamol/NSAIDs) if irritable hip symptoms present.
* Strict arrangement for early outpatient Paediatric Orthopaedic clinic follow-up.

### Acute SCFE ED Management
* **ORTHOPAEDIC EMERGENCY** (especially unstable slips).
* Immediate strict **Non-Weight Bearing (NWB)**.
* Strict bed rest in ED / Ward.
* Provide wheelchair. Do NOT allow patient to walk to X-ray or toilet.
* Admit to orthopaedic ward for urgent surgical intervention.
* Baseline bloods (TFTs, Renal panel) if <10 yrs old.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### DDH Definitive Management Algorithm
| Age Group | Definitive Management Indication / Technique |
| :--- | :--- |
| **0-6 Months** | **Non-Operative**: **Pavlik Harness** (maintains hip in flexion & abduction). 90% success rate. Strict weekly US monitoring. |
| **6-18 Months** | **Operative**: **Closed Reduction + Hip Spica Cast**. Arthrogram used intra-op to confirm concentric reduction. If failed → Open Reduction. |
| **>18 Months** | **Operative**: **Open Reduction** ± Femoral shortening osteotomy ± Pelvic osteotomy (Salter/Pemberton) to correct acetabular dysplasia. |

### Perthes Definitive Management Algorithm
| Age & Stage | Definitive Management Indication / Technique |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Age <8 & Herring A/B** | **Non-Operative**: Observation, activity modification, NSAIDs, physiotherapy (maintain ROM). |
| **Age >8 or Herring B/C** | **Operative (Containment)**: Keep femoral head contained within acetabulum during fragmentation/healing. Proximal femoral varus osteotomy or pelvic osteotomy. |
| **Late Deformity (Hinge Abduction)** | **Operative**: Valgus extension osteotomy to redirect deformed head away from acetabular rim. |

### SCFE Definitive Management Algorithm
| Slip Type | Definitive Management Indication / Technique |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stable Slip** | **Operative**: **In Situ Single Screw Fixation**. Center of head, perpendicular to physis. NO attempt at reduction (↑ AVN risk). |
| **Unstable Slip** | **Operative**: Urgent **In Situ Single Screw Fixation** ± capsulotomy to decompress joint hematoma (↓ AVN risk). Incidental gentle reduction may occur with positioning. |
| **Prophylactic Contralateral Pinning** | Indicated in high-risk patients: Endocrinopathy, severe initial slip, poor follow-up compliance, age <10. |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### DDH Post-Operative Rehabilitation
* Spica cast maintained for 6-12 weeks total.
* Changed under GA at 6 weeks for hygiene and re-assessment.
* After cast removal: Abduction brace at night.
* Serial US/X-ray to monitor ongoing acetabular development until skeletal maturity.

### Perthes Rehabilitation
* Focus on maintaining sphericity and ROM.
* Hydrotherapy to encourage active ROM in gravity-reduced environment.
* Avoid high-impact sports (jumping, contact sports) during fragmentation phase.
* Wheelchair use during severe symptomatic periods.

### SCFE Post-Operative Rehabilitation
* Stable slip: Toe-touch weight bearing (TTWB) with crutches for 6 weeks post-pinning, then progress to Full Weight Bearing (FWB).
* Unstable slip: Strict Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) for 6-8 weeks until radiographic consolidation of physis.
* Return to sports: 3-6 months post-op, pending evidence of physeal closure.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### DDH Complications
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN)**: Highest iatrogenic risk if forced extreme abduction in Pavlik harness or cast.
* Residual acetabular dysplasia → Early secondary Osteoarthritis.
* Redislocation / Subluxation.
* Pavlik harness specific: **Femoral Nerve Palsy** (hyperflexion).

### Perthes Complications
* **Coxa Magna**: Enlargement of the femoral head.
* **Coxa Plana**: Flattening of the femoral head.
* **Hinge Abduction**: Extruded lateral head impinges on acetabular rim during abduction, causing lateral joint subluxation and pain.
* Early Osteoarthritis.
* Leg length discrepancy.

### SCFE Complications
* **Chondrolysis**: Acute destruction of articular cartilage causing severe stiffness. Associated with unrecognized intra-articular pin penetration.
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN)**: Devastating complication. Highest risk in unstable slips or if aggressive forced reduction is attempted.
* Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI): Prominent anterior metaphyseal bump (Cam deformity) limits flexion/IR.
* Leg length discrepancy (from premature physeal closure).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate between a stable and unstable SCFE? Answer: A stable SCFE can bear weight (even with crutches), whereas an unstable SCFE cannot bear weight at all.
* Trap: Reassuring a 12-year-old boy with knee pain that it's just a sprain without examining the hip. Pearl: Always examine the hip in any paediatric patient presenting with knee or distal thigh pain to rule out SCFE, as pain is frequently referred via the obturator nerve.
* Pitfall: Attempting forceful closed reduction of a displaced SCFE in the ED or OT. This stretches the retinacular vessels, causing iatrogenic AVN.
* Viva Question: What is the most critical anatomical structure at risk during posterior approach to the pediatric hip? Answer: The medial circumflex femoral artery (MCFA) which supplies the femoral head.
* Trap: Diagnosing a "click" on neonatal hip screening as DDH. Pearl: Benign ligamentous clicks are common and distinct from the pathological "clunk" of an Ortolani/Barlow test which represents actual reduction/dislocation of the femoral head.
* Pitfall: Prolonged extreme abduction in a Pavlik harness or Spica cast. This severely increases the pressure on epiphyseal vessels, leading to iatrogenic AVN of the femoral head.
