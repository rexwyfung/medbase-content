# Common Hand Elective Conditions (常見手部擇期疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Trigger Finger (扳機指) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition**: Stenosing tenosynovitis of flexor tendons (FDS/FDP) at **A1 pulley**.
* **Epidemiology**: F > M (2-6x). Peak age: 5th-6th decade.
* **Risk Factors**: Diabetes Mellitus (DM), Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA), amyloidosis, repetitive gripping.
* **Prognosis**: DM patients have higher recurrence rates & lower response to conservative Tx.

### De Quervain's Tenosynovitis (狄奎凡氏症/媽媽手) Definition & MOI
* **Definition**: Stenosing tenosynovitis of **1st dorsal extensor compartment** of wrist.
* **MOI**: Repetitive thumb abduction/extension combined with wrist ulnar deviation.
* **Epidemiology**: F > M (10x). Common in postpartum women (lifting baby).

### Dupuytren's Contracture (掌腱膜攣縮症) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition**: Benign, progressive fibroproliferative disorder of **palmar aponeurosis** → fixed flexion deformity.
* **Epidemiology**: M > F. Common in Caucasian/Northern European descent.
* **Risk Factors**: Genetics (Autosomal Dominant), ↑ Age, Smoking, Alcoholism, DM, Epilepsy (Phenytoin use).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Trigger Finger Applied Anatomy (A1 Pulley)
* **Pulley System**: Annular (A1-A5) & Cruciate (C1-C3) pulleys hold tendons to phalanges.
* **A1 Pulley**: Located volar to MCP joint. Arises from volar plate & base of proximal phalanx.
* **Biomechanics**: Prevents bowstringing. Thickening of A1 pulley → mismatch between pulley size & tendon volume.

### De Quervain's Applied Anatomy (1st Dorsal Compartment)
* **Contents**: **Abductor Pollicis Longus (APL)** & **Extensor Pollicis Brevis (EPB)**.
* **Location**: Over radial styloid process.
* **Anatomical Variants**: Multiple APL slips common (up to 80%). Separate EPB sub-compartment present in ~40% (causes Tx failure if missed).

### Dupuytren's Contracture Pathoanatomy (Cords & Bands)
* **Normal Anatomy**: Normal fascial structures = **Bands**.
* **Pathology**: Myofibroblast proliferation turns Bands → pathological **Cords**.
* **Pretendinous Cord**: From pretendinous band → causes **MCP joint contracture**.
* **Spiral Cord**: From spiral band, lateral digital sheet, natatory ligament → causes **PIP joint contracture**.
* **NV Bundle Displacement**: Spiral cord displaces digital NV bundle **central & superficial** (highly vulnerable during surgery).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Trigger Finger Clinical Assessment
* **Look**: Digit may rest in flexion.
* **Feel**: Palpable, tender nodule over volar MCP joint (A1 pulley).
* **Move**: Painful clicking, catching, or locking of digit during active flexion/extension.

### De Quervain's Clinical Assessment
* **Look**: Swelling over radial styloid.
* **Feel**: Localized tenderness at 1st dorsal compartment.
* **Move**: Pain elicited by resisted thumb extension/abduction.
* **Special Tests**:
  * **Finkelstein's Test**: Passively stretch thumb into ulnar deviation → severe pain (+ve).
  * **Eichhoff's Test**: Patient clenches thumb in fist, examiner passively ulnar deviates wrist → pain (+ve).

### Dupuytren's Contracture Clinical Assessment
* **Look**: Palmar skin pits, palpable nodules, prominent longitudinal cords. Assess for ectopic disease: **Garrod's pads** (dorsal PIPJ), **Lederhose disease** (plantar fascia), **Peyronie's disease** (penis).
* **Feel**: Firm, non-tender cords. Assess skin adherence.
* **Move**: Fixed flexion deformity (FFD) of MCPJ and/or PIPJ. Unable to actively/passively extend.
* **Special Test**: **Hueston's Tabletop Test**: Patient unable to place palm completely flat on table (+ve).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Quinnell Grading System for Trigger Finger
| Grade | Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **0** | Normal movement |
| **I** | Uneven movement (crepitus), pain, NO locking |
| **II** | Actively correctable locking |
| **III** | Passively correctable locking (requires other hand to unlock) |
| **IV** | Fixed deformity (locked in flexion or extension) |

### Tubiana Staging System for Dupuytren's Contracture
| Stage | Total Flexion Deformity (Sum of MCPJ + PIPJ + DIPJ contractures) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | $0^\circ - 45^\circ$ |
| **II** | $45^\circ - 90^\circ$ |
| **III** | $90^\circ - 135^\circ$ |
| **IV** | $>135^\circ$ |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Hand Elective Conditions Imaging Modalities
* **Diagnosis**: 1° clinical. Imaging used to exclude DDx.
* **Plain Radiographs (AP/Lat Wrist & Hand)**:
  * Exclude **Carpometacarpal (CMC) Osteoarthritis (OA)** of thumb (often co-exists with De Quervain's).
  * Exclude MCPJ OA (DDx for Trigger Finger).
* **Ultrasound (USG)**:
  * **Dynamic USG**: Assesses flexor tendon gliding, thickness of A1 pulley.
  * Detects fluid within 1st extensor compartment (De Quervain's).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Trigger Finger Initial Conservative Management
* **Activity Modification**: Avoid repetitive gripping.
* **Immobilization**: Night splinting of MCPJ in extension.
* **Analgesia**: Oral NSAIDs (short-term symptom relief).

### De Quervain's Initial Conservative Management
* **Immobilization**: **Thumb Spica Splint** (must include wrist and thumb MCP joint).
* **Analgesia**: Oral/Topical NSAIDs.
* **Activity Modification**: Postural advice (esp. during childcare/lifting).

### Dupuytren's Contracture Initial Conservative Management
* **Observation**: For isolated palmar nodules without contracture (-ve Tabletop test).
* **Note**: Stretching exercises/splinting alone are generally ineffective in preventing progression.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Trigger Finger Definitive Management Options
| Modality | Indications | Technique / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Corticosteroid Injection** | 1st-line Tx for Grade I-III | Into tendon sheath (NOT tendon substance). 50-80% success. Less effective in DM patients. Max 2-3 injections. |
| **Open A1 Pulley Release** | Grade IV, failed injection, severe DM | Transverse/oblique incision at distal palmar crease. Divide A1 pulley. Immediate active ROM check intra-op. |
| **Percutaneous Release** | Single digit, distinct nodule | Using 18G needle to divide pulley blind. Higher risk of digital nerve injury (esp. thumb/index). |

### De Quervain's Definitive Management Options
| Modality | Indications | Technique / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Corticosteroid Injection** | 1st-line Tx | Injected into 1st dorsal compartment. ~80% success rate. |
| **Open Surgical Release** | Failed conservative/injections | Longitudinal/transverse incision over radial styloid. **MUST identify and release separate EPB sub-compartment**. Ensure SRN protection. |

### Dupuytren's Contracture Definitive Management Options
| Modality | Indications | Technique / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Surgical Indications** | MCPJ contracture $>30^\circ$, ANY PIPJ contracture, +ve Tabletop test | Shared decision-making on technique based on age/comorbidities. |
| **Needle Aponeurotomy** | MCPJ isolated cord, elderly, unfit for open Sx | Percutaneous division of cord with needle. High recurrence rate. |
| **Open Limited Fasciectomy** | Standard of care in HK HA | Excision of pathological cords only. Z-plasty/Bruner incisions for skin lengthening. |
| **Dermofasciectomy** | Severe recurrence, aggressive young onset | Resection of cord + overlying skin. Requires Full Thickness Skin Graft (FTSG). |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative Hand Rehabilitation Principles
* **Elevation**: Strict elevation in high-arm sling to ↓ edema.
* **Early ROM**: Immediate active ROM (AROM) exercises to prevent tendon adhesions and stiffness.
* **Scar Management**: Deep friction massage, silicone gel sheets (post-suture removal).
* **Splinting (Dupuytren's Specific)**: Night-time static extension splinting for 3-6 months to prevent recurrence of FFD.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Trigger Finger Release Complications
* **Digital Nerve Injury**: Radial digital nerve of thumb & radial digital nerve of index finger are most vulnerable due to superficial course.
* **Bowstringing**: Caused by iatrogenic injury to **A2 pulley**.
* **Stiffness/Adhesions**: 2° to inadequate early ROM.
* **Recurrence**: Usually 2° to incomplete release of proximal A1 pulley.

### De Quervain's Release Complications
* **Superficial Radial Nerve (SRN) Neuroma**: Most devastating complication. Severe neuropathic pain.
* **Tendon Subluxation**: Volar subluxation of tendons with wrist flexion 2° to over-resection/excessive release of the extensor retinaculum.
* **Recurrence**: Due to failure to recognize and decompress a separate EPB sub-compartment.

### Dupuytren's Surgery Complications
* **Neurovascular Injury**: **Spiral cord** pulls NV bundle superficially and centrally. Highest risk at PIPJ level.
* **Hematoma**: Most common early complication. Can → skin flap necrosis.
* **Wound Breakdown / Skin Necrosis**: Due to excessive tension on closure or devascularized flaps.
* **Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS)**: Exaggerated pain, stiffness, autonomic dysfunction.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common reason for failed steroid injection or surgical release in De Quervain's? Answer: Failure to identify and treat a separate septated EPB sub-compartment.
* Viva Question: During Dupuytren's fasciectomy, why is the neurovascular bundle at high risk of iatrogenic injury? Answer: The spiral cord wraps around and displaces the NV bundle centrally and superficially towards the midline of the digit.
* Trap: Releasing the A2 pulley while attempting an A1 pulley release for Trigger Finger. Pearl: Never cut the A2 pulley. Doing so causes flexor tendon bowstringing and profound loss of grip strength.
* Trap: Performing a percutaneous trigger finger release on the thumb. Pearl: The radial digital nerve of the thumb crosses obliquely over the A1 pulley and is at extreme risk during blind percutaneous techniques. Always perform open release for the thumb.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing De Quervain's without checking an X-ray in an older patient. Many older patients have primary CMC joint osteoarthritis which mimics radial sided wrist pain. Perform a grind test and obtain X-rays to differentiate.
* Pitfall: Releasing the 1st dorsal compartment too completely or volar. This leads to volar subluxation of the APL/EPB tendons during wrist flexion.
