# Avascular Necrosis of Femoral Head (股骨頭缺血性壞死)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Avascular Necrosis
* **Definition:** Cellular death of bone marrow & trabecular bone 2° to critical ischemia → microfractures → subchondral collapse → secondary osteoarthritis (繼發性骨關節炎).
* **Pathophysiology:** Ischemia (vascular occlusion/disruption) → osteocyte necrosis → creeping substitution (osteoclastic resorption outpaces osteoblastic bone formation) → structural weakness.

### Etiologies & Risk Factors (PLASTIC RAGS)
* **P**ancreatitis
* **L**upus (SLE)
* **A**lcohol (Common in HK)
* **S**teroids (High-dose/prolonged, e.g., post-SARS/COVID-19 treatment)
* **T**rauma (Neck of Femur Fracture (股骨頸骨折), Hip Dislocation)
* **I**diopathic (Legg-Calvé-Perthes in paeds)
* **C**aisson disease (Decompression sickness)
* **R**adiation / **R**heumatoid Arthritis
* **A**myloidosis
* **G**aucher disease
* **S**ickle cell disease / **S**moking

### Epidemiology of AVN in Hong Kong
* **Age:** 30-50 yrs (Active working population).
* **Gender:** M > F (Alcohol-driven), F > M (SLE/Steroid-driven).
* **Laterality:** Bilateral in ~50-80% of cases.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Vascular Supply of the Femoral Head
* **Extracapsular Arterial Ring:** Formed by **medial circumflex femoral artery (MCFA)** & **lateral circumflex femoral artery (LCFA)**.
* **Intracapsular Ascending Cervical Arteries:** Arise from ring → pierce capsule at base of neck.
* **Lateral Epiphyseal Arteries (Retinacular Vessels):** Terminal branches of MCFA; supply superior & anterior weight-bearing dome. Most critical & most vulnerable to disruption.
* **Artery of Ligamentum Teres:** Branch of **obturator artery**; negligible supply in adults.

### Biomechanics of Femoral Head Collapse
* **Load Distribution:** Anterosuperior quadrant of femoral head transmits peak joint reactive forces (up to 3-4x body weight during gait).
* **Stress Concentration:** Resorption phase of creeping substitution creates local mechanical deficit → trabecular microfractures accumulate at anterosuperior dome → massive macroscopic subchondral collapse.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Look Assessment for Hip AVN
* **Gait:** Antalgic gait, Trendelenburg gait.
* **Muscle:** Wasting of **gluteus maximus**, **gluteus medius**, & **quadriceps** (late stage).
* **Position:** Resting leg in external rotation & slight flexion (maximizes joint volume, ↓ capsular pressure).

### Feel Assessment for Hip AVN
* **Tenderness:** +ve over anterior joint capsule (Scarpa’s triangle / deep groin).
* **Bony Prominence:** Greater trochanter normally non-tender unless concurrent bursitis.

### Move Assessment for Hip AVN
* **Active/Passive ROM:** Painful globally.
* **Loss of Motion:** 1st motion lost = **internal rotation**, followed by **abduction**.
* **Special Test:** +ve Thomas test (fixed flexion deformity in advanced disease).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Ficat & Arlet Classification System
| Stage | Clinical Features | Radiographic (Plain Film) Findings | MRI/Bone Scan Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 0** | Asymptomatic | Normal | Normal MRI, ↓ uptake on Bone Scan |
| **Stage I** | Groin pain | Normal | +ve MRI (edema), ↑ uptake on Bone Scan |
| **Stage II** | Pain, ↓ ROM | Mixed sclerosis & cysts, diffuse osteopenia | +ve MRI (geographic defects) |
| **Stage III** | Sudden ↑ pain | **Crescent sign** (軟骨下新月徵), subchondral collapse | +ve MRI |
| **Stage IV** | Severe constant pain | Flattening of head, joint space narrowing, OA | +ve MRI, Acetabular changes |

### Kerboul Angle (MRI Prognostic Grading)
* **Measurement:** Combined necrotic angle on mid-coronal & mid-sagittal T1 MRI images.
* **< 200°:** Low risk of collapse.
* **> 200°:** High risk of collapse.

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiography (X-Ray) Findings
* **Views:** AP Pelvis, Frog-leg lateral of affected hip.
* **Early (Ficat II):** Patchy osteosclerosis, subchondral cysts.
* **Intermediate (Ficat III):** **Crescent sign** (subchondral radiolucent line indicating impending collapse).
* **Late (Ficat IV):** Femoral head flattening, loss of sphericity, joint space narrowing, osteophytes.

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) Findings
* **Sensitivity/Specificity:** Gold standard (~100% sensitive).
* **T1-Weighted:** Demarcated serpiginous band (low signal intensity) isolating the necrotic segment.
* **T2-Weighted:** **Double-line sign** (雙線徵) - highly specific pathognomonic feature.
    * **Inner Line:** Hyperintense (vascularized granulation tissue).
    * **Outer Line:** Hypointense (sclerotic dead bone).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Symptom Control & Splinting
* **Analgesia:** PO Paracetamol ± weak opioids (Tramadol).
* **Mechanical Unloading:** Issue bilateral axillary crutches or elbow crutches.
* **Weight-Bearing Status:** Strict Non-Weight-Bearing (NWB) or Touch-Weight-Bearing (TWB) to halt mechanical collapse progression.

### Emergency Department Disposition
* **Referral:** Urgent O&T outpatient clinic referral.
* **Admission:** Not indicated unless concurrent acute # (e.g., missed NOF#).

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Management Decision Matrix Table
| Indication | Management Strategy | Surgical Technique / Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asymptomatic / Small Lesion** (<15% head involvement) | Non-Operative | Observation, symptomatic relief |
| **Pre-Collapse (Ficat I-II)** | Joint-Preserving Surgery | Core Decompression (核心減壓術) ± Grafting |
| **Post-Collapse (Ficat III-IV)** | Joint-Replacing Surgery | Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA) |

### Non-Operative Management Details
* **Pharmacological:** Bisphosphonates (Alendronate) to ↓ osteoclast activity (debatable efficacy in HK guidelines but considered). Lipid-lowering statins (preventative for high-dose steroid users).
* **Physical:** Protected weight-bearing (rarely curative, just delays inevitable).

### Joint-Preserving Operative Techniques (Pre-Collapse)
* **Core Decompression (核心減壓術):**
    * **Mechanism:** Drilling a channel from lateral cortex into the necrotic lesion → ↓ intraosseous pressure → relieves pain → promotes neoangiogenesis.
* **Biologic Augmentation:**
    * **Non-Vascularized Bone Graft (NVBG):** Packing cancellous autograft (iliac crest) or allograft into the decompression tract.
    * **Vascularized Fibular Graft (VFG):** Free fibular graft with peroneal artery pedicle anastomosed to LCFA. Technically demanding, ↑ donor site morbidity.
    * **Stem Cells:** Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate (BMAC) injected into necrotic zone.

### Joint-Replacing Operative Techniques (Post-Collapse)
* **Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA) (全髖關節置換術):**
    * **Components:** Replaces both femoral head and acetabulum.
    * **Implants in Young (HK HA protocol):** Uncemented implants, highly cross-linked polyethylene, or ceramic-on-ceramic (CoC) bearing surfaces to maximize longevity and minimize wear debris.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Core Decompression Rehabilitation
* **Weeks 0-6:** Strict NWB with bilateral crutches. Active ROM exercises to prevent stiffness.
* **Weeks 6-12:** Partial Weight-Bearing (PWB) progressing guided by follow-up XR.
* **Months 3+:** Full Weight-Bearing (FWB). Avoid high-impact sports.

### Post-THA Rehabilitation (Posterior Approach)
* **Day 1:** Sit out of bed, commence mobilization FWB with walking frame.
* **Thromboprophylaxis:** LMWH or Rivaroxaban/Apixaban x 28-35 days + TED stockings.
* **Hip Precautions (x 6 weeks):** Avoid flexion > 90°, avoid adduction past midline (use abduction pillow), avoid internal rotation.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Disease-Specific Complications
* **Rapid Progression:** Femoral head collapse can occur within months of onset.
* **Secondary OA:** Irreversible destruction of acetabular cartilage 2° to incongruent femoral head.

### Core Decompression Complications
* **Iatrogenic Fracture:** Subtrochanteric fracture due to stress riser created by the lateral drill entry hole.
* **Failure to Halt Progression:** High rate of eventual collapse requiring THA.

### Total Hip Arthroplasty Complications
* **Aseptic Loosening:** Most common long-term failure mechanism, especially given the young age of AVN patients.
* **Dislocation:** Highest risk in first 6 weeks.
* **Periprosthetic Joint Infection (PJI):** Devastating complication requiring 2-stage revision.
* **Multiple Revisions:** A 30-year-old with THA will likely require 2 to 3 revision surgeries in their lifetime.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the pathognomonic MRI finding for early AVN of the hip? Answer: The "double-line sign" on T2-weighted images, representing inner hypervascular granulation tissue and outer sclerotic bone.
* Viva Question: What is the most critical blood vessel supplying the adult femoral head? Answer: Lateral epiphyseal arteries, which are terminal branches of the medial circumflex femoral artery (MCFA).
* Trap: A patient with severe groin pain and a history of chronic steroid use has a normal pelvic X-ray, ruling out AVN. Pearl: Ficat Stage I AVN has perfectly normal X-rays. You must urgently order an MRI if clinical suspicion is high.
* Pitfall: Recommending a hemiarthroplasty for Ficat Stage III/IV AVN in a young patient. Acetabular cartilage is often diseased from the mis-shapen femoral head; friction will rapidly erode the native acetabulum. Always perform a Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA).
* Pitfall: Discharging a post-SARS patient on long-term steroids presenting with sudden hip pain with simple analgesics. A high index of suspicion for AVN is mandatory in HK HA clinical practice; ensure strict NWB and urgent O&T referral.
