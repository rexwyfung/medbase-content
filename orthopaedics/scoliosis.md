# Scoliosis (脊柱側彎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Scoliosis Clinical Definition
* **3D spinal deformity** characterised by:
	* **Coronal plane:** Curve **>10°** (via Cobb angle).
	* **Axial plane:** Vertebral rotation.
	* **Sagittal plane:** Hypokyphosis/lordosis (loss of normal thoracic kyphosis).

### Etiological Classification of Scoliosis
* **Idiopathic (80%):** Diagnosis of exclusion. 
* **Congenital (10%):** Failure of formation (e.g., hemivertebra) or segmentation (e.g., unsegmented bar).
* **Neuromuscular:** UMN (e.g., Cerebral Palsy) or LMN (e.g., SMA, Polio).
* **Syndromic:** Marfan syndrome, Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1).

### Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (AIS) Epidemiology
* **Prevalence:** 2-3% of adolescents in HK.
* **Age of onset:** 10-18 years.
* **Sex distribution:** 
	* Small curves (10°): **F:M = 1:1**.
	* Severe curves requiring treatment (>30°): **F>>M (10:1)**.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Scoliosis Structural Anatomy
* **Apical Vertebra (頂椎):** Most rotated & laterally deviated vertebra; least tilted.
* **End Vertebrae (端椎):** Most tilted vertebrae at superior & inferior ends of the curve.
* **Pedicles (椎弓根):** Asymmetric in AIS; narrower on the **concave** side (crucial for pedicle screw placement).
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/11e2dd00374f98b4c5175ce23228f103.png)

### Scoliosis Pathobiomechanics (Hueter-Volkmann Principle)
* Altered mechanical loading dictates bone growth:
	* **Concave side:** ↑ Compression → **↓ epiphyseal growth**.
	* **Convex side:** ↑ Tension → **↑ epiphyseal growth**.
	* **Result:** Structural wedging of vertebral bodies and discs.
* **Axial Rotation Biomechanics:** Ribs pushed posteriorly on convex side → **rib hump (肋骨突出)**; ribs pushed anteriorly on concave side → ↓ thoracic volume.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Scoliosis Look (Inspection)
* **General:**
	* Café-au-lait spots/axillary freckling (NF1).
	* Sacral dimple/hairy patch (Spina bifida/spinal dysraphism).
	* Pes cavus.
	* Stigmata of Marfan.
* **Standing from Behind:**
	* Asymmetric shoulder height.
	* Asymmetric scapular prominence (convex side).
	* Waist crease asymmetry / flank asymmetry.
	* Pelvic tilt / apparent leg length discrepancy.
	* Coronal decompensation (plumb line from C7 spinous process does not bisect gluteal cleft).
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/4826cc8289f293ddd7c9ba64e774cd11.png)![image|300](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/f1cb354b6126d34c85dbfac7f3e0fe7f.png)

### Scoliosis Feel (Palpation)
* **Spinous processes:** Trace for coronal deviation.
* **Tenderness:** Evaluate for midline/paraspinal pain (AIS is typically **painless**; +ve pain suggests osteoid osteoma, infection, or syrinx).

### Scoliosis Move (Dynamic Assessment)
* **Adam's Forward Bend Test (亞當式前彎測試):** Pathognomonic screening tool.
	* Knees straight, feet together, bend forward 90° at waist.
	* Identifies **rotational deformity** (structural vs. postural).
* **Scoliometer measurement:** Angle of Trunk Rotation (ATR). **ATR >7°** warrants radiological workup.
	* Assess overall spinal ROM & hamstring tightness.
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/5498d5044abbef745249b0a2ae5de6bd.png)

### Scoliosis Neurovascular (NV) Status
* **Motor & Sensory:** Screen upper/lower limbs.
* **Reflexes:** Deep tendon reflexes, Babinski sign, Clonus (rule out UMN lesions).
* **Abdominal Reflexes:** Asymmetric/absent reflexes are a red flag for **Syringomyelia (脊髓空洞症)** or **Arnold-Chiari malformation**.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Lenke Classification System for AIS
* Used to define curve types and guide surgical fusion levels.

| Component                         | Description                                                               | Grading / Types                                                                                                                                                                      |
| :-------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Curve Type**                 | Based on major/minor structural curves.                                   | **1:** Main Thoracic (MT)<br>**2:** Double Thoracic (DT)<br>**3:** Double Major (DM)<br>**4:** Triple Major (TM)<br>**5:** Thoracolumbar/Lumbar (TL/L)<br>**6:** TL/L-MT (TL/L > MT) |
| **2. Lumbar Spine Modifier**      | Position of Center Sacral Vertical Line (CSVL) to apical lumbar vertebra. | **A:** CSVL between pedicles.<br>**B:** CSVL touches apical pedicle.<br>**C:** CSVL medial to apical pedicle.                                                                        |
| **3. Sagittal Thoracic Modifier** | Measured from T5-T12 on lateral radiograph.                               | **- (Hypo):** <10°<br>**N (Normal):** 10-40°<br>**+ (Hyper):** >40°                                                                                                                  |

### Risser Grading System for Skeletal Maturity
* Assesses ossification of the **iliac apophysis** (starts ASIS → PSIS).

| Grade | Ossification Excursion | Maturity Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | No ossification | Highly immature (High risk of progression) |
| **1** | 25% ossification | Immature |
| **2** | 50% ossification | Immature |
| **3** | 75% ossification | Maturing |
| **4** | 100% ossification (unfused) | Nearing cessation of growth |
| **5** | Fused to ilium | Skeletally mature |

![image|513](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/5ef309bc777545386c31112f6a9022b7.png)

### Triradiate Cartilage Status
* **Open:** Peak Height Velocity (PHV) not yet reached.
* **Closed:** Corresponds to Risser 1.

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Initial Scoliosis Radiographs
* **Standing Whole Spine PA & Lateral (站立位全脊柱X光):**
	* **PA view:** Preferred over AP in HKHA to **↓ breast/thyroid radiation** in young females.
* **EOS Imaging System:** Ultra-low dose biplanar X-ray (standard in modern HKHA centres).
* **Side-bending films:** Assess curve flexibility (determines structural vs. non-structural curves in Lenke classification).

### Cobb Angle (柯布氏角) Measurement
* **Step 1:** Identify superior end vertebra (most tilted superiorly).
* **Step 2:** Identify inferior end vertebra (most tilted inferiorly).
* **Step 3:** Draw line along superior endplate of upper vertebra.
* **Step 4:** Draw line along inferior endplate of lower vertebra.
* **Step 5:** Angle between perpendiculars drawn to these lines = **Cobb Angle**.
![image|212](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/63b6bd9881922f1852303d1ee409dbd7.png)

### MRI Whole Spine Indications (Red Flags)
* Mandatory if suspecting non-idiopathic etiology:
	* Atypical curve pattern (e.g., **Left-sided thoracic curve**).
	* Early onset (**<10 years old**).
	* Rapid progression (>1° per month / >5° between visits).
	* Painful scoliosis.
	* Abnormal neurology (e.g., asymmetric abdominal reflexes, pes cavus).

## 6. Initial ED Management (Triage & Urgent Interventions)

### Acute Presentations & Triage Protocols
* *Note: AIS is rarely an ED emergency. ED management applies to acute complications or alternative severe diagnoses.*
* **Acute Neurological Deficit:** Suspect compressive pathology (tumour, infection, acute syrinx bleed) → Urgent whole spine MRI + Neurosurgery/Spine team consult.
* **Acute Severe Back Pain:** Suspect osteoid osteoma, spondylolysis, or diskitis → Prescribe NSAIDs + refer to Ortho SOPD.
* **School Screening Referrals (Student Health Service):** Asymptomatic +ve Adam's test (ATR >7°) → Reassure parents, routine Ortho SOPD referral. No ED intervention required.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Non-Operative vs. Operative)

### AIS Management Decision Matrix
* Dictated by **Cobb angle** and **Remaining growth (Risser/Menarche status)**.

| Cobb Angle | Skeletal Status (Risser) | Definitive Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **<25°** | Any Risser (0-5) | **Observation** (Serial clinical + XR every 4-6 months). |
| **25°-45°** | Risser 0-2 (Immature) | **Rigid Bracing** (e.g., Boston brace). |
| **25°-45°** | Risser 4-5 (Mature) | **Observation** (Discontinue brace, growth ceased). |
| **>45°-50°** | Any Risser | **Operative Intervention** (PSF). |

### Non-Operative: Rigid Bracing (支架)
* **Goal:** Halt curve progression (Does NOT correct existing deformity).
* **Mechanism:** 3-point pressure system applied to the apex of the curve.
* **Types:**
	* **Boston Brace (TLSO):** Underarm brace for thoracolumbar/lumbar curves (worn 18-23 hrs/day).
	* **Providence Brace:** Night-time only hyper-corrective brace.
	* **Milwaukee Brace (CTLSO):** For high thoracic curves (apex >T8) - rarely used now due to poor compliance.

### Operative: Posterior Spinal Fusion (PSF) (後路脊柱融合術)
* **Indications:** Cobb >45°-50°, severe truncal shift, unresponsive to bracing.
* **Goal:** Prevent further progression, achieve safe 3D correction, balance spine.
* **Technique:**
	* Posterior midline approach.
	* Facetectomies (release & prepare for fusion).
* **Pedicle screw** insertion (Freehand vs. Navigation/Robotic-assisted).
* **Rod derotation:** Translates coronal deformity into normal sagittal kyphosis/lordosis.
	* Decortication and bone grafting (autograft/allograft) for solid arthrodesis.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Conservative Bracing Rehabilitation
* **Wear-time Protocol:** Efficacy is dose-dependent. Minimum **18 hours/day** (ideal 23 hours).
* **Physiotherapy:** Physiotherapy Scoliosis-Specific Exercises (PSSE), e.g., Schroth method (core strengthening, breathing mechanics).
* **Weaning:** Gradual weaning once skeletally mature (Risser 4/5 or 2 years post-menarche).

### Post-Operative PSF Protocol (HKHA Standard)
* **Day 0:** Bed rest. Monitor lower limb neurology hourly. Check wound drain.
* **Day 1-2:** Sit out of bed, transition to oral analgesia. Check erect XR.
* **Day 2-3:** Mobilise with physiotherapist (walking aids if needed).
* **Precautions (First 3-6 months):**
	* **NO** BLT (Bending, Lifting >5lbs, Twisting).
	* Log-rolling strictly in bed.
	* Return to non-contact sports at 6 months; contact sports at 12 months (surgeon dependent).

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Natural History Complications (Untreated Severe AIS)
* **Restrictive Lung Disease:** Occurs if Thoracic Cobb **>80°** (↓ Vital Capacity).
* **Cor Pulmonale:** Occurs if Thoracic Cobb **>100°** (Right heart failure due to pulmonary hypertension).
* **Chronic Back Pain:** Higher incidence of degenerative disc disease in adulthood.

### Operative Complications (PSF)
* **Neurological Injury (<1%):** Spinal cord traction/compression during correction. Prevented by **Intraoperative Neuromonitoring (SSEP + MEP)**.
* **Crankshaft Phenomenon:** Continued anterior spinal growth despite a solid posterior fusion in very young patients (Risser 0, open triradiate) → progressive rotational deformity.
* **Infection (1-2%):** Delayed P. acnes (Cutibacterium acnes) is common in posterior spine implants.
* **Pseudoarthrosis:** Failure of fusion leading to rod breakage/implant failure.
* **Adjacent Segment Disease:** Accelerated degeneration above/below the fusion construct.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important prognostic factor for curve progression in AIS? Answer: Remaining skeletal growth (Risser grade and age at diagnosis) and the magnitude of the curve at presentation.
* Trap: Assuming all thoracic scoliosis curves are right-sided. Pearl: A left-sided thoracic curve is a major red flag for underlying spinal cord pathology (e.g., syrinx, tumour) and mandates a whole spine MRI.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing AIS purely on physical examination. The Adam's forward bend test detects rotational deformity, but the definitive diagnosis and Cobb angle measurement require standing AP/PA and lateral whole-spine X-rays.
* Viva Question: During a posterior spinal fusion, the MEP (Motor Evoked Potentials) signal suddenly drops. What is the immediate sequence of actions? Answer: Inform surgeon immediately, raise MAP >90mmHg, check hemoglobin, reverse curve correction/remove rods, and consider a wake-up test if signals do not return.
* Trap: Counseling parents that a Boston brace will straighten their child's spine. Pearl: Always explicitly state that bracing prevents progression but does not correct the existing deformity.
