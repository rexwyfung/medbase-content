# Common Paediatric Orthopaedic Conditions (常見小兒骨科疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition**: Spectrum of hip instability ranging from shallow acetabulum (dysplasia) to complete dislocation of **Femoral Head (股骨頭)**.
* **Epidemiology**: 
  * Incidence: 1-1.5 per 1000 live births (HK HA data)
  * Left hip > Right hip (due to intrauterine positioning)
* **Risk Factors (4Fs + 1O)**:
  * Female sex (↑ relaxin sensitivity)
  * Firstborn (primigravida → tighter uterus)
  * Feet first (Breech presentation)
  * Family history (+ve)
  * Oligohydramnios (packaging disorder)

### Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition**: Posterior & inferior slippage of proximal **Femoral Epiphysis (股骨骨骺)** through the hypertrophic zone of the physis (**Salter-Harris Type I #** equivalent).
* **Epidemiology**:
  * Demographics: Obese adolescent boys (10-14 years)
  * Bilateral in 20-40% cases
* **Endocrine Associations** (Suspect if age <10 or >16, or slim habitus):
  * Hypothyroidism
  * Growth Hormone (GH) deficiency
  * Renal osteodystrophy

### Legg-Calvé-Perthes Disease (LCPD) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition**: Idiopathic **Avascular Necrosis (AVN) (缺血性壞死)** of the immature femoral head.
* **Epidemiology**:
  * Demographics: Boys aged 4-8 years
  * Male:Female ratio = 4:1
  * Bilateral in 10-15% (asynchronous presentation)
* **Associated Factors**:
  * Hyperactivity (ADHD)
  * Delayed skeletal maturity
  * Passive smoking exposure

### Congenital Talipes Equinovarus (CTEV) Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition**: Complex congenital foot deformity characterized by 3D malalignment of the talocalcaneonavicular complex.
* **Epidemiology**:
  * Incidence: 1 per 1000 live births
  * Male:Female ratio = 2:1
  * Bilateral in 50%
* **Associations**:
  * Idiopathic (most common)
  * Spina Bifida
  * Arthrogryposis


## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### DDH Pathoanatomy & Deforming Blocks
* **Primary Defect**: Failure of normal contact between femoral head and acetabulum during development.
* **Blocks to Reduction** (Anatomical obstacles):
  * Extra-articular:
    * Contracted **Iliopsoas tendon (髂腰肌腱)**
    * Contracted **Adductor longus (長收肌)**
  * Intra-articular:
    * Inverted **Acetabular Labrum / Limbus (髖臼唇)**
    * Hypertrophied **Ligamentum Teres (圓韌帶)**
    * Hypertrophied pulvinar (fibrofatty pad)
    * Constricted joint capsule (hourglass configuration)

### SCFE Anatomy & Biomechanics
* **Anatomical Weak Point**: Hypertrophic zone of physis (widened & weakened).
* **Biomechanical Forces**: ↑ Shear stress across physis (due to ↑ BMI & relative retroversion of femoral neck).
* **Direction of Slip**: 
  * Epiphysis stays in acetabulum
  * Metaphysis displaces Anteriorly & Superiorly
  * Relative position: Epiphysis slips Posteriorly & Inferiorly

### LCPD Vascular Anatomy & Pathogenesis
* **Vascular Supply to Immature Femoral Head**: 
  * Primarily relies on **Lateral Epiphyseal Artery (外側骨骺動脈)** (branch of Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery - MCFA).
  * Ligamentum teres vessel is non-functional at this age.
* **Pathological Stages** (Waldenström):
  * Infarction (ischemia)
  * Fragmentation (resorption of dead bone)
  * Reossification (new bone formation)
  * Remodeling (healed phase)

### CTEV Pathoanatomy (CAVE Deformity)
* **Medial Spin**: The calcaneonavicular complex rotates medially around the talus.
* **Deforming Muscle Forces**:
  * Contracted **Tibialis Posterior (脛後肌)** (Navicular subluxation)
  * Contracted **Achilles Tendon (跟腱)** (Equinus)
  * Contracted **Plantar Fascia (足底筋膜)** (Cavus)
* **Bone Deformity**: Talus neck deviates medially & plantarly.


## 3. Clinical Assessment

### DDH Neonatal Clinical Assessment (<6 Months)
* **Look**:
  * Asymmetric thigh/gluteal skin folds
  * Leg length discrepancy (LLD)
* **Feel & Move**:
  * **Barlow Maneuver (巴洛氏測試)**:
    * Adduct hip + Posterior force
    * +ve sign = Clunk of DISLOCATION
  * **Ortolani Maneuver (奧特蘭尼測試)**:
    * Abduct hip + Anterior elevation
    * +ve sign = Clunk of REDUCTION
  * ↓ Hip Abduction (<60° in flexion is abnormal)

### DDH Infant/Toddler Clinical Assessment (>6 Months)
* **Look**:
  * Shortened leg on affected side
  * Limping/waddling gait (if walking)
  * Hyperlordosis (in bilateral DDH)
* **Feel & Move**:
  * **Galeazzi Sign (蓋氏徵)**:
    * Hips/knees flexed at 90°
    * +ve sign = Unequal knee heights (shorter on affected side)
  * **Trendelenburg Sign (川德倫堡徵)**:
    * +ve sign = Pelvis drops on normal side when standing on affected leg (due to weak abductors from altered mechanics)

### SCFE Clinical Assessment
* **Look**:
  * Obese body habitus
  * Limp (antalgic gait)
  * Leg held in external rotation
* **Feel**:
  * Tenderness over anterior hip joint capsule
* **Move**:
  * **Drehmann Sign (德雷曼徵)**:
    * Passive flexion of hip
    * +ve sign = Obligatory External Rotation & Abduction of hip
  * ↓ Internal Rotation
  * ↓ Flexion
  * ↓ Abduction

### LCPD Clinical Assessment
* **Look**:
  * Mild thigh/calf atrophy
  * Limp (often painless initially, worsens with activity)
* **Feel**:
  * Minimal local tenderness
* **Move**:
  * Restricted Internal Rotation (in extension)
  * Restricted Abduction
  * Hip flexion contracture (Thomas test +ve)
* **Neurological**: Normal. (Always examine knee to rule out referred pain via **Obturator Nerve (閉孔神經)**).

### CTEV Clinical Assessment (CAVE)
* **Look** (The CAVE Deformities):
  * **C**avus (High arch of midfoot)
  * **A**dductus (Medial deviation of forefoot)
  * **V**arus (Inversion of hindfoot)
  * **E**quinus (Plantarflexion of ankle)
  * Deep medial/posterior skin creases
  * Empty heel pad
* **Feel**:
  * Tight Achilles tendon
  * Prominent lateral head of talus
* **Move**:
  * Rigid deformities (cannot be passively corrected to neutral)


## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### DDH Tönnis Classification (Radiographic)
| Grade | Position of Ossific Nucleus relative to Perkin's Line / Hilgenreiner's Line |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Medial to Perkin's, Inferior to Hilgenreiner's (Normal) |
| **II** | Lateral to Perkin's, Inferior to Hilgenreiner's (Subluxated) |
| **III** | Lateral to Perkin's, level with Hilgenreiner's (Dislocated) |
| **IV** | Lateral to Perkin's, Superior to Hilgenreiner's (High Dislocation) |

### SCFE Loder Classification (Clinical Stability)
| Classification | Weight-Bearing Status | Prognosis (Risk of AVN) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stable** | Able to bear weight (with or without crutches) | < 10% AVN risk |
| **Unstable** | UNABLE to bear weight (even with crutches) | > 40-50% AVN risk |

### SCFE Southwick Angle Classification (Radiographic Severity)
| Severity | Slip Angle (Difference between affected vs normal side) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | < 30° |
| **Moderate** | 30° - 50° |
| **Severe** | > 50° |

### LCPD Lateral Pillar (Herring) Classification
| Group | Height of Lateral Pillar of Femoral Head (on AP X-ray in fragmentation stage) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | 100% (No lateral pillar involvement) |
| **B** | > 50% lateral pillar height maintained |
| **B/C** | 50% lateral pillar height maintained (borderline) |
| **C** | < 50% lateral pillar height maintained |

### CTEV Pirani Scoring System
| Component | Score (0 = Normal, 0.5 = Mild, 1 = Severe) | Max Score |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Midfoot Score (MA)** | Medial crease, Curved lateral border, Lateral head of talus coverage | 3 |
| **Hindfoot Score (HA)** | Posterior crease, Empty heel, Rigid equinus | 3 |
| **Total** | Combines MA + HA | 6 |


## 5. Radiological Investigations

### DDH Ultrasound Guidelines (<6 Months)
* **Views**:
  * Coronal View
  * Transverse View
* **Graf Method (Coronal parameters)**:
  * **Alpha (α) angle**: Measures bony roof acetabular concavity (Normal > 60°).
  * **Beta (β) angle**: Measures cartilaginous roof (Normal < 55°).

### DDH X-Ray Parameters (>6 Months)
* **Views**:
  * AP Pelvis
* **Lines & Measurements**:
  * **Hilgenreiner's Line (H-line)**: Horizontal line through triradiate cartilages.
  * **Perkin's Line (P-line)**: Vertical line dropped from lateral margin of acetabulum.
  * **Normal Position**: Femoral head must be in the **Inferomedial Quadrant (下內側象限)**.
  * **Shenton's Line**: Continuous arc from medial femoral neck to inferior border of superior pubic ramus. (Broken = dislocation/subluxation).
  * **Acetabular Index**: Angle between H-line and acetabular roof. (Normal < 25° after 6 months; ↑ in dysplasia).

### SCFE X-Ray Findings
* **Views**:
  * AP Pelvis
  * Frog-leg Lateral Pelvis (Best for identifying mild slips)
* **Findings**:
  * **Trethowan's Sign**: **Klein's Line (克萊恩線)** (drawn along superior neck) fails to intersect lateral portion of the femoral epiphysis.
  * **Blanch Sign of Steel**: Double density created by overlapping of posteriorly displaced epiphysis and metaphysis.
  * Widened and irregular physis.

### LCPD X-Ray & MRI Findings
* **X-Ray Views**:
  * AP Pelvis
  * Frog-leg Lateral Pelvis
* **X-Ray Findings (Stage dependent)**:
  * Early: Sclerosis, widening of medial joint space, **Crescent Sign (新月徵)** (subchondral #).
  * Late: Fragmentation, **Coxa Magna (大股骨頭)**, Coxa Plana (flat head).
* **MRI Indications**:
  * Early diagnosis before X-ray changes
  * Assessing extent of necrosis

### CTEV Radiological Assessment
* **Note**: Diagnosis is primarily clinical. X-rays reserved for atypical cases or assessing surgical correction.
* **Views**:
  * AP Foot (Weight-bearing or simulated)
  * Lateral Foot (Maximal dorsiflexion)
* **Findings**:
  * Parallelism between talus and calcaneus (Normal Kite's angle is 20-40°; in CTEV it approaches 0°).


## 6. Initial ED Management

### DDH Initial ED/Clinic Management
* **Immediate Actions**:
  * Reassure parents (do NOT attempt forceful reduction).
  * Urgent referral to Paediatric Orthopaedic clinic.
* **Advice**:
  * AVOID tight swaddling with hips extended (promotes dislocation).
  * Encourage hip flexion/abduction postures.

### SCFE Initial ED Management
* **Immediate Actions**:
  * **Strict NON-WEIGHT BEARING (NWB)** immediately.
  * Provide wheelchair.
* **Medical**:
  * Analgesia.
  * Urgent orthopaedic admission for operative planning (to prevent progression from stable to unstable).

### LCPD Initial ED Management
* **Immediate Actions**:
  * Rest and restriction of high-impact activities.
  * Provision of crutches for symptom relief.
* **Medical**:
  * NSAIDs for pain and synovitis.
  * Outpatient Paediatric Orthopaedic referral.

### CTEV Initial ED/Maternity Ward Management
* **Immediate Actions**:
  * Reassure parents (highly treatable).
  * Urgent referral to specialized Ponseti clinic (ideal casting initiation within 1-2 weeks of life).
* **Intervention**: No acute splinting required in ED.


## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### DDH Definitive Management Algorithm
| Age Group | Primary Intervention | Rationale & Mechanics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0 - 6 Months** | **Pavlik Harness (帕夫利克吊帶)** | Dynamic splint; maintains flexion >90° & limits adduction. Relies on spontaneous reduction via muscle tension. |
| **6 - 18 Months** | **Closed Reduction + Hip Spica Cast** | Exam under anaesthesia (EUA). Arthrogram confirms safe zone of reduction. Cast limits movement for capsular remodeling. |
| **> 18 Months** | **Open Reduction +/- Osteotomy** | Direct clearing of intra-articular blocks (ligamentum teres, labrum). Often combined with Pelvic (Salter) or Femoral Varus osteotomy to stabilize joint. |

### SCFE Definitive Management Algorithm
| Stability / Severity | Operative Technique | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stable (Mild/Mod)** | **In situ single screw fixation (原位單螺釘固定術)** | 1 cannulated screw placed centrally to halt progression. DO NOT attempt reduction (↑ AVN risk). |
| **Unstable** | **Urgent in situ fixation +/- capsulotomy** | Decompression of joint hematoma (capsulotomy) to ↓ intracapsular pressure and ↓ AVN risk. |
| **Severe Deformity (Late)** | **Proximal Femoral Osteotomy (Imhäuser)** | Realigns mechanical axis to prevent impingement and early OA after fusion occurs. |

### LCPD Definitive Management Algorithm
| Patient Profile | Treatment Strategy | Specific Intervention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Age < 8, Herring A/B** | **Non-Operative (Observation)** | Activity modification. NSAIDs. ROM exercises to maintain sphericity ("biological plasticity"). |
| **Age > 8, Herring B/B-C** | **Operative (Surgical Containment)** | **Femoral Varus Osteotomy (股骨內翻截骨術)** OR **Pelvic Osteotomy**. Goals: Center the healing head deep within the acetabulum to act as a mold. |
| **Herring C (Severe)** | **Salvage / Supportive** | Valgus osteotomy (if hinge abduction present) to relieve pain. |

### CTEV Definitive Management Protocol (Ponseti Method)
* **Step 1: Serial Casting (Ponseti Casts)**
  * Weekly manipulation and Long Leg Cast application.
  * Sequence of correction (CAV):
    * Elevate 1st metatarsal ray to correct **C**avus.
    * Abduct forefoot around intact talus to correct **A**dductus & **V**arus.
* **Step 2: Percutaneous Achilles Tenotomy (跟腱切斷術)**
  * Indicated for residual **E**quinus (required in >80% cases).
  * Followed by final cast for 3 weeks.
* **Step 3: Maintenance**
  * Foot Abduction Orthosis (Denis Browne splint) to prevent relapse.


## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### DDH Post-Op / Spica Rehabilitation
* **Spica Care**: 
  * Keep cast dry and clean (diapering techniques).
  * Monitor for neurovascular compromise or pressure sores.
* **Post-Spica**:
  * Abduction bracing (e.g., Rhino cruiser) at night.
  * Serial clinical exam and X-rays until skeletal maturity (monitor for residual dysplasia).

### SCFE Post-Op Rehabilitation
* **Weight-Bearing Protocol (HK HA Preference)**:
  * Stable slips: Touch-down weight bearing with crutches for 6 weeks, progressing to full weight bearing.
  * Unstable slips: Strict NWB for 6-8 weeks until early consolidation is seen on X-ray.
* **Physiotherapy**: Focus on restoring hip flexion and internal rotation.

### LCPD Rehabilitation & Physiotherapy
* **Goal**: Prevent hinge abduction (lateral extrusion).
* **Exercises**:
  * Aggressive passive/active ROM exercises (specifically abduction and internal rotation).
  * Hydrotherapy / Non-weight bearing sports (swimming, cycling).

### CTEV Bracing Protocol
* **Denis Browne Splint (丹尼斯布朗夾板)**:
  * Boots attached to a bar (70° external rotation for affected foot, 40° for normal foot).
  * **Duration Schedule**:
    * 23 hours/day for the first 3 months post-tenotomy.
    * 12-14 hours/day (nighttime/naps) until age 4-5 years.
  * *Strict compliance is mandatory to prevent relapse.*


## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### DDH Complications
* **Iatrogenic AVN of Femoral Head**: Caused by extreme abduction in Pavlik/Spica (obliterates blood supply).
* **Residual Dysplasia**: Leading to early-onset osteoarthritis.
* **Redislocation**: Failure of conservative or operative management.

### SCFE Complications
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN)**: Up to 50% in unstable slips. Often related to vascular kinking or forceful reduction attempts.
* **Chondrolysis (軟骨溶解)**: Rapid destruction of articular cartilage causing severe stiffness. Associated with pin penetration into the joint space.
* **Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI)**: Cam-type impingement due to residual anterior metaphyseal prominence.

### LCPD Complications
* **Coxa Magna**: Enlarged, non-spherical femoral head.
* **Coxa Brevis**: Shortened femoral neck (premature physeal closure) leading to leg length discrepancy.
* **Premature Osteoarthritis**: Directly proportional to loss of congruency and patient age at onset.
* **Hinge Abduction**: Lateral extruded head impinges against acetabular rim during abduction, causing severe pain and preventing ROM.

### CTEV Complications
* **Relapse**: Highly correlated with non-compliance to bracing protocol.
* **Rocker-Bottom Foot**: Iatrogenic complication from forceful dorsiflexion against a tight Achilles without properly correcting midfoot first.
* **Overcorrection**: Valgus deformity.
* **Calf Atrophy**: Permanent but functional ↓ in calf circumference on the affected side (expected anatomical outcome, not necessarily iatrogenic).


## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate between DDH and a congenital short femur in a neonate? Answer: Check the Galeazzi sign; both have unequal knee heights, but in DDH, the Ortolani/Barlow tests will be positive and ultrasound will show dysplasia, whereas congenital short femur has a stable hip joint.
* Viva Question: What is the most devastating complication of forceful reduction in a SCFE? Answer: Iatrogenic Avascular Necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head due to disruption of the retinacular vessels.
* Trap: Recommending double-diapering as a treatment for mild DDH. Pearl: Double-diapering is ineffective and obsolete; refer for Pavlik harness or observation based on ultrasound Graf grading.
* Trap: Relying solely on AP X-rays to rule out a SCFE in an obese teenager with knee pain. Pearl: Always obtain a Frog-leg Lateral X-ray; mild posterior slips are easily missed on AP views, and knee pain is a classic referred symptom via the obturator nerve.
* Pitfall: Attempting to correct the CTEV equinus deformity before correcting the cavus, adductus, and varus. This causes a midfoot break and a severe iatrogenic "rocker-bottom" foot deformity.
* Pitfall: Over-tightening the Pavlik harness in extreme abduction (>60 degrees). This occludes the medial circumflex femoral artery branches, leading to catastrophic iatrogenic AVN.
* Viva Question: Why do we observe a child with Perthes disease if they are under 6 years old but operate if they are over 8? Answer: Younger children have immense biological plasticity and a longer time for the femoral head to remodel spontaneously; older children lack this remodeling potential, leading to early osteoarthritis without surgical containment.
