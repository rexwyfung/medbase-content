# Osteoporotic Related Fractures (骨質疏鬆性骨折)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Osteoporosis (骨質疏鬆症)
* **Systemic skeletal disease** characterized by ↓ bone mass & microarchitectural deterioration
* → ↑ bone fragility & ↑ susceptibility to fracture
* **Fragility Fracture**: Occurs spontaneously or from mechanical forces that would not ordinarily result in # (e.g., fall from standing height or less)

### Mechanism of Injury (MOI) for Fragility Fractures
* **Low-energy trauma**: Simple fall from standing height
* **Spontaneous**: Trivial activities (e.g., coughing, bending → Vertebral Compression #)
* **FOOSH**: Fall On Outstretched Hand → Distal Radius # / Proximal Humerus #
* **Direct impact**: Fall onto greater trochanter → Proximal Femur #

### Epidemiology & Risk Factors in Hong Kong Context
* **Incidence**: Exponential ↑ with aging population; F > M (4:1)
* **Primary Risk Factors**: Post-menopausal estrogen ↓, advanced age, Caucasian/Asian descent, low BMI (<19)
* **Secondary Risk Factors**: Prolonged systemic steroids, smoking, alcohol abuse, rheumatoid arthritis (RA), malabsorption

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Bone Microarchitecture & Remodeling Cycle
* **Osteoclast > Osteoblast activity**: Imbalance in remodeling cycle
* **Trabecular (Cancellous) Bone**: High turnover rate → early & severe loss
* **Cortical (Compact) Bone**: Thins progressively, ↑ porosity later
* **Metaphyseal vulnerability**: Areas with high trabecular-to-cortical ratio fail first

### Classic Sites of Fragility Fractures
* **Proximal Femur (股骨近端)**: Femoral neck (intracapsular), Intertrochanteric (extracapsular)
* **Thoracolumbar Spine (胸腰椎)**: T8-L4 vertebral bodies (anterior wedge compression)
* **Distal Radius (橈骨遠端)**: Corticocancellous junction (Colles' #)
* **Proximal Humerus (肱骨近端)**: Surgical neck / tuberosities

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Assessment of Proximal Femur Fractures (股骨頸骨折)
* **Look**: Shortened, externally rotated (ER) lower limb
* **Feel**: Tenderness over **greater trochanter** & groin
* **Move**: Extreme pain on any attempted ROM (especially internal rotation)
* **NV Status**: Assess distal pulses (DP, PT), sciatic nerve function (EHL, ankle dorsiflexion/plantarflexion)

### Assessment of Vertebral Compression Fractures (脊椎壓迫性骨折)
* **Look**: ↑ Thoracic kyphosis (Dowager’s hump), loss of height
* **Feel**: Focal midline spinous process tenderness
* **Move**: Pain exacerbated by flexion/weight-bearing, relieved by recumbency
* **NV Status**: Usually intact; must rule out spinal cord/cauda equina compromise (perianal sensation, sphincter tone, lower limb power/reflexes)

### Assessment of Upper Limb Fragility Fractures
* **Distal Radius (橈骨遠端骨折)**:
* **Look**: Dinner-fork deformity (dorsal displacement)
* **Feel**: Tenderness over distal radius, **anatomical snuffbox** (rule out scaphoid #)
* **Move**: Painful/restricted wrist ROM
* **NV Status**: Median nerve check (thenar eminence sensation, OK sign for AIN)
* **Proximal Humerus (肱骨近端骨折)**:
* **Look**: Extensive ecchymosis tracking down arm/chest wall
* **Feel**: Tenderness over surgical neck/tuberosities
* **Move**: Pseudoparalysis due to pain
* **NV Status**: Axillary nerve check (regimental badge sensation, deltoid contraction)

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### WHO Bone Mineral Density (BMD) Criteria
| Diagnostic Category | T-score (Comparison to young healthy adult) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | ≥ -1.0 |
| **Osteopenia** | -1.0 to -2.5 |
| **Osteoporosis** | ≤ -2.5 |
| **Severe Osteoporosis** | ≤ -2.5 + presence of ≥1 fragility fracture |

### FRAX Score (Fracture Risk Assessment Tool)
* **Function**: Estimates 10-year probability of major osteoporotic # & hip #
* **HK Validation**: HA uses HK-specific FRAX model to guide BMD testing & treatment thresholds
* **Variables used**: Age, Sex, Weight, Height, Previous #, Parent fractured hip, Current smoking, Glucocorticoids, RA, 2° osteoporosis, Alcohol (≥3 units/day), Femoral neck BMD

### Fracture-Specific Eponyms & Grading Systems
* **Neck of Femur**: Garden Classification (I-IV based on displacement)
* **Intertrochanteric**: Evans/Jensen Classification (based on stability & posteromedial comminution)
* **Vertebral**: Genant Semi-Quantitative Method (Grade 1-3 based on % height loss)
* **Distal Radius**: Frykman (intra vs extra-articular, ulnar styloid involvement) or Fernandez
* **Proximal Humerus**: Neer Classification (1-4 parts based on displacement >1cm or >45° angulation)

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Acute Fracture Imaging (Plain Radiographs)
* **Proximal Femur**: AP Pelvis, Cross-table Lateral of affected hip
* **Spine**: AP & Lateral Thoracolumbar spine (assess cortical steps, height loss)
* **Distal Radius**: PA & Lateral wrist (assess dorsal tilt, radial inclination/height, articular step-off)
* **Proximal Humerus**: Trauma series (True AP, Scapular Y, Axillary view)

### DEXA Scan (雙能量X光吸收測量儀) Parameters
* **Purpose**: Gold standard for BMD measurement
* **Target areas**: L1-L4 spine, Total Hip, Femoral Neck
* **T-score vs Z-score**: T-score for postmenopausal F / men >50; Z-score for premenopausal F / young men (compares to age-matched peers)

### Laboratory Workup for Secondary Osteoporosis
* **Bone Profile**: Calcium, Phosphate, ALP (usually normal in 1° osteoporosis; ALP ↑ in recent # or osteomalacia)
* **Renal & Liver**: RFT (CKD → renal osteodystrophy), LFT
* **Endocrine**: Vitamin D (25-OH), PTH, TFT (Hyperthyroidism)
* **Myeloma Screen**: Serum Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP), urine Bence-Jones protein

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Stabilization & Pain Control
* **Resuscitation**: ABCDEs (elderly patients often have multiple comorbidities)
* **Analgesia**: Multimodal; paracetamol, weak opioids, Fascia Iliaca Compartment Block (FICB) for hip # (HK HA protocol)
* **Pressure Care**: Ripple bed to prevent sacral/heel pressure sores
* **Fluid & Blood**: Group & Save, IV crystalloids (hip # can bleed 500-1000ml)

### Initial Reduction & Splintage Protocols
* **Proximal Femur**: Skin traction (max 5lb) to ↓ muscle spasm & pain (surgeon preference, not universally mandatory)
* **Distal Radius**: Hematoma block, closed reduction (exaggerate deformity → longitudinal traction → palmar flexion/ulnar deviation), apply below-elbow dorsal backslab
* **Proximal Humerus**: Broad arm sling or collar & cuff, body swathe for comfort
* **Vertebral Compression**: Strict log-roll initially if unstable, otherwise flat bed rest with analgesia

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Medical Management of Osteoporosis (Non-Operative)
| Medication Class | Mechanism of Action | Specific Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bisphosphonates** | Antiresorptive (inhibit osteoclasts) | Alendronate (PO), Zoledronate (IV yearly) |
| **RANKL Inhibitor** | Antiresorptive (blocks osteoclast maturation) | Denosumab (Prolia SC Q6M) |
| **Anabolic Agents** | Stimulates osteoblasts | Teriparatide (Forteo SC daily) |
| **Supplementation** | Substrate provision | Calcium 1-1.2g/day + Vitamin D 800-1000 IU/day |

### Operative vs. Non-Operative Fracture Management
| Fracture Site | Non-Operative Indications | Operative Indications (Definitive Fixation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Proximal Femur** | Unfit for anesthesia (absolute palliative care) | ALMOST ALL (To allow early mobilization & prevent fatal complications) |
| **Vertebral Body** | Stable wedge #, pain controlled, neurologically intact | Neurological deficit, severe kyphosis, refractory pain |
| **Distal Radius** | Undisplaced, stable post-reduction | Unstable, intra-articular step >2mm, volar shear (Barton's) |
| **Proximal Humerus** | 1-part #, minimally displaced 2-part # | Displaced 3/4-part #, head-splitting, severe valgus impaction |

### Surgical Principles in Osteoporotic Bone
* **Proximal Femur**:
* **Intracapsular (NOF)**: Undisplaced → Cannulated screws. Displaced → Hemiarthroplasty or THR (replaces head, bypassing poor bone healing).
* **Extracapsular (IT)**: Dynamic Hip Screw (DHS) or Cephalomedullary Nail (PFNA).
* **Spine**: Percutaneous Vertebroplasty / Kyphoplasty (cement augmentation).
* **Implant Considerations**: Use locking plates (fixed-angle constructs), cement augmentation, longer plates to span diaphysis, cortical button suspensory fixation.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative Weight-Bearing & Mobility
* **Hip Fractures**: Weight Bear As Tolerated (WBAT) immediately post-op (Day 1) to prevent DVT/pneumonia.
* **Spine**: Early mobilization with thoracolumbar orthosis (Jewett brace) if required for comfort.
* **Upper Limb**: Early active ROM of adjacent joints (fingers, elbow) to prevent stiffness; delayed weight-bearing/lifting.
* **MDT Approach**: Physiotherapy (PT) for gait training, Occupational Therapy (OT) for home ADL modifications.

### Fall Prevention Program (防跌計劃)
* **Geriatric Assessment**: Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA) by HA Geriatricians.
* **Medication Review**: Stop polypharmacy (sedatives, anti-hypertensives causing postural hypotension).
* **Visual/Sensory**: Cataract surgery, correct hearing aids.
* **Home Modification**: Grab bars, non-slip mats, adequate lighting, removal of loose rugs.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Bone & Implant Failure
* **Implant Cut-out**: DHS screw migrating through osteoporotic femoral head.
* **Loss of Reduction**: Collapse of osteoporotic bone under cyclical loading.
* **Non-union / Malunion**: Poor vascularity and poor bone stock.
* **Peri-prosthetic Fracture**: Stress riser at the end of a rigid implant in osteoporotic bone.

### Systemic Complications & Mortality
* **VTE**: DVT / Pulmonary Embolism (PE) due to prolonged immobility.
* **Infection**: Hypostatic pneumonia, UTI from catheterization, infected pressure ulcers.
* **Mortality**: 1-year mortality post-hip fracture is 20-30% in HK due to systemic decompensation.
* **Subsequent Fractures**: 50% risk of another fragility fracture within 5 years (Secondary # Cascade).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical immediate post-operative goal for an elderly patient with a fractured neck of femur? Answer: Immediate mobilization (Weight Bear As Tolerated) to prevent fatal medical complications like pneumonia, DVT, and pressure sores.
* Trap: Prescribing Bisphosphonates to a patient without checking their renal function. Pearl: Always check RFTs and serum Calcium before initiating Bisphosphonates, as they are contraindicated in severe CKD (eGFR <35) and can exacerbate hypocalcemia.
* Pitfall: Discharging a wrist fracture patient with only a cast check appointment. Always refer fragility fracture patients >50 years old for a DEXA scan and fall prevention clinic to prevent the secondary fracture cascade.
