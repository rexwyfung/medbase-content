# Musculoskeletal Infection (骨骼肌肉感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Acute Osteomyelitis (急性骨髓炎) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Acute purulent inflammation of bone & bone marrow (骨髓). Duration < 2 weeks.
* **MOI (Haematogenous):** Bacteremia seeding (most common in paediatrics).
* **MOI (Contiguous):** Direct spread from adjacent infected soft tissue (e.g., diabetic foot ulcer).
* **MOI (Direct Inoculation):** Open #, surgical implant, penetrating trauma.
* **Epidemiology:** Bimodal peak (children < 5 yrs, adults > 50 yrs). Males > Females.

### Chronic Osteomyelitis (慢性骨髓炎) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Persistent bone infection (> 6 weeks) characterised by necrotic bone (**Sequestrum**; 死骨) & reactive new bone formation (**Involucrum**; 包殼).
* **MOI:** Undertreated acute osteomyelitis, infected non-union, chronic orthopaedic implant infection.

### Septic Arthritis (化膿性關節炎) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Purulent infection of a joint space. True orthopaedic emergency due to rapid cartilage destruction (chondrolysis).
* **MOI:** Haematogenous spread (synovium highly vascular), direct inoculation (intra-articular injection), contiguous spread (osteomyelitis breaking into joint).
* **Epidemiology:** Highest risk in elderly, RA patients, immunocompromised, prosthetic joints. Knee > Hip > Shoulder.

### Suppurative Flexor Tenosynovitis (化膿性屈肌腱腱鞘炎) Definition & MOI
* **Definition:** Closed space infection within the synovial sheath of a flexor tendon in the hand.
* **MOI:** Penetrating trauma to volar digit (e.g., animal bite, rose thorn, needle prick).

### Pathogenic Epidemiology in Musculoskeletal Infections
| Patient Cohort | Most Common Pathogen |
| :--- | :--- |
| **All ages (Overall)** | *Staphylococcus aureus* (金黃色葡萄球菌) (>50%) |
| **Paediatrics (< 4 yrs)** | *Kingella kingae*, *Group A Streptococcus* |
| **Neonates (< 2 mos)** | *Group B Streptococcus*, *E. coli* |
| **Sickle Cell Disease** | *Salmonella spp.* |
| **IV Drug Users (IVDU)** | *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, MRSA |
| **Sexually Active Young Adults** | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* |
| **Puncture wound through shoe** | *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* |
| **Animal Bites (Dog/Cat)** | *Pasteurella multocida* |

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Paediatric Metaphyseal Vascular Anatomy
* **Pathophysiology:** Arterial loops in metaphysis (骺板) empty into large venous sinusoids.
* **Biomechanics:** Sluggish venous blood flow creates a low-oxygen environment favourable for bacterial deposition/proliferation.
* **Barrier Mechanism:** Physis (growth plate) acts as a barrier to intra-articular spread, EXCEPT in joints where metaphysis is intra-capsular (Hip, Shoulder, Ankle, Radial neck). In these joints, metaphyseal osteomyelitis → primary Septic Arthritis.

### Pathophysiology of Sequestrum & Involucrum
* **Ischaemic Cascade:** Purulent exudate ↑ intraosseous pressure → occludes medullary blood vessels → cortical ischemia.
* **Sequestrum (死骨):** Segment of avascular, necrotic cortical bone serving as a nidus for biofilm.
* **Involucrum (包殼):** Viable periosteum separated by subperiosteal abscess lays down reactive sleeve of new bone surrounding sequestrum.
* **Cloaca:** Defect/hole in involucrum allowing pus decompression → forms draining sinus tract to skin.

### Flexor Tendon Sheath Anatomy
* **Synovial Sheaths:** Digits 2, 3, 4 have separate synovial sheaths ending at distal palmar crease.
* **Ulnar Bursa:** Sheath of 5th digit communicates directly with ulnar bursa in palm.
* **Radial Bursa:** Sheath of thumb (FPL) communicates directly with radial bursa.
* **Horseshoe Abscess:** Connection between Ulnar & Radial bursae in space of Parona (distal forearm) allows rapid spread of infection.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Acute Osteomyelitis Physical Findings
* **Look:** Erythema, swelling, limb held in resting posture. Pseudo-paralysis in infants (refusal to move limb).
* **Feel:** Warmth, extreme point tenderness over metaphysis.
* **Move:** Painful arc of motion (but usually allows some passive joint movement compared to septic arthritis).
* **NV:** Usually intact.

### Chronic Osteomyelitis Physical Findings
* **Look:** Discharging sinus tract, thickened/deformed limb contour, overlying skin scarring/changes.
* **Feel:** Bony hypertrophy (involucrum), indurated surrounding soft tissues.
* **Move:** May have restricted ROM if adjacent joint contracture present.
* **NV:** Must check for peripheral neuropathy (especially in diabetic foot MOI).

### Septic Arthritis Physical Findings
* **Look:** Grossly swollen joint, erythema, joint held in position of maximum volume (e.g., Hip: flexion, abduction, external rotation. Knee: 20-30° flexion).
* **Feel:** Warmth, tense effusion, global joint tenderness.
* **Move:** Severe pain with *any* micro-motion. True limitation of BOTH active & passive ROM.
* **NV:** Intact unless gross swelling causes compartment syndrome.

### Kanavel's Four Cardinal Signs of Flexor Tenosynovitis
* **Look (Sign 1):** Fusiform (sausage-like) symmetrical swelling of entire digit.
* **Look (Sign 2):** Digit held in resting flexed posture.
* **Feel (Sign 3):** Extreme tenderness along entire course of flexor tendon sheath.
* **Move (Sign 4):** Severe excruciating pain on passive extension of digit (most sensitive, early sign).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Cierny-Mader Classification for Osteomyelitis (Anatomy)
| Type | Anatomical Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1** | Medullary (confined to intramedullary surfaces) |
| **Stage 2** | Superficial (exposed necrotic cortical bone) |
| **Stage 3** | Localised (full-thickness cortical sequestration, stable bone) |
| **Stage 4** | Diffuse (circumferential disease, mechanically unstable bone) |

### Cierny-Mader Classification for Osteomyelitis (Host)
| Class | Host Physiological Status |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Good normal host |
| **B** | Compromised host (Bs = Systemic e.g., DM; Bl = Local e.g., PVD) |
| **C** | Treatment worse than disease (palliative/amputation needed) |

### Modified Gächter Classification for Septic Arthritis (Arthroscopic)
| Stage | Arthroscopic Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Opacity of fluid, redness of synovial membrane, petechiae |
| **II** | Fibrinous deposits, severe inflammation, no cartilage damage |
| **III** | Thickening of synovial membrane, compartment formation, early cartilage damage |
| **IV** | Aggressive pannus formation, extensive cartilage destruction/osteolysis |

### Michon's Staging for Flexor Tenosynovitis
| Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Serous exudate, synovial inflammation. No necrosis. |
| **II** | Purulent exudate within sheath. Early tendon necrosis. |
| **III** | Extensive tendon & sheath necrosis. Subcutaneous extension. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiograph (X-ray) Findings in Musculoskeletal Infection
* **Early Acute (0-10 days):** Often normal. Deep soft tissue swelling, loss/blurring of fat planes.
* **Late Acute (10-14 days):** Regional osteopenia, irregular radiolucent lesions (bone destruction), periosteal reaction (new bone parallel to cortex).
* **Chronic:** Thickened sclerotic bone (involucrum), dense isolated bone fragment (sequestrum), radiolucent cloaca.
* **Septic Arthritis:** Rapid joint space narrowing (cartilage loss), subchondral bone destruction.

### Advanced Imaging Modalities for Infection
* **MRI with IV Gadolinium Contrast:** Gold standard. Highly sensitive for early marrow edema, soft tissue abscess, and joint effusion. T1 ↓ signal, T2/STIR ↑ signal in infected marrow.
* **Ultrasound:** Rapid bedside tool in HK HA EDs to identify joint effusion (esp. paediatric hip) & guide aspiration.
* **White Blood Cell (Indium-111) Scan:** Useful to distinguish infection from aseptic loosening in arthroplasty or Charcot arthropathy.

### Laboratory & Microbiological Investigations
* **Blood Markers:** ↑ WBC (neutrophilia), ↑ ESR (紅血球沉降率, peaks at 3-5 days), ↑ CRP (C-反應蛋白, peaks at 48 hrs, best for monitoring treatment response).
* **Blood Cultures:** Minimum 2 sets taken from different peripheral sites BEFORE antibiotics.
* **Joint Aspirate (Arthrocentesis):** Send for Gram stain, Culture & Sensitivity (C/S), Cell count, Crystals (rule out gout/pseudogout).
* **Aspirate Cell Count Thresholds:** WBC > 50,000/mm³ with > 90% PMNs = Septic Arthritis until proven otherwise.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### ED Resuscitation & Initial Workup Protocol
* **Resuscitation:** Assess ABCDEs. Initiate Sepsis Six if systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) criteria met.
* **NBM:** Keep patient Nil By Mouth (NBM) for emergency surgery.
* **Diagnostic Aspiration:** Arthrocentesis (for suspected septic arthritis) or deep tissue aspiration MUST be performed in ED prior to empirical antibiotics (unless septic shock).

### Empirical Antibiotic Therapy Protocol (HK HA Context)
* **Principle:** High-dose, Intravenous (IV) administration.
* **Standard Adult (Native Joint/Bone):** IV Flucloxacillin/Cloxacillin 1g Q6H (covers MSSA).
* **Penicillin Allergic / MRSA Suspected:** IV Vancomycin + target trough level monitoring.
* **Gram -ve Suspected (Elderly/UTI/IVDU):** Add IV Ceftriaxone or Ceftazidime.
* **Gonococcal Arthritis:** IV Ceftriaxone.

### Acute Splinting & Immobilisation
* **Resting Splint:** Apply Plaster of Paris (POP) backslab or orthosis to rest the infected joint/limb.
* **Benefit:** ↓ pain, ↓ muscle spasm, prevents early contractures, ↓ theoretical haematogenous dissemination.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Definitive Management Algorithm for Septic Arthritis
| Indication | Management Strategy |
| :--- | :--- |
| **All native joints (Standard)** | Emergency Surgical Arthrotomy (Open) & Washout. Copious irrigation (6-9 Litres saline) + Synovectomy. |
| **Knee / Shoulder joints (Early)** | Arthroscopic Washout. Enables thorough debridement of loculations with less morbidity. |
| **Paediatric Hip** | Emergency Open Arthrotomy via anterior (Smith-Petersen) approach. Capsulotomy left OPEN for continuous drainage. |
| **Medically Unfit for Surgery** | Serial needle aspirations + prolonged IV antibiotics (suboptimal, high risk of joint destruction). |

### Definitive Management Algorithm for Acute Osteomyelitis
| Indication | Management Strategy |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Early (<48 hrs), No abscess** | Non-operative: IV Antibiotics exclusively (4-6 weeks total). |
| **Subperiosteal Abscess / No improvement on Abx in 48-72h** | Operative: Surgical Incision & Drainage (I&D). Drill holes (fenestration) into cortex to decompress medullary canal. |

### Definitive Management Algorithm for Chronic Osteomyelitis
| Indication | Management Strategy |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Presentation** | Operative: Radical Sequestrectomy (excision of all dead bone until 'paprika sign' - punctate cortical bleeding). |
| **Resulting Dead Space** | **Dead Space Management:** Implantation of PMMA Antibiotic Beads (Gentamicin/Vancomycin) → delayed bone grafting (Papineau technique) OR vascularised muscle flap (e.g., Soleus/Gastrocnemius flap). |
| **Gross Instability (Cierny-Mader 4)** | Operative: Resection margins + Circular External Fixation (Ilizarov frame) for bone transport (Distraction Osteogenesis). |

### Definitive Management Algorithm for Flexor Tenosynovitis
| Indication | Management Strategy |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Michon Stage I (Early <24h)** | Non-operative trial (rare): Elevation, strict immobilisation, IV Abx for 24h. |
| **Michon Stage II / III / No improvement** | Operative: Emergency I&D. Standard: Proximal (A1 pulley) & Distal (A5 pulley) incisions → catheter irrigation with saline. If necrotic → Open mid-axial debridement. |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Infection Mobilisation & Physiotherapy
* **Septic Arthritis:** Immediate continuous passive motion (CPM) post-washout to prevent adhesions/ankylosis & nourish remaining cartilage. Transition to active ROM as pain subsides.
* **Flexor Tenosynovitis:** Early protected active gliding exercises within 24-48 hours post-op to prevent severe tendon adhesions/stiffness.

### Antibiotic Suppression & Transition Therapy
* **IV to PO Switch:** Transition to oral antibiotics only when: clinical signs resolve AND inflammatory markers (CRP/ESR) show significant downward trend (usually 1-2 weeks IV).
* **Total Duration:** Generally 4-6 weeks for Osteomyelitis/Septic Arthritis.
* **OPAT:** Outpatient Parenteral Antimicrobial Therapy (OPAT) utilized in HK HA for prolonged IV regimens (e.g., Ceftriaxone once daily) to facilitate early discharge.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Complications of Septic Arthritis
* **Chondrolysis:** Rapid enzymatic destruction of articular cartilage by bacterial proteases and host MMPs.
* **Secondary Osteoarthritis (OA):** Irreversible joint degeneration requiring future arthroplasty.
* **Fibrous/Bony Ankylosis:** Complete fusion and loss of joint movement.
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN):** Especially in paediatric septic hip (capsular distension occludes retinacular vessels → femoral head AVN).

### Complications of Chronic Osteomyelitis
* **Pathological Fracture:** Due to altered bone mechanics, osteopenia, and cortical defects.
* **Marjolin's Ulcer:** Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) undergoing malignant transformation within a chronic discharging sinus tract (requires amputation).
* **Systemic Amyloidosis:** Deposition of AA amyloid in kidneys due to chronic systemic inflammation.

### Hand-Specific Infection Complications (Flexor Tenosynovitis)
* **Tendon Rupture:** Ischaemic necrosis of flexor tendon.
* **Boutonnière/Swan Neck Deformities:** Due to joint capsular/extensor mechanism disruption from severe swelling.
* **Digital Necrosis:** Compartment syndrome of the digit necessitating amputation.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Starting empirical IV antibiotics in the ED immediately for a swollen, red knee before any invasive workup.
* Pearl: ALWAYS perform a diagnostic joint aspiration before administering antibiotics. Giving Abx first rapidly sterilises the synovial fluid, yielding negative cultures and destroying the chance for targeted therapy. (Exception: Patient in overt septic shock).
* Pitfall: Ruling out acute osteomyelitis because the admission plain X-ray is normal. Plain radiographs require 30-50% bone mineral loss to show changes; osteopenia and periosteal reactions do not appear until 10-14 days post-infection.
* Viva Question: What is the 'Paprika Sign' in chronic osteomyelitis surgery?
* Answer: Punctate bleeding observed from cortical bone during sequestrectomy. It confirms the surgeon has reached healthy, vascularised, viable bone capable of healing.
* Viva Question: An 8-year-old boy presents with knee pain but the knee exam is entirely normal. Where must you examine next?
* Answer: The hip. Referred pain from a septic hip or slipped upper femoral epiphysis (SUFE) often presents purely as distal thigh or knee pain via the obturator nerve.
* Trap: Misdiagnosing flexor tenosynovitis as a superficial cellulitis of the finger.
* Pearl: Cellulitis lacks Kanavel's signs. If the patient has exquisite pain on *passive extension* of the digit, it is flexor tenosynovitis until proven otherwise and requires urgent surgical consultation, not just oral antibiotics.
* Viva Question: What is Marjolin's ulcer?
* Answer: A highly aggressive Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) that develops in the chronic granulation tissue of a long-standing discharging sinus tract, typically in chronic osteomyelitis or severe burn scars.
