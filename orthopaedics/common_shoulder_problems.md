# Common Shoulder Problems (常見肩關節問題)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Subacromial Impingement & Rotator Cuff Syndrome Definition and MOI
* **Definition**: Compression of **rotator cuff tendons** and **subacromial bursa** between humeral head and **coracoacromial arch**.
* **Epidemiology**: Most common cause of shoulder pain. Incidence ↑ with age (>40 yrs).
* **MOI (Chronic)**: Repetitive overhead activities → microtrauma → tendinosis → partial tear → full-thickness tear.
* **MOI (Acute)**: Fall on outstretched hand (FOOSH) or sudden heavy lifting in older patients. 
![image|452](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/a53d890a4dbe6a11fb203fd0537e2e16.png)

### Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder / 沾黏性肩關節囊炎) Definition and MOI
* **Definition**: Idiopathic thickening and contracture of **glenohumeral joint capsule**.
* **Epidemiology**: Females > Males, 40-60 yrs. Strong association with **Diabetes Mellitus (DM)** and **Thyroid disorders**.
* **MOI**:
	* 1° (idiopathic) vs 2° (post-traumatic/post-surgical).
	* Progressive inflammation → fibrosis → ↓ joint volume.

### Anterior Shoulder Instability (肩關節前脫位) Definition and MOI
* **Definition**: Loss of normal articulation between humeral head and glenoid. 95% are anterior.
* **Epidemiology**:
	* Bimodal (young athletes, elderly fallers).
	* High recurrence rate in young (<20 yrs = >80% recurrence).
* **MOI (Anterior)**: Forced **Abduction + External Rotation (ER)**.
* **MOI (Posterior)**: Seizures, electrocution, direct anterior blow (rare, ~4%).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Rotator Cuff Anatomy & Biomechanics
| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Supraspinatus (棘上肌)** | **Suprascapular nerve** | Abduction (initiates first 15°) |
| **Infraspinatus (棘下肌)** | **Suprascapular nerve** | External Rotation (ER) |
| **Teres Minor (小圓肌)** | **Axillary nerve** | External Rotation (ER) |
| **Subscapularis (肩胛下肌)** | **Upper/Lower Subscapular nerves** | Internal Rotation (IR) |
* **Biomechanics**: Functions as a single unit to compress humeral head into glenoid fossa (dynamic concavity compression) → resists superior translation from **Deltoid** pull during abduction.
![image|574](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/e9e8f22b63af2bf8fd126ff763a6deae.png)

### Glenohumeral Joint Stability Stabilizers
* **Static Stabilizers**:
    * **Glenoid Labrum**: Deepens socket by 50%.
    * **Capsuloligamentous complex**: Superior (SGHL), Middle (MGHL), and **Inferior Glenohumeral Ligaments (IGHL)**. 
    * **IGHL anterior band**: Primary restraint to anterior translation at 90° abduction.
    * Negative intra-articular pressure.
* **Dynamic Stabilizers**:
    * **Rotator Cuff** muscles.
    * **Biceps Long Head (LHB)** tendon.
    * Periscapular muscles (Trapezius, Serratus Anterior, Rhomboids).
![image|319](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/a8cc8bdf36d4ef6319f62644e87139db.png)

### Subacromial Space Anatomy
* **Roof**: **Acromion**, **Coracoacromial (CA) ligament**, **Coracoid process**.
* **Floor**: Superior surface of **rotator cuff** (mainly **Supraspinatus**) and **humeral head**.
* **Contents**: Subacromial bursa, **Supraspinatus** tendon, **LHB** tendon.
* **Vascularity**: **Critical zone of Codman** (hypovascular area ~1cm proximal to **Supraspinatus** insertion on **Greater Tuberosity**) → vulnerable to degeneration/tears.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### General Look and Feel of the Shoulder
* **Look**:
	* Muscle wasting (**Supraspinous/Infraspinous fossa**, **Deltoid**).
	* Asymmetry.
	* Scars.
	* "Popeye" sign (LHB rupture).
	* Squaring of shoulder (anterior dislocation).
* **Feel**: Tenderness over **Greater Tuberosity**, **Bicipital groove**, **AC joint**, **Coracoid process**.
![image|681](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/bab362af7d2aa60ac05c7104fc281f7e.png)


### Move: Supraspinatus Pathology Special Tests

| Special Test | Positive Finding |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Jobe's (Empty Can) Test** | Pain or weakness with resisted elevation in scapular plane with IR. |
| **Drop Arm Test** | Inability to smoothly control descent from 90° abduction. |

![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/0e296e98304699b1ae34e259bb0206b5.png)

### Move: Infraspinatus Pathology Special Test

| Special Test                   | Positive Finding                 |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| **Resisted External Rotation** | Weakness in ER with arm at side. |


### Move: Subscapularis Pathology Special Tests

| Special Test | Positive Finding |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gerber's Lift-Off Test** | Inability to lift dorsum of hand off lumbar spine. |
| **Belly Press Test** | Elbow drops posteriorly when pressing abdomen. |

![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/48bc43be30958bcc2f866515d9c99990.png)

### Move: Impingement Pathology Special Tests

| Special Test             | Positive Finding                                            |
| :----------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Neer's Test**          | Pain with passive forward flexion while internally rotated. |
| **Hawkins-Kennedy Test** | Pain with passive IR at 90° forward flexion.                |
| **Painful Arc**          | Pain actively abducting between 60°–120°.                   |


### Move: Instability Special Tests
* **Anterior Apprehension Test**: Supine, arm abducted 90°, ER → patient expresses fear/resistance (feels like dislocating).
* **Relocation (Jobe's) Test**: Posterior force on humeral head during apprehension test → relief of apprehension/pain.
* **Sulcus Sign**: Inferior traction on arm → visible dimple below acromion (+ve for multidirectional instability).
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/ed88a57f6ba5ea39a43312fb47790e4a.png)

### Move: Adhesive Capsulitis Range of Motion
* Severe, global restriction of both **Active AND Passive ROM**.
* Pathognomonic: Loss of **Passive External Rotation** (capsular pattern).

### Neurovascular Assessment of the Upper Limb
* **Axillary Nerve (腋神經)**: Check sensation over **regimental badge area** (lateral deltoid). Assess **Deltoid** contraction. (Highest risk in anterior dislocation).
* **Musculocutaneous Nerve**: Lateral forearm sensation.
* **Radial, Median, Ulnar Nerves**: Standard distal testing.
* **Vascular**: Radial/Ulnar pulses, capillary refill (rule out **Axillary Artery** injury in elderly dislocators).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Neer's Classification of Subacromial Impingement
| Stage | Pathology | Typical Age | Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Edema & Hemorrhage | < 25 yrs | Reversible with conservative Rx. |
| **II** | Fibrosis & Tendinitis | 25-40 yrs | Recurrent pain, mechanical catching. |
| **III** | Bone spurs & Tendon Rupture | > 40 yrs | Full-thickness tear, significant weakness. |

### Cofield Classification of Rotator Cuff Tears
| Size | Measurement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Small** | < 1 cm |
| **Medium** | 1 - 3 cm |
| **Large** | 3 - 5 cm |
| **Massive** | > 5 cm (or involving >2 tendons) |

### Bigliani Acromion Morphology Classification
* **Type I**: Flat (low risk of impingement).
* **Type II**: Curved (moderate risk).
* **Type III**: Hooked (high risk of impingement & tears).
![image|460](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/b9d6806b2cc7fe73b1ec3f6a4bff0865.png)

### Stanmore Classification of Shoulder Instability
* **Polar Type I**: Traumatic structural (e.g., Bankart lesion). Requires surgery.
* **Polar Type II**: Atraumatic structural (capsular dysfunction). Rehab first, surgery if failed.
* **Polar Type III**: Muscle patterning non-structural. Pure rehab. Surgery contraindicated.
![image|397](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/08827f30030f524ab93ff48746fd2f2e.png)

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Standard Shoulder X-Ray Views & Pathologies
* **AP View**:
    * Impingement/Cuff Tear: ↓ Subacromial space (<7mm indicates massive tear), sclerosis/cysts at **Greater Tuberosity**.
    * Instability: **Hill-Sachs lesion** (posterolateral humeral head compression #).
* **Axillary View**:
    * Dislocation direction (humeral head anterior vs posterior to glenoid).
    * **Os acromiale** (unfused acromial apophysis).
* **Scapular Y-View**:
    * Normal: Humeral head centered on Y (coracoid, acromion, scapular body).
    * Anterior dislocation: Head medial to Y (under coracoid).
![image|650](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/21513dbf43249c31c32a475b3e2d7d49.png)

### Advanced Imaging (Ultrasound & MRI) for Shoulder
* **Ultrasound (USG)**:
	* Operator-dependent but excellent for dynamic impingement, measuring full vs partial cuff tears, and LHB tendinopathy.
	* Commonly used in HA outpatient clinics.
* **MRI Shoulder (Plain or Arthrogram)**:
    * Gold standard for evaluating full rotator cuff anatomy, fatty infiltration (Goutallier classification), and muscle atrophy.
    * MR Arthrogram: Highly sensitive for labral tears (**Bankart lesion**, **SLAP lesion**).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Anterior Shoulder Dislocation Reduction Techniques
* **Pre-requisite**: Plain X-rays to rule out fracture-dislocation (surgical neck #). Document pre-reduction NV status (**Axillary nerve**).
* **Analgesia**: IV Midazolam/Fentanyl or Entonox. Intra-articular Lignocaine injection (highly effective, avoids conscious sedation risks).
* **Cunningham Technique**: Massage biceps/deltoid + patient shrugs shoulders back. Zero traction. 
* **Milch Technique**: Slow abduction + ER + thumb pressure on humeral head.
* **Traction-Countertraction**: Sheet around patient's thorax, practitioner pulls arm in axial traction.
* **Post-reduction**: Confirm with post-reduction X-rays. Document post-reduction NV status.
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/a6180f5768940d0f437e63e4a4f96c33.png)![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/ee0cd81f59c83b113abf3002dd3f1caa.png)

### Post-Reduction Immobilization & Care
* Apply **Broad Arm Sling** or **Polysling** in internal rotation for 3 weeks (younger) or 1-2 weeks (older, to prevent stiffness).
* Provide adequate analgesia (NSAIDs/Paracetamol).
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/4704246782240a5dfb9e391f0143b8c3.png)

### Acute Traumatic Rotator Cuff Tear Initial Management
* Simple sling for comfort.
* Analgesia.
* Early Orthopaedic outpatient referral for expedited MRI and potential early surgical repair (< 3-6 weeks) to prevent muscle atrophy/retraction.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Impingement Syndrome & Rotator Cuff Tear Algorithm
| Indication                                             | Management Strategy      | Surgical Principle                                                                                                      |
| :----------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Impingement / Partial Tear / Elderly**               | **Non-Operative**        | Physiotherapy (scapular stabilizers, cuff strengthening). NSAIDs. Subacromial Corticosteroid injection (max 2-3 times). |
| **Acute traumatic full-thickness tear in active pt**   | **Operative (Early)**    | **Arthroscopic Rotator Cuff Repair (ARCR)** (single vs double row). +/- Acromioplasty.                                  |
| **Chronic full-thickness tear failed conservative Rx** | **Operative (Elective)** | **ARCR**. If irreparable with pseudo-paralysis in elderly → **Reverse Total Shoulder Arthroplasty (RTSA)**.             |

![image|550](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/3be1f5631519295f5e7b0bbc2f87dc35.png)

### Anterior Shoulder Instability Management Algorithm
| Indication                                             | Management Strategy         | Surgical Principle                                                                                           |
| :----------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **First-time dislocator > 30 yrs without bone loss**   | **Non-Operative**           | Sling immobilization → Physiotherapy (cuff and periscapular strengthening).                                  |
| **Young athlete (<20 yrs) OR Recurrent Dislocators**   | **Operative (Soft Tissue)** | **Arthroscopic Bankart Repair** (anchor labrum back to glenoid rim). +/- Remplissage if engaging Hill-Sachs. |
| **Glenoid bone loss > 20-25% (Inverted Pear Glenoid)** | **Operative (Bony)**        | **Latarjet Procedure** (coracoid transfer to anterior glenoid with conjoint tendon acting as dynamic sling). |

![image|550](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/937e999a9d8c303ff556e2b33f7a2064.png)

### Adhesive Capsulitis Management Options
* **Phase 1 (Freezing/Painful)**:
	* Pain control. NSAIDs, intra-articular Corticosteroid injection.
	* Gentle ROM. *Avoid aggressive manipulation.*
* **Phase 2 (Frozen/Stiff)**: Physiotherapy to stretch capsule.
* **Phase 3 (Thawing/Recovery)**: Continued mobilization. 
* **Refractory (> 6-12 months)**: Manipulation Under Anaesthesia (MUA) or Arthroscopic Capsular Release.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Rotator Cuff Repair Rehab Timeline
* **0-4 Weeks**: Polysling with abduction pillow. Passive ROM only (avoid active tension on repair). Pendulum exercises.
* **4-8 Weeks**: Active-Assisted ROM. Discontinue sling.
* **8-12 Weeks**: Active ROM. Gentle isometric strengthening.
* **> 12 Weeks**: Isotonic strengthening, resistance training. Return to sport at 6 months.

### Post-Bankart Repair Rehab Timeline
* **0-4 Weeks**: Sling immobilization. NO External Rotation > 0° (protects anterior capsulolabral repair).
* **4-8 Weeks**: Progressive ROM. Limit ER to 30°.
* **> 12 Weeks**: Strength training. Return to contact sports at 6 months.

### Adhesive Capsulitis Rehab Strategy
* Focus on restoring **External Rotation** first.
* Aggressive passive stretching → active assisted → active ROM. Home exercise program using pulley systems and wall walking.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Complications of Anterior Shoulder Dislocation
* **Neurological**: **Axillary nerve palsy** (5-10%, highest in elderly), Brachial plexus injury.
* **Vascular**: **Axillary artery** injury (rare, seen in older patients with fragile vessels).
* **Structural**:
    * **Bankart lesion** (anteroinferior labral tear).
    * **Hill-Sachs lesion** (chondral impaction # of posterolateral humeral head).
    * **Bony Bankart** (glenoid rim fracture).
    * Rotator cuff tear (incidence > 50% in patients > 40 yrs with dislocation).
* **Recurrence**: Inversely proportional to age at first dislocation. 
![image|550](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/b121b637c9a18637718d552aea2c151d.png)

### Complications of Rotator Cuff Tears & Repair
* **Non-operative**: Cuff tear arthropathy (superior migration of humeral head → impingement on acromion → secondary OA).
* **Operative**:
	* Re-tear (up to 20-30% in massive tears).
	* Joint stiffness (post-op frozen shoulder).
	* Infection.
	* Hardware (anchor) failure.

### Complications of Adhesive Capsulitis
* Residual stiffness (up to 10% have permanent mild restriction).
* Iatrogenic proximal humerus fracture or dislocation during Manipulation Under Anaesthesia (MUA).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the "Terrible Triad" of the shoulder in an older adult? Answer: Anterior dislocation + Rotator cuff tear + Neuropraxia (usually Axillary nerve).
* Trap: Assuming a patient > 40 years old with weakness post-shoulder dislocation has an isolated axillary nerve neurapraxia. Pearl: You must rule out a concomitant massive rotator cuff tear, which is extremely common in this demographic. Order an USG or MRI.
* Pitfall: Vigorously testing active ROM in an acute rotator cuff tear. You will cause severe pain and guarding. Assess passive ROM to ensure joint mobility, then carefully test specific cuff muscles.
* Viva Question: What is a SLAP tear? Answer: Superior Labrum Anterior to Posterior tear, involving the origin of the Long Head of Biceps tendon. ![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/ff11cc988865c64543728e2648ac7e44.png)
* Trap: Trying to forcibly reduce a dislocated shoulder using the Hippocratic method (foot in axilla). Pearl: This is outdated and risks severe brachial plexus traction injury. Use gentle traction-countertraction or Cunningham's technique.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing "frozen shoulder" in a patient with normal passive external rotation. If passive ER is completely normal, the diagnosis is likely impingement or a cuff tear, NOT adhesive capsulitis.
* Viva Question: Why do we prefer RTSA (Reverse Total Shoulder Arthroplasty) for Cuff Tear Arthropathy? Answer: RTSA changes the center of rotation medially and inferiorly, allowing the Deltoid to recruit a longer lever arm to elevate the arm, compensating for the absent rotator cuff.
