# Pelvic & Acetabular Fractures (骨盆及髖臼骨折)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Pelvic Ring & Acetabular Fractures
* **Pelvic Ring Fracture (骨盆環骨折):** Disruption of bony/ligamentous ring formed by sacrum + paired innominate bones (ilium, ischium, pubis). Requires breaks in ≥2 places to displace.
* **Acetabular Fracture (髖臼骨折):** Intra-articular # involving the hip socket (articular surface of innominate bone).

### Mechanism of Injury (MOI) for Pelvic Trauma
* **High-Energy Trauma:** RTA, motorcycle accidents, falls from height (>3m), crush injuries. → Young adults (males > females).
* **Low-Energy Trauma:** Simple ground-level falls. → Elderly (osteoporotic insufficiency #).
* **Avulsion Fractures:** Sudden violent muscle contraction in adolescents (e.g., ASIS by Sartorius, AIIS by Rectus Femoris, Ischial tuberosity by Hamstrings).

### Epidemiology of Pelvic & Acetabular Fractures
* Accounts for 3% of all skeletal #.
* **High Mortality:** 10-16% overall; up to 40-50% in hemodynamically unstable patients (hypovolemic shock).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Pelvic Ring Stability & Ligaments
* **True Pelvis (骨盆腔):** Contains GU/GI organs.
* **Posterior Weight-Bearing Arch:** Transmits force from spine → sacrum → SI joints → acetabulum. **Main weight-bearing axis.**
* **Anterior Tie Arch:** Symphysis pubis + pubic rami. Prevents hemipelvis from opening. (Provides only 40% of stability).
* **Crucial Ligaments:**
  * **Posterior SI Ligaments (骶髂後韌帶):** Strongest in body. Keystone for pelvic vertical/rotational stability.
  * **Sacrotuberous/Sacrospinous Ligaments (骶結節/骶棘韌帶):** Resist rotational and vertical shearing forces.
  * **Symphysis Pubis Ligaments (恥骨聯合韌帶):** Resist external rotation (open book).

### Acetabular Column Concept (Letournel)
* **Inverted "Y" Concept:** Acetabulum supported by 2 columns of bone.
* **Anterior Column:** Anterior ilium → anterior articular surface → superior/inferior pubic rami.
* **Posterior Column:** Greater sciatic notch → posterior articular surface → ischial tuberosity. Contains **Posterior Wall** (most common acetabular #).

### Pelvic Vascular Supply & Bleeding Sources
* **Massive Hemorrhage Anatomy:**
  * **Venous (80%):** Pre-sacral venous plexus, paravesical plexus, fractured cancellous bone faces.
  * **Arterial (20%):** Branches of **Internal Iliac Artery (髂內動脈)**. Superior Gluteal Artery (SGA) → injured in posterior ring #; Obturator/Internal Pudendal Arteries → injured in anterior ring #.

### Neurological Anatomy in Pelvic Trauma
* **Lumbosacral Plexus (腰骶神經叢):** Exits via sacral foramina and crosses anterior SI joint.
* **L5 Nerve Root:** Crosses sacral ala; highly vulnerable in sacral # or vertically unstable injuries.
* **Sciatic Nerve (坐骨神經):** Exits via greater sciatic foramen. Highly vulnerable in **Posterior Wall/Column Acetabular #** or posterior hip dislocation.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Pelvic Trauma Inspection (Look)
* **Scrotal/Perineal Ecchymosis (Destot's Sign):** Indicates deep pelvic bleeding Tracking superficially.
* **Morel-Lavallée Lesion:** Closed degloving injury over greater trochanter/flank (fluctuant mass from liquefied fat/hematoma).
* **Limb Position:** Leg length discrepancy (LLD), shortened + externally rotated limb (anterior-posterior compression or posterior hip dislocation).
* **Urethral Meatus:** Look for blood (DO NOT insert Foley catheter if +ve).
* **Open Wounds:** Check perineum, rectum, vagina for occult open #.

### Pelvic Trauma Palpation (Feel)
* **Pelvic Spring Test:** Gentle compression/distraction of iliac crests. **WARNING:** Perform ONLY ONCE by the most senior clinician to avoid dislodging hemostatic clots. Do NOT perform if clinically obvious # or unstable patient.
* **Symphysis Pubis:** Palpable gap (diastasis).
* **Digital Rectal Exam (DRE):** Assess sphincter tone (sacral root injury), high-riding prostate (urethral tear), palpable bone spicules/blood (open #).
* **Vaginal Exam (PV):** Lacerations/bone fragments (open #).

### Pelvic Trauma Range of Motion (Move)
* **Log-rolling:** Essential to inspect spine/sacrum. Keep pelvis wrapped.
* **Hip ROM:** DEFER active/passive ROM if pelvic ring disruption suspected to prevent catastrophic hemorrhage.

### Pelvic Trauma Neurovascular (NV) Assessment

| Nerve / Root | Motor Assessment (Action) | Sensory Assessment (Dermatome) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **L5 Root** | Extensor Hallucis Longus (Big toe extension) | 1st dorsal web space |
| **S1 Root** | Gastrocnemius/Soleus (Plantarflexion) | Lateral border of foot |
| **Sciatic Nerve (Common Peroneal)** | Tibialis Anterior (Ankle dorsiflexion) | Dorsum of foot |
| **Sciatic Nerve (Tibial)** | Flexor Hallucis Longus (Big toe flexion) | Plantar surface of foot |
| **Pudendal Nerve (S2-S4)** | Anal sphincter tone (via DRE) | Perianal sensation |

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Young-Burgess Classification (Pelvic Ring)
Classifies by MOI & Direction of Force. Predicts associated injuries & hemorrhage volume.

| Type | Subtype | Description (Force & Injury) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anterior-Posterior Compression (APC)** | **APC I** | Symphysis widening <2.5cm. Intact posterior ligaments. |
| | **APC II** | "Open Book". Symphysis widening >2.5cm. Ruptured anterior SI, sacrotuberous/spinous ligaments. Intact posterior SI ligaments. |
| | **APC III** | Complete disruption. Symphysis + Anterior & Posterior SI ligaments torn. Unstable hemipelvis. Highest blood loss. |
| **Lateral Compression (LC)** | **LC I** | Oblique pubic rami # + ipsilateral anterior sacral crush #. |
| | **LC II** | Rami # + ipsilateral iliac wing # (crescent #). |
| | **LC III** | Windswept pelvis. Ipsilateral LC + Contralateral APC (external rotation force to opposite hemipelvis). |
| **Vertical Shear (VS)** | **VS** | Axial load to 1 hemipelvis (e.g., fall from height). Symphysis diastasis + complete posterior disruption + vertical displacement. |
| **Combined Mech (CM)** | **CM** | Complex combinations (e.g., LC/VS). |

### Tile Classification (Pelvic Ring)
Classifies by Stability.
* **Type A:** Stable. (A1: Avulsion #, A2: Iliac wing/stable ring #).
* **Type B:** Rotationally unstable, Vertically stable. (B1: Open book/APC, B2: Lateral compression, B3: Bilateral B injuries).
* **Type C:** Rotationally AND Vertically unstable. Complete disruption of pelvic floor/posterior complex. (C1: Unilateral, C2: Bilateral, C3: Associated with acetabular #).

### Judet-Letournel Classification (Acetabulum)

| Category | Fracture Types (5 of each) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Elementary (Simple)** | 1. Posterior Wall (most common) <br> 2. Posterior Column <br> 3. Anterior Wall <br> 4. Anterior Column <br> 5. Transverse |
| **Associated (Complex)** | 1. Posterior Column + Posterior Wall <br> 2. Transverse + Posterior Wall <br> 3. T-shaped <br> 4. Anterior Column/Wall + Posterior Hemitransverse <br> 5. Both Columns (classic "Spur Sign") |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Standard Pelvic X-ray Series
* **AP Pelvis:** Initial trauma screen. Assesses symphysis (>2.5cm abnormal), sacral foramina symmetry, iliopectineal/ilioischial lines, teardrop.
* **Inlet View:** 45° caudal tilt. Assesses AP translation, sacral impaction, rotation of hemipelvis (widening of SI joint).
* **Outlet View:** 45° cephalad tilt. Assesses vertical translation of hemipelvis, sacral foramina #, pubic rami #.

### Judet Views for Acetabulum
* **Obturator Oblique (OO):** 45° rotation towards uninjured side. Highlights **Anterior Column** & **Posterior Wall**. (Shows obturator foramen en face).
* **Iliac Oblique (IO):** 45° rotation towards injured side. Highlights **Posterior Column** & **Anterior Wall**. (Shows iliac wing en face).

### Advanced Imaging & Trauma Scans
* **FAST Scan (Ultrasound):** Performed in trauma bay. Checks for intra-abdominal free fluid (does NOT evaluate retroperitoneal pelvic hematoma).
* **CT Pelvis with 3D Reconstruction:** Gold standard for pre-operative planning, joint congruity, intra-articular fragments, occult sacral #.
* **CT Angiography:** To identify active arterial extravasation (blush) indicating need for embolization.
* **Retrograde Urethrogram (RUG):** Mandatory before Foley catheter insertion if urethral injury suspected (blood at meatus, high-riding prostate).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### ATLS & Hemodynamic Resuscitation
* **Primary Survey (cABCDE):** Prioritize hemorrhagic shock control.
* **Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP):** Activate early (1:1:1 ratio PRBCs:FFP:Platelets). Do NOT rely on crystalloids.
* **Tranexamic Acid (TXA):** Administer 1g IV within 3 hours of injury, followed by 1g over 8 hours.

### Acute Pelvic Stabilization
* **Pelvic Binder (骨盆束帶):** Apply IMMEDIATELY in ED for suspected pelvic ring injuries (esp. APC/Open book).
* **Placement:** Must be centered exactly over the **Greater Trochanters** (NOT the iliac crests).
* **Mechanism:** Closes the pelvic volume, promotes tamponade of venous bleeding, stabilizes # ends.
* **Caution:** May worsen deformity in Lateral Compression (LC) fractures, but still applied initially until imaging is obtained.
* **Time limit:** Remove or convert to Ex-Fix within 24-48 hours to prevent skin necrosis.

### Hemorrhage Control Protocols (Hong Kong HA Guidelines)
* **Angiography & Embolization (血管造影及栓塞術):** Indicated for hemodynamically unstable patients with arterial blush on CT, or ongoing shock despite binder/fluid resuscitation. Usually targets Internal Iliac Artery branches.
* **Pre-peritoneal Pelvic Packing (骨盆填塞術):** Surgical packing of the retrozius space. Done if angio is unavailable/delayed, or in extremis requiring concurrent laparotomy.
* **Skeletal Traction:** Proximal tibial or distal femoral pin to pull vertically displaced hemipelvis (VS #) down, aiding tamponade and alignment.

### Acute Urological Management
* **Suprapubic Catheter (SPC):** Inserted by Urology if urethral tear confirmed via RUG.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Management Algorithm for Pelvic Ring Fractures

| Treatment Modality | Indications |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | LC I, APC I, isolated pubic rami #. Hemodynamically stable. Intact posterior ring. Displacement <1cm. |
| **Operative (Surgical Fixation)** | Symphysis diastasis >2.5cm (APC II/III). <br> Vertical displacement >1cm (VS). <br> Unstable posterior ring (SI joint disruption, complete sacral #). <br> Open fractures. |

### Surgical Options for Pelvic Ring
* **External Fixation (外固定器):** Temporary or definitive for anterior ring. Pins placed in iliac crest or supra-acetabular corridor. Rapidly controls pelvic volume.
* **INFIX (Subcutaneous Internal Fixation):** Pedicle screws in anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS) connected by subcutaneous rod. Avoids pin-tract infections of Ex-Fix.
* **ORIF Anterior Ring:** Symphysis pubis plating (e.g., dynamic compression plate).
* **Posterior Ring Fixation:** Percutaneous Iliosacral (SI) Screws (inserted into S1/S2 bodies) or posterior tension band plating.

### Management Algorithm for Acetabular Fractures

| Treatment Modality | Indications |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | Non-displaced # (<2mm step-off). <br> Roof arc angles >45° (fracture is outside weight-bearing dome). <br> Secondary congruence (head perfectly aligns with dome despite #). <br> Significant medical co-morbidities. |
| **Operative (ORIF)** | Displacement >2mm in weight-bearing dome. <br> Intra-articular loose bodies. <br> Posterior wall # with hip instability (>20-50% of wall involved). <br> Irreducible hip dislocation. |

### Surgical Approaches for Acetabulum
* **Kocher-Langenbeck Approach:** Posterior approach. Best for Posterior Wall / Posterior Column #.
* **Ilioinguinal Approach (Letournel):** Anterior approach. Best for Anterior Wall / Anterior Column #. Exposes via 3 "windows" around iliopsoas/femoral NV bundle.
* **Modified Stoppa Approach:** Intrapelvic approach. Alternative to ilioinguinal. Excellent for accessing the quadrilateral plate.
* **Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA):** Considered acutely in elderly with severe comminution/osteopenia where ORIF will fail.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Immediate Post-Op Care
* **DVT Prophylaxis:** Mandatory (LMWH or NOACs) as pelvic trauma carries highest risk of DVT/PE in orthopaedics. Continue for 4-6 weeks.
* **Mobilization:** Early mobilization to chair to prevent chest infections/bedsores.

### Weight-Bearing Restrictions
* **Pelvic Ring (Posterior injuries):** Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) or Toe-Touch Weight Bearing (TTWB) on affected side for 6-12 weeks.
* **Pelvic Ring (Anterior isolated):** Weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT).
* **Acetabulum (ORIF):** Strict TTWB / NWB for 6-8 weeks to protect articular cartilage healing and hardware.

### Long-term Physiotherapy Goals
* **Acetabular #:** Early passive/active-assisted ROM of hip (CPM machine) to stimulate cartilage healing and prevent stiffness.
* Core strengthening and gait training.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Acute Life-Threatening Complications
* **Hemorrhagic Shock (出血性休克):** Leading cause of early death.
* **ARDS / Multi-Organ Failure:** Secondary to massive trauma/transfusions.
* **Venous Thromboembolism (DVT/PE):** Extremely high risk due to pelvic vein intimal damage and stasis.

### Genitourinary Complications
* **Urethral Tears:** Mostly males; strictures common late complication.
* **Sexual Dysfunction:** Impotence (erectile dysfunction) in males due to parasympathetic plexus injury; dyspareunia in females.

### Neurological Deficits
* **L5 Nerve Root Injury:** Common in sacral fractures. → Foot drop.
* **Sciatic Nerve Palsy:** 10-15% of posterior acetabular # / dislocations. Peroneal division most vulnerable. → Foot drop, loss of sensation.

### Late Musculoskeletal Complications
* **Post-Traumatic Osteoarthritis (創傷後關節炎):** Up to 30-50% after acetabular #, requiring delayed THA.
* **Heterotopic Ossification (HO):** Ectopic bone formation in muscles, esp. after Kocher-Langenbeck approach. Prophylaxis: Indomethacin or single-dose localized radiotherapy.
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN) of Femoral Head:** Disruption of medial circumflex femoral artery during hip dislocation.
* **Malunion/Non-union:** Pelvic asymmetry → intractable lower back/SI joint pain, leg length discrepancy, gait disturbances.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of mortality in the first 24 hours of a severe pelvic fracture? Answer: Hemorrhagic shock, primarily from the pre-sacral venous plexus and bleeding cancellous bone.
* Trap: Placing the pelvic binder over the iliac crests. Pearl: The binder MUST be centered over the greater trochanters to effectively close the pelvic ring and tamponade bleeding.
* Pitfall: Inserting a Foley catheter in a trauma patient with a pelvic fracture. If there is blood at the urethral meatus, a high-riding prostate, or scrotal hematoma, you must perform a Retrograde Urethrogram (RUG) first to rule out urethral disruption.
* Viva Question: In an APC III (open book) fracture, what crucial posterior ligaments are disrupted? Answer: Both the anterior AND posterior sacroiliac (SI) ligaments, rendering the hemipelvis completely unstable rotationally and vertically.
* Trap: Assuming a stable pelvic X-ray in a young patient means no serious injury after a high-speed RTA. Pearl: The pelvis can spring open at impact, tearing ligaments and vessels, then recoil back to a near-normal position on a static AP X-ray.
* Pitfall: Missing a sciatic nerve injury before reducing a posterior wall acetabular fracture or hip dislocation. Always document active ankle dorsiflexion and EHL function (peroneal division) before any intervention.
* Viva Question: What is the radiographic "Spur Sign" on a pelvic CT/X-ray? Answer: Pathognomonic for a Both Column acetabular fracture; it represents the intact ilium projecting posteriorly above the displaced acetabular columns.
* Trap: Leaving a pelvic binder on for 3 days. Pearl: Binders should be removed or converted to external fixation within 24-48 hours to prevent severe skin necrosis over the greater trochanters.
