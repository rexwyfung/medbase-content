# Periprosthetic Joint Infection (人工關節周圍感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Periprosthetic Joint Infection (PJI)
* **Periprosthetic Joint Infection (PJI) (人工關節周圍感染)**: Deep infection involving a joint prosthesis and adjacent tissue.
* Catastrophic complication of **Total Joint Arthroplasty (TJA) (人工關節置換術)**.
* Major cause of revision arthroplasty globally and locally in HK HA.

### Pathogenesis & Biofilm Formation
* Planktonic (free-floating) bacteria adhere to inert implant surface.
* Bacteria produce glycocalyx matrix → forms **Biofilm (生物膜)**.
* Biofilm shields bacteria from host immune system (macrophages) & systemic antibiotics.
* Dominant pathogens: Coagulase-negative *Staphylococcus* (*S. epidermidis*), *S. aureus*, *Streptococcus spp.*, Gram -ve bacilli.

### Mechanism of Infection (Routes of Entry)
* **Direct Inoculation**: Intra-operative contamination (Accounts for early/delayed PJI).
* **Hematogenous Spread**: Bloodborne seeding from remote site (e.g., dental abscess, UTI, cellulitis) → Accounts for late acute PJI.
* **Contiguous Spread**: From adjacent osteomyelitis or infected soft tissue flap.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Implant Interface & Avascular Dead Space
* Metal/Polyethylene implants lack microcirculation.
* Cement-bone interface creates an avascular "dead space".
* Inadequate vascularity ↓ local penetration of systemic antibiotics.
* **Polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) (骨水泥)** acts as a massive foreign body, lowering the local inoculum threshold required to establish infection.

### Biomechanical Consequences of PJI
* Inflammatory response → ↑ osteoclast activation → severe **Osteolysis (骨質溶解)**.
* Destruction of bone-cement or bone-implant integration → loss of mechanical fixation.
* Result: Component micromotion, eccentric polyethylene wear, and gross **Aseptic/Septic Loosening (組件鬆動)**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Local Physical Findings (Look)
* **Sinus tract** communicating directly with prosthesis (Pathognomonic for PJI).
* Erythema, induration, and swelling around the surgical scar.
* Wound dehiscence or ongoing purulent drainage (particularly in early post-op).

### Local Physical Findings (Feel & Move)
* **Feel**:
  * Localized warmth (calor) over the joint.
  * Tense capsular effusion.
  * Tenderness along the component-bone interface.
* **Move**:
  * ↓ Range of Motion (ROM) 2° to massive effusion/capsular distension.
  * Pain significantly exacerbated by active/passive micromotion.

### Systemic & Neurovascular (NV) Status
* **Systemic**: Fever, chills, rigors (More prominent in early post-op or acute hematogenous spread; often ABSENT in chronic low-grade PJI).
* **NV**: Distal pulses and neurology typically intact, unless massive abscess causes mass effect on local neurovascular bundles.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems (Eponyms & Grading)

### Tsukayama Classification of PJI (Timing-Based)
| Type | Name | Timing | Pathogenesis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Positive Intra-op Culture | Incidental finding | Asymptomatic; 2+ positive cultures during revision for presumed aseptic loosening |
| **II** | Early Post-operative | $<4$ weeks post-op | Peri-operative direct inoculation |
| **III** | Acute Hematogenous | Any time | Hematogenous seeding of a previously well-functioning, painless joint |
| **IV** | Late Chronic | $>4$ weeks post-op | Low-grade/indolent direct inoculation with delayed clinical presentation |

### Musculoskeletal Infection Society (MSIS) Criteria for PJI
| Criteria Type | Requirements (Need 1 Major OR $\ge3$ Minor) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Major** | 1. Sinus tract communicating with prosthesis <br> 2. Phenotypically identical pathogen isolated from $\ge2$ separate tissue/fluid cultures |
| **Minor** | 1. Elevated serum ESR & CRP <br> 2. Elevated synovial WBC count <br> 3. Elevated synovial PMN % <br> 4. Presence of purulence in affected joint <br> 5. Isolation of organism in 1 periprosthetic culture <br> 6. $>5$ neutrophils per HPF in $\ge5$ high-power fields on histology |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plain Radiographs (X-rays) for PJI
* **Views**: AP & Lateral of affected joint (Must visualize entire prosthesis length).
* **Findings (Early)**: Often completely normal.
* **Findings (Late/Chronic)**:
  * Rapidly progressive, diffuse radiolucent lines $>2$mm at bone-cement interface.
  * Focal osteolysis / endosteal scalloping.
  * Periosteal reaction or new bone formation (Infection hallmark).
  * Prosthesis subsidence / migration.

### Joint Aspiration (Arthrocentesis) & Fluid Analysis
* Critical diagnostic step. Must be done strictly aseptically.
* **Synovial WBC**: Threshold is MUCH lower than native joint septic arthritis. (PJI threshold: TKA $>10,000/\mu L$; THA $>3,000/\mu L$).
* **Synovial PMN %**: $>90\%$ in TKA; $>80\%$ in THA.
* **Gram Stain & Culture**: Hold cultures for 14 days to detect slow-growers (*Cutibacterium acnes*).
* **Biomarkers**: Alpha-defensin (α-defensin) immunoassay, Leukocyte esterase test strips.

### Advanced Imaging & Serology
* **Blood**: ↑ ESR, ↑ CRP (Initial screening tests).
* **Bone Scan (Triple Phase Tc-99m + Indium-111 WBC)**: High sensitivity; helps differentiate septic from aseptic loosening if inflammatory markers are equivocal.
* **CT/MRI**: Limited utility due to metal artifact. MARS-MRI may identify peri-articular abscesses.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute ED Interventions for Suspected PJI
* Keep patient NPO for potential emergency theatre.
* Immobilize joint with splint/brace to minimize pain.
* **HOLD empirical IV antibiotics** UNTIL deep tissue/fluid cultures are obtained (unless patient is hemodynamically unstable/septic).
* Draw peripheral blood cultures $\times2$ if patient is febrile ($>38^\circ C$).
* Urgent Orthopaedic consult for diagnostic joint aspiration.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Indications for Non-Operative vs. Operative Management
| Management | Indications |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative (Suppressive ABx)** | Patient medically unfit for surgery; short life expectancy; implant well-fixed without soft-tissue compromise; organism highly susceptible to oral ABx. |
| **Operative (Surgery)** | Fit for surgery; systemic sepsis; loose implants; failure of suppressive ABx; presence of sinus tract. |

### Operative Technique 1: DAIR
* **DAIR**: Debridement, Antibiotics, and Implant Retention.
* **Indications**: Acute early PJI ($<4$ weeks post-op) OR Acute hematogenous PJI ($<3$ weeks of symptoms) WITH well-fixed components and healthy soft tissue.
* **Principles**:
  * Open arthrotomy.
  * Aggressive synovectomy & copious high-volume pulsatile lavage.
  * **Modular exchange**: Must exchange polyethylene liner/head to access posterior recesses.
  * Retention of fixed metal components.
  * Post-op: 6 weeks of pathogen-specific IV antibiotics.

### Operative Technique 2: Two-Stage Revision Arthroplasty
* **Gold standard** in HK HA for delayed/chronic PJI or failed DAIR.
* **Indications**: Chronic PJI, presence of biofilm, loose implants, sinus tract.
* **Stage 1 (Explantation)**:
  * Remove ALL implants and cement.
  * Radical debridement of dead bone/scar.
  * Insert **Antibiotic-loaded PMMA Spacer (抗生素骨水泥)** (typically Vancomycin + Tobramycin/Gentamicin) to maintain soft tissue tension and deliver high local ABx.
  * Give 6 weeks of targeted IV antibiotics.
* **Stage 2 (Re-implantation)**:
  * Performed after ESR/CRP normalize and repeat aspiration is culture -ve.
  * Remove spacer, re-debride, and implant new revision prosthesis (often requiring stems/wedges/bone graft).

### Salvage Procedures
* **One-Stage Revision**: Direct exchange. Strictly for healthy hosts, intact bone stock, known highly sensitive pathogen, and NO sinus tract.
* **Resection Arthroplasty**: E.g., Girdlestone procedure (hip). For medically frail patients with severe bone loss.
* **Arthrodesis**: Joint fusion (more common in knee).
* **Amputation**: Above-knee amputation (AKA) for life-threatening necrotizing sepsis or intractable recurrent PJI.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-DAIR Rehabilitation
* Treat as standard primary arthroplasty rehab.
* Early mobilization and ROM exercises.
* Weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT) if components remain mechanically stable.

### Post-Stage 1 (Antibiotic Spacer) Rehabilitation
* Precautions depend on spacer type (Static vs. Articulating).
* **Articulating Spacers**: Allow limited ROM. Partial weight-bearing (PWB) with walking frame.
* **Static Spacers**: Joint immobilized (e.g., knee cylinder cast). Non-weight-bearing (NWB) or touch-down weight-bearing (TDWB).
* High risk of spacer fracture, dislocation, or bone erosion with excessive loading.

### Post-Stage 2 (Re-implantation) Rehabilitation
* Slower progression compared to primary TJA.
* WBAT often permitted with fully porous-coated revision stems.
* PWB/NWB for 6-8 weeks if massive structural allografts or impaction bone grafting utilized.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Systemic & Medical Complications
* Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS) / Septic Shock.
* Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) 2° to prolonged nephrotoxic IV antibiotics (Vancomycin + Aminoglycosides).
* DVT/PE due to prolonged immobility and multiple surgeries.

### Local Operative Complications
* **Recurrent PJI**: Failure of 2-stage revision requiring lifelong suppression or amputation.
* Periprosthetic fracture during component explantation/cement removal.
* Massive bone stock loss requiring tumor megaprostheses.
* Spacer dislocation (Highly common in hip articulating spacers).
* Extensor mechanism disruption (especially during repeat TKA approaches).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the defining feature of a chronic PJI that dictates the need for complete implant removal? Answer: The formation of a mature biofilm on the implant surface, which cannot be eradicated by systemic antibiotics or simple debridement, necessitating complete explantation (Two-stage revision).
* Trap: Starting empirical antibiotics immediately in the ED for a swollen, painful TKA without systemic shock. Pearl: Always withhold antibiotics until definitive deep tissue cultures or synovial fluid aspirates are obtained to avoid causing a diagnostically difficult culture-negative PJI.
* Pitfall: Relying on native joint septic arthritis synovial WBC thresholds ($>50,000/\mu L$) to diagnose PJI. The cutoff for PJI is much lower (typically $>3,000$ for THA and $>10,000$ for TKA) due to the lack of physiological synovium.
* Viva Question: Why must the modular polyethylene liner be exchanged during a DAIR procedure? Answer: To physically debride the biofilm adhering to the polyethylene and to gain essential surgical access to the posterior recesses of the joint for complete lavage.
* Trap: Assuming a negative plain radiograph rules out PJI. Pearl: X-rays are often completely normal in acute/early PJI; radiolucency and osteolysis take months to manifest radiographically.
