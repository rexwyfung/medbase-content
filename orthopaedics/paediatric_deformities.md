# Paediatric Deformities: CTEV, Pes Planus, Torticollis (小兒骨科畸形：馬蹄內翻足、扁平足、斜頸症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Congenital Talipes Equinovarus (CTEV) (先天性馬蹄內翻足): Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Complex 3D structural deformity of foot & ankle. Mnemonic **CAVE**: **C**avus (midfoot), **A**dductus (forefoot), **V**arus (hindfoot), **E**quinus (hindfoot).
* **Epidemiology:** 1-2/1000 live births in HK. M:F = 2:1. 50% bilateral.
* **Aetiology:** Idiopathic (most common) vs Syndromic/Teratologic (e.g., Arthrogryposis, Spina Bifida, Larsen syndrome).
* **Genetics:** Polygenic inheritance. 3% risk if one sibling affected; 33% if both parents affected.

### Pes Planus (扁平足): Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Loss/collapse of medial longitudinal arch (MLA) of foot.
* **Epidemiology:** Physiological (normal) in most toddlers <4 yrs due to fat pad & ligamentous laxity. Prevalence ↓ with age.
* **Aetiology:** 
  * Flexible: Ligamentous laxity (Down syndrome, Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos), tight **Achilles tendon (跟腱)**.
  * Rigid: Tarsal coalition (距骨聯合), congenital vertical talus (CVT), inflammatory arthritis.

### Congenital Muscular Torticollis (CMT) (先天性肌性斜頸): Definition & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Postural deformity of neck with head tilt towards affected side & chin rotation to contralateral side.
* **Epidemiology:** 1/250 live births. Associated with Developmental Dysplasia of Hip (DDH) in up to 20% cases.
* **Aetiology:** Intrauterine crowding (breech presentation), birth trauma → ischaemia/fibrosis of **Sternocleidomastoid (SCM) muscle (胸鎖乳突肌)**.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### CTEV: Pathoanatomy & Deforming Forces
* **Tarsal Bone Dysplasia:** **Talus (距骨)** neck shortened & deviated medially/plantarly. **Navicular (舟狀骨)** displaced medially.
* **Muscle Contractures:** 
  * Posteromedial structures tight: **Tibialis Posterior**, FDL, FHL, **Achilles Tendon**.
  * Lateral structures (peroneals) stretched/weak.
* **Vascular:** Diminished/absent **Anterior Tibial Artery (脛前動脈)** in 85% of cases (relies on posterior tibial supply).

### Pes Planus: Arch Stabilizers
* **Dynamic Stabilizer:** **Tibialis Posterior (脛後肌)** (inverts/plantarflexes, locks transverse tarsal joint).
* **Static Stabilizers:** **Spring Ligament (plantar calcaneonavicular) (彈簧韌帶)**, plantar fascia, short plantar ligaments.
* **Biomechanics:** Arch collapse → hindfoot valgus → forefoot abduction → altered weight-bearing (midfoot pain).

### Torticollis: SCM Anatomy
* **SCM Attachments:** Originates at manubrium & medial clavicle; inserts at mastoid process.
* **Action:** Unilateral contraction → ipsilateral lateral flexion + contralateral axial rotation.
* **Pathology:** Fibrous tumour (fibromatosis) forms within SCM (weeks 2-4) → replaces muscle with inelastic fibrous tissue → contracture.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### CTEV: LFM & NV Assessment
* **Look:** 
  * Foot: **CAVE** deformity (Cavus, Adductus, Varus, Equinus).
  * Leg: Calf muscle atrophy (ipsilateral), deep medial & posterior skin creases.
* **Feel:** 
  * Empty heel pad (calcaneus pulled up/in).
  * Prominent lateral head of **Talus**.
* **Move:** 
  * Rigid deformities (cannot be passively corrected to neutral).
  * Assess spine (rule out sacral dimple/spina bifida).
* **NV:** Intact distally, check capillary refill.

### Pes Planus: LFM & Special Tests
* **Look:** 
  * Standing: Flat arch, hindfoot valgus, **"Too many toes" sign (多趾徵)** from behind.
  * Gait: In-toeing or out-toeing, early heel rise if Achilles tight.
* **Feel:** Talar head prominent medially. Tenderness over navicular/tibialis posterior.
* **Move:** 
  * **Jack's Toe Raise Test:** Passive dorsiflexion of hallux → reconstitutes arch (+ve in flexible).
  * **Tip-toe Test:** Hindfoot corrects from valgus to varus on tip-toe (+ve in flexible).
  * Rigid flatfoot: Arch fails to reconstitute; restricted subtalar motion.
* **NV:** Intact.

### Torticollis: Physical Findings
* **Look:** 
  * Head tilted to affected side, chin rotated to normal side.
  * **Plagiocephaly (斜頭畸形)** (flattening of occiput on contralateral side).
  * Facial asymmetry (hemifacial hypoplasia on affected side).
* **Feel:** Firm, painless, non-tender "olive-like" mass in SCM (resolves by 3-6 months, leaving tight band).
* **Move:** ↓ passive & active cervical rotation and lateral flexion.
* **Associated:** Check hips for DDH (Ortolani/Barlow tests).

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### CTEV: Pirani Scoring System
* **Use:** Assesses severity & monitors progress (Scale 0-6; 0 = normal, 6 = severe).

| Component | Signs (Score 0, 0.5, or 1 each) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hindfoot Score (HS)** | Posterior crease, Empty heel, Rigid equinus |
| **Midfoot Score (MS)** | Medial crease, Curvature of lateral border, Reducibility of lateral talar head |

### Pes Planus: Aetiological Classification
| Type | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Flexible (生理性)** | Arch present non-weight bearing, disappears on standing. Mobile subtalar joint. |
| **Rigid (病理性)** | Arch absent always. Stiff subtalar joint. Suspect tarsal coalition or CVT. |

### Torticollis: Clinical Subtypes
| Grade | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Postural** | Mildest. Postural preference, no muscle tightness, no mass. |
| **Muscular** | SCM tightness and restricted ROM, no palpable mass. |
| **SCM Mass** | Palpable fibrotic "olive" tumour in SCM, severe ROM restriction. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### CTEV: Radiographic Assessment
* **Indications:** Usually NOT required for diagnosis. Used for atypical/resistant cases or post-op evaluation.
* **Views:** Simulated weight-bearing AP & Lateral foot.
* **Key Findings:** Parallelism of Talus & Calcaneus (loss of normal talocalcaneal divergence). **Kite's Angle** < 20° (Normal 20-40°).

### Pes Planus: Weight-Bearing Radiographs
* **Views:** Weight-bearing AP & Lateral foot, Oblique (for coalition).
* **Lateral View:** 
  * **Meary's Angle:** Talus-1st Metatarsal line (>4° convex downwards = flatfoot).
  * **Calcaneal Pitch:** <15° indicates flatfoot.
* **Oblique View:** "Anteater sign" (calcaneonavicular coalition), "C-sign" (talocalcaneal coalition).

### Torticollis: Imaging Protocols
* **Ultrasound Neck:** Diagnostic for SCM mass (fusiform thickening, homogeneous mass). Rule out solid lymph nodes.
* **AP/Lat C-Spine X-ray:** Rule out congenital cervical spine anomalies (e.g., Klippel-Feil syndrome, hemivertebrae, atlantoaxial rotatory subluxation).
* **Pelvis X-ray/US:** Mandatory screening for DDH.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### CTEV: Neonatal Initial Management
* **Action:** Reassure parents. Urgent referral to Paediatric O&T clinic.
* **Avoid:** Strictly advise parents against forceful stretching or DIY bandaging.
* **Screening:** Full neonatal examination to rule out syndromic causes (spine, hips, hands).

### Pes Planus: Initial Clinic Encounter
* **Action:** Reassurance for children <4 years (physiological). Education on appropriate footwear (supportive heel counter, wide toe box).
* **Referral:** Red flags warranting specialist O&T referral: unilateral, painful, rigid, severe calf tightness.

### Torticollis: Neonatal Advice
* **Action:** Paediatric physio referral. 
* **Positioning:** Encourage "Tummy Time" when awake. Position crib toys to encourage child to actively turn head towards affected side.
* **Screening:** Clinical hip exam in clinic.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### CTEV: Ponseti Method & Surgical Algorithm
| Approach | Indications | Technique |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ponseti Method (Non-Op)** | Gold standard 1° Tx. Start at 1-2 weeks of life. | Serial long-leg casts weekly. Corrects **CAVE** in order: **C**avus (elevate 1st ray) → **A**dductus/ **V**arus (abduct foot around head of talus) → **E**quinus. |
| **Percutaneous Tendo-Achilles Tenotomy (Op)** | Required in ~85-90% to correct residual **Equinus** after serial casting. | Performed under LA in clinic (neonates) or GA. Followed by final cast for 3 weeks. |
| **Tibialis Anterior Tendon Transfer (TATT)** | Dynamic supination during swing phase (relapse in toddlers >2.5 yrs). | Transfer whole TA tendon to lateral cuneiform. |
| **Extensive Soft Tissue Release (Op)** | Resistant/Syndromic CTEV failing Ponseti. | Posteromedial Release (PMR). High risk of stiffness. |

### Pes Planus: Management Algorithm
| Approach | Indications | Technique |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Op** | Asymptomatic flexible flatfoot. | Observation. Symptomatic flexible: UCBL orthotics, medial arch supports. |
| **Operative (Soft Tissue)** | Tight Achilles with flexible flatfoot. | Gastrocnemius recession / Tendo-Achilles lengthening (TAL). |
| **Operative (Bony)** | Painful, rigid flatfoot >8 yrs (e.g., failed conservative, severe structural collapse). | Calcaneal lengthening osteotomy (Evans). Subtalar arthroereisis. Triple arthrodesis (salvage for severe rigid adults). |

### Torticollis: Non-Op vs Operative Management
| Approach | Indications | Technique |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Op** | 1° Tx for infants <1 year. | Intensive physiotherapy (passive stretching, active positioning). 90% success if started early. |
| **Operative** | Failed conservative Tx >1 year of age. Established facial asymmetry/plagiocephaly. Tight band with ROM deficit >15°. | Unipolar or Bipolar **SCM Release** (open tenotomy/Z-plasty). |

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### CTEV: Bracing Protocol
* **Device:** **Denis Browne Splint** / Foot Abduction Orthosis (FAO) applied immediately after final cast removal.
* **Position:** Feet externally rotated 70° (affected) or 40° (normal), ankles dorsiflexed 15°.
* **Regimen:** 23 hours/day for first 3 months → Night & nap time (12-14 hours/day) until age 4-5 years. Strict HA protocol; non-compliance is the #1 cause of relapse.

### Pes Planus: Physio Protocol
* **Stretching:** Aggressive Achilles tendon and gastrocnemius stretching exercises.
* **Strengthening:** Tibialis posterior and intrinsic foot muscle strengthening (towel scrunches, marble pick-ups).

### Torticollis: Stretching Regimen
* **Regimen:** Physiotherapist-guided parental stretching.
* **Technique:** Stabilize ipsilateral shoulder → passively laterally flex neck to normal side → rotate chin to affected side. Hold for 10-15 seconds, repeat frequently daily.
* **Post-Op:** Cervical collar (TOT collar) to maintain position, followed by intensive ROM physio.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### CTEV: Treatment Complications
* **Relapse:** Most common (up to 50% if FAO bracing non-compliant). Usually presents as recurrent equinus or dynamic supination.
* **Rocker-Bottom Foot:** Iatrogenic. Caused by premature attempted correction of equinus before hindfoot varus/midfoot cavus is corrected → breaks the midfoot.
* **Cast Sores:** Pressure ulcers over talar head or heel.
* **Overcorrection:** Leading to iatrogenic pes planovalgus.

### Pes Planus: Progressive Complications
* **Plantar Fasciitis:** Over-stretching of plantar fascia.
* **Posterior Tibial Tendon Dysfunction (PTTD):** In adulthood, chronic stress on dynamic stabilizer.
* **Osteoarthritis:** Talo-navicular and subtalar joint degeneration in chronic rigid cases.

### Torticollis: Neglect Complications
* **Craniofacial Asymmetry:** Permanent flattening of face on affected side if uncorrected >1 year.
* **Plagiocephaly:** Unresolved positional skull deformity.
* **Cervical Scoliosis:** Secondary spinal curvature to compensate for visual horizon.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the sequence of correction in the Ponseti method for CTEV? Answer: CAVE - Cavus (first), then Adductus & Varus (simultaneously by abducting forefoot around talus), then Equinus (last, often requiring tenotomy).
* Trap: Recommending Denis Browne bracing immediately for an untreated clubfoot in the clinic. Pearl: Bracing is ONLY used to maintain correction after serial casting and tenotomy have achieved a plantigrade foot.
* Pitfall: Attempting to forcibly dorsiflex the ankle to correct equinus in a cast before varus is corrected. This causes a midfoot break and an iatrogenic rocker-bottom foot.
* Viva Question: A 2-year-old child presents with flat feet. The arch appears when he stands on his toes. What is the diagnosis and management? Answer: Flexible pes planus. Reassure parents, as this is physiological and usually resolves by age 4. No orthotics required unless symptomatic.
* Trap: Diagnosing congenital muscular torticollis based solely on a head tilt without checking the hips. Pearl: Up to 20% of CMT cases have concomitant Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH). Always perform Ortolani/Barlow tests and request pelvic US/X-ray.
* Pitfall: Releasing the SCM surgically in a 3-month-old. Surgery is strictly contraindicated before 1 year of age as >90% resolve with diligent physiotherapy.
