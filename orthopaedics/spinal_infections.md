# Spinal Infections (脊柱感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Definition of Spondylodiscitis & Spondylitis
* **Spondylodiscitis**: Infection primarily involving the intervertebral disc and adjacent vertebral endplates (typical of pyogenic infections).
* **Spondylitis**: Infection primarily involving the vertebral body (typical of granulomatous/TB infections).
* **Pott's Disease (脊柱結核)**: Tuberculous spondylitis, classically causing anterior wedging and gibbus deformity.

### Organisms & Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Pyogenic**: *Staphylococcus aureus* (>50%), *Streptococcus spp.*, Gram-ve bacilli (*E. coli*, *Pseudomonas* - associated with IVDU/UTI).
* **Granulomatous**: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Endemic in HK → high index of suspicion required).
* **Fungal/Atypical**: *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Brucella* (Immunocompromised/HIV).
* **Bimodal Age Distribution**: Peaks in childhood and >50 years old.

### Mechanisms of Pathogen Spread
* **Hematogenous Spread (Most Common)**: Arterial route (childhood) or venous route (adults).
* **Direct Inoculation**: Post-operative (e.g., discectomy, spinal fusion), post-procedural (lumbar puncture, epidural block).
* **Contiguous Spread**: Adjacent infection (e.g., retropharyngeal abscess, aortic graft infection).

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Vascular Supply to the Spine
* **Segmental Arteries**: Bifurcate to supply adjacent vertebral metaphyses. End-arteries in adults → trapping of septic emboli at endplates.
* **Batson's Venous Plexus (巴特森靜脈叢)**: Valveless, high-capacity, low-pressure system bypassing the caval system. Allows retrograde spread of infection from pelvic/GU tract (e.g., UTI, prostate surgery) into the lumbar spine during ↑ intra-abdominal pressure.

### Pathoanatomy: Pyogenic vs. Tuberculous Spread
* **Pyogenic Spread**:
  * Originates at **metaphyseal endplate** (rich blood supply).
  * Rapidly produces proteolytic enzymes → **early destruction of avascular intervertebral disc**.
  * Spreads to adjacent vertebrae across the disc space.
* **Tuberculous Spread**:
  * Originates in **anteroinferior vertebral body**.
  * Lacks proteolytic enzymes → **late preservation of intervertebral disc**.
  * Spreads subligamentously beneath **Anterior Longitudinal Ligament (ALL)** to involve multiple adjacent segments (skip lesions).

### Biomechanics of Spinal Infection
* **Anterior Column Destruction**: Both TB and pyogenic infections predominantly destroy the anterior vertebral body.
* **Progressive Kyphosis (Kyphotic Deformity)**: Anterior collapse + intact posterior column elements acting as a tension band → progressive angular kyphosis (gibbus).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Look (Inspection)
* **Gibbus Deformity**: Sharp angular kyphosis (classic for Pott's disease).
* **Posture**: Patient refuses to sit or stand, marked rigidity.
* **Soft Tissue**: Cold abscesses (e.g., pointing in groin from psoas abscess, lumbar triangle, or retropharyngeal space).
* **Skin**: Sinus tracts (+ve discharge), surgical scars, IV drug tracks.
* **General**: Cachexia, severe weight loss.

### Feel (Palpation)
* **Localized Tenderness**: Severe tenderness over involved spinous processes.
* **Percussion**: +ve tap tenderness over the spine.
* **Step-off**: Palpable posterior bony prominence due to anterior collapse.
* **Paravertebral Spasm**: Intense, board-like muscle spasm.

### Move (Range of Motion)
* **ROM**: Globally ↓ ROM in all planes due to severe pain and muscle spasm.

### Neurovascular (NV) Examination
* **Motor**: Complete myotome testing. Assess for progressive paraparesis.
* **Sensory**: Pinprick and light touch along dermatomes. Check saddle anesthesia (S2-S4).
* **Reflexes**:
  * UMN Lesion (Cord Compression): Hyperreflexia, +ve Babinski, sustained clonus.
  * LMN Lesion (Cauda Equina/Nerve Root): Hyporeflexia, flaccid weakness.
* **Autonomic**: Rectal tone (PR exam), perianal sensation, post-void residual (PVR) volume.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Pathological Classification (Pyogenic vs. Granulomatous)
| Feature | Pyogenic Spondylodiscitis | Tuberculous Spondylitis (Pott's) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Acute / Subacute | Insidious / Chronic |
| **Pain** | Severe, constant, mechanical | Mild/moderate, dull ache |
| **Systemic Features** | High fever, chills, rigors | Low-grade fever, night sweats, weight loss |
| **Disc Involvement** | Early destruction | Late preservation |
| **Spine Level** | Lumbar > Thoracic > Cervical | Thoracic > Lumbar > Cervical |

### ASIA (American Spinal Injury Association) Impairment Scale
| Grade | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Complete: No motor/sensory function in sacral segments S4-S5. |
| **B** | Incomplete: Sensory preserved, but no motor function below neurological level. |
| **C** | Incomplete: Motor preserved below neuro level, >50% key muscles have grade < 3. |
| **D** | Incomplete: Motor preserved below neuro level, ≥50% key muscles have grade ≥ 3. |
| **E** | Normal: Motor and sensory functions are normal. |

## 5. Radiological & Laboratory Investigations

### Laboratory Investigations
* **Infectious Markers**: ↑ ESR, ↑ CRP. (Pyogenic: ↑↑ CRP, ↑ ESR; TB: ↑↑ ESR, CRP may be normal/mild ↑).
* **Hematology**: CBC (Leukocytosis with ↑ neutrophils in pyogenic; relative lymphocytosis in TB).
* **Microbiology**:
  * Blood cultures x3 sets (aerobic/anaerobic) at febrile peaks.
  * TB specific: T-SPOT.TB / QuantiFERON-TB Gold, early morning urine x3 for AFB.

### Radiological Imaging
* **Plain Radiographs (AP & Lateral Spine)**:
  * Pyogenic: Disc space narrowing, endplate erosion/fuzziness, osteopenia.
  * TB: Anterior wedging, vertebral collapse (gibbus), calcified paravertebral shadows (psoas abscess).
  * *Note: X-rays lag behind clinical symptoms by 2-4 weeks.*
* **MRI Whole Spine with Gadolinium (Gold Standard)**:
  * T1: ↓ signal (dark) in disc and adjacent bone marrow.
  * T2 / STIR: ↑ signal (bright) representing edema.
  * Contrast: Enhancement of disc, endplates, and epidural abscess/phlegmon. Check for skip lesions.

### Tissue Diagnosis
* **CT-Guided Needle Biopsy**: Mandatory for causative organism identification. Send for Gram stain, C&S, AFB smear, TB culture, fungal culture, histology, and TB PCR (GeneXpert).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Resuscitation & Immobilization
* **Resuscitation**: ABCDE protocol. IV fluid challenge if septic shock.
* **Immobilization**: Strict log-roll precautions to prevent neurological deterioration. Fit with immediate rigid orthosis (e.g., TLSO or Miami J collar).

### Antimicrobial Stewardship Algorithm
* **Hemodynamically Stable + No Neuro Deficit**: STRICTLY HOLD empirical antibiotics. Wait for CT-guided biopsy/blood cultures to yield organism.
* **Hemodynamically Unstable OR Progressive Neuro Deficit**: Draw blood cultures STAT → commence empirical broad-spectrum IV antibiotics immediately (e.g., IV Vancomycin + Ceftriaxone) → arrange emergency surgical decompression/biopsy.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Non-Operative Management Indications
| Indication | Management Plan |
| :--- | :--- |
| Known pathogen, mechanically stable | Antimicrobial therapy + rigid orthosis. |
| No neurological deficit | Serial clinical/inflammatory marker monitoring. |
| High surgical risk (frail, comorbidities) | Conservative management. |

### Medical Therapy Protocols (Hong Kong HA Guidelines)
* **Pyogenic**: Pathogen-directed IV antibiotics for 2-6 weeks, followed by PO step-down. Total duration: 6-12 weeks based on clinical and CRP/ESR trend.
* **Tuberculous (DOTS Regimen)**: Total 9-12 months.
  * Intensive phase (2 months): Isoniazid (H), Rifampicin (R), Pyrazinamide (Z), Ethambutol (E) + Vitamin B6.
  * Continuation phase (7-10 months): HR.

### Operative Management Indications
| Indication Category | Specific Triggers |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Neurological** | Progressive neurological deficit, epidural abscess causing cord compression. |
| **Mechanical** | Spinal instability, progressive kyphotic deformity (e.g., >30°). |
| **Medical Failure** | Unresponsive to Abx, intractable pain, sepsis, failure to yield organism via percutaneous biopsy. |

### Surgical Principles & Techniques
* **Anterior Approach (Hong Kong Operation)**: Gold standard for extensive anterior column destruction. Radical anterior debridement (corpectomy), autologous strut bone grafting (rib or iliac crest).
* **Posterior Instrumentation**: Added for mechanical stabilization (pedicle screw fixation). Often performed via a single posterior or combined AP approach.
* **Minimally Invasive**: Endoscopic/VATS debridement + percutaneous pedicle screws (for select stable patients with primarily abscess).

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Orthotic Management
* **Rigid Bracing**: TLSO (Thoracolumbosacral orthosis) or Cervical Collar for 3-6 months until radiological fusion/consolidation is seen.

### Mobilization & Physiotherapy
* **Early Mobilization**: Once mechanically stabilized (surgically or via rigid brace), sit out of bed to prevent chest/DVT complications.
* **Neurological Rehab**: Aggressive PT/OT for patients with residual deficits (gait training, wheelchair transfers).

### Monitoring
* Follow-up at 2 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year.
* Serial CRP/ESR monitoring (CRP normalizes rapidly in successful pyogenic Tx; ESR remains elevated longer, especially in TB).

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Neurological & Mechanical Complications
* **Pott's Paraplegia**:
  * Early onset (Active disease): Inflammatory edema, epidural abscess, caseous material, or mechanical instability compressing the cord. (Better prognosis with decompression).
  * Late onset (Healed disease): Stretching of cord over progressive severe bony kyphotic ridge, or reactivation of TB. (Poor prognosis).
* **Severe Kyphosis**: Compensatory hyperlordosis elsewhere, restrictive lung disease, late cord compromise.

### Infection-Related Complications
* **Chronic Sinus Formation**: Persistent purulent discharge, risk of secondary bacterial superinfection in TB tracts.
* **Sepsis / Endocarditis**: Hematogenous spread to other organs (valves, joints).
* **Non-union / Pseudarthrosis**: Failure of surgical fusion construct requiring revision.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why does pyogenic spondylodiscitis cause early disc destruction while TB spine preserves the disc initially? Answer: Pyogenic organisms produce proteolytic enzymes that rapidly destroy the avascular disc. TB lacks these enzymes, preserving the disc until late stages.
* Viva Question: Where do you typically see cold abscesses in Pott's disease of the lumbar spine? Answer: Psoas abscess pointing in the groin (below inguinal ligament), or posterior lumbar triangle of Petit.
* Trap: Starting empirical antibiotics in a stable patient presenting with suspected spondylodiscitis in the ED. Pearl: Hold antibiotics until blood cultures and a CT-guided biopsy are obtained, unless the patient is septic or has a rapidly progressive neurological deficit.
* Trap: Assuming normal X-rays rule out early spinal infection. Pearl: X-ray changes lag behind infection by up to 2-4 weeks; MRI with gadolinium is the gold standard for early detection.
* Pitfall: Depending solely on CRP to track the response of Tuberculous spondylitis. While CRP is excellent for pyogenic infections, ESR is often more reliably elevated and useful for tracking disease activity in spinal TB.
