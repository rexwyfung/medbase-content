# Other Foot and Ankle Conditions (其他足踝疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Plantar Fasciitis: Definition, MOI & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** 1° degenerative condition (fasciosis, not purely -itis) of plantar fascia at medial calcaneal tuberosity insertion.
* **MOI:** Repetitive microtrauma → microtears → collagen degeneration & angiofibroblastic hyperplasia.
* **Epidemiology:** Peak age 40-60. Risk factors: ↑ BMI, prolonged standing (HK service workers), pes planus (flatfoot), tight Achilles tendon.

### Morton's Neuroma: Definition, MOI & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Perineural fibrosis of common digital nerve (not a true tumor).
* **MOI:** Entrapment neuropathy. Intermetatarsal bursa swelling or tight deep transverse metatarsal ligament (DTML) compresses nerve during push-off phase of gait.
* **Epidemiology:** ♀:♂ = 4:1. Peak age 40-50. Associated with narrow toe-box shoes / high heels.

### Hallux Rigidus: Definition, MOI & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Osteoarthritis (OA) of 1st metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint.
* **MOI:** Idiopathic, repetitive dorsiflexion trauma, or structural (elevated 1st metatarsal / hypermobile 1st ray). ↑ joint contact stress → cartilage wear & dorsal osteophyte formation.
* **Epidemiology:** Most common arthritic condition of the foot. Peak age 30-60.

### Charcot Arthropathy: Definition, MOI & Epidemiology
* **Definition:** Progressive, painless, destructive arthropathy in patients with severe sensory neuropathy.
* **MOI:** 
  * **Neurotraumatic Theory:** Loss of protective sensation (LOPS) → unperceived repetitive microtrauma → joint destruction.
  * **Neurovascular Theory:** Autonomic neuropathy → sympathectomy → ↑ arteriovenous shunting → bone hyperemia → localized osteopenia.
* **Epidemiology:** Most commonly 2° to Diabetes Mellitus (DM). Occurs in 1% of all DM patients; ~30% in DM patients with severe neuropathy.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Plantar Fascia: Anatomy & Windlass Mechanism
* **Anatomy:** Originates at **medial calcaneal tuberosity**; divides into 5 slips inserting into proximal phalanges.
* **Windlass Mechanism:** Dorsiflexion of toes tightens plantar fascia → elevates medial longitudinal arch → structurally locks midfoot for rigid push-off.

### Morton's Neuroma: 3rd Web Space Anatomy
* **Pathoanatomy:** 80% occur in **3rd web space** (between 3rd/4th metatarsal heads).
* **Biomechanics:** 3rd space contains anastomosis of medial & lateral plantar nerves → thicker nerve bundle → tighter space beneath **deep transverse metatarsal ligament (DTML)**.

### Hallux Rigidus: 1st MTP Joint Biomechanics
* **Anatomy:** Articulation between 1st metatarsal head & proximal phalanx base. 
* **Biomechanics:** Normal gait requires minimum 65° dorsiflexion at 1st MTP. Dorsal impingement blocks roll-off → alters gait mechanics (forces transfer to lateral border).

### Charcot Arthropathy: Neurovascular Anatomy
* **Sensory:** Stocking-glove loss (Peripheral nerve degeneration).
* **Vascular:** Bounding dorsalis pedis/posterior tibial pulses due to autonomic dysfunction (hyperdynamic flow).
* **Biomechanics:** Midfoot collapse (Lisfranc/Chopart) → rocker-bottom deformity → ↑ peak plantar pressures on midfoot → ulceration.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Plantar Fasciitis: Assessment
* **Look:** Generally normal. +/- pes planus or pes cavus.
* **Feel:** Point tenderness exquisitely at **plantar medial calcaneal tuberosity**.
* **Move:** Pain exacerbated by passive dorsiflexion of toes (Windlass test). Tight Achilles (↓ ankle dorsiflexion).
* **NV:** Normal. Must rule out tarsal tunnel syndrome (Tinel's sign -ve).

### Morton's Neuroma: Assessment
* **Look:** Normal web spaces. No splaying of toes (if splaying present, suspect bursitis).
* **Feel:** **Mulder's Click** (+ve): Squeeze metatarsal heads together + plantarly press webspace → palpable click & sharp pain.
* **Move:** Normal ROM. 
* **NV:** Altered sensation (hyperalgesia or numbness) in adjacent sides of affected toes.

### Hallux Rigidus: Assessment
* **Look:** Swollen 1st MTP joint. Prominent **dorsal bunion** (osteophyte). Callus on lateral border of foot (weight shifting).
* **Feel:** Joint line tenderness (dorsal > plantar).
* **Move:** ↓↓ active & passive dorsiflexion (<20°). Pain at extreme range. Plantarflexion relatively preserved.
* **NV:** Normal.

### Charcot Arthropathy: Assessment
* **Look:** **Acute:** Erythematous, swollen, edematous foot. **Chronic:** "Rocker-bottom" deformity (midfoot collapse), bony prominences.
* **Feel:** Warmth (+2°C compared to contralateral side). Bounding pulses. 
* **Move:** Grossly unstable joints, painless crepitus.
* **NV:** Severe LOPS (unable to feel 10g Semmes-Weinstein monofilament). Diminished proprioception.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Coughlin & Shurnas Classification (Hallux Rigidus)

| Grade | Clinical Range of Motion (Dorsiflexion) | Radiographic Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | Normal (40-60°) | Normal joint space |
| **1** | Mild restriction (30-40°) | Dorsal osteophyte, minimal joint narrowing |
| **2** | Moderate restriction (14-30°) | Flattening of MT head, <50% joint narrowing |
| **3** | Severe restriction (<15°) | >50% joint narrowing, large osteophytes |
| **4** | <15° + **Pain at mid-range** | Same as Grade 3 |

### Eichenholtz Classification (Charcot Arthropathy)

| Stage | Name | Clinical Presentation | Radiographic Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | At Risk | Warm, swollen, erythema | Normal X-ray (potential osteopenia) |
| **1** | Fragmentation | Acute inflammation, hyperemic | Joint dislocation, periarticular debris, fragmentation |
| **2** | Coalescence | ↓ Swelling, ↓ warmth | Absorption of debris, early bony fusion |
| **3** | Consolidation | Cold, deformed, stable foot | Remodeling, mature arthrodesis |

### Brodsky Anatomic Classification (Charcot Arthropathy)
* **Type 1 (60%):** Lisfranc joint complex (Tarsometatarsal).
* **Type 2 (30%):** Chopart joint complex (Talonavicular & Calcaneocuboid).
* **Type 3 (10%):** Ankle joint / Subtalar joint.

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Plantar Fasciitis: Imaging Findings
* **Lateral Weight-Bearing (WB) X-ray:** May show calcaneal spur (traction osteophyte). *Note: Spur correlates poorly with pain intensity.*
* **USG:** Thickened plantar fascia (>4mm at insertion), hypoechoic changes.

### Morton's Neuroma: Imaging Findings
* **WB X-rays (AP/Lat):** Usually normal. Used to exclude stress # or arthritis.
* **USG:** Hypoechoic intermetatarsal mass.
* **MRI (T1/T2):** Dumbbell-shaped mass extending between metatarsal heads.

### Hallux Rigidus: Imaging Findings
* **WB X-rays (AP/Lat/Oblique):**
  * Dorsal osteophyte formation (lateral view).
  * 1st MTP joint space narrowing (symmetric or asymmetric).
  * Subchondral sclerosis and cysts.
  * Flattening of the 1st metatarsal head.

### Charcot Arthropathy: Imaging Findings
* **WB X-rays:** Look for the "5 Ds":
  * **D**estruction (cartilage/bone).
  * **D**islocation.
  * **D**isorganization.
  * **D**ebris (bony fragmentation).
  * **D**ecreased bone density (osteopenia).
* **MRI (with Contrast):** Used to differentiate Acute Charcot (subchondral bone marrow edema) from Osteomyelitis (diffuse marrow replacement, cortical destruction, abscess).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Plantar Fasciitis: Acute / Initial Management
* **Advice:** Relative rest, activity modification (avoid prolonged standing/running).
* **Pharmacology:** Short course oral NSAIDs.
* **Orthotics:** Silicone heel cups (shock absorption), arch supports.
* **Physical Therapy:** Cryotherapy (rolling foot on frozen water bottle).

### Morton's Neuroma: Acute / Initial Management
* **Footwear Modification:** Wide toe-box shoes, low heels.
* **Orthotics:** Metatarsal dome pad (placed *proximal* to the metatarsal heads to splay them apart).

### Hallux Rigidus: Acute / Initial Management
* **Footwear Modification:** Stiff-soled shoes, rocker-bottom sole (bypasses 1st MTP motion during toe-off).
* **Pharmacology:** Oral NSAIDs.
* **Taping:** Limit MTP dorsiflexion.

### Charcot Arthropathy: Acute ED / Clinic Management
* **Strict Immobilization:** **Total Contact Cast (TCC)** is the gold standard for Stage 0 & 1. Redistributes plantar pressures.
* **Weight Bearing Status:** Strict Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) with crutches/wheelchair.
* **Monitoring:** Serial temperature checks (target <2°C difference from normal foot) and TCC changes every 1-2 weeks as edema subsides.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Plantar Fasciitis: Definitive Management 
* **1st Line (Non-Op, >90% success):** 
  * Physiotherapy: Plantar fascia-specific stretching, eccentric Achilles stretching.
  * Night splints (maintains ankle in 90° to prevent fascial contracture overnight).
* **2nd Line:** 
  * Extracorporeal Shock Wave Therapy (ESWT) to induce neoangiogenesis.
  * Corticosteroid injection (approached medially to avoid plantar fat pad atrophy).
* **3rd Line (Operative, rare):**
  * **Indication:** Failure of conservative treatment >6-12 months.
  * **Procedure:** Partial Plantar Fasciotomy (endoscopic or open, releasing medial 1/3 only to prevent arch collapse).

### Morton's Neuroma: Definitive Management 
* **1st Line (Non-Op):** Steroid + Local Anesthetic injection into affected webspace. (Diagnostic & Therapeutic).
* **2nd Line (Operative):**
  * **Indication:** Persistent pain despite orthotics & injections.
  * **Procedure:** Neurectomy of common digital nerve.
  * **HK Protocol:** Dorsal approach preferred (avoids painful plantar scar). Proximal resection mandatory to allow stump to retract deep into intrinsic muscles.

### Hallux Rigidus: Definitive Algorithm

| Grade (Coughlin) | Non-Operative | Operative Principle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grade 1-2** | Stiff shoes, intra-articular steroid injection | **Cheilectomy:** Resection of dorsal 1/3 of MT head + dorsal osteophyte. |
| **Grade 3-4** | Rocker-bottom shoes, Carbon fiber insoles | **Arthrodesis (Gold Standard):** Fusion of 1st MTP (10-15° valgus, 15° dorsiflexion). |

* *Note on Arthroplasty (Silicone/Metallic):* High failure rate, reserved for older, low-demand patients refusing fusion.

### Charcot Arthropathy: Definitive Algorithm
* **Non-Operative (Gold Standard for most):**
  * Prolonged TCC casting (3-6 months) until clinical/radiographic transition to Stage 3 (Consolidation).
  * Transition to CROW (Charcot Restraint Orthotic Walker) boot or custom-molded accommodative footwear.
* **Operative Indications & Procedures:**
  * **Exostectomy:** Shaving bony prominences (e.g., midfoot rocker-bottom) to prevent recurrent ulceration.
  * **Arthrodesis / Superconstructs:** Realignment fusion utilizing rigid fixation (beaming, locking plates, fine-wire frames) for severely unstable, non-plantigrade feet.
  * **Amputation:** Unsalvageable severe infection, osteomyelitis, or intractable pain.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Plantar Fasciitis & Morton's Neuroma Rehab
* **Fasciitis:** Morning stretch protocol before first step. Isometric toe curls (intrinsic muscle strengthening).
* **Neuroma:** Immediate heel-weight bearing in post-op stiff shoe for 2 weeks post-neurectomy. Suture removal at 2 weeks.

### Hallux Rigidus Rehab (Post-Arthrodesis)
* **0-2 weeks:** NWB or heel-WB in plaster/boot.
* **2-6 weeks:** Progressive WB in stiff-soled post-op shoe.
* **6 weeks:** X-ray to confirm fusion. Transition to regular footwear. No high heels allowed post-fusion.

### Charcot Arthropathy Rehab
* **Lifetime Surveillance:** Daily foot inspections, diabetic foot screening.
* **Orthotic Management:** Permanent use of custom accommodative shoes with rigid rocker soles. Total lifetime ban on barefoot walking.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Plantar Fasciitis Complications
* **Post-Steroid Injection:** Plantar fascia rupture, irreversible plantar fat pad atrophy.
* **Post-Operative:** Lateral column pain syndrome (due to destabilization of medial column after release), flattening of longitudinal arch.

### Morton's Neuroma Complications
* **Post-Operative:** Stump neuroma (if nerve cut too distally; extremely painful and difficult to treat). Dorsal wound dehiscence. Numbness in cleft (expected, must consent patient).

### Hallux Rigidus Complications
* **Post-Cheilectomy:** Recurrence of osteophytes/progression of OA.
* **Post-Arthrodesis:** Non-union (5-10%), malunion (excessive plantarflexion → ↑ tip pressure; excessive dorsiflexion → shoe-wear difficulty), transfer metatarsalgia to 2nd/3rd rays.

### Charcot Arthropathy Complications
* **Disease Progression:** Irreversible midfoot collapse.
* **Secondary:** Plantar ulceration → deep soft tissue infection → Osteomyelitis.
* **Surgical:** Hardware failure (osteopenic bone), high rates of non-union, amputation.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate Acute Charcot Arthropathy from Cellulitis in a diabetic patient? Answer: Elevate the leg for 10-15 minutes. Dependent rubor in Charcot will resolve with elevation, whereas infective erythema in cellulitis will persist.
* Trap: Recommending a plantar approach for a Morton's neuroma excision to "visualize it better". Pearl: Always use a dorsal approach. A plantar scar on weight-bearing skin can result in a painful keratosis that is worse than the original neuroma.
* Pitfall: Recommending 1st MTP arthroplasty (joint replacement) for a young, active patient with Grade 4 Hallux Rigidus. High failure rates and aseptic loosening in young patients. Arthrodesis is the definitive gold standard.
* Viva Question: What is the ideal fusion position for a 1st MTP arthrodesis? Answer: 10-15° of valgus and 10-15° of dorsiflexion to allow normal gait roll-off.
* Trap: Missing a concurrent Tarsal Tunnel Syndrome when diagnosing Plantar Fasciitis. Pearl: Always percuss the posterior tibial nerve behind the medial malleolus (Tinel's test) during your foot examination.
* Pitfall: Withholding a Total Contact Cast (TCC) in early Acute Charcot because "the X-rays are normal". Eichenholtz Stage 0 has normal X-rays but requires immediate TCC to prevent catastrophic joint fragmentation.
