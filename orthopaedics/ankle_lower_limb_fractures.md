# Ankle Fractures (踝關節骨折)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Ankle Fractures
* **Definition:** Disruption of the osseous-ligamentous boundaries of the ankle mortise (talocrural joint).
* **Components:** Involves **Lateral Malleolus (外踝)**, **Medial Malleolus (內踝)**, **Posterior Malleolus (後踝)**, and/or ligamentous complexes.
* **Bimalleolar #:** # of medial + lateral malleoli.
* **Trimalleolar #:** # of medial, lateral + posterior malleoli.

### Mechanism of Injury (MOI) for Ankle Fractures
* **Low-Energy Trauma:** Simple trips, slips, or twisting injuries (common in elderly).
* **High-Energy Trauma:** Motor vehicle accidents (MVA), falls from height (axial loading + rotation).
* **Rotational Forces:** Eversion, inversion, external rotation, internal rotation (basis of **Lauge-Hansen**).

### Epidemiology of Ankle Fractures
* **Bimodal Distribution:** Young active males (sports/trauma) & older females (osteoporosis).
* **Incidence:** Most common weight-bearing articular #.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### The Ankle Ring Concept (踝關節環狀結構)
* **Principle:** Ankle is a closed structural ring composed of 3 bones + 3 ligamentous complexes.
* **Bones:** **Tibia (脛骨)**, **Fibula (腓骨)**, **Talus (距骨)**.
* **Ligaments:** **Deltoid Ligament (三角韌帶)** (medial), **Lateral Collateral Ligament Complex** (lateral), **Syndesmosis (下脛腓韌帶聯合)**.
* **Rule of Stability:** Break in 1 part of the ring = Stable. Break in ≥2 parts of the ring = Unstable → requires fixation.

### Ligamentous Stabilizers of the Ankle
* **Lateral Complex (Resists Inversion):**
  * **Anterior Talofibular Ligament (ATFL)** (Weakest, most commonly torn).
  * **Calcaneofibular Ligament (CFL)**.
  * **Posterior Talofibular Ligament (PTFL)** (Strongest).
* **Medial Complex (Resists Eversion):**
  * **Deltoid Ligament (三角韌帶):** Superficial (resists eversion) & Deep (resists lateral talar shift).
* **Syndesmosis (Connects Tibia-Fibula):**
  * Anterior Inferior Tibiofibular Ligament (AITFL).
  * Posterior Inferior Tibiofibular Ligament (PITFL).
  * Interosseous Membrane (IOM).

### Vascular Supply to the Ankle
* **Anterior:** Anterior Tibial Artery (ATA) → Dorsalis Pedis.
* **Posterior:** Posterior Tibial Artery (PTA) & Peroneal Artery.
* **Clinical Relevance:** Poor soft tissue envelope + thin skin over medial malleolus → ↑ risk of skin necrosis/wound breakdown.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Look: Inspection of Ankle Fractures
* **Gross Deformity:** Talus often dislocated posteriorly/laterally.
* **Swelling:** Rapid, massive effusion.
* **Skin Compromise:**
  * Skin tenting (especially over medial malleolus).
  * Fracture blisters (hemorrhagic vs. clear).
  * Open wounds (check for medial transverse lacerations).

### Feel: Palpation & Ottawa Ankle Rules
* **Palpate Entire Fibula:** Rule out **Maisonneuve Fracture** (proximal 1/3 fibula #).
* **Palpate Medial/Lateral Malleoli:** Assess for step-offs, crepitus, local tenderness.
* **Ottawa Ankle Rules (OAR) (Indication for X-ray):**
  * Bone tenderness at posterior edge or tip of lateral malleolus (distal 6cm).
  * Bone tenderness at posterior edge or tip of medial malleolus (distal 6cm).
  * Bone tenderness at base of 5th Metatarsal.
  * Bone tenderness at Navicular.
  * Inability to bear weight (4 steps) immediately and in ED.

### Move: Range of Motion Assessment
* **Active ROM:** Usually impossible due to pain.
* **Passive ROM:** Highly restricted; do NOT force if obvious deformity.

### Neurovascular (NV) Status of the Lower Limb
* **Vascular:**
  * Palpate Dorsalis Pedis (DP) & Posterior Tibial (PT) pulses.
  * Capillary refill time (CRT) < 2 seconds.
* **Neurological:**
  * **Superficial Peroneal Nerve:** Sensation over dorsum of foot.
  * **Deep Peroneal Nerve:** Sensation over 1st web space, active dorsiflexion.
  * **Tibial Nerve:** Sensation over plantar foot, active plantarflexion.
  * **Sural Nerve:** Sensation over lateral border of foot.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Danis-Weber Classification of Ankle Fractures
| Classification | Level of Fibular Fracture | Syndesmosis Status | Stability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type A** | Below the level of the syndesmosis (infrasyndesmotic). | Intact. | Usually stable. |
| **Type B** | At the level of the syndesmosis (transsyndesmotic). | Intact or partially torn. | Variable stability. |
| **Type C** | Above the level of the syndesmosis (suprasyndesmotic). | Torn (Syndesmotic disruption). | Unstable. |

### Lauge-Hansen Classification System
| Mechanism | Acronym | Sequential Stages of Injury |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Supination-Adduction** | SAD | 1. Transverse lateral malleolus # (or collateral lig tear) <br> 2. Vertical medial malleolus #. |
| **Supination-External Rotation** | SER | 1. AITFL tear <br> 2. Spiral lateral malleolus # <br> 3. PITFL tear/Posterior malleolus # <br> 4. Medial malleolus #/Deltoid tear. |
| **Pronation-Abduction** | PA | 1. Medial malleolus #/Deltoid tear <br> 2. AITFL tear <br> 3. Transverse/Comminuted fibula # above joint. |
| **Pronation-External Rotation** | PER | 1. Medial malleolus #/Deltoid tear <br> 2. AITFL tear <br> 3. High fibula # (e.g., Maisonneuve) <br> 4. PITFL tear/Posterior malleolus #. |

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Standard Ankle Radiographs (X-rays)
* **AP View:** Assess medial/lateral malleoli, lateral clear space.
* **Lateral View:** Assess posterior malleolus, anterior/posterior talar subluxation.
* **Mortise View:** (15-20° internal rotation): Evaluates joint space symmetry.
  * **Normal Medial Clear Space (MCS):** < 4mm.
  * **Normal Tibiofibular Overlap:** > 1mm.
  * **Normal Tibiofibular Clear Space:** < 5mm.

### Advanced Imaging & Joint X-rays
* **Full-Length Tibia-Fibula (Knee to Ankle) X-ray:** Mandatory for isolated medial malleolus # or wide MCS to rule out **Maisonneuve #**.
* **CT Scan Ankle:** Done after external fixation (spanning ex-fix) for complex intra-articular # (e.g., pilon) or pre-op planning for posterior malleolus #.

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Resuscitation & Initial Immobilization
* **ATLS Protocol:** A-B-C-D-E if high-energy trauma.
* **Analgesia:** IV Morphine / Fentanyl.
* **Immediate Immobilization:** Plaster of Paris (POP) Below-Knee (BK) backslab if undisplaced.

### Urgent Closed Reduction (CR)
* **Indication:** Gross deformity, talar dislocation, skin tenting, absent pulses.
* **Technique (Quigley maneuver):**
  * Provide conscious sedation (e.g., Propofol/Ketamine) or hematoma block.
  * Exaggerate the deformity → Longitudinal traction → Reverse the MOI → Apply well-molded BK U-slab / backslab.
* **Post-Reduction:** MUST repeat NV assessment and post-reduction X-rays.

### Soft Tissue & Open Fracture Management in ED
* **Open Fractures:** Immediate IV Antibiotics (e.g., Cefazolin/Co-amoxiclav ± Gentamicin for Hong Kong HA guidelines) + Tetanus toxoid.
* **Wound Care:** Remove gross debris, cover with sterile saline-soaked gauze.
* **Elevation:** Strict limb elevation (Braun frame or pillows) to ↓ edema.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Indications for Non-Operative vs. Operative Management
| Approach | Indications |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Operative** | Undisplaced/Stable # (Weber A, stable Weber B). <br> Intact syndesmosis. <br> Medial clear space < 4mm on stress views. <br> High surgical risk (severe PVD, neuropathy). |
| **Operative (ORIF)** | Unstable # (Bimalleolar, Trimalleolar). <br> Syndesmotic disruption (Weber C, Maisonneuve). <br> Talar shift / widened mortise (MCS > 4mm). <br> Open fractures. <br> Failure of closed reduction. |

### Operative Principles: Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF)
* **Timing:** Done immediately if open/dislocated, OR delayed 7-14 days until soft tissue swelling subsides ("wrinkle sign" +ve).
* **Lateral Malleolus Fixation:** Interfragmentary lag screw + lateral neutralization plate (or posterolateral antiglide plate).
* **Medial Malleolus Fixation:** Partially threaded cancellous lag screws (2x) OR Tension Band Wiring (TBW) for avulsion #.
* **Posterior Malleolus Fixation:** Fixed if involves >25-30% of articular surface or if joint remains subluxated. Fix with AP or PA lag screws or posterior buttress plate.

### Syndesmotic Fixation Principles
* **Intra-op Assessment:** "Cotton Test" (lateral pull on fibula with bone hook) under fluoroscopy.
* **Fixation Methods:**
  * **Syndesmotic Screws:** 3 or 4 cortices. Must be placed parallel to joint line. *Locally in HK, screws are often removed at 8-12 weeks before full weight-bearing to prevent breakage.*
  * **Suture Button (TightRope):** Dynamic fixation, allows physiologic micromotion, no need for routine removal.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Post-Operative Weight-Bearing Protocol
* **0-2 Weeks:** Strict Non-Weight Bearing (NWB) in BK POP or Aircast boot. Suture removal at 14 days.
* **2-6 Weeks:** NWB to Touch-down Weight Bearing (TDWB) in boot.
* **6-12 Weeks:** Advance to Partial Weight Bearing (PWB) → Full Weight Bearing (FWB) as tolerated, guided by radiographic union.
* **If Syndesmotic Screw used:** Strict NWB for 8-12 weeks until screw removed (unless using TightRope).

### Range of Motion (ROM) Rehabilitation
* **Early Phase:** Start active dorsiflexion/plantarflexion early if rigid internal fixation achieved.
* **Avoid:** Passive inversion/eversion until 6 weeks to protect ligaments and medial/lateral construct.
* **Late Phase:** Proprioceptive training (wobble board), peroneal strengthening.

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Early Complications of Ankle Fractures
* **Soft Tissue:** Skin necrosis, wound dehiscence, fracture blisters (exacerbated by operating through edematous skin).
* **Infection:** Superficial wound infection (cellulitis) or deep surgical site infection (osteomyelitis).
* **Compartment Syndrome:** Rare in isolated ankle # but possible in high-energy trauma.
* **Vascular:** DVT/PE due to immobilization.

### Late Complications of Ankle Fractures
* **Post-Traumatic Osteoarthritis (OA):** Risk ↑ directly with degree of residual talar shift (1mm lateral shift ↓ tibiotalar contact area by 42%).
* **Non-Union / Delayed Union:** Especially at medial malleolus.
* **Malunion:** Shortening/external rotation of fibula → valgus talar tilt.
* **Hardware Prominence:** Common over lateral malleolus; may require implant removal.
* **Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS).**

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important radiographic predictor of long-term outcome in ankle fractures? Answer: The anatomical restoration of the fibula length and perfect correction of the medial clear space (preventing talar shift).
* Viva Question: What is a Maisonneuve fracture? Answer: A proximal third fibular shaft fracture associated with a distal medial malleolar fracture or deltoid ligament tear, indicating a severe PER-type syndesmotic disruption.
* Trap: Assuming an isolated lateral malleolar fracture on X-ray is stable. Pearl: Always assess the medial side clinically (tenderness, bruising) and radiographically (gravity stress view) to rule out a bimalleolar-equivalent injury with deltoid ligament rupture.
* Trap: Applying a circular cast in the ED for an acute ankle fracture. Pearl: Always use a backslab/U-slab acutely to accommodate for mandatory secondary swelling; circular casts risk compartment syndrome and skin necrosis.
* Pitfall: Operating on an ankle with massive edema and fracture blisters. Delay surgery, elevate strictly, and wait for the "wrinkle sign" to appear before proceeding with definitive ORIF to prevent disastrous wound breakdown.
* Pitfall: Failing to image the entire fibula in a patient with isolated medial ankle pain and widened mortise. Always order full-length tibia/fibula X-rays to catch high fibular fractures.
