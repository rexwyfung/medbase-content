# Foot Ulcers (足部潰瘍)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Epidemiology

### Foot Ulcer Clinical Definition
* **Foot Ulcer (足部潰瘍)**: Breakdown of epidermal/dermal tissue over foot/ankle, commonly extending to SubQ tissue, tendon, or bone.
* **Etiological Triad**: 
  * Neuropathy (sensory, motor, autonomic)
  * Ischemia (Peripheral Arterial Disease - PAD)
  * Infection (typically 2° complication)

### Mechanism of Injury (MOI) & Pathophysiology
* **Neuropathic (神經病變性)**: 
  * Sensory loss → unrecognized repetitive microtrauma.
  * Motor neuropathy → intrinsic muscle wasting → foot deformities (claw toes) → abnormal pressure points.
  * Autonomic neuropathy → ↓ sweating → dry, cracked skin (fissures) → bacterial entry.
* **Ischemic (缺血性)**: 
  * Atherosclerosis (macrovascular) + capillary basement membrane thickening (microvascular) → ↓ O2 delivery → tissue necrosis from minor trauma.
* **Neuroischemic**: Combination of both (most common in advanced DM).

### Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* Highly prevalent in HK Hospital Authority (HA) SOPC/Wards 2° to ↑ DM incidence.
* Leading cause of non-traumatic lower limb amputations.
* 5-year mortality post-major amputation approaches 50-70%.

## 2. Applied Orthopaedic Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Vascular Supply to the Foot
* **Angiosomes (血管體)**: 3-dimensional tissue blocks supplied by specific source arteries. Crucial for targeted revascularization.
* **Posterior Tibial (PT) Artery**: Supplies plantar aspect (medial/lateral plantar arteries).
* **Anterior Tibial (AT) / Dorsalis Pedis (DP) Artery**: Supplies dorsum of foot.
* **Peroneal Artery**: Supplies lateral heel.
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/2fccf93986cabdf72ca3a1643625ebf3.png)

### Foot Biomechanics and Deforming Forces
* **Intrinsic Muscle Wasting**: Lumbricals & interossei weakness → unopposed Extensor Digitorum Longus (EDL) & Flexor Digitorum Longus (FDL) → **Claw Toes (爪狀趾)**.
* **Altered Pressure distribution**: Plantar fat pad migrates distally → prominent **Metatarsal Heads (蹠骨頭)** → ↑ peak plantar pressure.
* **Charcot Arthropathy (夏科氏關節病)**: Midfoot collapse → **Rocker-bottom foot (搖椅底足)** → midfoot plantar ulceration.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Foot Ulcer Inspection (Look)
* **Location**: 
  * Neuropathic: Plantar aspect, metatarsal heads, heel, tips of claw toes.
  * Ischemic: Margins of foot, tips of toes, heel, dorsal aspect.
* **Wound Characteristics**: Size (cm x cm), depth, base (granulation vs slough/eschar), margins (punched-out vs sloping).
* **Surrounding Skin**: Hyperkeratosis (callus) surrounding neuropathic ulcers; shiny, hairless skin in ischemia.
* **Infection Signs**: Erythema, fluctuance, purulent discharge, sinus tracts.

### Foot Ulcer Palpation (Feel)
* **Temperature**: 
  * Warm = Inflammation/Infection/Charcot.
  * Cold = Ischemia.
* **Probe-to-Bone Test (探骨測試)**: Sterile blunt probe gently advanced into ulcer. Palpating hard, gritty bone is highly specific for **Osteomyelitis (骨髓炎)**.
* **Capillary Refill Time (CRT)**: Prolonged (>2s) in ischemia.

### Foot Ulcer Range of Motion (Move)
* **Equinus Contracture**: Tight Achilles tendon / Gastrocnemius → ↑ forefoot plantar pressure.
* **Joint Stiffness**: Restricted Ankle/Subtalar/MTP joint ROM ↑ local shear forces.

### Foot Ulcer Neurovascular (NV) Status
* **Vascular**:
  * Palpate **Dorsalis Pedis (DP)** & **Posterior Tibial (PT)** pulses.
  * Handheld Doppler (monophasic vs biphasic/triphasic waveforms).
* **Neurological**:
  * **10g Semmes-Weinstein Monofilament (單絲測試)**: Tests protective sensation (test 10 sites per foot).
  * **128Hz Tuning Fork**: Tests vibration sense (early loss in DM neuropathy).
  * **Ankle Jerk Reflex**: Often absent.

## 4. Orthopaedic Classification Systems

### Meggitt-Wagner Ulcer Classification System
| Grade | Clinical Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **0** | Pre-ulcerative lesion, healed ulcer, or presence of bony deformity |
| **1** | Superficial ulcer (partial/full thickness skin), without SubQ involvement |
| **2** | Deep ulcer penetrating to tendon, capsule, or bone |
| **3** | Deep ulcer with abscess, osteomyelitis, or joint sepsis |
| **4** | Localized gangrene (e.g., forefoot or heel) |
| **5** | Extensive gangrene of the entire foot |

### WIfI (Wound, Ischemia, foot Infection) Classification
* Estimates 1-year amputation risk and benefit of revascularization.
* Grades **Wound** (0-3), **Ischemia** (0-3 by ABI/TBPI), and **foot Infection** (0-3 by SIRS criteria).

## 5. Radiological Investigations

### Foot Ulcer Radiography (Plain X-rays)
* **Views**: AP, Lateral, Oblique of affected foot.
* **Osteomyelitis (OM)**: Cortical destruction, periosteal reaction, osteopenia, focal osteolysis (requires 30-50% bone loss to be visible, lags clinical picture by 10-14 days).
* **Vascular**: **Mönckeberg's arteriosclerosis (蒙克伯格動脈硬化)** (medial calcification of vessels).
* **Deformity**: Charcot changes (destruction, subluxation, dislocation, debris).
* **Soft Tissue**: Gas tracking (indicates gas-forming organisms e.g., *Clostridium* or *Bacteroides*).
![image|600](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/b2daca93042656f08f087a71204ba499.png)

### Advanced Foot Ulcer Imaging
* **MRI without contrast**: Gold standard for early OM (marrow edema, low T1, high T2/STIR) and deep abscess localization.
* **Vascular Imaging**: Duplex Ultrasound, CT Angiography (CTA), or MR Angiography (MRA) to map arterial tree for intervention.
* **White Blood Cell (WBC) Scan**: Indicated if MRI contraindicated (e.g., pacemaker) or to distinguish Charcot from OM.
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/paediatrics/31b2e3cb097b2818d5dbe87664ed11d2.png)

### Bedside Vascular Investigations
* **Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI)**:
	* Normal 0.9-1.3. <0.9 = PAD.
	* 1.3 = heavily calcified, incompressible vessels (common in DM, yields false negative).
* **Toe-Brachial Pressure Index (TBPI)**:
	* Digital vessels less prone to calcification.
	* Normal >0.7. <0.2 = severe ischemia.
* **Transcutaneous Oximetry (TcPO2)**:
	* Predicts healing potential (>40 mmHg heals well; <20 mmHg will not heal).

## 6. Initial ED Management

### Acute Medical Resuscitation
* **Sepsis Protocol**: ABCDEs, IV fluid resuscitation.
* **Empirical IV Antibiotics**: Broad-spectrum for severe infections (e.g., **Augmentin + Metronidazole** or **Piperacillin-Tazobactam** per HK HA guidelines). Adjust post-culture.
* **Strict Glycemic Control**: Sliding scale insulin (hyperglycemia impairs leukocyte function and wound healing).

### Acute ED Wound Interventions
* **Swab/Tissue Culture**: Obtain deep tissue specimen *after* initial cleansing, before Abx if possible (do not just swab superficial slough).
* **Bedside Debridement**: Sharp debridement of superficial necrotic tissue and thick callus blocking drainage.
* **Pus Drainage**: Immediate incision & drainage (I&D) of superficial fluctuant abscesses in ED/ward.

### Acute Offloading & Immobilization
* **Strict Bed Rest**: Elevate limb to ↓ edema.
* **Dressings**: Non-adherent, absorptive dressings (e.g., Alginate for high exudate, hydrogel for dry necrotic).
* **Splinting**: Temporary posterior slab if active Charcot or severe infection to restrict movement and prevent contractures.

## 8. Rehabilitation Protocol

### Podiatry & Orthotics Protocol
* **Custom Footwear**: Extra-depth, wide-toe box shoes to prevent friction.
* **Custom Insoles**: Molded EVA (Ethylene-vinyl acetate) to distribute weight away from metatarsal heads.
* **Rocker-Bottom Soles**: ↓ pressure on forefoot during the propulsive phase of gait.

### Amputee Rehabilitation Protocol
* **Stump Maturation**: Use of shrinker socks and rigid removable dressings to shape the stump (conical shape ideal for BKA).
* **Prosthetic Fitting**: Coordinated by physiotherapist and prosthetist. Requires adequate stump healing and patient cardiovascular fitness.
* **Gait Training**: Parallel bars → walking frame → sticks. Energy expenditure ↑ 20-30% for BKA, ↑ 60-100% for AKA.

### Secondary Prevention Protocol
* Daily foot inspection (using a mirror for plantar aspect).
* Strict prohibition of barefoot walking.
* Regular podiatry reviews (callus paring, nail care).
* Daily moisturization (avoiding interdigital spaces to prevent fungal infection).

## 9. Orthopaedic Complications

### Infectious Complications
* **Refractory Osteomyelitis**: Requiring repeat bone resections.
* **Ascending Cellulitis / Necrotizing Fasciitis**: Surgical emergencies requiring immediate radical debridement/guillotine amputation.
* **Systemic Sepsis**: Can rapidly progress to multi-organ failure and death.

### Surgical & Post-Amputation Complications
* **Stump Breakdown / Dehiscence**: Due to premature suture removal, falls, or undiagnosed proximal ischemia.
* **Phantom Limb Pain (幻肢痛)**: Neuropathic pain perceived in the amputated limb (treated with Gabapentin/Pregabalin, mirror therapy).
* **Flexion Contractures**: Knee flexion contracture post-BKA (prevented with prone lying, hamstring stretching, stump boards on wheelchairs).
* **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)**: 2° to immobility post-op.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate a neuropathic ulcer from an ischemic ulcer? Answer: Neuropathic ulcers are plantar, warm, painless, with bounding pulses and surrounding callus. Ischemic ulcers are at the toes/margins, cold, painful, pulseless, with shiny, hairless skin.
* Trap: Relying solely on the Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI) to assess vascularity in long-standing diabetic patients. Pearl: DM causes vessel wall calcification (Mönckeberg’s arteriosclerosis), leading to falsely elevated ABI (>1.3). Always use Toe-Brachial Pressure Index (TBPI) or assess Doppler waveforms.
* Pitfall: Underestimating the significance of a positive probe-to-bone test in the clinic. A positive test has high specificity for underlying osteomyelitis and mandates aggressive further evaluation (MRI/X-ray) and likely surgical debridement.
* Viva Question: What is the most common deforming force leading to plantar forefoot ulceration in diabetics? Answer: Motor neuropathy leading to intrinsic muscle wasting, resulting in unopposed long flexors/extensors causing claw toes, which subsequently drives the metatarsal heads into the plantar aspect.
* Trap: Performing a local toe amputation for a gangrenous toe before assessing and correcting proximal arterial supply. Pearl: Always evaluate vascular inflow (TcPO2, CTA) first; amputating an ischemic limb guarantees wound failure and proximal necrosis. Revascularize first.
