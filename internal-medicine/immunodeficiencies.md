# Immunodeficiencies (免疫缺陷)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Immunodeficiency
* **Immunodeficiency (免疫缺陷)**: State of suboptimal immune system function → ↑ susceptibility to infections, ↑ risk of malignancy, and paradoxically ↑ risk of autoimmunity/allergy.
* **Primary Immunodeficiency (PID) (原發性免疫缺陷)**: Intrinsic/genetic defects of the immune system (usually manifest in childhood, but CVID can present in adulthood).
* **Secondary Immunodeficiency (SID) (繼發性免疫缺陷)**: Acquired impairment due to external factors (infections, drugs, systemic disease).

### Primary Immunodeficiencies (PID) Etiologies
* **Humoral/B-cell (Most common, ~70%)**: Selective **IgA Deficiency** (IgA缺乏症), **Common Variable Immunodeficiency (CVID)** (常見變異型免疫缺陷), **X-linked Agammaglobulinemia (XLA)** (X連鎖無丙種球蛋白血症).
* **Cell-Mediated/T-cell & Combined (15%)**: **Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID)** (重症聯合免疫缺陷), **DiGeorge Syndrome** (迪喬治綜合症), Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome, Ataxia-Telangiectasia.
* **Phagocytic Defects (10%)**: **Chronic Granulomatous Disease (CGD)** (慢性肉芽腫病), Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency (LAD).
* **Complement Deficiencies (1-5%)**: Terminal complement (C5-C9) deficiency, C1q/C2/C4 deficiency.

### Secondary Immunodeficiencies (SID) Etiologies
* **Infectious**: **HIV/AIDS** (愛滋病) (Most important globally/locally), Measles, HTLV-1.
* **Iatrogenic**: Corticosteroids, biologic therapies (e.g., Rituximab, Anti-TNF), chemotherapy, radiotherapy.
* **Malignancy**: Hematological malignancies (CLL, Multiple Myeloma, Lymphoma).
* **Protein Loss**: Nephrotic syndrome (loss of IgG), Protein-losing enteropathy.
* **Metabolic/Nutritional**: Severe malnutrition, poorly controlled Diabetes Mellitus (impaired phagocytosis), Uremia.
* **Asplenia**: Surgical splenectomy, functional asplenia (Sickle cell disease).

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of Immunodeficiencies
* **HIV/AIDS**: ~9000 reported cases in HK; predominantly MSM transmission, followed by heterosexual contact. ↑ prevalence of specific endemic opportunistic infections (OIs) in advanced HIV, notably **Tuberculosis (TB) (結核病)** and **Talaromyces marneffei (馬爾尼菲籃狀菌)**.
* **PID**: Estimated prevalence ~1:2000 to 1:10000. CVID is the most common symptomatic PID encountered in adult internal medicine in HA hospitals.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Humoral (B-cell) Deficiencies
* **Selective IgA Deficiency**: Defect in B-cell differentiation into IgA-secreting plasma cells → mucosal surface vulnerability (respiratory, GI).
* **XLA (Bruton's)**: Mutation in **BTK (Bruton tyrosine kinase)** gene on X-chromosome (Xq22.1) → block in B-cell maturation at pre-B cell stage → absent mature B-cells & absent all Ig isotypes.
* **CVID**: Heterogeneous genetic defects (e.g., TACI, BAFF-R mutations) → defective B-cell differentiation → ↓ IgG and ↓ IgA/IgM, with dysfunctional specific antibody memory responses. Paradoxical ↑ autoantibody production.

### Pathophysiology of Cell-Mediated (T-cell) & Combined Deficiencies
* **SCID**: Variable mutations (e.g., IL-2Rγ X-linked, ADA deficiency) → failure of T-cell development, with secondary B-cell and NK-cell dysfunction → profound vulnerability to all pathogens.
* **DiGeorge Syndrome**: 22q11.2 microdeletion → failure of 3rd and 4th pharyngeal pouches development → thymic hypoplasia/aplasia → profound T-cell deficiency.

### Pathophysiology of Phagocytic & Complement Defects
* **CGD**: Mutation in **NADPH oxidase** complex (usually X-linked CYBB gene) → inability to generate reactive oxygen species (superoxide) → failure of respiratory burst → intracellular survival of **catalase-positive** organisms inside macrophages → granuloma formation.
* **Complement Defect (C5-C9)**: Failure to form Membrane Attack Complex (MAC) → inability to lyse heavily encapsulated *Neisseria* species.

### Natural History & Pathophysiology of HIV Infection
* **Viral Entry**: HIV gp120 binds to **CD4** receptor and coreceptor (**CCR5** or **CXCR4**) on T-helper cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells.
* **Acute Infection Phase**: Massive viral replication in GALT (Gut-Associated Lymphoid Tissue) → severe depletion of mucosal CD4+ T-cells → transient plasma viremia.
* **Clinical Latency Phase**: Robust immune response traps virus in lymph nodes. Gradual ↑ viral load (VL) and steady ↓ CD4+ count (approx. 50-100 cells/µL per year).
* **AIDS Phase**: Critical depletion of CD4+ T-cells (<200 cells/µL) → profound loss of cell-mediated immunity → emergence of fatal Opportunistic Infections (OIs) and malignancies.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Clues for Immunodeficiency (The "SPURR" Mnemonic)
* **S**evere: Infections requiring IV antibiotics or hospitalization.
* **P**ersistent: Prolonged duration despite appropriate standard therapy.
* **U**nusual: Atypical organisms (e.g., *Pneumocystis*, non-tuberculous mycobacteria) or unusual sites (e.g., liver/brain abscess).
* **R**ecurrent: >2 pneumonias/year, >4 ear infections/year, >2 deep-seated infections.
* **R**uns in Family: Consanguinity, family history of early infant death or known PID.

### System-Specific History in Immunodeficiency
* **Respiratory**: Recurrent sinusitis, chronic productive cough (bronchiectasis), progressive dyspnea.
* **Gastrointestinal**: Chronic unremitting diarrhea, severe malabsorption/weight loss.
* **Constitutional**: Failure to thrive (pediatrics), profound unintentional weight loss, persistent unexplained fever/night sweats.
* **Sexual/Social (HIV Risk)**: Unprotected intercourse, MSM, IVDU, prior blood transfusions (pre-1985), history of Syphilis/Gonorrhea/Chlamydia.

### Physical Examination Findings in Primary Immunodeficiency
* **Lymphoid Tissue**: **Absent tonsils/lymph nodes** (XLA, SCID) vs **Lymphadenopathy/Splenomegaly** (CVID, CGD).
* **Dermatological**: Severe recalcitrant eczema (Wiskott-Aldrich, Hyper-IgE), extensive cutaneous warts, non-healing skin abscesses (CGD).
* **Syndromic Facies**: Cleft palate, micrognathia, low-set ears (DiGeorge - *CATCH-22* phenotype).
* **Respiratory**: Coarse crackles, clubbing (manifestations of chronic bronchiectasis).

### Physical Examination Findings in HIV/AIDS
* **General**: Wasting syndrome (cachexia), generalized lymphadenopathy.
* **Oral Cavity**: **Oral candidiasis** (thrush - easily scrapable), **Oral Hairy Leukoplakia** (corrugated white plaques on lateral tongue edges - EBV driven, CANNOT be scraped off), Kaposi Sarcoma (violaceous palatal lesions).
* **Dermatological**: **Talaromyces marneffei** lesions (classic umbilicated papules on face/trunk in HK context), Seborrheic dermatitis, multidermatomal Herpes Zoster.
* **Ophthalmological**: "Pizza pie" retinopathy (CMV retinitis).
* **Neurological**: Focal deficits (Toxoplasmosis, PML), global cognitive slowing (HIV-Associated Neurocognitive Disorder).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Pathogen Susceptibility by Immune Defect
| Defect Type | Classic Susceptible Pathogens | Prototypic Diseases |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **B-cell / Antibody** | Encapsulated bacteria (*S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*), *Giardia* | XLA, CVID, IgA Deficiency |
| **T-cell / Cell-Mediated** | Intracellular bacteria, Viruses (CMV, HSV, VZV), Fungi (PCP, *Candida*) | SCID, DiGeorge, HIV/AIDS |
| **Phagocytic** | Catalase +ve bacteria (*S. aureus*, *Serratia*, *Burkholderia*), *Aspergillus* | CGD, LAD |
| **Complement (C5-C9)** | *Neisseria meningitidis*, *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* | MAC Deficiency |

### Differentiating Structural vs Immunological Causes of Recurrent Infections
* **Cystic Fibrosis / Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia**: Prominent recurrent sinopulmonary infections, but normal systemic immunity. Clubbing early; typical pathogens are *Pseudomonas*, *S. aureus*.
* **Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency**: Lower zone emphysema/bronchiectasis + Liver disease. Normal immunoglobulin levels.
* **Anatomical Defects**: Recurrent meningitis → query CSF leak / basilar skull fracture. Recurrent UTI → query vesicoureteral reflux.

### Differentiating HIV Oral Lesions
* **Oral Candidiasis**: White curdy plaques on palate/buccal mucosa → **Scrapes off** easily, leaving an erythematous/bleeding base.
* **Oral Hairy Leukoplakia**: Corrugated white ridges strictly on lateral tongue borders → **Cannot be scraped off** (caused by EBV, CD4 usually <200).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Initial Blood Investigations for Immunodeficiency Workup
* **Bedside**: HIV point-of-care rapid test.
* **Complete Blood Profile (CBP) + Diff**: Check for absolute lymphopenia (T-cell defect / HIV) or neutropenia (Phagocyte defect).
* **Peripheral Blood Smear**: Howell-Jolly bodies (functional or anatomical asplenia).
* **HIV Screening**: HIV 4th generation Ag/Ab Combo assay (Standard of care in HA).

### Immunological Blood Workup (Primary Immunodeficiency Panel)
* **Serum Immunoglobulins**: Quantitative IgG, IgA, IgM, and IgE.
* **Lymphocyte Subsets (Flow Cytometry)**: Absolute counts of CD3+ (Total T), CD4+ (T-helper), CD8+ (T-cytotoxic), CD19+/CD20+ (B-cells), CD16+/CD56+ (NK cells).
* **Functional Antibody Response**: Assess specific IgG titers to protein vaccines (Tetanus/Diphtheria) and polysaccharide vaccines (Pneumococcal) pre- and 4-weeks post-vaccination.

### Advanced & Genetic Testing for Specific PIDs
* **Phagocyte Function**: **Nitroblue Tetrazolium (NBT)** test (fails to turn blue in CGD) or **Dihydrorhodamine (DHR) 123 flow cytometry** (↓ fluorescence in CGD).
* **Complement Function**: **CH50** (screens classical pathway), **AH50** (screens alternative pathway).
* **Genetic Sequencing**: Whole Exome Sequencing (WES) or targeted PID gene panels (identifies specific mutations e.g., BTK, CYBB).

### HIV/AIDS Specific Investigations & Staging
* **HIV Confirmation**: HIV-1/HIV-2 differentiation immunoassay or Western Blot.
* **Disease Staging**: **CD4+ T-cell count** (determines OI risk) and **Plasma HIV-1 RNA Viral Load** (determines baseline viremia & treatment response).
* **Resistance Genotyping**: Genotypic antiretroviral resistance testing (pol gene sequencing) BEFORE starting ART.
* **Baseline Co-infection Screen**: HBsAg, anti-HBc, anti-HCV, VDRL/RPR, IGRA/Mantoux (for latent TB), Toxoplasma IgG, CMV IgG.
* **Pharmacogenomics**: HLA-B*5701 testing (mandatory before prescribing Abacavir to prevent fatal hypersensitivity).

### Diagnostic Criteria for CVID (European Society for Immunodeficiencies)
| Criteria (Must meet ALL) | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. ↓ Immunoglobulins** | Markedly ↓ serum IgG AND markedly ↓ IgA and/or IgM (measured at least twice). |
| **2. Impaired Ab Response** | Poor response to vaccines OR absent isohaemagglutinins. |
| **3. Age of Onset** | >4 years old. |
| **4. Exclusion** | Exclusion of defined monogenic PIDs and secondary causes of hypogammaglobulinemia. |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Suspected Sepsis in Immunodeficiency
* **Resuscitation**: ABCDE approach, IV fluids (crystalloids), vasopressors if hypotensive (septic shock).
* **Empiric Antimicrobials**: MUST cover *Pseudomonas* and resistant organisms. Broad-spectrum IV Abx within 1 hour (e.g., IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam OR IV Meropenem). Add IV Vancomycin if MRSA risk or central line present.
* **Source Control**: Urgent contrast imaging to identify abscesses; percutaneous drainage if identified.

### Acute Management of Severe Opportunistic Infections (HIV/AIDS)
* **Pneumocystis jirovecii Pneumonia (PCP/PJP)**: High-dose **IV Co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX)** for 21 days. Add **IV Hydrocortisone/PO Prednisolone** if hypoxic (PaO2 <70 mmHg on room air) to prevent paradoxical deterioration from dying fungi.
* **Talaromyces marneffei (Endemic to HK)**: **IV Amphotericin B** (liposomal) x 2 weeks → switch to PO Itraconazole.
* **Cerebral Toxoplasmosis**: PO Pyrimethamine + Sulfadiazine + Folinic acid (Leucovorin) rescue.
* **Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Retinitis/Systemic**: **IV Ganciclovir** or PO Valganciclovir.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of B-Cell Deficiencies (CVID / XLA)
* **Immunoglobulin Replacement**: Life-long **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)** every 3-4 weeks OR **Subcutaneous Immunoglobulin (SCIG)** weekly. Target trough IgG level >5-8 g/L.
* *Note: IgA Deficiency does NOT require IVIG (IVIG contains mostly IgG and can cause anaphylaxis in IgA deficient patients due to anti-IgA antibodies).*
* **Prophylactic Antibiotics**: Azithromycin or Co-trimoxazole if recurrent infections persist despite optimized IVIG, or if structural lung damage (bronchiectasis) is present.
* **Vaccination**: **AVOID LIVE VACCINES** (BCG, MMR, Yellow Fever, oral Polio) in severe B/T cell defects. Administer killed/recombinant vaccines (Influenza, Pneumococcal).

### Chronic Management of Specific PIDs
* **SCID**: Medical emergency. Strict isolation. Curative **Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT)** is mandatory.
* **CGD**: Lifelong antibacterial prophylaxis (PO Co-trimoxazole) + antifungal prophylaxis (PO Itraconazole). **Subcutaneous IFN-γ** (Interferon gamma) 3x/week upregulates macrophage oxidative burst.

### Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) Algorithm in Hong Kong
* **Principle**: Initiate ART in ALL HIV-infected individuals regardless of CD4 count (Test and Treat strategy).
* **1st Line HA Formulary Regimens (Integrase Inhibitor-based)**:
  * **Biktarvy** (Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / Tenofovir Alafenamide [TAF]): 1 pill QD. High genetic barrier to resistance.
  * **Triumeq** (Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine): Requires negative HLA-B*5701 prior to use.
* **Monitoring**: Repeat HIV Viral Load at 1-2 months, then every 3-6 months. Goal = Undetectable VL (<50 copies/mL) within 6 months. U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable).

### Antimicrobial Prophylaxis Algorithm in HIV/AIDS
| CD4 Count threshold | Target Pathogen | First-line Prophylaxis Drug |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **< 200 cells/µL** | *Pneumocystis jirovecii* (PCP) | PO Co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX) |
| **< 100 cells/µL** | *Toxoplasma gondii* | PO Co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX) covers both |
| **< 100 cells/µL (HK/SE Asia)** | *Talaromyces marneffei* | No routine primary prophylaxis, but high clinical suspicion required. Secondary prophylaxis: Itraconazole. |
| **< 50 cells/µL** | *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC) | PO Azithromycin (only if active MAC ruled out, though routine use now declining if immediate ART starts) |

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### WHO Clinical Staging of HIV/AIDS
| Stage | Defining Clinical Conditions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1 (Asymptomatic)** | Asymptomatic OR Persistent Generalized Lymphadenopathy (PGL). |
| **Stage 2 (Mild Symptoms)** | Unexplained weight loss <10%, recurrent URTI, Herpes zoster, angular cheilitis, seborrheic dermatitis. |
| **Stage 3 (Advanced)** | Weight loss >10%, unexplained chronic diarrhea >1 month, oral candidiasis, oral hairy leukoplakia, severe bacterial infections (pneumonia), pulmonary TB. |
| **Stage 4 (Severe/AIDS)** | PCP, Toxoplasmosis, Cryptococcal meningitis, extrapulmonary TB, CMV disease, Kaposi sarcoma, HIV encephalopathy, Wasting syndrome. |

### CD4 Count Risk Stratification in HIV
* **>500 cells/µL**: Normal/near-normal immunity.
* **<200 cells/µL**: Defines "AIDS" immunologically. High risk for PCP.
* **<100 cells/µL**: High risk for Toxoplasmosis, Cryptococcus, *Talaromyces marneffei*.
* **<50 cells/µL**: Profound immunosuppression. Risk for MAC, CMV retinitis, Primary CNS Lymphoma.

### Prognosis & Warning Signs in Primary Immunodeficiencies
* **CVID Prognosis**: ↑ risk of developing gastric cancer, Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma, and Granulomatous-Lymphocytic Interstitial Lung Disease (GLILD). Requires active malignancy screening.
* **SCID Prognosis**: Universally fatal by age 1 without HSCT.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Primary Immunodeficiencies
* **Pulmonary**: Progressive bronchiectasis (due to recurrent pyogenic pneumonias in XLA/CVID) → Cor pulmonale.
* **Autoimmune (especially CVID)**: Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), Autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA), Pernicious anemia.
* **Gastrointestinal**: Nodular lymphoid hyperplasia, chronic *Giardia* lamblia infection leading to severe malabsorption.

### Complications of HIV/AIDS (AIDS-Defining Illnesses)
* **Neurological**: Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML - JC virus), Cryptococcal meningitis, AIDS Dementia Complex.
* **Oncological**: Kaposi Sarcoma (HHV-8), Primary CNS Lymphoma (EBV), Invasive Cervical Carcinoma (HPV).
* **Ophthalmological**: CMV retinitis (leads to blindness).

### Adverse Effects of Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) & IVIG
* **Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS)**: Paradoxical worsening of preexisting infections (e.g., TB, Cryptococcus) shortly after starting ART due to rapid recovery of CD4+ cells driving massive inflammation.
* **Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF)**: Nephrotoxicity (Fanconi syndrome), loss of bone mineral density (osteoporosis). *Note: TAF (in Biktarvy) has significantly lower risk.*
* **Abacavir**: Fatal systemic Hypersensitivity Reaction in HLA-B*5701 positive patients.
* **IVIG Complications**: Anaphylaxis (especially in IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies), aseptic meningitis, volume overload, hyperviscosity syndrome (thrombosis).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common primary immunodeficiency and how does it present? Answer: Selective IgA deficiency. Most are asymptomatic, but can present with recurrent sinopulmonary and GI (Giardia) infections, and concurrent autoimmune diseases (Celiac, asthma).
* Viva Question: A patient with known HIV presents with a CD4 of 45 and worsening vision (floaters/flashes). Fundoscopy shows "cottage cheese and ketchup" lesions. Diagnosis? Answer: Cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis.
* Trap: Giving an HIV patient with suspected TB immediate ART. Pearl: Delay ART for 2-8 weeks after starting anti-TB therapy to prevent severe TB-IRIS (Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome), especially if CD4 >50. If CD4 <50, start ART within 2 weeks.
* Trap: Ordering IVIG for a patient newly diagnosed with Selective IgA Deficiency to "boost" their immunity. Pearl: This is contraindicated. IVIG contains trace IgA; giving it to a severely IgA deficient patient with anti-IgA antibodies can trigger fatal anaphylaxis.
* Pitfall: Missing the diagnosis of *Talaromyces marneffei* in Hong Kong. Always consider it in an advanced HIV patient presenting with fever, weight loss, hepatosplenomegaly, and central umbilicated papules resembling Molluscum contagiosum.
* Pitfall: Using standard pulse oximetry alone to guide steroid use in PCP. You must check an Arterial Blood Gas (ABG); steroids are indicated ONLY if room air PaO2 <70 mmHg or A-a gradient >35 mmHg.
