# Viral Hepatitis (病毒性肝炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition & Etiology of Viral Hepatitis
* **Definition:** Systemic viral infection causing primary necroinflammatory liver injury.
* **Etiologies:** Primary hepatotropic viruses: **HAV** (甲型肝炎), **HBV** (乙型肝炎), **HCV** (丙型肝炎), **HDV** (丁型肝炎), **HEV** (戊型肝炎). 

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of Viral Hepatitis
* **HBV:** Highly endemic; ~7-8% prevalence.
* **HBV Transmission in HK:** Predominantly Mother-To-Child Transmission (MTCT) (母嬰傳播) prior to universal neonatal vaccination (introduced 1988).
* **HCV:** Low prevalence (~0.3%), predominantly Intravenous Drug Users (IVDU) & contaminated blood products pre-1991.
* **HEV:** Most common cause of *acute* viral hepatitis in HK (linked to undercooked pig liver/pork). 
* **HAV:** Declining endemicity; mostly travel-related or shellfish consumption.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Viral Virology & Transmission Routes
* **HAV:** Single-stranded RNA (ssRNA); Fecal-oral transmission.
* **HBV:** Partially double-stranded DNA (dsDNA); Blood, sexual, MTCT.
* **HCV:** ssRNA; Blood (IVDU > sexual).
* **HDV:** Defective ssRNA; requires HBV surface antigen (HBsAg) for virion assembly; Blood, sexual.
* **HEV:** ssRNA; Fecal-oral (genotypes 1,2) or zoonotic (genotypes 3,4 via pork).

### Pathophysiology of Hepatocellular Injury
* **HBV & HCV:** Non-cytopathic. Hepatocyte necrosis mediated by host **Cytotoxic T-Lymphocytes (CD8+ T-cells)** targeting viral antigens on hepatocyte MHC-I.
* **HAV & HEV:** Directly cytopathic + immune-mediated lysis.
* **HDV Co-infection:** Simultaneous acute HBV + HDV → self-limiting usually.
* **HDV Super-infection:** HDV in chronic HBV → ↑ risk of fulminant liver failure (暴發性肝衰竭) & rapid cirrhosis progression.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking in Viral Hepatitis
* **Acute Hepatitis Symptoms:** Prodromal flu-like illness, fatigue, anorexia, nausea, Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) pain, dark urine (tea-colored), pale stools, pruritus.
* **Risk Factor Assessment:** Transfusions (pre-1991), IVDU, multiple sexual partners, tattoos, needle-stick injury, maternal HBV status, travel history, recent raw shellfish/pork ingestion.
* **Chronic Hepatitis Symptoms:** Often asymptomatic until decompensation (e.g., abdominal swelling, hematemesis, confusion).

### Physical Examination Findings in Viral Hepatitis
* **General:** Jaundice (黃疸) (scleral icterus, sublingual), excoriations (pruritus).
* **Hepatic:** Hepatomegaly (tender in acute phase, firm/nodular in chronicity), splenomegaly (if portal hypertension present).
* **Extrahepatic Immune-Complex Stigmata:** 
* **HBV:** Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN) (subcutaneous nodules, mononeuritis multiplex).
* **HCV:** Cryoglobulinemia (palpable purpura on lower limbs), Porphyria Cutanea Tarda (blistering skin lesions on sun-exposed areas).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Acute Hepatitic Injury
* **Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI):** Paracetamol overdose, Anti-TB drugs (Rifampicin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide), Statins, TCM herbal remedies.
* **Other Viruses:** EBV, CMV, HSV (typically prominent lymphadenopathy, atypical lymphocytosis).
* **Autoimmune Hepatitis:** +ve ANA, Anti-SMA, ↑ IgG; middle-aged females.
* **Wilson Disease:** Kayser-Fleischer rings, neuropsychiatric signs, young patients.
* **Ischemic Hepatitis ("Shock Liver"):** Rapid ↑ ALT in setting of severe hypotension/heart failure.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Basic Blood Investigations for Hepatitis
* **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** Hepatitic pattern (ALT & AST ↑↑ > ALP); ALT >1000 U/L suggests acute viral hepatitis, ischemic, or DILI.
* **Bilirubin:** ↑ Conjugated bilirubin.
* **Coagulation Profile:** Prolonged Prothrombin Time (PT) / ↑ INR indicates impaired synthetic function (heralds severe disease/acute liver failure).

### Hepatitis B Serology Interpretation
| HBsAg | Anti-HBs | Anti-HBc (IgG/Total) | IgM anti-HBc | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| +ve | -ve | -ve | +ve | **Acute HBV Infection** |
| +ve | -ve | +ve | -ve | **Chronic HBV Infection** |
| -ve | +ve | +ve | -ve | **Past/Resolved Infection** (Natural Immunity) |
| -ve | +ve | -ve | -ve | **Vaccinated** |
| -ve | -ve | +ve | +ve | **Window Period** |

### Additional HBV Markers (Replication & Infectivity)
* **HBeAg:** Marker of active viral replication & high infectivity.
* **Anti-HBe:** Indicates seroconversion (↓ infectivity).
* **HBV DNA (Quantitative PCR):** Direct measure of viral load; critical for treatment decisions.

### Serology for HAV, HCV, HDV, HEV
* **HAV:** IgM anti-HAV (+ve = acute); IgG anti-HAV (+ve = past/immune).
* **HCV:** Anti-HCV (+ve = current or past). **Must confirm active infection with HCV RNA PCR** (if PCR -ve, it means spontaneous/treated clearance).
* **HDV:** Anti-HDV antibody (in HBsAg +ve patients) → confirm with HDV RNA.
* **HEV:** IgM anti-HEV (+ve = acute).

### Non-Invasive Fibrosis Assessment (Imaging)
* **Ultrasound (USG) Hepatobiliary:** Rule out biliary obstruction; assess for cirrhosis (coarse echotexture, nodular margin) & portal hypertension (splenomegaly, ascites).
* **Transient Elastography (FibroScan):** Uses ultrasound shear-wave velocity to quantify liver stiffness (kPa). Determines degree of fibrosis (F0 to F4).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Viral Hepatitis Flare & Acute Liver Failure (ALF) Management
* **Supportive Care:** IV hydration, anti-emetics, correct hypoglycemia (D10W infusion if needed).
* **Avoid Hepatotoxins:** Strictly stop alcohol, Paracetamol, non-essential meds.
* **Coagulopathy Correction:** Vitamin K IV (limited efficacy if severe hepatocyte loss); only transfuse FFP if active bleeding or pre-invasive procedure.
* **Antiviral Rescue in Severe Acute HBV:** Initiate oral nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) immediately (e.g., Entecavir) if complicated by coagulopathy (INR >1.5) or encephalopathy.
* **Transplant Center Referral:** Early transfer for worsening encephalopathy or rising INR.

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): Needle-Stick Injury
* **Source HBsAg +ve / Exposed Anti-HBs -ve (<10 mIU/mL):** Give Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG) STAT + Initiate HBV Vaccine series.
* **Source HCV +ve:** No PEP available. Monitor HCV RNA at 4-6 weeks; treat if +ve.
* **Source HIV +ve:** 3-drug oral ART for 28 days (e.g., Tenofovir/Emtricitabine + Raltegravir/Dolutegravir) within 72 hours.

### Prevention of Mother-To-Child Transmission (MTCT)
* **Maternal Antiviral:** Start Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) at 24-28 weeks gestation if maternal HBV DNA > 200,000 IU/mL.
* **Neonatal Prophylaxis:** HBIG + HBV Vaccine (1st dose) given to newborn within 12 hours of birth.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic HBV Natural History Phases
| Phase | HBeAg | HBV DNA | ALT | Liver Inflammation/Fibrosis | Old Terminology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1: HBeAg+ Chronic Infection** | +ve | Very High (>10^7) | Normal | Minimal | Immune Tolerant |
| **2: HBeAg+ Chronic Hepatitis** | +ve | High/Fluctuating | Elevated | Moderate-Severe | Immune Clearance |
| **3: HBeAg- Chronic Infection** | -ve | Low/Undetectable (<2000) | Normal | Minimal | Inactive Carrier |
| **4: HBeAg- Chronic Hepatitis** | -ve | Fluctuating (>2000) | Elevated | Moderate-Severe | Immune Escape |

### Chronic HBV Pharmacotherapy Thresholds
* **Treatment Indications (HA Guidelines):** 
* **Cirrhosis:** Treat ALL regardless of ALT or DNA levels.
* **Non-Cirrhotic:** Treat if Phase 2 or Phase 4 (i.e., ↑ ALT > 2x ULN **AND** HBV DNA > 2,000 IU/mL for HBeAg-ve OR > 20,000 IU/mL for HBeAg+ve).
* **Immunosuppression Prophylaxis:** Start NAs before starting systemic chemotherapy, Rituximab, or high-dose steroids (prevents fatal flare).

### First-Line Antivirals for Chronic HBV
* **Entecavir (ETV) (恩替卡韋):** High barrier to resistance; 0.5mg PO daily. Avoid if prior Lamivudine resistance.
* **Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) (替諾福韋):** Highly potent; 300mg PO daily. Preferred in pregnancy.
* **Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF):** Pro-drug of Tenofovir. Less bone/renal toxicity compared to TDF.

### Chronic HCV Pharmacotherapy (Direct-Acting Antivirals - DAAs)
* **Goal:** Sustained Virologic Response at 12 weeks (SVR12) (HCV RNA undetectable 12 weeks post-treatment) = CURE.
* **Regimens:** HA relies on Pan-genotypic DAAs for 8-12 weeks.
* **Sofosbuvir/Velpatasvir (Epclusa) (索非布韋/維帕他韋):** NS5B + NS5A inhibitor.
* **Glecaprevir/Pibrentasvir (Maviret):** NS3/4A + NS5A inhibitor. 
* **Pre-Treatment Screening:** MUST check HBV co-infection status; HCV clearance can cause massive HBV reactivation.

### HIV / Viral Hepatitis Co-Infection
* **HIV/HBV:** Use ART backbone covering both: **Tenofovir (TDF/TAF) + Emtricitabine** or **Lamivudine**. Never use Lamivudine alone for HBV in HIV (rapid resistance).
* **HIV/HCV:** Treat HIV with ART first; use standard DAAs for HCV (check drug-drug interactions with Protease Inhibitors).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Liver Fibrosis Risk Stratification Scores
* **APRI (AST to Platelet Ratio Index):** (AST / ULN) / Platelet count. Score > 1.5 suggests significant fibrosis.
* **FIB-4 Score:** Uses Age, AST, ALT, Platelets. Favorable non-invasive bedside tool.

### Prognosis & Viral Eradication Milestones
* **HBV Functional Cure:** HBsAg loss (rare, <1% per year spontaneously or with NA therapy).
* **HCV SVR12:** >95% cure rate with DAAs. Markedly ↓ risk of HCC and cirrhosis complications, but HCC risk remains if advanced fibrosis/cirrhosis was present prior to cure.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Extrahepatic End-Organ Complications
* **HBV-Associated Nephropathy:** Membranous Nephropathy (children > adults), Membranoproliferative Glomerulonephritis (MPGN).
* **HCV-Associated Nephropathy:** MPGN secondary to cryoglobulinemia.
* **HCV-Associated Malignancy:** B-cell Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma.

### Antiviral Drug Adverse Effects
* **Tenofovir (TDF):** Nephrotoxicity (Fanconi syndrome, ATN), Osteopenia/Osteoporosis. Monitor renal function and PO4.
* **Entecavir:** Lactic acidosis (rare, mostly in decompensated liver disease).
* **Pegylated Interferon (PEG-IFN) (historical/rare use):** Flu-like symptoms, depression/suicidality, bone marrow suppression, autoimmune thyroiditis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a resolved HBV infection from HBV vaccination on a serology panel?Answer: A vaccinated patient has isolated Anti-HBs +ve. A resolved infection has both Anti-HBs +ve AND Anti-HBc IgG +ve.
* Viva Question: A patient prescribed Rituximab for lymphoma is found to be HBsAg -ve, but Anti-HBc IgG +ve. What is the management? Answer: Prophylactic Entecavir or Tenofovir. Rituximab (Anti-CD20) strongly depletes B-cells and can cause massive reactivation of occult HBV.
* Trap: Presuming a positive Anti-HCV indicates active Hepatitis C requiring treatment. Pearl: Anti-HCV remains positive for life even after spontaneous clearance or DAA cure. You MUST check HCV RNA PCR to confirm active viremia before prescribing DAAs.
* Trap: Starting HCV DAA therapy without checking HBV serology. Pearl: Curing HCV rapidly removes viral interference, which can lead to a potentially fatal flare of occult or inactive HBV.
* Pitfall: Using Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) in an elderly HBV patient with CKD and osteoporosis. Use Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF) or Entecavir (ETV) instead to avoid worsening renal function and bone density.
