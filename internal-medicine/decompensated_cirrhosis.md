# Decompensated Cirrhosis & Portal Hypertension (失代償性肝硬化及門靜脈高壓)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Decompensated Cirrhosis
* **Compensated Cirrhosis**: Asymptomatic stage, structural liver damage without severe portal hypertension complications. Median survival >12 years.
* **Decompensated Cirrhosis**: Defined by the overt development of ≥1 of the following:
  * **Ascites (腹水)**
  * **Variceal Hemorrhage (靜脈曲張出血)**
  * **Hepatic Encephalopathy (肝性腦病 - HE)**
  * **Jaundice (黃疸)**
* **Prognosis**: Median survival drops sharply to ~2 years upon decompensation.

### Definition of Portal Hypertension
* **Pathological threshold**: Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG) >5 mmHg.
* **Clinically Significant Portal Hypertension (CSPH)**: HVPG ≥10 mmHg (required for varices formation).
* **Variceal Bleeding Risk**: HVPG ≥12 mmHg.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Leading Aetiologies**: Historically dominated by **Chronic Hepatitis B (CHB)**. Incidence of **Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD) / MASLD** is rising rapidly and overtaking CHB as the primary cause for new-onset cirrhosis.
* **Hepatitis C**: Low prevalence in HK (<0.5%) compared to the West.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Portal Hypertension
* **Structural factor (70%)**: Liver fibrosis/regenerative nodules → microvascular thrombosis → mechanical ↑ intrahepatic vascular resistance.
* **Dynamic factor (30%)**: Endothelial dysfunction → ↓ Hepatic Nitric Oxide (NO) + ↑ Endothelin/Thromboxane → intrahepatic vasoconstriction.
* **Hyperdynamic Circulation**: Splanchnic vasodilation (due to ↑ systemic NO/prostaglandins) → ↑ portal venous inflow, compounding portal pressure.

### Pathophysiology of Ascites (腹水)
* **Splanchnic Vasodilation**: Leads to ↓ Effective Arterial Blood Volume (EABV).
* **Neurohumoral Activation**: Baroreceptors sense ↓ EABV → massive activation of RAAS, SNS, and non-osmotic Vasopressin release.
* **Renal Effect**: Avid Na+ and H2O retention → hypervolemia → transudation into peritoneal cavity due to high portal hydrostatic pressure & ↓ oncotic pressure (hypoalbuminemia).

### Pathophysiology of Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE)
* **Ammonia Hypothesis**: Gut-derived neurotoxins (mainly NH3 from protein breakdown by gut flora) bypass liver clearance via portosystemic collateral shunts.
* **Cerebral Edema**: NH3 crosses Blood-Brain Barrier → converted to glutamine in astrocytes → osmotic gradient → astrocyte swelling & low-grade cerebral edema.

### Pathophysiology of Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)
* Extreme splanchnic vasodilation → critical drop in EABV → maximal RAAS/SNS activation → profound **renal cortical vasoconstriction**.
* **Functional AKI**: Histologically normal kidneys but functionally failing due to extreme hypoperfusion.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Cirrhosis Decompensation
* **Volume Overload**: Increasing abdominal girth, weight gain, bilateral pedal edema.
* **Bleeding**: Hematemesis (vomiting blood), melena (black tarry stools).
* **Neurological**: Altered sleep-wake cycle (insomnia at night, hypersomnolence by day), confusion, lethargy, poor memory.
* **Constitutional**: Profound fatigue, muscle wasting, pruritus.
* **Infection**: Fever, non-specific abdominal pain (rule out SBP).

### General & Neurological Physical Signs
* **Vitals**: Low baseline BP, tachycardia (hyperdynamic state), low-grade fever.
* **Neurological**: **Asterixis (撲翼樣震顫)** (flapping tremor on wrist extension), disorientation, sluggish pupillary response.
* **Facies/Skin**: Scleral icterus, severe jaundice, **Fetor hepaticus** (sweet, musty breath odor).
* **Musculoskeletal**: Severe temporal and proximal muscle wasting (sarcopenia).

### Skin & Endocrine Stigmata of Liver Disease
* **Vascular**: **Spider angiomas (蜘蛛痣)** (SVC distribution: face, neck, upper chest), **Palmar erythema (掌紅斑)**.
* **Nails**: **Leukonychia** (Terry's nails - white proximal 2/3 due to hypoalbuminemia), finger clubbing.
* **Endocrine (hyperestrogenism)**: Gynecomastia, testicular atrophy, loss of axillary/pubic hair.
* **Coagulopathy**: Bruising, petechiae, venepuncture site oozing.

### Abdominal Examination Signs
* **Ascites**: Distended abdomen, everted umbilicus, **shifting dullness (+ve)**, fluid thrill.
* **Liver**: May be shrunken/impalpable (late cirrhosis) or hepatomegaly (if steatotic/HCC). Nodular firm edge if palpable.
* **Spleen**: **Splenomegaly** (due to portal congestion).
* **Collaterals**: **Caput medusae (海蛇頭)** (distended paraumbilical veins draining away from umbilicus).
* **Hernias**: Prominent umbilical or inguinal hernias (due to ↑ intra-abdominal pressure).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Ascites
| Differential Diagnosis | Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cirrhotic Ascites** | Shifting dullness +ve, stigmata of chronic liver disease, SAAG ≥11 g/L. |
| **Malignant Ascites** | Cachexia, primary tumor signs, nodular hepatomegaly, SAAG <11 g/L, +ve cytology. |
| **Heart Failure (Cor Pulmonale)** | Elevated JVP, basal crepitations, orthopnea, SAAG ≥11 g/L but high ascitic protein. |
| **Tuberculous Peritonitis** | Endemic in HK. Fever, night sweats, "doughy" abdomen, SAAG <11 g/L, lymphocyte predominant. |

### Differentials for Altered Mental Status in Liver Disease
| Differential Diagnosis | Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hepatic Encephalopathy** | Asterixis +ve, ↑ ammonia, resolves with Lactulose, precipitant found (e.g., GI bleed). |
| **Subdural Hematoma** | High risk due to coagulopathy/falls. Focal neuro signs, persistent headache, abnormal CT brain. |
| **Wernicke-Korsakoff** | Common in alcoholic cirrhosis. Ataxia, ophthalmoplegia, nystagmus, confabulation. |
| **Hypoglycemia** | Liver failure impairs gluconeogenesis. Sweating, tachycardia, instantaneous response to IV Dextrose. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **ECG**: Rule out QT prolongation (cirrhotic cardiomyopathy).
* **Urine Dipstick & Microscopy**: Screen for UTI (HE precipitant), casts/RBCs (rule out intrinsic renal disease vs HRS).
* **Diagnostic Paracentesis (Ascitic Tap)**: MANDATORY in all patients with new ascites or admitted with decompensation to rule out SBP.

### Blood Tests for Decompensation & Organ Function
* **Liver Function Tests (LFT)**: ↓ Albumin (synthetic failure), ↑ Bilirubin (excretory failure), AST/ALT mildly elevated or normal (burnt-out cirrhosis), ↑ ALP/GGT.
* **Complete Blood Picture (CBP)**: **Thrombocytopenia** (earliest sign of portal HTN due to hypersplenism), macrocytic anemia, leukopenia.
* **Coagulation Profile**: ↑ PT/INR (synthetic failure of factors II, VII, IX, X).
* **Renal & U/E**: ↑ Creatinine (AKI/HRS), **Hyponatremia** (dilutional, correlates with severity of portal HTN), Hypokalemia (diuretic-induced).
* **Venous Ammonia**: Limited diagnostic utility for HE grading, but strong negative predictive value if normal.

### Ascitic Fluid Analysis & SAAG Criteria
* **Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient (SAAG)** = (Serum Albumin) - (Ascitic Albumin).
* **SAAG ≥ 11 g/L (Portal Hypertension)**:
  * Ascitic Protein < 25 g/L: Cirrhosis.
  * Ascitic Protein > 25 g/L: Right heart failure, Budd-Chiari syndrome.
* **SAAG < 11 g/L (Peritoneal Pathology)**:
  * Peritoneal carcinomatosis, TB peritonitis, Nephrotic syndrome, Pancreatitis.

### Imaging & Endoscopic Investigations
* **US Abdomen with Doppler**: 
  * First-line. Assess liver nodularity, spleen size >12cm.
  * **Portal vein Doppler**: Reversal of flow (hepatofugal), thrombosis (PVT), velocity <15 cm/s.
* **CT/MRI Abdomen**: Assess HCC, porto-systemic collaterals, pre-TIPS planning.
* **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (OGD)**: Gold standard for diagnosing and grading esophageal/gastric varices.

### Diagnostic Criteria for Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)
* **Diagnosis**: Ascitic fluid absolute neutrophil count (ANC) **≥ 250 cells/mm³**.
* **Culture**: Frequently negative (culture-negative neutrocytic ascites). Positive cultures typically yield *E. coli* or *Klebsiella*.

### Diagnostic Criteria for Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS-AKI)
* **Presence of cirrhosis and ascites**.
* **AKI**: Increase in serum Creatinine ≥ 26.5 µmol/L (0.3 mg/dL) within 48h, OR ≥ 50% increase from baseline within 7 days.
* **No response**: Lack of improvement in sCr after 2 days of diuretic withdrawal AND volume expansion with IV Albumin (1g/kg/day).
* **Absence of shock**, nephrotoxic drugs, and macrovascular kidney disease (no proteinuria/hematuria, normal US kidneys).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Variceal Bleeding Resuscitation Protocol
* **Airway**: Early intubation if hematemesis is massive or GCS <8.
* **Fluid & Blood Products**: Restrictive transfusion strategy (Target Hb **7-8 g/dL**; over-transfusion ↑ portal pressure and re-bleeding).
* **Vasoactive Agents**: **IV Terlipressin** (特利加壓素) 2mg Q4H or IV Octreotide (奧曲肽) bolus then infusion. Give BEFORE endoscopy.
* **Antibiotic Prophylaxis**: **IV Ceftriaxone** 1g daily for 7 days (reduces mortality and re-bleeding).
* **Endoscopy**: Urgent OGD within 12 hours for Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL).
* **Rescue Therapy**: Sengstaken-Blakemore tube (balloon tamponade) as temporary bridge (max 24h) to emergency TIPS.

### Acute Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) Management
* **Antibiotics**: **IV Cefotaxime** (or Amoxicillin-Clavulanate per local HK HA protocol) for 5-7 days.
* **Renal Protection (CRITICAL)**: **IV Albumin** (1.5 g/kg on Day 1; 1.0 g/kg on Day 3) to prevent HRS and reduce mortality, especially if Bilirubin >68 µmol/L or Creatinine >88 µmol/L.

### Acute Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE) Flare Management
* **Identify & Treat Precipitant**: GI bleed, sepsis/SBP, constipation, dehydration, hypokalemia, sedatives.
* **Ammonia Lowering Therapy**:
  * **PO/PR Lactulose (乳果糖)**: Osmotic laxative + acidifies gut (NH3 → NH4+). Titrate to **2-3 soft bowel movements/day**.
  * **PO Rifaximin (利福昔明)**: Non-absorbable antibiotic to alter gut flora (add-on if poor response to Lactulose).
* **Contraindication**: Strictly AVOID benzodiazepines/sedatives. Do NOT restrict dietary protein.

### Acute Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS-AKI) Management
* **Step 1**: Discontinue diuretics, beta-blockers, NSAIDs, and nephrotoxins.
* **Step 2**: Volume expansion with **IV Albumin** (1g/kg/day) for 48 hours.
* **Step 3**: If no response, initiate vasoconstrictors: **IV Terlipressin + IV Albumin**. (Alternative: Norepinephrine infusion in ICU).
* **Step 4**: Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT) as a bridge to liver transplantation.

### Acute Management of Tense Ascites
* **Large Volume Paracentesis (LVP)**: Drainage of ascites under sterile conditions.
* **Albumin Replacement Rule**: If >5 Liters drained, MUST infuse **8g of IV Albumin per 1 Liter** of ascites removed to prevent Paracentesis-Induced Circulatory Dysfunction (PICD).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Ascites Step-up Pharmacological Algorithm
* **Step 1: Lifestyle**: Dietary sodium restriction (<2000 mg/day or <90 mmol/day). Fluid restriction ONLY if severe hyponatremia (Na <120 mmol/L).
* **Step 2: Dual Diuretic Therapy**:
  * **PO Spironolactone (螺內酯)** starting at 100mg + **PO Furosemide (呋塞米)** starting at 40mg daily.
  * **Ratio Rule**: Maintain strictly at **100:40 ratio** to maintain normokalemia.
  * **Max Doses**: Spironolactone 400mg + Furosemide 160mg.
* **Step 3: Refractory Ascites**: Stop diuretics if recurrent AKI, refractory hyponatremia, or intractable HE. Serial therapeutic LVP or refer for TIPS/Transplant.

### Secondary Prophylaxis for Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)
* **Indication**: Any patient with a prior episode of SBP.
* **Regimen**: **PO Norfloxacin** 400mg daily or **PO Ciprofloxacin** 500mg daily indefinitely (or until transplant).

### Variceal Screening and Primary Prophylaxis
* **Screening**: OGD at diagnosis of cirrhosis. If no varices, repeat q2-3 years. If small varices, repeat q1-2 years.
* **Primary Prophylaxis (for medium/large varices OR small varices with red wale marks/Child-Pugh C)**:
  * **Non-Selective Beta-Blockers (NSBB)**: PO Propranolol or Carvedilol. Titrate to resting HR 55-60 bpm.
  * **Alternative**: Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL) if intolerant to NSBB.

### Secondary Prophylaxis for Variceal Bleeding
* **Combination Therapy**: **NSBB + serial EVL** (banding every 2-4 weeks until varices are obliterated).
* **Refractory**: TIPS (Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt) if recurrent bleeding despite combo therapy.

### Nutritional Management in Cirrhosis
* **Protein Target**: High protein diet **1.2 - 1.5 g/kg/day**. Protein restriction is strictly contraindicated.
* **Feeding Pattern**: Avoid prolonged fasting (causes rapid muscle breakdown). MUST have a **late-evening carbohydrate + protein snack** (e.g., milk/egg) to prevent overnight starvation.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Child-Pugh Scoring System
* Evaluates 1-year and 2-year survival. Uses mnemonic "Pour Another Beer At Eleven" (PT/INR, Ascites, Bilirubin, Albumin, Encephalopathy).

| Parameter | 1 Point | 2 Points | 3 Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bilirubin (µmol/L)** | < 34 | 34 - 50 | > 50 |
| **Albumin (g/L)** | > 35 | 28 - 35 | < 28 |
| **INR** | < 1.7 | 1.7 - 2.2 | > 2.2 |
| **Ascites** | None | Mild/Medically controlled | Severe/Refractory |
| **Encephalopathy** | None | Grade 1-2 | Grade 3-4 |

* **Class A**: 5-6 points (Well-compensated)
* **Class B**: 7-9 points (Significant functional compromise)
* **Class C**: 10-15 points (Decompensated, 1-year survival <50%)

### Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) Score
* Objective formula using purely laboratory values: **Bilirubin, Creatinine, INR, and Sodium** (MELD-Na).
* Predicts **90-day mortality**.
* **Clinical Utility**: Prioritization for liver transplantation allocation.
* **Thresholds**: Score >15 warrants transplant referral; Score >40 implies >70% 90-day mortality.

### West Haven Criteria for Hepatic Encephalopathy Grading
| Grade | Clinical Features | Neurological Signs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grade 1** | Trivial lack of awareness, altered sleep rhythm. | +/- Mild asterixis. |
| **Grade 2** | Lethargy, disorientation to time, inappropriate behavior. | **Asterixis +ve (obvious)**. |
| **Grade 3** | Somnolent but rousable, disorientation to place/person. | Asterixis +ve, hyperreflexia. |
| **Grade 4** | Coma. | Decerebrate posturing, absent asterixis. |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Cardiopulmonary Complications
* **Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS)**: Intrapulmonary vascular dilatations → right-to-left shunting. Triad of liver disease, hypoxia, and intrapulmonary shunting. Signs: **Platypnea** (dyspnea worsened upright) & **Orthodeoxia** (hypoxia worsened upright).
* **Portopulmonary Hypertension (POPH)**: PAH associated with portal HTN. Presents with exertional dyspnea and loud P2. Contraindicates liver transplant if severe.
* **Cirrhotic Cardiomyopathy**: Blunted inotropic response to stress, diastolic dysfunction, and prolonged QT interval.

### Hematological Complications
* **Hypersplenism**: Massive splenomegaly sequesters platelets and WBCs → pancytopenia.
* **Complex Coagulopathy**: Cirrhosis creates a precarious "rebalanced" hemostasis. ↓ Clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X) BUT parallel ↓ in Anticoagulants (Protein C, Protein S, Antithrombin). Result: Patients are at HIGH risk for BOTH bleeding and thrombosis (e.g., Portal Vein Thrombosis).

### Pharmacological Complications & Drug Cautions
* **Spironolactone**: Causes painful **gynecomastia** and hyperkalemia. (Switch to Amiloride if gynecomastia is severe).
* **Lactulose**: Overdosing causes massive osmotic diarrhea → profound dehydration → precipitates HE and HRS (vicious cycle).
* **Non-Selective Beta-Blockers**: Must be stopped in severe refractory ascites, SBP, or severe hypotension, as they can precipitate HRS by blunting the compensatory cardiac output required to maintain renal perfusion.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: When is IV Albumin strictly indicated in decompensated cirrhosis? Answer: 1) Large volume paracentesis (>5L removed), 2) Acute SBP (Day 1 & Day 3), 3) Diagnosis and treatment of Hepatorenal Syndrome.
* Trap: Restricting dietary protein to treat Hepatic Encephalopathy. Pearl: Sarcopenia worsens HE because skeletal muscle is the main secondary site for ammonia clearance. Always ensure high protein intake (1.2-1.5g/kg/day).
* Pitfall: Interpreting a normal INR as 'normal bleeding risk' or assuming high INR protects against clots. Advanced cirrhosis causes a balanced deficiency of pro- and anti-coagulants; patients frequently develop deep vein thrombosis or portal vein thrombosis despite an elevated INR.
* Trap: Treating asymptomatic hyponatremia with strict fluid restriction in a cirrhotic patient. Pearl: Cirrhotic hyponatremia is hypervolemic/dilutional due to severe splanchnic vasodilation triggering ADH. True management is addressing portal HTN; withhold diuretics if Na <120 mmol/L.
* Viva Question: What defines refractory ascites? Answer: Ascites that is unresponsive to maximal doses of diuretics (Spironolactone 400mg + Furosemide 160mg) or when diuretics cannot be titrated up due to complications (AKI, severe hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, or HE).
* Pitfall: Giving Benzodiazepines to an agitated patient with advanced liver disease. This is extremely dangerous as impaired hepatic metabolism leads to drug accumulation, instantly precipitating Grade 4 hepatic encephalopathy.
