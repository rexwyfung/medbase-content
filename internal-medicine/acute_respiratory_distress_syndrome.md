# Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (急性呼吸窘迫綜合症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome
* **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)**: Acute-onset, non-cardiogenic inflammatory pulmonary edema resulting in severe hypoxemia (低氧血症), ↓ lung compliance, and bilateral alveolar infiltrates.
* **Core Pathology**: Massive intrapulmonary right-to-left shunting (V/Q mismatch) refractory to high FiO2 supplementation.

### Direct Pulmonary Etiologies
* **Pneumonia (肺炎)**: Most common cause overall (e.g., *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, Influenza A, COVID-19/SARS-CoV-2, Tuberculosis in Hong Kong).
* **Gastric Aspiration (胃酸吸入)**: Especially in patients with ↓ GCS or bulbar palsy.
* **Pulmonary Contusion**: Secondary to severe blunt chest trauma.
* **Inhalational Injury**: Smoke, toxic chemicals, near-drowning.

### Indirect Systemic Etiologies
* **Sepsis (敗血症)**: Most common indirect cause (non-pulmonary source e.g., intra-abdominal, urosepsis).
* **Severe Trauma & Shock**: Prolonged hypoperfusion.
* **Acute Pancreatitis (急性胰臟炎)**: Release of systemic inflammatory enzymes.
* **Massive Blood Transfusion**: Leading to Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (**TRALI**).
* **Drug Overdose**: e.g., Aspirin, Opiates.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Peak Incidence**: Winter/spring seasons mirroring peak viral pneumonia activity (Influenza, RSV, COVID-19).
* **Demographics**: Elderly patients with multiple comorbidities in HA hospitals carry the highest mortality rate.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Exudative Phase (Days 1-7)
* **Initiation**: Insult → alveolar macrophage activation → release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α).
* **Endothelial & Epithelial Injury**: Neutrophil recruitment → release of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and proteases → damage to capillary endothelium and alveolar epithelium (Type I pneumocytes).
* **Surfactant Depletion**: Destruction of Type II pneumocytes → ↓ surfactant production → alveolar microatelectasis.
* **Edema Formation**: ↑ capillary permeability → protein-rich fluid exudates into alveoli → hyaline membrane formation (hallmark of ARDS) → profound V/Q mismatch and shunting.

### Proliferative Phase (Days 7-21)
* **Cellular Repair**: Type II pneumocytes proliferate and attempt to differentiate into Type I pneumocytes.
* **Exudate Organization**: Fibroblasts invade the alveolar space and transform the protein-rich exudate into cellular granulation tissue.
* **Clinical State**: Hypoxemia begins to stabilize, but lung compliance remains severely ↓.

### Fibrotic Phase (>21 Days)
* **Collagen Deposition**: Extensive interstitial and alveolar fibrosis.
* **Vascular Obliteration**: Pulmonary capillary bed destruction → ↑ pulmonary vascular resistance → **Pulmonary Hypertension (肺動脈高壓)**.
* **Outcome**: Chronic ventilatory dependence or long-term restrictive lung defect.

## 3. Clinical Assessment 

### Detailed History for ARDS
* **Onset**: Acute onset of severe dyspnea (呼吸困難) typically developing within 6 to 72 hours of an inciting event (must be <1 week).
* **Inciting Events**: Recent severe infection, trauma, acute abdominal pain (pancreatitis), or recent blood transfusions.
* **Symptoms**: Severe shortness of breath, rapid shallow breathing, altered mental status (due to hypoxia/hypercapnia), and symptoms of the underlying cause (e.g., fever, cough, abdominal pain).

### System-Specific Physical Signs
* **Vital Signs**: Profound tachypnea, tachycardia, fever (if infectious etiology).
* **General Appearance**: Central cyanosis (refractory to O2 therapy), severe respiratory distress, prominent accessory muscle use (sternocleidomastoid, scalenes), intercostal retractions, diaphoresis.
* **Pulmonary Examination**: Bilateral diffuse coarse crackles (crepitations) and bronchial breath sounds.
* **Discriminatory Cardiovascular Examination**: **Normal JVP (頸靜脈壓)**, absence of S3 gallop, absence of peripheral edema (crucial to distinguish from cardiogenic edema).
* **Signs of Underlying Etiology**: Abdominal tenderness (pancreatitis/peritonitis), surgical wounds, trauma stigmata.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table for Acute Hypoxemic Respiratory Failure

| Condition | Discriminatory History & Examination Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **ARDS** | inciting systemic/pulmonary insult; normal JVP; no S3 gallop; no peripheral edema. |
| **Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema (CHF)** | Hx of IHD/valvular disease; ↑ JVP; +ve S3 gallop; peripheral pitting edema; orthopnea/PND. |
| **Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage (DAH)** | Hemoptysis (咳血); signs of systemic vasculitis (e.g., purpura, hematuria in Goodpasture/GPA). |
| **Acute Exacerbation of IPF** | Chronic progressive dyspnea; clubbing (杵狀指); fine "Velcro-like" end-inspiratory crackles. |
| **Bilateral Pneumonia** | Purulent sputum; localized consolidation signs progressing to bilateral involvement; no rapid onset of multi-organ failure initially. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations 

### Bedside Investigations
* **Pulse Oximetry (SpO2)**: Severe hypoxemia often SpO2 < 85% despite high-flow O2.
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Hallmarks include severe hypoxemia (↓ PaO2), initial respiratory alkalosis (↓ PaCO2 due to hyperventilation), eventually progressing to hypercapnic respiratory acidosis as fatigue sets in.

### Blood Investigations
* **CBC**: Leukocytosis (infection) or leukopenia (severe sepsis), thrombocytopenia (DIC in sepsis).
* **Renal & Liver Function (U&E, LFT)**: Baseline for multi-organ failure assessment (AKI, shock liver).
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ↑ CRP, ↑ Procalcitonin (supports bacterial sepsis etiology).
* **Brain Natriuretic Peptide (BNP / NT-proBNP)**: Value <100 pg/mL strongly supports ARDS over cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
* **Pancreatic Enzymes**: Amylase/Lipase (if pancreatitis suspected).

### Imaging Modalities
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR)**: Bilateral patchy, diffuse alveolar opacities ("white-out" lungs); distinct lack of cardiomegaly, pleural effusions, or prominent upper lobe diversion.
* **CT Thorax (HRCT)**: Heterogeneous dependent ground-glass opacities and dense consolidation with sparing of non-dependent lung regions; traction bronchiectasis in later stages.
* **Echocardiography (TTE/TOE)**: Essential to exclude left ventricular failure or valvular abnormalities; assesses RV function (RV strain secondary to pulmonary hypertension).

### Diagnostic Criteria: The Berlin Definition
* **Timing**: Within 1 week of a known clinical insult or new/worsening respiratory symptoms.
* **Chest Imaging**: Bilateral opacities not fully explained by effusions, lobar/lung collapse, or nodules.
* **Origin of Edema**: Respiratory failure not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload (objective assessment like Echo required if no risk factor present).
* **Oxygenation (PaO2/FiO2 ratio)**: Calculated while on a minimum of 5 cmH2O CPAP/PEEP. (See Section 8).

### Special/Invasive Tests
* **Bronchoscopy with Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL)**: Excludes infection (culture, viral PCR, PCP/PJP) or hemorrhage (progressively bloody aliquots/hemosiderin-laden macrophages).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Initial Resuscitation & Source Control
* **Airway & Breathing**: Early endotracheal intubation (氣管插管) is usually required. Avoid prolonged trials of Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV) if PaO2/FiO2 <150.
* **Source Control**: Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g., Piperacillin-Tazobactam + Vancomycin in HA settings) within 1 hour for sepsis; surgical debridement for necrosis/trauma.
* **Conservative Fluid Strategy (FACTT Trial)**: Target even/negative fluid balance using diuretics (e.g., IV Furosemide) once hemodynamically stable (MAP >65 mmHg) to dry out the lungs and ↓ edema.

### ARDSNet Mechanical Ventilation Strategy (機械通氣)
* **Low Tidal Volume Ventilation (LTVV)**: 4 to 6 mL/kg of **Predicted Body Weight (PBW)** (NOT actual body weight) to prevent volutrauma/barotrauma.
* **Plateau Pressure (Pplat)**: Maintain < 30 cmH2O to prevent alveolar overdistension.
* **Permissive Hypercapnia**: Accept PaCO2 ↑ (even up to 60-70 mmHg) and pH ↓ (down to 7.20-7.25) to maintain LTVV goals.
* **Positive End-Expiratory Pressure (PEEP, 呼氣末正壓)**: Titrate PEEP (often 10-20 cmH2O) matching FiO2 tables to recruit collapsed alveoli and ↓ shunting (prevent atelectrauma).

### Rescue Adjunctive Therapies for Severe ARDS (PaO2/FiO2 < 150)
* **Prone Positioning (俯臥位) (PROSEVA Trial)**: > 16 hours/day. Improves V/Q matching by recruiting dorsal lung regions and draining secretions.
* **Neuromuscular Blockade**: Continuous IV Cisatracurium for 48 hours to prevent patient-ventilator dyssynchrony and ↓ O2 consumption.
* **Inhaled Pulmonary Vasodilators**: Inhaled Nitric Oxide (iNO) or prostacyclins (transiently improves oxygenation by vasodilating ventilated areas; no proven mortality benefit).
* **Veno-Venous Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (V-V ECMO, 體外膜氧合)**: Indicated for reversible severe refractory hypoxemia (Murray Score >3) despite optimal vent/proning.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Post-Intensive Care Syndrome (PICS) Management
* **Definition**: Physical, cognitive, and mental health impairments occurring post-ICU discharge.
* **Pulmonary Rehabilitation (肺部復康)**: Structured exercise training, breathing control techniques, and inspiratory muscle training to overcome profound ICU-acquired weakness.
* **Nutritional Optimization**: High-protein diet to reverse severe catabolic state induced by prolonged ICU stay.

### Management of Pulmonary Sequelae
* **Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT)**: May be required if diffusion capacity (DLCO) remains permanently impaired due to fibrosis.
* **Tracheostomy Weaning**: For patients failing extubation; gradual weaning via T-piece trials and speaking valves.
* **Pharmacotherapy**: NO specific chronic disease-modifying pharmacological agent (e.g., long-term steroids are NOT indicated for late-stage fibrotic ARDS as they do not improve mortality and ↑ myopathy risk).

### Neuropsychiatric Support
* **Cognitive Therapy**: Management of persistent deficits in memory, attention, and executive function.
* **Psychiatric Care**: High incidence of PTSD, depression, and anxiety requires aggressive screening and CBT/SSRI therapy post-discharge.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Berlin Criteria Oxygenation Severity Staging
Assessed on mechanical ventilation with PEEP/CPAP ≥ 5 cmH2O:

| Severity | PaO2/FiO2 Ratio (P/F Ratio) | Estimated Mortality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | 200 < PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 300 mmHg | ~ 27% |
| **Moderate** | 100 < PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 200 mmHg | ~ 32% |
| **Severe** | PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 100 mmHg | ~ 45% |

### Murray Lung Injury Score (LIS)
* **Components**: (1) CXR consolidation quadrants, (2) Hypoxemia (PaO2/FiO2), (3) PEEP level, (4) Respiratory Compliance.
* **Utility**: Used to risk-stratify for ECMO consideration (Score > 3.0 indicates severe lung injury often necessitating ECMO evaluation).

### Prognosis & Functional Recovery
* **Lung Function**: Usually plateaus 6-12 months post-discharge. Most patients regain near-normal static lung volumes, but mildly ↓ DLCO commonly persists.
* **Leading Cause of Death**: ARDS patients usually die from **multi-organ failure (MOF)** or **sepsis**, NOT refractory hypoxemia.

## 9. Multi-System Complications 

### Pulmonary Complications
* **Barotrauma**: Pneumothorax (氣胸), pneumomediastinum, pneumatocele from high plateau pressures/PEEP.
* **Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP)**: High risk due to prolonged intubation and microaspiration.
* **Pulmonary Fibrosis**: Develops in the late phase, leading to restrictive physiology.
* **Pulmonary Embolism (PE)**: High risk due to prolonged immobility and systemic inflammation.

### Extra-Pulmonary Complications
* **Critical Illness Polyneuropathy & Myopathy**: Diffuse flaccid weakness, failing to wean from ventilator (exacerbated by steroids + paralytics).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Stress ulcers (requires PPI prophylaxis), paralytic ileus, transaminitis.
* **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) secondary to hypotension, nephrotoxins, and positive pressure ventilation decreasing venous return.
* **Neurological**: Delirium, long-term cognitive impairment (anoxic brain injury).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you calculate predicted body weight (PBW) for ARDSNet ventilation? Answer: PBW is based strictly on the patient's biological sex and height, NOT their actual scale weight, because lung size does not increase with obesity.
* Trap: Treating suspected ARDS with high-volume IV fluids to "maintain blood pressure" during shock. Pearl: ARDS requires a conservative fluid strategy (keep them "dry"); aggressively treat shock with vasopressors (Noradrenaline) rather than massive fluid boluses to avoid worsening alveolar edema.
* Pitfall: Chasing a normal PaCO2 on the ventilator. Accept permissive hypercapnia (high PaCO2, low pH) as a necessary trade-off to maintain low tidal volumes (4-6 mL/kg) and protect the lungs from barotrauma.
* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of death in ARDS? Answer: Multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) and sepsis, not refractory hypoxemia.
* Trap: Assuming bilateral infiltrates on CXR always confirms ARDS. Pearl: You must rule out cardiogenic pulmonary edema. Check an echocardiogram or BNP, as severe heart failure perfectly mimics ARDS clinically and radiographically.
