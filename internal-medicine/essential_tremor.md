# Essential Tremor (原發性震顫)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Essential Tremor
* **Essential Tremor (原發性震顫)**: The most common adult-onset movement disorder, characterized primarily by a bilateral, highly symmetric action and postural tremor.

### Etiologies & Genetics of Essential Tremor
* **Familial Tremor (家族性震顫)**: Autosomal dominant inheritance pattern present in >50% of patients.
* **Key Genetic Loci**: Linkage to *ETM1* (DRD3 gene) on chromosome 3 and *ETM2* (HS1BP3) on chromosome 2.
* **Idiopathic**: Remainder of cases occur sporadically with no clear genetic trigger.

### Local Epidemiology of Essential Tremor in Hong Kong
* **Bimodal Age of Onset**: Peaks in late teens/early 20s, and then again >60 years of age.
* **Prevalence**: Affects ~1% of general population, rising to 4-5% in adults >65 years.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Essential Tremor
* **Central Oscillator Network**: Dysfunction within the **Cerebello-thalamo-cortical circuit (小腦-丘腦-皮層網絡)**.
* **Thalamic Hyperactivity**: Abnormal, rhythmic oscillatory activity localized primarily to the **Ventral Intermediate Nucleus (VIM)** of the thalamus.
* **Cerebellar Degeneration**: Post-mortem studies show Purkinje cell loss, axonal swelling (torpedoes), and abnormal GABAergic tone in the cerebellar dentate nucleus.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History in Essential Tremor
* **+ve Action/Kinetic Tremor**: Tremor worsens during voluntary movements (e.g., writing, pouring water, eating, shaving).
* **+ve Postural Tremor**: Tremor exacerbates when maintaining a position against gravity (e.g., holding arms outstretched).
* **+ve Alcohol Responsiveness**: Dramatic, temporary ↓ in tremor amplitude 15-30 minutes post-ingestion of low-dose ethanol (reported by >50% of patients).
* **+ve Family History**: Strong autosomal dominant inheritance history.
* **-ve Resting Tremor**: Classically absent (though long-standing severe ET may develop a resting component).

### Physical Examination in Essential Tremor
* **Upper Limbs**: Bilateral, highly symmetric, high-frequency (4-12 Hz) tremor predominantly affecting distal hands/forearms.
* **Head & Neck**: Titubation (rhythmic head tremor, usually "no-no" horizontal or "yes-yes" vertical movement).
* **Voice**: Tremulous, quavering speech (due to laryngeal muscle involvement).
* **Finger-to-Nose Test**: Tremor amplitude ↑ as target is approached (kinetic/intention component), but distinct from true cerebellar ataxia.
* **Archimedes Spiral Test**: +ve tremulousness on drawing spirals; classic single-axis orientation of tremor amplitude.
* **Neurological Status**: -ve bradykinesia, -ve rigidity, -ve gait instability (absence of Parkinsonian features).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Major Differentials for Tremor
* **Enhanced Physiological Tremor**: High-frequency, low-amplitude; 2° to anxiety, thyrotoxicosis, hypoglycaemia, or caffeine.
* **Drug-induced Tremor**: 2° to Beta-agonists (Salbutamol), Valproate, Lithium, Amiodarone, TCAs.
* **Wilson's Disease (威爾遜症)**: Young patients (<40), wing-beating tremor, hepatic + psychiatric manifestations.
* **Cerebellar Tremor**: Intention tremor, low frequency (<4 Hz), highly irregular, +ve prominent ataxia/nystagmus.

### Comparison: Essential Tremor vs Parkinson's Tremor
| Feature | Essential Tremor (原發性震顫) | Parkinson's Disease (柏金遜症) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Type** | Action/Postural | Resting (Pill-rolling) |
| **Frequency** | High (4-12 Hz) | Low (4-6 Hz) |
| **Symmetry** | Bilateral, highly symmetric | Unilateral onset, asymmetric |
| **Body Parts** | Hands, Head, Voice | Hands, Legs, Jaw (Head rare) |
| **Alcohol Response** | Tremor ↓ dramatically | No significant effect |
| **Family History** | >50% positive | Mostly sporadic |
| **Associated Signs** | None (Isolated tremor) | Bradykinesia, Rigidity, Postural instability |
| **Handwriting** | Large, tremulous, jagged | Micrographia (small, cramped) |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Diagnostic Criteria for Essential Tremor (MDS Criteria 2018)
* **Core Criteria 1**: Isolated tremor syndrome consisting of bilateral upper limb action tremor.
* **Core Criteria 2**: Duration >3 years.
* **Core Criteria 3**: With or without tremor in other locations (e.g., head, voice, or lower limbs).
* **Core Criteria 4**: Absence of other neurological signs (e.g., dystonia, ataxia, Parkinsonism).

### Bedside & Blood Investigations for Tremor
* **Thyroid Function Tests (TFTs)**: Exclude thyrotoxicosis.
* **Serum Ceruloplasmin & 24h Urinary Copper**: Mandatory if patient <40 years to exclude Wilson's disease.
* **Renal/Liver Function Tests (U&E, LFTs)**: Exclude uraemic/hepatic metabolic encephalopathy (asterixis).

### Imaging for Tremor
* **Structural Imaging (CT/MRI Brain)**: Unnecessary in classic ET. Indicated ONLY if atypical features (sudden onset, focal neurological deficits) to exclude structural lesions.
* **DaTscan (I-123 Ioflupane SPECT)**:
  * Normal in ET (presynaptic dopaminergic neurons intact).
  * Indicated ONLY when clinical differentiation between severe ET and tremor-dominant PD is ambiguous (PD shows ↓ basal ganglia uptake).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Situational Management of Essential Tremor
* ET is generally a chronic condition, not a medical emergency.
* **Situational Flares**: Tremor acutely exacerbates with massive sympathetic drive (e.g., exams, public speaking, intense stress).
* **Acute PRN Pharmacotherapy**:
  * **Propranolol (普萘洛爾)**: 10-40mg PO given 30-60 minutes prior to the stressful/performance event to acutely suppress peripheral tremor amplitude.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Lifestyle & Non-Pharmacological Management for Essential Tremor
* **Dietary Mods**: ↓ or eliminate dietary stimulants (caffeine, energy drinks).
* **Mechanical Aids**: Use of weighted utensils, thick-barreled pens, and wrist weights (added mass dampens high-frequency tremor amplitude).

### First-Line Pharmacotherapy Algorithm for Essential Tremor
* *Target: Patients with functional or severe psychosocial impairment.*
* **Propranolol (普萘洛爾)**: Non-selective β-blocker.
  * *Dosing*: Start low (10mg BD/TDS), titrate up to max 320mg/day based on efficacy and HR/BP tolerance.
  * *Contraindications*: Asthma, COPD, bradycardia, heart block.
* **Primidone (撲米酮)**: Anticonvulsant (metabolised to phenobarbital and PEMA).
  * *Dosing*: Must start ultra-low dose (12.5mg to 25mg at night) to prevent acute vestibular toxicity; titrate slowly up to 250mg TDS.
* *Combination Rx*: If monotherapy suboptimal at max tolerated dose, combine Propranolol + Primidone.

### Second-Line & Refractory Pharmacotherapy for Essential Tremor
* **Topiramate**: Anticonvulsant. Start 25mg/day, titrate up. Watch for cognitive dulling/renal stones.
* **Gabapentin**: Start 300mg/day. Less efficacious but better tolerated in elderly.
* **Alprazolam**: Benzodiazepine. Used sparingly for severe refractory cases with prominent anxiety component (addiction risk).
* **Botulinum Toxin A (肉毒桿菌毒素)**: Local IM injections. High efficacy for medically refractory **head and voice tremor** (Propranolol/Primidone are usually poor for head/voice).

### Surgical & Advanced Interventions for Refractory Essential Tremor
* *Indication: Medically refractory, severely disabling limb tremor.*
* **Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS, 深部腦刺激)**: Implantation of electrodes targeting the **Ventral Intermediate Nucleus (VIM)** of the thalamus. Continuous, adjustable, reversible. Gold standard surgery.
* **MRI-guided Focused Ultrasound (MRgFUS)**: Thermal ablation of the VIM (unilateral thalamotomy). Non-invasive, irreversible. Approved for unilateral treatment of dominant hand.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Clinical Scoring Systems for Essential Tremor
* **Fahn-Tolosa-Marin Tremor Rating Scale (TRS)**:
  * Evaluates tremor amplitude across all body parts.
  * Assesses specific motor tasks (pouring water, writing, Archimedes spirals).
  * Quantifies functional disability (ADLs).

### Prognosis & Disease Progression of Essential Tremor
* **Disease Trajectory**: Slowly progressive over decades.
* **Amplitude vs Frequency**: Tremor **amplitude ↑** slowly with age (causing progressive functional disability), while tremor **frequency ↓** slightly.
* **Life Expectancy**: Normal. It is non-fatal and does not cause severe cognitive/motor system failure like PD.
* **Future Risks**: Patients with ET have a mildly ↑ risk of developing Parkinson's disease and mild cognitive impairment in late-stage disease compared to the general population.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Essential Tremor
* **Functional Disability**: Inability to feed oneself, drink from a cup, dress, write legibly, or apply makeup (loss of independence).
* **Psychosocial Impact**: Severe embarrassment, social phobia, isolation, and secondary depression.

### Drug Adverse Effects in Essential Tremor Treatment
* **Propranolol**: Bradycardia, hypotension, exacerbation of reactive airway disease (bronchospasm), fatigue, cold extremities, vivid dreams.
* **Primidone**: "First-dose phenomenon" (acute severe ataxia, vertigo, nausea). Long-term effects: sedation, cognitive slowing, depression.
* **Topiramate**: Weight loss, cognitive blunting ("dopamax"), nephrolithiasis, paraesthesias.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic clinical response of essential tremor to alcohol consumption? Answer: Tremor amplitude dramatically but temporarily decreases 15-30 minutes after alcohol ingestion.
* Trap: Assuming a prominent, isolated unilateral hand tremor is early essential tremor. Pearl: Essential tremor is characteristically bilateral and highly symmetric; a prominent unilateral rest or action tremor strongly suggests Parkinson's disease or a structural basal ganglia lesion.
* Pitfall: Prescribing standard initial seizure doses of primidone (e.g., 250mg) for essential tremor. Patients will frequently experience severe acute vestibular toxicity (nausea, severe ataxia, profound sedation) known as the "first-dose phenomenon," leading to complete medical non-compliance; always start at 12.5mg or 25mg at bedtime.
