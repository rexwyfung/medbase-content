# Acute Tubular Necrosis (急性腎小管壞死)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)
* **Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)**: Intrinsic acute kidney injury characterized by destruction of tubular epithelial cells → sloughing, cast formation & abrupt ↓ GFR.
* **Epidemiology**: Most common cause of intrinsic AKI in hospitalized/ICU patients globally & in HK HA hospitals.

### Ischemic Etiologies of ATN
* **Prolonged Hypoperfusion**: Progression from uncorrected prerenal state.
* **Systemic Hypotension**: Shock (cardiogenic, septic, hypovolemic), massive hemorrhage.
* **Renal Vasoconstriction**: Sepsis (敗血症), severe heart failure, NSAIDs, calcineurin inhibitors (Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus).
* **Major Surgery**: Prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass, aortic cross-clamping.

### Nephrotoxic Etiologies of ATN
* **Exogenous Toxins**: 
  * Aminoglycosides (e.g., Gentamicin) → toxicity typically occurs after 5-7 days of Rx.
  * Contrast media (Contrast-Induced Nephropathy).
  * Heavy metals (Lead, Mercury).
  * Chemotherapeutics (Cisplatin).
  * Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) (中藥) containing aristolochic acid (馬兜鈴酸) or toxic heavy metals.
* **Endogenous Toxins**:
  * Myoglobin (Rhabdomyolysis / 橫紋肌溶解症) → crush injuries, prolonged immobilization, statin toxicity.
  * Hemoglobin (Massive intravascular hemolysis).
  * Light chains (Multiple Myeloma cast nephropathy).
  * Uric acid (Tumor Lysis Syndrome).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Cellular Pathophysiology of ATN
* **Ischemic/Toxic Insult** → Depletion of ATP in metabolically active S3 segment of proximal tubule & medullary thick ascending limb.
* **Cytoskeletal Breakdown** → Loss of apical brush border, disruption of tight junctions.
* **Cell Desquamation** → Sloughing of viable & necrotic cells into tubular lumen.
* **Tubular Obstruction** → Cells bind with Tamm-Horsfall glycoprotein → cast formation → ↑ intratubular pressure → Bowman’s capsule pressure > net ultrafiltration pressure → **abrupt ↓ GFR**.
* **Backleak Phenomenon** → Glomerular filtrate leaks back into peritubular capillaries via damaged epithelium.

### Three Clinical Phases of ATN
* **1. Initiation Phase (24-36 hours)**: Insult occurs → progressive decline in GFR. Mostly asymptomatic.
* **2. Maintenance Phase (1-3 weeks)**: Severe tubular damage established. Sustained severe ↓ GFR. Usually oliguric (<400 mL/day) or anuric. Fluid overload, uremia, electrolyte disturbances peak.
* **3. Recovery Phase (Weeks-Months)**: Tubular epithelial regeneration. Often heralded by **polyuria** (due to osmotic diuresis from retained urea & delayed recovery of tubular concentrating ability). Risk of profound hypokalemia.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History & Risk Factors for ATN
* **Fluid Status Hx**: Recent severe diarrhea, vomiting, bleeding, poor oral intake.
* **Medication Hx**: Recent commencement of NSAIDs, ACEI/ARB, Gentamicin, or herbal remedies.
* **Procedure Hx**: Recent IV contrast exposure (PCI, CT scan) or major surgery.
* **Systemic Symptoms**: Dark urine (myoglobinuria/hematuria), muscle pain, recent trauma.
* **Uremic Symptoms**: Nausea, vomiting, intractable pruritus, hiccups, fatigue, metallic taste.

### Physical Examination Findings in ATN
* **Vitals**: Hypotension & tachycardia (if underlying ischemic trigger), or hypertension (if established fluid overload).
* **Volume Status (Fluid Overload)**: ↑ JVP, bilateral basal fine crepitations (pulmonary edema), pitting edema, ascites, S3 gallop.
* **Uremic Stigmata**: Uremic frost (rare), asterixis (flapping tremor), pericardial friction rub (uremic pericarditis), altered mental status.
* **Musculoskeletal**: Exquisite muscle tenderness (suspicious for rhabdomyolysis).
* **Skin/Vascular**: Livedo reticularis or blue toes (if concurrent cholesterol emboli syndrome).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Prerenal Azotemia vs ATN
* **Prerenal Azotemia**: Physiologic response to ↓ perfusion. Intact tubules conserve Na+ & water. Rapidly reversible with volume repletion.
* **ATN**: Structural tubular damage. Fails to concentrate urine or resorb Na+. Does NOT immediately correct with fluid challenge.

### Other Intrinsic Renal Causes vs ATN
* **Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN)**: Hx of allergic trigger (Penicillins, PPIs). Classic triad of fever, rash, eosinophilia (though rare).
* **Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN)**: Presence of systemic vasculitic features (hemoptysis, purpura). Nephritic presentation.
* **Acute Cortical Necrosis**: Usually secondary to catastrophic obstetric complications (abruptio placentae). Often irreversible.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Urine Studies & Indices for ATN Diagnosis
* **Urine Microscopy**: **Muddy brown granular casts** (泥土色顆粒管型), renal tubular epithelial cells & epithelial cell casts. (Pathognomonic for ATN).
* **Urine Dipstick**: +ve for blood but NO RBCs on microscopy = myoglobinuria (Rhabdomyolysis).

| Parameter | Prerenal Azotemia | Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **BUN:Cr Ratio** | > 20:1 | < 15:1 |
| **Urine Specific Gravity**| > 1.020 (concentrated) | ~ 1.010 (isosthenuric) |
| **Urine Osmolality** | > 500 mOsm/kg | < 350 mOsm/kg |
| **Urine Na+** | < 20 mEq/L | > 40 mEq/L |
| **FENa (Fractional Excretion of Na)**| < 1% | **> 2%** |
| **FEUrea (Fractional Excretion of Urea)**| < 35% | **> 50%** |

### Blood Tests for ATN Evaluation
* **U&E**: Rapid ↑ Creatinine & Urea. ↑ K+ (hyperkalemia), ↓ HCO3- (high anion gap metabolic acidosis).
* **Bone Profile**: ↓ Ca2+ (loss of active Vit D synthesis), ↑ PO4- (impaired excretion).
* **Specific Toxins/Enzymes**: 
  * ↑ CK (>5x ULN = rhabdomyolysis).
  * Serum drug levels (Aminoglycosides, Paracetamol, Salicylates).
* **CBC**: Anemia (dilutional or ↓ EPO).
* **VBG/ABG**: Severe metabolic acidosis.

### Imaging & Biopsy in ATN
* **Renal USG**: Essential to exclude post-renal obstruction (hydronephrosis). In ATN, kidneys are normal-sized or slightly enlarged/echogenic.
* **Renal Biopsy**: RARELY required for ATN. Reserved for cases where non-recovery >4 weeks occurs or alternative Dx (e.g., RPGN, vasculitis) is strongly suspected.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Supportive Protocol for ATN
* **1. Discontinue Insults**: STOP all nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, ACEI/ARB, contrast, aminoglycosides).
* **2. Fluid Resuscitation**: If hypovolemic, challenge with crystalloids (Normal Saline / Plasmalyte).
* **3. Fluid Restriction**: Once euvolemic in oliguric phase, restrict total input to match output + insensible losses (~500mL/day).
* **4. Diuretic Therapy**: IV Furosemide strictly ONLY for established clinical fluid overload. *Does NOT alter ATN natural history or accelerate recovery.*
* **5. Hyperkalemia Protocol**: IV Calcium Gluconate (cardioprotective) → IV Insulin + Dextrose (shift) → Resonium / Sodium Zirconium Cyclosilicate (excretion).

### Indications for Acute Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT)
* **A - Acidosis**: Severe metabolic acidosis (pH <7.1) refractory to medical Rx.
* **E - Electrolytes**: Severe hyperkalemia (K+ >6.5) refractory to medical Rx or with ECG changes.
* **I - Intoxications**: Dialyzable toxins (Lithium, Salicylates, Toxic Alcohols).
* **O - Overload**: Refractory pulmonary edema unresponsive to massive-dose IV diuretics.
* **U - Uremia**: Uremic encephalopathy, uremic pericarditis, or severe uremic bleeding.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Post-ATN Recovery Management
* **Polyuric Phase Monitoring**: Aggressive daily monitoring of fluid balance & U&E. 
* **Electrolyte Repletion**: Prompt PO/IV replacement of K+, Mg2+, PO4- lost via profound diuresis.
* **Medication Adjustment**: Renal dosing of ALL maintenance medications until baseline GFR is re-established.
* **Discharge Follow-up**: Outpatient blood pressure & U&E monitoring at 1 month, 3 months, then 6-monthly to ensure complete resolution.

### Prevention Strategies for ATN
* **Contrast-Induced Nephropathy Prevention**: Pre/post-procedure IV hydration (Normal Saline or Bicarbonate). Stop Metformin 48h prior (risk of lactic acidosis).
* **Rhabdomyolysis Prevention**: Aggressive early IV fluid resuscitation to maintain high urine output.
* **Surgical Prevention**: Maintain MAP > 65mmHg intra-operatively.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognosis & Renal Recovery Timeline in ATN
* **Recovery Rate**: Most patients recover independent renal function within 1-3 weeks.
* **Incomplete Recovery**: 5-10% fail to recover fully and transition to Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) or require chronic RRT.
* **Polyuric Phase Duration**: Varies from days to weeks; volume depletion secondary to diuresis can cause a "second hit" ischemic ATN if not aggressively managed.

### Mortality Risk Factors in ATN
* **Overall Mortality**: Ranges from 10% (uncomplicated) to >50% (ICU/sepsis setting).
* **Poor Prognostic Indicators**: Oliguric ATN (worse than non-oliguric ATN), advancing age, baseline CKD, multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS), requirement for mechanical ventilation, sepsis.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Systemic Complications of ATN
* **Cardiovascular**: Arrhythmias (due to hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia), acute pulmonary edema, uremic pericarditis (risk of tamponade).
* **Neurological**: Uremic encephalopathy, seizures.
* **Gastrointestinal**: Uremic gastropathy (nausea/anorexia), ↑ risk of stress ulceration & GI bleeding (due to uremic platelet dysfunction).
* **Infectious**: Uremia → impaired leukocyte function → ↑ susceptibility to sepsis (leading cause of death in ATN).

### Treatment-Related Complications (RRT & Diuretics)
* **High-Dose Loop Diuretics**: Ototoxicity (tinnitus, deafness).
* **Dialysis-related**: Central line-associated bloodstream infections (CLABSI), intradialytic hypotension, dialysis disequilibrium syndrome.
* **Polyuric Phase Depletion**: Profound hypovolemia, hypokalemia (arrhythmias), hypomagnesemia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the pathognomonic finding on urine microscopy for Acute Tubular Necrosis? Answer: Muddy brown granular casts and renal tubular epithelial cells.
* Trap: Giving prophylactic loop diuretics or low-dose "renal" dopamine prevents ATN progression or hastens recovery. Pearl: Neither loop diuretics nor dopamine alter mortality, requirement for RRT, or recovery rate in ATN. Diuretics are ONLY indicated to manage symptomatic volume overload.
* Pitfall: Relying on a FENa < 1% to confidently diagnose prerenal azotemia in a patient receiving Furosemide. Diuretics actively force sodium into the urine, artificially elevating FENa even in a prerenal state. Calculate the Fractional Excretion of Urea (FEUrea) instead; FEUrea < 35% indicates prerenal etiology in patients on diuretics.
* Trap: Assuming oliguria is a mandatory requirement for diagnosing ATN. Pearl: Many forms of nephrotoxic ATN (especially aminoglycoside-induced) are non-oliguric. Non-oliguric ATN generally carries a better prognosis than oliguric ATN.
