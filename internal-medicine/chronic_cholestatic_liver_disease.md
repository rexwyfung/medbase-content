# Chronic Cholestatic Liver Disease (慢性膽汁淤積性肝病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Chronic Cholestatic Liver Disease
* **Cholestasis (膽汁淤積):** Impaired bile formation/flow → retention of biliary components (bile acids, bilirubin, cholesterol) in liver/systemic circulation.
* **Chronicity:** LFT abnormalities (↑ ALP/GGT) persisting >6 months.
* **Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC) (原發性膽汁性膽管炎):** Autoimmune destruction of intrahepatic interlobular small bile ducts.
* **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC) (原發性硬化性膽管炎):** Idiopathic fibrosing inflammation causing multifocal strictures of intrahepatic AND extrahepatic bile ducts.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **PBC:** Prevalence ~40-50 per 100,000. F:M ratio is 9:1. Peak onset 40-60 years.
* **PSC:** Much rarer in Asia/HK compared to the West. M:F ratio is 2:1. Peak onset 30-40 years. Highly associated with Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD).

### Non-Autoimmune Etiologies of Cholestasis
* **Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI):** Augmentin, flucloxacillin, anabolic steroids, chlorpromazine (brief mention; diagnosis of exclusion).
* **Genetic/Hereditary:** Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis (PFIC), Alagille syndrome.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC)
* **Autoimmune Target:** Antimitochondrial antibodies (AMA) specifically target the E2 subunit of Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex (PDC-E2) on the inner mitochondrial membrane.
* **Cellular Injury:** B-cell breakdown of tolerance → T-cell mediated (CD4/CD8) periductal inflammation.
* **Ductal Loss:** Selective destruction of small intralobular/septal bile ducts (<100 µm) → ductopenia → intrahepatic cholestasis.

### Pathophysiology of Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)
* **Inflammatory Cascades:** Dysregulated mucosal immunity (gut-liver axis cross-talk via aberrant lymphocyte homing, e.g., α4β7 integrins).
* **Fibrogenesis:** Chronic periductal inflammation → concentric "onion-skin" fibrosis → obliteration of large and small bile ducts.
* **Ductal Architecture:** Alternating strictures and dilations (beading) impede bulk bile flow.

### Molecular Mechanisms of Cholestatic Toxicity
* **Bile Acid Toxicity:** Hepatic retention of hydrophobic bile acids → direct hepatocyte detergent injury, mitochondrial dysfunction, apoptosis.
* **Fibrotic Progression:** Hepatic stellate cell activation → collagen deposition → biliary cirrhosis → portal hypertension.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Presenting Complaint in Cholestasis
* **Pruritus (痕癢):** Often precedes jaundice by years. Typically worsens at night and involves palms/soles. Exacerbated by heat.
* **Lethargy & Fatigue:** Disproportionate to liver disease severity; centrally mediated.
* **Gastrointestinal:** Steatorrhea (pale, bulky, foul-smelling stools due to fat malabsorption), Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) ache (distension of liver capsule).
* **Bone Symptoms:** Insidious bone pain/fractures (Osteomalacia/Osteoporosis).

### Systemic Physical Examination Signs
* **Cutaneous:** Jaundice (黃疸), widespread scratch marks/excoriations (prurigo nodularis), skin hyperpigmentation (melanin deposition).
* **Lipid Deposits:** Xanthelasma (periorbital plaques), Xanthomas (extensor surfaces/tendons).
* **Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease:** Spider nevi, palmar erythema, leuconychia, caput medusae, splenomegaly, ascites.

### Autoimmune Syndromic Associations in History
* **PBC Associations:** Dry eyes/mouth (Sjögren’s syndrome), thyroid disease (Hashimoto's), Raynaud's phenomenon, limited scleroderma (CREST).
* **PSC Associations:** Chronic bloody diarrhea, tenesmus (Ulcerative Colitis - present in 70-80% of PSC patients).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Features: PBC vs PSC Comparison Table

| Feature | Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC) | Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demographics** | Middle-aged Females (9:1) | Young Males (2:1) |
| **Duct Involvement** | Small intrahepatic ducts ONLY | Intra- AND Extrahepatic ducts |
| **Key Autoantibody** | AMA (Anti-M2) (+ve in 95%) | p-ANCA (+ve in 60-80%) |
| **Bowel Association** | None | IBD (mainly UC) (70-80%) |
| **Imaging (MRCP)** | Normal biliary tree | Strictures & dilations ("beading") |
| **Malignancy Risk** | Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) | Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) (10-15%), Colorectal CA |
| **Transplant Recurrence** | ~20-25% at 10 years | ~20-25% at 5 years |

### Other Differential Diagnoses for Cholestatic LFTs
* **IgG4-Related Sclerosing Cholangitis:** Clinically mimics PSC, but responds to steroids, often associated with Autoimmune Pancreatitis (AIP), ↑ serum IgG4.
* **Secondary Biliary Cirrhosis:** Long-standing obstruction (e.g., recurrent pyogenic cholangitis, chronic gallstone disease, post-surgical strictures).
* **Infiltrative Liver Disease:** Sarcoidosis, amyloidosis, primary/metastatic malignancy.

### Overlap Syndromes
* **PBC-AIH Overlap:** Features of PBC (AMA, cholestatic LFTs) AND Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH) (↑ ALT/AST, +ve ANA/SMA, ↑ IgG, interface hepatitis on biopsy).
* **PSC-AIH Overlap:** Seen mostly in children/young adults; typical PSC biliary strictures + biochemical/histological AIH.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Blood Investigations for Cholestatic Liver Disease
* **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** ↑↑ Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) & Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT) out of proportion to ALT/AST. Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia (late sign).
* **Lipid Profile:** ↑↑ Total cholesterol, ↑ HDL (early), ↑ LDL (late).
* **Immunoglobulins:** ↑ IgM in PBC; ↑ IgG or normal in PSC; ↑ IgG4 (to rule out IgG4-cholangitis).
* **Autoantibody Panel:**
  * **AMA (Anti-M2 subtype):** Highly specific/sensitive for PBC.
  * **ANA / SMA:** May be +ve (especially in overlap syndrome or ~30% of standard PBC).
  * **p-ANCA:** Atypical p-ANCA often +ve in PSC.

### Imaging Modalities for Biliary Tree
* **Ultrasound Abdomen (US):** 1st line. Excludes extrahepatic macroscopic biliary obstruction (gallstones/masses). Normal in PBC.
* **MRCP (Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography):** Gold standard for PSC.
  * **PSC finding:** Multifocal short annular strictures alternating with normal/dilated segments ("beaded" appearance) of intra/extrahepatic ducts.
* **ERCP:** Reserved for therapeutic intervention or if MRCP is contraindicated/equivocal.

### Histopathology (Liver Biopsy) Findings
* *Note: Biopsy is NOT routinely required if serology and typical MRCP/clinical features are present.*
* **PBC Histology:** Non-suppurative destructive cholangitis of interlobular ducts with periductal epithelioid granulomas ("Florid duct lesion"). Ductopenia.
* **PSC Histology:** Periductal concentric fibrosis ("Onion-skin" fibrosis). Obliterative cholangitis.

### Diagnostic Criteria for PBC
* Definite diagnosis requires **2 out of 3**:
  1. Biochemical evidence of cholestasis (↑ ALP) for >6 months.
  2. Presence of AMA at titer >1:40 (or AMA-M2 +ve).
  3. Histological evidence of non-suppurative destructive cholangitis (if AMA -ve).

### Diagnostic Criteria for PSC
* Definite diagnosis requires **ALL**:
  1. Chronic cholestatic liver biochemistries (↑ ALP).
  2. Typical cholangiographic features (MRCP/ERCP showing biliary stricturing/beading).
  3. Exclusion of secondary causes of sclerosing cholangitis (IgG4, recurrent pyogenic cholangitis, ischemia, HIV cholangiopathy).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Ascending Cholangitis Management (Common in PSC)
* **Resuscitation:** IV fluid challenge, correct coagulopathy (IV Vitamin K). Blood cultures prior to Abx.
* **Empiric IV Antibiotics (HK HA Guidelines):** IV Cefuroxime + IV Metronidazole; OR IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin) for severe/hospital-acquired cases.
* **Biliary Decompression:** Urgent ERCP (within 24-48h) for stricture stenting/dilatation if patient remains septic despite IV Abx, or Percutaneous Transhepatic Cholangiography (PTC) if ERCP fails.

### Management of Intractable Pruritus Crisis
* **Plasmapheresis:** Removes retained pruritogens directly from serum.
* **MARS (Molecular Adsorbent Recirculating System):** Extracorporeal albumin dialysis (bridge to transplant for severe intractable pruritus).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Disease-Modifying Algorithm for PBC
* **1st Line:** Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) 13-15 mg/kg/day orally.
  * Improves LFTs, delays progression to cirrhosis, improves transplant-free survival.
* **2nd Line (for UDCA incomplete responders after 1 year):**
  * **Obeticholic Acid (OCA):** FXR agonist. Added to UDCA. Decreases bile acid synthesis. (Contraindicated in decompensated cirrhosis due to hepatic decompensation risk).
  * **Fibrates (Off-label):** Bezafibrate or Fenofibrate. Dual PPAR activation, lowers ALP and improves pruritus.

### Chronic Management Strategy for PSC
* **Medical:** UDCA (moderate dose 13-15 mg/kg/day) is controversial but often used to normalize LFTs. *High-dose UDCA (28-30 mg/kg) is contraindicated* (increased adverse outcomes).
* **Endoscopic:** ERCP with balloon dilatation (± short-term stenting) for dominant strictures causing obstructive jaundice or recurrent cholangitis.
* **Oncology Surveillance:** Annual MRCP/US + CA 19-9 for Cholangiocarcinoma. Annual colonoscopy for colorectal cancer (if concomitant IBD).

### Tiered Algorithm for Cholestatic Pruritus Control

| Step | Pharmacotherapy | Mechanism / Clinical Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Line** | **Cholestyramine** (Bile acid sequestrant) | Binds bile salts in gut. Must space 2-4h from other meds (esp. UDCA). |
| **2nd Line** | **Rifampicin** (150-300 mg daily) | PXR agonist, alters bile acid metabolism. Hepatotoxic (monitor LFTs). |
| **3rd Line** | **Naltrexone** (Opioid antagonist) | Central mu-opioid blockade. Start low (risk of opiate withdrawal-like rxn). |
| **4th Line** | **Sertraline** (SSRI) | Central neuromodulator. |

### Nutritional & Metabolic Bone Disease Management
* **Fat-Soluble Vitamin Replacement:** Oral ADEK supplementation.
* **Osteoporosis Prevention:** Baseline DEXA scan (repeat every 2-3 years). Calcium (1000-1500mg/d) + Vitamin D (800-1000 IU/d).
* **Bone Targeted Rx:** Bisphosphonates (e.g., Alendronate) if T-score < -2.5.

### Liver Transplantation Indications
* **Criteria:** Decompensated cirrhosis (Child-Pugh B/C), intractable pruritus, recurrent life-threatening cholangitis (in PSC).
* **Contraindication:** Established macroscopic cholangiocarcinoma (absolute contraindication in most centers).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Scoring Systems for PBC
* **UK-PBC Risk Score:** Calculates 5, 10, and 15-year risk of liver transplant or liver-related death. Uses baseline albumin, platelets, bilirubin, and ALP/AST after 1 year of UDCA.
* **GLOBE Score:** Validated scoring system predicting transplant-free survival based on age, bilirubin, albumin, ALP, and platelets after 1 year of UDCA therapy.

### Prognostic Scoring Systems for PSC
* **Mayo PSC Risk Score:** Predicts survival based on age, bilirubin, albumin, AST, and history of variceal bleeding.
* **Amsterdam-Oxford Model:** Estimates survival probabilities using clinical parameters at diagnosis and during follow-up.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Biliary & Hepatic Complications
* **Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) (膽管癌):** 10-15% lifetime risk in PSC. Presents with rapidly worsening jaundice, dominant stricture on imaging, ↑ CA 19-9.
* **Gallbladder Carcinoma/Polyps:** High incidence in PSC. Cholecystectomy recommended for any polyp >8mm.
* **Liver Cirrhosis (肝硬化):** Portal hypertension, variceal hemorrhage, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC).

### Nutritional & Systemic Complications
* **Vitamin A Deficiency:** Night blindness, xerophthalmia.
* **Vitamin D Deficiency:** Osteomalacia, osteoporosis, ↑ fracture risk.
* **Vitamin E Deficiency:** Neuromuscular degeneration, ataxia, spinocerebellar syndrome.
* **Vitamin K Deficiency:** Coagulopathy, prolonged PT/INR, bruising.

### Drug Adverse Effects
* **UDCA:** Diarrhea, weight gain.
* **Obeticholic Acid (OCA):** Severe exacerbation of pruritus, hepatic decompensation (in severe cirrhosis).
* **Cholestyramine:** Bloating, constipation, interference with absorption of other drugs.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic finding on liver biopsy in Primary Biliary Cholangitis? Answer: Florid duct lesion characterized by non-suppurative destructive cholangitis of interlobular ducts with epithelioid granulomas.
* Viva Question: A PSC patient suddenly develops rapidly worsening jaundice, weight loss, and a new stricture with dilatation of intrahepatic ducts. What must you suspect? Answer: Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA).
* Trap: Assuming high-dose UDCA is better for primary sclerosing cholangitis. Pearl: High-dose UDCA (28-30 mg/kg/day) is actively contraindicated in PSC as trials showed increased risk of death, liver failure, or need for transplant.
* Trap: Giving Cholestyramine and UDCA at the exact same time. Pearl: Cholestyramine is a bile acid resin and will bind UDCA, rendering it ineffective. Space doses by at least 2-4 hours.
* Pitfall: Forgetting to screen for IBD in newly diagnosed PSC patients. Most PSC patients have underlying ulcerative colitis; always order a full colonoscopy at the time of PSC diagnosis, even if asymptomatic.
* Pitfall: Interpreting isolated elevated ALP as definitive hepatobiliary disease without confirmation. Always check GGT; if GGT is normal, the ALP may be of bone origin (e.g., Paget's disease, osteomalacia).
