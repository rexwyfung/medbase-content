# Viral Skin Infections (病毒性皮膚感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Etiology and Epidemiology of Cutaneous Warts (疣)
* **Pathogen**: **Human Papillomavirus (HPV)** (人類乳頭瘤病毒)
* **Common Warts (Verruca vulgaris)**: HPV types 1, 2, 4, 7
* **Plantar Warts (Verruca plantaris)**: HPV types 1, 2, 4
* **Plane/Flat Warts (Verruca plana)**: HPV types 3, 10
* **Epidemiology**: Extremely common in school-aged children and young adults; transmitted via direct contact or fomites.

### Etiology and Epidemiology of Molluscum Contagiosum (傳染性軟疣)
* **Pathogen**: **Molluscum contagiosum virus (MCV)** (a Poxvirus)
* **Epidemiology**: Peak incidence in childhood (1-10 years); highly contagious via skin-to-skin contact, fomites (towels, swimming pools), or autoinoculation. 
* **High-Risk Groups**: Atopic dermatitis (eczema) patients, HIV-positive patients (often extensive/recalcitrant).

### Etiology and Epidemiology of Cutaneous Herpes Simplex Virus (單純皰疹)
* **Pathogens**: **HSV-1** (predominantly orofacial) and **HSV-2** (predominantly genital, excluded here)
* **Eczema Herpeticum (Kaposi varicelliform eruption)**: Disseminated cutaneous HSV infection occurring in patients with pre-existing skin barrier defects (most commonly atopic dermatitis).
* **Herpetic Whitlow (皰疹性瘭疽)**: HSV infection of the distal phalanx; common in healthcare workers (dentists, ICU nurses) and children (via finger sucking).

### Etiology and Epidemiology of Orf (羊痘)
* **Pathogen**: **Orf virus** (a Parapoxvirus), also known as contagious pustular dermatitis virus.
* **Epidemiology**: Zoonotic infection from sheep and goats. 
* **Local HK Context**: Occupational hazard for abattoir workers, butchers, and wet market stall operators handling raw meat.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of HPV Cutaneous Infection
* **Target**: Virus infects **basal keratinocytes** through epidermal micro-abrasions.
* **Mechanism**: Viral DNA replicates within host nuclei as an episome → viral **E6/E7 proteins** disrupt cell cycle regulation → proliferation of basal cells.
* **Histological Manifestation**: Causes massive epidermal thickening (**hyperkeratosis**) and outward projection of dermal papillae (**papillomatosis**).
* **Immune Evasion**: Virus replicates above the basement membrane, delaying presentation to Langerhans cells, allowing persistent infection.

### Pathophysiology of Molluscum Contagiosum Virus
* **Target**: Selectively infects human epidermis.
* **Mechanism**: Viral replication occurs entirely within the **cytoplasm** of epithelial cells (characteristic of Poxviruses).
* **Histological Manifestation**: Forms large, eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies known as **Henderson-Paterson bodies** (molluscum bodies).

### Pathophysiology of Cutaneous HSV Infection
* **Primary Infection**: Virus penetrates mucocutaneous barriers → replicates in local epithelial cells causing lysis (vesicle formation).
* **Latency Mechanism**: Virus undergoes retrograde axonal transport along sensory nerves to the **dorsal root ganglion** (e.g., trigeminal ganglion for HSV-1).
* **Reactivation**: Triggers (UV light, stress, fever, immunosuppression) cause anterograde transport back to the epidermis → recurrent outbreaks (cold sores).

### Pathophysiology of Orf Virus Infection
* **Inoculation Route**: Direct inoculation of virus into broken skin during handling of infected animals/meat.
* **Response**: Localized viral replication triggers a massive, localized granulomatous and inflammatory response, mimicking pyogenic granuloma.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Physical Signs of Cutaneous Warts
* **Common Warts (Verruca vulgaris)**:
  * Firm, hyperkeratotic, exophytic, dome-shaped papules/nodules.
  * Rough, verrucous (cauliflower-like) surface.
  * **Thrombosed capillaries**: visible as pinpoint black dots upon paring down the hyperkeratotic surface.
* **Plantar Warts (Verruca plantaris)**:
  * Endophytic (growing inward) hyperkeratotic plaques on soles.
  * **Loss of normal dermatoglyphics** (skin lines do not cross the lesion).
  * Painful upon lateral pinching/squeezing.
* **Plane Warts (Verruca plana)**:
  * 1-5 mm flat-topped, smooth-surfaced, skin-colored or light brown macules/papules.
  * Often grouped; exhibit **Koebner phenomenon** (linear configuration along scratch lines).

### Physical Signs of Molluscum Contagiosum
* **Morphology**: 2-5 mm, firm, dome-shaped papules.
* **Surface**: Smooth, pearly, or waxy appearance.
* **Key Feature**: **Central umbilication** (a distinct central dimple).
* **Distribution**: Common on face, trunk, intertriginous areas; often arranged in clusters or linear patterns (autoinoculation).

### Physical Signs of Eczema Herpeticum
* **Morphology**: Clusters of **monomorphic, punched-out erosions**.
* **Evolution**: Vesicles → hemorrhagic crusts.
* **Systemic Signs**: +ve high fever, +ve regional lymphadenopathy, +ve malaise.
* **Distribution**: Predominantly localizes to areas of active or prior eczema (face, neck, flexures).

### Physical Signs of Herpetic Whitlow
* **Location**: Distal phalanx of the finger.
* **Morphology**: Deep-seated, tender, coalescing vesicles on an erythematous base.
* **Associated Signs**: +ve throbbing pain, +ve non-purulent fluid (unless secondarily infected).

### Physical Signs of Orf
* **Location**: Typically solitary lesion on hands/fingers.
* **Evolution Stages (Classic 6 stages)**:
  * Maculopapular targetoid lesion (red center, white ring, red halo) → weeping/weeping nodule → papillomatous/crusted lesion.
* **Associated Signs**: +ve mild regional lymphadenopathy; generally painless unless secondarily infected.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Diagnostic Differentials for Cutaneous Nodules/Plaques
| Presenting Condition | Key Differential | Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Plantar Wart** | **Callus / Corn (雞眼)** | Wart: Painful on *lateral pinch*; skin lines *interrupted*; punctate bleeding on paring. <br> Callus: Painful on *direct pressure*; skin lines *preserved*; clear core on paring. |
| **Molluscum Contagiosum** | **Cryptococcosis ( cutaneous)** | Occurs in severe HIV/AIDS (CD4 <100); lesions lack classic central umbilication; systemic signs present. |
| **Molluscum Contagiosum** | **Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)** | BCC is usually solitary, occurs in elderly (sun-exposed areas), has arborizing telangiectasias, tends to ulcerate. |
| **Eczema Herpeticum** | **Impetigo (膿疱瘡)** | Impetigo has "honey-colored" crusts, non-monomorphic erosions, responds to antibiotics, lacks punched-out ulcers. |
| **Herpetic Whitlow** | **Bacterial Felon / Paronychia** | Felon: Tense, extremely painful, purulent, single compartment. Whitlow: Vesicular, coalescing, clear fluid. |
| **Orf** | **Milker's Nodule** | Paravaccinia virus (cows vs. sheep/goats in Orf); morphologically indistinguishable, differentiated by exposure history. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Point-of-Care Investigations
* **Dermatoscopy for Warts**: Reveals tightly packed verrucous papillae containing central red/black dots (thrombosed capillary loops).
* **Dermatoscopy for Molluscum**: Shows central polylobular white/yellow amorphous structures (molluscum bodies) surrounded by a red corona.
* **Tzanck Smear (for HSV)**: Scraping of vesicle base → Giemsa/Wright stain → reveals **multinucleated giant cells** (low sensitivity, cannot differentiate HSV from VZV).

### Laboratory Bloods & Swabs
* **Viral Swab (HSV)**: Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) is the gold standard (highest sensitivity/specificity for HSV-1/2).
* **Viral Culture (HSV)**: Slower, replaced by PCR, but historically used to differentiate HSV from VZV.
* **HIV Testing**: Strictly indicated for adults presenting with sudden onset of extensive, giant, or highly recalcitrant molluscum contagiosum or HPV warts.

### Biopsy & Histopathological Diagnostic Criteria
* **Skin Biopsy**: Rarely needed; reserved for atypical, recalcitrant, or suspected malignant lesions.
* **HPV Histology**: Hyperkeratosis, papillomatosis, and **Koilocytes** (vacuolated keratinocytes with shrunken, raisin-like nuclei in the upper epidermis).
* **Molluscum Histology**: Cup-shaped epidermal invagination with large, eosinophilic intracytoplasmic **Henderson-Paterson bodies**.
* **HSV Histology**: Epidermal necrosis, ballooning degeneration of keratinocytes, and multinucleated giant cells.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management Protocol for Eczema Herpeticum (Medical Emergency)
* **Triage**: Urgent admission to dermatology or general medical ward; implement contact isolation.
* **Ophthalmology Consult**: Mandatory if lesions involve the periocular region or nasal tip (Hutchinson's sign equivalent) to rule out herpetic keratitis.
* **Antiviral Resuscitation**: 
  * Mild-to-moderate: PO Acyclovir or Valacyclovir.
  * Severe/Immunocompromised/Encephalitic: **IV Acyclovir**.
* **Immunomodulator Cessation**: **IMMEDIATELY STOP** all topical corticosteroids and topical calcineurin inhibitors (Tacrolimus/Pimecrolimus) in affected areas.
* **Anti-Bacterial Coverage**: Start empirical IV antibiotics (e.g., Cloxacillin or Cefazolin) to cover *Staphylococcus aureus* (secondary impetiginization is highly common).

### Acute Management for Herpetic Whitlow
* **Wound Care**: Apply dry, non-adherent dressings to prevent autoinoculation and nosocomial spread.
* **Analgesia**: PO NSAIDs or Paracetamol for throbbing pain.
* **Surgical Contraindication**: **ABSOLUTELY DO NOT incise and drain** (lancing causes viremia, delays healing, and risks secondary bacterial infection).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Cutaneous Warts
* **First-Line (Topical Destructive)**:
  * **Topical Salicylic Acid (15-40%)**: Apply daily after soaking and paring with a pumice stone. Keratolytic effect. Treatment duration: 12-16 weeks.
  * *Efficacy*: ~75% clearance; painless, safe for home use.
* **Second-Line (Physical Destructive)**:
  * **Cryotherapy (Liquid Nitrogen)**: Applied via spray or cotton bud every 3-4 weeks. Destroys infected cells via freezing/thawing.
* **Third-Line (Refractory/Specialized)**:
  * **Topical Imiquimod 5% cream**: Immune response modifier (off-label for non-genital warts).
  * **Electrosurgery / CO2 Laser Ablation**: For highly recalcitrant or extremely thick plantar warts.
* **Lifestyle/Prophylaxis**: Avoid walking barefoot in public showers; do not share towels/nail clippers.

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Molluscum Contagiosum
* **Conservative Management**: Watchful waiting is first-line for immunocompetent children (spontaneous resolution within 6-18 months).
* **Active Intervention (if cosmetic concern, autoinoculation risk, or adult)**:
  * **Cryotherapy**: First-line clinic treatment; single freeze-thaw cycle per lesion.
  * **Curettage**: Physical scraping with a sharp curette (highly effective, requires topical anesthetic like EMLA).
  * **Topical Agents**: Salicylic acid or Potassium hydroxide (KOH) 10-20% solution (causes localized inflammation and viral clearance).

### Suppressive Therapy for Recurrent Cutaneous HSV
* **Indication**: Frequent recurrences (>6 episodes per year) causing significant psychosocial or physical morbidity.
* **Regimen**: PO Acyclovir (400mg BD) or PO Valacyclovir (500mg daily) for 6-12 months.
* **Re-evaluation**: Cease therapy after 1 year to assess natural recurrence frequency.

### Chronic Management of Orf
* **Protocol**: Exclusively supportive. Self-limiting disease resolving in 6-8 weeks.
* **Wound Care**: Clean dressing to prevent secondary bacterial infection.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Host Immune Status Stratification
* **Immunocompetent Course**: Warts and Molluscum are benign, self-limiting, and resolve via cell-mediated immunity.
* **Immunocompromised Course** (HIV, solid organ transplant, biologics, poorly controlled DM):
  * Leads to giant, atypical, widespread, and therapy-resistant warts/molluscum.
  * ↑ risk of malignant transformation in HPV-infected skin (e.g., HPV warts progressing to Squamous Cell Carcinoma, particularly in epidermodysplasia verruciformis).

### Prognostic Timelines for Viral Skin Infections
* **Warts**: ~65% resolve spontaneously within 2 years in immunocompetent individuals.
* **Molluscum**: Individual lesions last ~2 months; overall disease resolves in 6-18 months.
* **Orf**: Spontaneous complete healing in 6-8 weeks, leaving minimal to no scarring.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Viral Infections
* **Eczema Herpeticum Dissemination**:
  * **Ophthalmic**: Herpetic keratitis → corneal scarring and permanent blindness.
  * **Neurological**: Viral encephalitis or meningitis (rare but fatal).
  * **Systemic**: HSV viremia leading to multi-organ failure (hepatic necrosis).
* **Secondary Impetiginization**: Breakdown of epidermal barrier (especially in eczema herpeticum or excoriated molluscum) strongly predisposes to *Staphylococcus aureus* or *Streptococcus pyogenes* infection, occasionally progressing to cellulitis or sepsis.

### Adverse Effects of Destructive & Pharmacological Therapies
* **Cryotherapy Complications**:
  * Intense procedural pain and hemorrhagic blistering.
  * **Hypopigmentation/Depigmentation**: High risk in Fitzpatrick Skin Types III-V (majority of HK population) due to melanocyte destruction; can be permanent.
  * **Neuropathy**: Damage to underlying digital nerves if aggressive freeze times are used on fingers/toes.
* **Salicylic Acid AEs**: Localized irritant contact dermatitis, erythema, peeling.
* **Acyclovir Systemic AEs**: **Crystal nephropathy** (precipitates in renal tubules if patient is dehydrated; mandatory to maintain aggressive IV hydration during high-dose IV Acyclovir therapy); GI upset; hepatotoxicity.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate a plantar wart from a callus at the bedside? Answer: A plantar wart interrupts normal skin lines (dermatoglyphics), exhibits pinpoint black dots (thrombosed capillaries) upon paring, and is exquisitely painful upon lateral pinching. A callus preserves skin lines, has a clear core, and hurts more on direct pressure.
* Trap: Recommending immediate incision and drainage for a patient presenting with painful, grouped vesicles on the distal phalanx of the index finger. Pearl: Do not lance a Herpetic Whitlow. Incision spreads the HSV infection, delays healing, and invites secondary bacterial pathogens. Manage with dry dressings and analgesia.
* Viva Question: What is the most critical initial investigation for an adult patient presenting with hundreds of generalized, large molluscum contagiosum lesions, especially involving the face? Answer: An HIV test to rule out severe immunosuppression (AIDS).
* Pitfall: Continuing the application of topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors on an eczema patient presenting with sudden-onset monomorphic, punched-out erosions. Stopping topical immunosuppressants immediately is mandatory, as continuing them fuels the dissemination of Eczema Herpeticum.
* Viva Question: What are the pathognomonic histological findings for HPV cutaneous warts and Molluscum contagiosum respectively? Answer: Koilocytes (vacuolated cells with raisinoid nuclei) for HPV warts, and Henderson-Paterson bodies (eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions) for Molluscum contagiosum.
* Trap: Being overly aggressive with liquid nitrogen cryotherapy on the dorsal hands or face in a local Hong Kong patient. Pearl: Asian skin (Fitzpatrick type III-V) is highly susceptible to permanent post-inflammatory hypopigmentation or hyperpigmentation from melanocyte freezing; use conservative freeze-thaw cycles.
