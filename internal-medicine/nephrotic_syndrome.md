# Nephrotic Syndrome (腎病綜合症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Nephrotic Syndrome Triad/Quartet
* **Classic Triad (經典三聯症)**:
* **Massive Proteinuria (大量蛋白尿)**: >3.5 g/day (or UPCR >350 mg/mmol)
* **Hypoalbuminemia (低白蛋白血症)**: Serum albumin <30 g/L
* **Edema (水腫)**: Peripheral or generalized (anasarca)
* **Fourth Component (Quartet)**:
* **Hyperlipidemia (高脂血症)**: ↑ Total cholesterol, ↑ LDL

### Primary Glomerulopathies by Age Group
* **Children (<15 years)**:
* **Minimal Change Disease (MCD) (微小病變)**: >80% of cases, highly steroid-responsive
* **Adults**:
* **Membranous Nephropathy (MN) (膜性腎病)**: Most common primary cause in Caucasian adults, 1/3 of primary cases in HK
* **Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) (局灶節段性腎小球硬化症)**: Most common in Black populations, increasing incidence globally
* **IgA Nephropathy (IgA腎病)**: Typically nephritic, but can present with nephrotic-range proteinuria (common in HK)

### Secondary Causes of Nephrotic Syndrome
* **Endocrine**: **Diabetes Mellitus (糖尿病)** (Diabetic Nephropathy - most common secondary cause worldwide)
* **Autoimmune**: **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) (系統性紅斑狼瘡)** (Lupus Nephritis Class V)
* **Infectious**:
* **Hepatitis B (乙型肝炎)** & **Hepatitis C (丙型肝炎)** (Secondary MN, MPGN)
* HIV (HIV-associated nephropathy / HIVAN → collapsing FSGS)
* Syphilis, Malaria
* **Malignancy**:
* Solid tumors (Lung, Colon, Breast) → **MN**
* Hematological (Hodgkin Lymphoma) → **MCD**
* **Infiltrative**: **Amyloidosis (澱粉樣變性)** (AL or AA type)
* **Drugs**: NSAIDs (MCD/MN), Penicillamine, Gold, Lithium

### Local Hong Kong Epidemiology
* **Hepatitis B Endemicity**: High prevalence of HBV-associated **MN** in HK Chinese; must screen HBsAg before immunosuppression
* **SLE Prevalence**: Very common in young HK Chinese females; ALWAYS rule out Lupus Nephritis
* **IgA Nephropathy**: Most common primary glomerulonephritis in HK; ~10% present with nephrotic syndrome

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Glomerular Filtration Barrier Dysfunction
* **Normal Physiology**: Podocyte foot processes, glomerular basement membrane (GBM), and fenestrated endothelium prevent macromolecule filtration via size and charge (heparan sulfate -ve charge) selectivity
* **Pathology**: Podocyte effacement / structural disruption → loss of -ve charge and size barrier → ↑ permeability to albumin and macromolecules → **Proteinuria (蛋白尿)**

### Mechanism of Edema (水腫) Formation
* **Underfill Hypothesis**: Massive albuminuria → ↓ intravascular oncotic pressure → fluid shift to interstitium → ↓ effective circulating volume (ECV) → RAAS & SNS activation → renal Na+/H2O retention
* **Overfill Hypothesis**: Primary defect in distal tubule Na+ excretion (ENaC overactivation by plasmin in tubular fluid) → primary Na+ retention → primary volume expansion → capillary hydrostatic pressure ↑ → fluid transudation

### Mechanism of Hyperlipidemia (高脂血症)
* ↓ Serum oncotic pressure stimulates hepatic lipoprotein synthesis (VLDL, LDL, Lipoprotein(a))
* ↓ Clearance of circulating lipoproteins due to urinary loss of lipoprotein lipase and altered lipid receptors

### Mechanism of Hypercoagulability (高凝狀態)
* Urinary loss of antithrombotic proteins: **Antithrombin III (ATIII)**, Protein C, Protein S
* Hepatic overproduction of prothrombotic factors: Fibrinogen, Factor V, VIII, von Willebrand factor
* ↑ Platelet aggregation + hemoconcentration (intravascular depletion) → High risk of **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE)**

### Mechanism of Immunodeficiency
* Urinary loss of **IgG** and complement factors (Factor B)
* Impaired T-cell function
* → High risk of infections (especially encapsulated organisms like *S. pneumoniae*)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for Nephrotic Syndrome
* **Presenting Symptoms**: Frothy urine (蛋白尿), rapid weight gain, progressive swelling (periorbital → lower limbs → genital → abdomen)
* **Thrombotic Symptoms**: Unilateral leg swelling (DVT), pleuritic chest pain/dyspnea (PE), loin pain + macroscopic hematuria (Renal Vein Thrombosis)
* **Secondary Cause Screen**:
* Rash, joint pain, mouth ulcers, photosensitivity (SLE)
* Polyuria, polydipsia, neuropathy (DM)
* Chronic infections, jaundice, right upper quadrant pain (HBV/HCV)
* Weight loss, night sweats, bone pain (Malignancy / Myeloma / Amyloidosis)
* Recent NSAID use (MCD/MN)

### General & Volume Status Examination
* **Vitals**: Blood pressure (Normotensive in MCD; Hypertensive in FSGS/Secondary causes), resting tachycardia (if hypovolemic or PE)
* **Facies**: **Periorbital edema (眼眶水腫)** (especially prominent in the morning)
* **Limbs**: Pitting edema (sacral if bedbound, pedal/pretibial if ambulant)
* **Abdomen**: Shifting dullness + fluid thrill (Ascites)
* **Genitalia**: Scrotal / vulvar edema
* **Chest**: Stony dullness on percussion, ↓ vocal resonance (Pleural effusion, usually bilateral or right-sided)
* **Skin**: **Xanthelasma**, eruptive xanthomas (due to severe hyperlipidemia), leukonychia (Muehrcke's lines - paired white lines on nails due to hypoalbuminemia)

### Systemic Signs of Underlying Etiologies
* **SLE (系統性紅斑狼瘡)**: Malar rash, discoid rash, alopecia, arthritis
* **Diabetic Nephropathy (糖尿病腎病)**: Retinopathy (fundoscopy), glove-and-stocking neuropathy
* **Amyloidosis (澱粉樣變性)**: Macroglossia (large tongue), periorbital purpura (raccoon eyes), hepatomegaly
* **Hep B/C**: Spider nevi, palmar erythema (if cirrhotic)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Nephrotic vs Nephritic Syndromes
| Feature | Nephrotic Syndrome (腎病綜合症) | Nephritic Syndrome (腎炎綜合症) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Insult** | Podocyte/Epithelial injury | Endothelial/Mesangial inflammation |
| **Proteinuria** | **>3.5 g/day** (Massive) | <3.5 g/day (Variable) |
| **Hematuria** | Usually absent / microscopic | **Macroscopic / Dysmorphic RBCs** |
| **RBC Casts** | Absent | **Present (Pathognomonic)** |
| **Edema** | Severe (Anasarca) | Mild (Periorbital/Pedal) |
| **Blood Pressure** | Usually normal (can be high) | **Hypertension** (common) |
| **Renal Function** | Usually normal initially | **AKI / ↑ Creatinine** |

### Differentials for Generalized Edema (Anasarca)
| System | Diagnosis | Discriminatory Features (History / Examination) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Renal** | Nephrotic Syndrome | Frothy urine, normal JVP, heavy proteinuria |
| **Cardiac** | Congestive Heart Failure | ↑ JVP, basal crepitations, gallop rhythm (S3), cardiomegaly |
| **Hepatic** | Liver Cirrhosis | Stigmata of chronic liver disease, normal JVP, ↓ albumin but no heavy proteinuria |
| **GI/Nutritional**| Protein-Losing Enteropathy / Malnutrition | Chronic diarrhea, profound weight loss, normal JVP, no proteinuria |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Urine Investigations
* **Urine Dipstick (尿液試紙)**: +++ to ++++ Protein (highly specific for albumin)
* **Urine Microscopy**: Exclude dysmorphic RBCs/RBC casts (rules out nephritic picture). May see **Oval Fat Bodies (Maltese cross appearance under polarized light)** and fatty casts.
* **Quantification**:
* **Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (UPCR)**: >350 mg/mmol indicates nephrotic-range
* **24-hour Urine Total Protein**: >3.5 g/day (Gold standard but cumbersome)

### Blood Investigations for Nephrotic Syndrome
* **Diagnostic Chemistry**:
* Renal Function Test (RFT): Urea, Creatinine (establish baseline eGFR)
* Liver Function Test (LFT): **Albumin <30 g/L** (diagnostic)
* Fasting Lipid Profile: ↑ Total Chol, ↑ LDL, ↑ Triglycerides
* **Secondary Causes Screen (Immunological & Serological)**:
* **Autoimmune**: ANA, Anti-dsDNA (SLE); Complement C3/C4 (Low in SLE, Cryoglobulinemia, MPGN)
* **Primary MN Biomarker**: **Anti-PLA2R antibody** (Primary Membranous Nephropathy - highly specific)
* **Viral Serology**: HBsAg, Anti-HCV, HIV Ag/Ab
* **Myeloma Screen (if >40 years)**: Serum & Urine Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP/UPEP), Serum Free Light Chains
* **Diabetic Screen**: HbA1c, Fasting Glucose

### Imaging in Nephrotic Syndrome
* **Ultrasound Kidneys & Urinary Tract (USG KUB)**: Assess renal size, cortical echogenicity, rule out structural abnormalities or obstructive uropathy prior to biopsy.
* **Doppler Ultrasound of Renal Veins**: If acute loin pain, hematuria, or unexplained acute function decline (rule out Renal Vein Thrombosis).
* **CXR**: Check for pleural effusions (transudative).

### Renal Biopsy Indications & Findings
* **Indications (When to biopsy)**:
* ALL adult-onset nephrotic syndromes without an obvious secondary cause (e.g., DM >10 yrs with retinopathy).
* Children ONLY IF: atypical features (age <1 or >10, gross hematuria, low C3, hypertension) OR steroid-resistant (no remission after 4-8 weeks of high-dose steroids).
* **Pathology Findings by Disease**:
* **Minimal Change Disease**: LM: Normal. IF: Negative. EM: **Podocyte foot process effacement**.
* **Membranous Nephropathy**: LM: Thickened GBM. IF: Granular IgG/C3 along capillary loops. EM: **Subepithelial immune deposits ("spike and dome" appearance)**.
* **FSGS**: LM: Sclerosis in parts (segmental) of some (focal) glomeruli. IF: IgM/C3 in sclerotic areas. EM: Podocyte effacement.
* **Amyloidosis**: LM: Eosinophilic amorphous deposits, **Congo Red stain +ve (Apple-green birefringence under polarized light)**.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Severe Edema / Fluid Overload Resuscitation
* **Assess airway & breathing**: Sit upright, high-flow O2 if pulmonary edema.
* **IV Diuretic Therapy**: **IV Furosemide** (higher doses required due to hypoalbuminemia altering drug binding and tubular delivery; e.g., 80-120 mg IV stat).
* **Diuretic Resistance**: Add oral **Metolazone** (thiazide-like) 30 mins *before* IV Furosemide for sequential nephron blockade.
* **IV Albumin Infusion (20%)**: Indicated ONLY if severe symptomatic hypovolemia (hypotension, AKI) or massive anasarca unresponsive to diuretics. Always co-administer with IV Furosemide to prevent exacerbating pulmonary edema.

### Acute Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) Protocol (PE / DVT / RVT)
* **Hemodynamic Instability (Massive PE)**: Thrombolysis (IV Alteplase) if no contraindications.
* **Stable VTE**: Immediate anticoagulation with **LMWH** (e.g., Enoxaparin) or **IV Heparin** (if high bleeding risk/renal impairment).
* Transition to oral anticoagulation (DOACs or Warfarin) for at least 3-6 months; lifelong if nephrotic state persists.
* Note: Warfarin efficacy may be altered; LMWH dosage requires adjustment per eGFR.

### Acute Infection & Sepsis Protocol
* High suspicion for **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)** or Cellulitis.
* Take blood, urine, and ascitic fluid (if >250 PMNs/mm3) cultures.
* Commence broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g., IV Ceftriaxone or Augmentin) covering *S. pneumoniae* and gram-negatives immediately.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### General Measures & Diet Modification
* **Sodium Restriction**: <2 g/day (strict) to minimize fluid retention.
* **Protein Intake**: Normal protein diet (0.8–1.0 g/kg/day). *Do NOT prescribe high-protein diets* (worsens hyperfiltration and proteinuria).
* **Fluid Restriction**: Generally 1-1.5 L/day if severe hyponatremia or unmanageable edema.
* **Vaccinations**: Pneumococcal (PCV13/PPSV23) and annual Influenza vaccines (administer BEFORE starting severe immunosuppression).

### Anti-Proteinuric Therapy (Renoprotection)
* **First-line**: **ACE inhibitors (e.g., Ramipril) OR ARBs (e.g., Losartan)**.
* Titrate to maximum tolerated dose.
* **Mechanism**: Dilates efferent > afferent arteriole → ↓ intraglomerular pressure → ↓ proteinuria.
* Monitor RFT and Serum K+ 1-2 weeks post-initiation (accept ≤30% rise in Creatinine).
* **SGLT2 Inhibitors (e.g., Dapagliflozin)**: Add-on therapy for proteinuria reduction (proven renoprotective in CKD/nephropathy).

### Chronic Edema Management Algorithm
* **Step 1**: Oral Loop Diuretic (**Furosemide** 40-120mg daily).
* **Step 2**: If ineffective, ↑ loop diuretic dose or switch to continuous infusion (if inpatient).
* **Step 3**: Sequential nephron blockade: Add thiazide (**Indapamide** or **Metolazone**) 30 mins before loop diuretic.
* **Step 4**: Add Potassium-sparing diuretic (**Spironolactone**) if hypokalemia develops or ascites is prominent.

### Disease-Specific Immunosuppression (Guided by Biopsy)
* **Minimal Change Disease (MCD)**:
* High-dose oral **Prednisolone** (1 mg/kg/day) for 4-16 weeks, then slow taper.
* Relapsers/Steroid-dependent: Cyclophosphamide, Calcineurin Inhibitors (Tacrolimus/Cyclosporine), or Rituximab.
* **Primary Membranous Nephropathy (MN)**:
* Based on risk of progression (Ponticelli Regimen).
* Alternating months of steroids and alkylating agents (Cyclophosphamide), OR Calcineurin Inhibitors, OR **Rituximab** (Anti-CD20 - emerging 1st line in HK HA).
* **FSGS**:
* Prolonged high-dose Prednisolone (up to 6 months).
* Secondary FSGS: Do NOT use immunosuppression; treat underlying cause + RAAS blockade.

### Hyperlipidemia & Prophylactic Anticoagulation
* **Lipid Lowering**: HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (**Statins**) - e.g., Atorvastatin. Helps prevent atherosclerotic CV disease.
* **VTE Prophylaxis (HK HA Guidelines)**:
* Indicated if **Serum Albumin < 20 g/L** (especially in Membranous Nephropathy).
* Use prophylactic **LMWH** or **DOACs** until albumin recovers to > 20-25 g/L.
* Adjust threshold (e.g., <25 g/L) if additional risk factors present (obesity, immobilization, heart failure).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Indicators in Nephrotic Syndrome
* **Favorable Prognosis**:
* Biopsy shows MCD.
* Complete remission (Proteinuria <0.3 g/day) within 4-8 weeks of steroid therapy.
* Normal baseline RFT.
* **Poor Prognostic Markers (High risk of ESRD)**:
* Biopsy shows FSGS (specifically collapsing variant).
* Steroid resistance (No remission after 16 weeks).
* Heavy persistent proteinuria, uncontrolled hypertension, tubulointerstitial fibrosis on biopsy.

### VTE Risk Stratification (Albumin Thresholds)
* **Highest Risk Pathology**: Membranous Nephropathy (Highest risk of Renal Vein Thrombosis).
* **Critical Biochemical Threshold**: Serum albumin strictly correlates with VTE risk.
* Albumin > 25 g/L: Low risk.
* Albumin < 20 g/L: High risk (Mandates pharmacologic prophylaxis).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications
* **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (Hypovolemia, Renal Vein Thrombosis, ATN), progression to ESRD.
* **Cardiovascular**: Accelerated atherosclerosis and Myocardial Infarction (due to chronic hyperlipidemia); VTE/PE.
* **Infectious**: Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP), severe pneumococcal pneumonia, cellulitis.
* **Endocrine/Metabolic**: Vitamin D deficiency and osteomalacia (urinary loss of Cholecalciferol-binding protein), Hypocalcemia, Protein malnutrition (muscle wasting hidden by edema).

### Immunosuppression-Related Complications (Steroid Toxicity)
* **Endocrine**: Iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome, Steroid-induced Diabetes Mellitus.
* **MSK**: Osteoporosis, Avascular Necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head, Myopathy.
* **Immunological**: Opportunistic infections (PCP, CMV, TB reactivation).
* **GI**: Peptic ulcer disease.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why do we avoid high-protein diets in patients with severe hypoalbuminemia from nephrotic syndrome?
* Answer: Increasing dietary protein paradoxically increases intraglomerular pressure and hyperfiltration, which worsens proteinuria and accelerates renal function decline.
* Viva Question: What is the classic presentation of Renal Vein Thrombosis?
* Answer: Sudden onset macroscopic hematuria, severe flank/loin pain, varicocele (if left-sided), and an acute unexplained rise in serum creatinine in a patient with known nephrotic syndrome (especially Membranous Nephropathy).
* Trap: Assuming all adult patients with nephrotic syndrome need an immediate renal biopsy.
* Pearl: Patients with a long-standing history (>10 years) of poorly controlled Diabetes Mellitus presenting with nephrotic-range proteinuria and established diabetic retinopathy have a high clinical certainty of Diabetic Nephropathy and do not routinely require a biopsy unless atypical features (e.g., sudden onset, RBC casts) are present.
* Pitfall: Giving IV Furosemide as a rapid bolus to a severely nephrotic patient without considering pharmacodynamics. Nephrotic patients have low albumin (reduced delivery of protein-bound Furosemide to the proximal tubule) and binding of the drug by intratubular albumin. They require higher single doses, and concurrent thiazides (sequential blockade) are highly effective.
* Trap: Recommending fluid restriction as the absolute first step for mild edema.
* Pearl: Sodium restriction (<2g/day) is far more important than fluid restriction in early nephrotic syndrome. Fluid restriction is usually reserved for those with concurrent hyponatremia or severe refractory edema.
* Viva Question: A 25-year-old female in Hong Kong presents with nephrotic syndrome, joint pains, and low complement C3/C4 levels. What is the most likely diagnosis?
* Answer: Lupus Nephritis (likely Class V Membranous or Class IV/V mixed), given the demographic (young HK female), systemic symptoms, and classical consumption of complements.
