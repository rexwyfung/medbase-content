# Infective Endocarditis (感染性心內膜炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Infective Endocarditis
* **Infective Endocarditis (感染性心內膜炎)**: Microbial infection of the endothelial surface of the heart, most commonly affecting the heart valves, leading to vegetation formation, tissue destruction, and systemic embolization.

### Microbiology & Causative Organisms of Infective Endocarditis
* **Staphylococcus aureus (金黃色葡萄球菌)**: Most common overall. Acute presentation, highly destructive, common in IV drug users (IVDU) (tricuspid valve) & healthcare-associated infections.
* **Viridans group Streptococci (草綠色鏈球菌)**: Subacute presentation, strongly associated with poor dentition/dental procedures. Affects native valves with prior abnormalities.
* **Enterococcus faecalis (腸球菌)**: Associated with genitourinary (GU) or gastrointestinal (GI) procedures/malignancies, particularly in older men.
* **Staphylococcus epidermidis (表皮葡萄球菌)**: Most common in early prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) (<1 year post-op) & cardiac device infections (pacemakers).
* **Streptococcus gallolyticus (S. bovis) (牛鏈球菌)**: Strongly associated with colorectal polyps/carcinoma.
* **HACEK group**: *Haemophilus, Aggregatibacter, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, Kingella*. Fastidious Gram -ve organisms. Frequent cause of culture-negative endocarditis.
* **Fungi (真菌)**: *Candida, Aspergillus*. Associated with IVDU, prolonged IV antibiotics, and immunocompromised states. Require surgical management.

### Risk Factors & Hong Kong Epidemiology for Infective Endocarditis
* **Patient Factors**: ↑ age, IVDU, hemodialysis (central lines), poor dental hygiene, immunosuppression.
* **Cardiac Factors**: Prosthetic valves, prior endocarditis, unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease, rheumatic heart disease.
* **Hong Kong Local Context**: 
  * Decreasing incidence of rheumatic valvular disease as a primary cause.
  * ↑ Incidence of degenerative valvular disease (calcific aortic stenosis, mitral annular calcification) in the aging HK population.
  * Frequent healthcare-associated MRSA bacteremia causing acute IE.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Infective Endocarditis Vegetation Formation
* **Endothelial Injury**: Turbulent blood flow (from structural heart defects) → focal endothelial damage.
* **Thrombus Formation**: Exposure of subendothelial collagen → localized platelet aggregation & fibrin deposition → **Non-Bacterial Thrombotic Endocarditis (NBTE)**.
* **Bacteremia & Colonization**: Transient bacteremia (e.g., from dental extraction, IV cannula) → bacteria adhere to NBTE via surface adhesins (fibronectin-binding proteins).
* **Vegetation Maturation**: Bacteria multiply & stimulate further tissue factor release → sequential layering of platelets and fibrin → envelops organisms, shielding them from host phagocytes and antibiotics.

### Mechanism of Embolic and Immunologic Phenomena in Infective Endocarditis
* **Embolization**: Friable vegetations fragment → systemic arterial circulation (left-sided) → infarction of brain, spleen, kidneys; OR pulmonary circulation (right-sided) → septic pulmonary emboli.
* **Immunologic Phenomena**: Chronic continuous bacteremia → immune system generates circulating immune complexes → deposition in microvasculature (glomeruli, skin, joints) → systemic vasculitis and hypocomplementemia.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Patient History in Infective Endocarditis
* **Constitutional Symptoms**: Prolonged fever of unknown origin (FUO), chills, night sweats, unexplained weight loss, fatigue.
* **Targeted History**: Recent dental procedures, GU/GI instrumentation, IV drug use, recent intravascular catheterization.
* **Systemic Symptoms**: Myalgia, arthralgia, acute back pain (suspect vertebral osteomyelitis/discitis).

### Cardiac Physical Examination Signs in Infective Endocarditis
* **Murmurs**: New or changing regurgitant murmurs (Aortic Regurgitation, Mitral Regurgitation).
* **Heart Failure Signs**: Basal crepitations, elevated JVP, peripheral edema.
* **Arrhythmias**: Irregular pulse (new-onset AF), bradycardia (suspect complete heart block secondary to aortic root abscess).

### Peripheral Stigmata of Infective Endocarditis
* **Osler's Nodes (奧斯勒結節)**: Painful, violaceous, raised nodules on the pads of fingers and toes (immunologic).
* **Janeway Lesions (詹韋氏病變)**: Painless, erythematous/hemorrhagic macules on palms and soles (micro-embolic).
* **Splinter Hemorrhages (甲下線狀出血)**: Non-blanching, linear reddish-brown streaks in the nail beds (micro-embolic).
* **Roth Spots (羅斯氏斑)**: Exudative, edematous hemorrhagic lesions of the retina with pale centers (immunologic).
* **Ocular/Mucosal**: Petechiae on conjunctivae, palate, and buccal mucosa.
* **Abdominal**: Splenomegaly (due to reactive hyperplasia or splenic infarcts).
* **Extremities**: Clubbing (杵狀指) (seen in prolonged/subacute cases >6 weeks).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Differentials for Infective Endocarditis
| Condition | Key Discriminatory Features vs. Infective Endocarditis |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Bacterial Thrombotic Endocarditis (NBTE)** | Often associated with advanced malignancy (Marantic endocarditis) or hypercoagulable states. Blood cultures sterile. No valvular destruction. |
| **Libman-Sacks Endocarditis (利布曼-薩克斯心內膜炎)** | Associated with SLE and Antiphospholipid Syndrome. Vegetations on *both* sides of the valve. Negative cultures. +ve ANA/dsDNA. |
| **Acute Rheumatic Fever (急性風濕熱)** | Hx of preceding Group A Strep pharyngitis. Follows Jones Criteria (polyarthritis, erythema marginatum, chorea). Negative serial blood cultures. |
| **Atrial Myxoma (心房黏液瘤)** | Can present with fever, emboli, and murmur ("tumor plop"). Echo shows mobile mass typically attached to atrial septum (fossa ovalis), not valves. |
| **Systemic Vasculitis (e.g., ANCA)** | Can cause fever, purpura, and GN. Negative blood cultures, Echo -ve for vegetations, +ve ANCA serology. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations for Infective Endocarditis
* **Blood Cultures (CRITICAL)**: Minimum 3 sets (aerobic + anaerobic) drawn from different venipuncture sites, spaced at least 30-60 mins apart, **BEFORE** initiating empirical Abx.
* **Electrocardiogram (ECG)**: Monitor for new AV blocks (1st degree block / PR prolongation) → highly suggestive of paravalvular abscess.
* **Basic Bloods**: 
  * Complete Blood Count (CBC): Normochromic normocytic anemia, leukocytosis.
  * Inflammatory markers: ↑ CRP, ↑ ESR (present in >90%).
* **Immunologic Workup**: +ve Rheumatoid Factor (RF) (in 50% of subacute cases), ↓ Complement levels (C3, C4).
* **Urinalysis**: Microscopic hematuria, proteinuria, RBC casts (immune complex glomerulonephritis).

### Imaging Studies for Infective Endocarditis
* **Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE) (經胸壁心臟超聲波)**: 1st line imaging for all suspected cases. Evaluates valve structure, gross vegetations (>5mm), and ventricular function.
* **Transesophageal Echocardiogram (TEE) (經食道心臟超聲波)**: Higher sensitivity. Mandatory if TTE is negative but clinical suspicion remains high, in patients with prosthetic valves, or to rule out local complications (abscess, fistula).
* **CT/MRI Brain**: If neurologic symptoms arise (embolic strokes, mycotic aneurysms).
* **CT Chest/Abdomen**: To identify silent septic pulmonary emboli (right-sided IE) or splenic/renal infarcts (left-sided IE).

### Modified Duke Criteria for Diagnosis of Infective Endocarditis
| Category | Criteria Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Major Criteria** | 1. **Blood Culture +ve**: Typical organisms from 2 separate cultures, OR persistently +ve cultures drawn >12h apart, OR single +ve culture for *Coxiella burnetii*.<br>2. **Evidence of Endocardial Involvement**: Echo +ve for vegetation, abscess, or new partial dehiscence of prosthetic valve, OR new valvular regurgitation. |
| **Minor Criteria** | 1. **Predisposing Heart Condition** or IVDU.<br>2. **Fever** >38.0°C.<br>3. **Vascular Phenomena**: Major arterial emboli, septic pulmonary infarcts, mycotic aneurysm, intracranial hemorrhage, conjunctival hemorrhages, Janeway lesions.<br>4. **Immunologic Phenomena**: Glomerulonephritis, Osler nodes, Roth spots, +ve RF.<br>5. **Microbiological Evidence**: +ve blood culture not meeting major criteria. |
| **Diagnosis Rules** | **Definite IE**: 2 Major OR 1 Major + 3 Minor OR 5 Minor.<br>**Possible IE**: 1 Major + 1 Minor OR 3 Minor.<br>**Rejected IE**: Firm alternate Dx, resolution with <4 days of Abx, no pathological evidence at surgery. |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Empiric Antibiotic Therapy Algorithms for Infective Endocarditis
* **Native Valve & Community-Acquired (HK HA Protocol)**:
  * Standard: **Amoxicillin/Clavulanate (阿莫西林/克拉維酸)** IV + **Gentamicin (慶大黴素)** IV.
  * Penicillin-allergic/MRSA suspected: **Vancomycin (萬古黴素)** IV + **Gentamicin** IV.
* **Prosthetic Valve / Healthcare-Associated**:
  * Regimen: **Vancomycin** IV + **Gentamicin** IV + **Rifampicin (利福平)** PO/IV. (Rifampicin is crucial for penetrating staphylococcal biofilms on hardware).
* **IVDU (Right-Sided Suspected S. aureus)**:
  * Regimen: **Vancomycin** IV (or **Cloxacillin** if known MSSA).

### Indications for Urgent Valvular Surgery in Infective Endocarditis
* **Heart Failure**: Severe valve dysfunction (acute AR or MR) causing refractory pulmonary edema or cardiogenic shock.
* **Uncontrolled Infection**: Paravalvular extension (abscess, false aneurysm, fistula, new AV block), persistently +ve blood cultures >7 days despite optimal Abx, fungal/highly resistant organisms.
* **Prevention of Embolism**: Mobile vegetation >10mm with prior embolic event, or any vegetation >15mm on the aortic/mitral valve.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Management Following Infective Endocarditis
* **Targeted Antibiotic Therapy**: Prolonged IV course (4-6 weeks) based strictly on Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) from blood cultures. 
* **OPAT (Outpatient Parenteral Antimicrobial Therapy)**: May be considered after 2 weeks of inpatient IV therapy if patient is afebrile, clinically stable, has no complications, and reliable access exists.
* **Surveillance Echocardiography**: Baseline TTE post-treatment completion to establish new baseline valve morphology/function.

### Endocarditis Prophylaxis (預防性抗生素) Algorithm
* **Indications (Highest Risk Patients ONLY)**:
  1. Prosthetic heart valves or prosthetic material used for valve repair.
  2. Prior history of Infective Endocarditis.
  3. Unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease (CHD), or repaired CHD with residual shunts/defects.
  4. Cardiac transplant recipients who develop cardiac valvulopathy.
* **Procedures Requiring Prophylaxis**: Dental procedures involving manipulation of gingival tissue, periapical region of teeth, or perforation of oral mucosa. (Not routine GI/GU scopes unless active infection present).
* **Pharmacology (Administered 30-60 mins prior)**:
  * Standard: **Amoxicillin** 2g PO.
  * Penicillin-allergic: **Clindamycin** 600mg PO OR **Clarithromycin** 500mg PO.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Factors & Mortality Risk in Infective Endocarditis
* **High-Risk Organisms**: *Staphylococcus aureus* (↑ mortality, highly destructive), Non-HACEK Gram-negatives, Fungi.
* **High-Risk Patient Features**: Advanced age, prosthetic valve involvement (PVE > NVE), heart failure (highest predictor of mortality), intracerebral hemorrhage.
* **Surgical Risk Stratification**: **EuroSCORE II** is utilized locally to estimate perioperative mortality in patients requiring valvular replacement for endocarditis complications.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Cardiac & Local Complications of Infective Endocarditis
* **Congestive Heart Failure (心臟衰竭)**: Most common complication and leading cause of death. Driven by acute valvular destruction and chordae tendineae rupture.
* **Paravalvular Abscess**: Extension into the myocardium. Aortic root abscess classically causes destruction of the bundle of His → Heart Block.
* **Myocarditis / Pericarditis**: Direct local extension or immune-complex mediated.

### Systemic Embolic & Immunologic Complications of Infective Endocarditis
* **Neurologic**: Ischemic stroke (MCA territory most common), infectious intracranial aneurysms (mycotic aneurysms) → risk of fatal subarachnoid hemorrhage.
* **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) due to immune complex Glomerulonephritis (腎小球腎炎) or direct renal infarction.
* **Drug Adverse Effects**: 
  * Gentamicin → Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity (requires therapeutic drug monitoring of trough levels).
  * Vancomycin → Red Man Syndrome (infusion rate related), nephrotoxicity.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the clinical significance of a new PR interval prolongation on an ECG in a patient with Infective Endocarditis?
  Answer: It is a hallmark sign of an aortic root abscess extending into the intraventricular septum and compressing the bundle of His, which is an absolute indication for urgent cardiothoracic surgery.
* Trap: Ruling out Infective Endocarditis because a patient has sterile (negative) blood cultures.
  Pearl: Culture-negative endocarditis accounts for 5-10% of cases. It is often due to prior antibiotic administration, fastidious organisms (HACEK), intracellular bacteria (*Coxiella*, *Bartonella*, *Chlamydia*), or fungi. Serology and PCR from excised valve tissue are required for diagnosis.
* Pitfall: Administering empiric antibiotics in a hemodynamically stable patient with suspected subacute endocarditis before drawing blood cultures.
  This significantly reduces the diagnostic yield of blood cultures, ruining the ability to direct targeted therapy. In subacute cases, withhold antibiotics until 3 sets of cultures are drawn over several hours.
* Viva Question: Why is Rifampicin added to the antibiotic regimen for Prosthetic Valve Endocarditis but NOT Native Valve Endocarditis?
  Answer: Rifampicin has unique abilities to penetrate the protective staphylococcal biofilm that forms on foreign hardware, whereas native valve vegetations are primarily fibrin and platelets which do not require biofilm penetration.
* Trap: Assuming every patient with an audible heart murmur and a dental procedure requires prophylactic antibiotics.
  Pearl: Current local and international guidelines strictly limit prophylaxis to patients with the highest risk of adverse outcomes (prosthetic valves, prior IE, specific congenital heart diseases). Routine valvular lesions like mitral valve prolapse or acquired aortic stenosis no longer require prophylaxis.
