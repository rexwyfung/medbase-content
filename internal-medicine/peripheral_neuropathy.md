# Peripheral Neuropathy (周邊神經病變)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Peripheral Neuropathy (周邊神經病變)**: Pathological disorder of peripheral nerves (motor, sensory, or autonomic) outside the brain and spinal cord.
* **Polyneuropathy**: Generalized, usually symmetrical, distally predominant (stocking-glove).
* **Mononeuropathy**: Isolated damage to a single nerve.
* **Mononeuropathy Multiplex (多發性單神經病變)**: Asymmetrical, asynchronous damage to ≥2 separate nerves.

### Etiologies of Peripheral Neuropathy (VITAMINS)
* **Vascular/Vasculitis**: Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN), Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA), Rheumatoid Arthritis.
* **Infective**: Leprosy (worldwide), HIV, Lyme, Syphilis, Hepatitis B/C, Diphtheria.
* **Toxic/Trauma**: Alcohol (酒精), Chemotherapy (Vincristine, Cisplatin), Isoniazid, Heavy metals (Pb, As).
* **Autoimmune**: Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS), Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy (CIDP), Paraneoplastic syndromes.
* **Metabolic**: **Diabetes Mellitus (糖尿病)** (Most common), Uremia, Hypothyroidism, Porphyria.
* **Inherited**: Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) disease, Familial Amyloid Polyneuropathy.
* **Nutritional**: B12 deficiency (Subacute combined degeneration), B1/B6 deficiency, Folate deficiency.
* **Structural/Neoplastic**: Compression (Carpal tunnel), Amyloidosis, Myeloma, Paraproteinemia (MGUS).

### Local Epidemiology of Peripheral Neuropathy in Hong Kong
* **Diabetic Neuropathy**: Accounts for >60% of polyneuropathy cases in Hospital Authority (HA) clinics.
* **Prevalence**: ~30% of all diabetic patients in HK develop peripheral neuropathy.
* **Toxin-induced**: Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) is rising locally due to prolonged oncological survival.
* **Infective**: Leprosy is now exceedingly rare in HK; HIV-related and Hepatitis B-associated PAN remain relevant.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Axonal Degeneration
* **Mechanism**: Metabolic derangement or toxicity directly damages the axon.
* **Dying-Back Phenomenon (Wallerian-like degeneration)**: Distal segments of the longest nerves undergo energy failure first → progressive proximal degeneration.
* **Recovery**: Slow (1-2 mm/day) via axonal sprouting; often incomplete.

### Pathophysiology of Segmental Demyelination
* **Mechanism**: Autoimmune (e.g., GBS) or inherited (e.g., CMT) damage to **Schwann cells** or myelin sheath.
* **Effect**: Loss of saltatory conduction → severe ↓ in conduction velocity or conduction block.
* **Recovery**: Rapid (days to weeks) via remyelination if axonal integrity remains intact.

### Pathophysiology of Small vs. Large Fiber Neuropathy
* **Small Fibers (Unmyelinated C / thinly myelinated Aδ)**: Mediate pain, temperature, autonomic function. Damage → burning pain, allodynia, dysautonomia.
* **Large Fibers (Heavily myelinated Aα / Aβ)**: Mediate vibration, proprioception, motor control. Damage → ataxia, weakness, loss of reflexes.

### Pathogenic Mechanisms in Diabetic Neuropathy
* **Metabolic**: Hyperglycemia → ↑ Polyol pathway flux → intracellular accumulation of sorbitol → osmotic stress & ↓ myo-inositol.
* **Vascular**: Microvascular damage to vasa nervorum → nerve ischemia.
* **Glycation**: Advanced Glycation End-products (AGEs) → structural protein disruption.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Onset & Progression**: Acute (days = GBS, Vasculitis), Subacute (weeks = Toxic, Nutritional), Chronic (years = DM, CMT, CIDP).
* **Sensory Symptoms**:
  * **Negative**: Numbness, "walking on cotton wool", unsteadiness in dark.
  * **Positive**: Burning, tingling, lancinating pain, allodynia (pain from non-painful stimulus).
* **Motor Symptoms**: Tripping (foot drop), difficulty manipulating small objects (buttons/keys), muscle cramps.
* **Autonomic Symptoms**: Postural dizziness, erectile dysfunction, altered sweating, early satiety, nocturnal diarrhea.
* **Systems Review**: Hx of DM, alcohol intake, recent GI/resp infection (GBS), occupational toxin exposure, family history (high arches/hammer toes).

### Motor Physical Signs in Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) Signs**:
  * **Muscle Wasting**: Distal predominance (thenar/hypothenar eminences, intrinsic foot muscles).
  * **Fasciculations**: Present but less common than in anterior horn cell disease.
  * **Weakness**: Pyramidal distribution absent; affects distal muscles first (e.g., ankle dorsiflexion → foot drop).
* **Reflexes**: Hyporeflexia or Areflexia (early loss of ankle jerks).

### Sensory Physical Signs in Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Stocking-Glove Distribution**: Symmetrical loss of sensation starting distally.
* **Large Fiber Signs**:
  * ↓ Vibration sense (test with 128Hz tuning fork at bony prominences).
  * ↓ Proprioception (joint position sense → positive Romberg’s sign).
* **Small Fiber Signs**:
  * ↓ Pinprick sensation.
  * ↓ Temperature sensation (hot/cold tubes).
* **Peripheral Nerves**: Palpable nerve enlargement (Leprosy, CMT, Amyloid, CIDP).

### Autonomic & Trophic Physical Signs in Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Autonomic**: Postural hypotension (>20mmHg drop in systolic BP), resting tachycardia.
* **Trophic Changes**: Hairless, shiny skin, brittle nails.
* **Structural Complications**: **Charcot joint (夏科氏關節)** (painless deformed joint), neuropathic ulcers (pressure points like metatarsal heads), pes cavus & hammer toes (CMT).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Neuropathy Patterns
| Pattern | Key Differentials | Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Symmetrical Polyneuropathy** | DM, Alcohol, B12 ↓, CKD, Drugs | Distal stocking-glove, gradual onset, bilateral. |
| **Mononeuropathy Multiplex** | Vasculitis (PAN, EGPA), Amyloidosis, DM, Leprosy | Asymmetrical, stepwise nerve involvement, systemic signs (rash, fever). |
| **Acute Ascending Neuropathy** | GBS, Porphyria, Diphtheria, Tick paralysis | Rapid onset, post-infectious, areflexia, motor > sensory. |
| **Predominantly Motor** | CMT, Lead toxicity, GBS, CIDP, Porphyria | Minimal sensory loss, prominent wasting/weakness. |
| **Predominantly Sensory** | Paraneoplastic (anti-Hu), Sjogren's, B12 ↓, Cisplatin | Sensory ataxia, severe proprioception loss, preserved motor power. |

### Topographical Differentiation: Neuropathy vs. Radiculopathy vs. Myopathy
| Feature | Polyneuropathy | Radiculopathy | Myopathy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Distribution** | Distal (Stocking-glove) | Dermatomal/Myotomal | Proximal (Shoulder/Hip girdle) |
| **Sensory Loss** | Yes (Distal) | Yes (Dermatomal) | No |
| **Reflexes** | Distal ↓ (Ankle) | Segmental ↓ | Normal until late stage |
| **Fasciculations** | Rare | Occasional | No |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Urine Dipstick**: For glucose (DM) and protein (Myeloma/Amyloidosis).
* **Postural BP**: Document dysautonomia (drop in SBP ≥20 mmHg or DBP ≥10 mmHg).
* **Monofilament Test (10g)**: Screening for loss of protective sensation in diabetic foot.

### First-Tier Blood Tests for Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Fasting Glucose & HbA1c**: DM screening.
* **Renal & Liver Function Tests (LRFT)**: Uremia, chronic liver disease, baseline for drug prescribing.
* **Complete Blood Picture (CBP) & Smear**: Macrocytosis (B12/Folate ↓, Alcohol), basophilic stippling (Lead).
* **Vitamin B12 & Folate**: R/O Subacute combined degeneration.
* **Thyroid Function Tests (TFT)**: Hypothyroidism.

### Second-Tier & Immunological Blood Tests for Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Serum & Urine Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP/UPEP)**: Paraproteinemia, Myeloma, POEMS syndrome.
* **Autoimmune Markers**: ANA, ENA, ANCA (Vasculitis), Rheumatoid Factor.
* **Paraneoplastic/Specific Antibodies**:
  * **Anti-Hu**: Sensory neuronopathy (SCLC).
  * **Anti-MAG**: IgM paraproteinemic neuropathy.
  * **Anti-GM1**: Multifocal Motor Neuropathy (MMN).
  * **Anti-GQ1b**: Miller Fisher Syndrome (variant of GBS).
* **Infective Screen**: HIV, HBsAg, Anti-HCV, VDRL/RPR, Lyme serology (if travel Hx).

### Neurophysiological Investigations for Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS)**:
  * **Axonal Loss**: ↓ Amplitude (Number of functioning axons is reduced).
  * **Demyelinating**: ↓ Conduction Velocity, prolonged distal latencies, conduction block, temporal dispersion.
* **Electromyography (EMG)**: Confirms denervation (fibrillation potentials, positive sharp waves) and re-innervation (large, prolonged polyphasic motor unit potentials).

### Biopsy & Special Investigations for Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Nerve Biopsy**: Usually Sural nerve. Indicated for suspected vasculitis, amyloidosis, or leprosy (demonstrates fibrinoid necrosis, amyloid deposits, or granulomas).
* **Skin Biopsy**: Intraepidermal nerve fiber density (IENFD) quantification. Gold standard for diagnosing Small Fiber Neuropathy (normal NCS/EMG).
* **Lumbar Puncture (CSF)**: Albuminocytologic dissociation (↑ protein, normal WCC) in GBS and CIDP.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation in Fulminant Neuropathy (e.g., GBS)
* **Airway & Breathing**: Monitor Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) q4h.
* **Intubation Criteria**: Intubate if FVC <15 mL/kg, bulbar palsy (aspiration risk), or rapidly progressing hypercapnia.
* **Hemodynamic Monitoring**: Continuous ECG & BP monitoring for autonomic storming (treat extreme hypertension with short-acting agents like Labetalol; treat bradycardia with Atropine).
* **Acute Disease Modification**: IV Immunoglobulin (IVIg) 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days OR Plasmapheresis (PE) to clear autoantibodies. (Do NOT use steroids).

### Acute Neuropathic Pain Crisis Management
* **Acute Pharmacotherapy**: IV Lidocaine infusion (specialist setting only) or strong short-acting opioids (Tramadol/Morphine) as bridge therapy, despite limited long-term efficacy.
* **Exacerbating Factor Reversal**: Strictly correct acute hyperglycemia, electrolyte derangements, or withdraw offending toxins (e.g., alcohol).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Disease-Modifying Chronic Therapies for Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Diabetic Neuropathy**: Strict glycemic control (HbA1c <7.0%) prevents progression but does not reverse established neuropathy.
* **Vasculitic Neuropathy**: High-dose Corticosteroids + Immunosuppressants (Cyclophosphamide or Rituximab).
* **CIDP**: Long-term IVIg pulses, Plasma exchange, or maintenance oral Corticosteroids.
* **Nutritional**: IM Vitamin B12 loading regimen; oral Thiamine/Folate supplementation.

### Pharmacological Algorithm for Chronic Neuropathic Pain (HA Formulary)
| Step | Drug Class | Specific Agents & Dosing (HA Context) | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First-Line** | TCAs | **Amitriptyline** 10-25mg ON (Titrate up). Avoid in elderly/cardiac pts. | Serotonin/Norepinephrine reuptake inhibition. |
| **First-Line** | SNRIs | **Duloxetine** 30-60mg Daily. Useful if comorbid depression. | Serotonin/Norepinephrine reuptake inhibition. |
| **First/Second-Line** | Gabapentinoids | **Pregabalin** (75mg BD) or **Gabapentin** (300mg ON). HA Special Drug (Requires justification). | Binds α2δ subunit of voltage-gated Ca2+ channels. |
| **Refractory/Adjunct** | Topicals | Capsaicin cream 0.075%, Lidocaine patches. | Depletes Substance P (Capsaicin); local anesthetic. |
| **Third-Line** | Opioid-like | Tramadol. Strictly limit duration due to addiction risk. | Weak mu-agonist + SNRI. |

### Multidisciplinary & Lifestyle Management
* **Podiatry**: Custom off-loading footwear, regular callus debridement to prevent diabetic foot ulcers.
* **Physiotherapy (PT)**: Ankle-Foot Orthosis (AFO) splints for foot drop to prevent falls. Gait retraining.
* **Occupational Therapy (OT)**: Home modification (grab bars, anti-slip mats) due to sensory ataxia.
* **Daily Foot Care**: Instruct patients to inspect feet daily with a mirror, never walk barefoot, cut nails straight across.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Clinical Scoring Systems for Neuropathy
* **Toronto Clinical Scoring System (TCSS)**: Validated for Diabetic Neuropathy.
  * Scores symptoms (pain, numbness, tingling, weakness, ataxia) + reflexes + sensory tests (pinprick, temperature, light touch, vibration, position).
  * **Score Interpretation**: <5 = No neuropathy; 5-11 = Mild/Moderate; >11 = Severe neuropathy.
* **MRC Sum Score (Medical Research Council)**: Tracks motor progression in GBS/CIDP based on muscle power in 6 bilateral muscle groups (Max score 60).

### Prognostic Indicators in Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Poor Prognostic Markers in Axonal Neuropathy**: Complete absence of SNAP/CMAP on nerve conduction studies, profound distal wasting.
* **Poor Prognostic Markers in GBS**: Older age, rapid onset (<7 days to nadir), antecedent Campylobacter jejuni infection (associated with axonal variant AMAN), require mechanical ventilation.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Peripheral Neuropathy
* **Dermatological/Surgical**: Neuropathic foot ulcers → osteomyelitis → lower extremity amputation (截肢).
* **Orthopedic**: **Charcot Neuroarthropathy** (rocker-bottom foot deformity, multiple microfractures due to loss of pain sensation and abnormal weight bearing).
* **Cardiovascular (Dysautonomia)**: Silent myocardial infarction (lack of angina), orthostatic syncope.
* **Gastrointestinal (Dysautonomia)**: Diabetic gastroparesis (severe vomiting, erratic glycemic control), profound constipation vs. overflow diarrhea.
* **Genitourinary**: Neurogenic bladder (retention, recurrent UTIs), erectile dysfunction.

### Medication Adverse Effects in Neuropathy Treatment
* **TCAs (Amitriptyline)**: Anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, urinary retention, blurry vision, constipation), prolonged QTc, orthostatic hypotension. Lethal in overdose.
* **Gabapentinoids (Pregabalin/Gabapentin)**: Dizziness, somnolence, peripheral edema, weight gain. Require renal dose adjustment.
* **SNRIs (Duloxetine)**: Nausea, insomnia, hypertension, hepatotoxicity (avoid in chronic liver disease).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)
* Viva Question: What is the most important bedside test for a diabetic patient presenting with peripheral neuropathy? Answer: Examine the feet for ulcers, check pulses, and test sensation using a 10g Semmes-Weinstein monofilament to assess ulceration risk.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a polyneuropathy from a mononeuropathy multiplex clinically? Answer: Polyneuropathy is symmetrical and length-dependent (stocking-glove), whereas mononeuropathy multiplex involves sequential, asymmetrical involvement of distinct individual peripheral nerves (e.g., right radial, then left peroneal).
* Viva Question: What CSF finding is classically seen in Guillain-Barré Syndrome? Answer: Albuminocytologic dissociation, meaning markedly elevated CSF protein with a normal or mildly elevated white cell count.
* Trap: Assuming symmetrical stocking-glove numbness is always diabetic neuropathy in a diabetic patient. Pearl: Always exclude treatable causes like Vitamin B12 deficiency (check for macrocytosis/hypersegmented neutrophils), uremia, and alcohol toxicity, even in established diabetics.
* Trap: Using Amitriptyline in an elderly male patient with neuropathic pain. Pearl: Avoid TCAs in the elderly, particularly males with BPH, due to high risk of acute urinary retention, confusion, and falls from anticholinergic effects; Gabapentinoids or SNRIs are preferred.
* Pitfall: Reassuring a patient with GBS solely based on preserved oxygen saturation. Oxygen saturation remains normal until late in neuromuscular respiratory failure; you must sequentially monitor Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) to anticipate the need for intubation.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing Small Fiber Neuropathy using Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS). Conventional NCS only measures large myelinated fibers; it will be completely normal in purely small fiber neuropathy, which requires a skin biopsy (intraepidermal nerve fiber density) for definitive diagnosis.
