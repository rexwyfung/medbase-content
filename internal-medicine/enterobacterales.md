# Enterobacterales (腸桿菌目)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Enterobacterales
* **Enterobacterales** (formerly Enterobacteriaceae): Large order of Gram-negative bacilli (GNB), non-spore-forming, facultative anaerobes.
* **Core Microbiological Triad**: Ferment glucose, Oxidase -ve, Reduce nitrates to nitrites.
* **Habitat**: Commensals of human/animal GI tract; ubiquitous in soil/water.

### Major Pathogenic Genera
* **Escherichia** (*E. coli*): Normal flora; commonest cause of community & nosocomial bacteremia.
* **Klebsiella** (*K. pneumoniae*): Mucoid capsule; lobar pneumonia, liver abscesses (hypervirulent strains).
* **Proteus** (*P. mirabilis*): Swarming motility; urease +ve.
* **Enterobacter, Serratia, Citrobacter** (ESC group): Inducible AmpC β-lactamases; strict nosocomial pathogens.
* **Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia**: Primary GI pathogens (clinical profiles excluded per prompt).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **ESBL Prevalence**: Endemic in HK; >30-40% of community-acquired *E. coli* are Extended-Spectrum Beta-Lactamase (超廣譜β-內酰胺酶) producers.
* **CPE/CRE Burden**: Carbapenemase-Producing Enterobacterales (產碳青黴烯酶腸桿菌) incidence ↑ in HA hospitals; predominant strains: NDM (New Delhi Metallo-beta-lactamase) & KPC.
* **Hypervirulent K. pneumoniae (hvKp)**: Highly prevalent in HK/Asia; uniquely causes primary pyogenic liver abscess in healthy individuals.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### General Virulence Factors
* **Endotoxin (Lipid A of LPS)**: Released on cell death → binds CD14/TLR4 on macrophages → massive release of IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α → Gram-negative septic shock (敗血性休克).
* **Capsule (K Antigen)**: Polysaccharide layer (prominent in *Klebsiella*) → resists phagocytosis & complement-mediated lysis.
* **Flagella (H Antigen)**: Provides motility (e.g., *Proteus* swarming); highly immunogenic.
* **Fimbriae/Pili**: Mediates uroepithelial or GI mucosal adhesion (e.g., P-fimbriae in *E. coli*).

### Genus-Specific Virulence Mechanisms
* **Proteus Urease Production**: Splits urea → NH3 + CO2 → ↑ urinary pH (>7.0) → precipitates Mg2+ & PO43- → **Struvite (Magnesium Ammonium Phosphate) stones** (鳥糞石) → staghorn calculi & persistent nidus for infection.
* **Serratia marcescens**: Produces **prodigiosin** (red pigment) at room temp.
* **Hypervirulent Klebsiella (hvKp)**: Hyper-mucoviscous phenotype (rmpA gene) + siderophores (aerobactin) → hematogenous seeding (eyes, meninges).

### ESBL Pathophysiology
* **Mechanism**: Plasmid-mediated enzymes (CTX-M, TEM, SHV families).
* **Function**: Hydrolyze β-lactam ring of penicillins, 1st–4th gen cephalosporins, and monobactams (Aztreonam).
* **Inhibitor Susceptibility**: Remain susceptible to in vitro β-lactamase inhibitors (Clavulanate, Tazobactam).
* **Co-resistance**: Plasmids often carry resistance to fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, TMP-SMX.

### Carbapenemase (CRE/CPE) Mechanisms
* **Class A (Serine Carbapenemases)**: KPC (Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase) → hydrolyzes all β-lactams.
* **Class B (Metallo-β-lactamases - MBLs)**: NDM, VIM, IMP → use Zn2+ at active site; hydrolyze all except Aztreonam; resistant to standard β-lactamase inhibitors.
* **Class D (Oxacillinases)**: OXA-48-like → weak carbapenemase activity but highly resistant when combined with porin loss.
* **AmpC Derepression**: SPACE/MYSPACE organisms (Serratia, Proteus, Acinetobacter, Citrobacter, Enterobacter) possess chromosomal AmpC → exposure to 3rd-gen cephalosporins selects for permanently derepressed (hyperproducing) mutants → clinical failure despite initial in vitro susceptibility.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History for Enterobacterales Sepsis
* **Source Identification**: Dysuria/flank pain (GU), cough/sputum (Resp), abdominal pain/biliary colic (GI/Biliary), central line presence (Intravascular).
* **Resistance Risk Hx**: Travel history (India/Mainland China ↑ CPE risk), recent hospitalization (<90 days), nursing home residence, prior broad-spectrum Abx use, known carrier status.
* **Host Factors**: Immunosuppression (DM, neutropenia, cirrhosis), structural uropathy, indwelling catheters.

### Physical Signs of Gram-Negative Sepsis
* **Vitals**: Fever/hypothermia, tachycardia, tachypnea, hypotension (wide pulse pressure initially → narrow later).
* **Perfusion Signs (Shock)**: Warm extremities (early hyperdynamic vasodilatory shock) → Cold, clammy peripheries with delayed capillary refill >2s (late shock).
* **Cutaneous Stigmata**: Petechiae, purpura fulminans, ecchymoses (signs of ensuing DIC), mucosal bleeding.
* **hvKp Specific Signs**: Right upper quadrant tenderness (Liver Abscess), decreased visual acuity/hypopyon (Endophthalmitis).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Non-Enterobacterales Gram-Negative Bacilli
| Organism | Discriminatory Features from Enterobacterales |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pseudomonas aeruginosa** (綠膿桿菌) | **Oxidase +ve** (crucial!), strictly aerobic, non-lactose fermenter, green pigment (pyocyanin), sweet fruity odor. |
| **Acinetobacter baumannii** (鮑曼不動桿菌) | Non-lactose fermenter, strictly aerobic, coccobacillary shape, usually purely hospital-acquired. |
| **Burkholderia pseudomallei** | Endemic in SEA/HK; causes Melioidosis; bipolar staining ("safety pin"); prolonged latent periods. |
| **Stenotrophomonas maltophilia** | Intrinsically resistant to Carbapenems; inherently sensitive to TMP-SMX; non-fermenter. |

### Gram-Negative Anaerobes
* **Bacteroides fragilis**: Strict anaerobe (requires anaerobic culture bottle), predominant GI flora, commonly co-infects with Enterobacterales in intra-abdominal abscesses.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Basic Microbiological Stains
* **Gram Stain**: Pink/red Gram-negative bacilli (no specific arrangement).
* **String Test (hvKp)**: Touching a colony with a loop generates a viscous string >5mm.

### Culture & Agar Differentiation (MacConkey Agar)
* **Principle**: MacConkey agar selects for Gram -ve (bile salts inhibit Gram +ve) & differentiates based on **Lactose Fermentation**.

| Reaction | Implications & Genera |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fast Lactose Fermenters** (Pink colonies) | *E. coli, Klebsiella, Enterobacter* |
| **Slow Lactose Fermenters** | *Citrobacter, Serratia* |
| **Non-Lactose Fermenters** (Colorless) | *Proteus, Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia* (also *Pseudomonas/Acinetobacter*) |

### Biochemical Identification Tests
* **Oxidase Test**: Strictly **Negative** for all Enterobacterales (distinguishes from *Pseudomonas/Campylobacter/Vibrio*).
* **Indole Test**: Differentiates *E. coli* (+ve) from *Klebsiella* (-ve); *Proteus vulgaris* (+ve) from *Proteus mirabilis* (-ve).
* **TSI (Triple Sugar Iron) Agar**: Detects glucose/lactose/sucrose fermentation and H2S production (H2S +ve = *Salmonella, Proteus*).

### Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST) & Molecular ID
* **MALDI-TOF MS**: Rapid species identification directly from positive blood culture bottles (<1 hour).
* **VITEK-2 / Disk Diffusion**: Determines Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) for targeted Abx.
* **CPE Multiplex PCR (e.g., Xpert Carba-R)**: Rapid genotypic detection of blaKPC, blaNDM, blaVIM, blaOXA-48, blaIMP from rectal swabs or cultures.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Sepsis Resuscitation Protocol (Surviving Sepsis)
* **Initial Bolus**: IV crystalloid (Normal Saline or Plasmalyte) 30 mL/kg within 1st 3 hours for hypotension or Lactate ≥4.
* **Vasopressors**: IV Noradrenaline (target MAP ≥65 mmHg) if fluid-unresponsive.
* **Source Control**: Urgent drain/surgery (e.g., PCN for blocked infected kidney, laparotomy for perforated viscus) <6 hours.

### Empiric Antibiotic Algorithms (Hong Kong HA Guidelines)
| Clinical Scenario | First-Line Empiric IV Therapy (HK HA Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Community-Acquired (Low ESBL Risk)** | Amoxicillin/Clavulanate (Augmentin) ± Gentamicin |
| **Healthcare-Associated (High ESBL Risk)** | Piperacillin/Tazobactam (Tazocin) OR Cefepime |
| **Penicillin Allergy (Non-severe)** | Cefuroxime + Gentamicin |
| **Penicillin Allergy (Anaphylaxis)** | Ciprofloxacin OR Aztreonam |
| **Known/High Risk CPE (Septic Shock)** | Meropenem + Polymyxin E (Colistin) OR Ceftazidime-avibactam |

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Targeted Antibiotic Therapy for ESBL Organisms
* **First-Line Definitive Rx**: Carbapenems (Meropenem or Ertapenem). *Note: Ertapenem preferred for outpatient parenteral antibiotic therapy (OPAT) as it is once-daily and spares Meropenem (anti-pseudomonal).*
* **Carbapenem-Sparing Options**:
  * Piperacillin/Tazobactam (only if MIC ≤8 and non-urinary source).
  * Aminoglycosides (Amikacin) for targeted UTI.
  * Fosfomycin or Nitrofurantoin (strictly for uncomplicated cystitis only).

### Targeted Antibiotic Therapy for AmpC Producers (SPACE/MYSPACE)
* **Drug of Choice**: Cefepime (4th gen Cephalosporin) or Carbapenems.
* **Avoid**: Ceftriaxone/Cefotaxime (3rd gen) EVEN IF reported sensitive in vitro, due to predictable failure from inducible resistance during treatment.

### Targeted Antibiotic Therapy for CPE/CRE
* **Class A (KPC)**: Ceftazidime-avibactam (Zavicefta) OR Meropenem-vaborbactam.
* **Class B (MBL/NDM)**: Aztreonam + Ceftazidime-avibactam (Aztreonam resists MBL; Avibactam protects Aztreonam from co-produced ESBLs) OR Cefiderocol.
* **Salvage Therapy**: Colistin (Polymyxin E) or Tigecycline (avoid Tigecycline in UTI/bacteremia due to poor serum/urine concentrations).

### Infection Control & Hospital Isolation Protocols
* **Contact Precautions**: Strict single room or cohorting for ESBL (in high-risk wards) and ALL CPE patients.
* **CPE Active Surveillance**: Rectal swabs on admission for high-risk patients (prior hospitalization outside HK, known contacts).
* **Decolonization**: Eradication of CPE gut carriage is generally ineffective; focus on strict hand hygiene and environmental cleaning (hypochlorite).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Factors for ESBL & CPE Acquisition
* **Healthcare exposure**: >2 days in hospital in past 90 days.
* **Geographic**: Medical tourism/hospitalization in endemic areas (e.g., India, Greece, Mainland China).
* **Pharmacologic**: Usage of Carbapenems or Fluoroquinolones in prior 30 days.

### Pitt Bacteremia Score
* **Purpose**: Predicts mortality in Gram-negative bacteremia.
* **Variables**: Temperature, Hypotension, Mechanical ventilation, Cardiac arrest, Mental status.
* **Interpretation**: Score ≥4 indicates high risk of mortality; mandates aggressive broad-spectrum/combination therapy.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Severe Enterobacterales Infection
* **Hematologic**: Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) driven by endotoxin → widespread microthrombi & bleeding.
* **Renal**: Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) secondary to septic shock; Struvite staghorn calculi leading to obstructive uropathy and chronic kidney disease (*Proteus*).
* **Metastatic Infection (hvKp)**: Panophthalmitis, brain abscess, necrotizing fasciitis.

### Adverse Effects of Anti-Gram-Negative Antibiotics
* **Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin/Amikacin)**: ATN (nephrotoxicity), irreversible vestibular/auditory ototoxicity, neuromuscular blockade.
* **Carbapenems (Imipenem > Meropenem)**: Proconvulsant effect (seizures), especially in renal impairment/CNS pathology.
* **Colistin (Polymyxin E)**: Severe dose-dependent nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity (paresthesias).
* **Tigecycline**: Severe nausea/vomiting, black tooth discoloration (tetracycline class).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why must you not use Ceftriaxone for an Enterobacter cloacae bacteremia that is reported as "sensitive" to Ceftriaxone on the initial lab report? Answer: Enterobacter carries a chromosomal AmpC gene. Exposure to 3rd-generation cephalosporins selectively derepresses this gene, leading to hyperproduction of beta-lactamases and clinical failure mid-treatment. Use Cefepime or a Carbapenem.
* Trap: Prescribing Tigecycline for a multi-drug resistant Pseudomonas or Proteus infection. Pearl: Tigecycline is intrinsically inactive against Pseudomonas, Proteus, and Providencia (the "3 Ps").
* Pitfall: Treating a urinary tract infection empirically with Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) in a patient with a known history of ESBL. In Hong Kong, ESBL producers frequently carry co-resistance plasmids, and Augmentin failure rates are high; use Nitrofurantoin (if lower UTI) or Fosfomycin based on prior sensitivities, or escalate to IV Ertapenem.
* Trap: Assuming all Carbapenem-Resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) are treatable with the novel drug Ceftazidime-avibactam. Pearl: Ceftazidime-avibactam is highly active against KPC (Class A) but completely ineffective against NDM (Class B metallo-beta-lactamases) which are prevalent in Asia. NDM requires Aztreonam combination therapy or Cefiderocol.
* Viva Question: A patient with right upper quadrant pain and a liver abscess is found to have endogenous endophthalmitis. What is the classic organism and its key virulence factor? Answer: Hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (hvKp). Its key virulence factors are the hyper-mucoviscous capsule (rmpA gene) and enhanced siderophores (aerobactin).
