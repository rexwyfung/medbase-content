# Ischaemic Colitis (缺血性結腸炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Ischaemic Colitis
* **Ischaemic Colitis (缺血性結腸炎):** Focal or segmental bowel wall ischaemia secondary to a sudden, transient ↓ in colonic blood flow.
* **Mechanism:** Represents a form of **Non-Occlusive Mesenteric Ischaemia (NOMI)** in the vast majority of cases. 
* **Distinction:** Differs from **Acute Mesenteric Ischaemia (AMI)** which typically involves the small bowel via major vessel occlusion.

### Aetiologies and Risk Factors for Ischaemic Colitis
* **Systemic Hypoperfusion (Low-flow states):**
  * **Cardiogenic:** Myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, arrhythmias [e.g., Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動)].
  * **Hypovolaemia:** Severe dehydration, haemorrhage, shock, haemodialysis-induced hypotension.
* **Medications & Toxins (Vasospastic/Hypotensive):**
  * **Vasopressors/Inotropes** (e.g., Noradrenaline, Dopamine).
  * **Constipating agents:** Opioids, anticholinergics (exacerbate luminal pressure).
  * **Others:** NSAIDs, Digitalis, illicit drugs (Cocaine, Methamphetamine), pseudoephedrine.
* **Mechanical / Interventional:**
  * Post-aortic aneurysm repair (ligation of Inferior Mesenteric Artery).
  * Post-colonoscopy (due to ↑ intraluminal pressure from insufflation).
  * Obstructing lesions (colon cancer, strictures) leading to upstream ischaemia.
* **Thrombophilic States:** Protein C/S deficiency, Factor V Leiden, Antiphospholipid Syndrome (less common in isolated colonic ischaemia).

### Epidemiology of Ischaemic Colitis in Hong Kong
* **Demographics:** Primarily affects the elderly population (>60 years) in Hong Kong.
* **Prevalence:** The most common form of gastrointestinal ischaemia seen in local HA hospitals.
* **Gender:** Slight female predominance.
* **Risk Profile:** Strongly associated with multiple cardiovascular risk factors (Hypertension, Diabetes Mellitus, Hyperlipidaemia, Ischaemic Heart Disease).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Vascular Anatomy and Watershed Areas in Ischaemic Colitis
* **Blood Supply:** Colon is supplied by the **Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA)** and **Inferior Mesenteric Artery (IMA)**.
* **Marginal Artery of Drummond & Arc of Riolan:** Provide collateral circulation.
* **Vulnerable Watershed Areas (分水嶺區域):** Areas lacking robust collateral supply.
  * **Griffiths' Point (Splenic Flexure):** Junction of SMA (middle colic artery) and IMA (left colic artery).
  * **Sudeck's Point (Rectosigmoid Junction):** Junction of IMA (superior rectal artery) and hypogastric/internal iliac arteries (middle/inferior rectal arteries).
* **Rectal Sparing:** The rectum is typically spared due to rich dual vascular supply (IMA + internal iliac arteries).

### Pathophysiological Mechanisms of Colonic Ischaemia
* **Phase 1: Hypoperfusion:** Transient ↓ mucosal blood flow → critical tissue hypoxia.
* **Phase 2: Ischaemic Injury:** Mucosal infarction → sloughing, ulceration, and intraluminal bleeding. Submucosal oedema and haemorrhage develop.
* **Phase 3: Reperfusion Injury:** Restoration of blood flow → release of Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) → further tissue damage.
* **Severity Spectrum:**
  * **Transient (Mucosal/Submucosal):** Reversible, superficial sloughing, heals without scarring (most common).
  * **Chronic (Mural):** Involves muscularis propria → healing by fibrosis and stricture formation.
  * **Fulminant (Transmural):** Full-thickness necrosis → gangrene and perforation.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Classic History and Symptoms of Ischaemic Colitis
* **Classic Presentation Triad:**
  1. **Acute, crampy lower abdominal pain** (typically Left Iliac Fossa).
  2. **Urgent desire to defecate** (tenesmus).
  3. **Haematochezia (便血)** or bloody diarrhoea (usually presents within 12-24 hours of pain onset).
* **Pain Characteristics:** Initially "pain out of proportion to physical findings", transitioning to localized somatic pain as inflammation progresses.
* **Precipitating Events:** Hx of recent hypotensive episode (e.g., dialysis session, heavy marathon running), new medications, or cardiac arrhythmia.

### Physical Examination Findings in Ischaemic Colitis
* **Vitals:** Tachycardia, ± Hypotension (identifying the low-flow precipitant).
* **Abdominal Examination:**
  * Localized tenderness over the affected segment (most commonly the left hemi-colon / Left Iliac Fossa).
  * ± Distension.
  * **Signs of Transmural Infarction/Perforation:** Guarding, rigidity, rebound tenderness, absent bowel sounds (silent abdomen).
* **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE):** Reveal fresh blood, blood-stained mucus, or malaena (rare).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Key Differential Diagnoses for Ischaemic Colitis

| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features from Ischaemic Colitis |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Mesenteric Ischaemia (AMI)** | Primarily small bowel (SMA territory); excruciating periumbilical pain; profound metabolic acidosis; life-threatening. |
| **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)** | Chronic relapsing Hx; younger demographic; extra-intestinal manifestations; prominent tenesmus in UC. |
| **Infectious Colitis** | Fever; recent travel/antibiotic Hx (C. difficile); prominent watery diarrhoea; stool cultures +ve. |
| **Diverticular Haemorrhage** | Massive, **painless** lower GI bleeding; typical in elderly. |
| **Colorectal Malignancy** | Chronic weight loss; altered bowel habits; iron deficiency anaemia; palpable mass. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside and Blood Investigations for Ischaemic Colitis
* **Venous Blood Gas (VBG):** ↑ Lactate, severe metabolic acidosis (suggests severe/transmural ischaemia).
* **Complete Blood Count (FBC):** ↑ WCC (Leukocytosis), ↓ Hb (Anaemia from PR bleeding).
* **Renal & Liver Function Test (RFT/LFT):** ↑ Urea/Cr (pre-renal AKI), ↓ Albumin.
* **Coagulation Profile (PT/APTT):** Rule out underlying coagulopathy.
* **Type and Screen (G&S):** Prepare for potential transfusion.
* **Stool Studies:** Clostridioides difficile toxin, multiplex PCR/culture (Rule out infectious mimics).
* **ECG:** Detect AF or acute MI.

### Radiological Imaging for Ischaemic Colitis
* **Contrast-Enhanced CT Abdomen & Pelvis (Modality of Choice):**
  * **Bowel wall thickening:** Usually segmental, predominantly in watershed areas.
  * **"Thumbprinting" sign (拇指印徵):** Prominent mucosal/submucosal oedema and haemorrhage projecting into the lumen.
  * **Target/Halo sign:** Concentric rings of alternating densities (oedematous submucosa).
  * **Pericolic fat stranding.**
  * **Severe/Late signs:** Pneumatosis intestinalis (gas in bowel wall), portomesenteric venous gas, pneumoperitoneum (free air).

### Endoscopy and Biopsy Findings in Ischaemic Colitis
* **Flexible Sigmoidoscopy / Colonoscopy (Diagnostic Gold Standard):**
  * Done within 48h to confirm Dx; requires **minimal insufflation** to avoid perforation.
  * **Macroscopic findings:** Segmental distribution, sharp transition to normal mucosa, pale/oedematous mucosa, petechial haemorrhages, blue-black haemorrhagic nodules.
  * **"Single-stripe sign" (單條紋徵):** Longitudinal mucosal ulceration along the taenia coli.
* **Histopathology (Biopsy):**
  * Haemorrhagic/oedematous lamina propria.
  * **"Ghost cells":** Coagulative necrosis of the crypts with preserved cellular outlines but lost nuclei.
  * Absence of chronic inflammatory features (differentiates from IBD).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Initial Resuscitation and Supportive Care for Acute Ischaemic Colitis
* **"Drip and Suck":** Nil by mouth (NBM), nasogastric tube insertion if ileus/vomiting present.
* **Aggressive IV Fluid Resuscitation:** Crystalloids to restore intravascular volume and perfuse the colon.
* **Haemodynamic Optimization:** Treat arrhythmias, optimize cardiac output, maintain MAP > 65 mmHg.
* **Vasopressor Avoidance:** Discontinue or minimize vasopressors (e.g., Noradrenaline) if possible, as they exacerbate splanchnic vasoconstriction.
* **Empirical IV Antibiotics:** For moderate-to-severe disease to prevent bacterial translocation.
  * *HA Protocol:* IV Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) OR IV Ceftriaxone + IV Metronidazole.
* **Analgesia:** Avoid NSAIDs.
* **Bowel Rest & Monitoring:** Serial abdominal examinations and VBG (lactate) monitoring.

### Indications for Surgical Intervention in Ischaemic Colitis
* **Surgical Consultation (General Surgery):** Required in ~15-20% of cases.
* **Indications for Laparotomy:**
  * Generalized peritonitis / clinical evidence of gangrene or perforation.
  * Haemodynamic instability refractory to resuscitation.
  * Unremitting pain or worsening profound lactic acidosis.
  * Massive, uncontrollable lower GI bleeding.
  * Development of toxic megacolon.
* **Surgical Procedure:** Resection of the ischaemic colonic segment with exteriorization (End colostomy / Hartmann's procedure). Primary anastomosis is absolutely contraindicated in the acute ischaemic setting.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Management and Secondary Prevention of Ischaemic Colitis
* **Cardiovascular Risk Optimization:** Strict control of DM, Hypertension, and Hyperlipidaemia.
* **Medication Review & Rationalization:**
  * Discontinue precipitants: NSAIDs, unnecessary anticholinergics, oral contraceptives, pseudoephedrine.
* **Arrhythmia/Heart Failure Management:** Ensure optimal rate/rhythm control and anticoagulation for AF (post-bleeding resolution, weighing CHA2DS2-VASc vs HAS-BLED).
* **Follow-up Endoscopy:** Surveillance colonoscopy at **4-6 weeks** post-discharge if the initial episode was ambiguous, to document mucosal healing, or to evaluate for occult malignancy or post-ischaemic strictures.
* **Lifestyle Modification:** High fibre diet and hydration to prevent constipation (avoiding ↑ intraluminal pressure).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Factors and Scoring for Ischaemic Colitis
* **Clinical Course:** 80-85% are transient and resolve fully with conservative management.
* **High-Risk Features indicating Severe Disease / Poor Prognosis:**
  * **Right-sided colonic involvement:** Suggests proximal SMA hypoperfusion and generally indicates more profound systemic ischaemia; significantly higher mortality rate.
  * **Pancolitis** (entire colon involvement).
  * **Haemodynamic instability** / Shock at presentation.
  * **Severe biochemical derangement:** ↑↑ Lactate, renal impairment.
  * **Absent bowel sounds / peritoneal signs.**
* **Risk Stratification (ACG Guidelines):**
  * **Mild:** Localized pain, mild bleeding, normal vitals, no peritonitis.
  * **Moderate:** Up to 3 risk factors, no severe signs.
  * **Severe:** >3 risk factors, peritonitis, right-sided involvement, sepsis.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Acute and Chronic Complications of Ischaemic Colitis
* **Acute Complications:**
  * Transmural Infarction → Gangrene → Bowel Perforation.
  * Faecal Peritonitis & Intra-abdominal abscess.
  * Septic Shock & Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS).
  * Severe haemorrhage.
* **Chronic Complications:**
  * **Post-Ischaemic Stricture (狹窄):** Occurs in 10-15% of cases due to transmural fibrosis. Presents with symptoms of subacute large bowel obstruction. May require endoscopic balloon dilatation or elective surgical resection.
  * **Segmental Ischaemic Colitis (Chronic):** Recurrent low-grade pain and bloody diarrhoea; macroscopically mimics IBD.
  * **Recurrence:** Occurs in roughly 5% of patients, usually due to uncorrected underlying systemic factors.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why is the rectum almost always spared in ischaemic colitis? Answer: The rectum receives a rich dual collateral blood supply from both the inferior mesenteric artery (superior rectal artery) and the internal iliac arteries (middle and inferior rectal arteries).
* Viva Question: What is the classic endoscopic "single-stripe sign"? Answer: A single longitudinal ulceration running along the longitudinal axis of the colon (usually along the taenia coli), highly specific for mild transient ischaemic colitis.
* Trap: Confusing Ischaemic Colitis with Acute Mesenteric Ischaemia (AMI) in management. Pearl: Ischaemic colitis is mostly non-occlusive, affects the large bowel, is typically self-limiting, and requires conservative "drip and suck" management. AMI involves the small bowel, is usually occlusive (embolus/thrombus), and requires emergent surgical exploration or endovascular revascularization.
* Pitfall: Performing a full colonoscopy with aggressive air insufflation during an acute flare. Too much intraluminal pressure can worsen the intramural blood flow (worsening ischaemia) and easily perforate the friable, necrotic bowel wall. Use minimal CO2 insufflation or limit to flexible sigmoidoscopy.
* Trap: Attributing right-sided colon ischaemia to typical watershed hypoperfusion. Pearl: Right-sided ischaemia is an independent predictor of worse outcomes, as the right colon is supplied by the SMA. Its involvement implies a much more profound drop in cardiac output or SMA disease, behaving more like Acute Mesenteric Ischaemia.
