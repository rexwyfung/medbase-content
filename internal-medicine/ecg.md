# Electrocardiogram Interpretation (心電圖判讀)

## 1. ECG Fundamentals, Lead Systems & Normal Waveforms

### Anatomical Contiguity of 12-Lead ECG & Coronary Territories
| Anatomical Territory  | Contiguous Leads                    | Culprit Artery (Typical)            |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| **Septal**            | V1, V2                              | Proximal LAD (左前降支)                 |
| **Anterior**          | V3, V4                              | Mid-LAD                             |
| **Lateral**           | I, aVL, V5, V6                      | LCx (左迴旋支) or Diagonal (LAD branch) |
| **Inferior**          | II, III, aVF                        | RCA (右冠狀動脈) (80%), LCx (20%)        |
| **Right Ventricular** | V1, V4R                             | Proximal RCA                        |
| **Posterior**         | Tall R & STD in V1-V3, STE in V7-V9 | PDA (RCA or LCx origin)             |

### Heart Rate Calculation & Cardiac Axis Determination
* **Regular HR:** 300 ÷ (Number of large sq between R-R) OR 1500 ÷ (Number of small sq)
* **Irregular HR:** (Number of R waves in 10 sec rhythm strip) × 6
* **Normal Axis:** -30° to +90° (Lead I +ve, Lead II +ve)
* **Left Axis Deviation (LAD):** < -30° (Lead I +ve, Lead II -ve, Lead aVF -ve)
* **Right Axis Deviation (RAD):** > +90° (Lead I -ve, Lead aVF +ve)
* **Extreme Axis:** -90° to -180° (Lead I -ve, Lead aVF -ve)

### Differential Diagnosis of QRS Axis Deviations
| Axis Deviation | Key Aetiologies |
| :--- | :--- |
| **LAD (< -30°)** | Left Anterior Fascicular Block (LAFB), LBBB, LVH, Inferior MI, Ventricular pacing |
| **RAD (> +90°)** | Left Posterior Fascicular Block (LPFB), RBBB, RVH, Lateral MI, Pulmonary Embolism (PE), Secundum ASD |
| **Extreme Axis** | VT, severe RVH, hyperkalaemia |

### Normal ECG Waves, Intervals & Physiological Correlates
* **P wave:** < 0.12 s (< 3 small sq), amplitude < 2.5 mm (Atrial depolarization)
* **PR interval:** 0.12–0.20 s (3-5 small sq) (AV node conduction time)
* **QRS complex:** < 0.12 s (< 3 small sq) (Ventricular depolarization)
* **QTc interval:** < 0.44 s (M), < 0.46 s (F). QTc = QT / √RR (Bazett’s formula). (Total ventricular depolarization & repolarization)
* **T wave:** Concordant with QRS vector (Ventricular repolarization)
* **U wave:** Small +ve deflection following T wave (Purkinje fibre repolarization; prominent in ↓K)

## 2. Chamber Enlargement & Hypertrophy Criteria

### Atrial Abnormalities (P-Wave Morphology)
* **Right Atrial Enlargement (P-pulmonale / 右心房擴大):**
  * Tall, peaked P waves > 2.5 mm in Lead II (classic in Cor Pulmonale/Pulmonary HTN)
* **Left Atrial Enlargement (P-mitrale / 左心房擴大):**
  * Broad, notched P wave > 0.12 s in Lead II
  * Biphasic P wave in V1 with deep, broad terminal -ve deflection (> 1 mm deep & > 1 small sq wide) (classic in Mitral Stenosis)

### Ventricular Hypertrophy Diagnostic Criteria
* **Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH / 左心室肥厚):**
  * **Sokolow-Lyon Criteria:** S wave in V1 + R wave in V5 or V6 ≥ 35 mm
  * **Cornell Criteria:** R wave in aVL + S wave in V3 > 28 mm (M) or > 20 mm (F)
  * **LV Strain Pattern:** Downsloping ST depression & asymmetrical T-wave inversion in lateral leads (I, aVL, V5, V6)
* **Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (RVH / 右心室肥厚):**
  * R > S in lead V1 (R/S ratio > 1)
  * Deep S waves in V5, V6
  * Associated with RAD and RV strain pattern (STD/T-inv in V1-V3)

### Differential Diagnosis of Dominant R Wave in V1 (R/S Ratio >1)
* RVH (Right Ventricular Hypertrophy)
* Posterior MI (reciprocal change to posterior Q waves)
* RBBB (rsR' morphology)
* WPW Syndrome (Type A - left-sided accessory pathway)
* Dextrocardia
* Normal variant in children/young adults

## 3. Atrioventricular Conduction Blocks & Intraventricular Delays

### Atrioventricular (AV) Blocks (房室傳導阻滯)
* **First-Degree AV Block:** Constant PR interval > 0.20 s. Benign; observation only.
* **Second-Degree AV Block (Mobitz Type I / Wenckebach):** Progressive PR prolongation until a QRS is dropped. Block at AV node. Usually benign.
* **Second-Degree AV Block (Mobitz Type II):** Constant PR interval with randomly dropped QRS complexes. Block at His-Purkinje system. High risk of progression to Complete Heart Block → requires pacemaker.
* **Third-Degree (Complete) AV Block:** AV dissociation. Regular P-P intervals, regular R-R intervals, but independent of each other. Atrial rate > Ventricular rate. High risk of asystole → emergent pacing.

### Bundle Branch Blocks (束支傳導阻滯)
* **General Criteria:** QRS duration ≥ 0.12 s
* **Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB):**
  * V1: rsR' ("Rabbit ears" or M-pattern)
  * V6: Broad, slurred S wave (W-pattern)
  * Mnemonic: MaRRoW
* **Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB):**
  * V1: Deep, broad QS or rS complex (W-pattern)
  * V6: Broad, notched R wave (M-pattern), absent septal Q waves
  * Mnemonic: WiLLiaM
  * *Note: New LBBB with ischaemic chest pain = STEMI equivalent in HA protocol.*

### Fascicular Blocks (Hemiblocks) & Bifascicular/Trifascicular Block
* **Left Anterior Fascicular Block (LAFB):** LAD (< -30°), qR in I/aVL, rS in II/III/aVF. Normal QRS width.
* **Left Posterior Fascicular Block (LPFB):** RAD (> +90°), rS in I/aVL, qR in II/III/aVF. Normal QRS width. Exclude RVH/Lateral MI first.
* **Bifascicular Block:** RBBB + LAFB (common) OR RBBB + LPFB (rare).
* **Trifascicular Block:** Bifascicular Block + 1st Degree AV Block. High risk of progression to Complete Heart Block if syncope occurs.

## 4. Acute Coronary Syndromes & Myocardial Ischaemia

### STEMI Diagnostic Criteria & Sgarbossa Criteria in LBBB
* **Standard STEMI Criteria (New STE at J-point in ≥ 2 contiguous leads):**
  * ≥ 1 mm STE in all leads EXCEPT V2-V3
  * V2-V3: ≥ 2 mm (M ≥ 40 y/o), ≥ 2.5 mm (M < 40 y/o), ≥ 1.5 mm (F)
* **Modified Sgarbossa Criteria (For suspected AMI in LBBB or Ventricular Pacing):**
  * Concordant STE ≥ 1 mm in any lead (Score 5)
  * Concordant STD ≥ 1 mm in V1, V2, or V3 (Score 3)
  * Discordant STE with STE / S-wave depth ratio < -0.25 (Smith modification) (Score 2)
  * *Interpretation: Score ≥ 3 is 98% specific for AMI.*

### High-Risk STEMI Equivalents (Requires Emergent PCI)
* **Wellens' Syndrome:** Critical proximal LAD stenosis. Pain-free at presentation. Deeply inverted or biphasic T waves in V2-V3.
* **de Winter's T Waves:** Proximal LAD occlusion. Upsloping STD > 1 mm at J-point in V1-V6 + tall, symmetric hyperacute T waves.
* **Left Main (LMCA) Occlusion / Triple Vessel Disease:** Widespread STD (≥ 6 leads) + STE in aVR (and/or V1).
* **Posterior MI:** Isolated STD in V1-V3 + dominant R waves + prominent upright T waves. Must confirm with posterior leads (V7-V9) showing ≥ 0.5 mm STE.

### Differential Diagnosis of Non-Ischaemic ST Elevation
* **Acute Pericarditis (急性心包炎):** Widespread concave STE + PR segment depression. (aVR shows reciprocal STD and PR elevation).
* **Benign Early Repolarization (BER):** Young, healthy patients. Concave STE, prominent J-point notching/slurring in precordial leads. Asymmetrical T waves.
* **Brugada Syndrome:** Coved STE > 2 mm in V1-V3 followed by -ve T waves.
* **Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy:** STE mimicking anterior AMI but lacks reciprocal changes.
* **Prinzmetal (Vasospastic) Angina:** Transient STE resolving with GTN/CCB.

## 5. Bradyarrhythmias & Escape Rhythms

### Sinus Node Dysfunction (Sick Sinus Syndrome - 病態竇房結綜合徵)
* **Manifestations:** Sinus bradycardia, sinus pauses (> 3 sec), sinoatrial exit block, chronotropic incompetence (failure to ↑ HR with exertion).
* **Tachy-Brady Syndrome:** Alternating episodes of SVT (often AF) and severe sinus bradycardia/pauses upon termination of tachyarrhythmia.

### Escape Rhythms vs. Premature Complexes
* **Junctional Escape Rhythm:** Rate 40-60 bpm. Narrow QRS. P waves absent, inverted in inferior leads, or buried in QRS.
* **Ventricular Escape Rhythm:** Rate 20-40 bpm. Broad QRS (> 0.12 s). AV dissociation.
* *Premature vs Escape:* Premature beats occur *early* (before expected next beat). Escape beats occur *late* (rescue mechanism after sinus pause).

### Acute Bradycardia Management Algorithm (AHA / HKHA Protocol)
* **1. Assess Clinical Stability:** Shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia, or acute heart failure?
* **2. Unstable Pathway:**
  * First-line: IV **Atropine** 0.5 - 1.0 mg bolus (max 3 mg).
  * If Atropine fails: Transcutaneous Pacing (TCP) OR IV **Dopamine** infusion (5-20 mcg/kg/min) OR IV **Adrenaline** infusion (2-10 mcg/min) OR IV **Isoprenaline**.
  * Definitive Rx: Urgent Transvenous Pacing (TVP).
* **3. Stable Pathway:** Observe, identify reversible causes (ischemia, hyperkalaemia, BB/CCB toxicity).

## 6. Narrow-Complex Tachycardias (Supraventricular Tachycardias)

### Differential Diagnosis & Classification of Narrow-Complex Tachycardia (QRS <0.12 s)
| Rhythm Type | Differential Diagnoses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Regular** | Sinus Tachycardia, AVNRT, AVRT (Orthodromic WPW), Atrial Flutter (with fixed 2:1 or 3:1 block), Atrial Tachycardia |
| **Irregular** | Atrial Fibrillation (AF), Atrial Flutter (with variable block), Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT - ≥3 different P-wave morphologies) |

### Pre-Excitation Syndromes: Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) Syndrome (預激綜合徵)
* **Pathophysiology:** Congenital accessory pathway (Bundle of Kent) bypassing AV node delay.
* **Classic Triad:** 
  1. Short PR interval (< 0.12 s)
  2. Slurred upstroke of QRS (Delta wave)
  3. Broad QRS duration (> 0.12 s)
* **Orthodromic AVRT (95%):** Antegrade down AV node, retrograde up Kent. Narrow QRS.
* **Antidromic AVRT (5%):** Antegrade down Kent, retrograde up AV node. Broad QRS (mimics VT).

### Acute & Chronic Management of Narrow-Complex Tachycardias (SVT)
* **Acute Haemodynamically Unstable:** Synchronized DC Cardioversion.
* **Acute Stable (AVNRT/AVRT):**
  * 1st Line: Vagal manoeuvres (modified Valsalva, carotid massage).
  * 2nd Line: IV **Adenosine** rapid push (6 mg → 12 mg → 12 mg) flushed with saline. (Contraindicated in severe asthma).
  * 3rd Line: IV **Verapamil** or IV **Diltiazem**.
* **Chronic Management:** Catheter ablation of accessory pathway/slow pathway (curative). Long-term Beta-blockers or CCB.

## 7. Broad-Complex Tachycardias & Ventricular Arrhythmias

### Differential Diagnosis of Broad-Complex Tachycardia (QRS ≥0.12 s)
| Rhythm Type | Differential Diagnoses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Regular** | Monomorphic Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) (80%), SVT with aberrancy (e.g., pre-existing BBB or rate-related BBB), Antidromic AVRT |
| **Irregular** | Polymorphic VT (Torsades de Pointes), AF with BBB, AF with pre-excitation (WPW) |
* *Golden Rule:* Treat ALL regular broad-complex tachycardias as VT until proven otherwise (especially in older patients with IHD).

### Differentiating Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) from SVT with Aberrancy (Brugada & Vereckei Criteria)
* **Features strongly favouring VT:**
  * **AV Dissociation:** Independent P waves marching through QRS complexes. (Most specific sign).
  * **Capture Beats:** Occasional narrow QRS indicating sinus node temporarily "captures" ventricles.
  * **Fusion Beats:** Hybrid QRS shape from simultaneous atrial and ventricular pacing.
  * **Concordance:** All precordial leads (V1-V6) show entirely +ve or entirely -ve QRS complexes.
  * **Extreme Axis Deviation:** ("Northwest axis").
  * **Brugada Criterion 1:** Absence of an RS complex in ALL precordial leads.

### Acute Management of Broad-Complex Tachycardias
* **Pulseless VT / VF:** CPR + Immediate unsynchronized Defibrillation + IV **Adrenaline** (1 mg every 3-5 mins) + IV **Amiodarone** (300 mg).
* **Unstable with Pulse (Hypotension, AMS, CP, HF):** Urgent Synchronized DC Cardioversion.
* **Stable Monomorphic VT:** IV **Amiodarone** 300 mg over 20-60 mins (HKHA protocol).
* **Stable Polymorphic VT (Torsades):** IV **Magnesium Sulphate** (2 g over 10 mins). Correct ↓K. Avoid Amiodarone if QT is prolonged.
* **AF with WPW (Irregular Broad Complex):** STRICTLY AVOID AV-nodal blockers (ABCD: Adenosine, BB, CCB, Digoxin). Use IV **Procainamide** or immediate Synchronized DCCV.

## 8. Inherited Channelopathies, Cardiomyopathies & Congenital Arrhythmic Syndromes

### High-Yield Inherited Channelopathies & Cardiomyopathies
* **Brugada Syndrome:** Autosomal dominant sodium channelopathy. Classic Type 1 ECG: Coved STE > 2 mm followed by inverted T wave in V1-V3. High risk of Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD) during sleep. Rx: Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD).
* **Long QT Syndrome (LQTS):** Congenital (Romano-Ward, Jervell & Lange-Nielsen). Risk of Torsades. Rx: High-dose Beta-blockers, ICD, avoid QT-prolonging drugs.
* **Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC):** Fibrofatty replacement of RV. ECG: Epsilon waves (small positive notch at end of QRS in V1-V3) + T-wave inversions V1-V3.
* **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HOCM / 肥厚型心肌病):** Deep, narrow "dagger-like" Q waves in lateral/inferior leads + extreme LVH voltage criteria.

### Acquired Causes of QT Prolongation (Crucial HK Ward Checklist)
* **Electrolytes:** Hypokalaemia, Hypomagnesaemia, Hypocalcaemia.
* **Antibiotics:** Macrolides (Clarithromycin, Erythromycin), Fluoroquinolones (Levofloxacin).
* **Psychotropics:** Haloperidol, Quetiapine, TCAs (Amitriptyline), SSRIs (Citalopram).
* **Antiarrhythmics:** Class Ia (Procainamide), Class III (Amiodarone, Sotalol).
* **Others:** Ondansetron, Methadone, Hypothermia, Ischaemia, raised ICP.

## 9. Metabolic, Electrolyte, Systemic & Drug-Induced ECG Patterns

### Potassium & Calcium Disturbances
* **Hyperkalaemia (高鉀血症):** Tall peaked T waves (narrow base) → Widened QRS → Flattened P waves/prolonged PR → Sine wave pattern → VF/Asystole. (Rx: IV Calcium Gluconate for cardioprotection).
* **Hypokalaemia (低鉀血症):** Prominent U waves, apparent QT prolongation (QU interval), ST depression, flattened T waves.
* **Hypercalcemia:** Shortened QT interval.
* **Hypocalcemia:** Prolonged QT interval (pure ST segment lengthening).

### Systemic Non-Cardiac Conditions with Classical ECG Signatures
* **Pulmonary Embolism (PE / 肺栓塞):** Sinus tachycardia (most common). Classical S1Q3T3 (Deep S in I, Q in III, T inversion in III). New RBBB. RAD.
* **Hypothermia:** Bradycardia, shivering artifact, Osborn waves (J-point elevation dome). Prolonged PR/QRS/QT.
* **Raised Intracranial Pressure (e.g., SAH):** "Cerebral T waves" (Deep, wide, symmetric, inverted T waves across precordium) + apparent QT prolongation. Mimics Wellens' syndrome.

### Drug Effects vs. Drug Toxicity (Digoxin & Sodium Channel Blockers)
* **Digoxin Effect (Therapeutic):** "Salvador Dali" sagging ST depression, flattened/biphasic T waves, prominent U waves. DOES NOT indicate toxicity.
* **Digoxin Toxicity:** Highly arrhythmogenic. Frequent PVCs, Atrial Tachycardia with AV block, bidirectional VT.
* **TCA Overdose (Sodium Channel Blockade):** Sinus tachycardia, widened QRS (> 100 ms predicts seizures, > 160 ms predicts ventricular arrhythmias), RAD, prominent terminal R wave in aVR (> 3 mm). Rx: IV Sodium Bicarbonate.

### Technical Artifacts & Lead Misplacements
* **LA/RA Lead Reversal:** Lead I completely inverted (P, QRS, T all -ve). Leads II and III switched. aVR and aVL switched. Normal precordial R-wave progression (differentiates from dextrocardia).
* **Motion/Tremor Artifact:** Mimics VF or AF. Check pulse and evaluate multiple leads to spot underlying regular QRS complexes marching through the baseline interference.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate Mobitz I from Mobitz II AV block clinically? Answer: Mobitz I improves with exercise/atropine (relief of vagal tone), while Mobitz II worsens with exercise (increased AV nodal rate exposes His-Purkinje block).
* Trap: Calling an inferior ST elevation accompanied by V1-V3 ST depression simply "Inferior MI with reciprocal changes". Pearl: Always request posterior leads (V7-V9); this is highly suspicious for a concomitant Posterior MI requiring immediate primary PCI activation.
* Pitfall: Giving AV nodal blockers (Adenosine, Verapamil, Metoprolol, Digoxin) to a patient with Atrial Fibrillation and WPW. This abolishes AV node conduction and forces 100% of atrial impulses down the accessory pathway, deteriorating into Ventricular Fibrillation.
* Viva Question: What is the most specific ECG finding for pulmonary embolism? Answer: The S1Q3T3 pattern (deep S in I, Q in III, inverted T in III), though sinus tachycardia or a new RBBB are statistically far more common.
* Trap: Assuming a broad complex tachycardia in an elderly patient is SVT with aberrancy just because they are haemodynamically stable. Pearl: Over 80% of broad complex tachycardias in adults are Ventricular Tachycardia; always treat as VT until proven otherwise, especially with a history of IHD.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing LVH by voltage criteria alone in young, thin patients (< 30 years old). High voltage in young athletes is physiological. Always look for the associated LV strain pattern before diagnosing pathological LVH.
