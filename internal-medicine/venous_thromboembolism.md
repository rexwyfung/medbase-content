# Venous Thromboembolism (靜脈血栓栓塞症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Venous Thromboembolism
* **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE)**: A disease spectrum encompassing Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) (深層靜脈血栓) and its complication, Pulmonary Embolism (PE) (肺栓塞).

### Etiologies and Virchow's Triad
* **Endothelial Injury**: Surgery (esp. orthopaedic/pelvic), trauma, central venous catheters, prior DVT.
* **Venous Stasis**: Prolonged immobilization (bedbound >3 days, long-haul flights), paralysis, pregnancy, obesity, severe heart failure.
* **Hypercoagulability (Acquired)**: Active Malignancy (癌症) (Trousseau syndrome), oral contraceptives (OCP), hormone replacement therapy (HRT), antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), nephrotic syndrome (loss of Antithrombin III), COVID-19.
* **Hypercoagulability (Inherited Thrombophilia)**: Factor V Leiden mutation (most common in Caucasians, rare in Asians), Prothrombin G20210A mutation, Protein C/S deficiency, Antithrombin III deficiency.

### Epidemiology of VTE in Hong Kong
* **Incidence**: Historically lower in Chinese populations compared to Caucasians, but incidence in Hong Kong is steadily ↑ due to aging population, ↑ obesity, and westernized diets.
* **Post-Op Risk**: High risk in local Hospital Authority (HA) orthopaedic patients (e.g., total knee/hip replacements); mandates routine chemical prophylaxis.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Deep Vein Thrombosis
* **Thrombus Formation**: Originates in venous valve pockets of deep calf veins (e.g., soleal sinusoids) → local stasis → hypoxemia → endothelial activation.
* **Coagulation Cascade**: Tissue factor expression → propagation of fibrin network trapping red blood cells (red thrombus).
* **Progression**: Thrombus propagates proximally (popliteal → femoral → iliac veins) → ↑ risk of embolization.

### Pathophysiology of Pulmonary Embolism
* **Embolization**: Proximal DVT breaks off → travels via Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) → right heart → pulmonary arterial tree.
* **Respiratory Compromise**: Alveolar dead space ↑ (ventilation without perfusion, V/Q mismatch) → hypoxemia. Local inflammatory mediators release → bronchoconstriction & surfactant depletion → atelectasis.
* **Hemodynamic Compromise (Massive PE)**: Thrombus physically obstructs pulmonary outflow → sudden ↑ pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) → Right Ventricular (RV) strain/dilation → interventricular septum bows leftward → ↓ Left Ventricular (LV) preload → ↓ cardiac output → obstructive shock & death.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Clinical History of Deep Vein Thrombosis
* **Pain**: Unilateral limb pain (usually calf or thigh), describing a severe cramping or "tight" sensation.
* **Swelling**: Gradual onset unilateral limb swelling.
* **Aggravating Factors**: Pain worsens with dependent positioning or walking.

### Clinical History of Pulmonary Embolism
* **Dyspnea**: Sudden onset, unexplained shortness of breath.
* **Pain**: Pleuritic chest pain (sharp, localized, worsens on inspiration).
* **Hemoptysis**: Coughing up blood (indicates pulmonary infarction).
* **Syncope**: Transient loss of consciousness (red flag for massive PE with hemodynamic collapse).

### Physical Examination Signs of Deep Vein Thrombosis
* **Visual Inspection**: Unilateral erythema (紅斑), visible superficial venous engorgement (collaterals).
* **Palpation**: Unilateral pitting edema, warmth, severe calf tenderness along deep venous system.
* **Measurements**: Calf circumference discrepancy >3cm (measured 10cm below tibial tuberosity).
* **Phlegmasia Cerulea Dolens**: Massive iliofemoral DVT → painful, cyanotic, swollen leg with absent pulses (venous gangrene risk).

### Physical Examination Signs of Pulmonary Embolism
* **Vitals**: Tachycardia (HR >100), Tachypnea (RR >20), Hypoxia (SpO2 <92% on room air), Hypotension (Systolic BP <90 mmHg in massive PE).
* **Cardiovascular**: RV heave, loud P2 (pulmonary component of 2nd heart sound), elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP).
* **Respiratory**: Pleural friction rub (if infarction present), localized crepitations.
* **Extremities**: Concomitant signs of DVT (unilateral leg swelling).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Deep Vein Thrombosis

| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cellulitis** | Fever, marked erythema, sharply demarcated edges, +ve skin entry wound, usually no deep vein tenderness. |
| **Ruptured Baker's Cyst** | Hx of knee osteoarthritis/RA, acute popliteal pain with fluid tracking down posterior calf ("crescent sign"). |
| **Superficial Thrombophlebitis** | Palpable, tender, hard cord along a superficial vein course; no massive whole-limb swelling. |
| **Calf Muscle Tear** | Sudden localized pain triggered by active exertion/sports; bruising often appears later. |

### Differential Diagnoses for Pulmonary Embolism

| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)** | Central, crushing chest pain (not pleuritic), radiates to jaw/arm. |
| **Aortic Dissection** | Sudden tearing/ripping chest pain radiating to the back, unequal BP in arms. |
| **Pneumothorax** | Sudden pleuritic pain, ↓ breath sounds, hyperresonant percussion note unilaterally. |
| **Pneumonia** | Fever, productive sputum, bronchial breath sounds, dull percussion note. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for VTE
* **Electrocardiogram (ECG)**: Sinus tachycardia (most common). Classical S1Q3T3 pattern (prominent S in I, Q and inverted T in III - specific but insensitive). T-wave inversions in V1-V4 (RV strain pattern), New-onset RBBB.
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Type 1 respiratory failure (hypoxemia), respiratory alkalosis (due to hyperventilation), ↑ A-a gradient.

### Blood Investigations for VTE
* **D-dimer**: Highly sensitive, low specificity. Useful ONLY for ruling out VTE in patients with low/moderate pre-test probability (Wells score).
* **Cardiac Biomarkers**: Troponin & proBNP/BNP. ↑ indicates RV myocardial microinfarction/stretch (prognostic for PE risk stratification).
* **Thrombophilia Screen**: Protein C/S, ATIII, Factor V Leiden, Lupus Anticoagulant, Anti-cardiolipin antibodies, Anti-beta-2 glycoprotein 1. (Perform ONLY in unprovoked VTE, young age <45, or strong family history; DO NOT perform during acute acute thrombosis or active anticoagulation).

### Imaging Investigations for VTE Diagnosis
* **Compression Doppler Ultrasound (US) of Leg**: 1st line for DVT. Diagnostic finding: non-compressibility of the common femoral or popliteal veins, loss of phasic flow, visible echogenic thrombus.
* **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA)**: Gold standard for PE. Diagnostic finding: intraluminal filling defect within pulmonary arteries. (Requires IV contrast).
* **Ventilation/Perfusion (V/Q) Scan**: Alternative for PE if CTPA contraindicated (e.g., severe renal failure, contrast allergy, pregnancy). Diagnostic finding: mismatched perfusion defects with normal ventilation.
* **Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE)**: Bedside tool for unstable PE. Diagnostic findings: RV dilatation, paradoxical septal motion, McConnell's sign (RV free wall akinesia with apical sparing), D-shaped LV in systole.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Resuscitation for Massive PE (Hemodynamically Unstable)
* **Definition**: PE causing hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg or drop of ≥40 mmHg) or obstructive shock.
* **Supportive**: High-flow O2, cautious IV fluid bolus (avoid fluid overload which worsens RV failure), Inotropes/Vasopressors (Noradrenaline) if fluids fail.
* **Systemic Thrombolysis**: IV Alteplase (tPA). Indicated to rapidly dissolve clot and reduce RV strain.
* **Rescue Therapies**: Surgical pulmonary embolectomy or percutaneous catheter-directed thrombolysis/embolectomy if systemic tPA is absolutely contraindicated (e.g., recent neurosurgery, active bleeding) or fails.

### Acute Anticoagulation for Submassive/Low-Risk PE & DVT
* **LMWH Bridging (HA Standard)**: Subcutaneous (SC) Low Molecular Weight Heparin (e.g., Tinzaparin, Enoxaparin) dosed by body weight (e.g., Tinzaparin 175 IU/kg SC daily). Used if bridging to Warfarin or pending definitive diagnosis.
* **Unfractionated Heparin (UFH)**: IV UFH infusion. Preferred over LMWH in severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) or high bleeding risk (easily reversible with Protamine).
* **Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs)**: Apixaban or Rivaroxaban can be started immediately (monotherapy with initial loading dose). Dabigatran or Edoxaban require 5 days of initial LMWH treatment first.

### Indications for Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) Filter Placement
* **Absolute Contraindication to Anticoagulation**: (e.g., recent hemorrhagic stroke, severe active GI bleed).
* **Failure of Anticoagulation**: Recurrent PE despite proven therapeutic levels of anticoagulation.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Pharmacological Treatment Algorithm for VTE
* **DOACs (1st Line)**: Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran, Edoxaban. Preferred over Warfarin due to lower intracranial bleeding risk and no INR monitoring. (Note: In HK HA, often Self-Financed Items [SFI]).
* **Warfarin (2nd Line / Specific Indications)**: Vitamin K antagonist. Target INR 2.0-3.0. Requires dietary vitamin K consistency. Preferred in APS, mechanical heart valves, or severe renal failure.
* **LMWH Monotherapy**: Historically 1st line for cancer-associated thrombosis (Malignancy), but DOACs (e.g., Edoxaban, Rivaroxaban) are now increasingly used unless there is high GI/GU bleeding risk.

### Duration of Anticoagulation Therapy
| Clinical Scenario | Recommended Duration |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Provoked VTE (Transient/Reversible risk factor e.g., surgery, trauma)** | 3 months. |
| **Unprovoked VTE (1st episode)** | 3-6 months minimum, then reassess for long-term/lifelong extension based on bleeding risk vs recurrence risk. |
| **Recurrent VTE or Active Malignancy** | Lifelong / Extended indefinitely. |
| **Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS)** | Lifelong (Warfarin is strictly required; DOACs contraindicated). |

### Lifestyle Modifications and Prevention
* **Mechanical Prevention**: Early ambulation post-surgery.
* **Graded Compression Stockings**: Help ↓ symptoms of leg swelling and pain, but no longer strongly recommended to prevent Post-Thrombotic Syndrome (PTS).
* **Risk Factor Modification**: Weight loss, smoking cessation, avoid OCP/HRT (switch to progestogen-only or non-hormonal contraception).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Wells Score for DVT Probability
| Clinical Feature | Points |
| :--- | :--- |
| Active cancer (treatment within last 6 months) | +1 |
| Paralysis, paresis, or recent plaster cast | +1 |
| Recently bedridden >3 days or major surgery within 12 weeks | +1 |
| Localized tenderness along deep venous system | +1 |
| Entire leg swollen | +1 |
| Calf swelling >3 cm larger than asymptomatic side | +1 |
| Pitting edema confined to symptomatic leg | +1 |
| Collateral superficial veins (non-varicose) | +1 |
| Previously documented DVT | +1 |
| Alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT | -2 |
* **Interpretation**: DVT Likely if score ≥2. DVT Unlikely if score <2.

### Wells Score for PE Probability
| Clinical Feature | Points |
| :--- | :--- |
| Clinical signs and symptoms of DVT | +3.0 |
| Alternative diagnosis less likely than PE | +3.0 |
| Heart rate > 100 beats per minute | +1.5 |
| Immobilization (>3 days) or surgery in previous 4 weeks | +1.5 |
| Previous DVT or PE | +1.5 |
| Hemoptysis | +1.0 |
| Active malignancy | +1.0 |
* **Interpretation**: PE Likely if score >4. PE Unlikely if score ≤4.

### Pulmonary Embolism Severity Index (PESI)
* **Purpose**: Stratifies 30-day mortality risk in confirmed PE to determine inpatient vs outpatient management.
* **Variables**: Age, male sex, cancer, heart failure, chronic lung disease, HR ≥110, SBP <100, RR ≥30, Temp <36°C, altered mental status, SpO2 <90%.
* **Prognosis**: Low PESI score (Class I/II) can often be managed with early discharge and outpatient DOACs.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Pathological Complications of VTE
* **Post-Thrombotic Syndrome (PTS)**: Chronic venous hypertension due to valve destruction by DVT → chronic leg pain, intractable swelling, stasis dermatitis, venous ulcers.
* **Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension (CTEPH)**: Organized, fibrotic thrombus fails to dissolve → chronic ↑ pulmonary pressure → Right heart failure (Cor Pulmonale). Curative Rx: Pulmonary endarterectomy.
* **Paradoxical Embolism**: VTE crosses via Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO) or Atrial Septal Defect (ASD) into arterial circulation → Ischemic Stroke or acute limb ischemia.

### Adverse Effects of Anticoagulant Therapy
* **Major Hemorrhage**: Intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), massive gastrointestinal bleeding.
* **Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT)**: Paradoxical pro-thrombotic state caused by IgG antibodies against Heparin-Platelet Factor 4 (PF4) complexes. Usually occurs 5-10 days after heparin initiation. Rx: Stop Heparin immediately, switch to non-heparin agent (e.g., Fondaparinux, Argatroban).
* **Reversal Agents for Bleeding Complications**:
  * **Warfarin**: IV Vitamin K + Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC).
  * **UFH**: Protamine Sulfate.
  * **Dabigatran**: Idarucizumab.
  * **Factor Xa Inhibitors (Apixaban/Rivaroxaban)**: Andexanet alfa (limited HA availability; off-label PCC often used).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically rule out PE in a low-risk patient? Answer: Calculate the Wells Score; if PE is 'Unlikely' (≤4), perform a high-sensitivity D-dimer. If D-dimer is negative, PE is ruled out without imaging.
* Viva Question: What is the classic triad of Virchow? Answer: Endothelial injury, venous stasis, and hypercoagulability.
* Trap: Relying on Homan's sign (calf pain on ankle dorsiflexion) to diagnose DVT. Pearl: Homan's sign is notoriously insensitive and non-specific; never rely on it to rule in or out DVT. Use Wells score and Doppler US.
* Trap: Checking thrombophilia screens routinely for every DVT patient. Pearl: Only check in unprovoked VTE, especially in young patients (<45) or those with strong family history. Never check during the acute thrombotic event or while on active anticoagulation, as acute phase consumes factors and yields false positives/negatives.
* Pitfall: Starting a patient with Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS) on a DOAC. DOACs are contraindicated in true APS due to a higher rate of recurrent thromboembolic events. You must use Warfarin.
* Pitfall: Withholding therapeutic anticoagulation in a highly suspicious PE case while waiting for the CTPA result. If clinical suspicion is high and bleeding risk is low, immediately administer a treatment dose of LMWH before sending the patient to the scanner.
