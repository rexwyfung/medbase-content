# Oncological Emergencies (腫瘤急症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Neutropenic Sepsis (嗜中性白血球缺乏性敗血症)
* **Definition**: Single oral temp ≥38.3°C OR ≥38.0°C sustained for >1 hr + Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) <0.5 × 10^9/L (or <1.0 expecting drop to <0.5 in 48h).
* **Etiology**: Cytotoxic chemotherapy (esp. haematological malignancies, intensive regimens) → mucosal barrier breakdown + immunosuppression.
* **Epidemiology (HK)**: Peak incidence 7-14 days post-chemo. Common pathogens: Gram -ve (Pseudomonas, E. coli, Klebsiella), Gram +ve (CoNS, Staph aureus, Enterococcus), Fungi (Candida, Aspergillus).

### Definition of Tumour Lysis Syndrome (腫瘤溶解綜合症)
* **Definition**: Massive tumour cell death causing metabolic catastrophe (hyperuricaemia, hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia).
* **Etiology**: Usually post-chemotherapy in high-turnover/bulky tumours (Burkitt lymphoma, ALL, AML). Rarely spontaneous.

### Definition of Malignant Spinal Cord Compression (惡性脊髓壓迫)
* **Definition**: Extradural compression of spinal cord/cauda equina by metastatic tumour mass.
* **Etiology**: Breast, prostate, lung cancers, myeloma (骨髓瘤). Most common in thoracic spine (70%).

### Definition of Superior Vena Cava Obstruction (上腔靜脈阻塞)
* **Definition**: Compression, invasion, or thrombosis of the SVC → compromised venous drainage of head/neck/upper limbs.
* **Etiology**: Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC), Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC), Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL). Central venous catheters (thrombosis).

### Definition of Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy (惡性高血鈣)
* **Definition**: Corrected serum calcium >2.6 mmol/L in presence of cancer.
* **Etiology**: Squamous cell carcinomas (lung, head/neck), breast cancer, multiple myeloma, renal cell carcinoma.

### Definition of Mechanical & Coagulative Emergencies
* **Raised Intracranial Pressure (ICP) (顱內壓升高)**: Due to primary brain tumours or metastases (lung, breast, melanoma).
* **Cardiac Tamponade (心包填塞)**: Malignant pericardial effusion (lung, breast, leukaemia/lymphoma).
* **DIC in Malignancy (瀰漫性血管內凝血)**: Uncontrolled coagulation cascade activation (APML, mucin-secreting adenocarcinomas like pancreatic/gastric).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Neutropenic Sepsis
* Chemotherapy → marrow stem cell apoptosis → absolute neutropenia.
* Chemotherapy → GI mucosal damage (mucositis) → translocation of endogenous gut flora into bloodstream.
* **Consequence**: Rapid progression to septic shock (敗血性休克) due to absent innate immune response.

### Pathophysiology of Tumour Lysis Syndrome
* Cell lysis → release of intracellular K+, PO4(3-), and nucleic acids.
* Nucleic acids → metabolized to uric acid (urate) → precipitation in renal tubules → acute kidney injury (AKI).
* Hyperphosphataemia → binds serum calcium → secondary hypocalcaemia → precipitation of calcium phosphate in kidneys (nephrocalcinosis).

### Pathophysiology of MSCC & SVCO
* **MSCC**: Hematogenous spread to vertebral body (Batson's venous plexus) → posterior expansion into epidural space → ischaemia/compression of cord → edema → irreversible infarction.
* **SVCO**: Extrinsic compression by tumor/lymph nodes vs intrinsic thrombosis. SVC is thin-walled and easily compressed → venous stasis → collateral vein formation over weeks.

### Pathophysiology of Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy
* **Humoral**: Ectopic secretion of Parathyroid Hormone-related Protein (PTHrP) → binds PTH receptors → ↑ bone resorption, ↑ renal Ca reabsorption (80% of cases, e.g., Squamous Cell CA).
* **Osteolytic**: Local osteoclast activation by bone metastases (e.g., Breast CA, Myeloma) via RANKL/MIP-1α.
* **Calcitriol-mediated**: 1-alpha-hydroxylase activity in lymphomas → ↑ active Vitamin D.

### Pathophysiology of Raised ICP, Tamponade & DIC
* **Raised ICP**: Mass effect + vasogenic edema disrupting blood-brain barrier → compromised cerebral perfusion.
* **Tamponade**: Fluid accumulation in rigid pericardial space → ↑ intrapericardial pressure → impaired diastolic ventricular filling → ↓ stroke volume (obstructive shock).
* **DIC**: Tumour cells express Tissue Factor (TF) and Cancer Procoagulant → systemic thrombin generation → fibrin deposition (microvascular thrombosis) + consumption of platelets/factors (bleeding).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Clinical Features of Neutropenic Sepsis
* **History**: Recent chemo (exact date), chills, rigors, dysuria, cough, diarrhea, central line status.
* **Signs**: Often ABSENT due to lack of neutrophils (e.g., no purulence/erythema at infection sites). Tachycardia, hypotension, altered mental status. Look for subtle skin lesions (Ecthyma gangrenosum - Pseudomonas).

### Clinical Features of Tumour Lysis Syndrome
* **History**: Muscle cramps, lethargy, nausea, palpitations, reduced urine output.
* **Signs**: Fluid overload (oedema), arrhythmias, tetany (Chvostek's/Trousseau's signs due to hypocalcaemia), seizures.

### Clinical Features of Malignant Spinal Cord Compression
* **History**: Progressively worsening back pain (worse on lying flat/coughing), radicular pain, limb weakness.
* **Late Symptoms**: Sensory loss, bowel/bladder incontinence, urinary retention.
* **Signs**: Localized spinal tenderness. UMN signs below lesion (hyperreflexia, upgoing plantars, spasticity, Babinski +ve). LMN signs at lesion level. Dermatomal sensory level (crucial localizing sign). Decreased anal tone.

### Clinical Features of Superior Vena Cava Obstruction
* **History**: Dyspnea, headache, facial fullness (worse on bending forward/lying down).
* **Signs**: Facial plethora/cyanosis, periorbital oedema, distended non-pulsatile jugular veins.
* **Pemberton’s Sign**: Elevating both arms above head for 1 min → facial cyanosis/engorgement and respiratory distress.
* **Chest**: Dilated collateral superficial veins on anterior chest wall.

### Clinical Features of Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy
* **History**: Polyuria, polydipsia, constipation, abdominal pain, nausea, severe fatigue, confusion, coma.
* **Signs**: Dehydration (↓ skin turgor, dry mucous membranes), hyporeflexia, lethargy, altered sensorium.

### Clinical Features of Raised ICP & Tamponade
* **Raised ICP**: Headache (worse in morning/with Valsalva), projectile vomiting, altered GCS.
* **ICP Signs**: Papilloedema, Cushing’s reflex (bradycardia, hypertension, irregular breathing - LATE). CN III/VI palsies.
* **Tamponade Hx**: Dyspnea, chest fullness, orthopnea.
* **Tamponade Signs**: Beck's Triad (hypotension, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds). Pulsus paradoxus (insp. drop in systolic BP >10mmHg). Tachycardia.

### Clinical Features of DIC in Malignancy
* **History**: Spontaneous bleeding from mucosal sites/IV lines, fever, breathlessness.
* **Signs**: Petechiae, ecchymoses, oozing from venepuncture sites, gangrene of digits (microthrombi).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for SVCO & MSCC
| Feature | Malignant SVCO | Benign SVCO (e.g., Thrombosis, Goitre) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Gradual (weeks) | Often sudden (if acute DVT) or very slow (Goitre) |
| **Associated signs** | Weight loss, cachexia, clubbing | Central line in situ, thyromegaly |
| **Feature** | **MSCC** | **Epidural Abscess / Haematoma** |
| **Pain type** | Worse recumbent | Fever, IVDU Hx (Abscess) / Anticoagulants (Haematoma) |
| **Progression** | Days to weeks | Hours to days |

### Differentials for Hypercalcaemia & TLS
* **Primary Hyperparathyroidism vs Malignancy**:
  * **Primary HPT**: Slow onset, mildly symptomatic, ↑ PTH, Cl/PO4 ratio >33.
  * **Malignancy**: Rapid onset, severely symptomatic, ↓ PTH, ↑ PTHrP.
* **TLS vs Spontaneous Renal Failure**:
  * **TLS**: Extreme ↑ Uric acid and PO4 with severe hypocalcaemia.
  * **Classical AKI**: Moderate ↑ PO4, modest/no early hypocalcaemia.

### Differentials for Shock & DIC in Malignancy
* **Septic Shock vs Cardiogenic (Tamponade)**:
  * **Septic**: Warm extremities, bounding pulse early, known neutropenia.
  * **Tamponade**: Cold extremities, narrow pulse pressure, elevated JVP.
* **DIC vs Severe Liver Metastases**:
  * **Liver Failure**: ↑ PT/APTT, normal/↓ plts, normal/mild ↑ D-dimer, normal FVIII.
  * **DIC**: ↑ PT/APTT, significantly ↓ plts, very ↑↑ D-dimer, ↓ FVIII.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Investigations for Neutropenic Sepsis
* **Bedside**: Vitals, continuous ECG, strict fluid balance.
* **Bloods**: CBP/Diff (stat ANC), RFT, LFT, CRP/Procalcitonin, Coagulation.
* **Cultures**: Paired blood cultures (Peripheral + Central line) BEFORE Abx, MSU culture, Sputum culture, Wound swabs.
* **Imaging**: CXR (often normal even with pneumonia due to lack of inflammatory cells). CT Thorax if persistent fever.

### Investigations for Tumour Lysis Syndrome
* **Bedside**: ECG (peaked T waves, flat P waves, wide QRS, sine wave).
* **Bloods**: Urate, K+, PO4(3-), Total/Corrected Ca, RFT (Creatinine, Urea), LDH (tumor burden marker).
* **Diagnostic Criteria (Cairo-Bishop Laboratory TLS)**:
  * 2 or more of the following occurring 3 days before to 7 days after chemotherapy:
  * Uric acid ≥476 µmol/L (8 mg/dL) or 25% increase.
  * Potassium ≥6.0 mmol/L or 25% increase.
  * Phosphorus ≥1.45 mmol/L (4.5 mg/dL) or 25% increase.
  * Calcium ≤1.75 mmol/L (7.0 mg/dL) or 25% decrease.

### Investigations for MSCC & SVCO
* **MSCC Imaging**: Urgent whole-spine MRI (Gold standard, detects multilevel lesions in ~30%).
* **SVCO Imaging**: CT Thorax with IV contrast (maps extent of compression, collateral vessels, biopsy targets).
* **Tissue Dx (if primary unknown)**: Sputum cytology, pleural fluid cytology, US/CT-guided biopsy.

### Investigations for Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy
* **Bedside**: ECG (shortened QT interval, prominent J/Osborn waves).
* **Bloods**: Total and Corrected Calcium, Albumin, PO4, ALP, Intact PTH (will be completely suppressed <20 pg/mL), PTHrP (if available), SPEP/UPEP (myeloma screen).

### Investigations for Raised ICP, Tamponade & DIC
* **Raised ICP**: CT Brain plain (look for midline shift, hydrocephalus, masses) BEFORE any LP. MRI Brain +/- contrast (definitive).
* **Tamponade**: Bedside Echocardiography (RA/RV diastolic collapse, swinging heart, IVC plethora). ECG (electrical alternans, low voltage QRS).
* **DIC**: CBP (↓ Platelets, Schistocytes on smear), Coag profile (↑ PT, ↑ APTT, ↓ Fibrinogen, ↑ D-dimer).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Neutropenic Sepsis
* **Immediate Protocol**: IV Antibiotics MUST be given within 60 minutes of presentation ("Golden Hour"). Do not wait for cultures if difficult to draw.
* **HK HA Empirical 1st Line**: IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin) OR IV Cefepime OR IV Meropenem (if high-risk/ESBL risk).
* **Additions**:
  * + IV Vancomycin (if MRSA risk, central line infection, severe mucositis, hemodynamic instability).
  * + IV Aminoglycoside (e.g., Amikacin, Gentamicin) if severe sepsis/shock.
* **Fluid Resuscitation**: Aggressive IV crystalloids for hypotension.

### Acute Management of Tumour Lysis Syndrome
* **Hydration**: Aggressive IV hydration (target urine output >100 ml/hr).
* **Hyperuricaemia**:
  * IV/PO Allopurinol (xanthine oxidase inhibitor - prevents new urate).
  * IV Rasburicase (recombinant urate oxidase - directly breaks down existing urate). *Requires HA special approval, CI in G6PD deficiency*.
* **Hyperkalaemia**: IV Calcium Gluconate (cardioprotective), IV Insulin + Dextrose, Salbutamol nebs.
* **Renal Support**: Early CVVH/Haemodialysis if refractory hyperkalaemia/anuria.

### Acute Management of Malignant Spinal Cord Compression
* **Immediate Corticosteroids**: IV Dexamethasone 16mg stat, then 16mg daily (divided doses) + PPI cover.
* **Spine Precautions**: Strict bed rest, log rolling only until spinal stability confirmed.
* **Definitive Rx (within 24h)**: Urgent oncology/ortho consult.
  * Urgent Radiotherapy (Standard for radio-sensitive tumours/widespread mets).
  * Decompressive Surgery (if spine unstable, radio-resistant tumor, or previous RT to area).

### Acute Management of Superior Vena Cava Obstruction
* **Positioning**: Sit patient up (reduces hydrostatic pressure), give O2.
* **Corticosteroids**: IV Dexamethasone (16mg/day) to reduce tumor edema (esp. for lymphoma).
* **Endovascular Rx**: Urgent SVC stenting (fastest symptom relief, preferred in HA for severe symptoms).
* **Oncological Rx**: Urgent chemo (SCLC, Lymphoma) or urgent radiotherapy (NSCLC).

### Acute Management of Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy
* **1. Aggressive Rehydration**: IV Normal Saline (3-4 Liters/day) - restores volume, promotes renal Ca excretion.
* **2. Loop Diuretics**: IV Furosemide (only AFTER fully rehydrated) - blocks distal Ca reabsorption.
* **3. Bisphosphonates**: IV Zoledronic acid or IV Pamidronate (onset takes 48-72h, lasts weeks).
* **4. Calcitonin**: SC/IM Calcitonin (rapid onset in 4-6h, tachyphylaxis after 48h).

### Acute Management of Raised ICP, Tamponade & DIC
* **Raised ICP**: Elevate head of bed 30°, IV Dexamethasone (16mg/day for vasogenic edema), IV Mannitol (if herniation risk), hyperventilation (temporary).
* **Tamponade**: Urgent echo-guided pericardiocentesis (心包穿刺). IV fluid bolus (maintains right heart filling pressure temporarily).
* **DIC**: Treat underlying malignancy. Transfuse Platelets (<50), Cryoprecipitate (if Fibrinogen <1.0), FFP (for ↑ PT/APTT + active bleeding).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Prophylaxis for Neutropenic Sepsis
* **Growth Factors**: SC G-CSF (e.g., Filgrastim, Pegfilgrastim) primary prophylaxis for chemo regimens with >20% risk of febrile neutropenia, or secondary prophylaxis if previous episode.
* **Antimicrobial Prophylaxis**: PO Ciprofloxacin/Levofloxacin and PO Fluconazole/Posaconazole for high-risk patients (e.g., acute leukemia induction).

### Chronic Maintenance for TLS Risk
* **Pre-Chemotherapy**: Allopurinol starting 24-48 hours before chemo. Aggressive PO/IV hydration protocols prior to intensive infusions.

### Chronic Maintenance for Bone Mets & Hypercalcaemia
* **Antiresorptive Therapy**: Monthly IV Zoledronic acid or SC Denosumab (RANKL inhibitor) to prevent skeletal-related events (SREs).
* **Dental Check**: MUST have full dental clearance before starting Bisphosphonates/Denosumab due to risk of Osteonecrosis of Jaw (ONJ).

### Long-Term Palliation for SVCO, MSCC & Tamponade
* **MSCC Rehabilitation**: Physiotherapy, occupational therapy, pressure sore prevention, bowel/bladder regimens (catheterization).
* **Tamponade Recurrence**: Surgical pericardial window or indwelling pericardial catheter (PleurX) for recurrent malignant effusions.
* **SVCO Management**: Long-term anticoagulation if SVC stent inserted (depending on patency/thrombus burden).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### MASCC Score for Neutropenic Sepsis
* **Purpose**: Identifies LOW-RISK patients who can potentially be treated with oral antibiotics as outpatients.
* **Variables**: Burden of illness (0-5), No hypotension (5), No COPD (4), Solid tumor or absent fungal infection (4), No dehydration requiring IV fluids (3), Outpatient status at onset (3), Age <60 (2).
* **Interpretation**: Score ≥21 = Low risk of severe complications. Score <21 = High risk (requires admission and IV Abx).

### TLS Risk Stratification
* **High Risk**: Burkitt's, ALL, AML (WBC >100), bulky disease. Requires prophylactic Rasburicase + aggressive hydration.
* **Intermediate/Low Risk**: Solid tumors, indolent lymphomas. Requires Allopurinol + hydration.

### Prognostic Factors in Malignant Spinal Cord Compression
* **Key Factor**: Neurological status at presentation is the absolute best predictor of outcome. Ambulatory at presentation → ~80% remain ambulatory. Paraplegic at presentation → <10% regain ability to walk.

### Prognostic Features in Malignancy-Associated Hypercalcaemia
* Generally implies advanced, end-stage disease with dismal prognosis. Median survival is 2-3 months from onset if solid tumour.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Neutropenic Sepsis
* ARDS, Acute Kidney Injury (ATN from hypotension), Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), Multi-organ Failure (MOF), Death.

### Complications of Tumour Lysis Syndrome
* **Renal**: Uric acid nephropathy, Calcium phosphate nephrocalcinosis → Permanent ESRD.
* **Cardiac**: Ventricular fibrillation / Asystole (from hyperkalaemia).

### Complications of MSCC & SVCO
* **MSCC**: Permanent paraplegia/tetraplegia, autonomic dysreflexia, recurrent UTIs, pressure ulcers.
* **SVCO**: Cerebral edema, airway compromise (tracheal edema), thrombosis of SVC stent.

### Drug Adverse Effects (Emergency Treatments)
* **Rasburicase**: Anaphylaxis, Methemoglobinemia, severe haemolysis in G6PD deficiency (Absolute Contraindication).
* **Zoledronic Acid**: Acute phase reaction (flu-like symptoms), Osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ), Atypical femoral fractures.
* **Dexamethasone**: Hyperglycemia, steroid-induced psychosis, proximal myopathy, peptic ulceration.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical time-dependent action in a patient presenting with neutropenic sepsis? Answer: Administration of broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g., Tazocin) within 60 minutes of arrival.
* Trap: Waiting for blood culture results or central line access before giving the first dose of antibiotics. Pearl: Give empirical antibiotics immediately; delay increases mortality by roughly 8% per hour.
* Pitfall: Sending a patient with suspected malignant spinal cord compression for a plain X-ray of the spine. Plain films miss 10-20% of bone mets and do not evaluate the cord. Urgent whole-spine MRI is the gold standard.
* Viva Question: Why is Allopurinol given before chemotherapy in high-risk patients but Rasburicase is preferred in established Tumour Lysis Syndrome? Answer: Allopurinol only stops new uric acid synthesis but doesn't clear existing levels. Rasburicase directly degrades existing uric acid into highly soluble allantoin.
* Trap: Giving Rasburicase without checking G6PD status in a Chinese patient. Pearl: Always check G6PD status as Rasburicase generates hydrogen peroxide during urate breakdown, causing massive haemolysis in G6PD deficiency.
* Pitfall: Giving IV Furosemide to a patient with severe hypercalcaemia before they are adequately fluid resuscitated. Loop diuretics will worsen volume depletion, reducing GFR and actually decreasing renal calcium excretion. Always hydrate first.
* Viva Question: What is Pemberton's sign and what does it indicate? Answer: Raising both arms above the head for 60 seconds causes facial cyanosis and engorgement; it indicates Superior Vena Cava Obstruction (often a thoracic inlet mass).
* Trap: Performing a lumbar puncture in a cancer patient with headache and vomiting without prior imaging. Pearl: Must perform a CT Brain first to rule out a space-occupying lesion (metastasis) to prevent fatal tonsillar herniation.
