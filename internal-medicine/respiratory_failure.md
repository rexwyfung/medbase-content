# Respiratory Failure (呼吸衰竭)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Respiratory Failure
* **Respiratory Failure (呼吸衰竭):** Syndrome of inadequate gas exchange due to dysfunction of respiratory system.
* **Diagnostic Threshold (Room Air, Sea Level):** PaO2 < 8.0 kPa (60 mmHg). 
* **Type 1 Respiratory Failure (T1RF):** Hypoxemia without hypercapnia. PaCO2 normal or ↓ (< 6.6 kPa / 50 mmHg).
* **Type 2 Respiratory Failure (T2RF):** Hypoxemia with hypercapnia. PaCO2 ↑ (> 6.6 kPa / 50 mmHg).

### Etiologies of Type 1 Respiratory Failure (Hypoxemic)
* **Pulmonary Disease:** **Pneumonia (肺炎)**, **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome [ARDS] (急性呼吸窘迫綜合症)**, fibrotic interstitial lung disease.
* **Vascular:** **Pulmonary Embolism [PE] (肺栓塞)**, pulmonary hypertension.
* **Cardiac:** **Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema (心源性肺水腫)**.
* **Pleural:** Large pneumothorax, massive pleural effusion.

### Etiologies of Type 2 Respiratory Failure (Hypercapnic)
* **Airway Obstruction:** **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease [COPD] (慢性阻塞性肺病)**, severe acute asthma, OSA, tracheal stenosis.
* **Neuromuscular Disease (NMD):** Motor neuron disease (ALS), Guillain-Barré Syndrome, myasthenia gravis, severe kyphoscoliosis.
* **Central Hypoventilation:** Opiate/sedative overdose, brainstem lesions, **Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome [OHS] (肥胖低通氣綜合症)**.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **COPD Burden:** Top 10 leading causes of death in HK; heavily drives local T2RF admissions (especially during winter influenza peaks).
* **Demographics:** Predominantly elderly males with historical smoking habits (though local smoking prevalence has ↓ to ~9.5%). 
* **Tuberculosis (TB):** Endemic in HK; post-TB bronchiectasis/fibrosis remains a significant structural cause of T1RF/T2RF locally.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiological Mechanisms of T1RF
* **Ventilation-Perfusion (V/Q) Mismatch:** Most common. Alveoli perfused but poorly ventilated (e.g., pneumonia, COPD) → low V/Q ratio.
* **Right-to-Left Shunt:** Alveoli completely unventilated (fluid-filled in ARDS/edema, or anatomical shunt). Hallmark: Hypoxemia refractory to 100% O2.
* **Diffusion Limitation:** Thickened alveolar-capillary membrane (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis). Worsens on exertion due to ↓ transit time.
* **Alveolar Hypoventilation:** High altitude (low inspired PiO2) → isolated hypoxemia.

### Pathophysiological Mechanisms of T2RF
* **Alveolar Hypoventilation:** Inadequate alveolar ventilation (Va) relative to CO2 production (VCO2). PaCO2 is directly proportional to VCO2 and inversely proportional to Va.
* **↑ Dead Space Ventilation:** Areas ventilated but not perfused (high V/Q). E.g., hyperinflated emphysema compressing capillaries.
* **Respiratory Muscle Fatigue:** Increased work of breathing (WOB) outstrips muscle capacity (e.g., severe asthma, COPD exacerbation).

### The "Hypoxic Drive" Phenomenon in Chronic T2RF
* **Normal Physiology:** Central chemoreceptors (medulla) sense CSF [H+] (proxy for PaCO2) → primary driver of respiration.
* **Chronic Hypercapnia:** Renal retention of HCO3- normalizes CSF pH despite ↑ PaCO2 → central chemoreceptors desensitize to CO2.
* **Peripheral Shift:** Respiration becomes primarily driven by peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid/aortic bodies) sensing hypoxemia (PaO2).
* **O2-Induced Hypercapnia Risk:** High FiO2 → eliminates hypoxic drive → hypoventilation → fatal CO2 narcosis. Also, Haldane effect (O2 displaces CO2 from Hb) and worsening V/Q mismatch contribute.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Clues for Respiratory Failure
* **Onset:** Sudden (PE, pneumothorax), gradual over hours/days (pneumonia, AECOPD), progressive over months (fibrosis, NMD).
* **Associated Symptoms:** Pleuritic chest pain (PE, pneumonia), orthopnea/PND (heart failure), purulent sputum/fever (infection).
* **Neurological Symptoms:** Morning headaches, daytime somnolence, lethargy, poor concentration (hallmarks of chronic hypercapnia / OHS).
* **Medication History:** Recent use of benzodiazepines, opiates, or non-compliance with home BiPAP/CPAP.

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Hypoxemia (T1RF)
* **General:** **Central cyanosis (中樞性發紺)** (blue lips/tongue, indicating SaO2 < 85% with > 5g/dL deoxygenated Hb), tachypnea, tachycardia.
* **Neurological:** Agitation, confusion, delirium, seizures (late).
* **Extremities:** Clubbing (fibrosis, bronchiectasis), peripheral cyanosis (poor perfusion).

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Hypercapnia (T2RF)
* **Neurological:** **Asterixis / Flapping tremor (撲翼樣震顫)**, altered mental status, depressed GCS, depressed tendon reflexes.
* **Ophthalmological:** **Papilledema (視乳頭水腫)** (due to CO2-induced cerebral vasodilation → ↑ ICP).
* **Cardiovascular:** Bounding pulse, warm peripheries, diaphoresis (CO2 is a potent peripheral vasodilator).

### Respiratory Physical Examination Findings
* **COPD/Asthma:** Hyperinflated barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing, use of accessory muscles, paradoxical abdominal breathing, expiratory polyphonic wheeze.
* **Pneumonia:** Bronchial breath sounds, localized coarse crackles, dullness to percussion, ↑ vocal resonance.
* **Pulmonary Edema:** Bilateral basal fine bibasilar crackles, S3 gallop, elevated JVP, peripheral edema.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table of Respiratory Failure Etiologies
| Condition | Primary RF Type | Discriminatory History | Discriminatory Signs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AECOPD** | T2RF (or T1RF) | Chronic smoker, progressive dyspnea | Barrel chest, wheeze, pursed lips, asterixis |
| **Pneumonia** | T1RF | Fever, productive cough, pleuritic pain | Localized crackles, bronchial breathing, fever |
| **Pulmonary Edema** | T1RF | Orthopnea, ischemic heart disease Hx | Elevated JVP, fine basal crackles, S3 |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | T1RF | Immobilization, malignancy, sudden dyspnea | Tachycardia, clear lungs, unilateral leg swelling |
| **Neuromuscular (e.g. ALS)** | T2RF | Progressive weakness, bulbar symptoms | Fasciculations, weak cough, shallow breathing |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Pulse Oximetry (SpO2):** Continuous monitoring. Inaccurate in severe hypoperfusion or CO poisoning.
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG):** **Gold Standard for Diagnosis**.
* **ECG:** Exclude acute MI, look for Right Heart Strain (S1Q3T3, Right Axis Deviation, RBBB in PE or Cor Pulmonale).
* **Spirometry:** (Performed when stable) Obstructive (FEV1/FVC < 0.7) vs Restrictive defect.

### Diagnostic Criteria via ABG (Room Air)
| Parameter | Type 1 RF | Type 2 RF | Normal Range |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **PaO2** | < 8.0 kPa | < 8.0 kPa | 10 - 13.3 kPa |
| **PaCO2** | Normal / < 6.6 kPa | > 6.6 kPa | 4.7 - 6.0 kPa |
| **A-a Gradient** | Elevated (>20 mmHg) | Normal (if pure hypoventilation) or Elevated | < 15 mmHg (age-dependent) |
| **HCO3- (Acute)** | Normal | Normal | 22 - 26 mmol/L |
| **HCO3- (Chronic)** | Normal | Elevated (>26 mmol/L) | 22 - 26 mmol/L |

### Blood Investigations
* **CBC:** ↑ WBC (infection), ↑ Hb/Hematocrit (secondary polycythemia from chronic hypoxia).
* **U&E & Cr:** Assess renal function, hypokalemia (diuretic use, limits muscle function).
* **Cardiac Biomarkers:** Troponin I/T (MI), NT-proBNP (Heart failure vs pulmonary cause).
* **Microbiology:** Sputum microscopy/culture, Blood cultures, Viral multiplex PCR (Influenza/COVID/RSV).

### Imaging & Advanced Modalities
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Consolidation (Pneumonia), hyperinflation/flat diaphragms (COPD), bat-wing opacities/Kerley B lines (Pulmonary Edema), white-out (ARDS/pleural effusion).
* **CT Pulmonary Angiogram (CTPA):** Diagnostic for PE.
* **HRCT Thorax:** For delineating interstitial lung disease or bronchiectasis.
* **Echocardiogram:** Assess LV ejection fraction, RV strain, estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PASP).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Type 1 Respiratory Failure
* **Goal:** Correct hypoxia. Target SpO2 **94-98%**.
* **Delivery:** Nasal cannula (1-4 L/min) → Simple face mask (5-10 L/min) → Non-Rebreather Mask (15 L/min).
* **High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC):** Provides heated, humidified O2 up to 60 L/min + mild PEEP.
* **Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP):** Indicated for cardiogenic pulmonary edema (splints open fluid-filled alveoli) or OSA.
* **Treat Reversible Cause:** IV antibiotics (Pneumonia), IV diuretics/nitrates (Pulmonary Edema), anticoagulation (PE).

### Acute Management of Type 2 Respiratory Failure
* **Goal:** Correct hypoxia WITHOUT worsening hypercapnia. Target SpO2 **88-92%**.
* **Delivery:** **Venturi Mask (文丘里面罩)** to deliver fixed FiO2 (start at 24% or 28%), titrate strictly based on serial ABGs.
* **Treat Reversible Cause:** Nebulized bronchodilators (Salbutamol/Ipratropium), systemic corticosteroids (Hydrocortisone/Prednisolone), reversal agents (Naloxone for opioids, Flumazenil for BZD - caution with seizures).

### Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP) Protocol for Acute T2RF
* **Indications (mainly AECOPD):** Persistent acidosis (pH < 7.35 and PaCO2 > 6.5 kPa) despite maximal medical therapy for 1 hour.
* **Contraindications:** Facial trauma/burns, fixed upper airway obstruction, undrained pneumothorax, vomiting (aspiration risk), GCS < 8 (unless purely CO2 narcosis), hemodynamic instability.
* **Settings:** IPAP (assists ventilation/clears CO2), EPAP (keeps airways open/improves oxygenation).
* **Monitoring:** Repeat ABG 1 hour post-initiation. Success = ↑ pH, ↓ PaCO2.

### Invasive Mechanical Ventilation (Intubation) Criteria
* Failure of NIV (progressive acidosis, pH < 7.25, or rising PaCO2).
* Respiratory arrest or agonal breathing.
* Exhaustion, worsening GCS, or hemodynamic collapse.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT) Algorithm
* **Indications (Measured on 2 occasions, 3 weeks apart, when clinical stable):**
  * PaO2 < 7.3 kPa (55 mmHg) at rest.
  * PaO2 7.3 - 8.0 kPa with evidence of target organ damage: pulmonary hypertension, cor pulmonale (peripheral edema), or secondary polycythemia (Hematocrit > 55%).
* **Regimen:** Must be used for **> 15 hours/day** (including overnight) to confer survival benefit.
* **Contraindications:** Active smoking (fire hazard).

### Domiciliary Non-Invasive Ventilation (Home BiPAP)
* **Indications:** 
  * Chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure in NMD (e.g., ALS with weak sniff nasal inspiratory pressure).
  * Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS) failing CPAP.
  * Selected severe COPD patients with frequent hypercapnic exacerbations requiring acute NIV.
* **Benefit:** Reduces WOB at night, resets central chemoreceptor sensitivity, improves daytime PaCO2 and symptoms.

### Pulmonary Rehabilitation & Lifestyle Modifications
* **Smoking Cessation:** Single most effective intervention to prevent progression of COPD-related respiratory failure. Offer nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) or Varenicline (subject to HA formulary).
* **Vaccinations:** Annual Influenza vaccine, Pneumococcal vaccines (PCV13 / PPSV23) locally recommended by HK Centre for Health Protection (CHP).
* **Exercise:** Multidisciplinary pulmonary rehab (physiotherapy, energy conservation techniques) significantly ↓ hospitalizations.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### BODE Index (COPD Prognosis)
* **B**MI.
* **O**bstruction (FEV1 % predicted).
* **D**yspnea (mMRC scale).
* **E**xercise capacity (6-minute walk distance).
* **Utility:** Predicts 4-year survival in COPD patients better than FEV1 alone.

### DECAF Score (AECOPD Mortality)
* **D**yspnea (eMRCD score).
* **E**osinopenia (<0.05 x 10^9/L).
* **C**onsolidation (CXR).
* **A**cidemia (pH < 7.3).
* **F**ibrillation (Atrial fibrillation).
* **Utility:** Accurately predicts inpatient mortality for acute exacerbations of COPD.

### CURB-65 (Pneumonia Risk Stratification)
* **C**onfusion.
* **U**rea > 7 mmol/L.
* **R**espiratory Rate ≥ 30/min.
* **B**P < 90 mmHg systolic or ≤ 60 mmHg diastolic.
* **65** Age ≥ 65.
* **Utility:** 0-1 (Outpatient), 2 (Ward admission), 3-5 (Severe, consider ICU).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Chronic Respiratory Failure
* **Cardiovascular:** **Cor Pulmonale (肺心病)** (RV hypertrophy and failure secondary to chronic hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction). Arrhythmias (AF/MAT).
* **Hematological:** Secondary **Polycythemia (紅血球增多症)** (EPO release driven by chronic hypoxia) → hyperviscosity, ↑ thrombosis risk.
* **Neurological:** Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, chronic cognitive impairment, impaired sleep architecture.
* **Renal:** Fluid retention via activation of RAAS due to perceived systemic hypoxia and hypercapnia.

### Iatrogenic & Drug Adverse Effects
* **Oxygen Toxicity:** In T2RF, unchecked high FiO2 causes fatal CO2 narcosis. Prolonged 100% FiO2 can also cause absorptive atelectasis and free radical alveolar damage.
* **NIV Complications:** Nasal bridge pressure sores, gastric distension, claustrophobia, eye irritation.
* **Steroid Complications:** Hyperglycemia, osteoporosis, proximal myopathy (worsens respiratory muscle weakness).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the target oxygen saturation for a patient with known COPD presenting with an exacerbation? Answer: 88-92% pending an ABG, to avoid exacerbating potential Type 2 respiratory failure.
* Trap: Assuming a normal PaCO2 in a severely asthmatic patient is a reassuring sign. Pearl: A "normal" PaCO2 in an acute asthma attack is a red flag indicating impending respiratory failure; a tachypneic asthmatic should normally be hypocapnic (respiratory alkalosis).
* Pitfall: Prescribing high-flow oxygen via a non-rebreather mask to an undifferentiated drowsy patient with a barrel chest. You risk precipitating fatal hypercapnic coma in a chronic CO2 retainer.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate clinically between central and peripheral cyanosis? Answer: Central cyanosis involves the mucous membranes (tongue, inner lips) and indicates arterial hypoxemia. Peripheral cyanosis affects only extremities and indicates poor peripheral perfusion/stagnation.
* Trap: Waiting for clinical cyanosis to diagnose hypoxemia. Pearl: Cyanosis is a late and unreliable sign; it requires at least 5 g/dL of absolute deoxygenated hemoglobin, meaning severely anemic patients may never appear cyanotic despite lethal hypoxia.
* Pitfall: Initiating Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT) during an acute admission. LTOT must only be prescribed when the patient is clinically stable, on optimal medical therapy, with ABGs taken on two separate occasions at least 3 weeks apart.
