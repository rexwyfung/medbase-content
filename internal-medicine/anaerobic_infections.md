# Anaerobic Infections (厭氧菌感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Anaerobic Infections
* **Definition:** Infections caused by bacteria requiring ↓ oxygen tension for growth/survival. Typically endogenous, polymicrobial (mixed aerobic/anaerobic), and characterized by abscess formation and tissue necrosis.
* **Core Triad:** Putrid odor, abscess/cavitation, gas production in tissues.

### Major Anaerobic Pathogens by Site
| Morphology | Pathogen | Primary Niche | Classic Clinical Infection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gram-Negative Bacilli** | *Bacteroides fragilis* (脆弱擬桿菌) | Lower GI tract | Intra-abdominal abscess, peritonitis |
| **Gram-Negative Bacilli** | *Fusobacterium* spp. | Oropharynx | Lemierre's syndrome, dental abscess |
| **Gram-Positive Bacilli (Spore-forming)** | *Clostridium perfringens* | Soil, gut flora | Gas gangrene (氣性壞疽), food poisoning |
| **Gram-Positive Bacilli (Spore-forming)** | *Clostridium tetani / botulinum* | Soil | Tetanus (破傷風) / Botulism (肉毒桿菌中毒) |
| **Gram-Positive Bacilli (Non-spore)** | *Actinomyces israelii* (放線菌) | Oral cavity, GI tract | Cervicofacial "lumpy jaw", pelvic abscess (IUD) |
| **Gram-Positive Cocci** | *Peptostreptococcus* spp. | Oral, GI, GU tracts | Aspiration pneumonia (吸入性肺炎), bite wounds |

### Local Hong Kong Epidemiology
* **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Leading cause of HA medical admissions/mortality in elderly nursing home (OAH) residents with dysphagia/stroke.
* **Diabetic Foot Infections (糖尿病足部感染):** Extremely high prevalence in HK GOPC/SOPD; *B. fragilis* and *Peptostreptococcus* are universally present in deep, chronic ulcers.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Conditions Fostering Anaerobiosis
* **Vascular Compromise:** Ischemia (peripheral arterial disease, strangulated bowel) ↓ O2 delivery → ↓ redox potential (Eh).
* **Tissue Destruction:** Trauma, crush injuries, deep puncture wounds create avascular necrotic niches.
* **Aerobic Synergy:** Co-infecting aerobes (e.g., *E. coli*, *S. aureus*) consume local O2, generating an obligate anaerobic microenvironment.

### Mechanisms of Abscess Formation
* ***Bacteroides fragilis* Capsule:** Polysaccharide capsule induces profound T-cell-mediated inflammatory response → fibrin encapsulation → classic **abscess (膿腫)** formation.
* **Host Sequestration:** Body walls off anaerobic foci to prevent systemic bacteremia, paradoxically impairing antibiotic penetration.

### Pathogen-Specific Virulence Factors
* ***Clostridium perfringens*:** Produces Alpha-toxin (lecithinase) → degrades cell membranes → massive hemolysis, myonecrosis, and subcutaneous gas production.
* ***Fusobacterium necrophorum*:** Produces hemagglutinin/leukotoxin → promotes platelet aggregation → internal jugular vein thrombosis (Lemierre's syndrome).
* ***Actinomyces*:** Forms dense, branching mycelial-like tangles (Granulomas) → evades phagocytosis → forms chronic draining sinuses discharging **sulfur granules (硫磺顆粒)**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History & Predisposing Factors
* **Neurological:** Hx of CVA, Parkinson's, advanced dementia → dysphagia/choking → macro-aspiration.
* **Dental/ENT:** Poor dentition, recent tooth extraction, chronic sinusitis.
* **Gastrointestinal:** Recent colonic surgery, diverticulitis, appendicitis, perforated viscus.
* **Gynecological:** Prolonged retention of Intrauterine Device (IUD) (specific for *Actinomyces*).
* **Vascular/Metabolic:** Poorly controlled DM with peripheral neuropathy and PAD.

### General Physical Signs of Anaerobic Infection
* **Foul-smelling Discharge:** Putrid, highly offensive odor (due to short-chain fatty acids).
* **Tissue Crepitus (捻發音):** Crackling sensation on skin palpation (subcutaneous gas).
* **Necrosis:** Black eschar, gangrenous tissue, sloughing fascial planes.

### Site-Specific Anaerobic Signs
* **Pleuropulmonary:** Copious foul-smelling purulent sputum, absence of rigors (compared to pneumococcal pneumonia), clubbing (if chronic abscess).
* **Cervicofacial (*Actinomyces*):** "Woody" induration at the angle of the mandible, painless slow-growing mass, multiple discharging sinus tracts.
* **Intra-Abdominal:** Localized guarding/rigidity over discrete tender mass (abscess).
* **Diabetic Foot:** Deep "probing-to-bone" ulcer, malodorous exudate, loss of monofilament sensation, absent pedal pulses.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Gas-Producing Tissue Infections
| Condition | Distinguishing Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Clostridial Myonecrosis (Gas Gangrene)** | Rapid onset, severe pain disproportionate to signs, bullae, palpable crepitus, systemic toxicity. |
| **Synergistic Necrotizing Fasciitis** | Spreads along fascial planes, dishwater pus, often mixed aerobic/anaerobic (*Streptococcus* + *Bacteroides*). |
| **Aerobic Gas-Producing Infection** | *E. coli* or *Klebsiella* (especially in DM patients); less rapid myonecrosis than Clostridium. |

### Differentials for Cavitating Lung Lesions
* **Tuberculosis (肺結核):** Upper lobe predominance, apical, acid-fast bacilli +ve, weight loss, night sweats. No putrid sputum.
* **Squamous Cell Carcinoma:** Thick-walled irregular cavity, central necrosis, smoking Hx, absence of acute infective systemic signs.
* **Anaerobic Lung Abscess:** Dependent segments (Right lower lobe superior segment), foul sputum, fluid level on CXR.

### Differentials for Chronic Sinus Tracts
* **Actinomycosis:** Cervicofacial/pelvic, "woody" induration, yellow sulfur granules.
* **Cutaneous Tuberculosis (Scrofuloderma):** Overlying caseating lymph nodes (e.g., neck), purplish skin, thin discharge.
* **Hidradenitis Suppurativa:** Axillary/groin/perianal regions, associated with acne, obesity, smoking. No sulfur granules.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Microbiology & Culture Techniques (CRITICAL)
* **Specimen Collection:** **Swabs are USELESS** (surface colonization + oxygen exposure kills anaerobes). Must use needle aspiration of pus, tissue biopsy, or blood.
* **Transport:** Must be immediately placed in dedicated **Anaerobic Transport Media** (e.g., Robertson's cooked meat broth or anaerobic vials) to prevent oxygen toxicity.
* **Gram Stain:** Often reveals polymicrobial picture but fails to grow on standard aerobic culture ("sterile pus").
* **Macroscopic:** Identification of yellow "sulfur granules" in *Actinomyces* pus.

### Bloods and Biomarkers
* **CBC:** Severe leukocytosis with left shift (or leukopenia in overwhelming clostridial sepsis).
* **Hemolysis markers:** ↑ LDH, ↓ Haptoglobin, ↑ Bilirubin (Clostridial alpha-toxin hemolysis).
* **Septic Markers:** ↑↑ CRP and Procalcitonin.
* **Metabolic:** Deranged glycemic control in DM, ↑ Lactate (tissue ischemia/sepsis).

### Imaging for Anaerobic Infections
* **Plain Radiography (X-ray):** Locules of gas in soft tissues (gas gangrene), air-fluid levels in lungs (lung abscess/empyema).
* **CT Scan with Contrast (HA Gold Standard):** 
    * Enhancing rim with low-attenuation center (classic abscess).
    * Identifies fascial plane gas (necrotizing fasciitis).
    * Detects exact location of intra-abdominal collections for radiological drainage.
* **Ultrasound:** Useful bedside tool for superficial abscess mapping or guided aspiration.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Surgical Resuscitation & Source Control
* **Source Control (Source Control is King):** Antibiotics alone CANNOT penetrate an established abscess or necrotic tissue.
* **Immediate Debridement:** Emergent surgical excision of all necrotic tissue for necrotizing fasciitis/gas gangrene (amputation may be life-saving).
* **Radiological Drainage:** Urgent percutaneous CT/US-guided pigtail catheter insertion for intra-abdominal/pelvic abscesses.
* **Empyema Management:** Intercostal chest drain insertion; possible Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery (VATS) for loculated collections.

### Acute Empiric Antibiotic Protocols (HA Guidelines)
* **Intra-Abdominal / Diabetic Foot (Polymicrobial):**
    * **Co-amoxiclav (Amoxicillin/Clavulanate) (阿莫西林/克拉維酸)** 1.2g IV Q8H (covers gut aerobes + *Bacteroides*).
    * *Penicillin-allergic/Severe:* Cefuroxime IV + **Metronidazole (甲硝唑)** 500mg IV Q8H.
    * *Critically ill (ICU):* Piperacillin/Tazobactam (Tazocin) IV OR Meropenem IV.
* **Aspiration Pneumonia:**
    * Co-amoxiclav 1.2g IV Q8H (Gold standard in HA for nursing home acquired).
    * *Alternative:* Ampicillin/Sulbactam IV.
* **Clostridial Gas Gangrene:**
    * High-dose Benzylpenicillin IV + **Clindamycin** IV (Clindamycin specifically switches off bacterial toxin production).

### Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy (HBOT)
* **Mechanism:** Increases tissue dissolved oxygen → directly bactericidal to strict anaerobes (e.g., *Clostridium*), ↓ toxin production, promotes wound healing.
* **Indication:** Refractory Clostridial myonecrosis, refractory diabetic foot ulcers, chronic osteomyelitis.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Antibiotic Algorithms
* **Actinomycosis Eradication:**
    * Requires extraordinarily prolonged therapy due to dense fibrotic tissue.
    * *Initial:* High-dose IV Penicillin G for 2-6 weeks.
    * *Step-down:* Oral Amoxicillin for **6-12 months**.
* **Lung Abscess Resolution:**
    * Prolonged oral Co-amoxiclav or Clindamycin for 4-6 weeks (until CXR shows cavity resolution or stable small scar).
* **Diabetic Foot Osteomyelitis:**
    * 6-12 weeks of targeted oral therapy (often incorporating Fluoroquinolone + Metronidazole or Clindamycin) based on bone biopsy culture.

### Chronic Wound & Risk Management
* **Diabetic Foot Care:** Aggressive offloading (specialized footwear), strict glycemic control (HbA1c < 7%), regular podiatry debridement.
* **Aspiration Prevention:** Speech and Language Therapy (ST) assessment for swallowing. Texture-modified diet (thickened fluids, pureed diet). Prop up > 30 degrees during and 1 hour post-feeding. Good oral hygiene/chlorhexidine mouthwash to reduce bacterial load.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### LRINEC Score for Necrotizing Soft Tissue Infections
* **Purpose:** Distinguishes severe cellulitis from necrotizing fasciitis (often synergistic anaerobic/aerobic).
* **Variables:**
    * CRP (mg/L): >150 (4 pts)
    * WBC (x10^9/L): 15-25 (1 pt), >25 (2 pts)
    * Hemoglobin (g/dL): 11-13.5 (1 pt), <11 (2 pts)
    * Sodium (mmol/L): <135 (2 pts)
    * Creatinine (umol/L): >141 (2 pts)
    * Glucose (mmol/L): >10 (1 pt)
* **Interpretation:** Score ≥ 6 = High suspicion for necrotizing fasciitis (proceed to urgent surgical exploration).

### Prognostic Indicators in Anaerobic Infections
* **Poor Prognosis (High Mortality):** Delayed source control (>12 hrs), advanced age, profound hypoalbuminemia, pre-existing structural lung disease (in aspiration), septic shock on presentation.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ and Systemic Complications
* **Sepsis Syndrome:** Refractory vasoplegic shock, Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS), ARDS.
* **Local Tissue Loss:** Extensive scarring, permanent deformity (e.g., cervicofacial actinomycosis), need for limb amputation (gas gangrene/DFU).
* **Vascular:** Suppurative thrombophlebitis (Lemierre's syndrome → septic pulmonary emboli).
* **Metastatic Infection:** Brain abscess (complicating anaerobic lung abscess or dental infection).

### Antimicrobial Adverse Effects
* **Metronidazole (Flagyl) Toxicity:**
    * **Disulfiram-like Reaction:** Severe nausea/vomiting, flushing if alcohol is consumed.
    * **Neurotoxicity:** Peripheral neuropathy (prolonged use), cerebellar ataxia, encephalopathy.
    * **GI:** Metallic taste.
* **Clindamycin Toxicity:**
    * Highest risk antibiotic for precipitating *Clostridioides difficile* associated diarrhea (CDAD) / Pseudomembranous colitis.
* **Co-amoxiclav Toxicity:**
    * Cholestatic jaundice (clavulanate component, higher risk in elderly).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate an anaerobic lung abscess from pneumococcal pneumonia? Answer: Anaerobic infections present insidiously (weeks) with foul-smelling putrid sputum, lack of rigors, and often tissue cavitation/abscess on CXR, whereas pneumococcal is acute, lobar, with rusty sputum and rigors.
* Viva Question: Why is Clindamycin added to Penicillin in the treatment of Clostridial gas gangrene? Answer: Penicillin destroys the cell wall, but Clindamycin binds to the 50S ribosomal subunit, rapidly shutting down the synthesis of the lethal alpha-toxin.
* Viva Question: What is the classic microbiological description of the pus from Actinomycosis? Answer: Putrid pus containing firm, yellow macroscopic "sulfur granules" which are actually dense tangles of bacterial filaments.
* Trap: Prescribing Metronidazole as monotherapy for an intra-abdominal abscess. Pearl: Intra-abdominal infections are polymicrobial. Metronidazole covers strict anaerobes (Bacteroides) but completely misses facultative Gram-negatives (E. coli). Always pair it with an agent like Cefuroxime or Ceftriaxone, or use Co-amoxiclav instead.
* Trap: Sending a standard superficial cotton swab to the lab for anaerobic culture. Pearl: Anaerobes die rapidly upon oxygen exposure. A superficial swab will only grow colonizing aerobes. You must aspirate deep pus with a syringe and inject it into an anaerobic transport vial, or send tissue biopsies.
* Pitfall: Treating Actinomycosis with standard 7-14 day antibiotic courses. You will face inevitably high relapse rates. Actinomycosis requires extremely prolonged therapy (high dose IV followed by 6-12 months of oral Penicillin) due to its dense, impenetrable granulomatous structure.
* Pitfall: Relying solely on IV antibiotics to cure a localized anaerobic infection. Antibiotics cannot penetrate an established abscess cavity or necrotic tissue. Source control (surgical incision, debridement, or percutaneous drainage) is the absolute mandatory first step.
