# Headache (頭痛)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Classification of Headache Syndromes
* **Primary Headaches (原發性頭痛):** No underlying structural/systemic pathology. Includes **Migraine (偏頭痛)**, **Tension-Type Headache (緊張性頭痛)**, **Cluster Headache (叢發性頭痛)**.
* **Secondary Headaches (繼發性頭痛):** Symptom of exogenous disorder. Includes **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH) (蛛網膜下腔出血)**, **Meningitis (腦膜炎)**, **Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA) (巨細胞動脈炎)**, **Intracranial Hypertension/Hypotension**, **Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH) (藥物過度使用性頭痛)**.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Tension-Type Headache:** Most common primary headache (lifetime prevalence ~80%).
* **Migraine:** Prevalent in ~12.5% of HK adults; ♀:♂ = 3:1. Peaks in 30s-40s.
* **Cluster Headache:** Rare (<0.1%); ♂:♀ = 3:1.
* **MOH:** High local burden due to over-the-counter NSAID/paracetamol abuse in HK population.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Migraine (偏頭痛)
* **Cortical Spreading Depression (CSD):** Wave of neuronal/glial depolarization spreading across cerebral cortex → triggers aura.
* **Trigeminovascular System Activation:** CSD stimulates trigeminal afferents (CN V) innervating meningeal blood vessels.
* **Neuropeptide Release:** Release of **Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP)**, **Substance P**, **Neurokinin A** → neurogenic inflammation, vasodilation, plasma protein extravasation.
* **Central Sensitization:** Sustained nociceptive input sensitizes 2nd/3rd-order neurons in brainstem/thalamus → allodynia (pain from non-noxious stimuli).

### Pathophysiology of Tension-Type Headache (緊張性頭痛)
* **Peripheral Sensitization:** Myofascial nociceptor sensitization in pericranial muscles (↑ muscle tenderness).
* **Central Sensitization:** Downregulation of descending inhibitory pain pathways (supraspinal mechanism) in chronic TTH.

### Pathophysiology of Cluster Headache (叢發性頭痛)
* **Hypothalamic Activation:** Posterior hypothalamic pacing abnormality → explains circadian/circannual rhythmicity.
* **Trigeminal Autonomic Reflex:** Parasympathetic activation via sphenopalatine ganglion → lacrimation, rhinorrhea, miosis/ptosis (partial Horner's).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### "SNOOP10" Red Flags for Secondary Headaches
* **S**ystemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, night sweats).
* **N**eoplasia history.
* **N**eurologic deficit or dysfunction (confusion, altered LOC, focal signs).
* **O**nset sudden/abrupt (**Thunderclap headache** → SAH).
* **O**lder age (onset >50 years → GCA, malignancy).
* **P**attern change (new/different, progressive).
* **P**ositional (worse upright → CSF leak; worse supine/coughing → ↑ ICP).
* **P**recipitated by Valsalva/exertion.
* **P**apilledema.
* **P**regnancy/puerperium (cortical vein thrombosis, pre-eclampsia).

### History Features of Primary Headaches
* **Migraine:** Unilateral, throbbing/pulsatile, mod-severe intensity, aggravated by routine physical activity. +ve photophobia, phonophobia, nausea/vomiting. Aura (visual fortification spectra, scotoma, sensory). Patient prefers to lie still in dark room.
* **Tension-Type:** Bilateral, band-like/pressing, mild-mod intensity. NO nausea/vomiting. Not aggravated by routine activity.
* **Cluster:** Strictly unilateral (periorbital/temporal), excruciating/stabbing pain. Lasts 15-180 mins. Occurs in clusters (same time daily). Patient is restless/agitated (pacing). Associated ipsilateral autonomic features.
* **Trigeminal Neuralgia (三叉神經痛):** Unilateral, electric shock-like, split-second duration, triggered by light touch/chewing/wind (V2/V3 distribution).

### Specific Physical Examination for Headache
* **Vitals:** Blood pressure (hypertensive crisis), Temperature (meningitis).
* **Fundoscopy:** Check for **papilledema** (blurring of optic disc margins, venous engorgement, flame hemorrhages) → ↑ ICP.
* **Cranial Nerves:** Visual fields (pituitary tumor), CN III/IV/VI palsies (aneurysm compression, cavernous sinus thrombosis). Partial Horner's (Cluster).
* **Temporal Arteries:** Palpate for tenderness, nodularity, absent pulse (GCA).
* **Meningeal Signs:** Nuchal rigidity, Kernig's sign, Brudzinski's sign.
* **Pericranial Exam:** Palpate cervical/masticatory muscles for myofascial trigger points (TTH).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Primary vs Secondary Headache Discriminators

| Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Migraine** | Pulsatile, unilateral, 4-72h, +nausea/photophobia/aura, prefers rest. |
| **Tension-Type** | Band-like, bilateral, pericranial tenderness, no nausea, lacks severe features. |
| **Cluster** | Unilateral orbital, 15-180m, ipsilateral autonomic signs, pacing/restless. |
| **SAH** | Thunderclap onset (max intensity <1 min), "worst headache of life", +meningism. |
| **GCA** | Age >50, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, visual symptoms, prominent temporal artery. |
| **Meningitis** | Fever, neck stiffness, photophobia, altered mental status, non-blanching rash (meningococcal). |
| **Space-Occupying Lesion** | Progressive, worse in morning/supine/Valsalva, focal neurology, papilledema. |
| **Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH)** | Young obese ♀, visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus, papilledema. |
| **Medication-Overuse** | Headache ≥15 days/month; regular overuse of abortive drugs for >3 months. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Diagnostic Criteria for Migraine (ICHD-3)

| Criterion | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | ≥5 attacks fulfilling B-D. |
| **B** | Attack lasts 4-72 hours (untreated or unsuccessfully treated). |
| **C** | ≥2 of: 1. Unilateral; 2. Pulsating; 3. Mod/severe; 4. Aggravated by routine physical activity. |
| **D** | ≥1 of: 1. Nausea and/or vomiting; 2. Photophobia AND phonophobia. |
| **E** | Not better accounted for by another ICHD-3 diagnosis. |

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **Bedside:** BP monitoring, ECG (baseline before commencing Triptans/TCA/Verapamil).
* **Bloods:**
  * **ESR/CRP:** MUST check in all patients >50 with new headache to rule out GCA.
  * **CBC & Blood cultures:** If meningitis/encephalitis suspected.
  * **TFTs:** Exclude secondary causes (e.g., hypothyroidism causing daily headache).

### Imaging & Invasive Investigations
* **CT Brain (Non-Contrast):** 1st line acute imaging for SAH (within 6h), trauma, space-occupying lesion, suspected intracranial bleed.
* **CT Venogram (CTV):** If cerebral venous sinus thrombosis suspected (hypercoagulable state, postpartum).
* **MRI Brain with Gadolinium:** Superior for posterior fossa lesions, Arnold-Chiari malformation, subtle white matter changes, pituitary lesions, spontaneous intracranial hypotension (pachymeningeal enhancement).
* **Lumbar Puncture (LP) & CSF Analysis:**
  * **Indications:** Suspected SAH (CT -ve; check for xanthochromia at 12h), meningitis (Gram stain, culture, viral PCR), IIH (measure opening pressure >25cmH2O).
  * **Contraindication:** NEVER perform if focal neurological signs or midline shift on CT (risk of coning).
* **Temporal Artery Biopsy:** Gold standard for GCA (look for multinucleated giant cells, skip lesions). Do not delay steroids for biopsy.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Abortive Therapy for Migraine (偏頭痛急性治療)
* **Step 1 (Mild-Mod):** Simple analgesia (NSAIDs e.g., Naproxen 500mg or Aspirin 900mg) + Antiemetic (Metoclopramide 10mg or Domperidone 10mg to ↑ gastric stasis/absorption).
* **Step 2 (Severe/Refractory):** **Triptans (5-HT1B/1D receptor agonists)** (e.g., Sumatriptan 50-100mg PO or Rizatriptan 10mg). *Contraindications:* IHD, previous CVA, uncontrolled HTN, variant (Prinzmetal) angina, hemiplegic migraine.
* **Status Migrainosus (>72h):** IV fluids, IV Ketorolac, IV Prochlorperazine/Metoclopramide. (Avoid opioids/pethidine).

### Acute Abortive Therapy for Cluster Headache (叢發性頭痛急性治療)
* **1st Line:** **100% High-Flow Oxygen** (12-15 L/min via non-rebreather mask for 15-20 mins).
* **Adjunct:** Subcutaneous (SC) Sumatriptan 6mg or Intranasal Zolmitriptan (PO absorbs too slowly).

### Acute Resuscitation for Secondary Emergencies
* **SAH:** Elevate head 30°, BP control (Nimodipine 60mg Q4H to prevent vasospasm), urgent neurosurgical referral (coiling/clipping).
* **GCA:** High-dose systemic steroids IMMEDIATELY (Prednisolone 40-60mg PO daily; IV Methylprednisolone 1g daily x 3 days if visual loss present).
* **Bacterial Meningitis:** IV Ceftriaxone 2g Q12H + IV Dexamethasone 10mg Q6H (give before/with 1st antibiotic dose) ± IV Ampicillin 2g Q4H (if Listeria risk: >50yo, immunocompromised).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Migraine Prophylaxis Algorithm (偏頭痛預防)
* *Indication:* ≥4 headache days/month, severe disability, or acute therapies ineffective/contraindicated.

| Tier | Pharmacological Options (HK HA Formulary) | Key Considerations / Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Line** | **Beta-blockers** (Propranolol 40-80mg BD) | Avoid in asthma, bradycardia, peripheral vascular disease. |
| **1st Line** | **Tricyclic Antidepressants** (Amitriptyline 10-25mg ON) | Good for co-existing TTH/insomnia. Avoid in glaucoma, arrhythmias. |
| **2nd Line** | **Anticonvulsants** (Topiramate 25-50mg BD) | Teratogenic, weight loss, paresthesia, cognitive dulling, renal stones. |
| **2nd Line** | **Valproate** (Sodium Valproate 400-600mg BD) | STRICTLY AVOID in women of childbearing age (teratogenic, PCOS). |
| **3rd Line** | **Flunarizine** (Calcium channel blocker) | Weight gain, depression, extrapyramidal symptoms (Parkinsonism). |
| **Novel / Advanced**| **CGRP Monoclonal Antibodies** (Erenumab, Fremanezumab) | SC monthly injections. Specialist prescribed, highly effective. Constipation. |
| **Chronic Migraine**| **OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox)** injections | Indicated for chronic migraine (≥15 days/month for >3 months). |

### Chronic Management of Other Primary Headaches
* **Tension-Type Prophylaxis:** Amitriptyline 10-25mg ON is 1st line.
* **Cluster Prophylaxis:** **Verapamil** (High dose: 240-480mg/day). MUST do baseline & serial ECGs (monitor for PR prolongation/heart block). Transitional bridge: Short course oral Prednisolone or greater occipital nerve (GON) block.

### Lifestyle & Non-Pharmacological Modifications
* **Trigger Avoidance:** Regulate sleep (avoid lack of sleep AND oversleeping), manage stress, hydrate. Dietary triggers (cheese/tyramine, chocolate, MSG, alcohol/red wine, caffeine withdrawal) only relevant if clearly linked in patient's headache diary.
* **MOH Prevention:** Restrict Triptans/NSAIDs to <10 days/month; Paracetamol to <15 days/month. Abrupt withdrawal is required for treatment (bridge with Naproxen or steroids).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Migraine Disability Assessment (MIDAS) Score
* 5-item questionnaire measuring lost days due to headache in past 3 months (work/school, household work, non-work activities).
* **Scoring:**
  * 0-5: Grade I (Little/no disability).
  * 6-10: Grade II (Mild disability).
  * 11-20: Grade III (Moderate disability) → Consider prophylaxis.
  * >21: Grade IV (Severe disability) → Strong indication for prophylaxis.

### Prognosis
* **Migraine:** Often improves post-menopause in females; varies widely. High risk of evolving into chronic migraine if analgesics overused.
* **Cluster:** Tends to recur periodically; lifelong remission is rare but cluster periods may space out with age.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Headache Syndromes
* **Status Migrainosus:** Debilitating migraine lasting >72h → severe dehydration due to intractable vomiting.
* **Migrainous Infarction:** Stroke occurring during a typical migraine with aura attack (rare). Migraine with aura ↑ risk of ischemic stroke (esp. in young ♀ on combined oral contraceptives).
* **GCA Complication:** Permanent anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (AION) → irreversible blindness. Aortic aneurysm/dissection (late complication).

### Adverse Effects of Headache Pharmacotherapy
* **Triptans:** Coronary vasospasm (Triptan sensation: chest/neck tightness), serotonin syndrome (if combined with SSRI/SNRI - theoretical but tested).
* **Topiramate:** Acute angle-closure glaucoma, urolithiasis, metabolic acidosis (carbonic anhydrase inhibition).
* **Methysergide (Historical/Rarely used):** Retroperitoneal, pleural, and cardiac valvular fibrosis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important first question to ask a patient presenting with a new onset severe headache? Answer: Was the onset sudden (thunderclap) or gradual? (To rule out SAH).
* Trap: Prescribing combined oral contraceptives (COCP) to a female patient who has migraine WITH aura. Pearl: COCP is absolutely contraindicated in migraine with aura due to exponentially increased risk of ischemic stroke. Use progestin-only or non-hormonal methods.
* Pitfall: Waiting for temporal artery biopsy results before starting steroids in suspected Giant Cell Arteritis. Start high-dose steroids immediately to prevent irreversible vision loss; biopsy remains positive for 1-2 weeks after starting steroids.
* Viva Question: A patient presents with daily morning headaches, papilledema, and a BMI of 35. CT brain is normal. What is the next diagnostic step? Answer: Lumbar puncture with opening pressure measurement to diagnose Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH).
* Trap: Treating a patient who has daily headaches and takes Panadol/Ibuprofen every single day with escalating doses of acute analgesics. Pearl: This is Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH). You must stop the offending acute analgesics completely and start a prophylactic agent.
* Pitfall: Giving 100% oxygen to a COPD patient for cluster headache without caution. Although 100% O2 is 1st line for cluster, in severe CO2 retainers, it may suppress respiratory drive. Ensure appropriate monitoring.
* Viva Question: What ECG finding MUST be monitored closely when starting a patient on Verapamil for Cluster Headache prophylaxis? Answer: PR interval prolongation (1st degree AV block or worse).
