# Renal Replacement Therapy (腎臟替代治療)

## 1. Indications for Renal Replacement Therapy
### Acute Dialysis Indications (AEIOU Criteria)
* **A**cidemia: Severe metabolic acidosis (pH <7.1) refractory to medical Rx (NaHCO3)
* **E**lectrolyte abnormalities: Severe/symptomatic hyperkalemia (K+ >6.5 mmol/L) with ECG changes, refractory to medical Rx
* **I**ntoxication: Dialyzable toxins (SLIME: **S**alicylates, **L**ithium, **I**sopropanol, **M**ethanol, **E**thylene glycol)
* **O**verload: Refractory pulmonary edema (肺水腫) unresponsive to high-dose IV diuretics
* **U**remia: Uremic encephalopathy (尿毒症性腦病), uremic pericarditis (尿毒性心包炎), uremic bleeding/neuropathy

### Chronic Dialysis Indications (End-Stage Renal Disease - 末期腎衰竭)
* **eGFR Threshold**: Usually initiated when eGFR <10-15 mL/min/1.73m² WITH uremic symptoms, or absolutely when eGFR <5-6 mL/min/1.73m² even if asymptomatic
* **Clinical Triggers**: Persistent malnutrition, medically refractory fluid overload, refractory hyperkalemia/hyperphosphatemia
* **HK HA Policy**: "PD-First" policy (腹膜透析優先政策); PD is the default initial modality for ESRD unless contraindicated (e.g., severe abdominal adhesions, loss of peritoneal function)

## 2. Hemodialysis (血液透析)
### Principles of Hemodialysis
* **Diffusion**: Solute clearance down concentration gradient (removes urea, creatinine, K+)
* **Ultrafiltration**: Fluid removal via hydrostatic pressure gradient (transmembrane pressure)
* **Frequency**: Typically 3 sessions/week, 4 hours/session (in-center)

### Vascular Access for Hemodialysis
* **Arteriovenous Fistula (AVF - 動靜脈瘻管)**: Gold standard; lowest infection/thrombosis risk. Needs 6-8 weeks to mature. Examine for thrill (觸覺震顫) and bruit (血管雜音)
* **Arteriovenous Graft (AVG)**: Synthetic PTFE tube; used if native vessels inadequate. Higher stenosis/infection risk than AVF
* **Central Venous Catheter (CVC - 中心靜脈導管)**: Internal jugular or femoral. Temporary (non-tunneled) for acute RRT or permanent (tunneled, e.g., Permcath) as bridge to AVF/AVG. Highest risk of CRBSI (catheter-related bloodstream infection)

## 3. Peritoneal Dialysis (腹膜透析)
### Principles of Peritoneal Dialysis
* **Membrane**: Uses patient's peritoneum as semi-permeable membrane
* **Osmosis**: Fluid removal driven by osmotic gradient (dextrose or icodextrin concentration in dialysate)
* **Diffusion**: Solute removal via concentration gradient between mesenteric capillaries and dialysate

### Modalities of Peritoneal Dialysis
* **Continuous Ambulatory Peritoneal Dialysis (CAPD)**: Manual exchanges 3-4 times/day. Dwells for 4-6 hrs (day) and 8-10 hrs (overnight)
* **Automated Peritoneal Dialysis (APD)**: Machine-assisted cyclical exchanges overnight (8-10 hrs). Daytime empty or single long dwell

### Dialysate Solutions in PD
* **Dextrose (葡萄糖)**: Standard osmotic agent (1.5%, 2.5%, 4.25%). Higher % → ↑ ultrafiltration (UF). Side effects: Hyperglycemia, weight gain, peritoneal membrane degradation over time
* **Icodextrin (愛考糊精)**: Glucose polymer for long dwells (overnight in CAPD, daytime in APD). Iso-osmolar, fluid removal via colloid osmosis. Avoids hyperglycemia

## 4. Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT - 連續性腎臟替代治療)
### Indications for CRRT
* **Hemodynamic Instability**: Primary indication over intermittent HD in ICU settings (e.g., septic shock requiring high-dose vasopressors)
* **Severe Brain Injury**: Prevents rapid fluid shifts and intracranial pressure spikes (avoids dialysis disequilibrium syndrome)

### CRRT Modalities
* **CVVH (Continuous Venovenous Hemofiltration)**: Convection-based clearance (solvent drag). Needs replacement fluid
* **CVVHD (Continuous Venovenous Hemodialysis)**: Diffusion-based clearance using countercurrent dialysate
* **CVVHDF (Continuous Venovenous Hemodiafiltration)**: Combines both convection and diffusion for maximal clearance

## 5. Renal Transplantation (腎移植)
### Pre-Transplant Evaluation
* **Donor Types**: Living Related, Living Non-Related, Deceased (Cadaveric - waitlist often >5-10 years in HK)
* **Crossmatching**: ABO compatibility, HLA matching (A, B, DR loci), Cytotoxic crossmatch (must be -ve)
* **Contraindications**: Active malignancy (requires clearance period), active systemic infection, severe irreversible extra-renal disease (e.g., severe heart failure, liver cirrhosis), active substance abuse

### Post-Transplant Immunosuppression (HK Protocol)
* **Induction**: Basiliximab (IL-2 receptor antagonist) or Anti-Thymocyte Globulin (ATG) + IV Methylprednisolone
* **Maintenance (Triple Therapy)**:
  * **Calcineurin Inhibitor (CNI)**: Tacrolimus (FK506) > Cyclosporine. Side effects: Nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, new-onset diabetes after transplant (NODAT)
  * **Anti-metabolite**: Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF). Side effects: GI upset, bone marrow suppression
  * **Corticosteroid**: Low-dose Prednisolone (漸減劑量)

## 6. Complications of Dialysis Modalities
### Hemodialysis Complications
* **Intradialytic Hypotension**: Most common. Rx: Pause UF, give Trendelenburg position, IV fluid bolus (NS or albumin)
* **Dialysis Disequilibrium Syndrome**: Rapid urea removal → reverse osmotic gradient → cerebral edema. Symptoms: Headache, vomiting, seizures. Prevention: Gentle, short initial dialysis sessions
* **Vascular Access Complications**: Thrombosis, stenosis, aneurysm, Steal Syndrome (distal hand ischemia due to blood diversion)

### Peritoneal Dialysis Complications
* **PD Peritonitis (腹膜炎)**: Symptoms: Cloudy effluent (abdominal pain/fever). Dx: Effluent WBC >100/µL with >50% polymorphonuclear cells (PMNs). Rx: Intraperitoneal (IP) antibiotics (e.g., Cefazolin + Ceftazidime)
* **Exit-Site/Tunnel Infection**: Erythema/purulence at catheter exit. Rx: Topical Mupirocin/oral antibiotics; may require catheter removal if refractory
* **Sclerosing Encapsulating Peritonitis (SEP)**: Rare, fatal complication of long-term PD. Thickened fibrotic peritoneum causing bowel obstruction
* **Mechanical Complications**: Abdominal wall hernia (due to ↑ intra-abdominal pressure), hydrothorax (diaphragmatic leak, usually right-sided)

## 7. Comparative Modality Selection
### HD vs PD Selection Criteria
| Feature | Hemodialysis (HD) | Peritoneal Dialysis (PD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hemodynamics** | Rapid fluid shifts; poor tolerance if low LVEF | Slow, continuous UF; better hemodynamic stability |
| **Residual Renal Function** | Declines rapidly | Preserved longer (crucial for survival) |
| **Diet/Fluid Restriction** | Strict restriction (fluid, K+, PO4-) | More liberal diet (continuous clearance) |
| **Lifestyle/Support** | Requires hospital/center visits (staff-assisted) | Home-based (requires patient/carer motivation & hygiene) |
| **Contraindications** | Poor vascular access, severe heart failure | Severe adhesions, uncorrectable hernia, ostomies, poor hygiene |

## 8. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the defining laboratory criterion for diagnosing PD peritonitis? Answer: PD effluent fluid WBC >100/µL with >50% neutrophils (PMNs), regardless of culture results.
* Viva Question: Why do we prefer Tacrolimus over Cyclosporine for maintenance immunosuppression in kidney transplants? Answer: Tacrolimus has a lower rejection rate and better cardiovascular/lipid profile, though it has a higher risk of post-transplant diabetes (NODAT).
* Trap: Assuming a patient with an AVF complaining of a cold, painful hand needs a rheumatology workup. Pearl: Always suspect AVF Steal Syndrome first; perform a physical exam checking radial pulses with the fistula manually compressed (occlusion test).
* Trap: Giving standard doses of insulin to a patient newly started on RRT. Pearl: Insulin clearance is significantly reduced in advanced CKD and fluctuates with RRT; closely monitor capillary blood glucose to avoid severe hypoglycemia.
* Pitfall: Missing a diaphragmatic leak in a PD patient presenting with shortness of breath. Always check for unilateral (usually right-sided) pleural effusion on CXR, which occurs when PD fluid crosses microscopic diaphragmatic defects.
* Pitfall: Aggressively dialyzing a severely uremic patient at maximum settings on their very first session. This rapidly drops serum osmolality, causing water to shift into brain cells, leading to potentially fatal Dialysis Disequilibrium Syndrome; always start with short, low-efficiency sessions.
