# Dementia (認知障礙症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Major Neurocognitive Disorder (Dementia)
* **Core Definition**: Acquired, progressive decline in ≥1 cognitive domains (complex attention, executive function, learning/memory, language, perceptual-motor, social cognition).
* **Functional Impairment**: Deficits must be severe enough to interfere with independence in everyday activities [Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs) or Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)].
* **Exclusion**: Deficits do not occur exclusively in the context of Delirium (譫妄) and are not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., Depression/Schizophrenia).

### Primary Etiologies of Dementia
* **Alzheimer's Disease (AD) (阿茲海默症)**: Most common (~60-70%); insidious onset, prominent early short-term memory loss.
* **Vascular Dementia (VaD) (血管性認知障礙症)**: ~15-20%; step-wise decline, temporally related to cerebrovascular events.
* **Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB) (路易氏體認知障礙症)**: ~10%; fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, parkinsonism.
* **Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD) (額顳葉認知障礙症)**: Early onset (<65 years); prominent behavioral/personality changes or language deficits (Primary Progressive Aphasia).
* **Mixed Dementia (混合型認知障礙症)**: Very common in elderly (usually AD + VaD).

### Hong Kong Local Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: ~10% in HK adults ≥70 years old; ~30% in ≥85 years old.
* **Demographic Trend**: Rapidly ↑ burden due to HK's aging population (highest life expectancy globally).
* **Subtype Distribution in HK**: High incidence of VaD and Mixed Dementia locally due to high prevalence of metabolic syndrome and stroke (中風).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Alzheimer's Disease (AD)
* **Extracellular Accumulation**: Cleavage of Amyloid Precursor Protein (APP) → insoluble **Amyloid-beta (Aβ) plaques**.
* **Intracellular Accumulation**: Hyperphosphorylation of structural tau proteins → **Neurofibrillary Tangles (NFTs)**.
* **Neurochemical Deficit**: Profound loss of cholinergic neurons in the basal nucleus of Meynert → ↓ Acetylcholine (ACh).
* **Atrophy Pattern**: Begins in medial temporal lobe/hippocampus (海馬體) → progressive global cortical atrophy.

### Pathophysiology of Vascular Dementia (VaD)
* **Macrovascular**: Large vessel thromboembolism → multi-infarct dementia (cortical/subcortical).
* **Microvascular**: Small vessel disease (lipohyalinosis) → chronic subcortical ischemia → leukoaraiosis (white matter changes) & lacunar infarcts.
* **Strategic Infarcts**: Single infarct in cognitive hub (e.g., thalamus, angular gyrus) → sudden profound dementia.

### Pathophysiology of Lewy Body Dementia (DLB)
* **Intracellular Inclusions**: Accumulation of misfolded **alpha-synuclein** protein → Lewy bodies.
* **Distribution**: Distributed diffusely throughout the cerebral cortex and brainstem (substantia nigra).
* **Neurochemical Deficit**: Depletion of both dopamine (causing parkinsonism) and acetylcholine (causing cognitive/visual symptoms).

### Pathophysiology of Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD)
* **Proteinopathies**: Accumulation of abnormal Tau (Pick bodies) or TDP-43 proteins.
* **Anatomical Atrophy**: Focal, asymmetrical degeneration of frontal and anterior temporal lobes.
* **Genetic Link**: High familial inheritance pattern (C9orf72, MAPT, GRN mutations).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Core Cognitive & Functional History (Collateral Required)
* **Memory Deficits**: Repeated questions, misplacing items, forgetting recent conversations (prominent in AD).
* **Executive Dysfunction**: Poor financial management, inability to plan meals, poor judgement.
* **Language Deficits**: Word-finding difficulty (anomia), semantic errors.
* **Visuospatial Deficits**: Getting lost in familiar neighborhoods, difficulty navigating home.
* **Functional Status (IADLs → ADLs)**:
  * **IADLs**: Managing finances, medications, transport, telephone, shopping.
  * **ADLs**: Bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, feeding.

### Subtype-Specific Historical Features
* **AD Profile**: Gradual, insidious onset; isolated short-term memory loss initially.
* **VaD Profile**: Step-wise deterioration; history of TIA/stroke, hypertension, diabetes, smoking.
* **DLB Profile**: 
  * **Fluctuations**: Episodes of staring into space, daytime drowsiness.
  * **Hallucinations**: Well-formed, detailed visual hallucinations (e.g., seeing small children/animals).
  * **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD)**: Acting out dreams, thrashing in sleep (often precedes dementia by years).
* **FTD Profile**: Apathy, disinhibition, socially inappropriate behavior, hyperorality (sweet craving), loss of empathy.

### Behavioral and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia (BPSD) History
* **Psychotic**: Delusions (often theft/infidelity), hallucinations.
* **Affective**: Depression, anxiety, apathy.
* **Hyperactive**: Agitation, pacing, wandering (遊走), physical/verbal aggression, sundowning (日落症候群 - worsening in late afternoon/evening).

### Focused Physical Examination Findings
* **Neurological Signs in VaD**: Focal signs (hemiparesis, visual field defects, extensor plantar reflex, pseudobulbar palsy).
* **Extrapyramidal Signs in DLB**: Bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity, masked facies, shuffling gait (Parkinsonism).
* **Frontal Release Signs (Late-stage or FTD)**: Grasp, snout, root, palmomental reflexes.
* **Gait Assessment**:
  * **Apraxic/Magnetic Gait**: Suggests Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH) or severe VaD.
  * **Festinating Gait**: Suggests DLB.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Dementia vs Normal Aging vs Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI)
| Feature | Normal Aging | Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) | Dementia |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cognitive Decline** | Mild subjective | Objective evidence of decline | Objective & significant decline |
| **ADLs/IADLs** | Intact | Intact (may use compensations) | **Impaired** (needs assistance) |
| **Progression** | Static | ~10-15% convert to dementia/year | Progressive deterioration |

### Dementia vs Pseudodementia (Depressive Episode)
| Feature | Dementia | Pseudodementia (Depression) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Insidious, slow | Rapid, precise onset |
| **Patient Attitude** | Unaware, conceals deficits | Distressed, highlights deficits |
| **Testing Effort** | Tries hard, guesses ("near misses") | Frequent "I don't know" answers |
| **Symptom Course** | Mood changes follow cognitive loss | Mood changes precede cognitive loss |

### Reversible Causes of Cognitive Impairment (Rule Out)
* **Endocrine**: Hypothyroidism (甲狀腺功能低下).
* **Nutritional**: Vitamin B12 deficiency, Thiamine (B1) deficiency, Folate deficiency.
* **Infectious**: Neurosyphilis, HIV Encephalopathy.
* **Structural**: Subdural Hematoma (SDH), Intracranial space-occupying lesion.
* **Fluid Dynamics**: Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH) - Triad of cognitive impairment, urinary incontinence, gait apraxia.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5 for Major Neurocognitive Disorder)
| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A. Cognitive Decline** | Significant decline from previous baseline in ≥1 cognitive domains. |
| **B. Objective Evidence** | Based on standardized neuropsychological testing (or quantified clinical assessment). |
| **C. Functional Interference** | Deficits interfere with independence in everyday activities (IADLs). |
| **D. Exclusion of Delirium** | Do not occur exclusively in the context of a delirium. |
| **E. No Alternative Axis I** | Not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., Major Depressive Disorder). |

### Bedside Tests & Continuous Monitoring
* **ECG**: Crucial before initiating Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors (AChEI) (check for bradycardia, heart block).
* **Bladder Scan**: Rule out urinary retention causing delirium/agitation.
* **Postural BP**: Assess for dysautonomia (common in DLB) or over-medication.

### Routine Bloods for Reversible Causes (HK "Dementia Screen")
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Exclude anemia, macrocytosis (B12/Folate), infection.
* **Renal/Liver Function (RFT/LFT)**: Exclude uremic/hepatic encephalopathy, establish baseline for drug dosing.
* **Thyroid Function Test (TFT)**: Exclude hypothyroidism.
* **Vitamin B12 & Folate**: Exclude nutritional deficiencies.
* **Syphilis Serology (VDRL/TPHA)**: Rule out neurosyphilis.
* **Bone Profile (Calcium)**: Exclude hypercalcemia.
* **HbA1c & Fasting Lipids**: Assess vascular risk factors (for VaD).

### Neuroimaging Protocols
* **Non-Contrast CT Brain**: 1st line in HK HA. Identifies structural lesions (tumors, SDH, NPH), overt infarcts, and patterns of cortical atrophy.
* **MRI Brain**: Higher sensitivity.
  * **T1 Coronal**: Mesial temporal lobe/hippocampal atrophy (strongly supports AD).
  * **T2/FLAIR**: Quantifies white matter hyperintensities/leukoaraiosis and microinfarcts (VaD).
* **PET/SPECT (Specialist Only)**: 
  * **FDG-PET**: Temporoparietal hypometabolism (AD); Occipital hypometabolism (DLB).
  * **DaTscan (Ioflupane I-123)**: ↓ Dopamine transporter uptake in striatum confirms DLB (differentiates from AD).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Severe BPSD (Agitation/Aggression Crisis)
* **Goal**: Immediate safety of patient, staff, and co-patients. Rule out superimposed delirium (pain, infection, constipation, retention).
* **De-escalation (Non-Pharm First)**:
  * **Environment**: Quiet, well-lit room, remove noxious stimuli/weapons.
  * **Approach**: Calm voice, avoid arguing/correcting delusions, distraction techniques.
* **Acute Pharmacological Restraint (Only if risk of imminent harm)**:
  * **First-line (Oral)**: **Haloperidol** 0.5mg PO or **Quetiapine** 12.5-25mg PO (preferred in Parkinsonism/DLB).
  * **Second-line (IM)**: **Haloperidol** 0.5-1mg IM or **Lorazepam** 0.5-1mg IM (Use benzodiazepines with extreme caution → risk of paradoxical agitation and falls).
  * **CRITICAL CONTRAINDICATION**: NEVER use typical antipsychotics (e.g., Haloperidol) in DLB/Parkinson's Disease Dementia → triggers life-threatening irreversible parkinsonian crisis / Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Step 1: Non-Pharmacological Management & Carer Support
* **Cognitive Training**: Reality orientation, reminiscence therapy.
* **Environmental Modification**: Clear signage, anti-slip mats, adequate lighting to prevent sundowning, removing trip hazards.
* **HK Community Support Resources**:
  * **CGAT (Community Geriatric Assessment Team)**: Outreach to Residential Care Homes for the Elderly (RCHE).
  * **DECC (District Elderly Community Centre)**: Day care, carer support groups.
  * **NGOs**: Jockey Club Centre for Positive Ageing (JCCPA).

### Step 2: Pharmacological Algorithm for Cognitive Decline (HK HA Formulary)
* **Indication**: Mild to Moderate AD, DLB, Mixed Dementia. (Ineffective for pure VaD; contraindicated in FTD).
* **First Line: Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors (AChEI) (乙酰膽鹼酯酶抑制劑)**:
  * **Donepezil**: 5mg daily PO, titrate to 10mg daily. (Long half-life).
  * **Rivastigmine**: Transdermal patch (preferred for compliance/fewer GI side effects) or oral.
  * **Galantamine**: Oral capsule.
  * **Requirement before Rx**: Check ECG (PR interval <0.22s, no sick sinus syndrome/heart block).
* **Second Line / Severe AD: NMDA Receptor Antagonist**:
  * **Memantine**: 5mg daily PO, titrate weekly to 20mg daily.
  * **Mechanism**: Blocks glutamate excitotoxicity.
  * **Combo Rx**: Donepezil + Memantine often used in moderate-severe AD.

### Step 3: Chronic Management of BPSD
* **General Principle**: "Start low, go slow". Taper and attempt to stop after 3-6 months.
* **Antidepressants (For depression/anxiety/irritability)**:
  * **SSRI**: **Sertraline** or **Citalopram** (Avoid Paroxetine due to anticholinergic burden).
  * **Atypical**: **Mirtazapine** (Useful if insomnia/anorexia coexists).
* **Antipsychotics (For severe psychosis/aggression ONLY)**:
  * **Risperidone** (0.25-0.5mg BD) or **Quetiapine** (12.5-25mg BD).
  * **Black Box Warning**: ↑ Risk of stroke and all-cause mortality in elderly dementia patients. Require documented consent discussion with family.
* **Avoid**: Benzodiazepines (↑ falls, cognitive worsening), Tricyclics (anticholinergic → worsens memory).

### Medicolegal: Mental Capacity & Advanced Care Planning (HK Context)
* **Mental Capacity Assessment (Mental Health Ordinance Cap. 136)**:
  * Capacity is *decision-specific* and *time-specific*.
  * **Test**: Can the patient 1) Understand, 2) Retain, 3) Weigh up the information, and 4) Communicate the decision?
  * If incapacitated: Guardianship Board application for major financial/welfare decisions.
* **Fitness to Drive (HK Transport Department)**:
  * Medical practitioners have a duty to advise dementia patients to stop driving and notify the Transport Department if they refuse.
* **Advance Care Planning (ACP) (預設照顧計劃)**:
  * Conducted early while capacity is intact. Discuss values regarding feeding tubes (PEG/Ryles), intubation, CPR.
* **Advance Directive (AD) (預設醫療指示)**:
  * Legally binding document in HK refusing life-sustaining treatments (CPR, mechanical ventilation, artificial nutrition) when the patient reaches the terminal stage of disease, persistent vegetative state, or irreversible coma.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Cognitive Screening Instruments
| Feature | MMSE (Mini-Mental State Exam) | MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Max Score** | 30 | 30 |
| **Cut-off for impairment** | < 24 (adjusted for education) | < 26 |
| **Strengths** | Quick, widely recognized | Highly sensitive for **MCI** and **Executive/Visuospatial** dysfunction |
| **Weaknesses** | Ceiling effect, lacks executive testing | Takes longer, high education bias |

### Global Deterioration Scale (GDS) for Prognostication
* **Stage 1-2**: Normal to very mild subjective decline.
* **Stage 3**: Mild Cognitive Impairment (deficits apparent in complex tasks).
* **Stage 4**: Mild Dementia (needs help with IADLs like finances).
* **Stage 5**: Moderate Dementia (needs help choosing clothes, early ADL impairment).
* **Stage 6**: Moderately Severe (needs help bathing/toileting, fecal/urinary incontinence).
* **Stage 7**: Severe Dementia (loss of speech, non-ambulatory, loss of swallowing reflex).

### Disease Trajectory & Prognosis
* **Life Expectancy**: Median survival from diagnosis is ~4-8 years (highly dependent on age at Dx and comorbidities).
* **Terminal Phase**: Often marked by recurrent infections (aspiration pneumonia, urosepsis), malnutrition, and pressure sores.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Late-Stage Physical & Medical Complications
* **Neurological**: Unprovoked seizures (10-20% of AD patients in late stages), myoclonus, contractures.
* **Gastrointestinal / Nutritional**: Severe dysphagia (吞嚥困難), profound weight loss, malnutrition.
* **Respiratory**: Impaired cough reflex → recurrent **Aspiration Pneumonia (吸入性肺炎)** (Most common cause of death).
* **Dermatological**: Immobility → **Pressure Ulcers (褥瘡)**, infection, sepsis.
* **Musculoskeletal**: Falls → Hip fractures → Immobilization syndrome (DVT, PE).

### Drug Adverse Effects (Targeted Toxicity)
* **Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors (AChEIs)**:
  * **Cardiac**: Bradycardia, syncope, AV block.
  * **GI**: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, weight loss (parasympathetic overactivity).
  * **GU**: Urinary incontinence.
* **Antipsychotics (Risperidone/Quetiapine)**:
  * Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS), Tardive Dyskinesia.
  * Cerebrovascular events (Stroke/TIA).
  * QTc prolongation.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of death in end-stage dementia? Answer: Aspiration pneumonia due to progressive bulbar palsy and impaired swallowing reflexes.
* Viva Question: A patient with visual hallucinations and parkinsonism is given Haloperidol for agitation and becomes completely rigid and unresponsive. What is the diagnosis? Answer: Severe neuroleptic sensitivity in Lewy Body Dementia (DLB).
* Trap: Assuming memory loss is an essential requirement for early dementia. Pearl: Memory is preserved early in Frontotemporal Dementia (behavioral/language changes) and Lewy Body Dementia (executive/visuospatial deficits).
* Trap: Recommending a percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) tube for an end-stage dementia patient to prevent aspiration. Pearl: PEG tubes do NOT reduce the risk of aspiration pneumonia in advanced dementia and do not improve survival; careful hand feeding is the standard of care.
* Pitfall: Prescribing a tricyclic antidepressant (e.g., Amitriptyline) for neuropathic pain or insomnia in an elderly patient with mild dementia. It causes profound anticholinergic burden, acutely worsening cognitive function and risking delirium.
* Pitfall: Relying solely on the MMSE to rule out dementia. The MMSE lacks sensitivity for executive dysfunction; use the MoCA if Vascular or Frontotemporal dementia is suspected.
