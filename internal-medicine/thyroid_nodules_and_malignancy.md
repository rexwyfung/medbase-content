# Thyroid Nodules & Malignancy (甲狀腺結節與惡性腫瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Thyroid Nodules
* **Thyroid Nodule (甲狀腺結節):** Discrete lesion within the thyroid gland radiologically distinct from surrounding parenchyma.
* **Clinical Significance:** Highly prevalent (up to 50-60% incidentalomas on US); primary goal is excluding **Thyroid Malignancy (甲狀腺癌)** (present in 5-10% of nodules).

### Etiologies of Thyroid Nodules
* **Benign (90-95%):**
  * **Multinodular Goiter (多結節性甲狀腺腫):** Colloid nodules, hyperplastic nodules.
  * **Follicular Adenoma:** Benign neoplasm.
  * **Thyroid Cysts:** Fluid-filled, often resulting from degenerating adenomas.
  * **Inflammatory:** Hashimoto's thyroiditis (focal nodule), Subacute thyroiditis.
* **Malignant (5-10%):**
  * **Primary:** Papillary, Follicular, Medullary, Anaplastic carcinomas.
  * **Secondary:** Lymphoma, Metastasis (e.g., renal cell carcinoma, breast).

### Epidemiology of Thyroid Malignancy in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** 10th most common cancer in HK; highest incidence among young females.
* **Gender Ratio:** F:M ≈ 4:1.
* **Incidence Peak:** 30-50 years (Differentiated cancers); >60 years (Anaplastic).
* **Histological Distribution:**
  * **Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma (PTC) (乳頭狀甲狀腺癌):** 80-85%.
  * **Follicular Thyroid Carcinoma (FTC) (濾泡狀甲狀腺癌):** 10-15%.
  * **Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma (MTC) (髓質甲狀腺癌):** 2-5%.
  * **Anaplastic Thyroid Carcinoma (ATC) (未分化甲狀腺癌):** <1%.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma (PTC)
* **Cell Origin:** Follicular cells.
* **Mutations:** *BRAF V600E* (most common, aggressive phenotype), *RET/PTC* rearrangements.
* **Pathology Features:** Orphan Annie eye nuclei (empty nucleoplasm), psammoma bodies (calcifications), nuclear grooves.
* **Dissemination:** Primarily **lymphatic spread** → cervical lymph nodes.

### Pathophysiology of Follicular Thyroid Carcinoma (FTC)
* **Cell Origin:** Follicular cells.
* **Mutations:** *RAS* point mutations, *PAX8-PPARγ* translocations.
* **Pathology Features:** Encapsulated; malignancy strictly defined by **capsular or vascular invasion** (cannot be diagnosed on FNA alone).
* **Dissemination:** Primarily **hematogenous spread** → bone, lungs.

### Pathophysiology of Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma (MTC)
* **Cell Origin:** Parafollicular C cells (neuroendocrine origin).
* **Mutations:** *RET* proto-oncogene point mutations.
* **Association:** 25% familial; associated with **Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 2 (MEN2A/MEN2B)**.
* **Secretion:** Calcitonin (降鈣素), CEA, serotonin, VIP.
* **Pathology Features:** Amyloid stroma (stains with Congo red).

### Pathophysiology of Anaplastic Thyroid Carcinoma (ATC)
* **Cell Origin:** Undifferentiated follicular cells (often dedifferentiated from pre-existing PTC/FTC).
* **Mutations:** *TP53* mutations, *TERT* promoter mutations.
* **Dissemination:** Highly aggressive, rapid local tissue invasion (trachea, esophagus) and early distant metastases.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History for Thyroid Nodules & Malignancy
* **Presentation:** Often asymptomatic incidental finding; or patient-noticed painless neck lump.
* **Red Flag Symptoms (Locoregional Invasion):**
  * **Hoarseness (聲嘶):** Recurrent laryngeal nerve involvement.
  * **Dysphagia (吞嚥困難):** Esophageal compression.
  * **Stridor / Dyspnea:** Tracheal compression.
* **MTC-Specific Symptoms:** Secretory diarrhea, facial flushing (due to VIP/calcitonin/serotonin).
* **Risk Factors:**
  * Childhood head/neck irradiation (strongly linked to PTC).
  * Family history of thyroid cancer, MEN2 (pheochromocytoma, primary hyperparathyroidism), or familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP).
  * Rapid growth in weeks/months.

### Physical Examination Signs in Thyroid Nodules
* **Nodule Palpation:**
  * **Malignancy Signs:** Hard consistency, irregular borders, fixation to surrounding structures (non-mobile with swallowing).
* **Cervical Lymphadenopathy (頸淋巴結腫大):**
  * Palpable, firm, non-tender nodes in central or lateral neck compartments.
* **Airway/Vocal Signs:**
  * Audible stridor at rest.
  * Bovine cough / whispery voice (suggests vocal cord palsy).
* **Pemberton's Sign:**
  * Bilateral arm elevation → facial plethora, cyanosis, distended neck veins (indicates retrosternal extension compressing superior vena cava).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Benign vs Malignant Thyroid Nodules
| Feature | Benign Nodule (e.g., Colloid) | Malignant Nodule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Growth Rate** | Slow, stable | Rapid growth |
| **Consistency** | Soft, fluctuant (cystic) | Firm, hard, fixed |
| **Pain** | Painful (if hemorrhage/thyroiditis) | Usually painless |
| **Lymph Nodes** | Unremarkable | Palpable cervical lymphadenopathy |
| **Vocal Cords** | Normal movement | Unilateral paralysis (RLN involvement) |

### Differential Diagnoses of Anterior Neck Mass
* **Thyroglossal Duct Cyst:** Midline, moves upward with tongue protrusion.
* **Branchial Cleft Cyst:** Lateral neck (anterior to SCM muscle).
* **Parathyroid Adenoma:** Usually non-palpable, associated with hypercalcemia.
* **Lymphoma:** Rapidly enlarging rubbery nodes, B symptoms (fever, night sweats).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations for Thyroid Nodules
* **First-line Bloods:**
  * **Serum TSH:** Crucial initial branch point.
    * If ↓ TSH: Functioning nodule (rarely malignant) → Proceed to Radionuclide Scan.
    * If Normal/↑ TSH: Non-functioning nodule (higher malignancy risk) → Proceed to US.
* **Specific Tumor Markers (Do NOT use for general screening):**
  * **Serum Calcitonin & CEA:** Measure if MTC suspected or family history of MEN2.
  * **Thyroglobulin (Tg) & Anti-Tg Ab:** Useless for initial diagnosis (produced by benign and malignant tissue); used *only* for post-op surveillance.

### Ultrasound Evaluation of Thyroid Nodules
* **Sonographic Features Highly Suspicious for Malignancy:**
  * Solid composition, marked **hypoechogenicity** (darker than strap muscles).
  * **Microcalcifications** (psammoma bodies).
  * **Irregular/infiltrative margins** (spiculated).
  * **Taller-than-wide shape** on transverse view (growth across tissue planes).
  * Extrathyroidal extension.
  * Abnormal cervical lymph nodes (loss of fatty hilum, cystic change).

### Radionuclide Thyroid Scintigraphy (Tc-99m or I-123)
* **Indication:** Only if serum TSH is suppressed.
* **Hot Nodule (Hyperfunctioning):** Tracer uptake > surrounding tissue. Malignancy risk extremely low (<1%).
* **Cold Nodule (Non-functioning):** Decreased tracer uptake. Malignancy risk ≈ 10-15%. Requires FNA.

### Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) & Bethesda System
* **Indication:** Driven by US characteristics (TIRADS) and size (typically ≥1cm solid, or abnormal lymph nodes).
* **Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology:**
| Category | Description | Malignancy Risk | Next Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Non-diagnostic / Unsatisfactory | 5-10% | Repeat FNA with US guidance |
| **II** | Benign | 0-3% | Clinical follow-up |
| **III** | AUS / FLUS (Atypia of Undetermined Sig.) | 10-30% | Repeat FNA or Molecular testing |
| **IV** | Follicular Neoplasm | 25-40% | Diagnostic lobectomy |
| **V** | Suspicious for Malignancy | 50-75% | Near-total / Total thyroidectomy |
| **VI** | Malignant | 97-99% | Total thyroidectomy |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Airway Compromise in Anaplastic Carcinoma
* **Presentation:** Rapid onset stridor, hypoxia, impending respiratory failure.
* **Immediate Resuscitation:**
  * **Airway:** Urgent involvement of ENT/Anesthesia. Endotracheal intubation usually impossible due to external compression.
  * **Surgical Airway:** Awake tracheostomy or cricothyroidotomy (often technically difficult due to tumor mass).
  * **Adjuncts:** IV Dexamethasone (reduce peritumoral edema), Heliox (improve laminar flow).
* **Definitive Acute Rx:** Urgent palliative external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) to shrink tumor mass.

### Acute Hemorrhage into a Thyroid Cyst
* **Presentation:** Sudden onset severe neck pain, rapid nodule expansion, mild dysphagia.
* **Management:**
  * Urgent US to confirm cystic hemorrhage (exclude acute suppuration).
  * **Bedside Intervention:** US-guided therapeutic aspiration of blood/fluid relieves pain instantly.
  * Analgesia (NSAIDs/Paracetamol).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Management Algorithm for Benign Nodules
* **Strategy:** Active surveillance.
* **Monitoring:** Serial US every 12-24 months.
* **Intervention Triggers:** Significant growth (>20% increase in ≥2 dimensions), new suspicious US features, or compressive symptoms.
* **Options:** Cyst aspiration (if recurrently symptomatic), Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA), or unilateral lobectomy.

### Treatment Algorithm for Differentiated Thyroid Cancers (PTC/FTC)
* **Step 1: Surgical Resection**
  * Total Thyroidectomy (preferred for tumors >4cm, extrathyroidal extension, or metastases).
  * Thyroid Lobectomy (acceptable for solitary T1/T2 tumors <4cm without metastases).
  * Therapeutic central neck lymph node dissection if nodes clinically/radiologically positive.
* **Step 2: Radioactive Iodine (RAI - I-131) Ablation**
  * **Indications:** Intermediate to high-risk patients (large tumors, macroscopic local invasion, distant mets).
  * **Mechanism:** Destroys remnant normal thyroid tissue and microscopic disease.
  * **Preparation:** Requires TSH stimulation (via thyroid hormone withdrawal or Recombinant human TSH/rhTSH injections) and Low-Iodine Diet to maximize cellular iodine uptake.
* **Step 3: TSH Suppression Therapy**
  * **Drug:** Levothyroxine (T4) (甲狀腺素).
  * **Target:** Suppress TSH to deprive residual cancer cells of growth stimulus. Target TSH <0.1 mIU/L (high-risk) or 0.1-0.5 mIU/L (low-risk).

### Treatment Algorithm for Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma (MTC)
* **Pre-operative Screening:** MUST exclude concomitant Pheochromocytoma (check plasma free metanephrines) to prevent intraoperative hypertensive crisis.
* **Surgery:** Total thyroidectomy + prophylactic bilateral central compartment neck dissection (high rate of early nodal spread).
* **RAI Utility:** **Ineffective** (C-cells do not take up iodine).
* **Targeted Therapy:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., Vandetanib, Cabozantinib) for unresectable/metastatic disease.

### Post-Treatment Surveillance & Thyroglobulin Monitoring
* **Differentiated Thyroid Cancers:**
  * Monitor **Serum Thyroglobulin (Tg)** and **Anti-Tg Antibodies** every 6-12 months.
  * Tg should be undetectable post-Total Thyroidectomy + RAI. Rising Tg indicates recurrence.
  * Neck US every 6-12 months.
* **Medullary Thyroid Cancer:**
  * Monitor **Serum Calcitonin** and **CEA** doubling times (prognostic indicator for recurrence).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Ultrasound Risk Stratification (ACR TI-RADS)
| TI-RADS Category | Description | Points | FNA Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TR1** | Benign | 0 | No FNA |
| **TR2** | Not Suspicious | 2 | No FNA |
| **TR3** | Mildly Suspicious | 3 | FNA if ≥ 2.5 cm |
| **TR4** | Moderately Suspicious | 4-6 | FNA if ≥ 1.5 cm |
| **TR5** | Highly Suspicious | ≥ 7 | FNA if ≥ 1.0 cm |

### MACIS Prognostic Scoring for Papillary Carcinoma
* **Utility:** Predicts 20-year survival in PTC.
* **Components:**
  * **M**etastasis (Distant)
  * **A**ge (Cutoff usually >40 or 45 years indicates higher risk)
  * **C**ompleteness of surgical resection
  * **I**nvasion (Extrathyroidal)
  * **S**ize of tumor (cm)
* **Prognosis:** Differentiated cancers generally have excellent >90% 10-year survival. ATC has <10% 1-year survival.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Advanced Thyroid Malignancy
* **Locoregional Invasion:**
  * **Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve (RLN) Palsy:** Unilateral → hoarseness; Bilateral → airway obstruction/stridor.
  * **Tracheomalacia/Obstruction:** Direct invasion into tracheal cartilage.
  * **Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Syndrome:** Massive substernal extension blocking venous return.
* **Distant Metastasis:**
  * Lungs (nodular opacities, pleural effusion).
  * Bones (osteolytic lesions, pathological fractures).

### Adverse Effects of Radioactive Iodine (RAI) Therapy
* **Acute/Subacute:**
  * Radiation Sialadenitis (salivary gland inflammation/swelling).
  * Xerostomia (dry mouth) and altered taste (Dysgeusia).
  * Radiation thyroiditis/neck pain.
* **Chronic/Long-term:**
  * Permanent Hypothyroidism (expected outcome).
  * Slightly ↑ risk of secondary primary malignancies (e.g., leukemia, salivary gland tumors) with high cumulative doses.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a follicular adenoma from a follicular carcinoma? Answer: You cannot do it via FNA or cytology. Follicular carcinoma requires histological evidence of capsular or vascular invasion, which necessitates surgical excision (lobectomy) for full architectural assessment.
* Trap: Checking serum Thyroglobulin (Tg) to screen a patient for a newly discovered thyroid nodule. Pearl: Tg is produced by normal thyroid tissue and benign nodules. It is only useful as a tumor marker for recurrence surveillance *after* total thyroidectomy and RAI ablation.
* Pitfall: Sending a patient with Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma directly to surgery without checking blood pressure and metanephrines. Always screen for Pheochromocytoma (MEN2) first to avoid a fatal intraoperative hypertensive crisis.
* Viva Question: What is the significance of "Orphan Annie eye" nuclei and psammoma bodies? Answer: They are pathognomonic histological features of Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma (PTC).
* Trap: Reassuring a patient with a rapidly growing neck mass because their TSH is normal. Pearl: Most thyroid malignancies present as non-functioning cold nodules with completely normal thyroid function tests. Euthyroid status does not exclude cancer.
* Pitfall: Prescribing standard replacement doses of levothyroxine post-thyroidectomy for high-risk PTC. You must prescribe *suppressive* doses to keep TSH <0.1 mIU/L, as TSH is a trophic hormone that stimulates residual cancer cell growth.
