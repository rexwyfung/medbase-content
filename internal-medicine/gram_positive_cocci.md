# Gram-Positive Cocci (革蘭氏陽性球菌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Taxonomy & Primary Classification of GPC
* **Catalase-Positive (+ve) GPC**: *Staphylococcus* species (葡萄球菌)
* **Catalase-Negative (-ve) GPC**: *Streptococcus* (鏈球菌) & *Enterococcus* (腸球菌) species

### Etiological Subtypes of Staphylococcus
* **Coagulase-Positive (+ve)**: *Staphylococcus aureus* (金黃色葡萄球菌)
* **Coagulase-Negative (-ve) (CoNS)**: *S. epidermidis* (表皮葡萄球菌), *S. saprophyticus* (腐生葡萄球菌), *S. haemolyticus*, *S. lugdunensis* (highly virulent, behaves like *S. aureus*)

### Local Hong Kong Epidemiology of Resistant GPC
* **Methicillin-Resistant S. aureus (MRSA)**: Endemic in HK Hospital Authority (HA) facilities; accounts for ~40-50% of all *S. aureus* isolates in public hospitals
* **Vancomycin-Resistant Enterococcus (VRE)**: Emerging healthcare-associated pathogen in HK; predominantly *E. faecium*
* **Penicillin-Resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae (PRSP)**: High local prevalence of macrolide resistance (>70%) and intermediate/high penicillin resistance due to widespread community antibiotic use

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Staphylococcus aureus Virulence Factors & Toxins
* **Protein A**: Binds Fc region of IgG → prevents opsonization and phagocytosis
* **Toxic Shock Syndrome Toxin-1 (TSST-1)**: Superantigen → cross-links MHC-II & T-cell receptors (TCR) → massive cytokine storm (IL-1, IL-2, TNF-α, IFN-γ) → multi-organ failure
* **Exfoliative Toxins (ETA, ETB)**: Proteases that cleave desmoglein-1 in stratum granulosum → Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS)
* **Panton-Valentine Leukocidin (PVL)**: Pore-forming toxin targeting neutrophils → recurrent severe skin infections & necrotizing pneumonia (highly associated with Community-Acquired MRSA)

### Streptococcus Virulence Factors
* **S. pyogenes (Group A Strep, GAS)**:
  * **M Protein**: Resists phagocytosis; molecular mimicry → Rheumatic Fever
  * **Streptolysin O**: Oxygen-labile hemolysin (highly immunogenic → ASO antibodies)
  * **Erythrogenic Toxin**: Superantigen → Scarlet Fever
* **S. pneumoniae**:
  * **Polysaccharide Capsule**: Primary virulence factor; prevents phagocytosis (basis for pneumococcal vaccines)
  * **IgA Protease**: Cleaves mucosal IgA → colonizes respiratory tract

### Mechanisms of Antibiotic Resistance in GPC
* **MRSA (Methicillin Resistance)**:
  * Mediated by **mecA** gene (carried on SCCmec cassette)
  * Encodes altered Penicillin-Binding Protein 2a (**PBP2a**) → extremely low affinity for all β-lactams (except Ceftaroline)
* **VRE (Vancomycin Resistance)**:
  * Mediated by **vanA** or **vanB** gene clusters
  * Alters cell wall precursor target from D-alanyl-D-alanine to **D-alanyl-D-lactate** → loss of Vancomycin binding affinity
* **PRSP (Penicillin-Resistant S. pneumoniae)**:
  * Transformation and recombination events → mutated indigenous PBPs → ↓ affinity for Penicillins

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Clues for Resistant GPC Infections
* **Healthcare-Associated MRSA / VRE Risks**:
  * Prolonged HA hospital admission (>14 days)
  * Residence in Residential Care Homes for the Elderly (RCHE)
  * Recent broad-spectrum antibiotic exposure (Cephalosporins, Fluoroquinolones)
  * Presence of indwelling medical devices (CVC, Foley catheters)
* **Community-Acquired MRSA Risks**:
  * Close contact sports
  * Overcrowded living conditions
  * Intravenous drug use (IVDU)

### Physical Signs of Toxin-Mediated GPC Syndromes
* **Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS)**:
  * Diffuse macular erythroderma (sunburn-like rash)
  * Desquamation of palms and soles (occurs 1-2 weeks after onset)
  * Non-purulent conjunctival injection
  * Strawberry tongue
  * Hyperemia of oropharyngeal/vaginal mucous membranes
* **Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS)**:
  * Exfoliative bullous dermatitis
  * Positive (+ve) Nikolsky sign (epidermal detachment with gentle lateral pressure)
  * Sparing of mucous membranes (differentiates from TEN/SJS)
* **Scarlet Fever (猩紅熱)**:
  * Fine erythematous "sandpaper" rash
  * Circumoral pallor
  * Strawberry tongue

### Physical Signs of Systemic GPC Dissemination
* Splinter hemorrhages
* Janeway lesions
* Osler nodes
* Roth spots
* New-onset regurgitant heart murmurs
* Exquisite spinal tenderness (suggestive of discitis/epidural abscess)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Streptococcus by Hemolysis (溶血)
| Hemolysis Pattern | Appearance on Blood Agar | Pathogen Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alpha (α)** | Partial (Green/Brown zone) | *S. pneumoniae*, Viridans streptococci |
| **Beta (β)** | Complete (Clear zone) | *S. pyogenes* (GAS), *S. agalactiae* (GBS) |
| **Gamma (γ)** | None (No change) | *Enterococcus* spp., *S. gallolyticus* (Group D) |

### Lancefield Grouping of Beta-Hemolytic Streptococci
| Group | Pathogen | Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group A** | *S. pyogenes* | Pharyngitis, Impetigo, Necrotizing fasciitis, Rheumatic fever |
| **Group B** | *S. agalactiae* | Neonatal sepsis, meningitis (colonizes maternal vagina) |
| **Group C/G** | *S. dysgalactiae* | Cellulitis, bacteremia (often elderly/comorbid) |
| **Group D** | *S. gallolyticus* | Endocarditis strictly associated with colorectal carcinoma |

### Differentiating Staphylococcus Species
| Species | Coagulase | Novobiocin Test | Classic Discriminatory Niche |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ***S. aureus*** | +ve | Sensitive | Abscesses, severe device infections, native valve IE |
| ***S. epidermidis*** | -ve | Sensitive | Indwelling catheters, prosthetic joints, prosthetic valve IE |
| ***S. saprophyticus*** | -ve | Resistant | Uncomplicated UTI in young sexually active females |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Bench Microbiological Tests for GPC
* **Gram Stain**:
  * Clusters → *Staphylococcus* spp.
  * Chains / Pairs → *Streptococcus* or *Enterococcus* spp.
  * Lancet-shaped diplococci → *S. pneumoniae*
* **Catalase Test**: Drops of H2O2 → bubbles = *Staphylococcus*
* **Coagulase Test**: Rabbit plasma clot = *S. aureus*
* **Optochin Test**: Sensitive = *S. pneumoniae*; Resistant = Viridans streptococci
* **Bacitracin Test**: Sensitive = *S. pyogenes* (GAS); Resistant = *S. agalactiae* (GBS)

### Advanced Molecular Diagnostics
* **Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption/Ionization Time-of-Flight (MALDI-TOF) MS**:
  * Rapid species identification from culture plates or direct positive blood culture broth
* **Multiplex PCR (e.g., BioFire FilmArray)**:
  * Detects specific GPC species directly from blood culture
  * Identifies resistance genes simultaneously (*mecA*, *vanA/B*)

### Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST)
* **Disk Diffusion (Kirby-Bauer)**: Zone of inhibition determines Susceptible/Intermediate/Resistant
* **E-test (Epsilometer test)**: Determines precise Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)
* **Broth Microdilution**: Gold standard for determining Vancomycin MIC in MRSA

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation for Toxin-Mediated Shock (TSS)
* Aggressive intravenous crystalloid resuscitation
* Immediate removal of source (e.g., infected tampons, nasal packing, surgical debridement)
* Intravenous Clindamycin (suppresses bacterial ribosomal toxin production)
* Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG) (neutralizes circulating superantigens)

### Empirical Antibiotic Protocols for Undifferentiated Severe GPC Sepsis
* Intravenous Vancomycin
* Intravenous Linezolid (if poor renal function or high suspicion of VRE)
* Intravenous Daptomycin (if Vancomycin allergy; NOT for pneumonia)

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Targeted Pharmacological Algorithm for Specific GPC
| Pathogen | First-Line Therapy (HK HA Formulary) | Second-Line / Penicillin Allergy Alternatives |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MSSA** | IV Cloxacillin or IV Cefazolin | IV Vancomycin, IV Clindamycin |
| **MRSA** | IV Vancomycin | IV Linezolid, IV Daptomycin, IV Ceftaroline |
| **VRE (*E. faecium*)** | IV/PO Linezolid | IV Daptomycin (high dose), Quinupristin-dalfopristin |
| ***S. pyogenes* (GAS)** | IV/PO Penicillin G or V | IV/PO Clindamycin, IV Ceftriaxone |
| ***S. pneumoniae*** | IV Penicillin (if susceptible) or IV Ceftriaxone | IV Respiratory Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin) |
| ***Enterococcus faecalis***| IV Ampicillin (often + Gentamicin for synergy) | IV Vancomycin |

### High-Bioavailability Oral Step-Down Therapy for GPC
* PO Linezolid (100% bioavailability)
* PO Clindamycin (~90% bioavailability)
* PO Moxifloxacin/Levofloxacin (near 100% bioavailability)
* PO Co-trimoxazole (effective for CA-MRSA skin infections)

### MRSA Decolonization Protocol
* **Body Wash**: 4% Chlorhexidine gluconate solution daily for 5 days
* **Nasal Ointment**: 2% Mupirocin (Bactroban) applied to anterior nares twice daily for 5 days
* **Environment**: Change all bed linens and towels daily during treatment

### Chronic Suppressive Antibiotic Therapy
* Indicated for retained infected prosthetic materials (e.g., prosthetic joints) where surgery is contraindicated
* PO Rifampicin (never used as monotherapy due to rapid resistance emergence; must combine with another active agent like Fucidic acid or Co-trimoxazole)

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for Complicated S. aureus Bacteremia
* Positive blood cultures ≥ 72 hours after initiation of active antibiotics
* Presence of retained prosthetic intravascular devices
* Community-acquired S. aureus bacteremia without primary focus
* Delayed defervescence (>72 hours)
* *Prognosis: High risk of metastatic seeding (IE, osteomyelitis); mandates prolonged IV antibiotics (4-6 weeks) and TEE.*

### Vancomycin MIC Creep Risk Stratification
| Vancomycin MIC | Clinical Implication |
| :--- | :--- |
| **≤ 1.0 mg/L** | Favorable target; standard Vancomycin dosing effective |
| **1.5 mg/L** | Gray zone; increased risk of clinical failure |
| **≥ 2.0 mg/L** | High failure rate; switch to alternative (Linezolid/Daptomycin) immediately |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Metastatic Complications of GPC Bacteremia
* **Cardiac**: Destructive infective endocarditis (native/prosthetic valve)
* **Musculoskeletal**: Vertebral osteomyelitis, discitis, psoas abscess, septic arthritis
* **Neurological**: Epidural abscess, mycotic aneurysms, meningitis
* **Pulmonary**: Septic pulmonary emboli (especially with right-sided IE or PVL+ MRSA)

### Anti-GPC Pharmacotherapy Adverse Effects
* **Vancomycin**:
  * Red Man Syndrome (histamine release from rapid infusion; prevent by slowing infusion rate to max 10mg/min)
  * Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) (trough levels must be continuously monitored)
  * Ototoxicity (rare but irreversible)
* **Linezolid**:
  * Bone marrow suppression (Thrombocytopenia/Anemia) if duration > 14 days
  * Serotonin Syndrome (mild MAOI activity; interacts with SSRIs)
  * Optic/Peripheral neuropathy (with prolonged use > 28 days)
* **Daptomycin**:
  * Rhabdomyolysis and Myopathy (requires weekly CK monitoring)
  * Eosinophilic pneumonia

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the drug of choice for Methicillin-Susceptible S. aureus (MSSA) bacteremia? Answer: IV Cloxacillin (or Cefazolin). They have superior bactericidal activity against MSSA compared to Vancomycin.
* Trap: Prescribing Vancomycin for all severe *S. aureus* infections regardless of sensitivities. Pearl: Vancomycin is inferior to anti-staphylococcal penicillins for MSSA. Always de-escalate to Cloxacillin/Cefazolin once MRSA is ruled out.
* Pitfall: Using Daptomycin to treat MRSA pneumonia. Daptomycin is irreversibly inactivated by pulmonary surfactant and will result in clinical failure.
* Viva Question: A patient with colon cancer develops infective endocarditis. Which GPC is the most likely culprit? Answer: *Streptococcus gallolyticus* (formerly *S. bovis* biotype I).
* Trap: Using Linezolid in a patient taking Escitalopram without caution. Pearl: Linezolid is a reversible, non-selective MAOI and can precipitate fatal Serotonin Syndrome.
* Pitfall: Relying on positive blood cultures from a central line without confirming with peripheral cultures. Coagulase-negative Staphylococci (CoNS) are common skin flora and frequently contaminate line draws; always draw paired peripheral cultures to distinguish true bacteremia from contamination.
