# Phaeochromocytoma (嗜鉻細胞瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Phaeochromocytoma
* **Phaeochromocytoma (嗜鉻細胞瘤)**: Rare, catecholamine-secreting neuroendocrine tumor arising from chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla. 
* **Paraganglioma (副神經節瘤)**: Extra-adrenal sympathetic (secrete catecholamines) or parasympathetic (usually non-secretory, head/neck) ganglia tumors.

### The "Rule of 10s" for Phaeochromocytoma (Historical Rule of Thumb)
* **10% Bilateral**: ↑ risk in familial syndromes.
* **10% Extra-adrenal**: Paragangliomas.
* **10% Malignant**: Higher (up to 40%) in SDHB mutations.
* **10% Familial**: Actually obsolete; current data shows ~30-40% have germline mutations.
* **10% Pediatric**: Occurring in children.
* **10% Calcify**: Visible on plain films.

### Genetic Syndromes Associated with Phaeochromocytoma
* **MEN2A / MEN2B (多發性內分泌腫瘤)**: **RET** proto-oncogene mutation. (Assoc: Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma, Primary Hyperparathyroidism).
* **Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) Syndrome**: **VHL** tumor suppressor gene. (Assoc: Hemangioblastomas, Renal Cell Carcinoma).
* **Neurofibromatosis Type 1 (NF1)**: **NF1** gene. (Assoc: Café-au-lait spots, neurofibromas).
* **Familial Paraganglioma Syndromes**: **SDHx** (SDHB, SDHD) mutations. **SDHB** highly assoc with malignancy.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* Accounts for <0.2% of hypertension (高血壓) cases in HK HA clinics.
* Peak incidence: 30-50 years of age.
* High index of suspicion required for young patients with refractory HTN.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Catecholamine Synthesis Pathophysiology in Phaeochromocytoma
* Tumor autonomously secretes excessive catecholamines, escaping normal feedback loops.
* **Norepinephrine (NE)**: Most common predominant secretion (acts on α1, α2, β1).
* **Epinephrine (Epi)**: Tumors expressing PNMT enzyme (often adrenal or MEN2-assoc) (acts on β2).
* **Dopamine (DA)**: Often signifies immature tumor/paraganglioma; ↑ risk of malignancy.

### Receptor-Mediated Hemodynamic Effects of Phaeochromocytoma
* **Alpha-1 (α1) Hyperactivation**: Intense peripheral vasoconstriction → abrupt ↑ SBP/DBP.
* **Beta-1 (β1) Hyperactivation**: ↑ Inotropy & chronotropy → tachycardia (心動過速), palpitations.
* **Beta-2 (β2) Hyperactivation** (Epi-secreting): Episodic vasodilation, skeletal muscle tremor.
* **Chronic Volume Depletion**: Sustained α-mediated vasoconstriction shifts intravascular fluid to extravascular space → orthostasis.

### Metabolic Pathophysiology of Phaeochromocytoma
* **Hyperglycemia (高血糖)**: ↑ Glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis (β2) + ↓ insulin secretion (α2).
* **Hypermetabolic State**: ↑ Lipolysis and basal metabolic rate → severe weight loss.

## 3. Clinical Assessment 

### Classic Paroxysmal Triad of Phaeochromocytoma
* **Headache (頭痛)**: Sudden, severe, throbbing.
* **Palpitations (心悸)**: Fast, bounding heartbeat.
* **Sweating (出汗)**: Profuse, truncal diaphoresis.

### General History Findings in Phaeochromocytoma
* **Paroxysmal or Sustained Hypertension**: 50% sustained, 50% episodic.
* **Panic Attack-like Episodes**: Sense of impending doom, severe anxiety, tremor.
* **Postural Hypotension**: Lightheadedness upon standing (due to chronic volume contraction).
* **Metabolic Symptoms**: Unintentional weight loss despite normal/↑ appetite.
* **Triggers**: Attacks precipitated by positional changes, abdominal palpation, general anesthesia, or foods containing tyramine (e.g., cheese, wine).

### Physical Examination Signs in Phaeochromocytoma
* **Vital Signs**: ↑↑ BP, ↑ HR, significant orthostatic drop in BP > 20/10 mmHg.
* **Peripheral Exam**: Pallor during paroxysms (flushing is rare; if present, consider carcinoid). Tremor.
* **Fundoscopy**: Hypertensive retinopathy (flame hemorrhages, papilledema in malignant HTN).

### Stigmata of Associated Genetic Syndromes
* **NF1 Stigmata**: Café-au-lait macules (>6), axillary/inguinal freckling, cutaneous neurofibromas, Lisch nodules.
* **MEN2 Stigmata**: Anterior neck mass (Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma), Marfanoid habitus (MEN2B), mucosal neuromas of lips/tongue (MEN2B).
* **VHL Stigmata**: Visual field defects (retinal hemangioblastomas), ballotable flank mass (Renal Cell Carcinoma).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Paroxysmal Hypertension & Sweating
| Condition | Key Discriminatory Features vs Phaeochromocytoma |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Phaeochromocytoma (嗜鉻細胞瘤)** | Triad + pallor + highly elevated metanephrines. |
| **Panic Disorder (恐慌症)** | Normal biochemistry; prominent psychological triggers; lacks severe target-organ HTN damage. |
| **Carcinoid Syndrome (類癌綜合症)** | **Flushing** (not pallor), bronchospasm, diarrhea, right heart valvular lesions. ↑ 5-HIAA. |
| **Thyrotoxicosis (甲狀腺毒症)** | Sustained continuous tachycardia (not paroxysmal), heat intolerance, goiter, ↓ TSH, ↑ fT4. |
| **Essential Hypertension** | Absence of sweating/palpitation paroxysms; normal catecholamines. |
| **Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)** | Morning headaches, daytime somnolence, heavy snoring; normal daytime catecholamines. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations 

### Bedside Investigations for Phaeochromocytoma
* **ECG**: Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (strain pattern), sinus tachycardia, tachyarrhythmias (AF/SVT).
* **Urinalysis**: Glucosuria (+ve glucose dipstick secondary to hyperglycemia).

### Biochemical Blood Tests for Phaeochromocytoma
* **Plasma Free Metanephrines (血漿游離變腎上腺素)**: First-line screening. Highest sensitivity. 
* **Pre-test Protocol**: Must rest supine for 30 mins with IV cannula inserted prior to blood draw to ↓ stress-induced false +ves.
* **Fasting Glucose / HbA1c**: ↑ due to insulin suppression.

### Biochemical Urine Tests for Phaeochromocytoma
* **24-hour Urine Fractionated Metanephrines & Catecholamines**: Highest specificity.
* **Collection Protocol**: Requires acidic preservative in bottle.
* **Interference**: Must hold paracetamol, TCAs, SNRIs, L-dopa, and decongestants for 2 weeks prior (cause false +ves).

### Localization Imaging for Phaeochromocytoma
* **CT Abdomen & Pelvis (Non-contrast + Contrast)**: First-line imaging ONLY AFTER biochemical confirmation. Identifies adrenal mass (often >3cm, heterogeneous, cystic/necrotic areas, highly vascular).
* **MRI Abdomen**: Preferred if pregnant, pediatric, or iodine contrast allergy. Characteristic T2-weighted marked hyperintensity (**"Lightbulb" sign**).

### Functional and Nuclear Imaging for Phaeochromocytoma
* **123I-MIBG Scan (碘-123-間碘芐胍掃描)**: Highly specific functional scan. Taken up by chromaffin tissue. Identifies extra-adrenal tumors (paragangliomas) or metastases.
* **68Ga-DOTATATE PET-CT**: Superior sensitivity over MIBG, especially for metastatic SDHx-related paragangliomas. Emerging gold standard in HK HA tertiary centers.

### Genetic Testing Criteria for Phaeochromocytoma
* **Criteria**: ALL patients with confirmed Phaeochromocytoma/Paraganglioma should undergo germline mutation testing (regardless of age/family history) due to high prevalence (~40%).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation 

### Phaeochromocytoma Hypertensive Crisis Protocol
* **Definition**: Life-threatening SBP > 200 mmHg with end-organ ischemia (encephalopathy, ACS).
* **First-line Rx**: **IV Phentolamine (酚妥拉明)** (short-acting, non-selective, reversible α-blocker) bolus, followed by continuous infusion.
* **Alternative Vasodilators**: IV Sodium Nitroprusside or IV Nicardipine.
* **Beta-blocker Rule**: NEVER administer IV beta-blockers (e.g., Labetalol) before adequate alpha-blockade → causes unopposed alpha stimulation → catastrophic vasoconstriction & cardiac arrest.
* **Volume Resuscitation**: Concurrently give aggressive IV Normal Saline (vasodilation unmasks chronic volume contraction → high risk of precipitous shock).

### Intraoperative Hemodynamic Crisis Management
* **Hypertensive Crisis (Tumor Manipulation)**: Deepen anesthesia → bolus IV Phentolamine or IV Sodium Nitroprusside.
* **Tachyarrhythmias (Catecholamine Surge)**: IV **Esmolol (艾司洛爾)** (ultra-short-acting β1-selective blocker).
* **Post-ligation Hypotension**: Instant drop in catecholamines upon adrenal vein ligation. Rx: Stop vasodilators → IV fluid boluses → IV Vasopressin / Noradrenaline if refractory.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Pre-operative Pharmacological Blockade Algorithm
| Sequence | Timing | Therapy / Drug Class | Clinical Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1: Alpha-Blockade** | 10-14 days pre-op | **Phenoxybenzamine** (irreversible non-selective α-blocker) OR **Doxazosin/Prazosin** (selective α1-blocker) | Normalize BP, prevent intra-op crisis, reverse volume contraction. |
| **Step 2: Fluid Expansion** | 2-3 days post-Step 1 | **High-sodium diet** + liberal fluid intake | Replete intravascular volume to prevent post-resection shock. |
| **Step 3: Beta-Blockade** | 2-3 days pre-op | **Propranolol** or **Atenolol** (β-blockers) | ONLY if tachycardia/arrhythmias develop AFTER α-blockade. |

### Definitive Surgical Management Strategy
* **Procedure**: Laparoscopic or Robotic-assisted Adrenalectomy. 
* **Open Laparotomy**: Reserved for tumors >6cm or suspected invasive malignancy.
* **Cortex-Sparing Surgery**: Considered in bilateral disease (e.g., MEN2) to prevent lifelong Addisonian adrenal insufficiency.

### Long-term Medical Management of Inoperable / Metastatic Disease
* **Chronic Pharmacotherapy**: Lifelong α-blockade (Phenoxybenzamine).
* **Metyrosine (α-methyl-L-tyrosine)**: Competitive inhibitor of tyrosine hydroxylase (rate-limiting enzyme) → ↓ catecholamine synthesis.
* **Targeted Radionuclide Therapy**: 131I-MIBG therapy or 177Lu-DOTATATE for unresectable, avid metastatic lesions.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### PASS Score (Phaeochromocytoma of the Adrenal Gland Scaled Score)
* **Purpose**: Histological evaluation to stratify risk of malignant behavior.
* **Components**: Nuclear pleomorphism, mitotic figures, atypical mitoses, capsular invasion, vascular invasion, tumor necrosis.
* **Interpretation**: 
  * PASS < 4: Benign behavior likely.
  * PASS ≥ 4: High potential for biologically aggressive/malignant behavior.

### Prognosis and Post-Operative Surveillance
* **Malignancy Definition**: Absolute diagnosis of malignancy requires evidence of distant metastases in non-chromaffin tissue (bones, liver, lungs, lymph nodes). Histology alone cannot confirm malignancy.
* **Surgical Cure Rate**: ~90% for benign, completely resected tumors.
* **Follow-up Protocol**: Plasma/urine metanephrines at 2-6 weeks post-op, then annually for at least 10 years (lifelong if syndromic/paraganglioma).

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### Cardiovascular Complications of Phaeochromocytoma
* **Catecholamine-Induced Cardiomyopathy**: Toxic direct effect + ischemia → global hypokinesis / inverted Takotsubo morphology → Acute Heart Failure.
* **Myocardial Infarction**: Type 2 MI (supply/demand mismatch from severe tachycardia + intense coronary vasospasm).
* **Arrhythmias**: Atrial fibrillation, VT, VF (fatal).

### Neurological Complications of Phaeochromocytoma
* **Stroke (中風)**: Hemorrhagic (due to catastrophic HTN spikes) or Ischemic.
* **Encephalopathy**: Hypertensive encephalopathy presenting with confusion, seizures, papilledema.

### Iatrogenic / Post-Operative Complications
* **Severe Refractory Hypotension**: Loss of tumor catecholamines + residual pre-op alpha-blockade + unmasked chronic hypovolemia.
* **Post-op Hypoglycemia**: Sudden withdrawal of catecholamine-mediated insulin suppression → rebound hyperinsulinemia. Requires strict q1h capillary blood glucose monitoring.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why must alpha-blockade absolutely precede beta-blockade in phaeochromocytoma? Answer: Beta-blockade removes β2-mediated vasodilation. If alpha receptors are unblocked, the catecholamine surge causes unopposed α1 vasoconstriction, leading to a catastrophic hypertensive crisis and cardiac arrest.
* Trap: Relying on plasma catecholamines for biochemical diagnosis. Pearl: Use plasma free metanephrines instead; tumors metabolize catecholamines continuously into metanephrines, making metanephrines highly sensitive, whereas catecholamines are secreted episodically and may be normal between attacks.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing a phaeochromocytoma as malignant based purely on aggressive local histological invasion. True malignancy is defined strictly by the presence of chromaffin tumor cells in tissues where they are not normally found (i.e., distant metastases to bone, liver, lungs, or lymph nodes).
* Viva Question: What is the classic intra-operative hemodynamic pattern when the surgeon ligates the main adrenal vein? Answer: An immediate, precipitous drop in blood pressure due to the sudden cessation of catecholamine output into the systemic circulation.
* Trap: Fasting a patient strictly without IV hydration prior to phaeochromocytoma resection. Pearl: These patients are chronically volume contracted; they require generous IV crystalloid loading pre-operatively once alpha-blocked to prevent severe hypotensive shock upon tumor removal.
