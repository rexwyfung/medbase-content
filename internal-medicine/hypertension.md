# Hypertension (高血壓)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Hypertension
* **Definition:** Persistent elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure (BP).
* **Clinic Threshold:** Systolic BP (SBP) ≥140 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP (DBP) ≥90 mmHg on ≥2 separate visits.
* **Home/Ambulatory Threshold:** Average SBP ≥135 mmHg and/or DBP ≥85 mmHg.
* **Isolated Systolic Hypertension (單純收縮期高血壓):** SBP ≥140 mmHg + DBP <90 mmHg (common in elderly due to ↓ aortic compliance).

### Primary and Secondary Etiologies
* **Primary (Essential) Hypertension (原發性高血壓):** 90-95% of cases. Idiopathic. Associated with genetic predisposition, obesity, ↑ Na+ intake, alcohol, aging.
* **Secondary Hypertension (繼發性高血壓):** 5-10% of cases. Suspect if young (<30 yrs), resistant to ≥3 drugs, or sudden onset.
* **Renal Causes:** Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) (most common), Renal Artery Stenosis (RAS) (atherosclerosis in elderly, fibromuscular dysplasia in young females), Adult Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD).
* **Endocrine Causes:** Primary hyperaldosteronism (**Conn's Syndrome**), Phaeochromocytoma, **Cushing's Syndrome**, Acromegaly, Hyper/Hypothyroidism, Hyperparathyroidism.
* **Mechanical/Vascular Causes:** Coarctation of the aorta (主動脈縮窄).
* **Sleep Disorders:** Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) (睡眠窒息症).
* **Drug-Induced Causes:** NSAIDs, oral contraceptives (OCP), systemic corticosteroids, sympathomimetics (e.g., pseudoephedrine), SNRIs, licorice, illicit drugs (cocaine, amphetamines).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** ~30% of HK adult population (Population Health Survey). 
* **Control Rate:** Nearly 50% of hypertensive individuals in HK are undiagnosed; among diagnosed, ~40-50% achieve target control.
* **Burden:** Leading modifiable risk factor for stroke (中風) and ischemic heart disease (冠心病) locally.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### RAAS and Sympathetic Nervous System Pathophysiology
* **Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS) Overactivity:** ↑ Catecholamines → α1-receptor stimulation (vasoconstriction → ↑ Systemic Vascular Resistance [SVR]) + β1-receptor stimulation (↑ Heart Rate, ↑ Inotropy → ↑ Cardiac Output [CO]).
* **Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS):** 
* **Renin Release:** Triggered by ↓ renal perfusion, SNS activity, or ↓ macula densa Na+.
* **Angiotensin II (AT-II):** Potent direct vasoconstrictor → ↑ SVR. Stimulates pituitary ADH release. 
* **Aldosterone:** Secreted by adrenal zona glomerulosa → ↑ Na+/H2O reabsorption + ↑ K+/H+ excretion in distal convoluted tubule (DCT) and collecting duct → ↑ plasma volume → ↑ CO.

### Endothelial Dysfunction and Vascular Remodeling Mechanisms
* **Endothelial Dysfunction:** Imbalance between vasodilators (Nitric Oxide, Prostacyclin) and vasoconstrictors (Endothelin-1).
* **Vascular Remodeling:** Chronic ↑ pressure → smooth muscle cell hypertrophy/hyperplasia in tunica media (arteriolosclerosis) → ↓ lumen diameter → irreversible ↑ SVR.
* **Aortic Stiffening:** Elastin fragmentation and collagen deposition in large arteries (aging) → ↑ pulse wave velocity → early wave reflection → ↑ SBP (Isolated Systolic HTN) and widened pulse pressure.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Specific to Secondary Hypertension and Target Organ Damage
* **OSA Symptoms:** Snoring, witnessed apneas, morning headaches, excessive daytime somnolence.
* **Phaeochromocytoma Triad:** Episodic headaches, sweating, palpitations/tachycardia.
* **Cushing's Symptoms:** Progressive weight gain, proximal muscle weakness, easy bruising.
* **Cardiovascular Risk Factors:** Smoking, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, family Hx of premature CAD.
* **End-Organ Symptoms:** Chest pain (angina/ischemia), dyspnea/orthopnea (heart failure), focal neurological deficits (TIA/stroke), visual disturbances (retinopathy).

### Physical Signs of Secondary Hypertension
* **General:** Central obesity, moon facies, buffalo hump, purple abdominal striae (**Cushing's**).
* **Head & Neck:** Carotid bruits (atherosclerosis), enlarged thyroid (thyrotoxicosis).
* **Cardiovascular:** Radio-femoral delay, weak/absent femoral pulses (**Coarctation**).
* **Abdomen:** Renal artery bruits auscultated periumbilically/flanks (**RAS**), ballotable bilateral flank masses (**ADPKD**).

### Hypertensive Retinopathy Physical Grading (Keith-Wagener-Barker)
* **Grade 1:** Mild generalized retinal arterial narrowing (silver/copper wiring).
* **Grade 2:** Focal arteriopelvic narrowing + Arteriovenous (AV) nipping (venous compression at arterial crossings).
* **Grade 3:** Grade 2 + Flame-shaped hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots (nerve fiber layer infarctions), hard exudates (lipid deposits).
* **Grade 4 (Malignant):** Grade 3 + **Papilledema** (optic disc swelling).

### Cardiovascular End-Organ Physical Signs
* **Palpation:** Displaced sustained apex beat (Left Ventricular Hypertrophy [LVH]).
* **Auscultation:** S4 gallop (atrial contraction against stiff LV), prominent A2 (loud aortic closure), early diastolic murmur (aortic regurgitation from aortic root dilatation).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Clinical Differentials for Elevated Blood Pressure Readings
* **White Coat Hypertension:** BP ≥140/90 mmHg in clinic, but normal (<135/85) on Daytime Ambulatory BP Monitoring (ABPM) or Home BP Monitoring (HBPM). Driven by autonomic anxiety response.
* **Masked Hypertension:** Clinic BP is normal (<140/90), but ABPM/HBPM is elevated. High risk of undetected target organ damage.
* **Pseudohypertension:** Falsely elevated BP due to heavily calcified, non-compressible brachial arteries (Monckeberg's arteriosclerosis) in elderly/CKD. SBP may read >200 mmHg without target organ damage. +ve **Osler's sign** (radial pulse remains palpable despite cuff inflation > SBP).
* **Acute Sympathetic Surge:** Transient ↑ BP due to acute pain, urinary retention, severe anxiety, or acute substance intoxication (e.g., methamphetamines).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Diagnostic Threshold Criteria for Hypertension
| Setting | Normal BP (mmHg) | HTN Diagnostic Threshold (mmHg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Clinic / Office BP** | < 130/85 | **≥ 140/90** |
| **ABPM (Daytime avg)** | < 135/85 | **≥ 135/85** |
| **ABPM (24-hour avg)** | < 130/80 | **≥ 130/80** |
| **ABPM (Nighttime avg)** | < 120/70 | **≥ 120/70** |
| **HBPM (Home avg)** | < 135/85 | **≥ 135/85** |

### Bedside and Ambulatory Investigations
* **Ambulatory Blood Pressure Monitoring (ABPM):** Gold standard for confirming diagnosis, ruling out white coat/masked HTN, and assessing nocturnal "dipping" (normal is >10% drop at night; non-dippers have ↑ CV risk).
* **Home Blood Pressure Monitoring (HBPM):** Alternative if ABPM unavailable. Require twice-daily readings for 7 days (discard day 1 average).
* **12-lead ECG:** Assess for LVH (Sokolow-Lyon criteria: S in V1 + R in V5/V6 ≥35 mm), ischemia, or arrhythmias (Atrial Fibrillation).
* **Urine Dipstick:** Screen for proteinuria/hematuria (underlying renal disease or hypertensive nephropathy).
* **Urine Albumin:Creatinine Ratio (ACR):** Detects early microalbuminuria (target organ damage).

### Blood Investigations for Target Organ Damage and Secondary Causes
* **Renal Profile (U&E, Cr, eGFR):** Assess baseline kidney function; check K+ (Hypokalemia suggests Conn's, Cushing's, or diuretic use; Hyperkalemia suggests CKD).
* **Metabolic Profile:** Fasting plasma glucose & HbA1c (comorbid DM), Fasting Lipid Profile (risk stratification).
* **Specialist Secondary Screens (if indicated):** 
  * Plasma Aldosterone-to-Renin Ratio (ARR) → +ve if ↑ Aldosterone, ↓ Renin (**Conn's**).
  * 24-hr urinary fractionated metanephrines (**Phaeochromocytoma**).
  * Overnight 1mg Dexamethasone suppression test (**Cushing's**).

### Imaging and Special Investigations
* **Echocardiogram (TTE):** Indicated if ECG shows LVH, or heart failure suspected. Assesses LV mass, diastolic dysfunction, LA size.
* **Renal Ultrasound with Doppler:** Screen for polycystic kidneys, asymmetrical kidney sizes, and Renal Artery Stenosis.
* **CT/MRI Abdomen:** Adrenal adenoma/hyperplasia localization.
* **Polysomnography:** If OSA suspected.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Definitions of Hypertensive Crises
* **Hypertensive Emergency (高血壓急症):** SBP >180 and/or DBP >120 mmHg **WITH** evidence of acute target organ damage (e.g., encephalopathy, acute pulmonary edema, ACS, acute kidney injury, aortic dissection, eclampsia). Requires *IV therapy*.
* **Hypertensive Urgency (高血壓次急症):** SBP >180 and/or DBP >120 mmHg **WITHOUT** acute target organ damage. Requires *oral therapy* over hours/days.

### Acute Resuscitation Protocol for Hypertensive Emergency
* **General BP Target:** Reduce Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) by **maximum 20-25% within the first hour**, then to 160/100 mmHg over 2-6 hours. Too rapid ↓ BP → cerebral/coronary/renal watershed ischemia.
* **IV Pharmacotherapy Options (HA Formulary):**
* **IV Labetalol** (α1 and non-selective β blocker): First-line for most emergencies (e.g., encephalopathy). Contraindicated in asthma, heart block, bradycardia.
* **IV Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN)** (Nitric oxide donor → venodilation): First-line for acute pulmonary edema and ACS.
* **IV Sodium Nitroprusside** (Arterial + Venous dilator): High potency. Risk of thiocyanate toxicity in renal/hepatic failure.
* **IV Hydralazine** (Direct arterial vasodilator): Primarily used in eclampsia. 

### Acute Management Exceptions (Specific BP Targets)
* **Acute Aortic Dissection:** REQUIRES RAPID REDUCTION. Target SBP **<120 mmHg** + Heart Rate <60 bpm within 20 mins. Rx: IV Esmolol/Labetalol (MUST block β before α-vasodilators to prevent reflex tachycardia and ↑ aortic shearing stress).
* **Acute Ischemic Stroke:** PERMISSIVE HYPERTENSION. Do NOT lower BP unless SBP >220 or DBP >120 mmHg, OR if planning thrombolysis (Alteplase) / mechanical thrombectomy → keep <185/110 mmHg.
* **Intracerebral Hemorrhage:** Target SBP 140-180 mmHg to minimize hematoma expansion while maintaining cerebral perfusion pressure.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Lifestyle Modifications for Hypertension
* **Diet:** DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) - rich in fruits, veg, low-fat dairy.
* **Sodium Restriction:** <2.0g Sodium (or <5g NaCl) per day (↓ SBP by 2-8 mmHg).
* **Weight Reduction:** Maintain BMI <23 kg/m² (Asian cut-off). ↓ SBP by ~1 mmHg per 1 kg lost.
* **Exercise:** ≥150 mins/week moderate-intensity aerobic exercise.
* **Alcohol Restriction:** ≤2 units/day (males), ≤1 unit/day (females).

### Stepwise Pharmacological Algorithm (NICE/Local Guidelines)
| Step | Age <55 and Non-Black | Age ≥55 OR Black Patient of any age |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | **A** (ACEI or ARB) | **C** (CCB) |
| **Step 2** | **A + C** | **A + C** |
| **Step 3** | **A + C + D** (Thiazide-like Diuretic) | **A + C + D** |
| **Step 4** | **Resistant HTN:** Check adherence. Add **Low-dose Spironolactone** (if K+ ≤4.5) OR **Alpha-blocker/Beta-blocker** (if K+ >4.5). | Same as left column. |

### First-Line Antihypertensive Classes (A, C, D)
* **A = ACE Inhibitors / ARBs:** e.g., Lisinopril, Enalapril / Losartan, Valsartan. (Renoprotective, 1st line in DM/CKD).
* **C = Calcium Channel Blockers (Dihydropyridines):** e.g., Amlodipine, Nifedipine SR. (Potent peripheral vasodilators).
* **D = Thiazide-like Diuretics:** e.g., Indapamide, Chlorthalidone. (Preferred over HCTZ due to longer half-life and proven CV benefit).

### Target Blood Pressure Goals
* **General Target (Age <80):** Clinic BP <140/90 mmHg.
* **Elderly Target (Age ≥80):** Clinic BP <150/90 mmHg.
* **High-Risk Target (DM, CKD with proteinuria, Established CVD):** Clinic BP <130/80 mmHg (tolerability permitting).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Cardiovascular Risk Scoring Systems
* **QRISK3 / SCORE2:** Calculates 10-year risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) (myocardial infarction, stroke). 
* **Parameters included:** Age, sex, ethnicity, SBP, total cholesterol/HDL ratio, smoking status, Hx of diabetes, RA, CKD, AF.
* **Clinical Utility:** Influences threshold for initiating lipid-lowering therapy (Statins) alongside BP control. If 10-yr risk >10%, aggressive modification warranted.

### Prognostic Implications of Poorly Controlled Hypertension
* **Rule of Halves/Doubles:** For every 20 mmHg increase in SBP or 10 mmHg increase in DBP above 115/75 mmHg, the risk of cardiovascular mortality **doubles**.
* **Life Expectancy:** Untreated severe HTN reduces life expectancy by 10-20 years, primarily via accelerated atherosclerosis and target organ failure.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Hypertension
* **Cardiac:** Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH), Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF) → Heart Failure with Reduced EF (HFrEF), Coronary Artery Disease (CAD), Atrial Fibrillation (due to LA stretch).
* **Neurological:** Ischemic stroke (atherothromboembolism, lacunar infarcts), Intracerebral hemorrhage (Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms), Vascular dementia.
* **Renal:** Hypertensive nephrosclerosis (hyaline arteriolosclerosis of afferent arterioles), CKD, proteinuria.
* **Vascular:** Aortic dissection, Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA), Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD).
* **Ocular:** Hypertensive retinopathy, central retinal vein/artery occlusion.

### Major Adverse Effects of Antihypertensive Drugs
* **ACE Inhibitors:** Dry bradykinin cough (switch to ARB), Hyperkalemia, Angioedema, acute renal failure in bilateral RAS.
* **CCB (Dihydropyridines):** Peripheral (pedal) edema, flushing, headache, palpitations.
* **Thiazide Diuretics:** Hypokalemia, Hyponatremia, Hyperuricemia (precipitates gout), Hyperglycemia, Hypercalcemia.
* **Spironolactone (Aldosterone antagonist):** Hyperkalemia, Gynecomastia (binds androgen/progesterone receptors).
* **Beta-blockers:** Bradycardia, bronchospasm (in asthma), cold extremities, masking of hypoglycemic symptoms.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most definitive clinical difference between hypertensive emergency and hypertensive urgency? Answer: The presence of acute target organ damage (e.g., encephalopathy, acute pulmonary edema, papilledema) distinguishes emergency from urgency, NOT the absolute numerical BP value.
* Viva Question: Why do we prefer Thiazide-like diuretics (Indapamide) over pure Thiazides (Hydrochlorothiazide) in HA? Answer: Thiazide-like diuretics have a longer duration of action and more robust evidence for reducing major cardiovascular events and mortality.
* Trap: Giving an ACE inhibitor to a patient presenting with an abdominal bruit and uncontrolled hypertension. Pearl: An abdominal bruit suggests Renal Artery Stenosis. Giving ACE inhibitors in bilateral RAS blocks efferent arteriolar constriction (which is maintaining GFR), leading to catastrophic acute kidney injury.
* Trap: Rapidly normalizing blood pressure to 120/80 in a patient presenting with hypertensive encephalopathy. Pearl: Chronically hypertensive patients have right-shifted cerebral autoregulation curves. Rapid normalization causes acute cerebral hypoperfusion/ischemia. Maximum reduction is 20-25% MAP in the first hour.
* Pitfall: Forgetting to check renal function (U&E/Cr) 1-2 weeks after initiating an ACEI/ARB. A mild rise in creatinine (<30%) is acceptable and hemodynamic, but a massive spike indicates undiagnosed bilateral renal artery stenosis.
* Pitfall: Giving Hydralazine or Sodium Nitroprusside monotherapy for aortic dissection. Direct vasodilators cause reflex sympathetic activation (tachycardia and ↑ LV contractility), which increases aortic shearing stress (dP/dt) and worsens the tear. Always give negative inotropes/chronotropes (Beta-blockers like Esmolol/Labetalol) FIRST.
