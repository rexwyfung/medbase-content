# Infection Control & Antimicrobial Resistance (感染控制及抗菌藥物耐藥性)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Healthcare-Associated Infections (HAIs)
* **Healthcare-Associated Infection (HAI):** Infection acquired in a hospital/healthcare facility (e.g., Old Age Home [OAH]) that was neither present nor incubating at the time of admission.
* **Temporal Cutoff:** Typically defined as symptom onset >48 hours after admission or within 30 days of surgery/discharge.

### Epidemiology of Multidrug-Resistant Organisms (MDROs) in Hong Kong
* **Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA):** Endemic in HK Hospital Authority (HA) hospitals; highly prevalent in OAH residents.
* **Carbapenemase-Producing Enterobacteriaceae (CPE):** Increasing local incidence; highly associated with medical tourism or hospitalization outside HK (esp. Mainland China, India).
* **Vancomycin-Resistant Enterococci (VRE):** Prone to massive hospital ward outbreaks; easily contaminates environments (bed rails, BP cuffs).
* **Candida auris:** Emerging highly resistant fungal pathogen; frequent environmental persistence & ICU outbreaks in HK.

### Mechanisms of Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)
* **Intrinsic Resistance:** Natural structural features (e.g., *Mycoplasma* lacks cell wall → inherently resistant to β-lactams).
* **Acquired Resistance:** Genetic mutation or horizontal gene transfer (plasmids, transposons).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### The Chain of Infection in Healthcare Settings
* **Infectious Agent:** Bacteria (MRSA, VRE, CPE, C. diff), Viruses (SARS-CoV-2, Influenza, Norovirus), Fungi.
* **Reservoir:** Colonized patients, HCW hands, contaminated medical equipment (stethoscopes), ward environment (sinks, bedpan washers).
* **Portal of Exit:** Respiratory tract (droplets/aerosols), GI tract (feces), skin scales, blood.
* **Mode of Transmission:** Direct contact (skin-to-skin), Indirect contact (fomites), Droplet, Airborne.
* **Portal of Entry:** Invasive lines (CVC, PIVC), Foley catheters, endotracheal tubes, surgical wounds, mucous membranes.
* **Susceptible Host:** Immunosuppressed, elderly, severe underlying disease, prolonged ICU stay.

### Molecular Mechanisms of Antimicrobial Resistance
* **Target Site Modification:** Altered Penicillin-Binding Protein 2a (PBP2a) via *mecA* gene → MRSA. Altered peptidoglycan precursors (D-ala-D-ala to D-ala-D-lac) → VRE.
* **Enzymatic Degradation:** Production of Carbapenemases (KPC, NDM, OXA-48, VIM) → CPE. Extended-Spectrum Beta-Lactamases (ESBL) hydrolyze 3rd-gen cephalosporins.
* **Efflux Pumps:** Active expulsion of drug from bacterial cell (e.g., *Pseudomonas* pumping out fluoroquinolones/carbapenems).
* **Porin Channel Loss:** ↓ Outer membrane permeability → Carbapenem-resistant *Acinetobacter baumannii* (CRAB).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for Infection Control Risk Assessment
* **Healthcare Exposure:** Recent hospital admissions (<12 months), ICU stays, prolonged length of stay (LOS).
* **Residential Status:** Old Age Home (OAH) or Disability Care Home resident (high MRSA/MDRO prevalence).
* **Travel History:** Medical care, hospitalization, or hemodialysis outside HK in the past 12 months (crucial for CPE screening).
* **Medical Interventions:** Recent broad-spectrum Abx use (esp. carbapenems, fluoroquinolones, cephalosporins), presence of indwelling devices (Foley, CVC, PEG tube).
* **Previous MDRO Status:** Known history of MRSA, VRE, CPE, or MDRA colonization/infection.

### Physical Examination for Sources of Healthcare-Associated Infections
* **Vascular Access Sites:** Inspect PIVC/CVC/PICC sites for erythema, induration, purulent exudate, palpable cord (phlebitis / CRBSI).
* **Respiratory System:** Assess for purulent sputum, new crackles, worsening hypoxia in intubated/tracheostomy patients (VAP / HAP).
* **Gastrointestinal & Genitourinary Systems:** Suprapubic tenderness, cloudy/foul-smelling urine in catheterized patients (CAUTI).
* **Skin & Soft Tissue:** Inspect all surgical incisions for dehiscence/pus (SSI). Staging of sacral/heel pressure ulcers; check for foul odor, slough, deep tissue exposure.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Colonization vs. Active Infection
| Feature | Colonization (定植) | Active Infection (感染) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Host Response** | Absent | +ve (Fever, leukocytosis, ↑ CRP/PCT) |
| **Tissue Invasion** | Absent (superficial presence) | +ve (Pus, inflammation, necrosis) |
| **Clinical Symptoms** | Asymptomatic | Symptomatic (Cough, dysuria, pain) |
| **Management** | Infection control precautions ± Decolonization | Antimicrobial therapy + Source control |

### Discriminatory Features of Transmission Routes
| Route | Particle Size | Travel Distance | Classic Pathogens |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Contact** | N/A | Direct touch / Fomite | MRSA, VRE, CPE, *C. difficile*, Norovirus |
| **Droplet** | > 5 μm | < 1-2 meters (drops rapidly) | Influenza, RSV, N. meningitidis, Mycoplasma |
| **Airborne** | < 5 μm | Airborne for hours / travels via HVAC | Measles, Varicella, M. tuberculosis (TB) |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Active Surveillance Culture (ASC) Screening Protocols for MDROs
* **MRSA Screening:** Swabs from Anterior Nares, Groin, Axilla, and any open wounds/ulcers.
* **VRE Screening:** Rectal swab (must show visible feces) or stool sample.
* **CPE Screening:** Rectal swab (visible feces) cultured on chromogenic agar or tested via rapid PCR (e.g., Cepheid Xpert Carba-R).
* **Candida auris Screening:** Axilla and Groin swabs.

### Environmental Investigation & Outbreak Sampling
* **Environmental Swabs:** Taken from high-touch surfaces (bed rails, call bells, overbed tables) during declared outbreaks.
* **Water Sampling:** Tested for *Legionella* spp. or *Pseudomonas* during specific HA ward outbreaks.
* **Air Sampling:** Utilized to verify negative pressure room efficacy, not routine for diagnosis.
* **Genomic Sequencing:** Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS) of isolates to confirm clonal spread / establish epidemiological linkage during an outbreak.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Immediate First Aid for Needlestick Injury (NSI) (針刺意外)
* **Skin Exposure:** Wash wound immediately with soap and water under running water. **DO NOT** squeeze or rub the wound (forces virus deeper). **DO NOT** use strong antiseptics (bleach/alcohol) on broken skin.
* **Mucosal Exposure (Eyes/Mouth):** Irrigate copiously with sterile saline or tap water.
* **Reporting:** Inform Ward Manager immediately. Consult AED or OESH (Occupational Safety and Health) immediately for risk assessment.
* **Source Tracing:** Draw blood from the source patient (with consent) urgently for HIV Ag/Ab, HBsAg, anti-HCV.

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) Protocols for Healthcare Workers
| Bloodborne Virus | PEP Protocol & Timing | Follow-up & Monitoring |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HIV** | Start within 1-2h (max 72h). Regimen: Tenofovir/Emtricitabine (Truvada) + Raltegravir/Dolutegravir for 28 days. | Test HCW HIV serology at baseline, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months. |
| **Hepatitis B (HBV)** | If HCW unimmunized/non-responder: Give HBIG (Hepatitis B Immune Globulin) STAT + HBV Vaccine series. | Test HBsAb/HBsAg at baseline and 6 months. |
| **Hepatitis C (HCV)** | **No PEP available.** Do not give immunoglobulins or antivirals acutely. | Monitor HCV RNA at 4-6 weeks and anti-HCV at 6 months. Treat if +ve. |

### Acute Ward Outbreak Containment Protocol
* **Contact Tracing:** Identify all contacts (e.g., patients sharing the same cubicle with the index case for >24 hours).
* **Isolation / Cohorting:** Move contacts to a cohort ward. Halt new admissions to the affected cubicle ("close the cubicle").
* **Enhanced Environmental Cleaning:** Use 1,000 ppm hypochlorite (bleach) for daily and terminal cleaning (5,000 ppm for blood spills / C. diff).
* **Hand Hygiene Audit:** Strict surveillance of WHO 5 moments compliance by Infection Control Nursing Officer (ICNO).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Hand Hygiene Protocol (WHO 5 Moments)
* **Moment 1:** Before touching a patient.
* **Moment 2:** Before clean/aseptic procedures.
* **Moment 3:** After body fluid exposure risk.
* **Moment 4:** After touching a patient.
* **Moment 5:** After touching patient surroundings.
* **Modality Choice:** Alcohol-Based Hand Rub (ABHR) is standard. **Must use Soap & Water** when hands are visibly soiled, or when dealing with spore-forming organisms (*Clostridioides difficile*) or non-enveloped viruses (Norovirus).

### Transmission-Based Precautions (傳播途徑防護措施)
| Precaution Type | Indication Examples | Room Placement | PPE Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard** | ALL patients | Normal ward | Gloves/Gown/Mask if fluid risk |
| **Contact** | MRSA, VRE, CPE, Scabies | Single room / Cohort | Gown + Gloves |
| **Contact (Enteric)** | *C. difficile*, Norovirus | Single room + dedicated toilet | Gown + Gloves + Soap/Water Wash |
| **Droplet** | Influenza, N. meningitidis | Single room (>1m spacing) | Surgical Mask |
| **Airborne** | TB, Measles, Varicella | Airborne Infection Isolation Room (AIIR) (-ve pressure) | N95 Respirator (Fit-tested) |

### MDRO Decolonization Protocols
* **MRSA Decolonization:**
  * **Body:** Chlorhexidine gluconate 4% (Hibiclens) body wash daily for 5 days.
  * **Nares:** Mupirocin 2% nasal ointment applied to anterior nares TDS for 5 days.
  * **Hair:** Chlorhexidine shampoo on days 1 and 3.
  * **Post-Rx:** Screen swabs 48h after completion to document clearance.
* **VRE/CPE:** No routine decolonization protocols proven fully effective; management relies on strict contact isolation until multiple negative surveillance swabs.

### Antimicrobial Stewardship Program (ASP) (抗菌藥物導向計劃)
* **Core Principle (The 4 Ds):** Right **D**rug, Right **D**ose, Right **D**uration, Timely **D**e-escalation.
* **Formulary Restriction:** "Big Gun" antibiotics (e.g., Meropenem, Linezolid, Daptomycin, Colistin) require authorization/prescription by Infectious Disease (ID) physicians or Clinical Microbiologists.
* **IV to PO Switch:** Transition to oral therapy once patient is clinically stable and absorbing enterally (e.g., Ciprofloxacin, Linezolid have excellent oral bioavailability).
* **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Cease prophylactic antibiotics within 24 hours post-operatively (single dose usually sufficient).

### Sterilization & Disinfection Policies
* **Critical Items (Enter sterile tissue/vascular system):** E.g., Surgical instruments. Require **Sterilization** (Autoclaving/Steam under pressure → destroys all microbial life including spores).
* **Semi-Critical Items (Contact mucous membranes):** E.g., Endoscopes, laryngoscopes. Require **High-Level Disinfection (HLD)** (e.g., Glutaraldehyde, Ortho-phthalaldehyde).
* **Non-Critical Items (Contact intact skin):** E.g., BP cuffs, stethoscopes. Require **Low-Level Disinfection** (Alcohol wipes).

### Statutory Reporting in Hong Kong
* **Notifiable Diseases (法定須呈報傳染病):** 50+ diseases legally required to be reported to the Centre for Health Protection (CHP) via Central Notification Office (CENO) / NDORS system.
* **Key Examples:** TB, Dengue, Malaria, Measles, Legionnaires' disease, invasive pneumococcal disease, COVID-19, Mpox.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for Needlestick Injury Bloodborne Transmission
* **High-Risk Injury Features:** Hollow-bore needle, deep intramuscular penetration, visible blood on device, needle used in source patient's artery/vein.
* **Average Transmission Risks (Rule of 3s):**
  * HBV: ~30% (if source HBeAg +ve and HCW non-immune)
  * HCV: ~3%
  * HIV: ~0.3% (percutaneous)

### Risk Stratification for CPE Acquisition (HK HA Screening Criteria)
* **High Risk (Mandatory Screening):** Hospitalization outside HK in the past 12 months; Dialysis outside HK; Known contact with CPE patient.
* **Moderate Risk:** Living in OAH with known CPE endemicity.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Healthcare-Associated Infections (HAIs)
* **Systemic:** Sepsis, septic shock, multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS).
* **Healthcare Impact:** Prolonged hospitalization, ↑ morbidity/mortality, ↑ healthcare costs, disruption of planned surgeries/therapies.

### Complications of Antimicrobial Misuse
* **Gastrointestinal:** Microbiome depletion → *Clostridioides difficile* Infection (CDI) (toxic megacolon, pseudomembranous colitis).
* **Renal:** Nephrotoxicity (Aminoglycosides, Vancomycin, Colistin).
* **Ecological:** Selective pressure driving generation of Pan-Drug Resistant (PDR) organisms.

### Complications of Decolonization & PPE
* **Dermatological:** Chlorhexidine-induced irritant contact dermatitis, anaphylaxis (rare).
* **PPE-related:** Facial pressure injuries from prolonged N95 use, heat stress/exhaustion in full PPE gear.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the most critical intervention to prevent the transmission of VRE in the ward environment? Answer: Strict hand hygiene and fastidious environmental cleaning (bleach), as VRE survives for prolonged periods on dry environmental surfaces.
* Trap: Prescribing HIV PEP 5 days after a needlestick injury. Pearl: PEP must be initiated as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours, and is generally not recommended if >72 hours have elapsed since exposure.
* Pitfall: Using alcohol-based hand rub after examining a patient with *Clostridioides difficile*. Alcohol does not kill spores; you must wash hands thoroughly with soap and water to mechanically remove them.
* Viva Question: Which common antibiotics have >90% oral bioavailability, making IV-to-PO switch highly effective? Answer: Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin), Linezolid, Metronidazole, Clindamycin, and Doxycycline.
* Trap: Assuming droplet precautions are sufficient for a patient with open pulmonary Tuberculosis. Pearl: TB requires Airborne Precautions, mandating patient placement in an Airborne Infection Isolation Room (AIIR) and HCWs wearing fit-tested N95 respirators.
* Pitfall: Treating asymptomatic bacteriuria in an elderly patient with a Foley catheter. Avoid treating a positive urine culture in the absence of systemic or local signs of infection (e.g., fever, suprapubic pain, altered mental status), as it represents colonization and promotes resistance.
