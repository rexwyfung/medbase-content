# Parathyroid Disorders (甲狀旁腺疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definitions of Parathyroid Function Abnormalities
* **Primary Hyperparathyroidism (1° HPT) (原發性甲狀旁腺功能亢進):** Autonomous, inappropriate overproduction of Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) by parathyroid glands → hypercalcaemia.
* **Secondary Hyperparathyroidism (2° HPT):** Appropriate ↑PTH compensation in response to chronic hypocalcaemia/hyperphosphataemia (usually CKD/Vit D deficiency).
* **Tertiary Hyperparathyroidism (3° HPT):** Autonomous ↑PTH secretion following prolonged 2° HPT (glands become hyperplastic and unresponsive to serum Ca).
* **Hypoparathyroidism (甲狀旁腺功能減退):** Inadequate PTH production → hypocalcaemia.

### Etiologies of Primary Hyperparathyroidism
* **Parathyroid Adenoma (甲狀旁腺腺瘤):** ~85% (single benign tumour, sporadic).
* **Parathyroid Hyperplasia (甲狀旁腺增生):** ~10-15% (all 4 glands enlarged, often associated with MEN1 or MEN2A).
* **Parathyroid Carcinoma:** <1% (severe hypercalcaemia, palpable neck mass).

### Etiologies of Hypoparathyroidism
* **Iatrogenic:** Most common (post-thyroidectomy, parathyroidectomy, radical neck dissection).
* **Autoimmune:** Autoimmune polyglandular syndrome type 1 (APS-1).
* **Genetic:** DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion → agenesis of parathyroids).
* **Infiltrative:** Haemochromatosis, Wilson's disease, metastasis.
* **Functional (Reversible):** Severe hypomagnesaemia (Mg is required for PTH synthesis & release).

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong Context)
* **1° HPT:** F:M ratio ~3:1, peak incidence 50-60 years. Often detected incidentally on routine HA biochemistry panels.
* **2°/3° HPT:** Highly prevalent in HK due to large endemic ESRF population on long-term dialysis.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Calcium-Sensing Receptor (CaSR) (鈣敏感受體) Physiology
* **Location:** Chief cells of parathyroid gland & renal tubules.
* **Mechanism:** G-protein coupled receptor. Binds ionized Ca²⁺.
* **Effect of ↑Ca²⁺:** Activates CaSR → suppresses PTH secretion & ↑renal Ca excretion.
* **Effect of ↓Ca²⁺:** Deactivates CaSR → ↑PTH secretion.

### Pathophysiology of Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) Actions
* **Bone:** Stimulates osteoblast RANKL → activates osteoclasts → ↑bone resorption (releases Ca & PO4).
* **Kidney (Tubules):** ↑Ca²⁺ reabsorption (distal tubule), ↓PO4 reabsorption (proximal tubule).
* **Kidney (Enzymatic):** Upregulates 1-α-hydroxylase → converts 25-OH-Vit D to 1,25-(OH)₂-Vit D (Calcitriol).
* **Gut:** Calcitriol ↑intestinal Ca & PO4 absorption.

### Pathophysiology of CKD-Mineral Bone Disorder (CKD-MBD)
* ↓GFR → ↓PO4 excretion → hyperphosphataemia.
* ↑PO4 binds serum Ca²⁺ → hypocalcaemia.
* ↓Renal mass → ↓1-α-hydroxylase → ↓active Vit D → ↓gut Ca absorption.
* **Net effect:** Chronic ↓Ca, ↑PO4 → chronic CaSR deactivation → continuous chief cell stimulation → 2° HPT hyperplasia.

### Pathophysiology of Familial Hypocalciuric Hypercalcaemia (FHH) (家族性低尿鈣性高血鈣症)
* Autosomal dominant inactivating mutation in *CaSR* gene.
* **Parathyroid:** "Faulty thermostat" needs ↑↑Ca to suppress PTH (normal/slightly ↑PTH despite hypercalcaemia).
* **Kidney:** Faulty CaSR in tubules → perceives Ca as low → massive Ca reabsorption (hypocalciuria).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### History of Hyperparathyroidism (Hypercalcaemia)
* **General:** Fatigue, lethargy, dehydration (polyuria/polydipsia from nephrogenic DI).
* **Renal ("Stones"):** Hx of nephrolithiasis, renal colic.
* **Skeletal ("Bones"):** Bone pain, pathological fractures, arthralgia (pseudogout).
* **GI ("Abdominal groans"):** Dyspepsia (↑gastrin), constipation, acute pancreatitis, peptic ulcer disease.
* **Neuropsychiatric ("Psychiatric overtones"):** Depression, confusion, memory loss, poor concentration.
* **Syndromic clues:** Personal/family Hx of pituitary/pancreatic tumours (MEN1) or medullary thyroid cancer/phaeochromocytoma (MEN2A).

### Physical Signs of Hypercalcaemia
* **Neurological:** Proximal muscle weakness, hyporeflexia.
* **Ocular:** Band keratopathy (calcium precipitation in cornea - requires slit lamp).
* **Volume status:** Signs of profound dehydration (dry mucous membranes, ↓skin turgor).
* **Neck:** Usually NO palpable mass (adenomas are small). Palpable mass suggests parathyroid carcinoma.

### History of Hypoparathyroidism (Hypocalcaemia)
* **Neurological:** Perioral numbness, distal paraesthesia, muscle cramps, spasms.
* **Past Medical Hx:** Previous total thyroidectomy, neck irradiation.
* **Severe:** Seizures, laryngeal stridor (laryngospasm).

### Physical Signs of Hypocalcaemia
* **Chvostek's Sign:** Tapping facial nerve (anterior to earlobe) → ipsilateral facial muscle twitching.
* **Trousseau's Sign:** Inflate BP cuff > systolic BP for 3 mins → carpopedal spasm (flexion of wrist/MCP joints, extension of IP joints).
* **Neurological:** Hyperreflexia, tetany.
* **Ocular:** Subcapsular cataracts.
* **Dermatological:** Dry skin, brittle nails, alopecia.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnosis of Hypercalcaemia (PTH-Dependent vs Independent)

| Disease Category | Corrected Ca | PTH Level | PO4 Level | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1° HPT** | ↑ | ↑ or Inappropriately high-normal | ↓ or Low-normal | Asymptomatic/stones/bones; high urine Ca. |
| **FHH** | ↑ | Normal/mildly ↑ | Normal | Family Hx; Urine CCCR <0.01; asymptomatic. |
| **Malignancy (PTHrP)** | ↑↑ | ↓↓ (Suppressed) | ↓ | Rapid onset; weight loss; known cancer. |
| **Malignancy (Bone Mets)**| ↑↑ | ↓↓ (Suppressed) | ↑ | Bone pain; osteolytic lesions on imaging. |
| **Vit D Toxicity** | ↑ | ↓↓ (Suppressed) | ↑ | Hx of over-the-counter supplements. |
| **Sarcoidosis/Granuloma**| ↑ | ↓↓ (Suppressed) | Normal/↑ | Ectopic 1-α-hydroxylase; bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy. |
| **Thiazide Diuretics** | ↑ | Normal/mildly ↑ | Normal | Medication Hx; resolves on stopping. |

### Discriminatory Features: Primary HPT vs 2°/3° HPT
* **1° HPT:** High Ca, High PTH.
* **2° HPT:** Low/Normal Ca, High PTH.
* **3° HPT:** High Ca, Extremely High PTH (hx of ESRD/dialysis).

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Bedside & ECG Investigations
* **ECG (Hypercalcaemia):** Shortened QT interval, Osbourne (J) waves in severe cases, arrhythmias.
* **ECG (Hypocalcaemia):** Prolonged QT interval (risk of Torsades de Pointes).

### Blood Tests for Calcium Disorders
* **Bone Profile:** Corrected Calcium (must correct for albumin: Ca + 0.02 × [40 - Albumin]), Phosphate, ALP (↑ indicates active bone turnover).
* **Parathyroid Axis:** Intact PTH (iPTH).
* **Renal & Electrolytes:** Urea, Creatinine, eGFR (assess for CKD), Magnesium (exclude hypomagnesaemia causing hypocalcaemia).
* **Vitamin D:** 25-OH-Cholecalciferol (assess Vit D deficiency).

### Urine Investigations & Diagnostic Criteria
* **24-hour Urine Calcium:** Differentiates 1° HPT (often hypercalciuria) from FHH.
* **Calcium-Creatinine Clearance Ratio (CCCR):** [Urine Ca × Serum Cr] / [Serum Ca × Urine Cr].
* **Diagnostic Criteria for FHH:** CCCR < 0.01.
* **Diagnostic Criteria for 1° HPT:** CCCR > 0.02.

### Imaging for Parathyroid Localisation (Only if surgery indicated)
* **Ultrasound Neck:** Operator-dependent, localizes enlarged glands, assesses concomitant thyroid nodules.
* **99mTc-Sestamibi Scintigraphy (MIBI Scan):** Radiotracer taken up by both thyroid & parathyroid but washes out of thyroid faster. Retained uptake indicates parathyroid adenoma/hyperplasia.
* **4D-CT Neck:** Used in re-operative cases or negative/discordant US/MIBI. Shows contrast wash-in and wash-out kinetics.

### Investigations for End-Organ Damage
* **DEXA Scan:** T-score at lumbar spine, hip, and distal 1/3 radius (cortical bone heavily affected by PTH).
* **KUB X-ray / CT KUB:** Detects nephrolithiasis or nephrocalcinosis.
* **Renal Ultrasound:** Detects nephrocalcinosis.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Resuscitation Protocol for Hypercalcaemic Crisis
* **Definition:** Serum Ca > 3.5 mmol/L or severe symptoms (coma, arrhythmias).
* **Step 1: Aggressive Rehydration (First line)**
  * IV 0.9% Normal Saline (3-6 L/24h) to restore GFR and promote calciuresis.
  * *Caution:* Monitor JVP/lung bases for fluid overload (especially in elderly/HF).
* **Step 2: Calciuresis (Only if fluid overloaded)**
  * IV Furosemide. *Strictly contraindicated until fully rehydrated* (worsens volume depletion).
* **Step 3: Osteoclast Inhibition (Definitive lowering)**
  * IV Bisphosphonates (e.g., Zoledronic acid 4mg or Pamidronate 60-90mg). *Onset: 48-72h*.
* **Step 4: Rapid Temporary Lowering (Adjunct)**
  * SC/IM Calcitonin (e.g., 4 IU/kg Q12H). *Onset: 4-6h. Tachyphylaxis occurs after 48h*.
* **Step 5: Renal Replacement Therapy**
  * Haemodialysis with zero/low-calcium dialysate if anuric, severe severe HF, or refractory.

### Acute Resuscitation for Symptomatic Hypocalcaemia (Tetany)
* **Airway:** Secure airway if laryngospasm suspected.
* **IV Calcium Replacement:**
  * IV 10% Calcium Gluconate (10-20 mL) over 10-20 minutes with continuous ECG monitoring.
  * Followed by IV Calcium Gluconate infusion (e.g., 100 mL of 10% in 1L NS over 24h).
* **Electrolyte Correction:** MUST check and treat hypomagnesaemia (IV MgSO4); calcium will not correct if Mg is low.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Surgical Indications for Asymptomatic 1° HPT (Parathyroidectomy)
* *Note: Symptomatic patients (stones, bone pain, fragility fractures) ALWAYS require surgery.*
* **NIH Guidelines for Asymptomatic 1° HPT:**
  | Parameter | Threshold for Surgery |
  | :--- | :--- |
  | **Age** | < 50 years |
  | **Serum Calcium** | > 0.25 mmol/L (1.0 mg/dL) above upper limit of normal |
  | **Skeletal** | T-score ≤ -2.5 (any site) OR vertebral fracture on imaging |
  | **Renal (Function)** | eGFR < 60 mL/min |
  | **Renal (Stones)** | 24h Urine Ca > 10 mmol/day (400 mg/day) + ↑ stone risk OR nephrocalcinosis/stones on imaging |

### Pharmacological Algorithm for 1° HPT (If Surgery Contraindicated/Refused)
* **Calcimimetics (Cinacalcet - 增鈣敏劑):** Allosteric modulator of CaSR. ↑ sensitivity to Ca → suppresses PTH release. Normalises serum Ca but *does not* improve bone mineral density.
* **Antiresorptives (Bisphosphonates e.g., PO Alendronate):** ↑ bone mineral density, prevents fractures. Does *not* significantly lower serum Ca.
* **Hydration:** Advise copious fluid intake (≥2.5L/day) to prevent stones. Avoid thiazides/lithium.

### Chronic Management of Secondary HPT (CKD-MBD)
* **Step 1: Dietary PO4 Restriction.**
* **Step 2: Phosphate Binders (taken with meals):**
  * Non-calcium based: Sevelamer, Lanthanum (preferred if Ca is high/normal).
  * Calcium based: Calcium carbonate (if Ca is low).
* **Step 3: Active Vitamin D Analogues:**
  * PO Alfacalcidol or Calcitriol to suppress PTH (monitor closely for hypercalcaemia).
* **Step 4: Calcimimetics:**
  * Cinacalcet (used if PTH remains high despite normal/high Ca).
* **Step 5: Parathyroidectomy:**
  * Indicated for refractory severe 3° HPT, severe calciphylaxis, or intractable pruritus.

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Hypoparathyroidism
* **Goal:** Maintain Ca in the *low-normal range* (2.0-2.1 mmol/L) to relieve symptoms but prevent hypercalciuria (lack of PTH means kidneys dump calcium → nephrocalcinosis risk).
* **1st Line (Dual therapy):**
  * PO Calcium supplements (Calcium carbonate/citrate 1-2g elemental Ca daily).
  * PO Active Vitamin D (Alfacalcidol/Calcitriol).
* **Thiazide Diuretics:** Added if hypercalciuria develops (e.g., Hydrochlorothiazide stimulates renal Ca reabsorption).
* **Recombinant human PTH (rhPTH 1-84):** Reserved for refractory cases.

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### End-Organ Complications of Hyperparathyroidism
* **Skeletal:** Osteitis fibrosa cystica, brown tumours (osteoclastomas), osteoporosis, chondrocalcinosis (pseudogout).
* **Renal:** Nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis, progressive CKD, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.
* **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, left ventricular hypertrophy, vascular calcification, short QT arrhythmias.
* **Gastrointestinal:** Pancreatitis, peptic ulcer disease (calcium stimulates gastrin release).

### Complications of Chronic Hypoparathyroidism (and its treatment)
* **Neurological:** Basal ganglia calcification (Fahr's syndrome), extrapyramidal movement disorders, seizures.
* **Ocular:** Early-onset subcapsular cataracts.
* **Iatrogenic Renal Damage:** Nephrocalcinosis/renal failure due to excessive calcium/Vit D supplementation causing hypercalciuria (due to absence of PTH's calcium-retaining effect on tubules).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Prescribing IV Normal Saline AND IV Furosemide simultaneously upon admission for hypercalcaemia. Pearl: Never give loop diuretics until the patient is thoroughly fluid-resuscitated. Furosemide in a dehydrated hypercalcaemic patient will worsen hypovolaemia and paradoxically decrease renal calcium clearance.
* Viva Question: Why do we check urine calcium and CCCR before referring a primary HPT patient for surgery? Answer: To rule out Familial Hypocalciuric Hypercalcaemia (FHH). Operating on FHH is futile as a subtotal parathyroidectomy will not cure the hypercalcaemia.
* Pitfall: Pushing IV Calcium Gluconate too rapidly through a peripheral line. It can cause severe tissue necrosis if extravasation occurs and can precipitate fatal arrhythmias. Give slowly and ideally via a central line if high concentrations are used, with continuous ECG monitoring.
* Viva Question: A CKD patient on dialysis has a corrected Ca of 2.75 mmol/L and an intact PTH 15 times the upper limit of normal. What is the diagnosis? Answer: Tertiary hyperparathyroidism. The parathyroid glands have become autonomously hyperactive and are no longer responding to the negative feedback of hypercalcaemia.
* Trap: Trying to correct hypocalcaemia with massive doses of calcium and Vitamin D without checking other electrolytes. Pearl: Always check Magnesium. Hypomagnesaemia induces functional hypoparathyroidism by inhibiting PTH synthesis, release, and peripheral action. Calcium will not normalize until Magnesium is replaced.
* Viva Question: Which exact sites are measured on a DEXA scan for a patient with primary hyperparathyroidism? Answer: Lumbar spine, hip, and specifically the distal 1/3 of the radius, because PTH preferentially depletes cortical bone (rich in the radius) over trabecular bone.
