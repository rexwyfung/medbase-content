# Bacterial Skin Infections (細菌性皮膚感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Superficial Infections
* **Impetigo (膿皰瘡)**: Superficial epidermal infection; bullous or non-bullous.
* **Ecthyma (深膿皰瘡)**: Deep epidermal infection extending into dermis forming ulcerative lesions.
* **Folliculitis (毛囊炎)**: Superficial inflammation of hair follicles.
* **Furuncle (癤) / Boil**: Deep, walled-off purulent hair follicle infection.
* **Carbuncle (癰)**: Conglomeration of multiple furuncles with subcutaneous spread and multiple drainage sinuses.

### Definition of Deep & Toxin-Mediated Infections
* **Cellulitis (蜂窩組織炎)**: Deep dermal and subcutaneous tissue infection.
* **Erysipelas (丹毒)**: Superficial dermal and upper lymphatic infection.
* **Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome / SSSS (葡萄球菌燙傷樣皮膚綜合徵)**: Systemic toxin-mediated exfoliative dermatitis.
* **Erysipeloid (類丹毒)**: Zoonotic occupational skin infection.

### Etiologies & HK Epidemiology
* **Non-Bullous Impetigo**: *Staphylococcus aureus* (金黃色葡萄球菌) + *Streptococcus pyogenes* / Group A Strep / GAS (A組鏈球菌).
* **Bullous Impetigo**: Exclusively *S. aureus* (phage type 71).
* **Cellulitis/Erysipelas**: Predominantly GAS; occasionally *S. aureus* (especially in IVDU, penetrating trauma).
* **Erysipeloid**: *Erysipelothrix rhusiopathiae* (exposure to raw meat/fish/poultry).
* **Local Epi (HK)**: High prevalence of CA-MRSA (社區型耐甲氧西林金黃色葡萄球菌) in HK community. ↑ Incidence in summer/humid months.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Superficial Tissue Invasion
* **Direct Inoculation**: Minor epidermal breach (scratch, insect bite, tinea pedis) → bacterial entry.
* **Follicular Obstruction**: Maceration/friction + bacterial overgrowth → follicle rupture → dermal inflammatory response (Furuncle).

### Pathophysiology of Cellulitis & Erysipelas
* **Lymphatic Involvement**: GAS invades superficial dermal lymphatics (Erysipelas) → intense local inflammation, vasodilation, dermal edema.
* **Subcutaneous Spread**: Deeper penetration into subcutaneous fat (Cellulitis) → diffuse neutrophilic infiltrate → cytokine release (IL-1, TNF-α) → systemic symptoms (SIRS).

### Pathophysiology of Exfoliative Toxins (SSSS)
* **Toxin Production**: *S. aureus* releases Exfoliative Toxins A & B (ETA, ETB).
* **Target Cleavage**: Toxins act as serine proteases → cleave **Desmoglein-1 (橋粒芯糖蛋白-1)** in stratum granulosum.
* **Mucosal Sparing**: Mucous membranes express Desmoglein-3 (not cleaved by ETA/ETB) → no mucosal lesions.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History for Skin Infections
* **Risk Factors**: DM, immunosuppression, obesity, chronic venous insufficiency, lymphedema, tinea pedis (足癬) (major portal of entry for lower limb cellulitis).
* **Occupational Exposure**: Butchers, fishmongers, abattoir workers (Erysipeloid).
* **Contact History**: Close contacts with similar lesions (Impetigo, MRSA outbreaks in care homes).

### Physical Signs of Impetigo & Ecthyma
* **Non-Bullous Impetigo**: Honey-colored crusted (黃金結痂) plaques on face/extremities.
* **Bullous Impetigo**: Flaccid, fluid-filled bullae → rupture leaving collarette of scale.
* **Ecthyma**: Punched-out ulcers (鑿孔狀潰瘍) covered by necrotic crust with raised violaceous margins.

### Physical Signs of Follicular Infections
* **Folliculitis**: Multiple small <5mm erythematous follicular papules/pustules.
* **Furuncle**: Tender, firm, erythematous nodule centered on a hair follicle, ± central fluctuance/purulence.
* **Carbuncle**: Large, deeply tender inflammatory plaque discharging pus from multiple follicular orifices (fossa).

### Physical Signs of Erysipelas vs Cellulitis
* **Erysipelas (丹毒)**:
  * **Margins**: Sharply demarcated (界線分明), raised borders.
  * **Appearance**: Fiery-red, tense, shiny plaque.
  * **Peau d'orange (橘皮樣外觀)**: Prominent lymphatic edema.
  * **Location**: Lower limbs > Face (butterfly distribution).
* **Cellulitis (蜂窩組織炎)**:
  * **Margins**: Poorly demarcated (界線模糊), fades into surrounding skin.
  * **Appearance**: Deep erythema, warmth, severe tenderness, induration.
  * **Location**: Unilateral lower limb (bilateral is exceptionally rare, usually venous stasis).

### Physical Signs of SSSS & Erysipeloid
* **SSSS**:
  * Diffuse blanching erythema (starts flexural/periorificial) → flaccid blisters.
  * **Nikolsky Sign (尼科斯基徵) +ve**: Gentle lateral pressure separates epidermis.
  * **Mucosa**: Spared (no oral/conjunctival lesions).
* **Erysipeloid**: Violaceous (紫紅色), well-demarcated plaque on hands/web spaces; distinct lack of suppuration/pus.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table: Acute Red Leg Syndromes
| Feature | Cellulitis | Venous Stasis Dermatitis | Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) | Contact Dermatitis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Laterality** | Strictly Unilateral | Usually Bilateral | Unilateral | Varies (site of contact) |
| **Onset** | Acute (hours-days) | Chronic/Insidious | Acute/Subacute | Acute |
| **Systemic Features** | Fever, chills, tachycardia | Absent | Absent (unless PE) | Absent |
| **Inflammatory Signs** | Intense heat, severe pain | Mild warmth, itchy | Swelling prominent, mild red | Intensely itchy, vesicular |
| **Response to Elevation** | Minimal acute change | Redness fades significantly | Minimal change | Minimal change |

### Differentials for SSSS
* **Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) (中毒性表皮壞死鬆解症)**: Deep cleavage (dermo-epidermal junction), mucosal involvement prominent, drug-induced.
* **Pemphigus Vulgaris (尋常型天皰瘡)**: Autoimmune, mucosal involvement, adults.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Skin Swab**: Culture & Sensitivity (C&S) for exudate, pus, or ruptured bullae.
* **Skin Marking**: Draw line around erythematous border with skin marker (assess progression/regression).
* **Wood's Lamp (伍德氏燈)**: -ve in bacterial (used to rule out erythrasma → coral red fluorescence).

### Blood Investigations
* **CBC**: Leukocytosis with neutrophilic left shift.
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ↑ CRP, ↑ ESR (baseline for monitoring).
* **Renal/Liver Function Tests**: U&E (assess baseline before high-dose IV Abx), LFTs.
* **Blood Cultures**: Mandatory if systemic toxicity, high fever, shivering, or Eron Class III/IV.

### Imaging Investigations
* **Bedside Ultrasonography (POCUS)**:
  * Soft tissue: "Cobblestone" appearance (卵石徵) in cellulitis.
  * Rule out: Underlying abscess (hypoechoic collection) or DVT (venous compressibility).
* **Plain Radiograph (X-ray)**: Rule out underlying osteomyelitis or subcutaneous gas (if gas present → suspect necrotizing fasciitis).

### Diagnostic Criteria for Admission (Cellulitis/Erysipelas)
* **Indications for Hospital Admission**:
  * Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS).
  * Rapidly spreading erythema.
  * Significant comorbidities (poorly controlled DM, immunosuppression).
  * Failure of outpatient oral antibiotics after 48-72 hours.
  * Facial/periorbital involvement.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Cellulitis & Erysipelas (Inpatient)
* **Initial Resuscitation**: Sepsis 6 protocol if hypotensive (IV fluids, O2, draw bloods).
* **Limb Elevation**: Strictly elevate affected limb above heart level (reduces edema, speeds recovery).
* **Empiric IV Antibiotics (HK HA Formulary)**:
  * Standard: IV **Cloxacillin** + IV **Penicillin G** (or IV **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate / Augmentin**).
  * Penicillin-allergic: IV **Cefazolin** (if mild allergy) or IV **Clindamycin** / IV **Vancomycin**.
* **Transition to Oral**: Switch to oral Flucloxacillin or Augmentin once afebrile for 24-48h and erythema regressing.

### Acute Management of Furuncles/Carbuncles/Abscesses
* **Surgical**: **Incision & Drainage (I&D) (切開引流術)** is the definitive primary treatment.
* **Adjunct Antibiotics**: Only if surrounding cellulitis, systemic signs, or immunocompromised.

### Acute Management of SSSS
* **IV Antibiotics**: IV **Cloxacillin** to eradicate toxigenic *S. aureus*.
* **Supportive Care**: Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation (insensible skin losses).
* **Wound Care**: Non-adherent dressings, bland emollients. NO topical corticosteroids.
* **Analgesia**: IV paracetamol/opioids (painful condition).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Outpatient Antibiotic Algorithm for Mild Infections
* **Localized Impetigo**:
  * Topical **Mupirocin 2%** ointment or Fusidic acid cream (TDS for 5-7 days).
* **Widespread Impetigo / Ecthyma / Mild Cellulitis**:
  * Oral **Flucloxacillin** (or Cephalexin) for 7-10 days.
  * Penicillin-allergic: Oral Clarithromycin or Clindamycin.
* **Suspected CA-MRSA (Abscess/Furunculosis)**:
  * Oral **Doxycycline**, **TMP-SMX (Bactrim)**, or **Clindamycin** (based on local HA antibiogram).

### Management Algorithm for Recurrent Cellulitis
* **Step 1: Eradicate Portals of Entry**:
  * Aggressive treatment of Tinea Pedis (topical Terbinafine / Clotrimazole).
  * Treat dry skin with heavy emollients.
* **Step 2: Edema Management**:
  * Prescribe graduated compression stockings (TED hose) for chronic venous stasis (once acute infection resolves).
* **Step 3: Pharmacological Prophylaxis**:
  * Indication: ≥ 3-4 episodes of cellulitis per year.
  * Therapy: Long-term oral **Penicillin V** 250mg BD.

### MRSA Decolonization Protocol (For Recurrent MRSA Infections)
* **Nasal Carriage**: Topical **Mupirocin 2%** ointment to anterior nares BD for 5 days.
* **Body Wash**: **Chlorhexidine (Hibiclens)** 4% body wash daily for 5 days.
* **Environmental**: Dilute bleach baths, daily washing of towels/bedding in hot water.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Eron Classification for Cellulitis
| Class | Clinical Description | Management Disposition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Class I** | No systemic toxicity, no uncontrolled comorbidities. | Outpatient oral antibiotics. |
| **Class II** | Systemic illness OR poorly controlled comorbidity (DM, peripheral vascular disease). | Outpatient parenteral therapy (OPAT) or short admission. |
| **Class III** | Significant systemic toxicity (tachycardia, tachypnea, hypotension) OR limb-threatening. | Urgent hospital admission (IV antibiotics). |
| **Class IV** | Sepsis syndrome / severe necrotizing infection. | ICU admission & urgent surgical consult. |

### Prognosis of Bacterial Skin Infections
* **Cellulitis/Erysipelas**: Excellent with prompt Abx; 20-30% recurrence rate if underlying edema/tinea not managed.
* **SSSS Mortality**:
  * Children: <5% (excellent prognosis, heals without scarring due to superficial cleavage).
  * Adults: Up to 60% (usually secondary to underlying immunosuppression, renal failure, or overwhelming sepsis).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Infectious Complications
* **Renal**: **Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)**. Complicates Strep impetigo. *Note: Acute Rheumatic Fever (ARF) does NOT follow skin GAS infections, only pharyngitis.*
* **Systemic Spread**: Bacteremia, infective endocarditis, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis.
* **Toxin-Mediated**: Streptococcal or Staphylococcal Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS) → multiorgan failure.

### Local Structural Complications
* **Lymphedema**: Recurrent episodes of cellulitis destroy lymphatic channels → chronic irreversible swelling (Elephantiasis nostras verrucosa).
* **Abscess Formation**: Loculation of pus requiring surgical intervention.

### Drug Adverse Effects (Antibiotics)
* **Penicillins/Cephalosporins**: Type I hypersensitivity (Anaphylaxis), Type IV (Maculopapular rash), interstitial nephritis.
* **Clindamycin**: High risk of *Clostridioides difficile*-associated pseudomembranous colitis.
* **Doxycycline**: Photosensitivity, esophagitis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Bilateral red legs in an elderly patient is assumed to be bilateral cellulitis. Pearl: True bilateral cellulitis is exceptionally rare; bilateral red legs almost always indicate venous stasis dermatitis or lipodermatosclerosis.
* Pitfall: Swabbing unbroken skin in cellulitis. The yield is near zero; only swab exudate, pus, or open ulcers. Blood cultures are the appropriate micro test for systemic cellulitis.
* Trap: Treating an isolated, large fluctuant abscess with antibiotics alone. Pearl: The primary treatment for an abscess is Incision and Drainage (I&D). Antibiotics cannot penetrate walled-off collections.
* Viva Question: Why doesn't Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS) affect the mucous membranes? Answer: SSSS toxins target Desmoglein-1 (found in the superficial epidermis). Mucous membranes express Desmoglein-3, which is unaffected by the toxins.
* Viva Question: Can treating Streptococcal impetigo with antibiotics prevent Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)? Answer: No. Antibiotics prevent transmission and speed healing but do not alter the risk of developing PSGN (unlike Acute Rheumatic Fever, where early antibiotics for pharyngitis do prevent ARF).
* Pitfall: Discharging a recurrent lower limb cellulitis patient without examining between their toes. Always check for and aggressively treat Tinea pedis, as the maceration provides the primary portal of entry for GAS.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate erysipelas from cellulitis at the bedside? Answer: Erysipelas has sharply demarcated, raised edges and is fiery red due to superficial lymphatic involvement. Cellulitis is deeper, with poorly defined margins blending into normal skin.
