# Non-Fermenting Gram-Negative Rods (非發酵性革蘭氏陰性桿菌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Non-Fermenting Gram-Negative Rods
* Group of aerobic, non-spore-forming Gram-negative bacilli (GNB).
* Cannot catabolize glucose via fermentation (distinguishes them from Enterobacteriaceae like *E. coli* or *Klebsiella*).
* Opportunistic pathogens → thrive in moist environments (sinks, ventilators, respiratory equipment).

### Core Pathogens in this Class
* *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (綠膿桿菌)
* *Acinetobacter baumannii* (鮑氏不動桿菌)
* *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia* (嗜麥芽窄食單胞菌)
* *Burkholderia cepacia* complex (洋蔥伯克氏菌)
* *Burkholderia pseudomallei* (類鼻疽伯克氏菌) 

### Hong Kong Hospital Authority (HA) Epidemiology
* High prevalence of Nosocomial Infections (院內感染) in ICUs.
* **MDR-PA** (Multidrug-resistant *P. aeruginosa*) & **MDRA** (Multidrug-resistant *A. baumannii*) are endemic in HK HA ICUs.
* Major causes of Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP), Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infections (CLABSI), and severe burns/wound infections.
* *S. maltophilia* incidence ↑ locally due to widespread Carbapenem (碳青黴烯) use.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Pseudomonas aeruginosa
* **Biofilm Production:** Alginate secretion → protects colonies from phagocytosis & Abx (critical in prosthetic joints/catheters).
* **Exotoxin A:** Inactivates Elongation Factor 2 (EF-2) via ADP-ribosylation → halts host protein synthesis → cell death.
* **Pigments:** Pyocyanin (generates reactive oxygen species → tissue damage) & Pyoverdine (siderophore, binds iron).
* **Enzymes:** Elastase (degrades elastin/collagen → vessel damage → Ecthyma gangrenosum) & Phospholipase C (degrades cell membranes).

### Pathophysiology of Acinetobacter baumannii
* **Environmental Persistence:** Highly resistant to desiccation & common hospital disinfectants → survives weeks on dry inanimate surfaces.
* **OmpA (Outer membrane protein A):** Mediates adherence to epithelial cells and induces apoptosis.
* **Capsule:** Prevents complement activation.

### Intrinsic Antimicrobial Resistance Mechanisms
* **Efflux Pumps:** Upregulation actively extrudes beta-lactams, fluoroquinolones, and aminoglycosides (common in *Pseudomonas*).
* **Porin Mutations:** Downregulation/loss of OprD porin → blocks Carbapenem entry.
* **Beta-lactamases:** 
  * Chromosomal AmpC (inducible cephalosporinase).
  * Metallo-beta-lactamases (MBLs) → intrinsic to *S. maltophilia*, hydrolyzes all carbapenems (except Monobactams).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### High-Risk History for Non-Fermenter Infections
* Recent prolonged ICU stay or mechanical ventilation >48 hrs.
* Prior broad-spectrum Abx exposure within 90 days.
* Indwelling devices (CVP lines, Foley catheters, ETT).
* Structural lung disease (Bronchiectasis).
* Severe thermal burns or neutropenia (Absolute Neutrophil Count <0.5).
* Hot tub use or contact lens wear (*Pseudomonas*).

### Physical Signs of Pseudomonas aeruginosa Infection
* **Dermatological:** Ecthyma gangrenosum (綠膿桿菌性壞疽) → painless, necrotic, hemorrhagic vesicles/bullae evolving into black eschars with surrounding erythema.
* **Wounds/Burns:** Blue-green purulent discharge, distinct sweet/fruity/grape-like odor.
* **HEENT:** Malignant Otitis Externa (惡性外耳炎) → severe otalgia, purulent otorrhea, temporal bone tenderness, cranial nerve palsies (CN VII, IX, X, XI).
* **Ophthalmological:** Corneal ulceration → rapidly progressive, gray-white opacity in contact lens wearers.

### Physical Signs of Acinetobacter & Stenotrophomonas
* Primarily present as undifferentiated severe sepsis/septic shock or VAP.
* Signs of VAP: Purulent endotracheal secretions, ↑ work of breathing, fever, coarse crepitations on auscultation.
* Puncture site/wound infections: Induration, cellulitis progressing to fulminant necrosis without specific pathognomonic odors/colors.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### VAP & Healthcare-Associated Pneumonia Differentials
| Pathogen | Discriminatory Clinical/Epidemiological Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **P. aeruginosa** | Structural lung disease, late-onset VAP, neutropenia. |
| **A. baumannii** | Outbreak in ICU, environmental contamination, extreme MDR. |
| **S. maltophilia** | Recent prolonged Carbapenem therapy. |
| **MRSA** | Post-viral (Influenza) pneumonia, cavitating lesions, IV drug use. |
| **Klebsiella pneumoniae** | Currant jelly sputum, background of diabetes/alcoholism. |

### Ecthyma Gangrenosum Differentials
* **Aspergillus species:** Angioinvasive, necrotic skin lesions in profoundly immunosuppressed; requires biopsy for hyphae.
* **Vibrio vulnificus:** Hemorrhagic bullae, seawater exposure, liver cirrhosis.
* **Aeromonas hydrophila:** Freshwater exposure, necrotizing fasciitis.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Microscopic Staining
* **Gram Stain:** Non-fermenters appear as Gram-negative bacilli (pink rods). *Acinetobacter* may appear as pleomorphic coccobacilli (often mistaken for *Neisseria*).
* **Oxidase Test:** 
  * *P. aeruginosa*: Oxidase +ve.
  * *A. baumannii*: Oxidase -ve.
  * *S. maltophilia*: Oxidase -ve.
  * *B. cepacia*: Weakly Oxidase +ve or -ve.

### Bloods & Microbiological Cultures
* **Blood Cultures:** Minimum 2 sets (aerobic & anaerobic) prior to Abx.
* **Culture Media:** 
  * MacConkey Agar: Non-lactose fermenting (colorless colonies).
  * Cetrimide Agar: Selective for *Pseudomonas* (fluorescent green/yellow colonies under UV).
* **Susceptibility Testing (CRITICAL):** Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) via broth microdilution or E-test to guide therapy.

### Respiratory & Fluid Diagnostics
* **Endotracheal Aspirate (ETA):** Quantitative culture (>10^6 CFU/mL indicates infection vs colonization).
* **Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL):** Bronchoscopic sampling (>10^4 CFU/mL indicates VAP).
* **Pleural Fluid:** pH <7.2, low glucose, ↑ LDH (exudate criteria), sent for Gram stain & culture.

### Radiological Imaging
* **CXR / HRCT Thorax:** Patchy bilateral infiltrates, consolidation, or cavitations (common in necrotizing *Pseudomonas* pneumonia).
* **CT Temporal Bone:** Essential for diagnosing/staging Malignant Otitis Externa (bony erosions, skull base osteomyelitis).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Empiric Treatment for Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Anti-Pseudomonal Agents)
* **Beta-Lactams:** Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin) OR Ceftazidime OR Cefepime OR Meropenem/Imipenem.
* **Double Coverage Rationale:** Use Beta-lactam + Aminoglycoside (Amikacin/Gentamicin) OR Fluoroquinolone (Ciprofloxacin/Levofloxacin) ONLY empiricly in severe shock/neutropenia until sensitivities return.

### Empiric Treatment for Acinetobacter baumannii
* **1st Line (if susceptible):** Ampicillin-Sulbactam (Sulbactam has intrinsic activity against *Acinetobacter*).
* **Carbapenem-Resistant A. baumannii (CRAB):** 
  * Targeted Rx: Colistin (多粘菌素) IV +/- inhaled (for VAP).
  * Alternative: Tigecycline IV OR Cefiderocol.

### Empiric Treatment for Stenotrophomonas & Burkholderia
* **S. maltophilia:** 
  * **1st Line:** High-dose Co-trimoxazole (Bactrim) IV.
  * **Alternative:** Levofloxacin OR Minocycline.
  * *Never use Carbapenems (intrinsic resistance via MBLs).*
* **B. cepacia:** Co-trimoxazole OR Meropenem OR Ceftazidime.

### Hemodynamic Resuscitation in Septic Shock
* **Fluid Challenge:** IV crystalloids 30 mL/kg within 1st 3 hours.
* **Vasopressors:** Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine) via central line to target MAP ≥65 mmHg.
* **Source Control:** Immediate removal of infected CVP lines, debridement of burn eschars, drainage of abscesses.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### De-escalation & Pharmacological Step-Down
* **Targeted Monotherapy:** Once susceptibilities are known, immediately de-escalate double-coverage to a single effective beta-lactam.
* **IV to PO Switch:** Transition to oral Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, or Co-trimoxazole (highly bioavailable) once hemodynamically stable, afebrile for 48h, and absorbing enteral feeds.
* **Duration:** 7-8 days for VAP; up to 14 days for CLABSI; 6-8 weeks for Malignant Otitis Externa/Osteomyelitis.

### HA Infection Control & Eradication Protocols
* **Contact Precautions:** Strict isolation/cohorting for MDRA, MDRPA, and Carbapenemase-Producing Enterobacteriaceae (CPE).
* **Environmental Decontamination:** Enhanced terminal cleaning with hypochlorite or hydrogen peroxide vapor for rooms occupied by *Acinetobacter* patients.
* **Device Stewardship:** Daily review & prompt removal of unnecessary Foley catheters, CVCs, and prompt ventilator weaning.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Factors for MDR Non-Fermenter Acquisition
| Risk Factor Category | Specific Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Healthcare Exposure** | Hospitalization >5 days, ICU admission, residence in long-term care facility. |
| **Prior Therapeutics** | IV antibiotics in the preceding 90 days, prolonged corticosteroid use. |
| **Patient Comorbidities** | Structural lung disease, ARDS, hemodialysis, profound immunosuppression. |

### Clinical Pulmonary Infection Score (CPIS) for VAP
* Used to predict VAP likelihood & guide early Abx discontinuation.
* Parameters: Temperature, Blood leukocytes, Tracheal secretions, PaO2/FiO2 ratio, CXR infiltrates.
* Score >6 correlates highly with active VAP (guides initiation of anti-pseudomonal coverage).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Pathogen-Mediated End-Organ Complications
* **Pulmonary:** ARDS, permanent lung function decline (bronchiectasis progression), empyema.
* **Cardiovascular:** Right-sided infective endocarditis (IV drug users with *Pseudomonas*), mycotic aneurysms.
* **Skin/Soft Tissue:** Extensive flap/graft failure in burn patients.

### Iatrogenic & Drug-Induced Adverse Effects
* **Aminoglycosides (Amikacin/Gentamicin):** Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) leading to Acute Kidney Injury (AKI); irreversible sensorineural hearing loss & vestibular toxicity. Requires therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM).
* **Colistin (Polymyxin E):** Severe, dose-dependent Nephrotoxicity & Neurotoxicity (paresthesia, neuromuscular blockade).
* **Piperacillin-Tazobactam + Vancomycin:** Synergistic nephrotoxicity (↑ AKI risk compared to either agent alone or Cefepime + Vanc).
* **Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin):** QTc prolongation, tendon rupture, *C. difficile* infection risk.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why is Ertapenem NOT used for empiric coverage of suspected hospital-acquired pneumonia? Answer: Ertapenem (unlike Meropenem and Imipenem) lacks intrinsic activity against Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Acinetobacter species (APE mnemonic: Acinetobacter, Pseudomonas, Enterococcus are not covered).
* Viva Question: A patient on prolonged Meropenem for an intra-abdominal infection develops a new VAP. Sputum grows a Gram-negative bacillus. What is the most likely non-fermenting organism? Answer: Stenotrophomonas maltophilia, as it possesses intrinsic metallo-beta-lactamases making it resistant to carbapenems, allowing it to opportunistically overgrow.
* Trap: Assuming Ampicillin-Sulbactam is only effective against Gram-positive and anaerobic organisms. Pearl: The Sulbactam component has direct intrinsic bactericidal activity against Acinetobacter baumannii, making high-dose Amp-Sulbactam a first-line agent.
* Pitfall: Continuing double anti-pseudomonal coverage (e.g., Pip-Tazo + Amikacin) for the entire 7-day course of VAP treatment. Pearl: Double coverage is strictly for empiric therapy to increase the probability of initial active coverage; once sensitivities are known, strictly de-escalate to monotherapy to prevent nephrotoxicity.
* Pitfall: Treating an asymptomatic catheterized patient whose urine culture grows Pseudomonas. Pearl: Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria or simple colonization; removal or exchange of the Foley catheter is the definitive management unless the patient shows systemic signs of infection.
