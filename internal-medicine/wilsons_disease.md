# Wilson's Disease (威爾遜症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Wilson's Disease
* **Definition**: Rare autosomal recessive disorder of systemic copper metabolism causing pathological copper accumulation in liver, brain, cornea, and other organs.
* **Alternative Name**: Hepatolenticular Degeneration (肝豆狀核變性).

### Genetic Etiology and Inheritance Pattern
* **Gene**: Mutations in **ATP7B** gene on chromosome 13.
* **Inheritance**: Autosomal Recessive (AR).
* **Physiologic Role**: *ATP7B* codes for a transmembrane copper-transporting ATPase required for hepatic copper excretion into bile and incorporation into **Ceruloplasmin (血漿銅藍蛋白)**.

### Hong Kong Epidemiology and Demographics
* **Prevalence**: ~1 in 30,000 globally; higher prevalence in Asian populations.
* **Age of Onset**: Classic presentation between ages 5 and 35 (Hepatic usually <20 yrs; Neurological usually >20 yrs).
* **Local Genetics**: The **Arg778Leu (R778L)** mutation is the most prevalent *ATP7B* mutation among Southern Chinese/Hong Kong patients.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### ATP7B Mutation and Hepatic Copper Accumulation
* **Primary Defect**: Non-functional ATP7B ATPase in hepatocytes → impaired copper excretion into biliary canaliculi + failure to link copper to apoceruloplasmin.
* **Intracellular Toxicity**: Free copper accumulates in hepatocyte cytoplasm and mitochondria.
* **Oxidative Stress**: Free Cu generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) via **Fenton reaction** → widespread hepatocyte necrosis and apoptosis → progressive fibrosis/Cirrhosis (肝硬化).

### Extrahepatic Systemic Dissemination
* **Spillover Effect**: Hepatocyte death triggers sudden release of massive unconjugated, free (non-ceruloplasmin-bound) copper into systemic circulation.
* **Neurotoxicity**: Cu crosses blood-brain barrier → preferentially deposits in **Basal Ganglia (putamen & globus pallidus)** → neuronal loss and astrogliosis → extrapyramidal symptoms.
* **Corneal Deposition**: Cu deposits in **Descemet's membrane** of the cornea → Kayser-Fleischer (KF) rings.
* **Erythrocyte Lysis**: Sudden circulating Cu spikes cause direct oxidative damage to RBC membranes → acute Coombs -ve intravascular hemolysis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Clues for Copper Overload
* **Family History**: Consanguinity, siblings with unexplained chronic liver disease (CLD) or early-onset movement disorders.
* **Symptom Onset**: Unexplained fatigue, jaundice flares, behavioral changes, deteriorating school/work performance, or new-onset clumsiness.

### Hepatic Physical Signs
* **Acute/Fulminant**: Deep jaundice, hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis), tender hepatomegaly.
* **Chronic (Stigmata of CLD)**: Spider naevi, palmar erythema, caput medusae, splenomegaly, ascites, peripheral edema.

### Neurological Physical Signs
* **Movement Disorders**: 
  * **Parkinsonism**: Bradykinesia, cogwheel rigidity, masked facies.
  * **Tremors**: Resting tremor, intention tremor, characteristic **"Wing-beating" tremor** (proximal, coarse tremor exacerbated by sustained posture with arms abducted).
* **Bulbar/Motor**: Dysarthria (slurred speech), dysphagia, drooling (sialorrhea), severe dystonia, choreoathetosis.

### Psychiatric Physical Signs
* **Cognitive/Behavioral**: Disinhibition, emotional lability, impulsivity, declining academic performance.
* **Affective**: Major depression, overt psychosis, schizophrenia-like features.

### Ophthalmic & Dermatologic Physical Signs
* **Cornea**: **Kayser-Fleischer (KF) rings (凱瑟-弗萊舍環)** (golden-brown/copper pigmentation at the limbus/periphery of cornea).
* **Lens**: **Sunflower cataracts (向日葵白內障)** (spoke-like deposits).
* **Nails**: Azure lunulae (blue discoloration at nail base).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Differentials for Wilson's Disease
| Differential Diagnosis | Distinguishing Clinical & Pathological Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH)** | Prominent +ve ANA, ASMA, Anti-LKM1; hypergammaglobulinemia (↑ IgG); normal ceruloplasmin; rapid response to steroids. |
| **Viral Hepatitis (HBV/HCV)** | +ve HBsAg, HCV RNA; lack of neuro-psychiatric features; lack of KF rings; common in HK (endemic HBV). |
| **Early-Onset Parkinson's** | Age usually >40; purely neuro/motor without liver impairment; good initial response to Levodopa; normal Cu studies. |
| **Essential Tremor** | Postural/action tremor (usually hands/head); +ve FHx (Autosomal Dominant); no rigidity/bradykinesia; normal liver/Cu profiles. |
| **Hemochromatosis** | Bronze skin, diabetes mellitus (Bronze Diabetes), arthropathy, cardiomyopathy; ↑ Ferritin, ↑ Transferrin saturation; older age. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Ophthalmology Consult**: **Slit-lamp examination** (gold standard to confirm KF rings, invisible to naked eye early on).

### Blood & Urine Investigations
* **Copper Studies**:
  * **Serum Ceruloplasmin**: ↓ markedly (<0.2 g/L). *Note: acute-phase reactant, can be falsely normal in severe inflammation.*
  * **24-hour Urine Copper**: ↑ markedly (>100 µg/24h or >1.6 µmol/24h) in symptomatic patients.
  * **Serum Free Copper**: ↑ (Total serum Cu - [Ceruloplasmin × 3]).
  * **Total Serum Copper**: ↓ (Paradoxical, due to drastically ↓ ceruloplasmin which normally carries 90% of blood Cu).
* **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**: ↑ AST & ALT. **AST:ALT ratio often >2** (similar to alcoholic liver disease). ↑ Bilirubin, ↑ ALP (but typically **ALP:Bilirubin ratio <4**, highly specific for WD in acute liver failure).
* **Hematology**: ↓ Hb, ↑ Reticulocytes, ↑ LDH, ↓ Haptoglobin, Coombs -ve (Hemolytic crisis).

### Imaging & Biopsy Investigations
* **MRI Brain**: Hyperintensities in basal ganglia (T2-weighted). Pathognomonic **"Face of the giant panda" sign** in midbrain.
* **Liver Biopsy**: Gold standard for ambiguous cases.
  * **Histology**: Micro/macrovesicular steatosis, glycogenated nuclei, bridging fibrosis.
  * **Quantitative Hepatic Copper**: **>250 µg/g dry weight** (diagnostic).
  * **Stains**: Rhodanine or Orcein stains (highlight Cu deposits).
* **Genetics**: *ATP7B* mutation analysis (confirms Dx, useful for screening first-degree relatives).

### Diagnostic Criteria for Wilson's Disease (Leipzig Score)
| Parameter | Findings & Score |
| :--- | :--- |
| **KF Rings** | Present (+2), Absent (0) |
| **Neurological Symptoms** | Severe (+2), Mild (+1), Absent (0) |
| **Serum Ceruloplasmin** | <0.1 g/L (+2), 0.1-0.2 g/L (+1), Normal (0) |
| **Coombs -ve Hemolysis** | Present (+1), Absent (0) |
| **Liver Copper (µg/g dry wt)** | >250 (+2), 50-250 (+1), Normal (0) |
| **Urine Copper (baseline)** | >2x ULN (+2), 1-2x ULN (+1), Normal (0) |
| **Mutation Analysis** | 2 mutations (+4), 1 mutation (+1), None (0) |
| **Diagnosis Threshold** | **≥4 points = Definite WD**; 3 points = Probable (needs more tests); ≤2 points = Unlikely |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Liver Failure (ALF) Resuscitation Pathway
* **Criteria**: Rapid onset encephalopathy, coagulopathy (INR >1.5), deep jaundice.
* **Airway/Breathing**: Intubate if GCS <8 due to encephalopathy.
* **Medical Bridging**: 
  * **Plasmapheresis / Continuous Veno-Venous Hemodiafiltration (CVVHDF)**: Rapidly clears systemic free copper to stabilize hemodynamics and limit hemolysis/renal failure.
  * Avoid heavy chelation (Penicillamine) in severe ALF → can worsen renal function.
* **Definitive Rx**: Urgent referral to Queen Mary Hospital (HK's transplant center) for **Orthotopic Liver Transplantation (OLT)**. OLT is curative for hepatic defect.

### Hemolytic Crisis Management
* **Aggressive Hydration**: IV crystalloids to maintain urine output and prevent pigment nephropathy (Cu-induced acute tubular necrosis).
* **Transfusion**: Packed RBCs for severe symptomatic anemia.
* **Note**: Hemolysis usually ceases once plasmapheresis or transplantation corrects the free copper overload.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Pharmacological Step-Up Algorithm (Hong Kong HA Context)
| Phase / Drug | Mechanism | Indication & HA Prescribing Context | High-Yield Side Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. D-Penicillamine (D-青黴胺)** | Systemic copper chelator → promotes renal excretion. | First-line in symptomatic patients (Hepatic/Neuro). HA standard formulary. Must co-administer with **Pyridoxine (Vit B6)** to prevent neuropathy. | Early worsening of neuro symptoms (10-20%), Lupus-like syndrome, nephrotic syndrome, bone marrow suppression. |
| **2. Trientine** | Alternative chelator. | Used if intolerant to Penicillamine or if neuro worsening occurs. (Requires special HA approval/named patient). | Sideroblastic anemia, milder rash/cytopenia than Penicillamine. |
| **3. Zinc Salts (e.g., Zinc Acetate/Sulfate)** | Induces metallothionein in enterocytes → traps dietary Cu in gut → excreted in feces. | Maintenance therapy after initial de-coppering OR first-line for presymptomatic patients. | GI upset (gastritis, nausea). Do NOT give simultaneously with chelators (separately by >1 hr). |

### Dietary and Lifestyle Modifications
* **Lifelong Low Copper Diet**: 
  * **Strictly Avoid**: Shellfish, organ meats (liver), nuts, chocolate, mushrooms.
* **Water Supply**: Test well-water or old copper plumbing; use water filters.
* **Monitoring Compliance**: Check 24-hour urine copper output (should ↑ initially with chelation, then stabilize), LFTs, and CBC every 3-6 months.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Revised Wilson Index (King's College Criteria for WD ALF)
| Parameter | Score 0 | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 | Score 4 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bilirubin (µmol/L)** | <100 | 100 - 150 | 151 - 200 | 201 - 300 | >300 |
| **AST (U/L)** | <100 | 100 - 150 | 151 - 300 | 301 - 400 | >400 |
| **INR** | <1.3 | 1.3 - 1.6 | 1.7 - 1.9 | 2.0 - 2.4 | >2.4 |
| **WBC (×10^9/L)** | <6.7 | 6.8 - 8.3 | 8.4 - 10.3 | 10.4 - 15.3 | >15.3 |
| **Albumin (g/L)** | >45 | 34 - 44 | 25 - 33 | 21 - 24 | <21 |
* **Interpretation**: **Score ≥ 11** predicts mortality without liver transplantation (100% specificity and sensitivity for fatality) → immediate listing for Tx.

### Long-term Prognostic Trajectory
* **Compliant Patients**: Normal life expectancy if diagnosed and treated before irreversible cirrhosis or severe basal ganglia necrosis occurs.
* **Non-Compliant/Undiagnosed**: Progresses inevitably to fatal ALF or severe debilitating neurological sequelae (anarthria, contractures).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications
* **Hepatic**: Decompensated Cirrhosis, Portal Hypertension, bleeding varices. *Note: Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) risk is elevated but remarkably lower than in Hemochromatosis.*
* **Neurological**: Irreversible spasticity, contractures, permanent cognitive decline.
* **Renal**: Copper toxicity to proximal tubules → **Fanconi Syndrome** (aminoaciduria, glycosuria, phosphaturia), Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA), nephrolithiasis.
* **Musculoskeletal**: Premature osteoarthritis, osteoporosis, osteomalacia (secondary to renal phosphate loss).

### Pharmacological Adverse Effects
* **D-Penicillamine Toxicity**:
  * **Immunologic**: Drug-induced Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), Myasthenia gravis, Goodpasture syndrome.
  * **Dermatologic**: Elastosis perforans serpiginosa (skin lesion resembling pluckable chicken skin).
  * **Neurologic**: "Paradoxical worsening" of neuro symptoms rapidly after initiation (due to mobilization of hepatic Cu into the bloodstream/brain).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the classic LFT pattern suggesting Wilson's Disease in acute liver failure? Answer: An ALP to Total Bilirubin ratio of < 4 and an AST to ALT ratio > 2.
* Trap: Assuming a normal serum ceruloplasmin rules out Wilson's disease. Pearl: Ceruloplasmin is an acute-phase reactant and can be falsely elevated into the normal range during active hepatic inflammation or pregnancy.
* Pitfall: Starting high-dose D-Penicillamine in a patient with severe neurological Wilson's disease. This can cause irreversible paradoxical worsening of neurological symptoms. Start slow or use Trientine/Zinc instead.
* Viva Question: Why do patients with Wilson's disease present with Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia? Answer: Massive hepatic necrosis releases free copper directly into the bloodstream, which causes overwhelming oxidative damage to erythrocyte membranes, leading to direct hemolysis.
* Trap: Forgetting to prescribe Pyridoxine with D-Penicillamine. Pearl: Always co-prescribe Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) to prevent D-Penicillamine-induced peripheral neuropathy.
* Pitfall: Looking for Kayser-Fleischer rings with the naked eye and documenting "absent KF rings". You must request a formal slit-lamp examination by an ophthalmologist, as early rings are invisible to the naked eye.
