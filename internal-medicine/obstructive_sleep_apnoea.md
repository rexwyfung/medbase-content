# Obstructive Sleep Apnoea (阻塞性睡眠窒息症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Obstructive Sleep Apnoea (OSA)
* **Obstructive Sleep Apnoea (OSA)**: Characterized by recurrent episodes of partial (hypopnea) or complete (apnoea) upper airway collapse during sleep.
* **Mechanism**: Occurs despite ongoing respiratory effort.
* **Consequence**: → sleep fragmentation & intermittent hypoxemia.

### Etiologies and Risk Factors for OSA
* **Anatomical Factors**:
  * Micrognathia / Retrognathia (下頜後縮)
  * Macroglossia (大舌) (e.g., Down syndrome, Acromegaly)
  * Tonsillar/adenoid hypertrophy (common in pediatrics)
* **Systemic Factors**:
  * **Obesity**: High BMI → ↑ pharyngeal fat deposition.
  * Hypothyroidism (myxedema of airway)
  * Male gender & post-menopausal state.
* **Exacerbating Lifestyle Factors**:
  * Alcohol consumption pre-sleep.
  * Sedative/hypnotic use (e.g., benzodiazepines).
  * Supine sleeping position.

### Local Epidemiology of OSA in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: ~4-5% of middle-aged males in HK; heavily underdiagnosed.
* **Asian Phenotype Paradox**: Chinese patients develop OSA at lower BMI cut-offs (BMI >23) compared to Caucasians due to craniofacial skeletal restriction (e.g., steeper mandibular plane, narrower retro-lingual space).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Upper Airway Neuromechanical Collapse Mechanism in OSA
* Sleep onset → ↓ central neural drive to pharyngeal dilator muscles (e.g., **genioglossus**).
* Diaphragmatic contraction generates negative intrathoracic pressure during inspiration.
* **Pathological threshold**: Negative inspiratory pressure > pharyngeal dilator muscle tone → upper airway suction and structural collapse.
* Apnoea → rising PaCO2 & falling PaO2 → arousal from sleep → restoration of muscle tone → airway opens (loud snort/gasp) → cycle repeats.

### Sympathetic Overdrive and Hemodynamic Pathophysiology in OSA
* **Intermittent Hypoxemia** (間歇性缺氧) → triggers carotid body chemoreceptors.
* **Sympathetic Surge**: Massive release of catecholamines during apneic phases.
* → peripheral vasoconstriction → transient ↑ blood pressure, ↑ heart rate, ↑ myocardial oxygen demand.
* Loss of normal physiological nocturnal blood pressure "dipping".

### Endothelial Dysfunction & Oxidative Stress Pathways in OSA
* Re-oxygenation phases generate **Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS)**.
* → systemic inflammatory cascade (↑ CRP, ↑ IL-6, ↑ TNF-α).
* → **Endothelial dysfunction**: ↓ nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability → accelerated atherosclerosis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Patient History & Symptomatology in OSA
* **Nocturnal Symptoms**:
  * Loud, habitual snoring (打鼻鼾).
  * Witnessed apnoeic pauses, choking, or gasping by bed partner.
  * Frequent awakenings & unrefreshing sleep.
  * Nocturia.
* **Diurnal Symptoms**:
  * Excessive daytime somnolence (日間嗜睡) - falling asleep in meetings or while driving.
  * Morning headaches (due to nocturnal hypercapnia causing cerebral vasodilation).
  * Poor concentration, memory deficits, irritability.

### General & Anthropometric Physical Signs of OSA
* ↑ BMI (>25 in Asians is obese).
* ↑ Neck circumference (>40 cm / 16 inches in males; >37 cm / 15 inches in females).

### Craniofacial & Airway Physical Signs of OSA
* **Facies/Jaw**: Retrognathia (receding chin), micrognathia (small jaw).
* **Nasal cavity**: Septal deviation, nasal polyps, turbinate hypertrophy.
* **Oropharynx**: 
  * Enlarged tonsils (kissing tonsils).
  * Macroglossia (tongue scalloping).
  * Elongated, low-lying uvula & thick soft palate.
  * Crowded posterior oropharynx.

### Cardiovascular Physical Signs of Chronic OSA
* Hypertension (often refractory to multi-drug regimens).
* Signs of Cor Pulmonale (right heart failure from chronic hypoxia):
  * ↑ JVP, parasternal heave, palpable P2, bilateral pedal edema.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table of Sleep-Disordered Breathing & Somnolence
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features vs OSA |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Central Sleep Apnoea (CSA)** | Cheyne-Stokes respiration (HF/stroke). **Absence** of respiratory effort during apnoea. |
| **Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome** | BMI ≥30 + **Awake daytime hypercapnia** (PaCO2 >45 mmHg). Often overlaps with OSA. |
| **Narcolepsy** | Cataplexy (loss of muscle tone with emotion), sleep paralysis, hypnagogic hallucinations. Rapid REM sleep onset. |
| **Inadequate Sleep Hygiene** | Simply insufficient hours of sleep; improves when sleep time is extended. |
| **Hypothyroidism** | Cold intolerance, weight gain, bradycardia, delayed ankle reflexes. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for OSA Suspicion
* **Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS)**: Self-administered questionnaire to quantify subjective daytime sleepiness.
* **STOP-BANG Questionnaire**: High sensitivity bedside screening tool for perioperative / clinical risk stratification.

### Blood Investigations for Complications & Secondary Causes of OSA
* **Fasting Glucose / HbA1c**: Screen for concomitant insulin resistance / DM.
* **Fasting Lipid Profile**: Screen for dyslipidemia.
* **Thyroid Function Tests (TFTs)**: R/o secondary hypothyroidism causing macroglossia/myxedema.
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Check for **secondary polycythemia** (↑ Hematocrit) due to chronic hypoxia.
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Taken while awake to r/o daytime hypercapnia (diagnosing Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome).

### Definitive Diagnostic Imaging: Polysomnography (PSG)
* **Level I Polysomnography (In-lab, supervised)**: Gold standard.
  * Monitors: EEG (sleep staging), EOG (eye movements), EMG (chin/leg tone), ECG, nasal/oral airflow, thoracic/abdominal effort belts, pulse oximetry.
* **Level III Home Sleep Apnoea Testing (HSAT)**: Portable monitor measuring airflow, effort, and oximetry. Acceptable in HA clinics for high-probability, uncomplicated patients.

### Diagnostic Criteria for OSA (AASM Criteria)
| Component | Criteria Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Apnoea** | ≥90% ↓ in airflow for ≥10 seconds. |
| **Hypopnea** | ≥30% ↓ in airflow for ≥10 seconds AND ≥3% O2 desaturation or an EEG arousal. |
| **Diagnosis Confirmed** | AHI ≥15 events/hour (asymptomatic) **OR** AHI ≥5 events/hour + OSA symptoms (e.g., somnolence, witnessed gasping) or cardiovascular comorbidities. |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Perioperative Management of the Undiagnosed/Severe OSA Patient
* High risk for complete airway collapse post-extubation.
* Avoid heavy opioid analgesia & sedatives; use multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia.
* Extubate fully awake in semi-upright position.
* Continuous oximetry monitoring in ward.
* Early application of CPAP/BiPAP post-operatively.

### Acute Hypercapnic Respiratory Failure in OSA/OHS Overlap
* Presentation: Altered mental status, CO2 narcosis (PaCO2 > 60 mmHg).
* **Acute Rx**: Urgent Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV) - usually BiPAP to provide both inspiratory support (to blow off CO2) and expiratory positive pressure (to splint airway).
* Target SpO2 88-92% (prevent ablation of hypoxic drive).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Step 1: Conservative & Lifestyle Management for OSA
* **Weight Reduction**: Crucial disease-modifying intervention. Dietician referral. Bariatric surgery if morbidly obese (BMI >35 with comorbidities).
* **Positional Therapy**: Sleep wedge / tennis ball technique to prevent supine sleeping.
* **Sleep Hygiene**: Avoid alcohol, smoking, and muscle relaxants/sedatives within 4-6 hours of sleep.

### Step 2: Positive Airway Pressure Therapy (First-Line Gold Standard)
* **Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP)**:
  * Acts as a pneumatic splint maintaining positive pharyngeal transmural pressure.
  * **Auto-CPAP (APAP)**: Self-adjusts pressure based on detected airflow limitations (common HA ambulatory prescription).
  * Requires strict compliance (≥4 hours/night for ≥70% of nights).
* **Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure (BiPAP)**:
  * Reserved for patients with high CPAP pressure requirements, CPAP intolerance, or overlapping COPD/OHS requiring ventilation support.

### Step 3: Mandibular Advancement Devices (MAD)
* **Indication**: Mild-to-moderate OSA, or CPAP-intolerant patients without severe obesity.
* **Mechanism**: Custom-fitted dental appliance that pulls the mandible and base of tongue forward, enlarging the retro-lingual airway.

### Step 4: Surgical Management Options for OSA
* **Tonsillectomy & Adenoidectomy**: First-line for pediatric OSA.
* **Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty (UPPP)**: Excision of tonsils, uvula, and posterior palate. Highly variable success; primarily for isolated palatal obstruction.
* **Maxillomandibular Advancement (MMA)**: Highly effective but invasive surgery to move jaws forward.
* **Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation**: Implantable device stimulating genioglossus during inspiration (rare in HK HA).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) for Symptom Stratification
| Score Range | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **0 - 9** | Normal daytime sleepiness |
| **10 - 14** | Mild excessive daytime sleepiness |
| **15 - 18** | Moderate excessive daytime sleepiness |
| **19 - 24** | Severe excessive daytime sleepiness |

### STOP-BANG Questionnaire for OSA Pre-test Probability
| Acronym | Clinical Feature (1 point each, High Risk if ≥3) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **S**noring | Loud enough to be heard through closed doors |
| **T**ired | Frequent daytime tiredness/fatigue |
| **O**bserved | Apnoeas, gasping, or choking observed |
| **P**ressure | Known hypertension |
| **B**MI | > 35 kg/m² (or Asian cut-offs contextually) |
| **A**ge | > 50 years |
| **N**eck Size | > 40cm (M) or > 37cm (F) |
| **G**ender | Male |

| Severity Grade | Apnoea-Hypopnea Index (AHI) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mild OSA** | 5 to 14.9 events/hour |
| **Moderate OSA** | 15 to 29.9 events/hour |
| **Severe OSA** | ≥ 30 events/hour |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Cardiovascular & Hemodynamic Complications of Chronic OSA
* **Resistant Hypertension**: Classical cause of secondary HTN; resistant to ≥3 antihypertensives.
* **Arrhythmias**: Highly arrhythmogenic; heavily linked to **Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動)** and nocturnal bradyarrhythmias.
* **Structural Heart Disease**: Right ventricular hypertrophy (RVH) → Cor Pulmonale. Increased risk of systolic/diastolic heart failure.
* **Cerebrovascular**: ↑ risk of TIA and ischemic stroke.

### Metabolic & Endocrine Complications of Chronic OSA
* **Metabolic Syndrome**: Synergistic relationship with severe insulin resistance and Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.
* **Hepatic**: Progression of Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD) / Steatohepatitis.
* **Reproductive**: Erectile dysfunction, ↓ libido, ↓ testosterone.

### Neuropsychiatric & Societal Complications of Chronic OSA
* Cognitive impairment, impaired executive function, and early-onset dementia risk.
* Clinical depression and mood lability.
* Massive ↑ risk of Motor Vehicle Accidents (MVA) and occupational hazard injuries.

### Device-Related Adverse Effects (CPAP Complications)
* **Dermatological**: Nasal bridge pressure sores / ulcerations from tight mask seals.
* **Mucosal**: Nasal congestion, epistaxis, extreme dry mouth (Rx: Heated humidifier attachment).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Aerophagia (swallowing air) → gastric distension, flatulence.
* **Psychological**: Claustrophobia leading to poor long-term compliance.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What strictly differentiates an apnoea from a hypopnea on polysomnography? Answer: An apnoea is a ≥90% reduction in airflow for at least 10 seconds. A hypopnea is a ≥30% reduction in airflow for 10 seconds but MUST be accompanied by a ≥3% desaturation in O2 OR an EEG arousal.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate Central vs Obstructive Sleep Apnoea on a sleep study tracing? Answer: Look at the thoracic and abdominal effort belts. In Central Sleep Apnoea, there is a flat line (no respiratory effort). In OSA, there is paradoxical or increasing respiratory effort against the closed airway.
* Trap: Prescribing benzodiazepines/z-drugs for a patient complaining of frequent nocturnal awakenings without taking a sleep history. Pearl: Always screen for OSA first; sedatives relax pharyngeal tone and severely blunt the hypoxic drive, precipitating prolonged and life-threatening apnoeas.
* Pitfall: Attributing severe awake daytime hypercapnia solely to OSA. OSA alone typically causes only transient nocturnal hypercapnia. If PaCO2 > 45 mmHg during the daytime, you must investigate for overlapping Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS) or underlying severe COPD.
