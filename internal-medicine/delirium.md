# Delirium (譫妄)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Delirium
* **Core Concept**: Acute, fluctuating syndrome of global cognitive dysfunction primarily characterized by impaired attention and awareness.
* **Key Hallmarks**:
  * Acute onset (hours → days)
  * Fluctuating course (often worse at night - "sundowning")
  * Inattention (inability to focus, sustain, or shift attention)

### Subtypes of Delirium
* **Hypoactive Subtype**:
  * Most common subtype (~50-75%)
  * Often missed in busy Hong Kong Hospital Authority (HA) medical/geriatric wards
  * Features: Lethargy, sedation, psychomotor retardation, slow responses
* **Hyperactive Subtype**:
  * Highly visible, distressing to staff/relatives
  * Features: Agitation, restlessness, hallucinations, wandering, aggression
* **Mixed Subtype**:
  * Fluctuates between hypoactive and hyperactive states throughout the day

### Predisposing Factors for Delirium (Baseline Vulnerability)
* **Demographics**: Advanced age (>65 years)
* **Neurological**: Pre-existing **Dementia** (認知障礙症) (highest risk), Parkinson's disease, previous stroke
* **Sensory**: Significant visual or hearing impairment
* **Medical**: Frailty, severe illness, multiple comorbidities, chronic renal/hepatic impairment
* **Pharmacological**: Polypharmacy, baseline use of psychoactive drugs, alcohol dependence

### Precipitating Factors for Delirium (DIMES / PINCH ME)
* **D**rugs: Anticholinergics, sedatives, opiates, steroids, withdrawal (alcohol/benzodiazepines)
* **I**nfection: Pneumonia, UTI, sepsis, cellulitis, meningitis
* **M**etabolic: Hypo/hypernatremia, hypercalcemia, uremia, hepatic encephalopathy, hypoxia
* **E**nvironment: Admission to ICU/acute ward, sleep deprivation, physical restraints
* **S**tructural: Acute stroke, subdural hemorrhage (SDH), myocardial infarction, urinary retention, severe constipation (fecal impaction)
* **Alternative Mnemonic (PINCH ME)**: **P**ain, **I**nfection, **C**onstipation, **H**ydration, **M**edication, **E**nvironment

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: Very high in HA acute medical admission wards (~20-30% of elderly admissions)
* **Orthopedics**: Up to 50% incidence in elderly patients post-neck of femur (NOF) fracture surgery
* **Impact**: Major driver for prolonged Length of Stay (LOS) and institutionalization (discharge to Residential Care Homes for the Elderly - RCHE)

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Neurotransmitter Imbalance Hypothesis of Delirium
* **Acetylcholine (乙酰膽鹼) Deficiency**:
  * Central cholinergic failure → impaired attention and consciousness
  * Explains why anticholinergic drugs severely precipitate delirium
* **Dopamine (多巴胺) Excess**:
  * Dopaminergic overactivity → hyperactive symptoms and psychosis/hallucinations
  * Explains therapeutic efficacy of dopamine antagonists (antipsychotics)
* **Other Neurotransmitters**:
  * ↑ Glutamate (excitotoxicity)
  * Altered Serotonin and GABA (explains benzodiazepine/alcohol withdrawal delirium)

### Neuroinflammatory Hypothesis of Delirium
* **Systemic Inflammation**: Peripheral infection/trauma → ↑ pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α)
* **Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) Permeability**: Cytokines cross compromised BBB (esp. in elderly)
* **Microglial Activation**: Activation of CNS immune cells → local neuroinflammation → impaired synaptic plasticity and neuronal network dysfunction

### Oxidative Stress & Metabolic Dysfunction Hypothesis
* **Global Cerebral Hypoxia**: Systemic hypoxia/hypoperfusion → ↓ oxidative metabolism in brain
* **Network Disconnect**: Reduced cerebral metabolism → functional disconnection of prefrontal cortex and non-dominant parietal lobe (areas governing attention and spatial awareness)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Collateral History Taking in Delirium
* **Timeline**: Exact onset of confusion, chronicity, and fluctuation pattern (distinguishes from dementia)
* **Baseline Function**: Premorbid cognitive status, Activities of Daily Living (ADLs), Instrumental ADLs
* **Medication Review**: Recent additions, dose changes, over-the-counter (OTC) meds, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)
* **Substance Hx**: Alcohol intake (risk of delirium tremens), illicit drugs
* **Systemic Symptoms**: Fever, cough, dysuria, pain, bowel habit changes, recent falls

### General & Neurological Examination for Delirium
* **General Appearance**: Agitated, picking at bedsheets, drowsy, neglect, evidence of self-harm
* **Vitals**: Fever, tachycardia, hypoxia, hypotension
* **Hydration Status**: Dry mucous membranes, ↓ skin turgor
* **Consciousness Level**: GCS or AVPU scale assessment
* **Neurological Signs**:
  * **Asterixis** (撲翼樣震顫): Points to hepatic, renal, or hypercapnic etiology
  * **Focal deficits**: Unilateral weakness, pronator drift, facial droop (suspect stroke/SDH)
  * **Meningism**: Neck stiffness, photophobia (suspect meningitis)

### System-Specific Examination for Delirium Precipitants
* **Chest**: Basal crepitations, bronchial breathing (Pneumonia/Pulmonary edema)
* **Abdomen**: Palpable bladder (Urinary retention), fecal loading on DRE (Constipation), peritonism
* **Skin**: Pressure sores, cellulitis, infected IV cannula sites, jaundice
* **Cardiovascular**: Irregular pulse (AF), new murmurs (Endocarditis)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Delirium vs Dementia vs Depression
| Feature | Delirium (譫妄) | Dementia (認知障礙症) | Depression (抑鬱症) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Acute (hours/days) | Insidious (months/years) | Variable (weeks/months) |
| **Course** | Fluctuating (worse at night) | Slowly progressive | Diurnal variation (worse in AM) |
| **Attention** | **Markedly impaired** | Usually preserved until late | Normal (but poor effort/"I don't know") |
| **Consciousness** | Altered (Clouded/Cloudy) | Clear | Clear |
| **Hallucinations** | Common (mostly Visual) | Rare (except Lewy Body) | Auditory (mood-congruent, in severe) |
| **Reversibility** | Usually reversible | Irreversible | Reversible with treatment |

### Other Differential Diagnoses for Acute Confusion
* **Non-convulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE)**: Consider if subtle facial twitching, prolonged unresponsiveness, Hx of epilepsy
* **Acute Psychosis (Schizophrenia/Mania)**: Usually clear consciousness, intact attention, primary auditory (not visual) hallucinations
* **Aphasia (Wernicke's/Receptive)**: Alert, no fluctuation, specific language deficit (fluent but nonsensical speech) rather than global confusion

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Delirium
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG/HemoCue)**: Rule out hypoglycemia immediately
* **Urine Dipstick & Culture**: Screen for UTI
* **Bladder Scan**: Quantify post-void residual volume (Urinary retention)
* **ECG**: Identify silent MI, arrhythmias (AF), prolonged QTc (baseline before antipsychotics)
* **Pulse Oximetry / ABG**: Rule out hypoxia, hypercapnia (Type II respiratory failure)

### Blood Investigations for Delirium (Delirium Screen)
* **CBC**: Leukocytosis (Infection), Anemia (Bleeding/Hypoxia)
* **LRFTs**: Uremia (AKI/CKD), Deranged LFTs (Hepatic encephalopathy), Hypo/hypernatremia
* **Bone Profile**: Hypercalcemia (Malignancy/Hyperparathyroidism)
* **Infection Markers**: CRP, Procalcitonin, Blood Cultures
* **TFTs**: Thyrotoxicosis or Myxedema coma
* **B12 / Folate**: Vitamin deficiencies
* **Ammonia**: If hepatic encephalopathy suspected

### Imaging & Special Investigations for Delirium
* **CXR**: Consolidation, pulmonary edema
* **CT Brain (Non-contrast)**: Indicated ONLY IF:
  * Focal neurological signs present
  * Recent head trauma/fall
  * Taking anticoagulants (Warfarin/DOACs)
  * Undifferentiated delirium not responding to treatment of systemic causes
* **Lumbar Puncture (LP)**: Indicated if high suspicion of meningitis/encephalitis (fever + meningism)
* **EEG**: Indicated if NCSE suspected or to differentiate psychiatric vs organic causes (diffuse slowing in delirium)

### DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria for Delirium
| Criterion | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A (Attention)** | Disturbance in attention and awareness |
| **B (Timeline)** | Disturbance develops acutely (hours-days) and tends to fluctuate |
| **C (Cognition)** | Additional disturbance in cognition (memory, orientation, language, perception) |
| **D (Exclusion)** | Not better explained by pre-existing neurocognitive disorder, not in coma |
| **E (Etiology)** | Evidence from Hx/Exam/Labs that it is a direct physiological consequence of another medical condition, substance, or toxin |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Medical Resuscitation in Delirium
* **ABCDE Approach**: Secure airway, optimize oxygenation (target SpO2 >94%), circulatory support (IV fluids for shock/dehydration)
* **Immediate Reversal**:
  * Treat Hypoglycemia (IV Dextrose)
  * Treat Hypoxia (Oxygen therapy)
  * Treat Sepsis (IV Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour)
  * Relieve Retention (Urinary catheterization)

### Non-Pharmacological Bundle for Acute Agitation (FIRST-LINE)
* **Environment**: Quiet, well-lit room during day, dim at night. Visible clock and calendar (Reorientation)
* **Sensory Aids**: Ensure patient has their glasses and hearing aids on
* **Communication**: Clear, simple instructions, calm voice, frequent reorientation
* **Family Presence**: Involve relatives/carers for familiar faces and de-escalation
* **Safety**: Nurse in highly visible area, bed in low position, avoid physical restraints (increases agitation and injury risk)

### Pharmacological Management for Severe Agitation
* **Indications**: ONLY if severe agitation poses immediate risk of harm to self/others, or impedes life-saving treatment (e.g., pulling out central lines/ETT). NEVER use solely for sedation/quieting a wandering patient.
* **First-line (Typical Antipsychotic)**:
  * **Haloperidol (氟哌啶醇)**: 0.5mg PO/IM stat. Reassess after 30-60 mins. Max 2-5mg/day in elderly.
  * Avoid in Parkinson's disease or Lewy Body Dementia (use Quetiapine instead).
* **Second-line (Atypical Antipsychotic)**:
  * **Quetiapine (喹硫平)**: 12.5-25mg PO. Preferred if high risk of extrapyramidal symptoms.
  * **Risperidone (利培酮)**: 0.25-0.5mg PO.
* **Avoid Benzodiazepines**: Strictly avoid (worsens delirium) UNLESS delirium is due to alcohol/sedative withdrawal, or in Parkinson's disease where antipsychotics are contraindicated.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Post-Delirium Recovery & Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA)
* **MDT Approach**: Geriatrician, Physiotherapist (PT), Occupational Therapist (OT), Medical Social Worker (MSW)
* **Mobility**: Early mobilization to prevent deconditioning, pressure sores, and DVT
* **Nutrition/Hydration**: Dietitian input, thickened fluids if dysphagic, close monitoring of intake/output
* **Cognitive Rehabilitation**: Gradual reintroduction to cognitive tasks, cognitive stimulation therapy

### Delirium Prevention Strategies (HELP Protocol equivalents)
* **Sleep Optimization**: Avoid nighttime obs/interventions unless critical. Reduce noise/light. Avoid hypnotic drugs.
* **Hydration/Bowel Care**: Strict prophylactic laxatives for immobile/opioid-treated patients. Encourage oral fluids.
* **Early Infection Detection**: Regular monitoring for signs of aspiration, UTI, or line infections.

### Medication Optimization & Deprescribing Algorithm
* **Target 1**: Stop strongly anticholinergic drugs (e.g., Amitriptyline, Chlorpheniramine, Oxybutynin)
* **Target 2**: Wean and stop Benzodiazepines (Diazepam, Lorazepam) and Z-drugs (Zopiclone)
* **Target 3**: Optimize analgesia (Use Paracetamol regularly, minimize Opiates like Tramadol/Morphine if possible)
* **Target 4**: Promptly discontinue any antipsychotics started acutely for delirium once the episode resolves (do NOT discharge on new Haloperidol without clear plan).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### The 4AT Scoring Tool for Delirium (Rapid Bedside Tool)
| Category | Assessment | Score |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Alertness** | Normal (0), Mild sleepiness (0), Clearly abnormal (4) | 0 or 4 |
| **2. AMT4** | Age, DOB, Place, Current Year. No errors (0), 1 error (1), 2+ errors/untestable (2) | 0, 1, or 2 |
| **3. Attention** | Months backward. >=7 months (0), <7 months (1), Untestable/refuses (2) | 0, 1, or 2 |
| **4. Acute Change** | Fluctuation or acute change in alertness/cognition over 24h? No (0), Yes (4) | 0 or 4 |
| **Interpretation** | **Score >= 4**: Delirium/Cognitive Impairment highly likely. | |

### Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) Criteria for Delirium
* **Requirement**: Diagnosis requires Features 1 AND 2, PLUS either Feature 3 OR 4.
* **Feature 1**: Acute onset and fluctuating course
* **Feature 2**: Inattention (difficulty focusing/keeping track)
* **Feature 3**: Disorganized thinking (rambling, illogical flow)
* **Feature 4**: Altered level of consciousness (vigilant, lethargic, stupor, coma)

### Prognosis and Post-Delirium Outcomes
* **Mortality**: Significantly ↑ inpatient mortality and 1-year mortality
* **Cognitive Trajectory**: Delirium accelerates cognitive decline. ↑ Risk of developing incident Dementia
* **Functional Decline**: Higher rates of ADL dependence post-discharge
* **Length of Stay**: Prolongs hospitalization by average 5-8 days

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Direct Physical Complications of Delirium Syndrome
* **Traumatic**: High risk of falls → NOF fractures, head trauma, subdural hematoma
* **Pulmonary**: Aspiration pneumonia (due to altered sensorium and impaired gag reflex)
* **Dermatological**: Pressure ulcers (due to immobility in hypoactive delirium)
* **Nutritional**: Severe dehydration, malnutrition, electrolyte imbalances (due to refusal to eat/drink)

### Iatrogenic & Pharmacological Complications
* **Antipsychotic Toxicity**:
  * **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS)**: Parkinsonism, acute dystonia, akathisia (esp. Haloperidol)
  * **Cardiac**: QTc prolongation → Torsades de pointes / Ventricular fibrillation
  * **Cerebrovascular**: ↑ Risk of stroke and overall mortality in elderly dementia patients
  * **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)**: Rare but fatal (hyperthermia, rigidity, CK elevation)
* **Restraint-Related Complications**:
  * Strangulation, brachial plexus injuries, skin abrasions
  * Psychological trauma, paradoxical worsening of agitation

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common subtype of delirium in the elderly and why is it dangerous? Answer: Hypoactive delirium. It is dangerous because it is often misdiagnosed as depression or fatigue, leading to delayed treatment of the underlying life-threatening precipitant (e.g., sepsis).
* Trap: Prescribing benzodiazepines to sedate an agitated delirious patient. Pearl: Benzodiazepines exacerbate delirium and cognitive impairment. Only use them if the delirium is strictly caused by alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal.
* Pitfall: Discharging a patient on haloperidol that was started acutely for delirium. Always chart antipsychotics as short-term "PRN" or with a strict stop date to prevent chronic inappropriate prescribing and long-term EPS/stroke risk.
* Trap: Assuming acute confusion in an elderly patient is "just their dementia progressing". Pearl: Dementia progression is insidious (months to years). Any acute cognitive decline (hours to days) is delirium until proven otherwise and requires a full septic, metabolic, and neurological screen.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically test for inattention at the bedside? Answer: Ask the patient to recite the days of the week or months of the year backwards, or use the "serial 7s" subtraction test.
* Pitfall: Using physical restraints to prevent line removal in a delirious patient. Physical restraints dramatically increase agitation, risk of injury/strangulation, and worsen the delirium itself. Always prioritize the non-pharmacological bundle, 1-to-1 nursing, and treating the underlying cause.
