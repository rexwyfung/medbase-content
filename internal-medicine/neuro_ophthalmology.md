# Neuro-Ophthalmology (神經眼科)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Key Neuro-Ophthalmic Syndromes
* **RAPD (Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect / Marcus Gunn Pupil):** Unilateral/asymmetrical optic nerve/retinal disease causing diminished pupillary constriction to light.
* **Horner's Syndrome (霍納氏綜合症):** Oculosympathetic paresis manifesting as classical triad: ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis.
* **Argyll Robertson Pupils:** Bilateral, small, irregular pupils exhibiting light-near dissociation.
* **Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO / 核間性眼肌麻痺):** Disordered conjugate gaze with impaired adduction of affected eye and abducting nystagmus of contralateral eye.
* **Papilloedema (視乳頭水腫):** Bilateral optic disc swelling strictly secondary to ↑ Intracranial Pressure (ICP).
* **Optic Neuritis (視神經炎):** Inflammatory, demyelinating optic neuropathy presenting with painful visual loss.

### Etiology of Pupillary Abnormalities
* **Horner's Syndrome (1st Order - Central):** Brainstem stroke (Wallenberg syndrome), syringomyelia, tumour.
* **Horner's Syndrome (2nd Order - Preganglionic):** Pancoast tumour (lung apex), thoracic aortic aneurysm, neck trauma.
* **Horner's Syndrome (3rd Order - Postganglionic):** Carotid artery dissection, cavernous sinus thrombosis, cluster headache.
* **Argyll Robertson Pupil:** Neurosyphilis (Tertiary Syphilis - 晚期梅毒), diabetic neuropathy (rarely).

### Etiology of Ocular Motor Palsies (眼球運動神經麻痺)
* **CN III (Oculomotor):** 
  * Microvascular ischaemia (DM, HTN) → Pupil-sparing.
  * Compressive [Posterior Communicating Artery (PCoA) Aneurysm, uncal herniation] → Pupil-involving.
* **CN IV (Trochlear):** Closed head trauma (most common, due to long dorsal course), congenital, microvascular.
* **CN VI (Abducens):** ↑ ICP (false localizing sign due to long intracranial course over clivus), microvascular, pontine glioma.

### Etiology of Optic Nerve Pathology & ICP Elevation
* **Papilloedema:** Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH), Space-Occupying Lesion (SOL), venous sinus thrombosis, malignant hypertension.
* **Optic Neuritis:** Idiopathic, NMO/AQP4-IgG disease, MOGAD, post-viral. (MS excluded).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of the Pupillary Light Reflex & RAPD
* **Normal Pathway:** Afferent: CN II → optic chiasm (partial decussation) → optic tract → Pretectal nucleus (midbrain) → bilateral Edinger-Westphal (EW) nuclei. Efferent: Parasympathetic fibers via CN III → ciliary ganglion → sphincter pupillae.
* **RAPD Mechanism:** Unilateral severe retinal/optic nerve lesion → ↓ afferent input from diseased eye → weaker bilateral EW nucleus activation → pupils constrict less when light swings from healthy to diseased eye (appearing to paradoxically dilate).

### Pathophysiology of Horner's Syndrome (霍納氏綜合症)
* Disruption of 3-neuron oculosympathetic pathway.
* ↓ Sympathetic tone to superior tarsal muscle (Müller's muscle) → mild ptosis.
* Unopposed parasympathetic tone to sphincter pupillae → miosis.
* Denervation of facial sweat glands → anhidrosis (pattern depends on lesion level: 1st/2nd order = whole face; 3rd order = absent/forehead only as sudomotor fibers branch off at carotid bifurcation).

### Pathophysiology of Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO)
* **Lesion:** Medial Longitudinal Fasciculus (MLF).
* **Mechanism:** MLF physically connects CN VI nucleus (pons) to contralateral CN III nucleus (midbrain). Disconnection prevents coordinated ipsilateral adduction during horizontal gaze.
* Contralateral abducting eye exhibits nystagmus (compensatory ↑ firing rate attempting to drive the paretic adducting eye).

### Pathophysiology of Papilloedema (視乳頭水腫)
* ↑ ICP transmitted through subarachnoid space surrounding optic nerve.
* Mechanical compression of optic nerve sheath → stasis of axoplasmic flow at lamina cribrosa.
* Accumulation of intracellular organelles/fluid → swollen optic disc axons → venous engorgement → secondary microvascular compromise (haemorrhages/exudates).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Assessment of Pupillary Abnormalities
* **Swinging Flashlight Test (RAPD):** Paradoxical dilatation of affected pupil when light moves from normal eye to affected eye.
* **Horner's Syndrome Triad:** 
  * Ptosis (mild, 1-2mm).
  * Miosis (more prominent in dim lighting - dilation lag).
  * Anhidrosis (ipsilateral face/neck).
  * Enophthalmos (apparent, due to narrow palpebral fissure).
* **Argyll Robertson Pupil:** Pupils small, irregular, fail to constrict to direct light, but briskly constrict to accommodation (near target).

### Assessment of Cranial Nerve III, IV, VI Palsies
* **CN III Palsy (Oculomotor):**
  * Eye deviated "Down and Out" (unopposed action of Superior Oblique and Lateral Rectus).
  * Severe ptosis (Levator palpebrae superioris paralysis).
  * Fixed, dilated pupil (if parasympathetic fibers involved).
* **CN IV Palsy (Trochlear):**
  * Eye deviated slightly upward/extorted.
  * Head tilt contralateral to lesion (Bielschowsky head tilt test +ve to neutralize diplopia).
  * Vertical/torsional diplopia, worse on downgaze (reading, stairs).
* **CN VI Palsy (Abducens):**
  * Convergent strabismus (esotropia) in primary position.
  * Horizontal diplopia worsening on gazing toward affected side.
  * Defective abduction of affected eye.

### Assessment of INO & Nystagmus
* **INO:** Ask patient to look laterally. Affected eye fails to adduct; contralateral eye abducts with coarse horizontal nystagmus.
* **Nystagmus (眼球震顫):** Note direction (horizontal, vertical, rotary, pendular, jerk). Fast phase defines direction.

### Assessment of Raised ICP & Optic Pathologies
* **Symptoms of ↑ ICP:** Morning headache (worse with Valsalva/coughing), pulsatile tinnitus, transient visual obscurations (seconds-long vision loss with postural changes), projectile vomiting.
* **Optic Neuritis:** Subacute monocular visual loss, retrobulbar pain (worse with eye movement), central scotoma.
* **Fundoscopy (Papilloedema):** Blurry disc margins, loss of physiological cup, hyperaemic disc, venous engorgement, peripapillary flame-shaped haemorrhages, Paton's lines (retinal folds).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Causes of Anisocoria (瞳孔大小不一)
| Feature | Horner's Syndrome | CN III Palsy (Compressive) | Adie's Tonic Pupil | Physiologic Anisocoria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Abnormal Pupil** | The smaller pupil | The larger pupil | The larger pupil | Neither (benign) |
| **Light Condition** | Anisocoria worse in dark | Anisocoria worse in light | Anisocoria worse in light | Equal in all lighting |
| **Light Reflex** | Normal | Absent/Sluggish | Sluggish (worm-like) | Normal |
| **Accommodation** | Normal | Absent/Sluggish | Brisk (tonic redilation) | Normal |
| **Associated Signs** | Ptosis, anhidrosis | Ptosis, EOM deficits | Absent deep tendon reflexes | None |

### Differentiating Third Nerve Palsy: Medical vs Surgical
* **Medical (Microvascular):** Pupil is SPARED. Central core of nerve infarcted (parasympathetic fibers run peripherally). Ptosis and ophthalmoplegia present. Painful onset common.
* **Surgical (Compressive):** Pupil is INVOLVED (dilated, unreactive). External compression (e.g., PCoA aneurysm) hits peripheral parasympathetic fibers first. High risk of SAH.

### Visual Field Defect Localisation (視野缺損定位)
| Lesion Location | Characteristic Visual Field Defect | E.g. Pathologies |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Optic Nerve** | Monocular visual loss / Central scotoma | Optic neuritis, Glaucoma |
| **Optic Chiasm** | Bitemporal hemianopia | Pituitary adenoma, Craniopharyngioma |
| **Optic Tract** | Incongruous contralateral homonymous hemianopia | Stroke, Tumour |
| **Meyer's Loop (Temporal)** | Contralateral Superior Quadrantanopia ("Pie in the sky") | Temporal lobe infarct/lesion |
| **Baum's Loop (Parietal)** | Contralateral Inferior Quadrantanopia ("Pie on the floor")| Parietal lobe infarct/lesion |
| **Occipital Cortex** | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia WITH MACULAR SPARING | PCA infarct (macula supplied by MCA) |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Clinical Investigations
* **Visual Acuity:** Snellen chart (↓ in optic neuritis; normal early in papilloedema).
* **Colour Vision:** Ishihara plates (dyschromatopsia is an early, sensitive sign of optic neuritis).
* **Red Desaturation Test:** Red target appears "washed out" or pink/orange in the eye with optic neuropathy.
* **Pharmacological Pupil Testing (Horner's):** 
  * 4% Cocaine drops (fails to dilate Horner's pupil).
  * 1% Apraclonidine (dilates Horner's pupil due to denervation hypersensitivity).

### Blood Investigations
* **Neurosyphilis Screen:** VDRL / RPR (disease activity), TPPA / FTA-ABS (confirmatory).
* **Ischaemic Risk Profile (Microvascular CN palsies):** Fasting glucose, HbA1c, fasting lipid profile.
* **Vasculitis Screen (AION workup):** ESR, CRP (stat if Temporal Arteritis suspected).
* **Optic Neuritis Autoantibodies:** Anti-AQP4 (Neuromyelitis Optica), Anti-MOG.

### Neuroimaging Protocols
* **Urgent CT Brain / CTA (Circle of Willis):** Gold standard to rule out PCoA aneurysm in pupil-involving CN III palsy or SAH.
* **MRI Brain & Orbits with Gadolinium:** 
  * Modality of choice for Optic Neuritis (optic nerve enhancement).
  * IIH signs: Empty sella, flattened posterior sclera, distended optic nerve sheaths.
  * Brainstem lesions for INO or CN palsies.
* **MR Venogram (MRV):** Mandatory in presumed IIH to rule out Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST).

### Invasive Procedures & Special Tests
* **Lumbar Puncture (LP):** 
  * *Strictly contraindicated until mass lesion ruled out by neuroimaging.*
  * Essential for IIH diagnosis (Opening Pressure >25 cmH2O, normal constituents).
  * CSF VDRL/FTA-ABS for neurosyphilis.
* **Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT):** Quantifies Retinal Nerve Fiber Layer (RNFL) thickness (monitors papilloedema/optic neuritis progression).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation of Suspected Compressive CN III Palsy
* **Immediate Action:** Urgent neurosurgery consultation.
* **Imaging:** Stat CT/CTA Brain.
* **Haemodynamic Control:** Strict BP control (IV Labetalol / Nicardipine) to prevent PCoA aneurysm rupture.
* **Intervention:** Urgent endovascular coiling or surgical clipping.

### Acute Management of Raised ICP & Vision-Threatening Papilloedema
* **Posture:** Elevate head of bed 30°.
* **Osmotherapy:** IV Mannitol 20% or Hypertonic Saline if impending herniation (bridge to surgery).
* **Medical Decompression (IIH):** PO Acetazolamide (Diamox) stat dose.
* **Surgical Decompression:** Urgent neurosurgery/ophthalmology referral for Optic Nerve Sheath Fenestration (ONSF) or External Ventricular Drain (EVD) / VP Shunt if rapidly progressive visual loss.

### Acute Management of Optic Neuritis (視神經炎)
* **Corticosteroids:** IV Methylprednisolone 1g daily for 3-5 days.
* *Note:* Accelerates visual recovery but does NOT alter final visual outcome. Avoid oral steroids monotherapy (increases recurrence risk).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH)
* **Lifestyle Modification (Primary therapy):** Aggressive weight loss (target >10% reduction of total body weight). Dietician referral.
* **Pharmacological Algorithm:**
  * First-line: PO Acetazolamide (Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor) → ↓ CSF production.
  * Second-line/Add-on: PO Topiramate (migraine prophylaxis + weak carbonic anhydrase inhibition + weight loss side effect).
  * Refractory/Intolerant: Loop diuretics (Furosemide).
* **Surgical Step-up (If failing medical Rx or progressive visual loss):** VP shunt, LP shunt, or venous sinus stenting (if focal stenosis proven).

### Chronic Management of Microvascular CN Palsies
* **Risk Factor Modification:** Tight glycaemic control (HbA1c <7%), aggressive BP and lipid management.
* **Symptomatic Rx:** Opaque patch over one eye to relieve diplopia.
* **Orthoptic Referral:** Prism lenses for persistent diplopia.
* **Surgical:** Strabismus surgery or Botulinum toxin injection into antagonist extraocular muscle ONLY IF spontaneous recovery fails to occur after 6-12 months.

### Chronic Management of Neurosyphilis (Argyll Robertson)
* **Antibiotics:** IV Aqueous Crystalline Penicillin G (18-24 million units/day) for 10-14 days.
* **Follow-up:** Serial LP every 6 months to ensure normalization of CSF pleocytosis and ↓ VDRL titer.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Modified Dandy Criteria for IIH Diagnosis
* Symptoms and signs solely attributable to ↑ ICP (e.g., papilloedema, headache).
* Elevated CSF opening pressure (≥ 25 cmH2O in adults).
* Normal CSF composition.
* No localising neurological signs (except unilateral or bilateral CN VI palsy).
* Normal neuroimaging (no hydrocephalus, mass, structural lesion, or venous sinus thrombosis).

### Prognostication of Ocular Motor Palsies
* **Microvascular CN Palsies:** Excellent prognosis. Spontaneous, complete resolution within 3 to 6 months in >80% of patients.
* **Traumatic CN IV Palsy:** Often permanent if nerve avulsed; may require surgical correction.

### Optic Neuritis Risk Stratification (Progression to MS)
* **Normal baseline Brain MRI:** ~25% risk of developing clinical MS at 15 years.
* **≥1 demyelinating white matter lesion on MRI:** ~72% risk of developing clinical MS at 15 years.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Papilloedema & Raised ICP
* **Neurological:** Brain herniation (tonsillar, uncal) → coma/death.
* **Ophthalmological:** Secondary optic atrophy → concentric visual field constriction → irreversible blindness.

### Complications of Corticosteroid Therapy (High-Dose IV)
* **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Severe hyperglycaemia (requires sliding scale insulin), hypokalaemia, weight gain.
* **Musculoskeletal:** Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head, severe osteoporosis (requires calcium/Vit D/bisphosphonate prophylaxis).
* **Psychiatric:** Steroid-induced psychosis, severe insomnia.

### Complications of Acetazolamide (Diamox)
* **Electrolyte/Acid-Base:** Non-anion gap metabolic acidosis, profound hypokalaemia.
* **Renal:** Renal calculi formation (calcium phosphate stones due to alkaline urine).
* **Neurological:** Distal limb paraesthesia (tingling in fingers/toes), dysgeusia (metallic taste).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate a surgical third nerve palsy from a medical one? Answer: A surgical palsy (e.g., PCoA aneurysm) is pupil-involving (fixed/dilated) due to compression of peripheral parasympathetic fibers, whereas a medical palsy (e.g., diabetic microvascular) is pupil-sparing due to central nerve infarction.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with a CN VI palsy and a headache has a focal pontine lesion. Pearl: CN VI has the longest intracranial course; its palsy is a classic "false localising sign" of globally raised ICP. Always check the fundus for papilloedema.
* Pitfall: Performing a lumbar puncture in a patient with suspected papilloedema without prior imaging. You risk precipitating fatal tonsillar herniation if a space-occupying lesion is present.
* Viva Question: Where is the lesion in a patient with a left homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing? Answer: The right occipital cortex. The macula is spared due to dual blood supply from both the PCA and the MCA at the occipital pole.
* Trap: Prescribing oral prednisolone monotherapy for acute typical optic neuritis. Pearl: The ONTT trial showed that oral prednisone alone actually increases the risk of recurrent optic neuritis compared to IV methylprednisolone or placebo. Use high-dose IV methylprednisolone.
* Pitfall: Overlooking the cause of an Argyll Robertson pupil. Neurosyphilis must be actively excluded using serum and CSF VDRL/TPPA.
