# Multiple Myeloma (多發性骨髓瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Multiple Myeloma
* **Multiple Myeloma (多發性骨髓瘤):** A systemic haematological malignancy characterized by the clonal proliferation of malignant plasma cells in the bone marrow, typically producing a monoclonal paraprotein (M-protein), leading to end-organ damage (CRAB criteria).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Incidence:** ~1-2% of all cancers in HK; accounts for ~10% of haematological malignancies.
* **Age:** Disease of the elderly; median age of onset ~65-70 years.
* **Gender:** Male > Female (slight predominance).
* **Trend:** Rising incidence locally due to an aging population and improved detection.

### Etiologies & Risk Factors for Multiple Myeloma
* **Precursor States:** Almost all cases progress from **Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance (MGUS; 意義不明的單株丙種球蛋白血症)** (progresses at ~1%/year) or Smouldering Multiple Myeloma (SMM).
* **Environmental/Occupational:** Ionizing radiation, agricultural chemicals (pesticides/herbicides).
* **Genetic:** Familial clustering exists but rare; higher incidence in individuals of African descent globally.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Plasma Cell Clonal Proliferation Mechanisms
* Primary genetic events (e.g., translocations of IgH gene at 14q32) immortalize a post-germinal center plasma cell.
* Secondary mutations (e.g., NRAS, KRAS, TP53 mutations, MYC dysregulation) drive symptomatic clonal expansion within the bone marrow microenvironment.

### Pathophysiology of Bone Destruction (Lytic Lesions)
* **Uncoupling of Bone Remodeling:**
* Myeloma cells secrete RANKL, MIP-1α, and IL-6 → ↑ Osteoclast activation (bone resorption).
* Myeloma cells secrete DKK1 and sFRP-2 → Block Wnt signaling → ↓ Osteoblast differentiation (no bone formation).
* **Net Result:** Punched-out osteolytic lesions, pathological fractures, and ↑ Ca²⁺ release.

### Pathophysiology of Renal Impairment (Myeloma Kidney)
* **Cast Nephropathy:** Monoclonal free light chains (FLC) filter through the glomerulus → co-precipitate with Tamm-Horsfall protein in distal tubules → tubular obstruction and giant cell inflammatory reaction.
* **Other Mechanisms:** Light chain amyloidosis (AL amyloid), light chain deposition disease, hypercalcaemia-induced nephrogenic DI and renal vasoconstriction.

### Pathophysiology of Immunoparesis & Anemia
* **Immunoparesis:** Massive overproduction of monoclonal Ig suppresses normal polyclonal Ig production → profound hypogammaglobulinemia → ↑ risk of encapsulated bacterial infections (e.g., *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*).
* **Anemia:** Marrow crowding by plasma cells + ↑ inflammatory cytokines (IL-6) inducing hepcidin (anemia of chronic disease) + renal EPO deficiency.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Clinical History of Multiple Myeloma (CRAB Symptoms)
* **Bone Pain:** Classically in back (spine), ribs, and pelvis; exacerbated by movement. Hx of loss of height or pathological fractures.
* **Fatigue & Lethargy:** Secondary to progressive anemia or renal failure.
* **Hypercalcaemia Symptoms:** Polyuria, polydipsia, constipation, confusion, nausea.
* **Recurrent Infections:** Especially chest (pneumonia) and urinary tract.
* **Neurological Symptoms:** Radicular pain, limb weakness, numbness, bowel/bladder incontinence (cord compression).
* **Bleeding/Hyperviscosity:** Epistaxis, visual blurring, headache, mucosal bleeding.

### Physical Examination Findings in Multiple Myeloma
* **General:** Pallor, dehydration signs (dry mucosa, ↓ skin turgor), purpura/ecchymosis (if amyloidosis or thrombocytopenia).
* **Skeletal:** Exquisite bony tenderness (spinal percussion, rib springing), kyphosis, visible bony swellings (extramedullary plasmacytomas).
* **Neurological:** Sensory level, flaccid/spastic paraparesis, hyperreflexia, extensor plantar responses (cord compression), stocking-glove sensory loss (neuropathy).
* **Renal/Cardiovascular:** Peripheral oedema, raised JVP (if AL amyloidosis causing restrictive cardiomyopathy or severe nephropathy).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Plasma Cell Dyscrasias
| Diagnosis | M-Protein (Serum/Urine) | Bone Marrow Clonal Plasma Cells | End-Organ Damage (CRAB) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MGUS** | <30 g/L | <10% | Absent |
| **Smouldering Myeloma** | ≥30 g/L | 10 - 59% | Absent (Asymptomatic) |
| **Active Multiple Myeloma** | Any level | ≥10% (or plasmacytoma) | Present (CRAB or SLiM criteria) |

### Other Differentials for Monoclonal Gammopathy
* **Waldenström’s Macroglobulinemia:** Monoclonal IgM ONLY, presents with profound hyperviscosity syndrome, lymphadenopathy, and hepatosplenomegaly. Lytic bone lesions are ABSENT.
* **AL Amyloidosis:** M-protein present, but presents with nephrotic syndrome, macroglossia, periorbital purpura ("raccoon eyes"), and heart failure (restrictive).

### Differentials for Lytic Bone Lesions
* **Solid Organ Metastases:** Breast, Lung, Thyroid, Kidney, Prostate (PB KTL). Note: Prostate is typically osteoblastic, but others can be lytic. No M-band on SPEP.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Suspected Myeloma
* **Urine Dipstick:** Often FALSELY NEGATIVE for protein (dipstick primarily detects albumin, not Bence Jones light chains).
* **ECG:** Shortened QT interval (due to hypercalcaemia).

### Blood Investigations for Multiple Myeloma
* **CBP & Smear:** Normocytic normochromic anemia, **Rouleaux formation** (RBCs stacked like coins).
* **Renal & Bone Profile:** ↑ Urea, ↑ Creatinine, ↑ Ca²⁺, Normal/↓ PO4, **Normal Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)** (unless resolving fracture).
* **Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP):** Monoclonal band (M-spike) in gamma region (IgG > IgA > IgM).
* **Serum Free Light Chain (SFLC) Assay:** ↑ Kappa (κ) or Lambda (λ) chains; abnormal κ/λ ratio (<0.26 or >1.65).
* **Immunofixation (IFE):** Confirms clonality and determines heavy/light chain isotype.
* **Prognostic Baseline Markers:** β2-microglobulin (β2M), LDH, Serum Albumin.

### Urine Investigations for Multiple Myeloma
* **24-hour Urine Total Protein & UPEP:** Detection and quantification of **Bence Jones proteins** (monoclonal free light chains).

### Imaging for Skeletal Survey in Myeloma
* **Whole-Body Low-Dose CT (WBLDCT):** Current gold standard in HA. Replaces plain x-rays. Shows punched-out lytic lesions without sclerotic margins ("pepper-pot" skull).
* **MRI Whole Spine/Pelvis:** Highest sensitivity for detecting diffuse marrow infiltration and impending cord compression.
* **PET-CT:** Used to detect and monitor extramedullary plasmacytomas.
* *Note: Radionuclide bone scans are CONTRAINDICATED (rely on osteoblastic activity which is suppressed in MM).*

### Biopsy Investigations in Multiple Myeloma
* **Bone Marrow Aspirate & Trephine (BMAT):** Morphological confirmation of >10% clonal plasma cells (eccentric nucleus, basophilic cytoplasm, perinuclear halo).
* **Cytogenetics/FISH on BM:** Detects high-risk mutations [del(17p), t(4;14), t(14;16)].

### IMWG Diagnostic Criteria for Active Multiple Myeloma
* **Required:** Clonal BM plasma cells ≥10% OR biopsy-proven bony/extramedullary plasmacytoma.
* **PLUS ≥1 Myeloma Defining Event (CRAB or SLiM):**
| Criteria Type | Specific Parameters |
| :--- | :--- |
| **C**alcium Elevation | Serum Ca >0.25 mmol/L above upper limit, or >2.75 mmol/L |
| **R**enal Insufficiency | Creatinine clearance <40 mL/min or Serum Creatinine >177 µmol/L |
| **A**nemia | Hb <10 g/dL or >2 g/dL below lower limit of normal |
| **B**one Lesions | ≥1 osteolytic lesion on plain radiograph, CT, or PET-CT |
| **S**ixty percent BM | Clonal BM plasma cells ≥60% |
| **Li**ght chain ratio | Involved/uninvolved SFLC ratio ≥100 (involved FLC must be ≥100 mg/L) |
| **M**RI lesions | >1 focal lesion on MRI (each ≥5 mm in size) |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Hypercalcaemia in Myeloma
* **Aggressive IV Hydration:** 0.9% NaCl (3-4 L/day) to promote calciuresis.
* **IV Bisphosphonates:** **Zoledronic acid** or **Pamidronate** (onset in 48-72 hrs). Adjust dose for renal impairment.
* **SC Calcitonin:** For severe/symptomatic cases requiring rapid but transient ↓ in Ca²⁺ (tachyphylaxis occurs after 48 hrs).
* **Tumor-Directed Therapy:** IV **Hydrocortisone** or **Dexamethasone** to target underlying plasma cells.

### Acute Management of Myeloma Cast Nephropathy
* **Fluid Resuscitation:** IV 0.9% NaCl to maintain high urine output (>3 L/day) to flush out casts.
* **Nephrotoxin Avoidance:** Strict cessation of NSAIDs; avoid IV contrast.
* **Rapid Light Chain Reduction:** High-dose IV **Dexamethasone** immediately.
* **Renal Replacement Therapy:** Standard Haemodialysis for refractory uraemia, fluid overload, or hyperkalemia. (High-cut-off dialysis to remove FLC is controversial/case-by-case).

### Acute Management of Hyperviscosity Syndrome
* **Resuscitation:** Urgent Plasmapheresis (Plasma exchange) to rapidly remove circulating M-protein and restore microcirculation.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Treatment Algorithm for Transplant-Eligible Multiple Myeloma
* **Target Patient:** Generally <65 years, good performance status, normal organ function.
* **1. Induction (4-6 cycles):** Triplet or Quadruplet combinations.
  * **VRd:** **V**ortezomib (Proteasome Inhibitor) + **R**enalidomide (IMiD) + **d**examethasone.
  * **D-VTd:** **D**aratumumab (Anti-CD38 mAb) + **V**ortezomib + **T**halidomide + **d**examethasone (HA preferred for high-risk).
* **2. Consolidation:** High-dose **Melphalan** followed by Autologous Stem Cell Transplant (ASCT).
* **3. Maintenance:** Continuous **Lenalidomide** until disease progression or intolerance.

### Treatment Algorithm for Transplant-Ineligible Multiple Myeloma
* **Target Patient:** Elderly (>65) or significant comorbidities.
* **Continuous Therapy Strategy:**
  * **DRd:** **D**aratumumab + **R**enalidomide + **d**examethasone (Standard of care).
  * **VRd-lite:** Dose-reduced **Bortezomib** + **Lenalidomide** + **Dexamethasone**.
  * Note: High-dose Melphalan is avoided due to high toxicity.

### Relapsed/Refractory Myeloma Management
* **Class Switching:** Utilize drugs from unexposed classes or next-generation agents.
* **Second/Third-line Agents:** **Carfilzomib** (2nd-gen PI), **Pomalidomide** (3rd-gen IMiD), **Elotuzumab** (anti-SLAMF7 mAb).
* **Advanced Therapies:** Bispecific T-cell engagers (e.g., **Teclistamab**) or Anti-BCMA CAR-T cell therapy.

### Supportive Chronic Care in Multiple Myeloma
* **Bone Health:** Monthly IV **Zoledronic acid** or SC **Denosumab** (mandatory dental check prior to Rx to prevent MRONJ).
* **Infection Prophylaxis:** 
  * Oral **Aciclovir** (mandatory if on Bortezomib/Daratumumab to prevent VZV reactivation).
  * Oral **Co-trimoxazole** (PJP prophylaxis if on high-dose steroids).
  * Inactivated vaccines (Pneumococcal, Influenza, COVID-19); strictly NO live vaccines.
* **Thromboprophylaxis:** Required for IMiD (Lenalidomide/Thalidomide) + Steroid combinations. Low-dose **Aspirin** (low risk) or DOAC/LMWH (high risk).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Revised International Staging System (R-ISS) for Multiple Myeloma
* Combines tumor burden (ISS) with disease biology (Cytogenetics + LDH).
| Stage | Criteria | Median Survival |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage I** | Serum β2M <3.5 mg/L AND Serum Albumin ≥35 g/L AND Standard-risk cytogenetics AND Normal LDH | ~114 months |
| **Stage II** | Not fitting Stage I or Stage III | ~83 months |
| **Stage III** | Serum β2M >5.5 mg/L AND (High-risk cytogenetics OR High LDH) | ~43 months |

### Cytogenetic Risk Stratification (mSMART)
* **High Risk:** del(17p), t(4;14), t(14;16), t(14;20). (Indicates aggressive disease requiring quadruplet induction).
* **Standard Risk:** t(11;14), hyperdiploidy.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications of Multiple Myeloma
* **Skeletal:** Pathological fractures (femur, humerus), spinal cord compression (oncological emergency).
* **Renal:** End-stage renal disease (ESRD) requiring permanent haemodialysis.
* **Haematological:** Refractory cytopenias, bleeding diathesis (paraprotein interferes with platelet function and clotting factors).
* **Neurological:** AL Amyloid neuropathy (autonomic dysfunction, orthostatic hypotension), hyperviscosity-induced ischemic stroke.

### Treatment-Related Adverse Effects (Drug Toxicity)
* **Bortezomib:** Peripheral sensory neuropathy (dose-limiting), Herpes Zoster reactivation.
* **Lenalidomide / Thalidomide:** Venous Thromboembolism (DVT/PE), severe teratogenicity, myelosuppression (Lenalidomide).
* **Daratumumab:** Infusion-related reactions, pan-reactivity on RBC cross-matching (binds CD38 on RBCs; must notify blood bank).
* **Bisphosphonates:** Medication-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (MRONJ), atypical femoral fractures, nephrotoxicity.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Assuming a negative urine dipstick rules out myeloma renal disease. Pearl: Standard urine dipsticks detect albumin, NOT Bence Jones proteins (free light chains). You must send a dedicated 24-hour urine total protein and UPEP.
* Trap: Ordering a radionuclide bone scan for a patient with suspected myeloma bone pain. Pearl: Radionuclide scans rely on osteoblastic activity. Myeloma lytic lesions exclusively involve osteoclast activation with suppressed osteoblasts; bone scans will be falsely negative. Order a WBLDCT or MRI instead.
* Viva Question: Why does a patient with multiple myeloma have a normal Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) despite widespread lytic bone lesions? Answer: ALP is a marker of osteoblast activity. Myeloma uncouples bone remodeling, severely suppressing osteoblasts, meaning ALP remains normal unless there is a healing fracture.
* Pitfall: Ordering an IV contrast CT scan for a myeloma patient. Intravenous contrast can precipitate with light chains in the renal tubules, precipitating acute cast nephropathy and anuric acute kidney injury.
* Viva Question: What is the mandatory infectious disease prophylaxis when starting a patient on Bortezomib? Answer: Prophylactic oral Aciclovir is mandatory because proteasome inhibitors heavily suppress T-cell function, leading to a high rate of Herpes Zoster (VZV) reactivation.
* Trap: Assuming MGUS requires treatment. Pearl: MGUS (by definition <30g/L M-protein, <10% plasma cells, NO CRAB symptoms) requires "watch and wait" observation only, as the risk of progression is ~1% per year.
