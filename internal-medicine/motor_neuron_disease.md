# Motor Neuron Disease (運動神經元病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Motor Neuron Disease
* **Pathological Hallmmark:** Progressive, unremitting neurodegenerative disorder affecting both Upper Motor Neurons (UMN) in motor cortex and Lower Motor Neurons (LMN) in brainstem/anterior horn cells. 
* **Classic Presentation:** Purely motor. Sensation, eye movements, and autonomic/sphincter functions are classically spared.

### Clinical Phenotypes of MND
* **Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) (肌萎縮性脊髓側索硬化症):** Most common (80%). Mixed UMN + LMN signs.
* **Progressive Bulbar Palsy (PBP):** Brainstem LMN + UMN. Dysarthria, dysphagia. Often progresses to ALS.
* **Progressive Muscular Atrophy (PMA):** Pure LMN signs only.
* **Primary Lateral Sclerosis (PLS):** Pure UMN signs only. Slower progression.

### Local Epidemiology & Etiology
* **Incidence:** ~1-2 per 100,000 in HK/Asia. 
* **Demographics:** M > F (1.5:1). Peak onset 50-70 years.
* **Genetics:** 
  * 90% Sporadic.
  * 10% Familial: **C9orf72** repeat expansion (most common, linked to Frontotemporal Dementia), **SOD1**, **TARDBP** (TDP-43), **FUS**.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Upper & Lower Motor Neuron Degeneration
* **UMN Loss:** Demyelination and axonal loss in corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts → loss of descending inhibition → spasticity/hyperreflexia.
* **LMN Loss:** Apoptosis of anterior horn cells (spinal cord) and motor nuclei (brainstem CN IX, X, XII) → denervation of skeletal muscle → atrophy and fasciculations.

### Molecular Mechanisms of Neurotoxicity
* **Glutamate Excitotoxicity:** Dysfunction of astrocytic excitatory amino acid transporter 2 (EAAT2) → ↑ synaptic glutamate → massive Ca2+ influx → neuronal death.
* **Protein Aggregation:** Ubiquitinated **TDP-43** cytoplasmic inclusions form in >97% of ALS cases (except SOD1 mutations).
* **Oxidative Stress:** **SOD1** mutation → defective handling of reactive oxygen species (ROS) → mitochondrial dysfunction.
* **Axonal Transport Defect:** Disruption of neurofilament assembly impairs anterograde/retrograde transport.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Presenting Illness
* **Limb Onset (70%):** Insidious, asymmetric painless weakness. Frequent tripping (foot drop), difficulty manipulating small objects (buttoning shirts), dropping cups. 
* **Bulbar Onset (25%):** Dysphagia (liquids > solids initially), dysarthria (nasal/slurred speech), choking, excessive sialorrhea (drooling).
* **Respiratory Symptoms:** Orthopnea, unrefreshing sleep, morning headaches (nocturnal hypercapnia).
* **Cognitive Symptoms:** Co-existing Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD) in 15% (apathy, disinhibition, loss of executive function).
* **Negative Symptoms:** NO sensory loss, NO pain, NO bowel/bladder incontinence, NO diplopia.

### System-Specific Physical Signs
* **LMN Signs:** 
  * Flaccid tone.
  * Hyporeflexia / Areflexia.
  * Muscle wasting (especially intrinsic hand muscles: split-hand sign).
  * Prominent **fasciculations** (observable twitches in resting muscle, classically tongue, deltoid, thighs).
* **UMN Signs:**
  * Spastic catch / rigidity.
  * Hyperreflexia (brisk knee/ankle jerks, brisk jaw jerk).
  * Pathological reflexes: +ve Babinski sign (extensor plantar), +ve Hoffmann's sign, Clonus.
  * **Pseudobulbar affect:** Emotional lability (inappropriate laughing/crying unlinked to true mood).
* **Pathognomonic Presentation:** Wasted, fasciculating muscles coexisting with hyperreflexia in the *same* myotome.
* **Spared Functions:** Extraocular eye movements (CN III, IV, VI) and anal sphincter tone (Onuf's nucleus spared).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### MND vs Main Differentials
| Disease | Discriminating Features against MND |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy** | Neck pain, **sensory level/loss** present, normal bulbar/cranial nerves. |
| **Multifocal Motor Neuropathy (MMN)** | Pure LMN, **Conduction block** on NCS, Anti-GM1 Ab +ve, responds to IVIg. |
| **Myasthenia Gravis (MG)** | Fluctuating weakness/fatigability, ptosis/diplopia (ocular involvement), purely NMJ (no UMN signs). |
| **Kennedy Disease (SBMA)** | X-linked (males only), pure LMN bulbar/proximal weakness, **gynecomastia**, testicular atrophy. |
| **Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM)** | Asymmetric weakness of finger flexors and quadriceps, mild ↑ CPK, purely myogenic, no UMN signs. |
| **Benign Fasciculation Syndrome** | Fasciculations without weakness, normal EMG, common in medical students/anxiety. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **Bedside:** FVC (Forced Vital Capacity) / SNIP (Sniff Nasal Inspiratory Pressure) → monitor respiratory muscle decline.
* **Routine Bloods:** CBP, RFT, LFT, Bone, HbA1c, TFT, B12/Folate (exclude metabolic/endocrine mimics).
* **Specific Bloods:**
  * **Creatine Kinase (CK):** Mildly ↑ (usually <1000 U/L) due to muscle denervation.
  * **Serum Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP):** Rule out paraproteinemic neuropathy.
  * **Anti-GM1 Antibodies:** Rule out MMN.
  * **Genetic testing:** SOD1, C9orf72 if strong family history or young onset.

### Imaging & Electrophysiology
* **MRI Brain & Whole Spine:** Normal in MND. Crucial to **exclude** cervical myelopathy, syringomyelia, or foramen magnum lesions.
* **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS):** Motor and sensory conduction velocities are typically **normal** (excludes demyelinating neuropathies). No conduction block.
* **Electromyography (EMG):** The Gold Standard. Must show both:
  * **Active denervation:** Fibrillation potentials, positive sharp waves (PSWs).
  * **Chronic reinnervation:** Large amplitude, long duration, polyphasic motor unit action potentials (MUAPs).

### Diagnostic Criteria (Revised El Escorial / Awaji-Shima)
* **Principle:** Evidence of mixed LMN and UMN signs across multiple neuraxial regions.
* **Regions:** Bulbar, Cervical, Thoracic, Lumbosacral.
* **Clinically Definite ALS:** Clinical or electrophysiological evidence of LMN and UMN signs in ≥3 regions.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Type II Respiratory Failure
* **Trigger:** Intercurrent chest infection (LRTI) or aspiration.
* **Assessment:** ABG shows hypercapnia (↑ PaCO2), hypoxia (↓ PaO2), respiratory acidosis.
* **Management:** 
  * **DO NOT** give high-flow oxygen (exacerbates CO2 retention by blunting hypoxic drive).
  * Initiate emergency **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP)**.
  * Treat underlying trigger (IV antibiotics for pneumonia).
  * Secretion clearance: Physiotherapy, mechanical insufflation-exsufflation (CoughAssist).
  * **Avoid Invasive Ventilation/Intubation** unless it aligns with patient's Advance Directive (often leads to failure to wean and permanent tracheostomy).

### Acute Aspiration / Choking
* **Management:** Immediate suction, lateral decubitus positioning, NPO (nil by mouth).
* **Post-choking:** Chest X-ray to rule out aspiration pneumonitis/pneumonia. Broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., Co-amoxiclav) if febrile.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Disease-Modifying Pharmacotherapy
* **Riluzole (Glutamate antagonist):**
  * ONLY drug proven to extend survival (by ~2-3 months) and delay need for ventilation.
  * Dose: 50mg PO BD.
  * Monitoring: Monthly LFTs for first 3 months (risk of transaminitis), monitor for neutropenia.
* **Edaravone (Free radical scavenger):**
  * Modest slowing of functional decline in early-stage ALS. IV infusion. (Less commonly used in HA due to cost/benefit ratio).

### Symptomatic Management (HA Formulary Focus)
* **Spasticity:** Physiotherapy + **Baclofen** or **Tizanidine** or Gabapentin.
* **Sialorrhea (Drooling):** 
  * 1st line: Anticholinergics → **Hyoscine hydrobromide** (Scopolamine) patch, **Glycopyrronium bromide**, Amitriptyline.
  * 2nd line: Botulinum toxin injection into salivary glands.
* **Muscle Cramps:** Quinine sulphate (historic/caution with cardiac QT), Levetiracetam, Gabapentin.
* **Pseudobulbar Affect:** Amitriptyline, SSRIs (Citalopram), Dextromethorphan-Quinidine.
* **Terminal Dyspnea / Restlessness:** Subcutaneous/Syringe driver Morphine and Midazolam.

### Multidisciplinary & Interventional Management
* **Respiratory (Crucial):** Early home **NIV (BiPAP)** at night when FVC <50%, SNIP <40cmH2O, or symptomatic orthopnea. *Improves survival more than Riluzole.*
* **Nutrition:** Early **Radiologically Inserted Gastrostomy (RIG)** or **PEG**. Must insert BEFORE FVC drops <50% to minimize procedural respiratory risk. Prevents aspiration and cachexia.
* **Allied Health:** Speech Therapist (Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) devices, modified diet texture), Occupational Therapist (wheelchair, home modifications).
* **Advance Care Planning (ACP):** Crucial in HK HA setting. Early discussion of DNACPR (Do Not Attempt Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) and feeding/ventilation preferences.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Clinical Scoring Systems
* **ALSFRS-R (ALS Functional Rating Scale - Revised):**
  * 12-item questionnaire scoring 0-4 (Max 48 = normal).
  * 4 Domains: Bulbar, Fine Motor, Gross Motor, Respiratory.
  * Used to track disease progression rate.

### Prognosis & Poor Prognostic Factors
* **Median Survival:** 2 to 5 years from symptom onset.
* **Cause of Death:** Usually Type II Respiratory Failure / Bronchopneumonia.
* **Poor Prognosticators:**
  * Bulbar onset (rapid progression to aspiration).
  * Older age at onset.
  * Concomitant Frontotemporal Dementia.
  * Early onset of respiratory muscle weakness.
  * Rapid decline in ALSFRS-R score.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications
* **Pulmonary:** Aspiration pneumonia, Cor Pulmonale (secondary to chronic hypoxia/hypercapnia).
* **Gastrointestinal:** Severe cachexia/malnutrition, severe constipation (immobility and weak abdominal press).
* **Musculoskeletal:** Joint contractures, pressure ulcers, falls/fractures.
* **Vascular:** Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) / Pulmonary Embolism (PE) due to profound immobility.

### Treatment-Related Adverse Effects
* **Riluzole:** Transaminitis (↑ ALT), nausea, asthenia, interstitial lung disease (rare).
* **BiPAP:** Nasal bridge pressure sores, gastric distension, eye irritation.
* **PEG/RIG:** Peritonitis, stoma site infection, tube dislodgement.
* **Anticholinergics (for drooling):** Urinary retention, constipation, delirium, blurry vision.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Assuming sensory loss excludes MND. Pearl: MND is purely a MOTOR disease. If sensory signs are present, think Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy or peripheral neuropathy.
* Viva Question: Which motor functions are classically spared in MND? Answer: Extraocular eye movements (CN III, IV, VI) and sphincter functions (Onuf's nucleus in the sacral cord is spared).
* Pitfall: Giving high-flow oxygen to an MND patient with acute dyspnea. Worsens hypercapnic respiratory failure by blunting the hypoxic drive. Use BiPAP instead.
* Viva Question: What is the only disease-modifying drug proven to prolong survival in ALS? Answer: Riluzole (a glutamate release inhibitor).
* Trap: Diagnosing MND in a young man presenting with LMN signs, bulbar weakness, and gynecomastia. Pearl: This is Kennedy's disease (Spinobulbar muscular atrophy), an X-linked CAG repeat disorder, NOT MND.
* Viva Question: What is the classic EMG finding in Motor Neuron Disease? Answer: Active denervation (fibrillations, positive sharp waves) combined with chronic reinnervation (large amplitude, polyphasic MUAPs) in multiple regions.
* Pitfall: Delaying PEG tube insertion until the patient can no longer swallow. Insertion becomes highly dangerous if FVC falls below 50%. Must perform RIG/PEG early.
