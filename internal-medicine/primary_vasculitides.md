# Primary Vasculitides (原發性血管炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Primary Vasculitides
* **Primary Vasculitides (原發性血管炎)**: A clinicopathologic group of idiopathic, immune-mediated diseases characterized by inflammation and necrosis of blood vessel walls, leading to ischemia, aneurysm, or hemorrhage.
* **Classification**: Based on the Chapel Hill Consensus Conference (CHCC) 2012 nomenclature (size of predominantly affected vessels).

### Etiology and Pathogenesis Triggers
* **Etiology**: Idiopathic; likely environmental triggers in genetically susceptible hosts.
* **Infectious triggers**: Hepatitis B virus (HBV) → Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN); Hepatitis C virus (HCV) → Cryoglobulinemic Vasculitis.
* **Drug triggers**: Propylthiouracil (PTU), Hydralazine, Levamisole (adulterated cocaine) → ANCA-associated vasculitis (AAV).

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong Context)
* **Large Vessel Vasculitis (LVV)**: 
  * **Takayasu Arteritis (多發性大動脈炎)**: More common in Asia (including HK); classic demographic = young Asian females <40yo.
  * **Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA) (巨細胞動脈炎)**: Extremely rare in Chinese populations; predominantly affects Caucasians >50yo.
* **ANCA-Associated Vasculitis (AAV)**:
  * **Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA)** > **Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA)** in Chinese populations (reverse of Caucasian demographics).
  * **p-ANCA/MPO-ANCA** is the predominant antibody pattern in HK patients.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Large Vessel Vasculitis (GCA & Takayasu)
* **Initiation**: Dendritic cells in vessel adventitia activate CD4+ T-cells (Th1/Th17 pathway).
* **Propagation**: Release of IFN-γ and IL-6 → macrophage recruitment and giant cell formation.
* **End-Organ Effect**: Granulomatous inflammation of vasa vasorum → medial destruction (aneurysms) + intimal hyperplasia (luminal stenosis/occlusion).

### Pathophysiology of Medium Vessel Vasculitis (PAN)
* **Initiation**: Transmural necrotizing inflammation of muscular arteries.
* **Sparing**: Strictly spares capillaries and venules (∴ no glomerulonephritis).
* **End-Organ Effect**: Elastic lamina destruction → microaneurysm formation. Thrombosis of inflamed segments → patchy organ ischemia/infarction.

### Pathophysiology of ANCA-Associated Small Vessel Vasculitis (AAV)
* **Priming**: Cytokines (e.g., TNF-α) from intercurrent infection prime neutrophils to express intra-cytoplasmic PR3 or MPO antigens on their cell surface.
* **Activation**: **ANCA (Anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies)** bind to PR3/MPO → excessive neutrophil activation and degranulation.
* **End-Organ Effect**: Release of Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) and lytic enzymes → direct endothelial destruction → **Pauci-immune** (minimal immune complex) necrotizing vasculitis.

### Pathophysiology of Immune-Complex Small Vessel Vasculitis
* **Initiation**: High circulating levels of Antigen-Antibody complexes (e.g., IgA in HSP, Cryoglobulins).
* **Deposition**: Complexes lodge in post-capillary venules.
* **End-Organ Effect**: Classical complement pathway activation (C3/C4 consumption) → C5a recruits neutrophils → leukocytoclasis (nuclear dust) → fibrinous necrosis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History and Symptoms of Large Vessel Vasculitis
* **GCA**: New-onset temporal headache, jaw claudication (chewing pain), amaurosis fugax (transient visual loss), polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) symptoms (shoulder/hip stiffness).
* **Takayasu**: Arm claudication with exertion, presyncope/syncope, visual blurring, neck pain (carotodynia).

### Physical Signs of Large Vessel Vasculitis
* **GCA**: Tender, nodular, thickened, or pulseless temporal arteries.
* **Takayasu**: **Pulseless disease** (absent/asymmetric radial/brachial pulses), >10 mmHg BP discrepancy between arms, carotid/subclavian bruits, aortic regurgitation murmur (aortic root dilatation).

### History and Symptoms of Medium Vessel Vasculitis (PAN)
* **GI**: Post-prandial abdominal angina, melena.
* **GU**: Testicular/ovarian pain.
* **Neuro**: Sudden onset foot drop or wrist drop (**Mononeuritis Multiplex**).

### Physical Signs of Medium Vessel Vasculitis (PAN)
* **Derm**: **Livedo reticularis** (網狀青斑), subcutaneous nodules, digital gangrene.
* **Neuro**: Asymmetric sensory/motor neuropathy matching individual nerve territories.

### History and Symptoms of Small Vessel Vasculitis (AAV & Immune-Complex)
* **GPA (Wegener's)**: Recurrent epistaxis, bloody nasal crusting, hearing loss (otitis media), hemoptysis (alveolar hemorrhage).
* **EGPA (Churg-Strauss)**: Late-onset, difficult-to-control asthma, allergic rhinitis.
* **IgA Vasculitis (HSP)**: Colicky abdominal pain, large joint arthralgia.

### Physical Signs of Small Vessel Vasculitis (AAV & Immune-Complex)
* **ENT (GPA)**: **Saddle-nose deformity** (鞍鼻畸形), nasal septal perforation, proptosis (retro-orbital granuloma).
* **Derm**: **Palpable purpura** (可觸及的紫癜) over dependent areas (lower limbs/buttocks in HSP), splinter hemorrhages.
* **Resp (EGPA)**: Polyphonic wheezing, nasal polyps.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Infectious Mimics of Vasculitis
* **Infective Endocarditis (IE)**: Mimics SVV with purpura, hematuria, +ve ANCA (false +ve), but has murmurs and +ve blood cultures.
* **Syphilis**: Can cause aortitis mimicking Takayasu, but has +ve VDRL/RPR.
* **Hepatitis B/C or HIV**: Can drive secondary PAN/Cryoglobulinemia; viral serology differentiates from primary vasculitis.

### Malignancy and Embolic Mimics
* **Atrial Myxoma**: Causes constitutional symptoms + embolic phenomena (mimicking systemic vasculitis); identified by echo.
* **Lymphoma / Leukemia**: Constitutional symptoms + cytopenias; lacks specific ANCA/immune complexes.
* **Cholesterol Embolization Syndrome**: Occurs post-angiography; causes livedo reticularis + renal failure (eosinophilia present), mimics PAN/AAV.

### Secondary Autoimmune & Connective Tissue Diseases
* **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)**: Can cause secondary vasculitis, but has +ve ANA, +ve anti-dsDNA, ↓ C3/C4 (unlike normal complement in AAV).
* **Rheumatoid Vasculitis**: Occurs in long-standing erosive RA with high RF/anti-CCP titers.

### Structural / Vasospastic Mimics
* **Fibromuscular Dysplasia (FMD)**: Mimics PAN or Takayasu angiographically ("string of beads"), but typically affects young females without systemic inflammation (normal ESR/CRP).
* **Severe Raynaud's Phenomenon**: Digital ischemia without systemic inflammation.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Urinalysis (Mid-stream)**: +ve dysmorphic RBCs, +ve RBC casts (pathognomonic for glomerulonephritis), sub-nephrotic proteinuria.
* **ECG**: Ischemic changes (coronary arteritis in PAN/Takayasu), PR interval prolongation.

### Blood & Serological Investigations
* **Hematology**: CBC (Normocytic normochromic anemia of chronic disease, prominent **eosinophilia** in EGPA).
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ESR & CRP (markedly ↑↑).
* **Immunology / Autoantibodies**:
  * **c-ANCA (PR3)**: Highly sensitive/specific for GPA.
  * **p-ANCA (MPO)**: Highly sensitive/specific for MPA and EGPA.
  * **ANA, anti-dsDNA, C3/C4**: To exclude SLE/Hypocomplementemic urticarial vasculitis.
  * **Anti-GBM**: To rule out Goodpasture's disease (can coexist with ANCA).
  * **Cryoglobulins**: +ve in cryoglobulinemic vasculitis.
* **Virology**: HBsAg, anti-HCV, HIV (crucial before immunosuppression).

### Imaging & Angiography
* **CXR**: Nodules/cavities (GPA), fleeting pulmonary infiltrates (EGPA), diffuse ground-glass opacities (diffuse alveolar hemorrhage in MPA/GPA).
* **CT/MR Angiography (CTA/MRA) or PET-CT**:
  * **Takayasu**: Concentric aortic wall thickening, long-segment stenoses, occlusions of aortic arch branches.
  * **PAN**: Classic **"rosary sign"** (microaneurysms alternating with strictures) in mesenteric or renal arteries.

### Invasive Biopsy Patterns
* **Temporal Artery (GCA)**: Disrupted internal elastic lamina, giant cells, mononuclear infiltrates. *Note: Skip lesions common; requires >2cm biopsy.*
* **Renal (AAV)**: **Pauci-immune necrotizing crescentic glomerulonephritis** (no Ig/complement deposition on immunofluorescence).
* **Sural Nerve / Skin**: Leukocytoclastic vasculitis (SVV) or necrotizing arteritis (PAN).

### Chapel Hill Consensus Conference (CHCC) 2012 Classification Summary
| Vessel Size | Vasculitis Type | Key Diagnostic Hallmark |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Large** | Takayasu Arteritis | Aorta/major branches, onset <40yo. |
| **Large** | Giant Cell Arteritis | Aorta/carotid branches, onset >50yo, +ve PMR. |
| **Medium** | Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN) | Necrotizing, spares capillaries, +ve microaneurysms. |
| **Medium** | Kawasaki Disease | Coronary arteritis, muco-cutaneous lymph node syndrome. |
| **Small (AAV)** | GPA (Wegener's) | +ve PR3-ANCA, Granulomas, ENT/Lung/Renal. |
| **Small (AAV)** | MPA | +ve MPO-ANCA, No Granulomas, Lung/Renal. |
| **Small (AAV)** | EGPA (Churg-Strauss) | +ve MPO-ANCA, Eosinophilia, Asthma, Granulomas. |
| **Small (Immune)**| IgA Vasculitis (HSP) | IgA1 dominant immune deposits. |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Resuscitation for Organ/Life-Threatening Flares (e.g., Pulmonary Renal Syndrome)
* **Airway/Breathing**: Endotracheal intubation + mechanical ventilation for massive Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage (DAH) causing hypoxemic respiratory failure.
* **Circulation**: Aggressive blood transfusion for severe hemorrhagic shock 2° to DAH or ruptured aneurysm.

### Acute Blindness Prevention (Suspected GCA)
* **Immediate Corticosteroids**: Do NOT wait for temporal artery biopsy.
* **Dosing**: IV Methylprednisolone 500-1000 mg/day for 3 days (if visual loss present) → transition to PO Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day.

### Acute Remission Induction for Severe AAV (GPA/MPA/EGPA)
* **High-Dose Steroids**: IV Methylprednisolone 500-1000 mg/day for 3 days → followed by high-dose PO Prednisolone taper.
* **Cytotoxic Therapy (Choose ONE)**:
  * **IV Cyclophosphamide (CYC)**: Cyclical pulsed dosing (local HA protocol preferred to minimize cumulative toxicity).
  * **IV Rituximab (RTX)**: Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. Preferred in relapsing disease, women of childbearing age (avoids ovarian failure), or PR3-ANCA +ve disease.

### Role of Therapeutic Plasma Exchange (Plasmapheresis / PLEX)
* **Indications**: 
  1. Concurrent Anti-GBM disease.
  2. Very severe renal failure (Cr >500 µmol/L) or dialysis dependence at presentation.
  3. Severe DAH (hypoxemic).
* *Note: PEXIVAS trial showed PLEX reduces ESRD risk at 1 year but does NOT change overall mortality in AAV.*

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Maintenance Therapy Algorithm (AAV & PAN)
* **Goal**: Prevent relapse after achieving remission (usually given for 18-24 months).
* **First-line Agents**: 
  * **PO Azathioprine (AZA)** (Requires TPMT activity checking prior to use).
  * **PO Methotrexate (MTX)** (If normal renal function).
  * **IV Rituximab (RTX)** (500mg fixed dose every 6 months - highly effective for GPA maintenance).
* **Glucocorticoid Taper**: Gradually reduce PO Prednisolone to lowest effective dose (e.g., <5-7.5mg/day) or stop completely.

### Novel Biologics & Targeted Therapies
* **Avacopan**: Oral C5a receptor antagonist. Used as a steroid-sparing agent in AAV induction (eliminates need for high-dose steroids, ↓ side effects).
* **Tocilizumab**: Anti-IL-6 receptor monoclonal antibody. FDA approved for GCA; drastically ↓ cumulative steroid dose and relapse rates.
* **Mepolizumab**: Anti-IL-5 monoclonal antibody. Specifically used for relapsing/refractory EGPA to target eosinophils.

### Hepatitis-B Associated PAN Management
* **Antiviral Therapy**: First-line (e.g., Entecavir or Tenofovir) to clear/suppress viremia.
* **Immunosuppression**: Short course of steroids + Plasmapheresis (to clear immune complexes); **Avoid** prolonged cyclophosphamide as it exacerbates HBV replication.

### Supportive & Prophylactic Pharmacotherapy (Crucial in HK HA)
* **PCP Prophylaxis**: PO Co-trimoxazole 480mg daily (or 960mg thrice weekly) while on heavy immunosuppression. Also reduces nasal relapses in GPA.
* **Bone Protection**: PO Calcium + Vitamin D + Bisphosphonates (e.g., Alendronate) to prevent steroid-induced osteoporosis.
* **Gastric Protection**: PPI (e.g., Omeprazole) while on high-dose steroids.
* **Vaccination**: Influenza, Pneumococcal, and COVID-19 vaccines (Must be given prior to starting Rituximab; Live vaccines are strictly contraindicated).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Five-Factor Score (FFS) 2009 for AAV and PAN
* **Purpose**: Predicts 5-year mortality and dictates aggressiveness of therapy.
* **Criteria** (1 point each):
  1. Age > 65 years.
  2. Cardiac involvement.
  3. Gastrointestinal involvement (bleeding, perforation, infarction).
  4. Renal insufficiency (Creatinine > 150 µmol/L).
  5. Absence of ENT involvement (ENT involvement in GPA actually portends a *better* systemic prognosis).
* **Interpretation**: Score 0 = 5-year mortality 9%; Score ≥2 = 5-year mortality 40% (Requires Cyclophosphamide/Rituximab).

### Disease Activity Scoring
* **Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score (BVAS)**: Comprehensive checklist used in clinical trials and specialist clinics to quantify disease activity/flare versus remission.

### Prognosis and Relapse Rates
* **Untreated AAV**: >90% mortality at 1 year (usually from renal/respiratory failure).
* **Treated AAV**: 5-year survival ~80%.
* **Relapse Risk**: PR3-ANCA (GPA) has a significantly higher relapse rate (up to 50% at 5 years) compared to MPO-ANCA (MPA).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related End-Organ Complications
* **Renal**: End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) requiring lifelong hemodialysis or transplant.
* **Respiratory**: Pulmonary fibrosis, chronic subglottic stenosis (stridor).
* **Cardiovascular**: Aortic aneurysm rupture/dissection (Takayasu/GCA), accelerated atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction.
* **Neurological**: Permanent vision loss (GCA), permanent foot/wrist drop causing gait instability.

### Drug-Induced Adverse Effects (Immunosuppressants)
* **Cyclophosphamide (CYC)**:
  * **Hemorrhagic cystitis & Bladder cancer** (transitional cell carcinoma). *Prevention: IV Mesna + aggressive hydration.*
  * Premature ovarian failure/infertility.
  * Severe myelosuppression and opportunistic infections.
* **Rituximab (RTX)**:
  * Hypogammaglobulinemia (requiring IVIG).
  * Hepatitis B reactivation (Fulminant hepatic failure).
  * Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) via JC virus activation.
* **Corticosteroids**: Avascular necrosis of femoral head, iatrogenic Cushing's, diabetes mellitus, cataracts.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate between Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA) and Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA)? Answer: Both cause pulmonary-renal syndrome, but GPA classically involves the upper respiratory tract (saddle nose, sinusitis, otitis media) and has granulomas on biopsy, whereas MPA lacks destructive ENT disease and granulomas.
* Trap: Delaying steroids in suspected Giant Cell Arteritis until a temporal artery biopsy is obtained. Pearl: Start high-dose IV/PO steroids immediately to prevent irreversible blindness; biopsy remains diagnostic for up to 2 weeks after initiating therapy.
* Pitfall: Prescribing Cyclophosphamide without assessing viral hepatitis status. In Hong Kong, HBV is highly endemic. Potent immunosuppression without prophylactic antivirals (Entecavir) will trigger fatal HBV reactivation.
* Viva Question: What is the classic angiographic finding of Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN)? Answer: The "rosary sign," which consists of multiple microaneurysms interspersed with areas of vascular stenosis, typically seen in the mesenteric or renal arteries.
* Trap: Assuming a positive p-ANCA/MPO-ANCA test guarantees a diagnosis of vasculitis. Pearl: p-ANCA can be falsely positive in infective endocarditis, inflammatory bowel disease, and SLE. Always correlate with clinical findings and consider blood cultures.
* Pitfall: Using azathioprine for maintenance therapy without checking TPMT (Thiopurine S-methyltransferase) status. Patients with absent TPMT enzyme activity will develop severe, life-threatening myelosuppression.
