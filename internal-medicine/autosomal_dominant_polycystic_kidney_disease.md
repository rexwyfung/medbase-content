# Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (常染色體顯性多囊腎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of ADPKD
* **Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD)**: A systemic monogenic disorder characterized by progressive development and enlargement of bilateral renal cysts, eventually destroying normal parenchyma → **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) (末期腎衰竭)**.

### Genetic Etiologies of ADPKD
* **PKD1 Mutation (85%)**: Chromosome 16. Codes for **Polycystin-1**. More severe, earlier onset of ESRD (mean age 55).
* **PKD2 Mutation (15%)**: Chromosome 4. Codes for **Polycystin-2**. Milder phenotype, later onset of ESRD (mean age 74).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: ~1:400 to 1:1000 globally.
* **HK HA Impact**: Accounts for ~5-10% of all ESRD patients requiring renal replacement therapy in Hong Kong. Most common inherited kidney disease.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Cellular Pathophysiology of Cystogenesis
* **Polycystin Complex Defect**: Polycystin-1 (mechanoreceptor) & Polycystin-2 (Ca2+ channel) normally localize to primary cilia of renal tubular epithelial cells.
* **Intracellular Signaling Cascade**: 
  * Defective primary cilia sensing → ↓ intracellular Ca2+ → ↑ adenylate cyclase activity → **↑ cAMP**.
  * ↑ cAMP → hyperproliferation of tubular epithelium & stimulation of **CFTR** chloride channels → ↑ transepithelial fluid secretion.
* **Cyst Expansion**: Micro-cysts bud off from parent tubules (mainly collecting ducts) → independent expansion → compression of adjacent normal nephrons → tissue ischaemia, interstitial fibrosis, and **RAAS activation**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Symptomatic History of ADPKD
* **Pain**: Chronic vague flank/lower back ache (cyst enlargement) or acute sharp pain (haemorrhage, infection, stone).
* **Urological**: Gross haematuria (血尿) following minimal trauma or exercise.
* **Family History**: 3-generation pedigree assessing for ESRD, dialysis, premature death, or **Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH) (蛛網膜下腔出血)**.

### Physical Examination Findings
* **Vitals**: **Hypertension (高血壓)** (Often the earliest sign, present before eGFR decline).
* **Abdomen**: 
  * Bilateral, palpable, ballotable flank masses (craggy, irregular kidneys).
  * Palpable hepatomegaly (due to massive hepatic cysts).
  * **Abdominal bruits**: Secondary to distorted renal vasculature (renovascular HTN).
* **Cardiovascular**: Mid-systolic click (Mitral Valve Prolapse) or early diastolic murmur (Aortic Regurgitation).
* **General**: Surgical scars (prior nephrectomy/transplant), dialysis fistulas/catheters.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating ADPKD from Other Cystic Diseases
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **ARPKD (常染色體隱性多囊腎)** | Pediatric onset, congenital hepatic fibrosis, oligohydramnios (Potter sequence), microscopic cysts (large echogenic kidneys on USG). |
| **Acquired Cystic Kidney Disease (ACKD)** | Small/shrunken kidneys, occurs after years of chronic dialysis, high risk of RCC. |
| **Tuberous Sclerosis Complex (TSC)** | Angiomyolipomas (AMLs), epilepsy, adenoma sebaceum, ash-leaf spots. |
| **Medullary Sponge Kidney (MSK)** | Cysts limited to medullary collecting ducts, nephrocalcinosis, usually asymptomatic unless passing stones. |
| **Simple Renal Cysts** | Asymptomatic, cortical, thin-walled, no solid components, normal renal size and function. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **Urinalysis**: +ve for RBCs (haematuria) and sub-nephrotic proteinuria.
* **Bloods**: U&Es (Cr, urea for baseline eGFR).

### Imaging & Unified Ultrasound Diagnostic Criteria (Pei-Ravine Criteria)
* **First-line Imaging**: Renal Ultrasound.
* **Diagnostic Criteria**: For at-risk individuals (with a positive family history of ADPKD).

| Age of Patient | Minimum Number of Cysts for Diagnosis |
| :--- | :--- |
| **15 – 39 years** | ≥ 3 cysts (unilateral or bilateral) |
| **40 – 59 years** | ≥ 2 cysts in *each* kidney |
| **≥ 60 years** | ≥ 4 cysts in *each* kidney |
* *Note: Absence of any cysts by age 40 strongly excludes PKD1 but does not completely rule out PKD2.*

### Advanced Imaging & Genetics
* **CT/MRI Abdomen**: For precise **Total Kidney Volume (TKV)** measurement, assessing complicated cysts (haemorrhagic vs infected), or suspected malignancy.
* **Genetic Testing (Next-Gen Sequencing)**: Not routine. Indicated if:
  * Equivocal imaging/no family history (de novo mutation).
  * Pre-implantation genetic diagnosis (PGD) / family planning.
  * Living related kidney donor evaluation.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Cyst Haemorrhage Protocol
* **Conservative Rx**: Strict bed rest, aggressive oral/IV hydration (prevents obstructive cast formation), analgesia (paracetamol/opioids).
* *Avoid NSAIDs* (worsens renal function). Usually self-limiting within 2-7 days.
* **Refractory haematuria**: Interventional radiology (IR) embolization or nephrectomy (last resort).

### Acute Cyst Infection (囊腫感染) Protocol
* **Pathogens**: Usually *E. coli* or *Klebsiella* (ascending UTI or bacteraemia).
* **Antibiotic Selection (CRITICAL)**: Must use highly lipid-soluble drugs that penetrate cyst walls.
  * **1st Line**: **Fluoroquinolones** (e.g., Ciprofloxacin) or **TMP-SMX** (Co-trimoxazole) for 2-4 weeks.
  * *Note: Penicillins, cephalosporins, and aminoglycosides have poor cyst penetration.*
* **Refractory/Large Cysts (>5cm)**: Percutaneous drainage via US/CT guidance.

### Acute Nephrolithiasis (Renal Stones) Protocol
* **Rx**: Hydration, analgesia. Uric acid stones are common → alkalize urine (potassium citrate).
* **Intervention**: ESWL or ureteroscopy for obstructing stones.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Hemodynamic & Renoprotective Pharmacotherapy
* **Strict BP Control**: Prevents left ventricular hypertrophy and slows ESRD progression.
  * **Target**: ≤ 130/80 mmHg (standard) or ≤ 110/75 mmHg (if age <50 and eGFR >60).
  * **1st Line**: **ACE inhibitors or ARBs** (inhibits cyst-induced RAAS activation). 
  * **2nd Line**: CCBs or Beta-blockers.

### Disease-Modifying Therapy: Tolvaptan (托伐普坦)
* **Mechanism**: Selective **Vasopressin V2 Receptor Antagonist**. ↓ cAMP → ↓ cyst proliferation and fluid secretion.
* **Indications (HK HA Context)**: Named-patient basis for **rapid progressors** (high Mayo class, rapidly enlarging TKV, rapid eGFR decline).
* **Requirements**: Must ensure extremely high daily water intake (aquaresis) to prevent dehydration.

### Lifestyle & Dietary Modifications
* **Fluid Intake**: Drink >3L water daily (physiologically suppresses endogenous AVP/vasopressin).
* **Diet**: Restrict dietary sodium (<2g/day) to aid BP control; moderate protein restriction.
* **Physical Activity**: Avoid contact sports (rugby, martial arts) due to risk of cyst rupture.

### Intracranial Aneurysm Screening Protocol
* **Routine screening (MRA Brain) is NOT recommended** for all ADPKD patients.
* **Indications for MRA Screening**:
  * +ve Family history of SAH or berry aneurysms.
  * Previous personal history of aneurysm.
  * High-risk occupations (e.g., airline pilots, bus drivers).
  * Prior to major elective surgery (e.g., renal transplant).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Mayo Imaging Classification
* **Purpose**: Predicts rate of eGFR decline based on age-adjusted **Total Kidney Volume (TKV)** (measured by MRI/CT).
* **Classes**: 1A (slowest progression) to 1E (fastest progression).
* **Clinical Utility**: Identifies "rapid progressors" who qualify for Tolvaptan.

### PROPKD Score (Predicting Renal Outcomes in PKD)
* **Components**: 
  * Male sex (1 pt)
  * Onset of HTN <35 years (2 pts)
  * Urological event (haematuria/cyst infection) <35 years (2 pts)
  * PKD2 mutation (0 pts), PKD1 non-truncating (2 pts), PKD1 truncating (4 pts).
* **Prognosis**: Low risk (0-3), Intermediate risk (4-6), High risk (7-11) for early ESRD.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Renal & Hepatobiliary Complications
* **Renal**: ESRD (requiring haemodialysis/peritoneal dialysis/transplant). ↑ risk of RCC is *not* clearly established, but diagnosis is delayed by cysts.
* **Polycystic Liver Disease (PLD)**: Most common extra-renal manifestation. Massive hepatomegaly. Generally, liver function is preserved. More severe in females (estrogen-dependent growth).

### Cardiovascular & Systemic Complications
* **Neurological**: **Berry Aneurysms** in Circle of Willis → Rupture causes fatal SAH.
* **Valvular**: Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP) (up to 25%), Aortic Regurgitation (AR).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Colonic diverticulosis (high rate of diverticulitis or perforation), severe abdominal wall hernias (incisional/inguinal).

### Drug Adverse Effects (Tolvaptan)
* **Hepatotoxicity**: Idiosyncratic acute liver injury. Requires strict serial LFT monitoring (monthly for first 18 months).
* **Aquaresis effects**: Massive polyuria, nocturia, thirst, hypernatremia (if fluid replacement is inadequate).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common extra-renal manifestation of ADPKD? Answer: Hepatic cysts (Polycystic Liver Disease), which notably do NOT cause liver failure, though they can cause massive structural compression.
* Trap: Prescribing standard Beta-lactams (e.g., Piperacillin-Tazobactam) for an infected renal cyst in an ADPKD patient. Pearl: Beta-lactams have poor cyst wall penetration. Always prescribe lipid-soluble antibiotics like Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin) or TMP-SMX for suspected cyst infections.
* Pitfall: Assuming a patient does not have ADPKD because their ultrasound is normal at age 18. An ultrasound is insufficiently sensitive to exclude ADPKD in young adults. Use age-adjusted Pei-Ravine criteria; absence of cysts <30 years old does not rule out the disease (especially PKD2).
* Viva Question: Which ADPKD patients should be screened for intracranial berry aneurysms with an MRA? Answer: Only those with a positive family history of SAH/aneurysms, prior rupture, high-risk occupations (pilots), or those preparing for major surgery.
* Trap: Recommending water restriction for an ADPKD patient with polyuria. Pearl: High fluid intake (>3L/day) is actually therapeutic in ADPKD, as it suppresses endogenous vasopressin (AVP), which in turn decreases cAMP and slows cyst growth.
