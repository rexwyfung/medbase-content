# Scabies & Pediculosis (疥瘡及蝨病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Scabies & Pediculosis
* **Scabies (疥瘡)**: Highly contagious dermatosis caused by mite *Sarcoptes scabiei var. hominis* infesting stratum corneum.
* **Crusted Scabies (結痂性疥瘡)**: Hyperinfestation variant (Norwegian scabies) with millions of mites; seen in immunosuppressed/neurologically impaired.
* **Pediculosis (蝨病)**: Infestation of hairy parts of body or clothing by blood-sucking lice.

### Etiologies of Ectoparasitic Infections
* **Scabies Mite**: *Sarcoptes scabiei var. hominis*.
* **Head Louse (頭蝨)**: *Pediculus humanus capitis*.
* **Body Louse (體蝨)**: *Pediculus humanus corporis*.
* **Pubic Louse/Crab (陰蝨)**: *Phthirus pubis*.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Scabies Outbreaks**: Highly prevalent in Residential Care Homes for the Elderly (RCHE) (安老院舍) and long-term care facilities in HK.
* **Transmission Mode**: Direct, prolonged skin-to-skin contact (≥10 mins); fomites (bedding/clothing) significant ONLY in crusted scabies.
* **Head Lice**: Endemic in primary schools/kindergartens; female > male children.
* **Body Lice**: Associated with poor hygiene, homelessness, refugee camps.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Sarcoptes Scabiei
* **Life Cycle**: Female mite burrows into stratum corneum → lays 2-3 eggs/day → larvae hatch in 3-4 days → mature in 10-14 days.
* **Mite Lifespan**: ~30 days on human host; <72 hours off host (room temp).
* **Immunological Response**: Symptoms driven by Type IV Delayed Hypersensitivity (第四型遲發性過敏反應) to mite antigens (feces/scybala, saliva, eggs).
* **Incubation Period**: 
  * **Primary Infestation**: 3-6 weeks before symptoms (immune sensitization phase).
  * **Re-infestation**: 1-3 days (rapid memory T-cell response).

### Pathophysiology of Pediculosis
* **Feeding Mechanism**: Lice pierce skin → inject saliva containing vasodilators & anticoagulants → take blood meal.
* **Immunological Response**: Type I & IV hypersensitivity reactions to louse saliva/feces → intense pruritus.
* **Vector Potential**: Body lice (ONLY) vector for:
  * *Rickettsia prowazekii* (Epidemic typhus).
  * *Bartonella quintana* (Trench fever).
  * *Borrelia recurrentis* (Relapsing fever).

## 3. Clinical Assessment 

### History Features of Scabies & Pediculosis
* **Chief Complaint**: Pruritus, classically generalized (sparing head/neck in adults).
* **Nocturnal Exacerbation (夜間加劇)**: Hallmark of scabies itch.
* **Contact History**: Family members, sexual partners, or RCHE co-residents similarly affected.
* **Onset**: Gradual over weeks (if primary), acute (if re-infection).

### Physical Examination Findings in Classic Scabies
* **Burrows (隧道)**: Pathognomonic. Serpiginous, grayish/thread-like lines (2-10mm).
* **Burrow Distribution**: Finger webs, volar wrists, lateral borders of feet, axillae, periumbilical.
* **Papules/Vesicles**: Small erythematous papules at burrow ends.
* **Scabietic Nodules (疥瘡結節)**: Firm, erythematous, intensely pruritic nodules on male genitalia (scrotum/penis) and female areolae/axillae.
* **Excoriations**: Secondary scratch marks, often generalized.

### Physical Examination Findings in Crusted Scabies
* **Hyperkeratotic Plaques (過度角化斑塊)**: Thick, warty, grayish-white crusts.
* **Distribution**: Acral areas (palms, soles, subungual), scalp, ears, trunk.
* **Pruritus**: Often ABSENT or disproportionately mild (due to neuropathy or immune tolerance).
* **Nail Dystrophy**: Thickened, discolored nails with subungual debris.

### Physical Examination Findings in Pediculosis
* **Capitis**: Nits (ova) firmly attached to hair shafts (occipital & post-auricular areas); adult lice rarely seen; excoriations.
* **Corporis**: Excoriations on trunk/shoulders; lice/nits found in clothing seams, NOT on skin.
* **Pubis**: Maculae ceruleae (cerulean spots - slate-gray macules from louse saliva on abdomen/thighs); lice/nits in pubic hair, eyelashes (pediculosis ciliaris).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Generalized Pruritus & Papules
| Condition | Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Scabies (疥瘡)** | Nocturnal itch; web space burrows; genital nodules; positive contacts. |
| **Atopic Dermatitis (濕疹)** | Flexural involvement; personal/family atopy history; lacks burrows/genital nodules. |
| **Contact Dermatitis (接觸性皮炎)** | Confined to area of allergen/irritant exposure; lacks burrows. |
| **Insect Bites (昆蟲咬傷)** | Grouped papules ("breakfast, lunch, dinner" pattern in bedbugs); exposed areas. |
| **Papular Urticaria (丘疹性蕁麻疹)** | Hypersensitivity to bites; central punctum; crops of lesions. |
| **Bullous Pemphigoid (大皰性類天皰瘡)** | Prodromal itchy papules → tense bullae; elderly; negative mite prep. |
| **Psoriasis (牛皮癬)** | Well-demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scale; extensor surfaces; Auspitz sign +ve; mistaken for crusted scabies. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Bedside & Dermatoscopic Investigations
* **Dermatoscopy (皮膚鏡檢查)**: 
  * **Delta Wing Jet Sign (三角翼噴射機徵)**: Brown triangle (anterior part of mite) at the end of a burrow.
* **Wood's Lamp Examination (伍氏燈檢查)**: Identifies nits (fluoresce pale blue/white).
* **Adhesive Tape Test**: Clear tape applied to skin lesions → removed & placed on glass slide.

### Microscopic & Diagnostic Investigations
* **Skin Scraping (皮膚刮片)**: Gold standard for Scabies.
  * **Technique**: Mineral oil applied to burrow → scraped with #15 scalpel blade → microscopy.
  * **Positive Findings**: Direct visualization of mites, eggs, or scybala (feces).
* **Hair/Nit Microscopy**: Differentiates active pediculosis (nit <6mm from scalp) from past infection (nit >6mm from scalp, usually empty casing).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Acute Infection Control & Isolation Protocols
* **Contact Precautions (接觸傳播防護措施)**: Single room isolation in HA hospitals for suspected/confirmed cases.
* **Gown & Gloves**: Mandatory for all patient contact.
* **Duration of Isolation**: Maintain until 24 hours after completion of first effective topical treatment.
* **Crusted Scabies Isolation**: Requires strict isolation; massive shedding necessitates rigorous environmental cleaning.

### Management of Acute Secondary Complications
* **Impetiginization (Secondary Bacterial Infection)**:
  * **Pathogens**: *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus pyogenes*.
  * **Rx**: PO Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) OR PO Cephalexin.
  * **Severe/MRSA**: PO Clindamycin or Doxycycline.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Pharmacological Eradication Algorithms (Scabies)
| Therapy Line | Agent & Dosage | Application Rules in HK Practice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Line (Standard)** | **Topical Permethrin 5% cream (百滅寧)** | Apply from jawline downwards (include head/neck in infants/elderly). Leave for 8-14 hours (overnight), then wash off. **Repeat in 7-14 days**. |
| **Alternative 1st Line** | **Topical Benzyl Benzoate 25% (苯甲酸芐酯)** | Highly irritating (dilute 1:1 for kids). Apply neck down, leave 24h. Wash off. Repeat daily for 3 days. Common in HK HA formulary. |
| **2nd Line / Crusted** | **PO Ivermectin (伊維菌素)** 200 mcg/kg | Give as single dose on Day 1, repeat on Day 8. Must combine with topical Permethrin for Crusted Scabies. Avoid in pregnancy/children <15kg. |
| **Symptomatic Rx** | **Topical Crotamiton 10% / Antihistamines** | PO Chlorpheniramine at night for itch. Topical steroids for scabietic nodules (post-eradication). |

### Pharmacological Eradication Algorithms (Pediculosis)
* **Head/Pubic Lice**:
  * **Topical Permethrin 1% lotion/shampoo**: Apply to washed/towel-dried hair, leave 10 mins, rinse. Repeat in 7-10 days.
  * **Topical Malathion 0.5% lotion**: Apply to dry hair, leave 8-12 hours, wash.
  * **Wet Combing (濕梳法)**: Mechanical removal using fine-toothed nit comb + conditioner every 3-4 days for 2 weeks.
* **Body Lice**:
  * **Rx**: Usually requires NO body pediculicides. Treatment is hygiene + discarding/laundering infested clothing.

### Contact Tracing & Environmental Decontamination
* **Close Contacts**: ALL household members and sexual contacts must be treated simultaneously (even if asymptomatic) to prevent ping-pong transmission.
* **Fomite Decontamination**: 
  * Wash clothing/bedding in hot water (>60°C) and dry on hot cycle.
  * Items unable to be washed: Seal in plastic bag for 72 hours (Scabies) or 2 weeks (Lice).
* **RCHE Outbreaks**: Mass prophylactic treatment of all residents and staff in the affected wing.

### Management of Post-Scabietic Pruritus (疥瘡後瘙癢)
* **Mechanism**: Persistent hypersensitivity reaction to dead mites/antigens.
* **Duration**: Can last up to 4-6 weeks post-successful eradication.
* **Rx step-up**: 
  * 1. Emollients.
  * 2. Moderate-potency topical corticosteroids.
  * 3. Intralesional triamcinolone (for persistent genital nodules).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for Crusted Scabies Development
* **High-Risk Cohorts**:
  * Immunocompromised (HIV/AIDS, long-term systemic corticosteroids, post-transplant).
  * Neurologically impaired (Dementia, Stroke, Down Syndrome, Leprosy) → ↓ scratching reflex.
  * Institutionalized elderly (RCHE residents).
  * HTLV-1 infection (endemic regions).

### Prognosis & Eradication Failure Risks
* **Causes of Rx Failure**:
  * Non-compliance with application instructions (washing off too early, missing web spaces).
  * Failure to treat all close contacts simultaneously.
  * Failure to repeat dose at 7-14 days (topicals do not kill eggs; must kill newly hatched nymphs).
  * Emerging Permethrin resistance (rare but increasing).

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### Multi-System Complications of Ectoparasitosis
* **Dermatological**: Severe excoriations, eczematization, prurigo nodularis.
* **Infectious (Secondary)**:
  * Impetigo (膿疱瘡).
  * Cellulitis (蜂窩組織炎).
  * Sepsis (particularly *S. aureus* in crusted scabies).
* **Renal**: 
  * **Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)**: From secondary infection with nephritogenic strains of *Streptococcus pyogenes*.
* **Psychosocial**: Delusions of parasitosis (Ekbom syndrome) triggered by previous genuine infestation; severe social stigma; sleep deprivation from nocturnal itch.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate active head lice infection from past infection? Answer: Active infection has nits located within 6mm (1/4 inch) of the scalp; nits further down the hair shaft indicate past infection.
* Viva Question: What is the treatment of choice for Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies? Answer: Combination therapy with PO Ivermectin (multiple doses) PLUS Topical Permethrin 5% (applied daily for 7 days, then twice weekly).
* Trap: Treating scabies only in the symptomatic patient. Pearl: All household members and close contacts MUST be treated simultaneously, regardless of symptoms, due to the 3-6 week asymptomatic incubation period.
* Trap: Assuming treatment failure because the patient is still itching 2 weeks after Permethrin application. Pearl: Post-scabietic pruritus can last 4-6 weeks due to persistent hypersensitivity to dead mite antigens; treat with topical steroids and antihistamines, do not indiscriminately re-treat with scabicides unless new burrows appear.
* Pitfall: Applying scabicides only to the trunk and limbs in infants or the elderly. You must apply Permethrin to the entire head, neck, and face (sparing eyes/mouth) in infants, young children, and elderly patients, as scabies frequently involves the scalp in these populations.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing body lice by examining the patient's skin. Look for lice and nits in the seams of the patient's clothing, as body lice only move to the skin to feed.
