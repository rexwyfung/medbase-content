# Blistering Diseases (大皰性皮膚病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Blistering Diseases
* **Vesicles (水皰)**: Fluid-filled lesions <1 cm.
* **Bullae (大皰)**: Fluid-filled lesions >1 cm.
* **Acantholysis (棘層鬆解)**: Loss of intercellular connections (desmosomes) → intraepidermal blister formation.

### Etiologies of Intraepidermal Blistering Diseases
* **Pemphigus Vulgaris (PV) (尋常型天皰瘡)**: Autoimmune IgG targeting desmosomes.
* **Pemphigus Foliaceus (PF) (落葉型天皰瘡)**: Autoimmune IgG targeting superficial epidermis.
* **Paraneoplastic Pemphigus (副腫瘤性天皰瘡)**: Associated with lymphoproliferative malignancies (e.g., NHL, CLL).

### Etiologies of Subepidermal Blistering Diseases
* **Bullous Pemphigoid (BP) (大皰性類天皰瘡)**: Autoimmune IgG targeting hemidesmosomes.
* **Dermatitis Herpetiformis (DH) (疱疹樣皮炎)**: IgA autoantibodies; strongly linked to **Coeliac Disease (乳糜瀉)**.
* **Linear IgA Disease (線狀IgA大皰性皮病)**: Idiopathic or drug-induced (e.g., Vancomycin).
* **Porphyria Cutanea Tarda (PCT) (遲發性皮膚卟啉症)**: Acquired/inherited enzyme deficiency in heme synthesis.
* **Epidermolysis Bullosa (EB) (表皮溶解水皰症)**: Inherited genetic defects in basement membrane zone (BMZ) structural proteins.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **BP**: Most common autoimmune blistering disease in HK; ↑ incidence in elderly (>70 yrs) + neurological disease (stroke, dementia).
* **PV**: Rare but life-threatening; typically presents in middle age (40-60 yrs).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Pemphigus Vulgaris & Foliaceus
* **Target Antigens**: Desmoglein 3 (Dsg3) (deep epidermis/mucosa) & Desmoglein 1 (Dsg1) (superficial epidermis).
* **PV (Mucosal-dominant)**: Anti-Dsg3 antibodies only.
* **PV (Mucocutaneous)**: Anti-Dsg1 + Anti-Dsg3 antibodies.
* **PF**: Anti-Dsg1 antibodies only (no mucosal involvement).
* **Mechanism**: IgG autoantibodies bind desmosomes → steric hindrance & complement activation → **Acantholysis (棘層鬆解)** → intraepidermal cleavage.

### Pathophysiology of Bullous Pemphigoid
* **Target Antigens**: BP180 (Type XVII collagen) & BP230.
* **Mechanism**: IgG binds hemidesmosomes at dermo-epidermal junction (DEJ) → complement classical pathway activation (C3) → neutrophil/eosinophil recruitment → protease release → subepidermal cleavage.

### Pathophysiology of Dermatitis Herpetiformis
* **Target Antigens**: Epidermal transglutaminase (eTG / TG3).
* **Mechanism**: Dietary gluten → IgA formed against tissue transglutaminase (tTG) in gut → cross-reacts with eTG in skin → IgA/eTG immune complexes deposit in dermal papillae → neutrophil chemotaxis → microabscesses → subepidermal blisters.

### Pathophysiology of Porphyria Cutanea Tarda
* **Target Enzyme**: Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase (UROD) ↓ activity.
* **Triggers**: Alcohol, Hepatitis C, HIV, estrogens, iron overload.
* **Mechanism**: Accumulation of photosensitizing porphyrins in skin → UV light exposure generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) → tissue damage & subepidermal blisters.

### Pathophysiology of Epidermolysis Bullosa
* **EB Simplex**: Keratin 5/14 mutations (intraepidermal).
* **Junctional EB**: Laminin-332 mutations (lamina lucida).
* **Dystrophic EB**: Type VII collagen mutations (sublamina densa).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History in Blistering Diseases
* **Onset & Evolution**: Sudden vs gradual, progression pattern.
* **Associated Symptoms**: Pruritus (severe in BP & DH), pain/burning (PV).
* **Systemic Review**: Dysphagia/weight loss (PV oral ulcers), GI symptoms/steatorrhea (DH coeliac link).
* **Triggers/Exposures**: UV light/alcohol (PCT), trauma/friction (EB), new medications (Linear IgA).
* **Neurological Hx**: Dementia, Parkinson's, stroke (highly associated with BP).

### Physical Signs of Pemphigus Vulgaris (PV)
* **Lesions**: Flaccid, easily ruptured vesicles/bullae on normal-appearing skin.
* **Mucosa**: Painful, non-healing erosions (buccal mucosa, palate, lips); often precedes skin lesions by months.
* **Nikolsky Sign (尼科爾斯基徵)**: +ve (lateral pressure on normal skin induces epidermal shearing/blister extension).
* **Asboe-Hansen Sign**: +ve (direct pressure on intact blister causes lateral spread).

### Physical Signs of Bullous Pemphigoid (BP)
* **Lesions**: Tense, robust, dome-shaped bullae on erythematous/urticarial base.
* **Distribution**: Flexures, lower abdomen, inner thighs.
* **Mucosa**: Rare involvement (10-20%).
* **Nikolsky Sign**: -ve.
* **Excoriations**: Extensive scratch marks due to severe pruritus.

### Physical Signs of Dermatitis Herpetiformis (DH)
* **Lesions**: Intensely pruritic, grouped (herpetiform) papulovesicles.
* **Distribution**: Highly symmetrical; extensor surfaces (elbows, knees, buttocks, scalp).
* **Morphology**: Often present only as excoriations/crusting due to intense scratching.

### Physical Signs of Porphyria Cutanea Tarda (PCT)
* **Lesions**: Fragile bullae, erosions, and milia on sun-exposed areas (dorsum of hands, forearms, face).
* **Cutaneous Signs**: Hypertrichosis (malar/temporal), hyperpigmentation, sclerodermoid plaques.
* **Urine Sign**: Dark/tea-colored urine (fluoresces pink under Wood's lamp).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Diagnostic Differentiation of Major Autoimmune Blistering Diseases
| Feature | Pemphigus Vulgaris | Bullous Pemphigoid | Dermatitis Herpetiformis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Age** | 40-60 | >70 | 20-40 |
| **Blister Type** | Flaccid, fragile | Tense, robust | Grouped vesicles |
| **Pruritus** | Minimal/Painful | Severe | Intensely severe |
| **Mucosa** | >90% involvement | <20% involvement | Rare |
| **Nikolsky** | Positive (+ve) | Negative (-ve) | Negative (-ve) |
| **Location** | Scalp, face, trunk | Flexures, abdomen | Extensors (elbows/knees) |

### Differentiating Blistering Diseases by Trigger/Context
| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Context |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Linear IgA** | Childhood onset ("string of pearls" sign) OR adult drug-induced (Vancomycin). |
| **PCT** | Hep C +ve, alcohol excess, sun-exposed blisters, dorsal hands. |
| **Epidermolysis Bullosa** | Neonatal/infant onset, blistering at sites of friction/trauma. |
| **Bullous SLE** | SLE stigmata (malar rash, arthritis), non-sun exposed blisters. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Blisters
* **Tzanck Smear**: Giemsa stain of blister roof/base → multinucleated giant cells (HSV/VZV) OR acantholytic cells (PV).
* **Wood's Lamp**: Exam of urine in PCT → emits coral pink fluorescence.

### Bloods & Serology for Blistering Diseases
* **ELISA**:
  * Anti-Dsg1 / Anti-Dsg3 (Dx & disease monitoring in PV).
  * Anti-BP180 / Anti-BP230 (Dx & disease monitoring in BP).
* **DH Serology**: Anti-tTG IgA, Anti-endomysial (EMA) IgA (rule out IgA deficiency first).
* **PCT Workup**: Plasma porphyrins, LFTs, HCV RNA, Iron studies (ferritin/TIBC).

### Histopathology (H&E Stain of Lesional Skin)
* **PV**: Intraepidermal suprabasal split; "tombstone" appearance of basal layer; acantholysis.
* **BP**: Subepidermal split; eosinophil-rich infiltrate in upper dermis.
* **DH**: Subepidermal split; neutrophil-rich microabscesses in dermal papillae.
* **PCT**: Cell-poor subepidermal split; "festooning" of dermal papillae.

### Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF of Perilesional Normal Skin) - GOLD STANDARD
| Disease | DIF Pattern | Immunoreactants |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **PV** | Intercellular "chicken-wire" pattern in epidermis | IgG and C3 |
| **BP** | Linear band at Dermo-Epidermal Junction (DEJ) | IgG and C3 |
| **DH** | Granular deposits in dermal papillae tips | IgA |
| **Linear IgA** | Linear band at DEJ | IgA |
| **PCT** | Linear deposits in blood vessels & DEJ | IgG and C3 |

### Indirect Immunofluorescence (IIF on Patient Serum)
* **Method**: Incubate patient serum with monkey esophagus (for PV) or salt-split skin (for BP).
* **BP (Salt-split skin)**: IgG binds to the **epidermal roof** of the split (targets BP180/230).
* **Epidermolysis Bullosa Acquisita (EBA)**: IgG binds to the **dermal floor** of the split (targets Type VII collagen).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Severe Pemphigus Vulgaris
* **Triage**: Admit to ICU/Burns unit if body surface area (BSA) involvement >30%.
* **Resuscitation**: IV fluids (crystalloids) to correct massive insensible fluid loss.
* **Thermoregulation**: Barrier nursing, temperature-controlled environment.
* **High-Dose Immunosuppression (First-line)**:
  * **IV Methylprednisolone** pulse therapy (e.g., 1g/day for 3-5 days).
  * **IVIG (靜脈注射免疫球蛋白)** or **Plasmapheresis (血漿置換)** for rapid removal of circulating autoantibodies in life-threatening flares.

### Wound Care & Infection Control
* **Skin Care**: Non-adherent dressings (e.g., Jelonet, Mepitel); avoid adhesive tapes.
* **Antimicrobial**: Swab clinically infected lesions; empirical IV antibiotics (e.g., Cloxacillin/Ceftriaxone) if sepsis suspected (Staph aureus & Pseudomonas common).
* **Mucosal Care**: Magic mouthwash (viscous lignocaine, topical steroid, antifungal), chlorhexidine mouthrinse.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Pemphigus Vulgaris
* **Phase 1 (Consolidation)**:
  * **PO Prednisolone** (1-1.5 mg/kg/day) until cessation of new blisters and healing of old lesions.
* **Phase 2 (Steroid Sparing / Remission Maintenance)**:
  * **First-line Biologic**: **IV Rituximab** (Anti-CD20 mAb; lymphoma protocol 375mg/m2 x4 OR RA protocol 1g x2).
  * **Alternative Immunosuppressants**: **PO Azathioprine** (check TPMT first) or **PO Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)**.
* **Phase 3 (Tapering)**: Gradual reduction of PO Prednisolone over 6-12 months.

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Bullous Pemphigoid
* **Mild/Localized Disease**:
  * **Superpotent Topical Steroids**: Clobetasol propionate 0.05% applied to whole body.
* **Moderate/Severe Disease**:
  * **PO Prednisolone** (0.5 mg/kg/day) (lower dose than PV).
  * **Steroid-sparing agents**: **PO Azathioprine**, **MMF**, or **Methotrexate**.
* **Frail/Elderly/Comorbid Patients (Minimal Immunosuppression)**:
  * **PO Doxycycline** + **PO Nicotinamide (Niacinamide)** (anti-inflammatory action).

### Chronic Management of Dermatitis Herpetiformis
* **Dietary Modification**: **Strict Gluten-Free Diet (GFD)** (Lifelong; takes months to improve skin lesions but resolves gut pathology).
* **Pharmacology**: **PO Dapsone** (rapid relief of pruritus in 48-72 hours). Must check G6PD status before initiation!

### Chronic Management of Porphyria Cutanea Tarda
* **Avoid Triggers**: Strict sun protection (barrier clothing, zinc oxide sunscreens), complete alcohol abstinence, stop estrogens.
* **HCV Management**: Direct-acting antivirals if HCV +ve.
* **Specific Therapy**:
  * **Venesection (放血療法)**: 500ml every 2 weeks until serum ferritin is at lower limit of normal.
  * **Low-dose PO Chloroquine / Hydroxychloroquine**: Mobilizes porphyrins for renal excretion (do NOT use standard rheum doses → acute hepatotoxicity).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Pemphigus Disease Area Index (PDAI)
* **Purpose**: Objectively quantify PV/PF severity to guide therapy.
* **Parameters**: Skin (0-120), Scalp (0-10), Mucosa (0-120).
* **Thresholds**: Moderate (15-44), Severe (≥45).

### BPDAI (Bullous Pemphigoid Disease Area Index)
* **Parameters**: Evaluates extent of erosions/blisters and urticarial/erythematous plaques independently, plus subjective pruritus score.

### Prognosis & Mortality
* **PV**: Untreated mortality ~99% (fluid loss, sepsis). With steroids/Rituximab, mortality ~5-10% (mostly due to iatrogenic immunosuppression).
* **BP**: 1-year mortality 10-40% (high due to advanced age and frail comorbidities of the typical demographic, rather than the disease itself).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Specific Complications
* **PV Complications**: Secondary bacterial infection (Staph aureus sepsis), extreme cachexia/malnutrition (due to severe painful dysphagia), strictures of esophagus.
* **DH Complications**: Enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL) if GFD is not strictly followed.
* **PCT Complications**: Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), hepatic cirrhosis.

### Pharmacological Adverse Effects
* **Prolonged Corticosteroids**: Osteoporosis, iatrogenic Cushing's, DM, cataracts, opportunistic infections (PCP, TB reactivation).
* **Dapsone**: **Hemolytic anemia (溶血性貧血)** (dose-dependent in normal pts, catastrophic in G6PD deficiency), methemoglobinemia, agranulocytosis.
* **Rituximab**: Infusion reactions, hypogammaglobulinemia, Hepatitis B reactivation (must screen HBsAg/core Ab prior).
* **Azathioprine**: Myelosuppression (↑ risk if TPMT deficient), hepatotoxicity.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you perform a skin biopsy for suspected autoimmune blistering disease? Answer: You need two biopsies. One for H&E taken from the EDGE of an intact blister, and one for Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF) taken from PERILESIONAL NORMAL skin (3-5mm away).
* Trap: Biopsying the blister base for DIF. Pearl: Proteases within the blister destroy immune complexes, leading to a false-negative DIF. Always biopsy normal-appearing perilesional skin.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing Bullous Pemphigoid solely on the presence of blisters. In up to 20% of BP patients, the disease presents with non-bullous, intensely pruritic urticarial plaques for months before blisters appear.
* Viva Question: How does indirect immunofluorescence (IIF) using salt-split skin differentiate BP from Epidermolysis Bullosa Acquisita (EBA)? Answer: The skin is split at the lamina lucida. BP antigens (BP180/230) stay on the roof (epidermal side). EBA antigens (Collagen VII) stay on the floor (dermal side).
* Trap: Treating Dermatitis Herpetiformis with Dapsone alone. Pearl: Dapsone rapidly clears the skin but does absolutely nothing for the underlying gluten-sensitive enteropathy or lymphoma risk; lifelong strict Gluten-Free Diet is mandatory.
* Pitfall: Starting a patient on Azathioprine without checking TMPT levels. TPMT (Thiopurine methyltransferase) deficiency leads to fatal severe bone marrow suppression.
* Viva Question: What is the classic Nikolsky sign mechanism? Answer: Application of mechanical pressure to the skin of a Pemphigus patient breaks the already-weakened desmosomes, causing the epidermis to shear off. It is positive in intraepidermal blisters (PV, SJS/TEN) and negative in subepidermal blisters (BP).
