# Trauma & Ocular Emergencies (眼外傷及眼科急症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Ocular Emergencies (眼科急症)
* **Chemical Injury (化學性灼傷)**: Direct ocular exposure to acidic/alkaline substances requiring immediate intervention.
* **Open Globe Injury (眼球穿透傷)**: Full-thickness defect of the eye wall (cornea/sclera) via penetrating, perforating trauma, or rupture.
* **Retrobulbar Haemorrhage (球後出血)**: Rapid accumulation of blood in the retrobulbar space → orbital compartment syndrome.

### Etiologies of Ocular Trauma
* **Chemical**: Industrial accidents, assault (drain cleaners, bleach, battery acid).
* **Blunt Trauma**: Fists, sports (squash/tennis balls), MVCs → Hyphaema (前房積血), Blowout fracture (爆裂性骨折).
* **Sharp/Penetrating Trauma**: Projectiles, glass, metallic shards (hammering/chiseling) → Intraocular foreign body (IOFB) (球內異物).

### Epidemiology of Ocular Trauma in Hong Kong
* **Bimodal Age Distribution**: Peaks in young adult males (occupational/assault) & elderly (falls).
* **Occupational Hazard**: High incidence in construction workers in HK; often related to non-compliance with protective eyewear.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Chemical Eye Injuries
* **Alkaline Agents (鹼性物質)** (e.g., ammonia, lye):
* **Saponification (皂化反應)** of cell membrane fatty acids → rapid, deep tissue penetration.
* **Liquefactive necrosis (液化性壞死)** → intraocular damage within minutes.
* **Acidic Agents (酸性物質)** (e.g., battery acid):
* **Coagulation necrosis (凝固性壞死)** → forms physical barrier of denatured proteins → limits deeper penetration (generally less severe than alkali).

### Pathophysiology of Retrobulbar Haemorrhage
* **Mechanism**: Blunt trauma → rupture of infraorbital artery/branches.
* **Orbital Compartment Syndrome**: Bony orbit is a closed space → ↑ retrobulbar pressure → compresses optic nerve (視神經) & Central Retinal Artery (CRA) (視網膜中央動脈) → ischaemia → irreversible blindness within 90-120 mins.

### Pathophysiology of Orbital Blowout Fracture
* **Mechanism**: Sudden ↑ intraorbital hydraulic pressure from blunt object (larger than orbital rim, >5cm).
* **Weakest Points**: Orbital floor (maxillary bone) > Medial wall (lamina papyracea).
* **Consequence**: Herniation of orbital fat & inferior rectus muscle (下直肌) into maxillary sinus.

### Pathophysiology of Hyphaema (前房積血)
* **Mechanism**: Blunt trauma → anterior-posterior compression of globe → equatorial expansion.
* **Result**: Tearing of blood vessels at iris root/ciliary body (睫狀體) → bleeding into Anterior Chamber (AC) (前房).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for Ocular Trauma
* **Mechanism**: Velocity of object, chemical agent type (alkali/acid).
* **Context**: Metal-on-metal striking (high index of suspicion for IOFB), use of protective eyewear.
* **Timing**: Exact time of injury.
* **Tetanus Status**: Date of last booster.

### Physical Signs of Chemical Injury
* **Periocular**: Facial burns, lid edema.
* **Conjunctiva (結膜)**: Hyperaemia, chemosis (edema), blanching (limbal ischaemia - poor prognostic sign).
* **Cornea (角膜)**: Epithelial defects, stromal haze/opacification.

### Physical Signs of Penetrating Globe Injury
* **Visual Acuity (VA)**: Significantly ↓.
* **Pupil**: Teardrop-shaped pupil (peaked towards the laceration).
* **Cornea/Sclera**: Visible laceration, prolapsed uveal tissue (dark brown tissue).
* **Anterior Chamber**: Shallow AC, hyphaema.
* **Seidel Sign (賽德爾氏徵)**: +ve (clear stream of aqueous fluid disrupting fluorescein dye).

### Physical Signs of Orbital Blowout Fracture
* **Ocular Motility**: Restricted upgaze/downgaze (inferior rectus entrapment).
* **Globe Position**: Enophthalmos (眼球內陷) (may be masked acutely by edema).
* **Neurological**: Infraorbital paraesthesia (V2 nerve damage).
* **Subcutaneous Emphysema**: Crepitus on palpation.

### Physical Signs of Retrobulbar Haemorrhage
* **Globe Position**: Proptosis (眼球突出) (firm, tense, unyielding globe).
* **Neurological**: +ve Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD) (相對性瞳孔傳入障礙).
* **Motility**: Ophthalmoplegia (frozen eye).
* **Fundoscopy**: Cherry-red spot (CRA occlusion), optic disc swelling.

### Physical Signs of Hyphaema
* **Anterior Chamber**: Visible blood level in dependent portion, or microhyphaema (RBCs circulating on slit-lamp).
* **Intraocular Pressure (IOP)**: Often ↑.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Orbital Trauma Presentations
| Feature | Blowout Fracture | Retrobulbar Haemorrhage | Cranial Nerve 3 Palsy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Globe Position** | Enophthalmos | Severe Proptosis | Normal |
| **Pupil** | Normal | RAPD +ve | Dilated, fixed |
| **Motility** | Restricted vertical | Restricted all directions | Down & out |
| **Vision** | Normal | Rapidly ↓ | Normal |
| **Periorbital** | V2 Anaesthesia | Very tight eyelids | Ptosis |

### Differentiating Globe Injuries
| Feature | Open Globe (Penetrating) | Closed Globe (Blunt) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AC Depth** | Shallow / Flat | Deep / Normal |
| **Pupil Shape** | Teardrop / Irregular | Round (may have sphincter tears) |
| **Seidel Test** | +ve (Aqueous leak) | -ve |
| **Uveal Prolapse** | Present | Absent |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Ocular Trauma
* **pH Testing**: Universal indicator paper in inferior fornix (Normal = 7.0-7.4).
* **Fluorescein Staining**: To detect epithelial defects & perform Seidel test.
* **Tonometry (IOP Measurement)**:
* **Contraindicated**: Suspected open globe.
* **Indicated**: Hyphaema, chemical burns, retrobulbar haemorrhage.
* **Forced Duction Test**: Under local anaesthesia (differentiates mechanical restriction in blowout fracture vs nerve palsy).

### Imaging Modalities for Ocular Trauma
* **Non-Contrast CT Orbit (Thin slices 1-2mm)**:
* **Gold Standard** for: Orbital fractures, retrobulbar haemorrhage, IOFB detection.
* **B-Scan Ultrasonography**:
* **Indication**: Dense cataracts or vitreous haemorrhage obscuring retina.
* **Contraindication**: Suspected open globe (pressure applied to eye).
* **MRI Orbit**:
* **Absolute Contraindication**: If ANY suspicion of metallic IOFB (magnetic torque → massive intraocular destruction).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Immediate First Aid for Chemical Ocular Injury
* **Do NOT check vision first**.
* **Copious Irrigation**: STAT with Normal Saline / Ringer's Lactate / Tap water.
* **Volume**: Minimum 1-2 Liters per eye.
* **Duration**: Continue until fornix pH remains 7.0-7.4 for 30 mins post-irrigation.
* **Mechanical Sweep**: Evert eyelids + use cotton bud to sweep fornices for particulate matter (e.g., lime/cement).

### Acute Management of Penetrating Globe Injury
* **Eye Shield**: Apply rigid Fox eye shield immediately (protect from external pressure).
* **NPO (Nil Per Os)**: Fasting for emergency surgery under GA.
* **Medications**: IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., Cefazolin/Ceftazidime + Vancomycin in HA), IV anti-emetics (prevent ↑ intraocular pressure from vomiting), tetanus toxoid.
* **Absolute Contraindications**:
* **NO** topical eye drops (preservatives are toxic to inner eye).
* **NO** pad & bandage (applies pressure).
* **NO** manipulation of protruding foreign bodies.

### Acute Management of Retrobulbar Haemorrhage
* **Emergency Decompression**: Lateral Canthotomy & Cantholysis (外眥切開術).
* **Indication**: ↓ VA, +ve RAPD, ↑↑ IOP (>40 mmHg).
* **Goal**: Disinsert lateral canthal tendon to allow globe to move forward, relieving compartment pressure.
* **Adjuncts**: IV Mannitol, IV Acetazolamide, topical anti-glaucoma agents.

### Acute Management of Traumatic Hyphaema
* **Position**: Strict bed rest, head elevated at 30-45° (allows blood to settle inferiorly, clears visual axis).
* **Eye Shield**: Hard shield over the eye.
* **Medications**: Topical Cycloplegics (e.g., Atropine) to paralyze ciliary body + relieve pain; Topical Steroids (reduce inflammation).
* **Avoid**: NSAIDs/Aspirin (increases rebleeding risk).

### Acute Management of Lid Lacerations (眼瞼裂傷)
* **Check for Canalicular Involvement**: If laceration involves medial 1/3 of lid → suspect tear duct system injury.
* **Management**: Early microsurgical repair with silicone stent intubation within 48 hours to prevent epiphora (流眼水).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Management of Chemical Burns
* **Topical Steroids**: Reduce inflammation (use cautiously after week 1 due to corneal melting risk).
* **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C)**: Topical/PO (promotes collagen synthesis).
* **Citrate Drops**: Inhibit neutrophils/collagenases.
* **Lubrication**: Intensive preservative-free artificial tears.
* **Surgical**: Amniotic membrane transplant (羊膜移植), limbal stem cell transplant (for late limbal stem cell deficiency).

### Long-Term Management of Blowout Fractures
* **Conservative (Initial 1-2 weeks)**: Instruct patient NOT to blow nose (prevents surgical emphysema/infection), PO broad-spectrum Abx (e.g., Augmentin).
* **Surgical Repair Indications (within 14 days)**:
* **Persistent diplopia (復視)** within primary 30° of gaze.
* **Enophthalmos** > 2mm.
* **Large fracture** (>50% of orbital floor).
* **White-eyed blowout fracture**: Trapdoor fracture in children (ischemia of entrapped muscle → severe vagal symptoms/bradycardia) → Requires **Urgent (<48h)** repair.

### Long-Term Management of Hyphaema
* **Monitoring**: High risk of secondary haemorrhage (clot retraction) on Day 3-5.
* **IOP Control**: PO Acetazolamide or topical beta-blockers if IOP spikes.
* **Special Consideration**: Sickle Cell trait/disease patients are at extreme risk of optic nerve damage from even mild ↑ IOP (avoid Acetazolamide in Sickle Cell → induces sickling in AC).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Roper-Hall Classification for Ocular Chemical Burns
| Grade | Prognosis | Corneal Appearance | Limbal Ischaemia (Blanching) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Excellent | Clear cornea (only epithelial damage) | None |
| **II** | Good | Hazy cornea, iris details visible | < 1/3 (120°) of limbus |
| **III** | Guarded | Total loss of epithelium, iris details obscured | 1/3 to 1/2 (120-180°) of limbus |
| **IV** | Poor | Opaque cornea (white out) | > 1/2 (>180°) of limbus |

### Prognostic Factors for Penetrating Globe Injuries
* **Ocular Trauma Score (OTS)**: Calculates visual prognosis based on:
* Initial Visual Acuity (Strongest predictor)
* Presence of Rupture
* Endophthalmitis
* Perforating injury
* Retinal detachment
* Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD)

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Traumatic Hyphaema
* **Secondary Haemorrhage**: Typically Day 3-5, often worse than initial bleed.
* **Corneal Blood Staining**: RBC breakdown products penetrate corneal stroma → prolonged opacity (requires corneal transplant).
* **Ghost Cell Glaucoma**: Degenerated RBCs block trabecular meshwork.
* **Peripheral Anterior Synechiae (PAS)**: Iris adheres to cornea → angle-closure glaucoma.

### Complications of Chemical Ocular Burns
* **Limbal Stem Cell Deficiency**: Inability to regenerate corneal epithelium → conjunctivalization of cornea, neovascularization.
* **Symblepharon (瞼球粘連)**: Adhesion between palpebral and bulbar conjunctiva.
* **Secondary Glaucoma**: Due to trabecular meshwork scarring.
* **Corneal Melting/Perforation**.

### Complications of Penetrating Eye Injuries
* **Endophthalmitis (眼內炎)**: Devastating intraocular infection; ↑ risk with rural/agricultural injuries (Bacillus cereus).
* **Sympathetic Ophthalmia (交感性眼炎)**: Rare, bilateral granulomatous panuveitis.
* **Mechanism**: Penetrating trauma to one eye (exciting eye) exposes previously sequestered ocular antigens → systemic autoimmune T-cell response → attacks the uninjured contralateral eye (sympathizing eye) weeks to years later.
* **Traumatic Cataract**: Direct capsular breach.
* **Retinal Detachment (視網膜脫落)**.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the single most important initial step in managing a chemical ocular burn? Answer: Immediate, copious irrigation with whatever neutral fluid is available, even before checking visual acuity.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with a "black eye" and normal visual acuity is safe to discharge immediately. Pearl: Always test ocular motility and assess for diplopia; missed blowout fractures can lead to permanent restrictive strabismus.
* Pitfall: Performing tonometry (IOP check) on a patient with a suspected penetrating eye injury. Applying pressure to an open globe can cause catastrophic extrusion of intraocular contents.
* Viva Question: A child presents with blunt eye trauma, normal-appearing external eye ("white eye"), but severe nausea, vomiting, and bradycardia. What is the diagnosis? Answer: "White-eyed" blowout fracture with vagal reflex from tight extraocular muscle entrapment, requiring urgent surgical release within 48 hours.
* Trap: Using topical NSAIDs to control pain in a patient with hyphaema. Pearl: NSAIDs inhibit platelet aggregation and significantly increase the risk of a secondary re-bleed (typically day 3-5), which is often worse than the primary hyphaema.
* Pitfall: Sending a patient with an intraocular foreign body (metal hammering injury) for an MRI orbit. A magnetic field can cause the metallic fragment to violently rotate or migrate, destroying the eye. Always use non-contrast CT for metallic IOFB.
* Viva Question: What specific complication must you monitor for in an African or Middle-Eastern patient presenting with traumatic hyphaema? Answer: Sickle cell trait/disease exacerbation; even mild IOP elevation can cause central retinal artery occlusion, and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (Acetazolamide) are contraindicated as they promote sickling.
* Trap: Reassuring a patient with severe alkali burn because their conjunctiva looks "white and uninflamed". Pearl: A white eye in severe chemical burn indicates limbal ischaemia (Grade IV Roper-Hall), meaning the blood supply and stem cells are destroyed; this is a very poor prognostic sign.
