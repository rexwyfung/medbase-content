# Interstitial & Occupational Lung Disease (間質性及職業性肺病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Interstitial & Occupational Lung Disease
* **Interstitial Lung Disease (間質性肺病)**: Heterogeneous group of >200 diffuse parenchymal lung diseases (DPLD) characterized by cellular infiltrates, interstitial inflammation, and/or fibrosis altering alveolar architecture.
* **Occupational Lung Disease (職業性肺病)**: Pulmonary pathology directly caused by inhalation of harmful dusts, chemicals, or proteins in the workplace setting.

### Etiological Classification of ILD
| Category | Key Etiologies |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Known Causes (Exposure)** | **Occupational**: Silica (Silicosis), Asbestos (Asbestosis), Coal worker's pneumoconiosis. <br> **Environmental**: Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis (HP) (avian, mold). <br> **Drugs**: Amiodarone, Bleomycin, Methotrexate, Nitrofurantoin. <br> **Radiation**: Post-radiotherapy (e.g., breast/lung Ca). |
| **Systemic Diseases** | **CTD-ILD**: Systemic Sclerosis (SSc), Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA), SLE, Dermatomyositis/Polymyositis (Anti-synthetase syndrome), Sjögren's. <br> **Granulomatous**: **Sarcoidosis (結節病)**. |
| **Idiopathic Interstitial Pneumonias (IIPs)** | **Major**: **Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (特發性肺纖維化, IPF)**, Non-Specific Interstitial Pneumonia (NSIP), Organizing Pneumonia (OP). <br> **Smoking-related**: Desquamative Interstitial Pneumonia (DIP), Respiratory Bronchiolitis-ILD (RB-ILD). |

### Hong Kong Epidemiology & Occupational Context
* **Silicosis (矽肺病)**: Highly prevalent in HK historically due to intensive construction, rock drilling, and tunneling (e.g., MTR expansions). Remains a major compensable disease under HK HA.
* **Tuberculosis (結核病)**: High endemicity in HK; post-TB fibrotic scarring frequently confounds ILD diagnosis and structural lung assessments.
* **IPF**: Most common IIP in HK; incidence ↑ with age (>60 yrs), male predominance.
* **Avian HP**: Often linked to keeping birds in small, poorly ventilated HK public housing.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Fibrotic Pathway (Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis)
* **Initial Trigger**: Repetitive micro-injuries to alveolar epithelial cells (AEC-II) in genetically susceptible individuals (e.g., *MUC5B* promoter polymorphism, telomerase mutations).
* **Aberrant Repair Mechanism**: Defective re-epithelialization → excessive release of profibrotic mediators (TGF-β, PDGF, FGF).
* **Fibroblast Activation**: Migration and proliferation of fibroblasts → differentiation into myofibroblasts.
* **Extracellular Matrix (ECM) Deposition**: Massive deposition of collagen and fibronectin in interstitium → disruption of gas exchange barrier → architectural distortion (honeycombing) → ↓ compliance, ↑ elastic recoil.

### Granulomatous & Inflammatory Pathways (HP & Sarcoidosis)
* **Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis (Type III/IV Hypersensitivity)**: Inhaled organic antigen → IgG-immune complex deposition (Type III) & T-cell mediated (Type IV) response → neutrophilic/lymphocytic alveolitis → poorly formed, non-caseating granulomas → chronic exposure leads to fibrosis.
* **Sarcoidosis**: Unknown antigen → macrophage antigen presentation to CD4+ Th1 cells → profound localized IL-2, IFN-γ release → robust, well-formed, non-caseating granulomas along lymphatics.
* **Asbestosis**: Asbestos fibers phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages → incomplete phagocytosis (frustrated phagocytosis) → lysosomal enzyme release → generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) → persistent inflammation & lower lobe fibrosis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Key History & Symptomatology in ILD
* **Insidious Dyspnea**: Progressive exertional shortness of breath (months to years).
* **Cough**: Persistent, severe, dry (non-productive) cough.
* **Systemic Symptoms**: Fatigue, weight loss, fever/night sweats (suggests Sarcoidosis, CTD, or subacute HP).
* **Chronology**: Sudden worsening suggests acute exacerbation, pulmonary embolism, or secondary pneumothorax.

### Detailed Occupational & Environmental History
* **Lifelong Occupational History**: Crucial to trace back 20-30 years (latency for Asbestosis/Silicosis). Ask specifically about construction, demolition, shipyards, stone cutting.
* **Environmental/Hobby History**: Pet birds (pigeons, parrots), mold in air conditioners, hot tubs, farming, ceramics/pottery (silica exposure).
* **Smoking History**: Essential for RB-ILD, DIP, pulmonary Langerhans cell histiocytosis (PLCH), and IPF risk.

### Respiratory Physical Signs of ILD
* **Inspection**: Tachypnea at rest, central cyanosis, **Clubbing (杵狀指)** (especially in IPF, Asbestosis; rare in Sarcoidosis/HP).
* **Palpation**: Symmetrical ↓ chest expansion.
* **Percussion**: Usually resonant (unless complicated by effusion in CTD or asbestos).
* **Auscultation**: **Fine, end-inspiratory "Velcro" crackles (爆裂音)** predominantly at lung bases.

### Extra-Pulmonary & Connective Tissue Disease (CTD) Signs
* **Skin/Facies**: Sclerodactyly, microstomia, telangiectasia (SSc); Gottron's papules, heliotrope rash, "mechanic's hands" (Dermatomyositis/Anti-synthetase syndrome); erythema nodosum (Sarcoidosis).
* **Musculoskeletal**: Symmetrical polyarthritis, rheumatoid nodules (RA, SLE).
* **Cor Pulmonale Signs**: Elevated JVP with prominent a/v waves, parasternal heave, loud P2, peripheral edema, tender hepatomegaly.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating ILD from Respiratory Mimics
| Condition | Discriminatory History & Signs |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Congestive Heart Failure** | Orthopnea, PND, bibasal crackles (wet, shifting), S3 gallop, peripheral edema preceding dyspnea. |
| **COPD / Asthma** | Obstructive history, wheezing, hyperinflated chest, ↓ breath sounds, prolonged expiration. |
| **Chronic TB Sequelae** | Hx of treated TB, localized upper lobe signs, often asymmetrical. |
| **Bronchiectasis** | Copious purulent sputum production, coarse pan-inspiratory crackles, frequent exacerbations. |

### Discriminatory Features of Specific ILD Subtypes
| ILD Subtype | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **IPF** | Age >60, males, basal/subpleural Velcro crackles, extreme clubbing, relentless progression. |
| **Sarcoidosis** | Often young adults, asymptomatic or systemic signs (erythema nodosum, uveitis, lymphadenopathy). |
| **Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis** | Clear temporal relationship to antigen exposure (e.g., bird cleaning), fevers/chills hours after exposure. |
| **CTD-ILD** | Female predominance, systemic multiorgan involvement (joints, skin, Raynaud's), positive serology. |
| **Silicosis** | Distinct occupational hx, frequently asymptomatic early on, extreme risk of active TB co-infection. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Lung Function Tests for ILD
* **Pulse Oximetry**: Resting hypoxemia, marked desaturation during 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT).
* **ECG**: Right axis deviation, right ventricular hypertrophy, P pulmonale, right bundle branch block (cor pulmonale signs).
* **Spirometry**: Restrictive pattern (↓ FVC, ↓ FEV1, normal/↑ FEV1/FVC ratio >0.70).
* **Lung Volumes (Plethysmography)**: ↓ Total Lung Capacity (TLC), ↓ Residual Volume (RV).
* **Diffusion Capacity**: **↓ DLCO** (most sensitive early indicator of alveolar-capillary membrane disruption).

### Autoimmune & Serological Blood Investigations
* **Complete Autoimmune Panel (Mandatory in all ILD)**: ANA, anti-dsDNA, ENA (Anti-Ro, Anti-La, Anti-Scl-70, Anti-Jo-1, Anti-Sm), Rheumatoid Factor (RF), Anti-CCP, ANCA.
* **Myositis Panel**: For suspected anti-synthetase syndrome.
* **Avian/Fungal Precipitins (Specific IgG)**: To screen for suspected HP (e.g., pigeon breeder's lung).
* **Serum ACE**: May be ↑ in Sarcoidosis (low specificity/sensitivity, poor diagnostic utility alone).

### High-Resolution CT (HRCT) Imaging Patterns (Gold Standard)
| Pattern | Key HRCT Findings | Associated Pathologies |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Usual Interstitial Pneumonia (UIP)** | Basal and subpleural predominance, **Honeycombing**, Traction bronchiectasis, absence of extensive ground glass. | **IPF** (hallmark), Rheumatoid Arthritis-ILD, Asbestosis. |
| **Non-Specific Interstitial Pneumonia (NSIP)** | Basal predominant, bilateral **Ground-glass opacities (GGOs)**, reticulation, subpleural sparing. | **CTD-ILD** (e.g., SSc), drug-induced. |
| **Upper Lobe Fibrosis / Nodular** | Upper/mid-zone nodules, conglomeration, progressive massive fibrosis (PMF). | **Silicosis**, Coal worker's, Ankylosing Spondylitis, TB. |
| **Perilymphatic Nodularity** | Nodules along bronchovascular bundles, subpleural fissures, bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy (BHL). | **Sarcoidosis**, Silicosis. |

### Tissue Biopsy & Bronchoscopy
* **Bronchoscopy with Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL)**:
  * Eosinophilia >25%: Eosinophilic pneumonia.
  * Lymphocytosis >15%: HP (often CD8+ predominant), Sarcoidosis (CD4/CD8 ratio >3.5), NSIP.
  * Neutrophilia: IPF, Asbestosis.
* **Endobronchial Ultrasound (EBUS-TBNA)**: Highly sensitive for Sarcoidosis (sampling hilar nodes).
* **Transbronchial Lung Cryobiopsy (TBLC) / VATS**: Indicated if HRCT is indeterminate for UIP. Provides histopathological confirmation (fibroblastic foci, patchy fibrosis).

### Diagnostic Criteria for IPF
* Exclusion of other known causes of ILD (domestic, occupational, CTD, drug exposure).
* Presence of a **UIP pattern on HRCT** (diagnostic on its own if clinical context fits).
* Specific combinations of HRCT and surgical lung biopsy patterns in patients subjected to tissue sampling (Multidisciplinary Team [MDT] consensus required).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Exacerbation of IPF (AE-IPF) Diagnostic Criteria
* Unexplained worsening or development of dyspnea over <1 month.
* New bilateral ground-glass opacities or consolidation on HRCT superimposed on a background UIP pattern.
* Deterioration not fully explained by cardiac failure, fluid overload, or massive pulmonary embolism.

### Resuscitation & Pharmacological Protocol for AE-IPF
* **Oxygen Therapy**: Titrate High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) to maintain SpO2 >90%.
* **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV)**: Trial of BiPAP for severe hypoxemic respiratory failure (avoid invasive mechanical ventilation unless as a bridge to transplant, due to extreme mortality).
* **High-Dose Corticosteroids**: IV Methylprednisolone (0.5-1g daily for 3 days), then switch to oral Prednisolone taper (controversial efficacy but widely used).
* **Empirical Antimicrobials**: Broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., Piperacillin/Tazobactam + Azithromycin) until atypical and typical bacterial pneumonia are excluded.
* **Thromboprophylaxis**: LMWH (unless contraindicated) as immobility and inflammation ↑ VTE risk.
* **Palliative Care Integration**: Early initiation of IV Morphine for dyspnea relief and Lorazepam for extreme anxiety if condition is terminal.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Pharmacological Algorithm for IPF
* **First-line (Antifibrotic Therapy)**: Slows FVC decline. Indicated if FVC is 50-80% predicted (local HA funding criteria apply).
  * **Pirfenidone**: Downregulates TGF-β production.
  * **Nintedanib**: Tyrosine kinase inhibitor (blocks PDGF, FGF, VEGF receptors).
* **Contraindicated in IPF**: Steroids + Azathioprine + N-acetylcysteine (PANTHER-IPF trial showed ↑ mortality/hospitalizations).
* **Management of GERD**: Routine initiation of PPI (Omeprazole) if symptomatic, as microaspiration drives fibrotic progression.

### Chronic Immunosuppressive Algorithm for CTD-ILD & Sarcoidosis
* **Induction/Acute Flare**: High-dose oral Prednisolone (0.5-1 mg/kg/day).
* **Maintenance/Steroid-Sparing**:
  * **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)** or **Azathioprine**: 1st line for SSc-ILD, RA-ILD, and NSIP.
  * **Cyclophosphamide (IV pulsed)**: Severe, rapidly progressive CTD-ILD.
  * **Rituximab / Tocilizumab**: Refractory ILD with severe autoimmune activity.

### Occupational Avoidance & Local Support Schemes
* **Immediate Removal**: Zero further exposure to offending agent (e.g., rehousing birds, job transfer away from silica dust).
* **Pneumoconiosis Compensation Fund Board (PCFB)**: Legal requirement in HK to notify Labour Department. Patients with confirmed Silicosis/Asbestosis receive monthly compensation, rehabilitation devices, and bereavement grants.

### Non-Pharmacological & Advanced Therapies
* **Pulmonary Rehabilitation**: Supervised exercise training to optimize skeletal muscle O2 utilization.
* **Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT)**: Indicated if resting PaO2 ≤ 55 mmHg (7.3 kPa) or PaO2 56-59 mmHg with cor pulmonale/polycythemia.
* **Lung Transplantation**: Definitive therapy. Refer early (BODE index/GAP score criteria) due to long waiting times at Grantham/Queen Mary Hospital (HK transplant centers).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### GAP Index for IPF Mortality Prediction
| Variable (GAP) | Points |
| :--- | :--- |
| **G**ender: Male | 1 |
| **A**ge: 61–65 yrs <br> > 65 yrs | 1 <br> 2 |
| **P**hysiology - FVC (% predicted): <br> 50–75% <br> <50% | <br> 1 <br> 2 |
| **P**hysiology - DLCO (% predicted): <br> 36–55% <br> ≤35% or cannot perform | <br> 1 <br> 2 <br> 3 |

* **Stage I (0-3 points)**: 1-year mortality 6%, median survival 8 years.
* **Stage II (4-5 points)**: 1-year mortality 16%, median survival 4 years.
* **Stage III (6-8 points)**: 1-year mortality 39%, median survival <2 years.

### Prognostic Trajectories of ILD Subtypes
* **IPF**: Relentless, irreversible decline. Median survival 3-5 years post-diagnosis without antifibrotics.
* **NSIP**: Better prognosis; cellular NSIP responds well to steroids, fibrotic NSIP has slower decline than UIP.
* **Sarcoidosis**: Often self-limiting; >50% have spontaneous remission (Löfgren syndrome has excellent prognosis).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Cardiopulmonary Complications
* **Pulmonary Hypertension (WHO Group 3)**: Chronic hypoxia → hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction → vascular remodeling.
* **Cor Pulmonale**: RV failure secondary to pulmonary hypertension → intractable systemic congestion.
* **Malignancy**: Significantly ↑ risk of bronchogenic carcinoma (especially in IPF, Asbestosis). Silicosis also predisposes to lung cancer.
* **Mycobacterial Infections**: Silicosis specifically impairs macrophage phagocytosis → high risk of *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Silicotuberculosis) and NTM infections.
* **Pneumothorax**: Rupture of subpleural blebs/honeycomb cysts.

### Adverse Effects of Antifibrotics & Immunosuppressants
* **Pirfenidone**: Severe photosensitivity rash (advise strict sun protection), GI upset (nausea, anorexia).
* **Nintedanib**: Severe diarrhea (requires loperamide co-prescription), hepatotoxicity (monitor LFTs), ↑ bleeding risk.
* **Steroids/Immunosuppressants**: Opportunistic infections (PJP, fungal), osteoporosis, metabolic syndrome.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic HRCT appearance of Usual Interstitial Pneumonia (UIP)? Answer: Basal and subpleural predominant reticular opacities, honeycombing, and traction bronchiectasis with an absence of significant ground-glass opacities.
* Viva Question: A patient with suspected IPF undergoes spirometry. What pattern do you expect, and what is the most sensitive early lung function test? Answer: A restrictive spirometry pattern (reduced FVC with a normal/high FEV1/FVC ratio), but a reduced DLCO is the earliest and most sensitive test.
* Trap: Assuming that finding a UIP pattern on an HRCT means the diagnosis is automatically IPF. Pearl: UIP is a radiological/pathological pattern, not a final diagnosis. You must rule out secondary causes of UIP (e.g., Rheumatoid Arthritis, Asbestosis) before diagnosing Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis.
* Trap: Giving high-dose systemic steroids and azathioprine for a patient newly diagnosed with true IPF. Pearl: This combination (plus N-acetylcysteine) was proven by the PANTHER trial to increase mortality and hospitalizations in IPF. Use antifibrotics (Pirfenidone/Nintedanib) instead.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing Silicosis but forgetting to screen for Tuberculosis. Active TB co-infection is extremely common in silicotic patients in Hong Kong due to impaired macrophage function (silico-tuberculosis); always send sputum for AFB smear and culture.
* Pitfall: Failing to identify clubbing during the physical exam of an ILD patient. Clubbing is a hallmark of fibrotic lung diseases like IPF and Asbestosis, but its absence does not rule out other ILDs (it is rare in Sarcoidosis and HP).
* Viva Question: What are the indications for prescribing antifibrotic medications under the Hong Kong Hospital Authority? Answer: Diagnosis of IPF confirmed by MDT, with an FVC between 50-80% predicted, as medications slow progression but do not reverse existing extensive fibrosis.
