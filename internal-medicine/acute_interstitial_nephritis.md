# Acute Interstitial Nephritis (急性間質性腎炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Acute Interstitial Nephritis
* **Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN)**: Immune-mediated, primarily non-dose-dependent inflammatory lesion of the renal interstitium and tubules, sparing glomeruli and vasculature.
* Characterized by sudden decline in renal function **AKI (急性腎損傷)**, typically 7–14 days post-exposure to inciting agent.

### Drug-Induced Etiologies of AIN
* Accounts for ~70-75% of AIN cases. 
* **Antibiotics**: **Beta-lactams** (Penicillins, Cephalosporins), **Rifampicin** (利福平), Ciprofloxacin, Sulfonamides.
* **Analgesics**: **NSAIDs** (非類固醇消炎止痛藥) (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen; onset can be delayed months).
* **Gastric Acid Suppressants**: **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)** (質子泵抑制劑) (e.g., Pantoprazole, Omeprazole; highly prevalent in HK HA elderly population, often insidious onset).
* **Others**: **Allopurinol** (別嘌醇), Phenytoin, Diuretics (Thiazides, Loop), Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (ICIs).

### Infectious & Autoimmune Etiologies of AIN
* **Infections (15%)**: Legionella, Leptospirosis, Streptococcus, CMV, EBV, HIV.
* **Systemic Diseases (10%)**: **SLE (系統性紅斑狼瘡)**, **Sjögren’s Syndrome (乾燥綜合症)**, Sarcoidosis, Tubulointerstitial Nephritis and Uveitis syndrome **(TINU)**, IgG4-related disease.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Drug-Induced AIN
* **Type IV Hypersensitivity (Cell-Mediated)**: Drug acts as a hapten → binds to Tubular Basement Membrane (TBM) antigens → alters immunogenicity.
* Dendritic cells present altered TBM antigen to CD4+ T-cells → cytokine release (IL-4, IL-5) → recruitment of CD8+ T-cells, macrophages, and eosinophils.
* Result: Interstitial edema + tubular cell apoptosis (Tubulitis) → ↑ intratubular pressure → ↓ GFR.

### Pathogenesis of NSAID-Induced AIN
* Unique mechanism: Often associated with **Nephrotic Syndrome** (Minimal Change Disease).
* Inhibition of COX → shunting of arachidonic acid to lipoxygenase pathway → ↑ leukotrienes → T-cell activation + increased glomerular permeability to proteins.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking in AIN
* **Medication History (CRITICAL)**: New drugs started 1-3 weeks prior (Beta-lactams) OR chronic use of NSAIDs/PPIs (months). Over-the-counter traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) usage.
* **Constitutional**: Fever, lethargy, generalized malaise.
* **Renal**: Flank pain (due to renal capsular distension from interstitial edema), oliguria (or polyuria due to tubular concentrating defect).
* **Systemic**: Visual changes/red eye (TINU), joint pains (Autoimmune).

### Physical Examination Findings in AIN
* **Classic Triad**: Present in <10% of patients (mostly with Methicillin/Beta-lactams).
  * **Fever (發燒)**
  * **Maculopapular Rash (斑丘疹)**
  * **Arthralgia / Eosinophilia** (Note: Eosinophilia is a lab finding, but triad clinically includes allergic stigmata).
* **Vitals**: Normotensive (unlike glomerulonephritis where HTN is common).
* **Abdomen**: Mild bilateral flank/renal angle tenderness.
* **Extremities**: Typically lacks severe edema (unless NSAID-induced concurrent Nephrotic Syndrome).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Differentials for Acute Kidney Injury
| Diagnosis | Key Differentiating Features from AIN |
| :--- | :--- |
| **ATN (急性腎小管壞死)** | Ischemic/toxic insult history. Muddy brown casts. Normal eosinophils. No rash/fever. |
| **Acute Glomerulonephritis** | HTN, generalized edema, prominent hematuria, dysmorphic RBCs/RBC casts, heavy proteinuria (>3.5g/d). |
| **Pre-renal Azotemia** | Dry mucous membranes, ↓ JVP, postural hypotension. Bland urine sediment. |
| **Acute Pyelonephritis** | High fever, severe rigors, dysuria, positive urine culture, toxic appearance. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for AIN
* **Urine Dipstick**: +ve Leukocytes, mild/trace Proteinuria (usually <1g/day, EXCEPT NSAID-AIN which can be >3.5g/day), +/- microhematuria.
* **ECG**: Rule out hyperkalemic changes (peaked T waves) if severe AKI present.

### Blood Investigations for AIN
* **CBC**: **Eosinophilia (嗜酸性白血球增多)** (Present in ~80% but frequently absent in NSAID/PPI-induced AIN).
* **Renal Panel**: ↑ Urea, ↑ Creatinine (AKI pattern), electrolyte derangements (↑ K+, metabolic acidosis).
* **Immunology (if suspected systemic cause)**: ANA, Anti-dsDNA, Anti-Ro/La (Sjögren's), Serum ACE (Sarcoidosis), IgG4 levels.

### Urine Microscopy and Specific Tests for AIN
* **Urine Microscopy**: **Sterile Pyuria (無菌性膿尿)**, **WBC Casts (白血球管型)**, non-dysmorphic RBCs. *Absence of muddy brown casts.*
* **Urine Eosinophils (Hansel Stain)**: Historically taught, but currently considered poor sensitivity/specificity. **NOT** recommended for ruling AIN in or out in modern practice.
* **Urine Culture**: Negative (differentiates from pyelonephritis).

### Renal Imaging for AIN
* **USG Kidneys (超聲波)**: Bilateral enlarged, echogenic kidneys (due to severe interstitial edema). Excludes obstructive uropathy.

### Renal Biopsy Findings in AIN (Gold Standard)
* **Indications**: Diagnostic uncertainty, lack of recovery 5-7 days after stopping culprit drug, or before starting empiric steroids.
* **Light Microscopy**: Patchy/diffuse interstitial inflammatory infiltrate (lymphocytes, macrophages, eosinophils), **Tubulitis** (WBCs invading tubular epithelium), Interstitial edema.
* **Immunofluorescence**: Typically negative (except in specific autoimmune causes like Anti-TBM disease).
* **Electron Microscopy**: Normal glomeruli (except foot process effacement in NSAID-induced MCD).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Initial Management of AIN
* **Immediate Cessation (停藥)**: Stop the offending drug immediately. This is the single most important intervention.
* **Supportive Care**: Fluid and electrolyte management. Correct acidosis and hyperkalemia.

### Pharmacological Acute Exacerbation Algorithm
| Step | Intervention | Indications | Regimen (HK HA standard) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Culprit Withdrawal** | All AIN cases. | Immediate stop of suspect agent. |
| **2** | **Systemic Corticosteroids (類固醇)** | Biopsy-proven AIN OR high clinical suspicion failing to improve 3-7 days post-withdrawal. | PO Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day (max 60mg) for 1-2 weeks. |
| **3** | **Steroid Tapering** | Upon stabilization/improvement of Creatinine. | Taper slowly over 4-6 weeks to prevent relapse. |
| **4** | **IV Methylprednisolone** | Severe, rapidly progressive AKI. | IV Methylprednisolone 250-500mg/day for 3 days, then switch to PO. |
| **5** | **Immunosuppressants** | Steroid-refractory AIN (rare). | Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF) or Cyclophosphamide (specialist decision). |

### Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT) Indications in AIN
* Initiate emergent Hemodialysis (血液透析) for standard refractory AKI indications: **AEIOU**
  * **A**cidosis (pH <7.1 refractory to medical Rx).
  * **E**lectrolytes (refractory Hyperkalemia).
  * **I**ntoxication (if culprit drug is dialyzable - rare in AIN context).
  * **O**verload (refractory Pulmonary Edema).
  * **U**remia (Uremic encephalopathy/pericarditis).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Pharmacological & System Management for AIN
* **Strict Avoidance**: Lifelong avoidance of the offending drug class. 
* **Cross-Reactivity Warning**: Avoid all NSAIDs if one caused AIN. Use alternative antibiotics (e.g., Macrolides if Penicillin caused AIN).
* **E-PRC Alert**: Must document the culprit drug as a severe adverse drug reaction (ADR) in the HK HA Clinical Management System (CMS) to prevent inadvertent re-prescription.

### Chronic Lifestyle & Follow-Up for AIN
* **MedicAlert**: Recommend carrying a warning card/bracelet.
* **Nephrology Follow-up**: Monitor for progression to Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD). Serial monitoring of U&E and UPCR.
* **BP Optimization**: Target BP <130/80 mmHg if residual CKD develops.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Factors for Incomplete Renal Recovery in AIN
* Prolonged duration of AKI (>3 weeks) prior to steroid initiation.
* Delayed cessation of the offending drug.
* **Renal Biopsy Findings**: Presence of **Interstitial Fibrosis / Tubular Atrophy (IFTA)** signifies irreversible chronic damage.
* Advanced age.
* **NSAID etiology**: Generally responds less favorably to steroids compared to Beta-lactam induced AIN.

### Prognosis and Recovery Timeline for AIN
* Renal function typically begins to improve within 1-2 weeks of stopping the drug and starting steroids.
* Up to 40% of patients will have some degree of residual baseline Creatinine elevation (permanent CKD).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Direct Renal Complications of AIN
* **Chronic Interstitial Nephritis / CKD (慢性腎病)**: Progression to end-stage renal disease (ESRD) requiring chronic dialysis.
* **Renal Papillary Necrosis**: Specifically associated with chronic NSAID abuse.
* **Tubular Dysfunction**: Type 1 or Type 4 Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA), Fanconi syndrome (glycosuria, phosphaturia, aminoaciduria), nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.

### Treatment-Related Adverse Effects (Steroid Toxicity)
* Due to prolonged Prednisolone use:
* **Infectious**: ↑ risk of opportunistic infections.
* **Metabolic**: Steroid-induced diabetes mellitus, weight gain.
* **Musculoskeletal**: Osteoporosis, Avascular Necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic clinical triad of acute interstitial nephritis, and how often is it actually seen? Answer: Fever, rash, and eosinophilia. It is seen in less than 10% of patients, making a high index of suspicion necessary.
* Trap: Relying on urine eosinophils to confirm or exclude AIN. Pearl: Urine eosinophils (Hansel stain) have poor sensitivity and specificity and are no longer recommended in modern guidelines to diagnose AIN.
* Pitfall: Assuming AIN only presents acutely with fever and rash. PPI-induced AIN often presents insidiously in elderly patients with asymptomatic, progressive renal impairment and no systemic allergic signs.
* Viva Question: What unique finding might you see on urine dipstick/quantification in NSAID-induced AIN compared to antibiotic-induced AIN? Answer: Nephrotic-range proteinuria (>3.5g/day), because NSAIDs can simultaneously cause Minimal Change Disease.
* Trap: Continuing PPIs in a patient presenting with unexplained AKI. Pearl: Always scrutinize the drug chart. PPIs are a massive, silent cause of AIN in HK HA hospitals; promptly switch to an H2-receptor antagonist (e.g., Famotidine) if AIN is suspected.
