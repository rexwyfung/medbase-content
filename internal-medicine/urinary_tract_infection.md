# Urinary Tract Infection (泌尿道感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Classification of UTI
* **Lower UTI (Cystitis / 膀胱炎)**: Infection confined to bladder & urethra.
* **Upper UTI [Pyelonephritis (腎盂腎炎)]**: Infection extending to renal pelvis & parenchyma.
* **Uncomplicated UTI**: Occurs in structurally/functionally normal urinary tract of healthy, premenopausal, non-pregnant females.
* **Complicated UTI**: Infection associated with factors ↑ risk of therapy failure (e.g., male sex, pregnancy, indwelling catheter, immunosuppression, structural/neurological abnormalities, healthcare-associated).
* **Recurrent UTI**: ≥2 episodes in 6 months OR ≥3 episodes in 12 months.

### Common Causative Organisms in UTI
* **Escherichia coli (E. coli)**: Accounts for 75-90% of uncomplicated cases (Uropathogenic E. coli - UPEC).
* **KEEPS** Mnemonic for typical organisms:
  * **K**lebsiella pneumoniae (frequent in HA/CAUTI)
  * **E**. coli
  * **E**nterococcus faecalis (often in elderly, catheterized)
  * **P**roteus mirabilis (associated with struvite stones ↑ urine pH)
  * **S**taphylococcus saprophyticus (common in young sexually active females)
* **Pseudomonas aeruginosa**: Common in structural defects, recent instrumentation, or indwelling catheters [Catheter-Associated UTI (CAUTI)].

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Ascending Infection Mechanism
* **Periurethral Colonization**: Fecal flora colonize vaginal introitus/periurethral area → ascend via urethra into bladder.
* **Female Predisposition**: Shorter urethra + anatomical proximity to anus ↑ susceptibility.
* **Upper Tract Ascent**: Flagella-mediated motility allows ascent against urine flow via ureters to renal pelvis.

### Host Defense vs Bacterial Virulence
* **Bacterial Virulence Factors**:
  * **Type 1 Fimbriae**: Bind mannose receptors on bladder uroepithelium → causes cystitis.
  * **P Fimbriae**: Bind globosides on renal epithelial cells → causes pyelonephritis (resists washout by urine flow).
  * **Urease Production**: By *Proteus* & *Klebsiella* → splits urea into ammonia → ↑ urine pH (alkaline) → precipitation of struvite (Mg-NH4-PO4) calculi.
  * **Biofilm Formation**: Extracellular polymeric matrix on foreign bodies (catheters) → protects pathogens from abx & host immunity.
* **Host Defenses**: Urine osmolality, low pH, regular mechanical washout, Tamm-Horsfall protein (binds Type 1 fimbriae).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History & Symptoms of Lower UTI
* **Storage Symptoms**: Frequency, urgency, nocturia.
* **Voiding Symptoms**: Dysuria (burning sensation on micturition), strangury (slow, painful voiding).
* **Gross Hematuria (血尿)**: Often terminal.
* **Pain**: Suprapubic heaviness or discomfort.
* **Elderly Presentation**: Atypical (confusion, delirium, functional decline, falls, lethargy).

### History & Symptoms of Upper UTI (Pyelonephritis)
* **Systemic Symptoms**: High fever, rigors (chills), nausea, vomiting, malaise.
* **Pain**: Unilateral or bilateral loin/flank pain radiating to iliac fossa or groin.
* **Concomitant Lower Symptoms**: May or may not have preceding dysuria/frequency.

### Physical Examination Findings in UTI
* **Vitals**: Tachycardia, pyrexia, hypotension (if evolving to urosepsis).
* **Abdominal Exam**:
  * Suprapubic tenderness (cystitis).
  * Renal angle tenderness / Costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness (pyelonephritis).
  * Palpable bladder (retention).
* **Genitourinary Exam**:
  * **Prostate (PR Exam)**: Extremely tender, warm, boggy prostate in Acute Bacterial Prostatitis (前列腺炎).
  * **Pelvic Exam**: Vaginal atrophy, discharge, or prolapse (especially in recurrent UTI).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Dysuria & Pelvic Pain
| Condition | Key Discriminatory Features (Hx & Exam) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **STI / Urethritis** | Gradual onset, new sexual partner, purulent/mucoid urethral discharge, no frequency/urgency. |
| **Vaginitis** | Vaginal discharge, pruritus, dyspareunia, distinct odor, no urinary frequency. |
| **Nephrolithiasis (腎結石)** | Colicky unilateral flank pain radiating to groin, restless patient (unable to find comfortable position). |
| **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease** | Bilateral lower abdominal pain, cervical motion tenderness (+ve Chandelier sign). |
| **Interstitial Cystitis** | Chronic pelvic pain (>6 weeks) relieved by voiding, negative cultures. |
| **Prostatitis** | Perineal/rectal pain, exquisitely tender prostate on PR, dysuria. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Urinalysis in UTI
* **Urine Dipstick**:
  * **Nitrite**: Indicates presence of Enterobacteriaceae (convert dietary nitrate to nitrite). High specificity, low sensitivity (false -ve in *Enterococcus*, *Pseudomonas*, or frequent voiding).
  * **Leukocyte Esterase (LE)**: Enzyme released by neutrophils. Indicates pyuria. High sensitivity.
* **Visual**: Cloudy, malodorous, macroscopic hematuria.

### Laboratory Investigations for UTI
* **Mid-Stream Urine (MSU) Microscopy**:
  * Pyuria (WBC > 10/hpf or > 10^4/mL).
  * Bacteriuria.
  * RBCs (hematuria).
* **MSU Culture & Sensitivity (C&S) [Gold Standard]**:
  * **Diagnostic Criteria**:
    * Symptomatic female: $\ge$ $10^3$ CFU/mL.
    * Symptomatic male: $\ge$ $10^3$ CFU/mL.
    * Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ASB): $\ge$ $10^5$ CFU/mL in 2 consecutive samples (female) or 1 sample (male).
    * Catheter specimen: $\ge$ $10^2$ CFU/mL.
* **Blood Tests (For Upper/Complicated UTI ONLY)**:
  * **CBC**: Leukocytosis with left shift.
  * **Renal Function (U&E/Cr)**: Assess for AKI, guide antibiotic dosing.
  * **CRP/ESR**: Markedly ↑ in pyelonephritis/prostatitis.
  * **Blood Cultures**: Taken during fever spikes (often +ve in pyelonephritis).

### Indications for Imaging in UTI
* **USG Kidneys, Ureters, Bladder (KUB) / CT KUB / CT Urogram**:
  * Indicated if: Males (exclude structural anomalies), recurrent UTI, poor response to IV Abx within 48-72 hrs, suspected renal abscess/obstruction (stones), visible hematuria (rule out malignancy).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Uncomplicated Cystitis Antibiotic Protocol
* *Note: Always adjust based on local Hospital Authority (HA) antibiogram.*
* **First-line (PO)**:
  * **Nitrofurantoin** 50mg QID / 100mg BD for 3-5 days (Avoid if CrCl < 45 mL/min).
  * **Fosfomycin** 3g stat single dose.
* **Second-line (PO)**:
  * **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin)** 375mg TDS for 3-5 days.
  * **Cefuroxime** 250mg BD for 3-5 days.
* *Avoid Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin) empirically for simple cystitis due to resistance & collateral damage.*

### Acute Complicated Cystitis & Pyelonephritis Protocol
* **Mild/Moderate (PO, Outpatient)**:
  * **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin)** 1g BD for 7-14 days.
  * **Ciprofloxacin** 500mg BD for 7 days (if local resistance < 10%).
* **Severe/Vomiting (IV, Inpatient)**:
  * **IV Amoxicillin-Clavulanate** 1.2g Q8H OR **IV Cefuroxime** 750mg Q8H.
  * Add **IV Gentamicin** (5mg/kg OD) if urosepsis suspected or severe illness (monitor renal function & ototoxicity).
  * Step down to PO when afebrile for 24-48 hrs. Total duration: 10-14 days.
* **Suspected ESBL (Extended-Spectrum Beta-Lactamase)**:
  * **IV Ertapenem** or **IV Meropenem**.

### Catheter-Associated UTI (CAUTI) Management
* **Criteria**: Symptoms + Urine culture $\ge$ $10^3$ CFU/mL + Catheter in place for > 48h.
* **Action**: Remove or exchange catheter prior to initiating Abx (removes biofilm).
* **Treatment**: Target Abx based on previous cultures; typical duration 7-14 days. Do NOT treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in catheterized patients.

### Asymptomatic Bacteriuria Management Protocol
* **Strict Rule**: DO NOT screen or treat ASB in elderly, diabetics, or catheterized patients.
* **Indications TO TREAT ASB**:
  1. **Pregnancy**: High risk of progression to pyelonephritis → pre-term labor. (Use Nitrofurantoin, Amoxicillin, or Cephalexin; Avoid Nitrofurantoin at term).
  2. **Prior to traumatic urological procedures** (e.g., TURP) where mucosal bleeding is anticipated.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Recurrent UTI Lifestyle Modifications
* Increase oral fluid intake (≥ 2-3L/day).
* Frequent and complete bladder emptying.
* Post-coital voiding.
* Wiping front-to-back.
* Avoid spermicidal jellies/diaphragms.
* **Cranberry extract / D-mannose**: Weak evidence, but disrupts bacterial adherence (Type 1 fimbriae).

### Recurrent UTI Pharmacological Prophylaxis
* **Post-menopausal Females**:
  * **Topical Vaginal Estrogen**: Reverses urogenital atrophy, restores lactobacilli, ↓ vaginal pH.
* **Antibiotic Prophylaxis** (Consider only after eradication of current UTI and failed conservative measures):
  * **Continuous low-dose**: Nitrofurantoin 50mg nocte OR Trimethoprim 100mg nocte (for 3-6 months).
  * **Post-coital prophylaxis**: Single dose Abx (e.g., Nitrofurantoin 50mg) immediately after intercourse (if temporally related).
  * **Patient-initiated self-start therapy**: Supply 3-day course of Abx for patient to take at symptom onset.

### Chronic Prostatitis Management
* **Pharmacology**: Requires Abx with excellent prostatic tissue penetration.
* **Regimen**: **PO Ciprofloxacin** 500mg BD OR **PO Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim)** for **4-6 weeks**.
* Avoid repeated prostatic massage in acute setting (risk of bacteremia).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for UTI Complications
* **High-Risk Factors for MDR/ESBL Pathogens**:
  * Recent hospitalization (within 3-6 months).
  * Residence in Long-Term Care Facility (LTCF) / Old Age Home (OAH).
  * Recent broad-spectrum Abx use.
  * Known structural urinary tract abnormalities or indwelling devices.
  * Travel to endemic areas (e.g., India, Southeast Asia).

### Prognostic Factors in Pyelonephritis
* **Criteria for Inpatient Admission**:
  * Inability to maintain oral hydration/medication (severe vomiting).
  * Hemodynamic instability (Tachycardia, Hypotension).
  * Pregnancy.
  * Immunocompromised state (Neutropenia, transplant recipient, poorly controlled DM).
  * Suspected obstruction (stone) + infection (urological emergency).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of UTI
* **Renal Abscess**: Suspect if persistent fever > 72 hours despite appropriate IV Abx. Requires CT for Dx and percutaneous drainage.
* **Emphysematous Pyelonephritis**: Severe necrotizing infection characterized by gas in renal parenchyma. Almost exclusive to Diabetics. High mortality. Requires urgent nephrectomy.
* **Papillary Necrosis**: Sloughing of renal papillae causing ureteric obstruction (associated with diabetes, sickle cell trait, analgesic abuse).
* **Pregnancy Complications**: Untreated UTI/ASB → Pyelonephritis → Pre-term labor, low birth weight, pre-eclampsia.

### Adverse Effects of UTI Pharmacotherapy
* **Nitrofurantoin**: Interstitial pulmonary fibrosis (chronic use), peripheral neuropathy, hemolytic anemia in G6PD deficiency. Ineffective if CrCl < 45.
* **Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin)**: Tendonitis/Achilles tendon rupture, QT prolongation, dysglycemia, C. difficile infection.
* **Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole (Septrin)**: Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), hyperkalemia, bone marrow suppression, hemolysis in G6PD.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: When do you treat Asymptomatic Bacteriuria? Answer: Only in pregnant women (to prevent pyelonephritis and premature labor) and patients undergoing traumatic urological interventions (e.g., TURP).
* Trap: Prescribing Nitrofurantoin for Pyelonephritis. Pearl: Nitrofurantoin achieves high concentrations in the bladder but poor tissue concentrations in the kidneys/blood. It is useless for pyelonephritis or urosepsis.
* Pitfall: Routinely doing urine dipsticks in catheterized patients with cloudy, foul-smelling urine. Catheterized patients invariably have bacterial colonization (biofilm). Do not test or treat unless they exhibit systemic signs of infection (fever, rigors, flank pain, altered mental status).
* Viva Question: What is the most common organism producing staghorn calculi, and what is the mechanism? Answer: *Proteus mirabilis*. It produces urease, splitting urea into ammonia, creating an alkaline urine environment that precipitates magnesium ammonium phosphate (struvite).
* Trap: Giving Amoxicillin empirically for uncomplicated cystitis. Pearl: High resistance rates of E. coli to Ampicillin/Amoxicillin (~50% locally). Always use Nitrofurantoin, Fosfomycin, or Amoxicillin-Clavulanate as per local HA antibiogram.
* Pitfall: Failing to examine male patients with UTI. UTI in males is considered "complicated" until proven otherwise. Always perform a PR exam to rule out prostatitis, and consider USG KUB to rule out obstruction or incomplete emptying (e.g., BPH).
