# Fever of Unknown Origin (不明原因發熱)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Classic Definition of FUO (Petersdorf & Beeson Criteria)
* **Temperature**: >38.3°C (101°F) on several occasions
* **Duration**: >3 weeks
* **Evaluation**: Failure to reach Dx despite 1 week of inpatient Ix OR 3 outpatient visits

### Subtypes of FUO Criteria
| Subtype | Patient Population | Definition / Criteria | Common Causes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classic** | General | >3 weeks duration, >3 outpatient visits or 3 days in-hospital without Dx | Infection, Malignancy, CTD |
| **Nosocomial** | Hospitalized | Temp >38.3°C, no infection present/incubating on admission, undiagnosed after 3 days | C. diff, DVT/PE, Drug fever, IV line infection |
| **Neutropenic** | ANC <500/μL | Temp >38.3°C, undiagnosed after 3 days with -ve cultures | Fungal (Aspergillus/Candida), Occult bacteremia |
| **HIV-associated**| HIV +ve | Temp >38.3°C, >4 weeks (outpatient) or >3 days (inpatient) | MAC, PCP, CMV, Lymphoma, TB |

### Local Epidemiology of FUO in Hong Kong
* **Tuberculosis (結核病)**: Endemic in HK; remains a top cause of FUO (esp. extrapulmonary/miliary TB).
* **Melioidosis (類鼻疽)**: Endemic in local soil/water (esp. Sham Shui Po cluster/post-typhoon); caused by *Burkholderia pseudomallei*.
* **Scrub Typhus (叢林斑疹傷寒)**: Hikers in HK country parks; caused by *Orientia tsutsugamushi* via chigger bites.
* **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC, 肝細胞癌)**: Endemic HBV background makes HCC a prominent malignant cause of FUO in HK.

### Core Etiological Categories of Classic FUO
* **Infections** (~30-40%): Abscesses, TB, Endocarditis, Osteomyelitis.
* **Malignancies** (~20-30%): Lymphoma (HL/NHL), Leukaemias, Solid tumors (RCC, HCC).
* **Connective Tissue Diseases (CTD)** (~10-20%): Adult-Onset Still’s Disease (AOSD), Vasculitis (GCA, PAN), SLE.
* **Miscellaneous / Drugs** (~10-15%): Drug fever, DVT/PE, Factitious fever, Thyroiditis.
* **Undiagnosed** (Up to 30%): Remains elusive despite extensive Ix; generally carries a benign prognosis.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Fever Generation
* **Exogenous Pyrogens**: Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (PAMPs) (e.g., LPS/endotoxin) bind Toll-Like Receptors (TLRs) on macrophages/monocytes.
* **Endogenous Pyrogens**: Macrophages release **IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ**.
* **Hypothalamic Activation**: Cytokines cross blood-brain barrier at Organum Vasculosum of the Lamina Terminalis (OVLT).
* **Prostaglandin Synthesis**: Endothelial cells release **PGE2** via COX-2 pathway.
* **Thermoregulatory Reset**: PGE2 acts on Preoptic Area (POA) of Hypothalamus (下丘腦) → ↑ cyclic AMP → ↑ thermoregulatory set-point → vasoconstriction & shivering (heat conservation/generation).

### Pathophysiology of Factitious Fever
* **Mechanism**: Artificial manipulation of thermometers or deliberate self-injection of contaminated substances.
* **Lack of Cytokine Cascade**: Normal thermoregulatory set-point; typically lacks accompanying signs of systemic inflammation (e.g., absent tachycardia/rigors corresponding to temp spikes).

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### History Taking in FUO
* **Travel History (旅遊史)**: Recent travel (Malaria, Typhoid, Dengue), pre-travel vaccines.
* **Occupational / Environmental**: Animal slaughter (Brucellosis/Q fever), hiking in brush (Scrub typhus), gardening/mud exposure (Melioidosis).
* **Medication History**: Recent Abx, anti-epileptics (phenytoin), allopurinol (Drug fever/DRESS).
* **Constitutional Symptoms**: Unintentional weight loss, drenching night sweats (TB/Lymphoma).
* **Localizing Symptoms**: Jaw claudication, visual loss, temporal headache (GCA).

### Physical Examination - Vital Signs & General Assessment
* **Faget Sign (Relative Bradycardia)**: Temp ↑ without corresponding HR ↑ (Typhoid fever, Legionella, Mycoplasma, Drug fever, CNS lesions).
* **Fever Pattern**:
  * **Quotidian** (daily spikes): Adult-Onset Still’s Disease (spikes in evening/night).
  * **Pel-Ebstein**: Cyclical fever lasting 1-2 weeks followed by 1-2 afebrile weeks (Hodgkin Lymphoma).
  * **Tertian/Quartan**: Spikes every 48/72 hours (Malaria).
* **General Appearance**: Cachexia, rigors, signs of self-harm/injection sites (factitious).

### Physical Examination - System-Specific Signs
* **HEENT**: Temporal artery thickening/tenderness (GCA), Roth spots (IE), thrush (HIV).
* **Lymphatic**: Generalized vs regional lymphadenopathy (淋巴結腫大). Check Virchow's node, axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal.
* **Cardiovascular**: New regurgitant murmur (Infective Endocarditis).
* **Abdominal**: Hepatomegaly, splenomegaly (肝脾腫大) (Lymphoma, EBV/CMV, Malaria).
* **Dermatological**:
  * **Eschar (焦痂)**: Painless black crust (Scrub typhus).
  * **Evanescent Salmon-Pink Rash**: Coincides with fever spikes (AOSD).
  * **Petechiae/Splinter Hemorrhages/Janeway Lesions/Osler Nodes**: (IE).
* **Musculoskeletal**: Joint effusions, spinal tenderness to percussion (Vertebral Osteomyelitis/Discitis).
* **Genitourinary**: Epididymal nodularity, prostate tenderness (chronic prostatitis/TB).

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Phase 1: Baseline Bloods & Bedside Investigations
* **Blood Cultures (血培養)**: Minimum 3 sets from different venipuncture sites off Abx. Retain for prolonged incubation (HACEK/fungal).
* **Smears**: Thick & thin blood films for malaria (x3 over 3 days).
* **Hematology**: CBC, peripheral blood smear (blasts/atypical lymphocytes).
* **Biochemistry**: RFT, LFT (↑ ALP suggests infiltrative liver disease/abscess), LDH (↑ in lymphoma), CK (myositis).
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ESR, CRP, Procalcitonin.
* **Immunology**: ANA, Rheumatoid Factor (RF).
* **Urinalysis**: Microscopy, culture, and sensitivity (MSU). Early morning urine for AFB x3 (if GU TB suspected).

### Phase 1: Baseline Imaging
* **CXR**: Rule out miliary TB, occult pneumonia, sarcoidosis, hilar lymphadenopathy.

### Phase 2: Serology & Specialized Bloods
* **Viral/Infectious Screen**: HIV Ag/Ab, HBV/HCV serology, Syphilis (VDRL), EBV/CMV IgM/PCR.
* **Atypical Serologies**: *Coxiella burnetii* (Q fever), *Brucella*, *Orientia tsutsugamushi* (Scrub typhus), *Burkholderia pseudomallei* serology/PCR.
* **Autoimmune Screen**: Anti-dsDNA, ENA panel, ANCA, C3/C4, Serum ACE level.
* **Tumor Markers**: Unhelpful as primary screen; use targeted if specific imaging findings (e.g., AFP for liver mass).

### Phase 2: Advanced Imaging & Echo
* **CT Thorax/Abdomen/Pelvis (Contrast)**: To detect occult abscesses, retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy, intra-abdominal malignancies.
* **Echocardiography (超聲心動圖)**: TTE first; TEE strongly indicated if high clinical suspicion of IE or non-diagnostic TTE.
* **Venous Doppler US**: Bilateral lower limbs to rule out silent DVT.

### Phase 3: Nuclear Medicine Imaging
* **18F-FDG PET-CT (正電子發射電腦斷層掃描)**:
  * **Indication**: Phase 1 & 2 Ix -ve. Gold standard for localizing occult inflammation, infection, and malignancy.
  * **Target**: Highly sensitive for large-vessel vasculitis, osteomyelitis, and lymphoma.
* **Gallium/Indium-Labeled WBC Scans**: Largely replaced by PET-CT in modern HK HA settings, but used if PET-CT contraindicated/unavailable.

### Phase 4: Invasive Tissue Biopsy Diagnostics
* **Lymph Node Biopsy**: Excision biopsy (切除活檢) mandatory; FNA is inadequate for lymphoma architecture.
* **Bone Marrow Aspirate & Trephine (BMA/T)**: Indicated if abnormal CBC (cytopenias), splenomegaly, or suspected hematological malignancy/miliary TB.
* **Temporal Artery Biopsy**: Mandatory if GCA suspected (length >2cm).
* **Liver Biopsy**: Considered if isolated ↑ ALP, hepatomegaly, or unknown granulomatous disease.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Hemodynamic Instability Protocol in FUO
* **Trigger**: Development of sepsis, septic shock, or acute end-organ failure during FUO workup.
* **Immediate Resuscitation**:
  * IV fluid bolus (Crystalloids 30mL/kg).
  * Draw urgent pan-cultures (Blood x2, Urine, Sputum, any fluid collections).
  * **Empiric Antimicrobials**: Initiate broad-spectrum Abx immediately AFTER cultures (e.g., IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam ± Vancomycin); delay is fatal.

### Acute Management of Neutropenic FUO (Oncology/Hematology)
* **Immediate Action**: Medical emergency. Do not wait for localized signs.
* **Protocol**: Draw cultures → IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam (or Meropenem) within 60 mins.
* **Fungal Coverage**: Add IV Caspofungin or Liposomal Amphotericin B if fever persists >72 hours on broad-spectrum Abx.

### Acute Management of Suspected GCA
* **Indication**: FUO + new severe headache + jaw claudication OR any visual symptoms.
* **Immediate Action**: IV Methylprednisolone or high-dose PO Prednisolone (1mg/kg) IMMEDIATELY to prevent irreversible blindness. Do NOT delay steroids for temporal artery biopsy.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Principles of Therapeutic Trials in FUO
* **General Rule**: Strictly avoid blind empiric therapeutic trials (antibiotics, anti-tuberculous drugs, corticosteroids) in stable classic FUO.
* **Rationale**: Empiric Rx masks symptoms, suppresses blood cultures, alters biopsy pathology, and delays definitive diagnosis.
* **Exception 1 (GCA)**: Visual symptoms → empiric steroids.
* **Exception 2 (Miliary TB)**: Rapid clinical deterioration + highly suggestive imaging → empiric Anti-TB Rx (Rifampicin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol).

### Management Algorithm for Undiagnosed FUO
* **Step 1: Discontinue Unnecessary Meds**: Stop all non-essential medications to rule out Drug Fever.
* **Step 2: Watchful Waiting**: Serial physical exams and repeat basic labs.
* **Step 3: Symptomatic Relief**:
  * NSAIDs (e.g., Naproxen, Ibuprofen) for constitutional symptoms.
  * Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) for fever suppression.
* **Step 4: Re-evaluation**: If fever persists >3-6 months without deterioration, prognosis is excellent. Fever resolves spontaneously in up to 50-100% of these idiopathic cases.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Risk Stratification in FUO
* **High-Risk Indicators (Poor Prognosis)**:
  * Age > 65 years (↑ risk of malignancy and multisystem CTD).
  * Involuntary weight loss > 10% body mass.
  * Progressive cytopenias (Anemia, Thrombocytopenia) → suggests marrow infiltration.
  * Immunosuppression (HIV, post-transplant, biologics).
* **Low-Risk Indicators (Good Prognosis)**:
  * Age < 35 years.
  * Normal CBC, ESR/CRP, and LFTs.
  * Prolonged duration (> 6-12 months) without end-organ damage (likely benign/idiopathic).

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### Systemic Complications of Prolonged Febrile Illness
* **Metabolic/Nutritional**: Cachexia, profound weight loss, protein-calorie malnutrition.
* **Neurological**: Delirium/Encephalopathy (especially in the elderly, leading to falls and prolonged hospitalization).
* **Renal**: Prerenal Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) secondary to insensible fluid losses (sweating) and poor oral intake.

### Iatrogenic Complications from Premature/Incorrect Treatment
* **Antibiotic Resistance**: From blind broad-spectrum use.
* **Disseminated Infection**: Fatal systemic spread if latent TB or deep abscess is incorrectly treated with empiric Corticosteroids for suspected autoimmune disease.
* **Diagnostic Delay**: Partial treatment of occult infection (e.g., partially treated IE) leads to weeks of -ve cultures and severe valvular destruction.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What constitutes the classic definition of FUO? Answer: Temperature >38.3°C, lasting >3 weeks, undiagnosed after 3 outpatient visits or 1 week of inpatient investigation.
* Viva Question: What is Faget sign and what does it suggest? Answer: Relative bradycardia (pulse does not rise appropriately with fever). Suggests Typhoid, Legionella, Mycoplasma, Drug fever, or CNS lesions.
* Trap: Recommending Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) for an enlarged lymph node in a patient with FUO and suspected Lymphoma. Pearl: FNA destroys lymph node architecture. You MUST request an excision biopsy to accurately diagnose and sub-type lymphoma.
* Trap: Delaying steroid administration in suspected Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA) while waiting for temporal artery biopsy results. Pearl: You must start high-dose steroids immediately to prevent permanent blindness. The biopsy remains positive for 1-2 weeks after starting steroids.
* Pitfall: Starting "empiric" anti-tuberculous therapy just because the patient is in Hong Kong and has a fever. Avoid empiric anti-TB therapy in stable patients unless microbiologically/histologically confirmed, as it commits them to 6 months of toxic drugs and obscures the true diagnosis.
* Pitfall: Stopping the FUO workup if an ANA returns weakly positive. Up to 15-20% of the healthy population has a low-titer ANA (e.g., 1:80). Interpret in the context of specific clinical signs (rash, arthritis, cytopenias) to avoid falsely diagnosing SLE.
