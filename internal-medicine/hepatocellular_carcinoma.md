# Hepatocellular Carcinoma (肝細胞癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)
* **Primary liver malignancy** arising from mature hepatocytes
* **Key feature:** Highly vascular tumor drawing 100% of blood supply from the hepatic artery (normal liver parenchyma receives 75% from portal vein, 25% from hepatic artery)

### Major Etiologies & Risk Factors
* **Viral Hepatitis:**
  * **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) (乙型肝炎):** Most common etiology in HK. Can cause HCC *without* preceding cirrhosis via insertional mutagenesis
  * **Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) (丙型肝炎):** Requires progression to cirrhosis to cause HCC
* **Metabolic & Toxin-induced:**
  * **Liver Cirrhosis (肝硬化):** 80% of all HCC cases occur in cirrhotic livers (regardless of underlying cause)
  * **Alcoholic Liver Disease:** Heavy chronic consumption
  * **Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD/NASH):** Rapidly rising etiology globally
  * **Aflatoxin B1:** Mycotoxin from *Aspergillus* (found in damp peanuts/grains); induces *TP53* mutation

### Local Hong Kong Epidemiology
* **Prevalence:** 5th most common cancer overall in HK
* **Mortality:** 3rd leading cause of cancer-related death in HK
* **Endemicity:** High local prevalence of chronic HBV (~8% of HK population) drives the high incidence
* **Gender:** Male > Female predominance (~3:1)

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Cirrhosis-Driven HCC
* Chronic hepatocyte injury (toxins/viruses) → Chronic inflammation
* Continuous necrosis → Compensatory cellular regeneration
* Accelerated cellular turnover → Accumulation of genetic epigenetic alterations (dysplasia)
* Dysplastic nodule (排斥性結節) → Early HCC → Progressed HCC

### HBV-Specific Oncogenic Mechanisms
* **Insertional Mutagenesis:** HBV DNA integrates into host hepatocyte genome
* **Oncogene Activation:** cis-activation of host oncogenes (e.g., *TERT* promoter, *MYC*)
* **HBx Protein Expression:** Viral regulatory protein binds and inactivates tumor suppressor *p53*

### Angiogenesis & Vascular Remodeling
* **Neoangiogenesis:** Tumor hypoxia → ↑ Hypoxia-Inducible Factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α)
* **Growth Factor Release:** ↑ Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) & Platelet-Derived Growth Factor (PDGF)
* **Arterialization:** Tumor shifts blood supply entirely to the newly formed chaotic hepatic arterial branches (basis for TACE therapy)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Pertinent History & Symptoms
* **Early Stage:** Mostly asymptomatic (incidental finding on surveillance)
* **Local Tumor Effects:**
  * Dull Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) abdominal pain
  * Early satiety (due to massive hepatomegaly)
  * Palpable abdominal mass
* **Systemic Malignancy Symptoms:**
  * Unintentional weight loss
  * Anorexia
  * Severe lethargy
* **Signs of Decompensation:**
  * Progressive abdominal distension (ascites)
  * Altered sleep-wake cycle or confusion (hepatic encephalopathy)
  * Hematemesis or melena (variceal bleeding)

### System-Specific Physical Examination Signs
* **Abdominal Palpation:**
  * **Hepatomegaly:** Hard, irregular, nodular liver edge
  * **Hepatic Bruit:** Audible arterial murmur over RUQ (due to hypervascularity)
  * **Hepatic Friction Rub:** Heard over the liver capsule (tumor invasion/inflammation)
* **Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease (CLD):**
  * Spider angiomas (蜘蛛痣)
  * Palmar erythema (紅斑)
  * Jaundice (icteric sclera/skin)
  * Gynecomastia (男性乳房發育)
  * Caput medusae (臍周靜脈曲張)
* **Signs of Portal Hypertension / Decompensation:**
  * Ascites (shifting dullness +ve)
  * Splenomegaly
  * Asterixis (flapping tremor)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Malignant Liver Lesions
* **Cholangiocarcinoma (膽管癌):**
  * Originates from biliary epithelium
  * Normal AFP levels
  * Associated with biliary dilatation and prominent jaundice
* **Metastatic Liver Disease (轉移性肝癌):**
  * Most common malignant liver tumor overall
  * Multiple scattered lesions on imaging
  * Primary site usually GI tract (Colorectal), Lung, or Breast
  * Tumor markers matched to primary (e.g., ↑ CEA, ↑ CA19-9)

### Benign Liver Lesions
* **Hepatic Hemangioma (血管瘤):**
  * Most common benign solid lesion
  * Imaging: Peripheral nodular enhancement with progressive centripetal fill-in
* **Focal Nodular Hyperplasia (FNH):**
  * Benign hyperplastic response to congenital vascular anomaly
  * Imaging: Classic central stellate scar with intense arterial enhancement
* **Hepatic Adenoma (肝腺瘤):**
  * Strongly associated with oral contraceptive pill (OCP) use in young females
  * High risk of spontaneous rupture and hemorrhage
  * Minor risk of malignant transformation

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### HCC Surveillance Protocol
* **Target Population:** All cirrhotic patients, high-risk chronic HBV carriers (Asian males >40, females >50)
* **Protocol:**
  * Abdominal Ultrasound (US) every 6 months
  * Serum Alpha-Fetoprotein (AFP) every 6 months

### Blood Investigations & Tumor Markers
* **Tumor Marker:**
  * Alpha-Fetoprotein (AFP) (甲胎蛋白): >400 ng/mL highly suggestive of HCC
  * *Note:* AFP can be normal in up to 30% of HCC cases; mild elevation seen in active hepatitis
* **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):**
  * ↑ Bilirubin, ↑ ALP, ↑ GGT (biliary obstruction or parenchymal destruction)
  * ↑ ALT/AST (ongoing necrosis/hepatitis)
* **Hepatic Synthetic Function (Child-Pugh components):**
  * ↓ Albumin
  * ↑ Prothrombin Time (PT) / ↑ INR
* **Viral Serology:**
  * HBsAg, Anti-HCV, HBV DNA load (essential for etiology workup)

### Diagnostic Imaging Studies (Triple-Phase)
* **Triple-Phase CT / Multi-parametric MRI Liver:**
  * **Arterial Phase:** Intense hyperenhancement (tumor takes up contrast rapidly via arterial supply)
  * **Portal Venous / Delayed Phase:** "Washout" (tumor appears darker/hypointense relative to surrounding normal parenchyma)
  * **Capsule Appearance:** Enhancing rim seen in delayed phase

### Diagnostic Criteria (LI-RADS)
* **LI-RADS (Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System):** Used to categorize lesions in cirrhotic/high-risk livers
  * **LR-1:** Definitely benign
  * **LR-2:** Probably benign
  * **LR-3:** Intermediate probability of HCC
  * **LR-4:** Probably HCC
  * **LR-5:** Definitely HCC (Requires NO biopsy)

### Role of Liver Biopsy
* **General Rule:** Biopsy is strictly AVOIDED if the lesion meets LR-5 criteria on imaging in a high-risk patient (risk of bleeding and needle-track tumor seeding)
* **Indications for Biopsy:**
  * Indeterminate lesion on imaging (LR-3 or LR-4)
  * Patient without cirrhosis or typical risk factors
  * Need for molecular profiling for systemic clinical trials

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Ruptured Hepatocellular Carcinoma (腫瘤破裂)
* **Clinical Presentation:**
  * Sudden onset severe abdominal pain
  * Hemodynamic instability (hypotension, tachycardia)
  * Rapidly progressive abdominal distension (hemoperitoneum)
  * Drop in hemoglobin
* **Acute Resuscitation (ABCDE):**
  * Secure airway and give high-flow O2
  * Establish 2x large-bore IV access
  * Initiate Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP) if in hemorrhagic shock
  * Correct coagulopathy (FFP, Platelets)
* **Definitive Hemostasis:**
  * **1st Line:** Urgent Angiography with Transarterial Embolization (TAE) to occlude bleeding vessel
  * **2nd Line:** Emergency laparotomy for perihepatic packing or palliative resection (if TAE unavailable or fails)

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Curative Intent Therapies (Very Early / Early Stage)
* **Surgical Resection (Hepatectomy):**
  * Treatment of choice for solitary tumors in non-cirrhotic or well-compensated cirrhotic livers (Child-Pugh A)
  * Prerequisites: Adequate Future Liver Remnant (FLR) and absence of clinically significant portal hypertension (normal bilirubin, PLT >100)
* **Liver Transplantation (肝臟移植):**
  * Cures both the tumor and the underlying cirrhosis
  * Must strictly fulfill **Milan Criteria** (see Section 8)
* **Percutaneous Local Ablation:**
  * Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA) or Microwave Ablation (MWA)
  * Indicated for small tumors (<3 cm) or poor surgical candidates
  * Deep-seated lesions preferred (superficial lesions risk capsular rupture/seeding)

### Locoregional Therapies (Intermediate Stage)
* **Transarterial Chemoembolization (TACE) (經動脈化療栓塞):**
  * Embolic microspheres coated with chemotherapy (e.g., Doxorubicin) injected directly into feeding hepatic artery
  * Induces tumor ischemia + high-dose localized cytotoxicity
  * Indicated for multinodular, unresectable HCC without portal vein invasion (BCLC B)
* **Transarterial Radioembolization (TARE / SIRT):**
  * Yttrium-90 (Y-90) radioactive microspheres injected into hepatic artery
  * Indicated for bulky disease or cases with portal vein branch thrombosis

### Systemic Pharmacotherapy (Advanced Stage)
* **First-Line Immunotherapy (Standard of Care in HK HA):**
  * **Atezolizumab** (anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibody) + **Bevacizumab** (anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody)
  * Contraindicated in untreated high-risk esophageal varices (bleeding risk from Bevacizumab)
* **First-Line Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs):**
  * **Sorafenib** or **Lenvatinib**
  * Used if immunotherapy is contraindicated
* **Second-Line Therapies:**
  * Regorafenib, Cabozantinib, Ramucirumab (for high AFP)

### Underlying Liver Disease Management
* **Antiviral Therapy:** Lifelong Nucleos(t)ide analogues (Entecavir or Tenofovir) for all HBV-related HCC patients
* **Lifestyle:** Absolute alcohol abstinence, optimize metabolic syndrome (HbA1c control, weight loss)

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### BCLC Staging System (Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer)
* **Function:** Integrates tumor burden, liver function (Child-Pugh), and performance status (ECOG) to guide specific treatment.

| BCLC Stage | Tumor Burden | Liver Function | ECOG PS | Recommended Rx | Median Survival |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0 (Very Early)** | Single ≤ 2 cm | Child-Pugh A | 0 | Resection / Ablation | > 5 years |
| **A (Early)** | Single OR ≤ 3 nodules (each ≤ 3 cm) | Child-Pugh A-B | 0 | Resection / Ablation / Transplant | > 5 years |
| **B (Intermediate)** | Multinodular, no vascular invasion | Child-Pugh A-B | 0 | TACE / Locoregional | ~ 2.5 years |
| **C (Advanced)** | Portal invasion OR Extrahepatic spread | Child-Pugh A-B | 1-2 | Systemic Therapy | ~ 1 - 1.5 years |
| **D (End-Stage)** | Any tumor burden | Child-Pugh C | 3-4 | Best Supportive Care | < 3 months |

### Milan Criteria for Liver Transplantation Eligibility
* **Function:** Determines if an HCC patient is a candidate for liver transplantation with acceptable recurrence rates.

| Milan Criteria (肝臟移植標準) | Parameter Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tumor Size / Number** | Single tumor ≤ 5 cm OR Up to 3 tumors, each ≤ 3 cm |
| **Vascular Invasion** | Absent (No macroscopic portal/hepatic vein involvement) |
| **Extrahepatic Spread** | Absent (No lymph node or distant metastases) |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Tumor Progression Complications
* **Portal Vein Tumor Thrombosis (PVTT):**
  * Direct macrovascular invasion into the portal venous system
  * Worsens portal hypertension → massive variceal bleeding or intractable ascites
* **Obstructive Jaundice:**
  * Direct compression or intrabiliary tumor growth
* **Extrahepatic Metastasis:**
  * Most common sites: Lungs, regional lymph nodes, bones

### Paraneoplastic Syndromes of HCC
* **Hypoglycemia:**
  * Due to tumor high metabolic consumption OR secretion of Insulin-like Growth Factor II (IGF-II)
* **Erythrocytosis (Polycythemia):**
  * Ectopic secretion of Erythropoietin (EPO) by tumor cells
* **Hypercalcemia:**
  * Secretion of Parathyroid Hormone-related Peptide (PTHrP) or osteolytic bone metastases
* **Watery Diarrhea:**
  * Secretion of Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide (VIP)

### Iatrogenic Treatment Adverse Effects
* **Post-Embolization Syndrome (TACE):**
  * Presents with transient fever, right upper quadrant pain, nausea, and massive spike in transaminases 24-48 hours post-procedure
  * Managed with supportive care and antipyretics
* **Bevacizumab Toxicity:**
  * Severe hypertension, proteinuria, gastrointestinal perforation, and major mucosal/variceal bleeding
* **Sorafenib/Lenvatinib Toxicity:**
  * Hand-foot skin reaction (HFSR) (palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia), profuse watery diarrhea, hypertension

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic imaging hallmark of HCC on multiphasic CT? Answer: Arterial phase hyperenhancement with portal venous or delayed phase washout.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with a normal AFP level cannot have HCC. Pearl: Up to 30% of confirmed HCC cases do not secrete AFP; diagnosis relies primarily on dynamic imaging criteria.
* Pitfall: Performing a percutaneous biopsy for a newly discovered classic 3cm liver nodule in a cirrhotic patient. Biopsy is strictly contraindicated if LI-RADS 5 criteria are met to prevent needle-track tumor seeding and severe bleeding.
* Trap: Recommending surgical resection purely based on a small, single tumor size. Pearl: You must always assess the underlying liver function and portal pressure first; severe portal hypertension (indicated by splenomegaly or PLT < 100) contraindicates resection due to the high risk of fatal post-hepatectomy liver failure.
* Viva Question: Why do we treat intermediate-stage HCC with TACE? Answer: HCC derives almost 100% of its blood supply from the hepatic artery, whereas normal liver parenchyma is supplied mainly by the portal vein. Embolizing the hepatic artery selectively kills the tumor while sparing normal liver tissue.
* Pitfall: Prescribing Bevacizumab/Atezolizumab for advanced HCC without a recent OGD (Oesophagogastroduodenoscopy). Bevacizumab (anti-VEGF) has a high risk of catastrophic bleeding; all patients must have varices screened and treated (banding) before initiation.
