# Acute Graft-versus-Host Disease (急性移植物抗宿主病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Acute GVHD
* **Acute Graft-versus-Host Disease (aGVHD)**: Systemic immune syndrome where immunocompetent donor T-cells (graft) recognize recipient human leukocyte antigens (HLA) as foreign and mount an immune attack against host tissues.
* **Context**: Occurs exclusively post-**Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (異體造血幹細胞移植)** (allo-HSCT) or donor lymphocyte infusion (DLI).

### Classification by Timing (NIH Consensus)
* **Classic aGVHD**: Occurs <100 days post-transplant (classic triad: skin, liver, gut).
* **Late-onset aGVHD**: Occurs >100 days post-transplant but exhibits classic acute inflammatory features (lacks fibrotic features of chronic GVHD).

### Local Epidemiology & Risk Factors
* **HK Epidemiology**: Highly relevant in Queen Mary Hospital (QMH), the primary adult allo-HSCT center in Hong Kong Hospital Authority (HKHA). Incidence ~30-50% in HLA-matched sibling donors; ↑ to 50-70% in mismatched/haploidentical or unrelated donors.
* **Risk Factors**: 
  * HLA disparity (degree of mismatch).
  * Peripheral blood stem cells (PBSC) > Bone marrow.
  * Female donor to male recipient (H-Y minor histocompatibility antigen).
  * Intense myeloablative conditioning (MAC) regimens.
  * Older patient/donor age.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Phase 1: Host Tissue Damage & Cytokine Storm
* Chemotherapy/radiotherapy conditioning → mucosal damage (gut/skin).
* Activation of host tissues → release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6).

### Phase 2: Donor T-Cell Activation
* Host Antigen-Presenting Cells (APCs) survive conditioning.
* Host APCs present host alloantigens to naive donor T-cells.
* Co-stimulation (CD28/B7) + cytokine milieu → Donor T-cell activation, clonal expansion, and differentiation into Th1/Th17 subsets.

### Phase 3: Cellular & Inflammatory Effector Phase
* Activated donor Cytotoxic T-Lymphocytes (CTLs) and Natural Killer (NK) cells migrate to target organs (Skin, Liver, GI tract).
* Effector mechanisms: Fas/FasL mediated apoptosis, Perforin/Granzyme release, macrophage activation.
* → Widespread target organ necrosis (dermoepidermal junction, bile ducts, intestinal crypts).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Dermatological Assessment in aGVHD
* **History**: Pruritus (often precedes rash), pain, timing (usually first organ involved, 2-3 weeks post-HSCT).
* **Physical Signs**:
  * **Maculopapular rash (斑丘疹)**: Erythematous, blanches on pressure.
  * Typical onset: Nape of neck, ears, shoulders, palms, and soles.
  * Progression: Confluence → generalized erythroderma.
  * Severe signs: Bullae formation, epidermal necrolysis/desquamation (TEN-like appearance), Nikolsky sign +ve.

### Gastrointestinal Assessment in aGVHD
* **History**: Anorexia, profound nausea/vomiting (Upper GI), severe abdominal cramping, explosive watery/green diarrhea, hematochezia (Lower GI).
* **Physical Signs**:
  * Abdominal distension, diffuse tenderness.
  * Hyperactive or absent bowel sounds (paralytic ileus in severe cases).
  * Signs of profound hypovolemia (↓ JVP, tachycardia, postural hypotension).

### Hepatic Assessment in aGVHD
* **History**: Dark urine, pale stools, pruritus.
* **Physical Signs**:
  * Icterus/Jaundice (黃疸) (often follows skin/gut involvement).
  * **Hepatomegaly (肝臟腫大)**, Right upper quadrant tenderness (stretching of Glisson's capsule).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Infectious Differential Diagnoses
* **Viral Infection**: 
  * CMV/HSV colitis → highly similar diarrhea; requires biopsy to differentiate.
  * Viral Hepatitis (HBV reactivation common in HKHA, HCV) → rule out before diagnosing hepatic GVHD.
  * HHV-6 / Parvovirus B19 → can mimic maculopapular rash.
* **Bacterial Infection**: C. difficile colitis (highly prevalent post-HSCT due to broad-spectrum antibiotics).

### Non-Infectious Differential Diagnoses
* **Sinusoidal Obstruction Syndrome (SOS) / Veno-occlusive disease (VOD)**:
  * Distinguishing features: SOS occurs earlier (<21 days), marked by rapid weight gain, ascites, painful hepatomegaly, and jaundice. aGVHD typically lacks ascites and weight gain.
* **Drug Toxicity / Reactions**: 
  * Chemo-induced mucosal barrier injury (resolves post-engraftment).
  * Antibiotic-induced rash (often spares palms/soles unlike aGVHD).
  * Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) from azole antifungals or calcineurin inhibitors.
* **Engraftment Syndrome**: Fever, rash, pulmonary edema coinciding exactly with neutrophil recovery; typically lacks severe GI/hepatic involvement.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Strict Fluid Balance**: Essential diagnostic metric for GI staging (quantify stool output in mL/day).
* **Stool Studies**: C. difficile toxin + PCR, viral multiplex PCR (Norovirus, Adenovirus, Rotavirus), O&P.

### Blood Investigations
* **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**: Cholestatic pattern pathognomonic (↑ Bilirubin, ↑ ALP, ↑ GGT). ALT/AST may be mildly ↑.
* **CMV / EBV DNA viral load (PCR)**: Rule out reactivation.
* **Trough Drug Levels**: Tacrolimus / Cyclosporine levels (ensure therapeutic baseline).

### Diagnostic Imaging
* **Abdominal Ultrasound**: Rule out biliary obstruction; Doppler to check portal flow (rule out SOS/VOD).
* **CT Abdomen/Pelvis**: 
  * Findings in GI aGVHD: Bowel wall thickening, mucosal hyperenhancement ("target sign"), fluid-filled dilated loops, mesenteric stranding.

### Histopathological Biopsies (Gold Standard)
* **Skin Biopsy**: 
  * Findings: Apoptotic keratinocytes at dermoepidermal junction, basal vacuolization, lymphocytic exocytosis.
* **Endoscopy / Sigmoidoscopy with Biopsy**: 
  * Findings: Crypt cell apoptosis (exploding crypts), crypt abscesses, mucosal ulceration. IHC strictly required to rule out CMV inclusion bodies.
* **Liver Biopsy**: 
  * Transjugular route preferred due to thrombocytopenia.
  * Findings: Small bile duct damage, endothelialitis, lymphocytic infiltration of portal tracts.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Systemic Resuscitation & GI Stabilization
* NBM (Nil by mouth) / bowel rest for severe GI aGVHD.
* Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation & electrolyte correction (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia common).
* Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN) via central line to maintain caloric intake during profound diarrhea.
* Strict reverse barrier nursing in positive-pressure isolation rooms (HEPA filtered).

### First-Line Immunosuppressive Therapy
* **High-Dose Corticosteroids**: 
  * Methylprednisolone 2 mg/kg/day IV (divided in 2 doses).
  * Optimize existing calcineurin inhibitors (CNI: Tacrolimus or Cyclosporine) to high therapeutic range.
* **Topical Adjuncts**:
  * Skin: Potent topical steroids (e.g., Betamethasone valerate 0.1%).
  * GI: Oral non-absorbable steroids (e.g., Budesonide 3mg TDS) for GI-specific disease.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Standard aGVHD Prophylaxis Regimens
* **Standard HKHA Protocol**: Calcineurin Inhibitor (Tacrolimus or Cyclosporine) + Short-course Methotrexate (MTX on Days 1, 3, 6, 11).
* **T-cell Depletion Strategies (High Risk)**:
  * Anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) (Rabbit/Horse) given during conditioning.
  * Post-Transplant Cyclophosphamide (PTCy): Given Day 3 and 4; induces in-vivo alloreactive T-cell tolerance (standard for haploidentical HSCT).

### Step-Up Therapy for Steroid-Refractory aGVHD (SR-aGVHD)
* *Definition*: Progression after 3 days, no improvement after 7 days, or incomplete response after 14 days of 2 mg/kg methylprednisolone.
* **Second-Line Agent (Current HA Standard)**:
  * **Ruxolitinib**: JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor. Blocks downstream cytokine signaling. Highly effective for GI/Skin SR-aGVHD.
* **Alternative / Third-Line Options**:
  * **Extracorporeal Photopheresis (ECP)**: Leukapheresis → UV light + methoxsalen → reinfusion (induces T-cell apoptosis without global immunosuppression).
  * **Basiliximab**: IL-2 receptor antagonist (anti-CD25).
  * **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)** or **Etanercept** (Anti-TNF).
  * **Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs)** (clinical trial basis).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Target Organ Staging (MAGIC Criteria)
| Stage | Skin (% Body Surface Area) | Liver (Bilirubin µmol/L) | Gut (Stool volume/day) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | No rash | < 34 | < 500 mL |
| **1** | Maculopapular rash < 25% | 34 - 50 | 500 - 999 mL |
| **2** | Maculopapular rash 25 - 50% | 51 - 102 | 1000 - 1500 mL |
| **3** | Maculopapular rash > 50% | 103 - 255 | > 1500 mL |
| **4** | Generalized erythroderma / Bullae | > 255 | Severe pain / Ileus |

### Overall Clinical Grading (Glucksberg Criteria)
| Grade | Skin Stage | Liver Stage | Gut Stage | Clinical Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I (Mild)** | 1-2 | 0 | 0 | Excellent |
| **II (Mod)** | 1-3 | 1 | 1 | Good |
| **III (Sev)** | 2-3 | 2-3 | 2-3 | Fair - Poor |
| **IV (Life-threatening)**| 4 | 4 | 4 | Extremely Poor |

### Prognostic Implications
* Grade I-II: Generally responds well to initial steroid therapy (~40-50% complete response rate).
* Grade III-IV: Extremely poor prognosis; mortality rate 70-90%, driven by refractory disease, profound immunosuppression, and secondary sepsis.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Iatrogenic & Drug-Induced Complications
* **Profound Opportunistic Infections**: Due to severe T-cell depletion (aGVHD itself) + heavy immunosuppression (Steroids/Ruxolitinib).
  * Fungal: Invasive Aspergillosis, Candida, Pneumocystis jirovecii (PCP).
  * Viral: CMV reactivation (colitis/pneumonitis), EBV-PTLD, Adenovirus.
* **Calcineurin Inhibitor Toxicity**: Nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity (PRES - Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome), thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA).
* **Steroid Toxicity**: Hyperglycemia, myopathy, avascular necrosis, osteoporosis.

### Structural & End-Organ Damage
* **GI**: Intestinal strictures, severe GI hemorrhage, bowel perforation.
* **Hepatic**: Progressive liver failure, coagulopathy.
* **Evolution**: 30-50% of patients surviving aGVHD will transition into **Chronic GVHD** (sclerodermatous skin, bronchiolitis obliterans, sicca syndrome).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the classic triad of organs involved in acute GVHD? Answer: Skin, Liver, and Gastrointestinal tract.
* Trap: Confusing Sinusoidal Obstruction Syndrome (SOS) with Hepatic aGVHD. Pearl: SOS presents very early (<3 weeks) with painful hepatomegaly, massive fluid retention/ascites, and weight gain, whereas aGVHD presents later with a primarily cholestatic pattern (high Alk Phos, high Bili) without massive ascites.
* Pitfall: Rushing to give high-dose steroids for suspected gut aGVHD without ruling out CMV colitis. Treating CMV colitis with high-dose steroids can lead to fatal viral dissemination; always secure a flexible sigmoidoscopy biopsy first.
* Viva Question: What is the first-line systemic therapy for established Acute GVHD? Answer: Intravenous Methylprednisolone at 2 mg/kg/day.
* Trap: Assuming a maculopapular rash post-engraftment is always a drug allergy. Pearl: Always examine the palms and soles; aGVHD characteristically involves palmar and plantar surfaces, which most drug eruptions spare.
* Viva Question: Which targeted biologic is now the standard of care for steroid-refractory acute GVHD? Answer: Ruxolitinib (JAK1/2 inhibitor).
