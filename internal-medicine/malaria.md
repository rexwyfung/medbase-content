# Malaria (瘧疾)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Malaria
* **Malaria (瘧疾)**: Acute, life-threatening vector-borne parasitic infection caused by protozoa of the genus **Plasmodium (瘧原蟲)**, transmitted via the bite of an infected female **Anopheles (按蚊)** mosquito.

### Pathogenic Plasmodium Etiologies
* **P. falciparum (惡性瘧原蟲)**: Most lethal, predominant in Sub-Saharan Africa. Responsible for almost all severe/cerebral cases.
* **P. vivax (間日瘧原蟲)**: Widely distributed (Asia, Latin America). Causes relapsing malaria.
* **P. ovale (卵形瘧原蟲)**: Primarily West Africa. Causes relapsing malaria.
* **P. malariae (三日瘧原蟲)**: Global but low prevalence. Causes chronic, low-grade infection.
* **P. knowlesi (諾氏瘧原蟲)**: Zoonotic (macaques), prevalent in Southeast Asia (e.g., Malaysia, Borneo). Fatal if untreated.

### Hong Kong Epidemiology & Public Health
* **Local Burden**: Predominantly **imported cases** (returning travelers/workers from Africa & South Asia). Local transmission is practically eliminated, though competent vectors (*Anopheles minimus*, *Anopheles jeyporiensis*) still exist in rural HK.
* **Statutory Requirement**: Statutorily notifiable infectious disease to the **Centre for Health Protection (CHP) (衞生防護中心)**.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Plasmodium Life Cycle
* **Sporozoite Phase (Inoculation)**: Mosquito injects sporozoites → migrate to liver within minutes.
* **Exoerythrocytic (Hepatic) Schizogony**: Sporozoites multiply in hepatocytes (asymptomatic, 1-2 weeks) → form schizonts → rupture to release **merozoites**.
* **Erythrocytic Schizogony (Clinical Phase)**: Merozoites invade RBCs → develop into trophozoites (ring form) → schizonts → RBC rupture → release more merozoites + pyrogens (→ triggers cyclical fever).

### Pathogenesis of P. falciparum Severity
* **Cytoadherence & Rosetting**: *P. falciparum* induces expression of **PfEMP1** (Plasmodium falciparum erythrocyte membrane protein 1) on RBC surface.
* **Microvascular Sequestration**: Infected RBCs bind to endothelial receptors (ICAM-1, CD36) in deep capillary beds (brain, kidneys) → prevents splenic clearance → microvascular occlusion → tissue ischemia & necrosis.
* **Lack of Reticulocyte Preference**: Invades RBCs of all ages → massive parasitemia (unlike *P. vivax* which prefers reticulocytes).

### The Hypnozoite Reservoir (Relapsing Malaria)
* **P. vivax & P. ovale Unique Mechanism**: A subset of sporozoites in the liver remain dormant as **hypnozoites (休眠子)**.
* **Relapse**: Reactivation occurs months to years later → late-onset clinical infection even if blood-stage parasites were eradicated.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### High-Yield Travel History & Risk Assessment
* **Travel Exposure**: Exact locations (endemicity), duration, and time since return.
* **Chemoprophylaxis**: Specific drug taken, adherence (missed doses?), continuation after return.
* **Incubation Period**: Typically 7–30 days. *Rule of thumb: Unlikely to be falciparum if >1 month post-return, but vivax/ovale can present months later.*

### Paroxysmal Fever Patterns (Classic but Unreliable)
* **Cold Stage**: Intense rigors, shivering (1-2 hours).
* **Hot Stage**: High-grade fever (up to 40-41°C), flushed, dry skin (2-6 hours).
* **Sweating Stage**: Diaphoresis, defervescence, profound exhaustion.
* **Periodicity**: 
  * **Tertian (隔日瘧)**: Fever every 48h (Days 1, 3, 5) → *P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. ovale*.
  * **Quartan (三日瘧)**: Fever every 72h (Days 1, 4, 7) → *P. malariae*.
  * **Quotidian (每日瘧)**: Daily fever → *P. knowlesi* (24h cycle) or mixed/early falciparum.

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Malaria
* **General**: High fever, rigors, diaphoresis, prostration.
* **HEENT**: Scleral icterus (hemolysis/hepatic dysfunction), conjunctival pallor (anemia).
* **Abdomen**: **Splenomegaly (脾腫大)** (splenic macrophage hypertrophy from RBC clearance), Hepatomegaly.
* **Neurological (Severe)**: Altered mental status, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) ↓, seizures, focal neurological deficits.
* **Respiratory (Severe)**: Tachypnea, bilateral crackles (pulmonary edema).
* **Skin/Renal**: Oliguria, dark/coca-cola urine (hemoglobinuria).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table for Returning Traveler with Fever
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory History & Physical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Malaria** | Cyclical fevers, splenomegaly, anemia, recent Africa/Asia travel. |
| **Dengue Fever (登革熱)** | Retro-orbital pain, severe myalgia/bone pain, biphasic fever, maculopapular rash, +ve tourniquet test. |
| **Typhoid Fever (傷寒)** | Step-ladder fever pattern, relative bradycardia, rose spots, abdominal pain/constipation. |
| **Leptospirosis (鈎端螺旋體病)**| Freshwater exposure (hiking/watersports), prominent conjunctival suffusion, calf myalgia. |
| **Chikungunya (基孔肯雅熱)** | Severe, debilitating polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis, rash. |
| **Bacterial Sepsis** | Obvious localizing source (UTI, pneumonia, cellulitis). |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs)
* **Principle**: Immunochromatographic detection of parasite antigens.
* **HRP-2 (Histidine-rich protein 2)**: Specific to *P. falciparum*.
* **pLDH (Parasite lactate dehydrogenase)** or **Aldolase**: Detects all Plasmodium species (pan-malarial).
* **Utility**: Fast (15 mins). Negative RDT does **NOT** rule out malaria; must proceed to microscopy.

### Diagnostic Blood Microscopy (Gold Standard)
* **Thick Blood Smear**: High sensitivity for **detecting** the presence of parasites. Quantifies **Parasitemia %** (infected RBCs / total RBCs).
* **Thin Blood Smear**: Preserves RBC morphology for **speciation** (identifying specific Plasmodium species).
* **Frequency**: If high clinical suspicion, perform 3 sets of smears over 48-72 hours (parasites may be sequestered and missed on a single smear).
* **Classic Morphologies**: 
  * *P. falciparum*: Multiple tiny rings per RBC, "headphone" appearance, banana-shaped gametocytes.
  * *P. vivax*: Enlarged RBCs, Schüffner's dots.
  * *P. malariae*: Band forms across RBC, "rosette" schizonts.

### Systemic Organ Dysfunction Blood Work
* **Complete Blood Count (FBC)**: Normochromic normocytic anemia (hemolysis), thrombocytopenia (common, but rarely causes severe bleeding). WBC usually normal.
* **Liver Function Tests (LFT)**: ↑ Unconjugated bilirubin (hemolysis), mild ↑ ALT/AST.
* **Renal Function (U&E)**: ↑ Urea/Creatinine (AKI from ATN/hypoperfusion).
* **Metabolic**: ↓ Glucose (parasite consumption + quinine toxicity), ↑ Lactate (tissue hypoxia).
* **Coagulation**: ↑ PT/APTT, ↓ Fibrinogen (DIC in severe cases).
* **G6PD Status**: **MANDATORY** check before initiating Primaquine.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Uncomplicated P. falciparum or P. knowlesi
* **First-line Therapy**: **Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT)**.
* **HA Formulary Standard**: Oral **Artemether-lumefantrine** (Riamet).
* **Alternative**: Oral Quinine + Doxycycline (or Clindamycin in pregnancy/children).
* **Duration**: Typically 3-day course for ACT.

### Acute Resuscitation for Severe / Cerebral Malaria
* **Escalation Trigger**: ANY feature of WHO Severe Malaria (see Sec 8). Requires ICU/HDU admission.
* **First-line Specific Therapy**: **IV Artesunate** (Requires ID consultation in HA hospitals).
* **Protocol**: Doses given at 0, 12, 24 hours, then daily until patient can tolerate oral medications.
* **Step-down Therapy**: Once clinically stable and taking orally, transition to a full 3-day course of oral ACT (e.g., Artemether-lumefantrine) to prevent recrudescence.
* **Alternative (if Artesunate unavailable)**: IV Quinine dihydrochloride (requires cardiac monitoring for arrhythmias and strict glucose monitoring).

### Acute Management of Uncomplicated P. vivax, P. ovale & P. malariae
* **Blood Stage Eradication**: Oral **Chloroquine** (if originating from chloroquine-sensitive areas) OR Oral **ACT** (Artemether-lumefantrine) if originating from chloroquine-resistant areas (e.g., Oceania, Indonesia).

### Acute Supportive Resuscitation Protocols
* **Airway/Breathing**: Early intubation if GCS ≤8 or ARDS develops.
* **Circulation**: Highly cautious IV fluid resuscitation (normal saline/Hartmann's). *P. falciparum* patients are extremely prone to fatal pulmonary edema. Use CVP/US guidance.
* **Hypoglycemia**: IV 10% or 50% Dextrose boluses.
* **Seizures**: IV Benzodiazepines (e.g., Lorazepam), followed by Levetiracetam/Phenytoin.
* **Severe Anemia**: Blood transfusion if Hb <7 g/dL.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Radical Cure for P. vivax & P. ovale (Hypnozoite Clearance)
* **Goal**: Prevent relapse by eradicating dormant liver hypnozoites.
* **Pharmacology**: Oral **Primaquine** for 14 days.
* **Prerequisite Protocol**: **G6PD assay MUST be resulted before administration**.
  * **G6PD Normal**: Full 14-day Primaquine course.
  * **G6PD Deficient**: Primaquine is contraindicated (causes massive fatal hemolysis). Consult ID; options include careful weekly dosing under strict observation or withholding.

### Malaria Chemoprophylaxis Algorithms for Travelers
* **Agent Selection**: Based on destination resistance patterns (consult WHO/CDC/CHP guidelines). Start *before* travel, continue *during*, and complete post-travel course.
| Prophylactic Agent | Timing / Dosing | Key Contraindications / Caveats |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Atovaquone-Proguanil (Malarone)** | Start 1-2 days before. Stop 7 days after. Daily dose. | Expensive. Avoid in severe renal impairment or pregnancy. |
| **Doxycycline** | Start 1-2 days before. Stop 4 weeks after. Daily dose. | Avoid in pregnancy, children <8 yrs. Causes photosensitivity, esophagitis. |
| **Mefloquine** | Start 2-3 weeks before. Stop 4 weeks after. Weekly dose. | Contraindicated in epilepsy, psychiatric history (depression/anxiety). |
| **Chloroquine** | Start 1-2 weeks before. Stop 4 weeks after. Weekly dose. | Only useful in Central America / Caribbean (massive global resistance elsewhere). |

### Lifestyle & Vector Control Modifications
* **Bite Prevention**: DEET-containing insect repellent (≥20-30%), permethrin-treated clothing/mosquito nets.
* **Environmental**: Stay in air-conditioned rooms, avoid dusk-to-dawn outdoor exposure.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### WHO Severe Malaria Diagnostic Criteria
*Presence of asexual P. falciparum parasitemia PLUS ≥1 of the following defines Severe Malaria (High Mortality Risk):*
| Category | Clinical / Laboratory Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Neurological** | Cerebral malaria (GCS <11 or multiple seizures). |
| **Respiratory** | ARDS or Pulmonary Edema (radiological or clinical). |
| **Cardiovascular**| Shock (Algid malaria), capillary leak syndrome. |
| **Renal** | Acute Kidney Injury (Creatinine >265 µmol/L or anuria). |
| **Hepatic** | Jaundice (Bilirubin >50 µmol/L) + Parasitemia >100,000/µL. |
| **Hematological** | Severe Anemia (Hb <7 g/dL) OR Bleeding/DIC. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypoglycemia (<2.2 mmol/L) OR Lactic Acidosis (Lactate >5 mmol/L). |
| **Parasitological**| Hyperparasitemia (>10% RBCs infected). |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Malaria-Induced End-Organ Complications
* **CNS**: Cerebral Malaria (腦瘧疾) → permanent neurocognitive deficits, coma, death.
* **Pulmonary**: Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema / ARDS (high mortality).
* **Renal**: **Blackwater Fever (黑水熱)** → massive intravascular hemolysis → hemoglobinuria → dark urine & acute tubular necrosis (ATN).
* **Spleen**: Splenic rupture (rare but catastrophic, especially in *P. vivax*).
* **Obstetric**: Placental malaria → maternal severe anemia, low birth weight, premature delivery, stillbirth.

### Antimalarial Drug Adverse Effects
* **Quinine toxicity (Cinchonism)**: Tinnitus, deafness, dizziness, visual disturbances, QT prolongation, profound hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia.
* **Primaquine**: Massive hemolytic crisis in G6PD deficiency (High prevalence in Southern Chinese population in HK).
* **Artesunate**: Delayed post-artemisinin hemolytic anemia (DAHA) occurring 1-3 weeks post-treatment.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How does the fever pattern of P. knowlesi differ from P. falciparum? Answer: P. knowlesi has a strict 24-hour (quotidian) replication cycle, causing daily fever spikes, whereas falciparum theoretically causes tertian (48h) fever, though in practice falciparum fever is often irregular or continuous.
* Viva Question: A returning traveler from Nigeria presents with fever. RDT is negative. Can you discharge them? Answer: No. RDTs have variable sensitivity. Gold standard is serial thick and thin blood smears over 48-72 hours to definitively rule out malaria.
* Trap: Assuming chloroquine is still a globally effective antimalarial. Pearl: *P. falciparum* is universally resistant to chloroquine in almost all parts of Africa and Asia. Always use ACT (e.g., Artemether-lumefantrine) as first-line for uncomplicated falciparum.
* Trap: Giving Primaquine immediately to a patient diagnosed with P. vivax. Pearl: You must wait for the quantitative G6PD assay results to return. In Hong Kong, G6PD deficiency is common (approx. 4.5% of males); giving Primaquine without checking will precipitate a fatal hemolytic crisis.
* Pitfall: Aggressively fluid-resuscitating a hypotensive patient with severe malaria. Malaria patients have highly permeable capillaries due to endothelial dysfunction. Over-resuscitation easily precipitates fatal non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema. Use minimal fluids and early vasopressors.
* Pitfall: Assuming a patient lacks malaria because they took chemoprophylaxis. Prophylaxis compliance is notoriously poor, and breakthrough infections (especially with widespread resistance) occur. Always investigate fever in a returning traveler.
