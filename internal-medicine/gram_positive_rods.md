# Gram-Positive Rods (革蘭氏陽性桿菌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Classification of Gram-Positive Rods (革蘭氏陽性桿菌分類)
* **Spore-Forming (產孢子):** *Bacillus* (aerobic), *Clostridium* (anaerobic).
* **Non-Spore-Forming (非產孢子):** *Listeria*, *Corynebacterium*.
* **Branching Filaments (分支絲狀):** *Nocardia* (aerobic, weakly acid-fast), *Actinomyces* (anaerobic, not acid-fast).
* **Mycobacteria (分枝桿菌):** Acid-fast bacilli (AFB) due to mycolic acid cell wall.

### Epidemiology of Gram-Positive Rods in Hong Kong (香港流行病學)
* **Diphtheria & Tetanus:** Extremely rare in HK due to universal **Hong Kong Childhood Immunisation Programme (HKCIP)** (DTaP/dTaP).
* **Listeria monocytogenes:** Endemic, primarily affecting pregnant women, neonates, and immunocompromised patients (e.g., post-transplant, malignancies).
* **Bacillus cereus:** Common cause of local food poisoning outbreaks (fried rice syndrome).
* **Nocardia & NTM (Non-Tuberculous Mycobacteria):** Increasing incidence in HA respiratory clinics, particularly in patients with pre-existing structural lung disease (e.g., Bronchiectasis).
* **Leprosy (痲瘋):** Very rare locally; mostly imported cases or historical remnants.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Bacillus Species (芽孢桿菌)
* **Bacillus anthracis:** Only bacterium with a **polypeptide capsule (poly-D-glutamate)**.
* **Anthrax Exotoxin:** A-B toxin. 
* **Protective Antigen (PA):** Mediates cell entry.
* **Edema Factor (EF):** Adenylate cyclase → ↑ cAMP → fluid secretion/edema.
* **Lethal Factor (LF):** Zinc-dependent metalloprotease → cleaves MAP kinase → tissue necrosis (black eschar).
* **Bacillus cereus:** Produces cereulide (preformed heat-stable enterotoxin) → vagus nerve stimulation → emesis within 1-5 hrs. Heat-labile toxin → ↑ cAMP → diarrhea (8-18 hrs).

### Pathophysiology of Clostridium Exotoxins (梭狀芽孢桿菌毒素)
* **C. tetani:** Produces **Tetanospasmin** → travels retrograde via axons to CNS → cleaves SNARE proteins → prevents release of inhibitory neurotransmitters (GABA & Glycine) from Renshaw cells → spastic paralysis.
* **C. botulinum:** Produces **Botulinum toxin** → absorbed in gut, enters bloodstream → cleaves SNARE proteins at peripheral presynaptic terminals → prevents acetylcholine (ACh) release → flaccid paralysis.
* **C. perfringens:** Produces **Alpha toxin (Lecithinase/Phospholipase C)** → degrades tissue cell membranes → myonecrosis and gas gangrene; causes hemolysis.
* **C. difficile:** Toxin A (enterotoxin) binds brush border → inflammation/diarrhea. Toxin B (cytotoxin) → actin cytoskeletal depolymerization → pseudomembrane formation. *(Note: Clinical/Rx siloed to Gastroenteritis notes).*

### Pathophysiology of Corynebacterium & Listeria (棒狀桿菌與李斯特菌)
* **C. diphtheriae:** **Diphtheria toxin** (phage-encoded A-B toxin) → ADP-ribosylation of Elongation Factor-2 (EF-2) → halts human protein synthesis → cell death & pseudomembrane formation.
* **L. monocytogenes:** Intracellular pathogen. **Listeriolysin O** creates pores in phagosomes → escape into cytoplasm. Uses **Actin rockets** for cell-to-cell spread, avoiding extracellular antibodies.

### Pathophysiology of Branching Gram-Positive Rods
* **Nocardia spp.:** Suppressive of cell-mediated immunity; obligate aerobe → forms abscesses in oxygen-rich tissues (Lungs, Brain). Weakly acid-fast due to intermediate mycolic acid chain length.
* **Actinomyces israelii:** Normal oral/GI flora; opportunistic infection following mucosal breach (e.g., dental extraction). Anaerobic growth → forms purulent sinus tracts with crystalline bacterial aggregates (sulfur granules).

### Pathophysiology of Mycobacteria Overview (分枝桿菌)
* **M. tuberculosis:** **Cord factor** (trehalose dimycolate) → macrophage activation, induces granulomas. **Sulfatides** inhibit phagolysosomal fusion. *(Note: Clinical/Rx siloed to TB notes).*
* **M. leprae:** Prefers cool temperatures (skin, superficial nerves).
* **Tuberculoid Leprosy:** Strong Th1 cell-mediated response (IL-2, IFN-γ) → limits disease, localized granulomas.
* **Lepromatous Leprosy:** Deficient cell-mediated response (Th2 shift: IL-4, IL-5, IL-10) → high bacterial load, systemic spread, lethal.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Clinical Signs of Bacillus Infections
* **Cutaneous Anthrax:** Painless papule → ulcer with vesicles → necrotic **Black Eschar** with massive surrounding non-pitting edema.
* **Inhalation Anthrax (Woolsorter's Disease):** Biphasic: mild flu-like prodrome → rapid onset severe dyspnea, diaphoresis, shock.

### Clinical Signs of Clostridial Infections (梭菌感染)
* **Tetanus (破傷風):** Trismus (lockjaw), **Risus sardonicus** (raised eyebrows, abnormal sustained smile), **Opisthotonos** (severe back arching spasm).
* **Botulism (肉毒桿菌中毒):** Symmetrical descending flaccid paralysis, ptosis, diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, absent pupillary light reflex.
* **Gas Gangrene (氣性壞疽) (C. perfringens):** Severe pain out of proportion to injury, crepitus on palpation, foul-smelling serosanguineous discharge (dishwater pus), bronze skin discoloration.

### Clinical Signs of Diphtheria (白喉)
* **Oropharyngeal Signs:** Thick, tightly adherent grayish-white **Pseudomembrane** over tonsils and pharynx (bleeds if scraped).
* **Cervical Signs:** Massive cervical lymphadenopathy → **"Bull neck" (牛頸)** appearance.
* **Cardiac Signs:** Arrhythmias, signs of acute heart failure (myocarditis).

### Clinical Signs of Listeriosis (李斯特菌症)
* **Maternal/Neonatal History:** Consumption of unpasteurized milk, soft cheeses, cold deli meats.
* **Granulomatosis Infantiseptica:** Disseminated microabscesses/granulomas in utero (often stillborn).
* **Adult Meningitis:** Nuchal rigidity, photophobia, altered mental status (especially in age >65 or immunocompromised).

### Clinical Signs of Branching Gram-Positive Rods & Leprosy
* **Actinomycosis (放線菌病):** Indurated, "lumpy jaw" swelling (usually at angle of mandible); draining sinus tracts extruding yellow "sulfur granules".
* **Nocardiosis:** Cutaneous nodules, chronic cough, focal neurological deficits (brain abscess).
* **Leprosy (Hansen's Disease):**
* **Tuberculoid:** Hypopigmented, well-demarcated hairless skin plaques with **absent sensation**. Thickened peripheral nerves (e.g., ulnar).
* **Lepromatous:** Symmetrical nodular skin lesions, **Leonine facies** (loss of eyebrows, collapsed nasal bridge, nodular earlobes), widespread glove-and-stocking neuropathy.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Laboratory Investigations & Microscopy (實驗室檢查)
* **Gram Stain Morphology:**
* *B. anthracis*: Large box-car shaped Gram-positive rods in chains.
* *C. perfringens*: Large Gram-positive rods, spores rarely seen.
* *C. diphtheriae*: Gram-positive pleomorphic club-shaped rods in V/Y arrangements (Chinese letters).
* *L. monocytogenes*: Small Gram-positive rods (can mimic pneumococcus).
* **Special Stains:**
* **Modified Acid-Fast Stain (Fite/Kinyoun):** Detects *Nocardia*.
* **Methylene Blue / Albert's Stain:** Highlights metachromatic granules (volutin) in *C. diphtheriae*.
* **Ziehl-Neelsen Stain (AFB):** For Mycobacteria (detects mycolic acid).

### Culture & Toxin Assays
* **Corynebacterium diphtheriae:**
* **Culture:** Tellurite agar (black colonies) or Loeffler medium.
* **Toxigenicity Test:** **Elek test** (in vitro immunoprecipitation to detect diphtheria toxin).
* **Listeria monocytogenes:**
* **Culture:** Cold enrichment (grows at 4°C).
* **Motility Assay:** Tumbling motility at room temp (25°C); reduced motility at 37°C.
* **Bacillus & Clostridium:**
* *B. anthracis*: Medusa head appearance on blood agar, non-hemolytic.
* *C. perfringens*: Double zone of hemolysis on blood agar. Nagler reaction +ve (alpha toxin breaks down egg yolk agar lecithin).

### Imaging & Invasive Procedures
* **Inhalation Anthrax:** CXR shows **widened mediastinum** (hemorrhagic lymphadenitis), pleural effusions; usually WITHOUT lung infiltrates.
* **Nocardia:** CT Brain for multi-loculated brain abscesses (ring-enhancing lesions).
* **Actinomyces:** Ultrasound/CT neck demonstrating contiguous soft tissue masses crossing fascial planes.
* **Leprosy Biopsy:**
* *Tuberculoid*: Skin biopsy shows non-caseating granulomas, sparse AFB.
* *Lepromatous*: Skin biopsy shows lipid-laden macrophages (foam/Virchow cells) packed with massive numbers of AFB. **Lepromin skin test -ve** (due to anergy).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation in Toxin-Mediated Emergencies
* **Tetanus Acute Rx:**
* **Airway:** Early intubation/tracheostomy if trismus or laryngeal spasm threatens airway.
* **Toxin Neutralization:** Human Tetanus Immune Globulin (HTIG) immediately (neutralizes unbound toxin only).
* **Muscle Spasm Control:** IV Diazepam/Midazolam infusions; Neuromuscular blockade (Vecuronium) if refractory.
* **Source Control:** Surgical debridement of wound + IV Metronidazole (or Penicillin G).
* **Botulism Acute Rx:**
* **Airway:** Monitor Vital Capacity; intubate for respiratory failure.
* **Toxin Neutralization (Adults):** Equine heptavalent botulinum antitoxin (HBAT).
* **Toxin Neutralization (Infants):** Human Botulinum Immune Globulin (BabyBIG).
* **Diphtheria Acute Rx:**
* **Toxin Neutralization:** Diphtheria antitoxin (DAT) MUST be given immediately (do not wait for lab confirmation).
* **Antibiotics:** IV Penicillin G or Erythromycin to halt toxin production.
* **Airway:** Direct visualization and removal of pseudomembrane if causing acute obstruction.

### Acute Antibiotic Management for Systemic Gram-Positive Infections
* **Listeria Meningitis (HA Guidelines):** Add **IV Ampicillin** to standard empirical meningitis regimen (Ceftriaxone + Vancomycin) for neonates, age >50, or immunocompromised. *(Note: Listeria is intrinsically resistant to Cephalosporins).*
* **Inhalation Anthrax:** IV Ciprofloxacin + Meropenem + Linezolid + Anthrax Antitoxin (Raxibacumab or Obiltoxaximab).
* **Gas Gangrene (*C. perfringens*):** Radical emergent surgical debridement/amputation + IV Penicillin G + IV Clindamycin (inhibits bacterial ribosome, halting toxin synthesis).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Antimicrobial Therapy for Branching Gram-Positive Rods
* **Actinomycosis (放線菌病):**
* **First-line:** High-dose IV Penicillin G for 2-6 weeks, followed by Oral Amoxicillin.
* **Duration:** Prolonged therapy required (6-12 months) to prevent relapse.
* **Nocardiosis (諾卡菌病):**
* **First-line:** Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX). Add Imipenem/Amikacin for severe/CNS disease.
* **Duration:** 6-12 months.

### Leprosy (Hansen's Disease) Multi-Drug Therapy (MDT)
* **Tuberculoid (Paucibacillary):** Oral Dapsone + Rifampin for 6 months.
* **Lepromatous (Multibacillary):** Oral Dapsone + Rifampin + Clofazimine for 1-2 years.

### Hong Kong Vaccination & Prophylaxis Protocols (HKCIP)
* **DTaP Vaccine (Diphtheria, Tetanus, acellular Pertussis):**
* Routine schedule: 2, 4, 6 months, 18 months, Primary 1 (DTaP-IPV), Primary 6 (dTap-IPV).
* **Tetanus Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (HK HA A&E Guidelines):**
* **Clean minor wound:** Give vaccine (Td) ONLY if <3 doses in lifetime or >10 years since last dose. No HTIG needed.
* **All other wounds (Dirty/Puncture):** Give vaccine (Td) if <3 doses or >5 years since last dose. Add **HTIG** if <3 doses in lifetime or uncertain history.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Spectrum of Leprosy (Ridley-Jopling Classification)
| Polar Extreme (Mild) | Borderline Spectrum | Polar Extreme (Severe) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tuberculoid (TT)** | Borderline Tuberculoid (BT) | **Lepromatous (LL)** |
| High Cell-Mediated Immunity | Mid-borderline (BB) | Low Cell-Mediated Immunity |
| Few lesions, localized | Borderline Lepromatous (BL) | Widespread lesions, systemic |
| Good prognosis | Variable stability | Poor prognosis, high infectivity |

### Prognostic Indicators in Gram-Positive Infections
* **Inhalation Anthrax:** Nearly 100% fatal without early antibiotics and antitoxin. Widened mediastinum indicates late stage.
* **Diphtheria:** Worst prognosis associated with bull neck, myocarditis (AV blocks), and extreme youth/old age.
* **Listeria in Pregnancy:** Typically presents as a mild self-limiting febrile illness in the mother, but carries up to a 20-30% rate of fetal demise or neonatal sepsis.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Specific Toxins
* **Diphtheria Toxemia:**
* **Cardiology:** Severe toxic myocarditis leading to cardiomegaly, heart failure, complete heart block, and ventricular arrhythmias (weeks 1-2).
* **Neurology:** Toxic demyelinating peripheral neuropathy (pharyngeal/palatal paralysis, oculomotor palsy) (weeks 2-6).
* **Anthrax Complications:** Hemorrhagic mediastinitis, hemorrhagic meningitis.
* **Tetanus Complications:** Autonomic instability (extreme swings in BP/HR, profuse sweating), rhabdomyolysis, vertebral compression fractures (from severe spasms), aspiration pneumonia.

### Adverse Effects of Specific Antimycobacterials & Antibiotics
* **Dapsone:** Hemolysis (check G6PD status before prescribing in HK, as G6PD deficiency is prevalent locally), Methemoglobinemia, Agranulocytosis.
* **Clofazimine:** Reversible reddish-brown skin discoloration.
* **Equine Antitoxins (Botulinum/Diphtheria):** High risk of Serum Sickness (Type III hypersensitivity) and Anaphylaxis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the only Gram-positive rod that produces an endotoxin-like molecule? Answer: Listeria monocytogenes, which possesses lipoteichoic acids that act similarly to lipopolysaccharide (LPS).
* Trap: Prescribing a 3rd generation cephalosporin (Ceftriaxone) alone for empirical bacterial meningitis in an elderly patient. Pearl: Cephalosporins are intrinsically inactive against Listeria due to structural differences in penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). Always add IV Ampicillin.
* Pitfall: Waiting for laboratory confirmation (culture or Elek test) before administering Diphtheria antitoxin. The antitoxin must be given immediately on clinical suspicion, as it only neutralizes unbound toxin in the bloodstream. Once the toxin is internalized by host cells, antitoxin is useless.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a Gram stain of Nocardia from Actinomyces? Answer: Both are Gram-positive branching rods, but Nocardia is weakly acid-fast positive while Actinomyces is acid-fast negative.
* Trap: Assuming infant botulism is caused by ingesting preformed toxin (like in adults eating improperly canned foods). Pearl: Infant botulism is caused by ingesting botulinum spores (e.g., from raw honey), which then colonize the immature GI tract and produce toxin in vivo.
* Pitfall: Debriding an anthrax black eschar. Do not surgically debride cutaneous anthrax, as manipulating the lesion can precipitate systemic dissemination of the bacteria and lethal toxins.
