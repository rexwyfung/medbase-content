# Osteoarthritis (骨關節炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Osteoarthritis
* **Osteoarthritis (骨關節炎) (OA):** A slowly progressive, degenerative disease of synovial joints characterized by progressive loss of articular cartilage (軟骨), subchondral bone remodeling, osteophyte (骨刺) formation, and low-grade synovial inflammation.
* **Paradigm Shift:** No longer considered purely "wear and tear"; it is an active, metabolically driven joint failure involving all joint tissues.

### Etiologies and Risk Factors for Osteoarthritis
* **Primary OA:** Idiopathic, aging-related.
* **Secondary OA:** Due to predisposing joint abnormalities.
* **Systemic Risk Factors:**
  * **Age:** Strongest predictor.
  * **Gender:** Female > Male (especially post-menopausal hand/knee OA).
  * **Genetics:** High heritability in nodal hand OA.
* **Biomechanical Risk Factors:**
  * **Obesity (肥胖):** ↑ mechanical load (knees/hips) & ↑ systemic adipokines (pro-inflammatory).
  * **Trauma/Injury:** Previous meniscal/ligamentous tears (e.g., ACL rupture).
  * **Occupational/Repetitive Stress:** Heavy lifting, prolonged kneeling.
  * **Joint Malalignment:** Varus/valgus deformities, developmental dysplasia of the hip.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* Most common joint disease in HK.
* Knee OA is highly prevalent among local elderly Chinese women.
* Contributes heavily to Hospital Authority (HA) Orthopaedics waiting lists for arthroplasty and physiotherapy services.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Cartilage Degradation
* **Chondrocyte Stress:** Abnormal mechanical loading or aging triggers chondrocytes to shift from matrix synthesis to degradation.
* **Enzymatic Destruction:** ↑ Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and ADAMTS enzymes degrade type II collagen and aggrecan.
* **Inflammatory Cytokines:** Local production of IL-1β and TNF-α amplifies catabolic pathways.
* **Result:** Cartilage fibrillation, fissuring, and eventual denudation down to bare bone.

### Bone and Synovial Changes in Osteoarthritis
* **Subchondral Bone:** Initial microfractures → ↑ bone turnover → subchondral sclerosis (eburnation).
* **Osteophyte Formation:** Endochondral ossification at joint margins (attempts to stabilize the hypermobile, degenerating joint).
* **Synovium:** Cartilage breakdown products induce mild, secondary synovitis (synovial hypertrophy).
* **Capsule/Ligaments:** Laxity and fibrotic thickening → joint instability and reduced range of motion (ROM).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History & Symptoms of Osteoarthritis
* **Pain Characteristics:**
  * **Mechanical Pain:** Insidious onset, deep aching. ↑ with weight-bearing/activity, ↓ with rest.
  * **Late-stage Pain:** Pain at rest or night pain (interferes with sleep).
* **Stiffness:**
  * **Morning Stiffness:** Brief, strictly **< 30 minutes** (differentiates from inflammatory arthritis).
  * **Gelling Phenomenon:** Transient stiffness after periods of inactivity, resolving rapidly with movement.
* **Functional Impairment:** ↓ walking distance, difficulty climbing stairs, inability to perform fine motor tasks (hand OA).

### Typical Joint Distribution in Osteoarthritis
* **Hands:**
  * Distal Interphalangeal (DIP) joints.
  * Proximal Interphalangeal (PIP) joints.
  * First Carpometacarpal (1st CMC) joint (base of thumb).
  * **Spared:** Metacarpophalangeal (MCP) and wrists (unless secondary to trauma/calcium pyrophosphate disease).
* **Lower Limbs (Weight-bearing):** Knees (medial compartment > lateral), Hips.
* **Spine:** Cervical and Lumbar facet joints.

### Physical Examination Signs in Osteoarthritis
* **Inspection:**
  * Bony enlargement (due to osteophytes).
  * **Heberden's Nodes (希伯登氏結節):** Bony swellings at DIP joints.
  * **Bouchard's Nodes (布夏氏結節):** Bony swellings at PIP joints.
  * Squaring of the thumb base (1st CMC OA).
  * Deformity: Genu varum (bow-legged) > Genu valgum (knock-kneed) in knee OA.
  * Muscle wasting (e.g., quadriceps atrophy due to disuse).
* **Palpation:**
  * Joint line tenderness.
  * Cool joint effusion (mild, non-inflammatory; distinct from hot rheumatoid/gout joints).
* **Movement:**
  * **Crepitus (關節摩擦音):** Palpable/audible crunching on movement.
  * Restricted active and passive ROM.
  * Pain at extremes of motion.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Osteoarthritis from Inflammatory Arthritides
| Feature | Osteoarthritis (OA) | Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) | Crystal Arthropathy (Gout/CPPD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathology** | Degenerative, mechanical | Autoimmune, inflammatory | Microcrystalline, inflammatory |
| **Morning Stiffness** | **< 30 mins** | **> 1 hour** | Variable (often acute severe onset) |
| **Joint Pattern** | DIP, PIP, 1st CMC, Knees, Hips (Asymmetrical) | MCP, PIP, Wrists, small joints of feet (Symmetrical) | 1st MTP (Gout), Knees/Wrists (CPPD) |
| **Joint Exam** | Bony hard, cool, crepitus | Boggy, warm, soft tissue swelling | Extremely tender, red, hot, swollen |
| **Systemic Symptoms** | Absent | Fatigue, weight loss, nodules | Fever (in severe acute attacks) |
| **Inflammatory Markers** | Normal ESR/CRP | ↑↑ ESR/CRP | ↑ ESR/CRP during flares |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Blood Investigations for Osteoarthritis
* **Principle:** Bloods are fundamentally normal in primary OA. Used purely to exclude differentials.
* **Markers:** Normal Complete Blood Count (CBC), ESR, and CRP.
* **Serology:** Rheumatoid Factor (RF) -ve, Anti-CCP -ve, ANA -ve.

### Radiological Findings in Osteoarthritis (Plain X-ray)
* Diagnosis is primarily clinical, but X-rays confirm disease and stage severity. Use weight-bearing views for lower limbs.
* **Mnemonic (LOSS):**
  * **L**oss of joint space (Assymetric/non-uniform; e.g., medial compartment of knee).
  * **O**steophytes (Marginal bone spurs).
  * **S**ubchondral sclerosis (Dense white bone underneath cartilage).
  * **S**ubchondral cysts (Geodes; fluid-filled sacs in bone).

### Synovial Fluid Analysis for Osteoarthritis
* Indicated only if an effusion is present and infection/crystal arthropathy must be ruled out.
* **Findings (Non-inflammatory pattern):**
  * Clear, highly viscous (string sign +ve).
  * WBC count: **< 2,000 cells/mm³** (mostly mononuclear cells).
  * Gram stain & Culture: -ve.
  * Crystals: -ve (though CPPD crystals can coexist in elderly).

### Clinical Diagnostic Criteria for Knee Osteoarthritis (ACR Criteria)
* Knee pain + Osteophytes on X-ray + At least 1 of the following:
  * Age > 50 years.
  * Morning stiffness < 30 minutes.
  * Crepitus on motion.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Management of Acute Osteoarthritis Flares
* **Clinical Context:** Sudden ↑ in pain/swelling, often post-exertional or due to coexisting crystal shedding. Always tap acute hot swollen joints to rule out septic arthritis.
* **Physical Measures:**
  * Rest the joint temporarily.
  * Local thermal modalities (Ice packs for acute swelling, heat for muscle spasm).
* **Pharmacological Rescue:**
  * **Short-course Oral NSAIDs / COX-2 inhibitors:** e.g., Celecoxib (西樂葆) 200mg daily.
  * **Intra-articular (IA) Corticosteroid Injection:** (e.g., Triamcinolone 40mg + Lignocaine). Highly effective for acute inflammatory flares with effusion. Max 3-4 times per joint/year to prevent accelerated cartilage atrophy.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Non-Pharmacological & Lifestyle Management (Core Therapy)
* **Weight Reduction:** Dietician referral. Loss of 1kg ↓ knee load by 4kg per step.
* **Physiotherapy (PT):** Muscle strengthening (quadriceps/hamstrings stabilize the knee), aerobic exercise, hydrotherapy.
* **Occupational Therapy (OT) & Assistive Devices:**
  * Walking stick (hold in contralateral hand).
  * Shock-absorbing footwear, insoles.
  * Joint braces / unloader valgus braces for medial knee OA.
  * Hand splints (thumb spica for 1st CMC OA).

### Pharmacological Step-Up Algorithm for Osteoarthritis
* *Note: No Disease-Modifying Osteoarthritis Drugs (DMOADs) currently exist. Rx is purely symptomatic.*
* **Step 1: Localised Analgesia**
  * **Topical NSAIDs:** (e.g., Diclofenac gel) First-line for superficial joints (hands/knees). Avoids systemic toxicity.
  * **Topical Capsaicin:** (Substance P depleter) Alternative adjunct.
* **Step 2: Simple Systemic Analgesics**
  * **Oral Paracetamol (撲熱息痛):** HA baseline therapy, though recent evidence shows minimal efficacy compared to placebo for OA. Max 4g/day.
* **Step 3: Oral NSAIDs / COX-2 Inhibitors (HA Standard Escalation)**
  * Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration.
  * **Non-selective NSAIDs** (e.g., Naproxen, Ibuprofen) + Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) for GI protection.
  * **COX-2 Inhibitors** (e.g., Celecoxib, Etoricoxib). Preferred in HA for ↓ GI risk, but avoid in severe ischaemic heart disease (IHD).
* **Step 4: Intra-articular Options (Chronic)**
  * **IA Hyaluronic Acid (Viscosupplementation):** Controversial efficacy, user-pays in HA. May provide modest relief for 3-6 months.
* **Step 5: Refractory Pain (Bridging to Surgery)**
  * **Weak Opioids:** (e.g., Tramadol). Use with caution in elderly (delirium, falls, constipation).
  * *Note: Glucosamine and Chondroitin are NOT recommended by major guidelines due to lack of evidence, though widely used over-the-counter.*

### Surgical Referral Criteria for Osteoarthritis
* Refer to Orthopaedics for joint-preserving (osteotomy) or replacing procedures (Total Knee/Hip Arthroplasty - TKA/THA).
* **Indications:**
  * Intractable pain unresponsive to optimal conservative/medical therapy.
  * Severe functional limitation impacting activities of daily living (ADLs) / quality of life.
  * Night pain preventing sleep.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Radiological Grading for Osteoarthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence System)
| Grade | Description | Radiological Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | Normal | No features of OA. |
| **1** | Doubtful | Minute osteophytes, doubtful joint space narrowing. |
| **2** | Minimal | Definite osteophytes, normal joint space. |
| **3** | Moderate | Moderate joint space narrowing, multiple osteophytes, some sclerosis. |
| **4** | Severe | Severe joint space narrowing, large osteophytes, severe sclerosis, deformity. |

### Clinical Scoring Systems for Osteoarthritis
* **WOMAC (Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index):** Validated questionnaire assessing Pain, Stiffness, and Physical Function in hip and knee OA. Used to track disease progression and post-operative recovery.

### Prognosis
* Progressive, irreversible anatomical disease.
* Rate of progression is highly variable.
* 10-20% of symptomatic knee OA patients will eventually require arthroplasty.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications of Osteoarthritis
* **Musculoskeletal:** Progressive joint deformity, contractures, profound muscle atrophy.
* **Neurological:** Spinal stenosis or radiculopathy secondary to cervical/lumbar facet OA and osteophytes.
* **Psychosocial:** Chronic pain syndrome, clinical depression, social isolation, severe sleep deprivation.
* **Geriatric Syndromes:** ↑ risk of falls and subsequent fractures due to instability.

### Iatrogenic Complications of Osteoarthritis Therapy (High Yield)
* **NSAID Toxicity (Most critical IM complication):**
  * **Gastrointestinal:** Peptic ulcer disease (PUD), GI bleeding/perforation (must co-prescribe PPI).
  * **Renal:** Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) via afferent arteriole constriction, chronic interstitial nephritis.
  * **Cardiovascular:** Fluid retention, exacerbation of Heart Failure (HF), ↑ risk of myocardial infarction/stroke (especially COX-2 inhibitors).
* **Opioid Toxicity (Tramadol/Codeine):** Delirium, constipation, urinary retention in the elderly.
* **IA Steroid Toxicity:** Local cartilage atrophy (if >3-4 inj/year), tendon rupture, risk of iatrogenic septic arthritis (rare but devastating 1:10,000).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate Heberden's from Bouchard's nodes clinically and anatomically? Answer: Heberden's nodes are osteophytes at the DIP joints. Bouchard's nodes are osteophytes at the PIP joints. Both are signs of hand OA.
* Trap: Treating the X-ray instead of the patient. Pearl: Radiological severity (Kellgren-Lawrence grade) correlates poorly with clinical pain. Many patients with severe X-ray OA are asymptomatic. Do not refer for surgery based on imaging alone; referral requires refractory pain and functional decline.
* Pitfall: Prescribing oral NSAIDs to an elderly patient with OA without assessing cardiovascular and renal status. Explanation: NSAIDs can precipitate acute heart failure, AKI, and GI bleeds. Always check renal function (eGFR), minimize duration, use topical preparations first, and co-prescribe a PPI if oral NSAIDs are mandatory.
* Trap: Assuming an acutely painful, warm, swollen knee in an OA patient is just an "OA flare". Pearl: Degenerative joints are highly susceptible to secondary crystal deposition (CPPD) and septic arthritis. Any sudden-onset large inflammatory effusion in a previously cool OA joint mandates diagnostic aspiration (arthrocentesis) before empirical steroid injection.
* Viva Question: Which joints are classically spared in primary Osteoarthritis? Answer: The metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints, wrists, elbows, and ankles are typically spared unless there is a history of trauma, occupation stress, or an underlying metabolic condition (like hemochromatosis or CPPD).
