# Sexually Transmitted Infections (性傳播感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Sexually Transmitted Infections
* **Definition**: Group of clinical syndromes caused by pathogens acquired and transmitted primarily through sexual contact (vaginal, anal, oral).
* **Core Syndromes**: Urethritis, cervicitis, genital ulcers, pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), and systemic dissemination.

### Major STI Pathogens & Etiologies
* **Bacterial**: 
  * *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* → **Gonorrhoea (淋病)**
  * *Chlamydia trachomatis* (Serovars D-K) → **Chlamydia (衣原體)**
  * *Treponema pallidum* → **Syphilis (梅毒)**
* **Viral**:
  * Human Papillomavirus (HPV) (Types 6, 11) → **Genital Warts (生殖器疣)**
  * Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV-1, HSV-2) → **Genital Herpes (生殖器皰疹)**
  * Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) → AIDS
* **Protozoal**:
  * *Trichomonas vaginalis* → **Trichomoniasis (滴蟲病)**

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Most Prevalent Bacterial STI**: Chlamydia (highest incidence among youth <25 years).
* **Syphilis Trends**: ↑ incidence among Men who have Sex with Men (MSM) in HK; frequent co-infection with HIV.
* **Gonococcal Resistance**: ↑ prevalence of fluoroquinolone-resistant and azithromycin-resistant strains in HK; cephalosporin resistance remains an emerging threat.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Gonorrhoea & Chlamydia
* **Target Tissue**: Columnar and transitional epithelium (urethra, cervix, rectum, pharynx, conjunctiva).
* **Gonococcal Mechanism**: Pili attach to mucosal cells → Opa proteins facilitate endocytosis → intracellular replication → release of endotoxin (LOS) → intense neutrophil-rich mucosal inflammation (purulent discharge).
* **Chlamydial Mechanism**: Obligate intracellular lifecycle → Elementary body (infectious) enters cell → transforms to Reticulate body (replicative) → induces localized cytokine release (often subclinical/asymptomatic inflammation).
* **Ascending Infection**: Cervical infection breaches mucus plug → ascends to endometrium/fallopian tubes → ciliary destruction & tubal scarring (PID/infertility).

### Pathogenesis of Syphilis
* **Invasion**: *T. pallidum* penetrates intact mucous membranes or microscopic dermal abrasions.
* **Dissemination**: Enters lymphatics and bloodstream within hours of exposure.
* **Tissue Injury Mechanism**: Pathogen lacks intrinsic endotoxins; damage is immune-mediated. 
* **Vascular Pathology**: Obliterative endarteritis (perivascular plasma cell/lymphocyte infiltrates) → tissue ischemia → painless ulcers (chancre) and late gummatous necrosis.

### Pathogenesis of Viral STIs (HSV & HPV)
* **HSV**: Mucosal entry → replication in basal epidermis → retrograde axonal transport to sensory ganglia (Sacral ganglia S2-S4) → lifelong latency → reactivation (anterograde transport) during stress/immunosuppression.
* **HPV**: Infects basal keratinocytes through micro-wounds → episomal viral DNA replication (low-risk types 6/11) → hyperkeratosis and koilocytosis → exophytic wart formation (condylomata acuminata).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Comprehensive STI History Protocol
* **The "5 Ps" Approach**:
  * **Partners**: Number (past 3-6 months), genders, concurrent partners, new partners.
  * **Practices**: Types of sex (vaginal, anal, oral), receptive vs. insertive.
  * **Protection**: Condom use frequency (always, sometimes, never).
  * **Past History**: Prior STIs, previous treatments, HIV status.
  * **Pregnancy**: Contraceptive use, Last Menstrual Period (LMP), pregnancy intention.

### Physical Signs of Gonorrhoea & Chlamydia
* **Males (Urethritis/Epididymitis)**:
  * Copious, thick, purulent urethral discharge (Gonorrhoea).
  * Scanty, clear/mucoid urethral discharge (Chlamydia).
  * Tender, swollen, unilateral epididymis; positive Prehn's sign.
* **Females (Cervicitis)**:
  * Often asymptomatic.
  * Mucopurulent discharge from cervical os.
  * Contact bleeding (friable cervix on speculum exam).

### Physical Signs of Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (盆腔炎)
* **Abdominal**: Lower abdominal tenderness, guarding.
* **Bimanual Exam**: Cervical excitation (chandelier sign), adnexal tenderness, uterine tenderness, palpable adnexal mass (tubo-ovarian abscess).

### Physical Signs of Syphilis Staging
* **Primary Syphilis (3-4 weeks post-exposure)**:
  * **Chancre**: Single, indurated, **painless** ulcer with clean base and raised margins.
  * Painless, rubbery regional lymphadenopathy.
* **Secondary Syphilis (4-10 weeks post-chancre)**:
  * Generalized, non-pruritic maculopapular rash (involves **palms and soles**).
  * Condylomata lata: Flat, moist, highly infectious lesions in intertriginous areas.
  * Patchy alopecia ("moth-eaten" appearance).
  * Snail-track oral ulcers.
* **Tertiary Syphilis (Years later)**:
  * Gummas: Destructive, painless granulomatous skin/bone lesions.
  * Cardiovascular: Aortic regurgitation murmur, pulsating neck veins (aortitis/aneurysm).
  * Neurosyphilis: Argyll Robertson pupils (accommodate but do not react to light), Romberg positive, sensory ataxia, areflexia (Tabes Dorsalis).

### Physical Signs of Genital Herpes & Warts
* **HSV**: Clustered, exquisitely **painful** vesicles on an erythematous base → rupture into shallow, tender ulcers. Bilateral tender inguinal lymphadenopathy.
* **HPV**: Flesh-colored, exophytic, cauliflower-like papules (Condylomata acuminata) on vulva, penis, perianal region. Non-tender.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnosis of Genital Ulcers
| Condition | Pain | Ulcer Characteristics | Lymphadenopathy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Syphilis (Primary)** | **Painless** | Single, indurated, clean base, raised edges | Painless, firm |
| **Genital Herpes (HSV)** | **Painful** | Multiple shallow ulcers/vesicles, erythematous base | Painful, tender |
| **Chancroid (*H. ducreyi*)** | **Painful** | Multiple, deep, purulent base, ragged edges | Tender, fluctuant (buboes) |
| **Lymphogranuloma Venereum (LGV)** | Painless (initial) | Small, transient, completely heals rapidly | **Tender**, unilateral, "groove sign" |
| **Donovanosis (*K. granulomatis*)** | Painless | Slowly expanding, beefy red, highly vascular | None (pseudobuboes) |

### Differential Diagnosis of Vaginal Discharge
| Condition | Pathogen | Discharge Quality | Associated Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bacterial Vaginosis (BV)** | *Gardnerella* (Dysbiosis) | Thin, homogenous, grey/white | Fishy odor, no inflammation |
| **Candidiasis** | *Candida albicans* | Thick, white, "cottage cheese" | Pruritus, erythema |
| **Trichomoniasis** | *T. vaginalis* | Frothy, yellow-green, copious | "Strawberry cervix", dysuria |
| **Cervicitis (STI)** | Gonorrhoea/Chlamydia | Mucopurulent | Contact bleeding, deep dyspareunia |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Universal STI Screening Protocol (HK HA Clinics)
* **Males**: First-catch urine (Not mid-stream) for Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT).
* **Females**: Clinician-collected endocervical swab OR self-collected vaginal swab for NAAT.
* **Extragenital**: Pharyngeal and rectal swabs for NAAT (indicated by sexual history/MSM).
* **Universal Bloods**: Syphilis serology (VDRL/RPR + TPPA) and HIV antigen/antibody test for ALL suspected STI patients.

### Specific Investigations for Gonorrhoea & Chlamydia
* **NAAT (PCR)**: Gold standard. Highly sensitive/specific for CT and NG.
* **Culture (Gonorrhoea ONLY)**: Indicated before treatment in all positive NAAT cases to perform **Antibiotic Susceptibility Testing (AST)**. Requires transport in Amies medium with charcoal or direct plating (Thayer-Martin agar).

### Specific Investigations for Trichomonas & Herpes
* **Trichomonas**: High vaginal swab (HVS) for wet mount microscopy (motile flagellates) OR NAAT (preferred, higher sensitivity).
* **HSV**: Swab of ulcer base/vesicle fluid for HSV-1/HSV-2 NAAT (viral culture obsolete).

### Syphilis Serology Interpretation Table
| Non-Treponemal (VDRL/RPR) | Treponemal (TPPA/FTA-ABS) | Clinical Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Negative (-)** | **Negative (-)** | No infection OR incubating (retest at 2-4 weeks) |
| **Positive (+)** | **Positive (+)** | Active Syphilis (Primary, Secondary, Latent) OR recent successful treatment |
| **Negative (-)** | **Positive (+)** | Successfully treated past infection OR Very early primary OR Late latent |
| **Positive (+)** | **Negative (-)** | **Biological False Positive** (e.g., SLE, APLS, pregnancy, acute viral illness, aging) |

### Diagnostic Criteria for PID
* **Empiric Diagnosis**: Initiated if clinically suspected in sexually active young women with pelvic/lower abdominal pain AND ≥1 of the following:
  * Cervical motion tenderness
  * Uterine tenderness
  * Adnexal tenderness
* **Supportive Criteria**: Fever >38.3°C, abnormal cervical mucopurulent discharge, elevated CRP/ESR, positive lab documentation of cervical CT or GC.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Pharmacological Protocol for Gonorrhoea & Chlamydia
* **Uncomplicated Gonorrhoea (HK HA Protocol)**:
  * **Ceftriaxone** 500mg - 1g IM single dose.
  * (Always co-treat for Chlamydia unless ruled out via NAAT).
* **Uncomplicated Chlamydia**:
  * **Doxycycline** 100mg PO BD for 7 days.
  * Alternative (Pregnancy/Allergy): **Azithromycin** 1g PO single dose.

### Acute Syphilis Management Protocol
* **Early Syphilis (Primary, Secondary, Early Latent <1 yr)**:
  * **Benzathine Penicillin G** 2.4 million units IM single dose.
  * Penicillin-allergic: Doxycycline 100mg PO BD for 14 days.
* **Late Latent (>1 yr) or Unknown Duration**:
  * **Benzathine Penicillin G** 2.4 million units IM weekly for 3 doses (Total 7.2 MU).
* **Neurosyphilis/Ocular Syphilis**:
  * **Aqueous Crystalline Penicillin G** 18-24 million units IV daily (administered as 3-4 MU IV Q4H) for 10-14 days.

### Acute Management of Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)
* **Outpatient Regimen (Mild/Moderate)**:
  * **Ceftriaxone** 500mg IM single dose PLUS
  * **Doxycycline** 100mg PO BD for 14 days PLUS
  * **Metronidazole** 400mg PO BD for 14 days (to cover anaerobes).
* **Inpatient Regimen (Severe, Tubo-ovarian abscess, Pregnant)**:
  * **IV Ceftriaxone** 2g daily + **IV Doxycycline** 100mg BD + **IV Metronidazole** 500mg Q8H.
  * Switch to PO when clinically improved for 24-48 hours; complete 14-day total course.

### Acute Management of Trichomoniasis & Herpes
* **Trichomoniasis**: **Metronidazole** 400mg PO BD for 7 days OR 2g PO single dose. (Must treat partner simultaneously).
* **First Episode Genital Herpes**: **Acyclovir** 400mg PO TDS for 7-10 days OR **Valacyclovir** 500mg PO BD for 7-10 days. (Initiate within 72 hrs of lesion onset).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Suppressive Therapy for Chronic Genital Herpes
* **Indication**: ≥6 recurrences per year OR severe psychosocial distress.
* **Regimen**: **Valacyclovir** 500mg PO daily OR **Acyclovir** 400mg PO BD for 6-12 months.
* **Monitoring**: Reassess need for continuation annually. Reduces frequency of recurrences and reduces (but does not eliminate) viral shedding/transmission risk.

### HPV Vaccination & Wart Management
* **Vaccination**: 9-valent HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9). Routine for boys and girls in HK before sexual debut (Primary 5/6 students).
* **Wart Clearance (Chronic/Recurrent)**:
  * Patient-applied: Imiquimod 5% cream (immune modulator) or Podophyllotoxin.
  * Clinician-applied: Cryotherapy (liquid nitrogen), Trichloroacetic acid (TCA), Surgical excision/cautery.

### Partner Notification & Contact Tracing (Contact Tracing)
* **Objective**: Prevent reinfection and interrupt community transmission.
* **Look-back Periods**:
  * Gonorrhoea (Symptomatic): All partners within past 2 months.
  * Chlamydia: All partners within past 6 months.
  * Syphilis (Primary): Past 3 months.
  * Syphilis (Secondary): Past 6 months.
* **Protocol**: Expedited Partner Therapy (EPT) not legally endorsed in HK; partners must physically attend Social Hygiene Clinics for empirical treatment and screening.
* **Abstinence Rule**: No sexual contact until 7 days post-treatment completion for both patient and partner.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for PID Complications
| Patient Characteristic / Finding | Risk of Long-term Sequelae |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Number of PID episodes** | 1 episode: ~8% risk of infertility<br>3 episodes: ~40% risk of infertility |
| **Chlamydial vs. Gonococcal** | Chlamydia causes more insidious scarring (higher ectopic risk) |
| **Presence of Tubo-Ovarian Abscess** | High risk of rupture (surgical emergency, sepsis) |

### Syphilis Treatment Efficacy & Prognostic Tracking
* **Parameter**: Serial quantitative Non-Treponemal Titers (VDRL/RPR).
* **Successful Treatment**: defined as a **4-fold drop** (e.g., 1:32 → 1:8) in VDRL/RPR titer within 6-12 months.
* **Serofast Reaction**: Titers drop but remain persistently low-positive (e.g., 1:2) despite adequate treatment. Monitor clinically; re-treatment not usually indicated if no symptoms and CSF normal.
* **Failure/Reinfection**: 4-fold increase in titers (e.g., 1:4 → 1:16). Requires retreat and evaluation for Neurosyphilis.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Untreated Gonorrhoea & Chlamydia
* **Disseminated Gonococcal Infection (DGI)**: Triad of tenosynovitis, dermatitis (painless pustules), and migratory polyarthralgia/septic arthritis.
* **Fitz-Hugh-Curtis Syndrome**: Perihepatitis complicating PID. Presents with Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) pain and "violin string" adhesions between liver capsule and peritoneum.
* **Reactive Arthritis (Reiter's Syndrome)**: Post-chlamydial immune complication. Triad: Urethritis, Conjunctivitis, Arthritis ("Can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree").
* **Obstetric/Neonatal**: Ectopic pregnancy, tubal factor infertility, neonatal conjunctivitis (Ophthalmia neonatorum), neonatal chlamydial pneumonia.

### Complications of Untreated Syphilis
* **Cardiovascular**: Ascending aortic aneurysm, aortic regurgitation, coronary ostial stenosis.
* **Neurological (Late)**: General paresis (dementia, personality change), Tabes dorsalis (sensory ataxia, lightning pains, bowel/bladder incontinence).
* **Congenital Syphilis**: Hutchinson's triad (peg-shaped incisors, interstitial keratitis, CN VIII deafness), saddle nose, saber shins.

### Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction (Treatment Complication)
* **Pathophysiology**: Acute, self-limiting febrile reaction occurring within 24 hours of initiating treatment for spirochetal infections (Syphilis). Massive destruction of spirochetes releases lipoproteins/cytokines.
* **Presentation**: Fever, chills, myalgia, tachycardia, exacerbation of syphilitic rash.
* **Management**: Supportive care only (Paracetamol). Do NOT stop antibiotics. Warn pregnant women (can induce fetal distress/premature labor).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the significance of a positive TPPA but negative VDRL in an asymptomatic patient? Answer: It indicates either a successfully treated historical syphilis infection, very early primary syphilis, or late latent syphilis. 
* Trap: Prescribing amoxicillin or cephalexin for an un-typed genital ulcer. Pearl: Standard beta-lactams do not adequately treat syphilis or chancroid; ulcers must be definitively investigated, and empiric syphilis treatment requires long-acting Benzathine Penicillin G IM.
* Pitfall: Reassuring a patient that their genital herpes is cured after acyclovir treatment. Explain that HSV establishes lifelong latency in sensory ganglia; antiviral therapy only treats acute outbreaks and reduces viral shedding, but cannot eradicate the virus.
* Viva Question: A female patient treated for PID 2 years ago now presents with 6 weeks of amenorrhea, abdominal pain, and vaginal spotting. What is the most critical diagnosis to rule out? Answer: Ectopic pregnancy, secondary to tubal scarring from previous PID.
* Trap: Treating gonorrhoea based solely on a positive NAAT without sending a culture in a symptomatic patient. Pearl: While NAAT is diagnostic, a culture is mandatory to perform antibiotic susceptibility testing given rising antimicrobial resistance in *N. gonorrhoeae*.
* Pitfall: Using mid-stream urine (MSU) for Chlamydia/Gonorrhoea screening. Always use First-Catch Urine (first 10-20mL) to capture the maximum bacterial load washed off the urethral mucosa.
