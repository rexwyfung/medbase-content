# Peptic Ulcer Disease (消化性潰瘍)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)
* **PUD**: Defect in gastric or duodenal mucosa extending through **muscularis mucosae** into deeper layers (submucosa/muscularis propria), typically >5mm in diameter.

### Primary Etiologies of PUD
* **Helicobacter pylori (幽門螺旋菌)**: Accounts for ~90% of Duodenal Ulcers (DU) and ~70% of Gastric Ulcers (GU).
* **NSAIDs & Aspirin**: Causes local mucosal injury and systemic prostaglandin inhibition.
* **Stress Ulcers**: **Curling's ulcer** (severe burns/hypovolemia) & **Cushing's ulcer** (↑ intracranial pressure → vagal hypersecretion of acid).
* **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (ZES)**: Gastrin-secreting neuroendocrine tumor (Gastrinoma).
* **Viral**: CMV/HSV (in immunocompromised).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: High *H. pylori* endemicity in HK/Southern China (~40-50% adult population).
* **DU vs GU**: DU historically more common, but GU incidence ↑ due to aging population and widespread NSAID/antiplatelet use.
* **Malignancy Risk**: HK has high rates of gastric adenocarcinoma; GU strictly requires repeat endoscopy to rule out malignancy.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Mucosal Defense vs Aggression Imbalance
* **Pathogenesis**: Occurs when aggressive factors (gastric acid, pepsin, bile salts, drugs) overwhelm mucosal defense mechanisms (mucus-bicarbonate layer, prostaglandins, mucosal blood flow, epithelial renewal).

### Pathophysiology of H. pylori Infection
* **Survival**: Gram -ve flagellated bacillus. Produces **Urease** → breaks down urea into ammonia + CO2 → creates alkaline microenvironment to survive gastric acidity.
* **Mucosal Damage**: Expresses cytotoxins (**CagA, VacA**) → disrupts tight junctions → mucosal inflammation (gastritis).
* **Acid Hypersecretion (DU)**: Antral-predominant infection → destruction of **D cells** → ↓ **Somatostatin** → uninhibited **G cells** → ↑ **Gastrin** release → ↑ parietal cell acid secretion → Duodenal acid load ↑ → Gastric metaplasia in duodenum → colonization by *H. pylori* → DU.
* **Hypochlorhydria (GU)**: Pangastritis → destruction of parietal cells → ↓ acid but direct mucosal ulceration → ↑ risk of Gastric Cancer.

### Pathophysiology of NSAID-Induced Ulcers
* **Mechanism**: Non-selective inhibition of **Cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1)**.
* **Effect**: ↓ synthesis of **Prostaglandins (PGE2, PGI2)**.
* **Consequence**: ↓ gastric mucus secretion, ↓ bicarbonate secretion, ↓ mucosal blood flow, ↓ epithelial proliferation. Direct local acidic damage via ion trapping inside epithelial cells.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Symptomatology & Pain Characteristics
* **Classic Presentation**: Epigastric pain (gnawing, burning, aching).
* **Duodenal Ulcer (DU) Pattern**: Pain occurs 2-3 hours post-prandial. **Relieved by food/antacids**. Frequently awakens patient at night (circadian acid peak).
* **Gastric Ulcer (GU) Pattern**: Pain exacerbated **shortly after eating**. Associated with nausea, food avoidance, and weight loss.

### ALARM Symptoms for Urgent Endoscopy
* **A**: Anemia (fatigue, dyspnea).
* **L**: Loss of weight (unintentional).
* **A**: Anorexia / early satiety.
* **R**: Recent onset of progressive symptoms (especially age >50).
* **M**: Melena (黑糞) or Hematemesis (吐血) / Dysphagia.

### System-Specific Physical Examination Findings
* **General**: Pallor (conjunctival/palmar) indicating occult bleeding. Cachexia (malignancy concern).
* **Abdominal Palpation**: Mild epigastric tenderness.
* **Signs of Complications (Surgical Emergencies)**:
  * **Perforation**: Board-like abdominal rigidity, generalized guarding, rebound tenderness, absent bowel sounds.
  * **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO)**: **Succussion splash** (heard >4 hours post-prandial when shaking patient's abdomen).
* **Rectal Exam**: DRE +ve for melena (tarry stool).
* **Stigmata of Malignancy**: Virchow's node (L supraclavicular), Sister Mary Joseph nodule (umbilical).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Epigastric Pain
| Condition | Key Discriminatory Features vs PUD |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)** | Retrosternal burning (heartburn), acid regurgitation, worse lying flat, water brash. |
| **Gastric Carcinoma (胃癌)** | Prominent weight loss, early satiety, persistent non-cyclical pain, refractory to PPI trial. |
| **Biliary Colic / Cholecystitis** | RUQ pain radiating to right scapula, post-prandial (fatty foods), +ve Murphy's sign. |
| **Acute Pancreatitis** | Severe constant epigastric pain radiating directly to back, relieved by leaning forward. |
| **Functional Dyspepsia** | Diagnosis of exclusion. Normal OGD, no structural/biochemical abnormalities. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **ECG**: Crucial to rule out inferior STEMI (atypical presentation of epigastric pain).
* **DRE**: Assess for melena.

### Laboratory Blood Tests
* **CBC**: Assess for microcytic hypochromic anemia (chronic occult bleed) or normocytic anemia (acute bleed).
* **Iron Studies**: Ferritin ↓, TIBC ↑ (Iron deficiency).
* **Renal Function Test (RFT)**: ↑ Urea/Creatinine ratio (>100:1) indicative of upper GI bleed (digestion of blood proteins).
* **LFTs & Amylase/Lipase**: Rule out biliary tree pathology and acute pancreatitis.
* **Fasting Serum Gastrin**: Only if ZES suspected (multiple/refractory ulcers, unusual locations e.g., distal duodenum/jejunum).

### Endoscopic Imaging & Biopsy (Gold Standard)
* **Oesophago-gastro-duodenoscopy (OGD / 胃鏡)**: Confirms diagnosis, allows intervention, enables biopsy.
* **Biopsy Protocol for GU**: Must take multiple biopsies from ulcer margin/base (high risk of underlying adenocarcinoma). Repeat OGD 6-8 weeks post-Rx to document healing.
* **Biopsy Protocol for DU**: Biopsy of ulcer not routinely needed (malignancy rare), but antral biopsy taken for *H. pylori*.

### Diagnostic Modalities for H. pylori
* **Invasive (Endoscopic)**:
  * **Rapid Urease Test (RUT / CLO Test)**: 1st line during OGD. Biopsy placed in urea + phenol red; urease turns pH indicator from yellow → red.
  * **Histology**: Giemsa stain (definitive).
* **Non-Invasive**:
  * **13C-Urea Breath Test (UBT)**: High sensitivity/specificity. Used to **confirm eradication** (done ≥4 weeks post-antibiotics, stop PPI 2 weeks prior).
  * **Fecal Antigen Test**: Alternative for diagnosis/eradication confirmation.
  * **Serology (IgG)**: Indicates past/present exposure. Cannot differentiate active vs treated infection. Not used in HK for eradication check.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Resuscitation of Acute Bleeding Peptic Ulcer
* **Airway/Breathing**: Protect airway if massive hematemesis/altered GCS.
* **Circulation**: 2x large-bore IV access (16G/18G). Aggressive crystalloid resuscitation.
* **Transfusion**: Group & Screen / Crossmatch. Transfuse PRBC if Hb <7 g/dL (or <8 g/dL in cardiovascular disease). Correct coagulopathy (FFP/Platelets/Prothrombin Complex Concentrate).
* **Pharmacotherapy**: **IV PPI therapy** (e.g., Pantoprazole/Esomeprazole 80mg bolus → 8mg/hr continuous infusion for 72 hours) ↓ rebleeding risk.
* **Prokinetics**: IV Erythromycin 250mg 30-120 mins pre-OGD to clear stomach of clots (HA local protocol dependent).

### Urgent Endoscopic Intervention
* **Timing**: Within 24 hours of presentation (within 12 hours if hemodynamically unstable).
* **Hemostatic Techniques (Dual Therapy Preferred)**:
  1. Injection: Dilute Epinephrine (1:10,000) causes vasoconstriction/tamponade.
  2. Thermal: Heater probe or Argon Plasma Coagulation (APC).
  3. Mechanical: Endoclips.

### Surgical Management of Perforated Ulcer
* **Presentation**: Pneumoperitoneum on erect CXR (gas under diaphragm).
* **Resuscitation**: Keep NPO, IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., Cefuroxime + Metronidazole or Co-amoxiclav), IV fluids, strict input/output charting.
* **Surgery**: Emergency Laparotomy with **Graham Patch** omentoplasty (plugging the hole with omentum) + thorough peritoneal lavage.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### H. pylori Eradication Regimens (Hong Kong HA Standard)
* **Requirement**: Stop PPI for 2 weeks before testing/re-testing to avoid false negatives.
* **First-Line Standard Triple Therapy (14 days)**:
  * **PPI** (e.g., Esomeprazole 20mg BD or Omeprazole 20mg BD) +
  * **Clarithromycin** 500mg BD +
  * **Amoxicillin** 1g BD (Substitute with Metronidazole 400mg BD if Penicillin-allergic).
* **Second-Line / High Macrolide Resistance Rescue (Bismuth Quadruple - 14 days)**:
  * **PPI** BD +
  * **Bismuth Subcitrate** 120mg QDS +
  * **Metronidazole** 400mg QDS +
  * **Tetracycline** 500mg QDS.
* **Confirmation of Eradication**: Perform UBT 4-6 weeks after completing antibiotics.

### Management of NSAID-Induced PUD
* **Action**: Discontinue offending NSAID/Aspirin if possible.
* **Healing Therapy**: Standard oral PPI daily for 4-8 weeks.
* **Maintenance**: If NSAID/Aspirin strictly required (e.g., post-PCI), coprescribe continuous prophylactic PPI (or use selective COX-2 inhibitor e.g., Celecoxib + PPI for highest risk).

### Lifestyle Modifications
* **Smoking Cessation**: Smoking delays healing and ↑ recurrence.
* **Alcohol Limit**: Avoid excessive consumption.
* **Diet**: Avoid specific trigger foods (spicy/acidic) if they precipitate dyspepsia, though strict bland diets are obsolete.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Pre-Endoscopic Risk: Glasgow-Blatchford Score (GBS)
* **Purpose**: Identifies patients with upper GI bleed who are "low risk" and can be managed safely as outpatients.
* **Variables Used**: Blood Urea, Hemoglobin, Systolic BP, Pulse >100, Melena, Syncope, Hepatic disease, Cardiac failure.
* **Threshold**: Score of **0** indicates very low risk (safe for early discharge).

### Post-Endoscopic Risk: Rockall Score
* **Purpose**: Predicts risk of rebleeding and mortality AFTER endoscopy.
* **Variables**: Age, Shock, Comorbidities, Endoscopic diagnosis (e.g., Mallory-Weiss vs Malignancy), Endoscopic stigmata of recent hemorrhage.

### Endoscopic Stratification: Forrest Classification of Bleeding Ulcers
| Forrest Class | Endoscopic Findings | Rebleeding Risk | Management Priority |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ia** | Active spurting arterial bleed | ~90% | Endoscopic Rx + IV PPI |
| **Ib** | Active oozing bleed | ~50% | Endoscopic Rx + IV PPI |
| **IIa** | Non-bleeding visible vessel | ~25-30% | Endoscopic Rx + IV PPI |
| **IIb** | Adherent clot | ~10-20% | Irrigate clot, Rx underlying lesion |
| **IIc** | Flat pigmented (hematin) spot | ~5-10% | Oral PPI, discharge early |
| **III** | Clean ulcer base | <5% | Oral PPI, discharge early |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### PUD Specific Complications
* **Hemorrhage**: Most common complication (15%). Erodes into gastroduodenal artery (posterior DU) or left gastric artery (lesser curvature GU).
* **Perforation**: Anterior DU most common site. Leads to chemical/bacterial peritonitis.
* **Penetration**: Ulcer erodes into adjacent organs (e.g., posterior DU into pancreas → causing acute pancreatitis).
* **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO)**: Due to acute edema or chronic fibrotic scarring at pylorus. Presents with projectile non-bilious vomiting containing undigested food. Hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis.
* **Malignancy**: GU can undergo malignant transformation; *H. pylori* is a Group 1 carcinogen for Gastric Adenocarcinoma and MALT Lymphoma.

### Complications of Chronic PPI Use
* **Infectious**: ↑ risk of *Clostridioides difficile* infection and community-acquired pneumonia.
* **Metabolic**: Hypomagnesemia, ↓ B12 absorption, ↓ Iron absorption, ↓ Calcium absorption (potential ↑ risk of osteoporotic fractures).
* **Renal**: Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why must we always biopsy a gastric ulcer but not necessarily a duodenal ulcer? Answer: Gastric ulcers have a significant risk of harboring underlying gastric adenocarcinoma or progressing to malignancy, whereas malignant transformation of duodenal ulcers is exceedingly rare.
* Viva Question: A patient with PUD presents with severe recurrent ulcers, diarrhea, and ulcers in the distal duodenum. What is the diagnosis? Answer: Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (Gastrinoma); screen with a fasting serum gastrin level.
* Trap: Checking H. pylori eradication success with serology (IgG). Pearl: Serology remains positive for life after exposure. You must use a C13-Urea Breath Test or Fecal Antigen test to confirm active eradication, at least 4 weeks post-treatment.
* Trap: Performing a Urea Breath Test while the patient is still taking a PPI. Pearl: PPIs suppress H. pylori urease activity, leading to false-negative results. PPIs must be stopped at least 2 weeks prior to a UBT.
* Pitfall: Discharging a patient with a clean-base ulcer without reviewing their NSAID history. Always explicitly document counseling to cease NSAIDs and arrange for H. pylori testing to prevent recurrent bleeding.
* Viva Question: What is the classic metabolic derangement in severe Gastric Outlet Obstruction secondary to chronic PUD? Answer: Hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis with paradoxical aciduria, secondary to prolonged loss of gastric hydrochloric acid and subsequent volume depletion activating the RAAS system.
