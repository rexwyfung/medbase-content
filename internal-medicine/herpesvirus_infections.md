# Herpesvirus Infections (疱疹病毒感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Herpesviridae
* **Classification**: Large, enveloped, double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) viruses.
* **Core Hallmark**: Establish lifelong latency following primary infection → periodic reactivation triggered by immunosuppression, stress, or concurrent illness.

### Etiologic Subfamilies
* **Alphaherpesvirinae**: Fast-replicating, cytolytic.
  * **HSV-1** (Herpes Simplex Virus 1)
  * **HSV-2** (Herpes Simplex Virus 2)
  * **VZV** (Varicella-Zoster Virus / HHV-3)
* **Betaherpesvirinae**: Slow-replicating, cytomegalic (enlarged cells).
  * **CMV** (Cytomegalovirus / HHV-5)
  * **HHV-6** & **HHV-7** (Roseolovirus)
* **Gammaherpesvirinae**: Lymphotropic, oncogenic potential.
  * **EBV** (Epstein-Barr Virus / HHV-4)
  * **HHV-8** (Kaposi Sarcoma-associated Herpesvirus / KSHV)

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of Herpesviruses
* **EBV**: >95% seroprevalence in adults. Crucial driver of **Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (鼻咽癌)**, which has a distinctively high incidence in Southern China/HK.
* **CMV**: >90% adult seroprevalence in HK → major implications for local solid organ (SOT) and hematopoietic stem cell transplants (HSCT).
* **VZV**: Near-universal adult exposure. Reactivation incidence ↑ dramatically after age 50.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Cellular Tropism & Latency Sites
* **Alpha (HSV-1/2, VZV)**: Primary infection of mucosal epithelial cells → retrograde axonal transport → latency in **Sensory Nerve Ganglia** (e.g., Trigeminal ganglion for HSV-1, Sacral ganglia for HSV-2, Dorsal root ganglia for VZV).
* **Beta (CMV)**: Latency primarily in **Mononuclear cells** (monocytes/macrophages), endothelial cells, and renal tubule cells.
* **Gamma (EBV, HHV-8)**: Latency in **Memory B-lymphocytes** and lymphoid tissues. EBV binds to **CD21** receptor to enter B cells.

### Oncogenic Mechanisms (Gammaherpesviruses)
* **EBV**: Viral proteins (LMP-1, EBNA) mimic B-cell receptor signaling (CD40) → drive immortalization and unchecked proliferation of B cells → Burkitt lymphoma, Hodgkin lymphoma, primary CNS lymphoma (in HIV). In epithelial cells → NPC.
* **HHV-8**: Encodes viral homologues of human cell cycle regulators (v-cyclin) and anti-apoptotic proteins (v-Bcl-2) → drives angiogenesis and endothelial spindle cell proliferation → **Kaposi Sarcoma (卡波西肉瘤)**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### HSV-1 and HSV-2 Clinical Assessment
* **Oral/Mucosal**: Painful grouped vesicles on erythematous base (cold sores/gingivostomatitis).
* **Genital**: Extremely painful, shallow, clustered genital ulcers + tender bilateral inguinal lymphadenopathy.
* **Ocular**: Red eye, photophobia, tearing (Herpes keratitis).

### VZV Clinical Assessment (Shingles & Syndromes)
* **Herpes Zoster / Shingles (帶狀疱疹)**: Unilateral, strictly dermatomal burning pain/dysesthesia preceding vesicular eruption. Midline is NOT crossed.
* **Herpes Zoster Ophthalmicus (HZO)**: V1 branch of Trigeminal nerve involvement.
  * **Hutchinson's Sign**: Vesicles on tip of nose (predicts high risk of ocular involvement).
* **Ramsay Hunt Syndrome**: Geniculate ganglion reactivation.
  * **Signs**: Ipsilateral lower motor neuron facial nerve palsy + vesicles in external auditory meatus/tympanic membrane + tinnitus/vertigo.

### EBV Infectious Mononucleosis (傳染性單核白血球增多症) Assessment
* **Classic Triad**: Fever + Exudative Tonsillar Pharyngitis + Cervical Lymphadenopathy (often posterior cervical chain).
* **Abdominal Signs**: Splenomegaly (risk of rupture), mild hepatomegaly.
* **Skin (Drug Interaction)**: Pruritic maculopapular rash developing specifically after administration of Ampicillin/Amoxicillin for presumed Strep throat.

### CMV Disease Assessment (Immunocompromised / HIV / Post-Transplant)
* **Systemic**: Prolonged fever, malaise, myalgias (Mono-like syndrome without severe pharyngitis).
* **Ocular**: Visual floaters, flashing lights, visual field deficits (CMV retinitis).
* **GI tract**: Dysphagia/odynophagia (esophagitis), abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea (colitis).
* **Respiratory**: Dry cough, severe progressive dyspnea (CMV pneumonitis - classic post-HSCT).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnosis for Genital Ulcers
| Disease | Pathogen | Ulcer Characteristics | Lymphadenopathy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Genital Herpes** | HSV-1 / HSV-2 | Multiple, grouped, **painful**, shallow | Tender, bilateral |
| **Syphilis (Chancre)** | *Treponema pallidum* | Single, **painless**, indurated, clean base | Non-tender, rubbery |
| **Chancroid** | *Haemophilus ducreyi* | Multiple, **painful**, deep, purulent base | Tender, fluctuant (buboes) |
| **LGV** | *Chlamydia trachomatis* (L1-3) | Small, shallow, painless (often missed) | Tender, draining fistulas (groove sign) |

### Differential Diagnosis for Mononucleosis-like Syndrome
| Feature | EBV (Classic Mono) | CMV (Mono-like) | Acute HIV Infection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pharyngitis** | Severe, exudative | Mild or absent | Mild |
| **Lymphadenopathy** | Prominent (posterior cervical) | Mild | Generalized |
| **Splenomegaly** | Very common | Common | Rare |
| **Heterophile Ab** | **+ve** (Monospot) | **-ve** | **-ve** |
| **Rash** | Post-amoxicillin | Rare | Common (mucocutaneous ulcers + generalized) |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Cellular Virological Tests
* **Tzanck Smear**: Identifies multinucleated giant cells (cannot distinguish HSV from VZV; low sensitivity, largely historical).
* **Viral Swabs**: Vesicle fluid/ulcer base for **Multiplex PCR** (Gold Standard for mucosal HSV/VZV).

### Blood Investigations for EBV & CMV
* **Monospot Test (Heterophile Antibodies)**: Rapid latex agglutination for EBV. High specificity, but high false-negative rate in 1st week or in children.
* **Peripheral Blood Smear (EBV)**: ↑ Lymphocytes, **Atypical Lymphocytes** (>10% of total leukocytes) - large, reactive CD8+ T-cells with indented "skirting" cytoplasm (Downey cells).
* **EBV Specific Serology**:
  * **VCA IgM +ve, VCA IgG -ve**: Acute infection.
  * **VCA IgG +ve, EBNA IgG +ve**: Past infection / Immunity (EBNA takes 6-12 weeks to appear).
* **CMV Viral Load**: Quantitative plasma CMV DNA PCR (Standard for monitoring SOT/HSCT patients).
* **CMV pp65 Antigenemia**: Detects CMV matrix protein in leukocytes (older test, largely replaced by PCR but still tested).

### Diagnostic Imaging & Endoscopy
* **Ophthalmology (Fundoscopy)**: CMV Retinitis shows "pizza pie" appearance (hemorrhage + full-thickness retinal necrosis + white exudates).
* **GI Endoscopy (CMV Colitis/Esophagitis)**: Large, shallow ulcers. Biopsy is mandatory.
* **Histopathology (Tissue Biopsy)**:
  * **CMV**: Classic cytomegalic cells with "owl's eye" intranuclear inclusion bodies.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Disseminated / Severe HSV & VZV Management
* **Indication**: Disseminated VZV/HSV, severe mucositis unable to tolerate PO, visceral involvement.
* **Rx**: **IV Aciclovir** (requires adequate hydration to prevent nephrotoxicity).
* **Ocular Emergencies (HZO/HSV Keratitis)**: Urgent Ophthalmology consult → Topical Antivirals (e.g., Aciclovir ophthalmic ointment) + PO/IV systemic antivirals. **NEVER give topical steroids alone** in HSV keratitis (risk of corneal melting/perforation).

### Acute Management of Invasive CMV Disease
* **CMV Retinitis / GI / Pneumonitis (HA Formulary)**:
  * **1st Line**: **IV Ganciclovir** OR **PO Valganciclovir** (if GI absorption intact and non-sight-threatening).
  * **2nd Line (Ganciclovir-resistant/UL97 mutation)**: **IV Foscarnet** or **IV Cidofovir**.
* **Intravitreal Therapy**: Ganciclovir/Foscarnet injections directly into eye for sight-threatening lesions near the macula.

### Acute EBV Complication Resuscitation
* **Impending Airway Obstruction (massive tonsillar hypertrophy)**: IV Dexamethasone/Hydrocortisone.
* **Splenic Rupture**: Immediate surgical consultation, strict bed rest, IV fluids + blood products.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Suppressive Therapy for Recurrent HSV
* **Indications**: Frequent genital recurrences (>6 episodes/year) or HSV erythema multiforme.
* **Rx**: **PO Valaciclovir** (QD) OR **PO Aciclovir** (BID). Step-down therapy after 1 year to assess baseline recurrence frequency.

### Management of Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN)
* **Definition**: Pain persisting >90 days after VZV rash onset.
* **First-line Rx**: 
  * **Gabapentinoids**: PO Pregabalin or Gabapentin.
  * **TCAs**: PO Amitriptyline or Nortriptyline.
  * **Topical**: 5% Lidocaine patches (for localized allodynia).
* **Second-line Rx**: Duloxetine, Tramadol.

### CMV Prophylaxis in Solid Organ Transplant (SOT)
* **Pre-emptive Strategy**: Weekly CMV PCR monitoring → Start PO Valganciclovir only if viral load exceeds institution threshold.
* **Universal Prophylaxis Strategy**: Routine **PO Valganciclovir** for 3–6 months post-transplant in high-risk patients.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### CMV Risk Stratification in Solid Organ Transplant
| Donor (D) CMV Status | Recipient (R) CMV Status | Risk Level | Prophylaxis Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **D (+ve)** | **R (-ve)** | **Highest Risk** | Universal prophylaxis (Valganciclovir for 6 months) |
| **D (+ve)** | **R (+ve)** | Intermediate Risk | Pre-emptive monitoring OR 3 months prophylaxis |
| **D (-ve)** | **R (+ve)** | Intermediate Risk | Pre-emptive monitoring OR 3 months prophylaxis |
| **D (-ve)** | **R (-ve)** | Lowest Risk | No specific CMV prophylaxis needed |

### Prognosis & Risk Factors for Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN)
* **High-Risk Factors for PHN development**:
  * Advanced age (>60 years is strongest predictor).
  * Severe acute pain or intense prodromal pain.
  * Severe rash burden during acute zoster.
  * Ophthalmic zoster (HZO).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Herpesviruses
* **VZV**: Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN), Herpes Zoster Ophthalmicus (visual loss), VZV Vasculopathy (stroke post-zoster), Acute Retinal Necrosis.
* **EBV**: Splenic rupture (avoid contact sports for 4 weeks), Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), X-linked lymphoproliferative disease (Duncan syndrome), Oral Hairy Leukoplakia (in HIV).
* **CMV**: Graft rejection in transplant recipients, retinitis leading to retinal detachment, sclerosing cholangitis (AIDS cholangiopathy).

### Adverse Effects of Anti-Herpetic Pharmacotherapy
* **Aciclovir / Valaciclovir**: 
  * **Obstructive Crystalline Nephropathy**: Drug crystallizes in renal tubules → acute kidney injury. Preventable with aggressive IV hydration.
  * **Neurotoxicity**: Encephalopathy, hallucinations (usually in renal failure if dose not adjusted).
* **Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir**:
  * **Bone Marrow Suppression**: Severe neutropenia, thrombocytopenia (requires dose reduction or switch to Foscarnet).
* **Foscarnet**:
  * **Electrolyte Derangements**: Massive renal wasting → ↓Ca2+, ↓Mg2+, ↓K+, ↓PO4 (causes seizures, arrhythmias).
  * **Nephrotoxicity**: Direct tubular toxicity.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic drug interaction that causes a rash in a patient with infectious mononucleosis? Answer: Administration of ampicillin or amoxicillin for mistakenly diagnosed streptococcal pharyngitis causes a widespread maculopapular rash.
* Trap: Mistaking the monospot test as completely reliable for ruling out EBV in the first few days of illness. Pearl: The heterophile antibody test (Monospot) can be falsely negative during the first week of infection; if clinical suspicion is high, repeat the test later or check EBV-specific serology (VCA IgM/IgG).
* Pitfall: Prescribing universal CMV prophylaxis for all solid organ transplant recipients regardless of donor serology. CMV mismatch (Donor positive, Recipient negative) is the only scenario universally mandating extended prophylaxis. D-/R- requires no prophylaxis.
* Viva Question: What is the histological hallmark of CMV infection on tissue biopsy? Answer: Large cytomegalic cells with prominent eosinophilic intranuclear inclusion bodies surrounded by a clear halo, known as "owl's eye" inclusions.
* Trap: Treating suspected herpes simplex keratitis with topical corticosteroids to reduce inflammation. Pearl: Topical steroids alone in HSV keratitis can exacerbate viral replication, leading to rapid corneal melting, ulceration, and perforation. Always combine with or use topical antivirals first under ophthalmology guidance.
* Viva Question: How do you prevent acute kidney injury when administering high-dose IV Aciclovir? Answer: Pre-hydrate and concurrently hydrate the patient with IV normal saline to prevent drug crystallization in the renal tubules.
