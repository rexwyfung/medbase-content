# Conn's Syndrome (康恩氏綜合症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Primary Aldosteronism (PA) (原發性醛固酮增多症)
* **Primary Aldosteronism**: Autonomous, renin-independent hypersecretion of **Aldosterone (醛固酮)** from adrenal cortex
* **Conn's Syndrome**: Historically denotes a solitary **Aldosterone-Producing Adenoma (APA)**; now often used interchangeably with PA, though strictly refers to the adenoma subset

### Etiologies of Conn's Syndrome & PA
* **Bilateral Adrenal Hyperplasia (BAH)** (60-70%): Most common cause, micro/macronodular
* **Aldosterone-Producing Adenoma (APA)** (30-40%): True Conn's Syndrome, usually unilateral, benign
* **Unilateral Adrenal Hyperplasia** (~2%)
* **Adrenocortical Carcinoma** (<1%): Rare, suspect if large mass (>4cm) or rapid virilization/Cushingoid features co-exist
* **Glucocorticoid-Remediable Aldosteronism (GRA)** (<1%): Familial Hyperaldosteronism Type I, autosomal dominant chimeric gene mutation (CYP11B1/CYP11B2)

### Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: 5-10% of all hypertensive patients; up to 20% in treatment-resistant hypertension
* **Demographics**: Peak onset 30-50 years; APA more common in females, BAH equal gender distribution

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Molecular Pathophysiology of Aldosterone Excess
* **Mineralocorticoid Receptor (MR) Activation**: Aldosterone binds intracellular MRs in Principal Cells of Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) & Cortical Collecting Duct (CCD)
* **Sodium Retention**: Upregulates **Epithelial Na+ Channels (ENaC)** & Na+/K+-ATPase → ↑ Na+ reabsorption → water follows → hypervolemia → **Hypertension (高血壓)**
* **Potassium & Hydrogen Wasting**: Electronegativity from Na+ influx drives K+ efflux via **ROMK channels** & H+ efflux via apical H+-ATPase → **Hypokalemia (低鉀血症)** & **Metabolic Alkalosis (代謝性鹼中毒)**
* **RAAS Suppression**: Chronic hypervolemia & hypertension → negative feedback on juxtaglomerular apparatus → profound ↓ **Renin (腎素)**

### Mechanism of Aldosterone Escape (醛固酮逃避)
* **Initial Phase**: ↑ Aldosterone → continuous Na+/fluid retention → hypervolemia
* **Escape Phase**: Hypervolemia stretches atria → ↑ **Atrial Natriuretic Peptide (ANP)** release
* **Compensatory Diuresis**: ANP triggers pressure natriuresis → overrides ENaC Na+ retention
* **Clinical Result**: Prevents overt hypernatremia and peripheral edema; patients achieve a new steady state of sustained hypertension without gross fluid overload

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### History & Symptoms of Conn's Syndrome
* **Hypertension History**: Early onset (<40 years), refractory to ≥3 antihypertensives, or incidental finding
* **Hypokalemic Symptoms**:
  * **Musculoskeletal**: Profound muscle weakness, myalgia, muscle cramps, rare ascending paralysis
  * **Cardiovascular**: Palpitations (arrhythmias)
  * **Renal (Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus)**: Polyuria (多尿), nocturia, polydipsia (due to K+ depletion impairing renal concentrating ability)

### Physical Examination Findings in Conn's Syndrome
* **Vitals**: Sustained, severe elevated blood pressure
* **Neuromuscular System**:
  * **Hyporeflexia**: ↓ Deep tendon reflexes (due to ↓ K+)
  * **Tetany / Trousseau’s & Chvostek’s signs**: Can occur if concurrent metabolic alkalosis causes ↓ ionized calcium
* **Volume Status**: ABSENCE of peripheral edema (due to Aldosterone Escape phenomenon)
* **Cardiovascular**: Loud S2, S4 gallop (if hypertensive LVH present)
* **Abdomen**: Usually normal; large palpable mass suggests adrenocortical carcinoma rather than APA

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Hypokalemic Hypertension
| Condition | Aldosterone | Renin | Pathophysiological Discriminator |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Aldosteronism (Conn's)** | ↑ High | ↓ Low (Suppressed) | Autonomous adrenal hypersecretion |
| **Secondary Aldosteronism** | ↑ High | ↑ High | Renovascular HTN (RAS), Renin-secreting tumor, Coarctation |
| **Liddle Syndrome** | ↓ Low | ↓ Low | Autosomal dominant activating mutation of ENaC (Pseudoaldosteronism) |
| **Syndrome of Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess (AME) / Licorice** | ↓ Low | ↓ Low | 11β-HSD2 inhibition/deficiency → Cortisol acts on MRs |
| **Cushing's Syndrome** | ↓ Low/Normal | ↓ Low/Normal | Excess cortisol overwhelms 11β-HSD2 |
| **Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH)** | ↓ Low | ↓ Low | 11β-hydroxylase or 17α-hydroxylase deficiency → ↑ DOC |

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Medication Washout Protocol Prior to PA Testing
* **Must Withdraw (4-6 weeks prior)**: 
  * **Aldosterone Antagonists**: Spironolactone, Eplerenone
  * **ENaC Blockers**: Amiloride
  * **K+-wasting Diuretics**: Thiazides, Loop diuretics
* **Must Withdraw (2 weeks prior)**:
  * ACE inhibitors, ARBs, Beta-blockers, Clonidine
* **Permitted Antihypertensives (Bridging Therapy)**:
  * **Alpha-blockers**: Doxazosin (多沙唑嗪), Prazosin
  * **Non-DHP CCBs**: Verapamil (維拉帕米)
  * **Direct Vasodilators**: Hydralazine

### Screening Test for PA: Aldosterone-Renin Ratio (ARR)
* **Condition Optimization**: Correct hypokalemia first (↓ K+ suppresses aldosterone, causing false -ve); ensure unrestricted salt intake
* **Measurement (Morning, Seated)**: Plasma Aldosterone Concentration (PAC) / Plasma Renin Activity (PRA)
* **Diagnostic Threshold (Local HA Labs)**: 
  * ARR > 20-30 (depending on assay units) WITH PAC > 15 ng/dL (416 pmol/L) → +ve screen

### Confirmatory Testing for PA
* **Intravenous Saline Infusion Test (SIT)**:
  * **Protocol**: Infuse 2L of 0.9% NaCl over 4 hours
  * **Criteria**: PAC fails to suppress < 5 ng/dL (<138 pmol/L) post-infusion → Confirms PA
* **Captopril Challenge Test** (Alternative if SIT contraindicated e.g., severe HTN/CCF):
  * **Criteria**: Failure of PAC suppression post-50mg Captopril oral dose

### Localization Imaging & Invasive Testing for PA
* **High-Resolution Adrenal CT**:
  * **Findings**: Identifies unilateral macroadenoma (>1cm), bilateral hyperplasia, or normal morphology
  * **Limitation**: Cannot differentiate true APA from non-functioning adrenal incidentaloma (common in >40yo)
* **Adrenal Venous Sampling (AVS) (腎上腺靜脈抽血)**:
  * **Indication**: Gold standard for ALL patients considering surgery, especially age >40 or equivocal CT
  * **Protocol**: Cannulate both adrenal veins + IVC; measure Aldosterone and Cortisol (to confirm correct catheter placement)
  * **Criteria**: Lateralization index (Aldo/Cortisol ratio side A vs side B) > 4:1 confirms unilateral APA; ratio < 3:1 suggests BAH

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Acute Severe Hypokalemia Resuscitation
* **Indications**: Serum K+ < 2.5 mmol/L, arrhythmias, muscle paralysis
* **Monitoring**: Continuous ECG telemetry (monitor for U waves, flat T waves, prolonged QT)
* **Intervention**: IV KCl infusion via large peripheral/central vein; max rate 10-20 mmol/hour to prevent phlebitis/arrhythmias

### Acute Hypertensive Crisis Management
* **Indications**: BP > 180/120 mmHg with acute target organ damage (encephalopathy, acute pulmonary edema)
* **Intervention**: IV Labetalol or IV Sodium Nitroprusside infusion
* **Goal**: Reduce MAP by max 20-25% in first hour to preserve cerebral perfusion

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Surgical Management Pathway for Unilateral PA (APA)
* **Definitive Treatment**: Unilateral Laparoscopic Adrenalectomy (腹腔鏡腎上腺切除術)
* **Pre-operative Optimization**:
  * Initiate **Spironolactone (螺內酯)** 3-4 weeks pre-op to normalize BP and K+
  * Correct hypokalemia completely to prevent peri-operative arrhythmias
* **Post-operative Care**:
  * Stop Spironolactone and K+ supplements immediately post-op
  * Monitor for post-op hyperkalemia (due to suppression of contralateral normal adrenal gland) → may require transient Fludrocortisone if prolonged hypoaldosteronism occurs

### Pharmacological Management Algorithm for Bilateral PA (BAH)
* **Indication**: BAH, failed surgery, or high surgical risk patients
* **First-line**: **Spironolactone (螺內酯)**
  * Titrate from 12.5mg up to 100-400mg daily depending on BP and K+ control
* **Second-line (if Spironolactone intolerant/anti-androgenic side effects)**:
  * **Eplerenone (依普酮)**: Selective MR antagonist (no gynecomastia), requires BID dosing
* **Adjunctive Therapy**:
  * **Amiloride (阿米洛利)**: ENaC inhibitor, K+-sparing
  * Add standard anti-hypertensives (Thiazides, CCBs, ACEI) if BP remains elevated despite normalized K+
* **Lifestyle**: Strict dietary sodium restriction (<100 mmol/day) enhances efficacy of MR antagonists

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### PASO (Primary Aldosteronism Surgical Outcome) Criteria
* **Complete Clinical Success**: Normal BP without anti-hypertensive drugs (occurs in ~50% of APA resections)
* **Partial Clinical Success**: BP same/lower with fewer medications
* **Complete Biochemical Success**: Normalization of hypokalemia and ARR (occurs in >90% of APA resections)
* **Predictors of Poor HTN Cure Rate**: Age >50, duration of HTN >5 years, family history of essential HTN, multiple pre-op medications

### Prognostic Trajectory of PA
* **Cardiovascular Risk**: Patients with PA have higher risk of stroke, MI, and AF compared to essential hypertension patients matched for age and BP level (Aldosterone is directly cardiotoxic and profibrotic)
* **Target Organ Resolution**: Adrenalectomy or adequate MR blockade significantly reverses LVH and reduces microalbuminuria

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### Cardiovascular & Renal End-Organ Damage
* **Cardiac**: Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH), Myocardial fibrosis, Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動)
* **Vascular**: Increased arterial stiffness, accelerated atherosclerosis, Ischemic Stroke
* **Renal**: Hypertensive Nephrosclerosis, declining eGFR, significant proteinuria

### Drug Adverse Effects in PA Therapy
* **Spironolactone Toxicity**: 
  * Anti-androgenic: Gynecomastia (男乳發育症) (dose-dependent, up to 50%), erectile dysfunction, ↓ libido
  * Progestogenic: Menstrual irregularities in females
  * Renal: Hyperkalemia, acute kidney injury (if over-diuresed)

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Expecting peripheral edema in Conn's Syndrome due to profound sodium and water retention. Pearl: PA rarely presents with clinical edema due to the "Aldosterone Escape" mechanism driven by ANP-induced pressure natriuresis.
* Viva Question: Why must hypokalemia be corrected before performing an Aldosterone-Renin Ratio (ARR) screening? Answer: Hypokalemia is a potent negative regulator of aldosterone secretion; testing while hypokalemic can cause a false-negative low aldosterone level despite an autonomous adenoma.
* Pitfall: Sending a patient over 40 with a unilateral adrenal nodule on CT directly for surgery without further tests. Non-functioning adrenal incidentalomas are extremely common with age; you must perform Adrenal Venous Sampling (AVS) to definitively lateralize aldosterone hypersecretion before removing the gland.
* Trap: Stopping all antihypertensives 4 weeks prior to ARR testing and risking a hypertensive crisis. Pearl: Safely bridge the patient with non-interfering medications like Verapamil (Non-DHP CCB) and Doxazosin (Alpha-blocker) while washing out RAAS inhibitors and diuretics.
* Viva Question: What is the classic metabolic triad of Conn's Syndrome? Answer: Hypertension, Hypokalemia, and Metabolic Alkalosis.
* Pitfall: Assuming a normal potassium level rules out Primary Aldosteronism. Only ~30-50% of PA patients present with spontaneous hypokalemia; normokalemic hypertension is actually the most common presentation of PA today.
