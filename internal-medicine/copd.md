# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (慢性阻塞性肺病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of COPD
* **COPD (慢性阻塞性肺病):** Common, preventable, treatable disease characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation.
* **Core Anomaly:** Airway and/or alveolar abnormalities caused by significant exposure to noxious particles or gases.

### Etiologies of COPD
* **Tobacco Smoking:** Primary cause (>80% of cases in developed settings).
* **Occupational Exposures:** Coal dust, silica, cadmium.
* **Environmental/Indoor Pollution:** Biomass fuels (wood, crop residue) for cooking/heating.
* **Genetic Factor:** Alpha-1 Antitrypsin (AAT) Deficiency (α1-抗胰蛋白酶缺乏症) (typically European descent, rare in HK Chinese, consider if basal emphysema or young age onset).

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong)
* **Prevalence:** Highly prevalent in elderly males, mirroring historical smoking rates.
* **Healthcare Burden:** Major cause of acute medical admissions in HA hospitals, especially during winter (influenza/viral surges).
* **Mortality:** Top 10 leading causes of death in HK.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Chronic Bronchitis
* **Definition:** Clinical diagnosis (productive cough for ≥3 months/year for ≥2 consecutive years).
* **Mechanism:** Mucous gland hyperplasia, goblet cell metaplasia → mucus hypersecretion.
* **Ciliary Dysfunction:** Impaired mucociliary escalator → retained secretions, ↑ infection risk.

### Pathophysiology of Emphysema (肺氣腫)
* **Definition:** Pathological diagnosis (abnormal permanent enlargement of airspaces distal to terminal bronchioles + wall destruction).
* **Mechanism:** Protease/Antiprotease imbalance (Neutrophil elastase > Alpha-1 antitrypsin) → destruction of elastin.
* **Consequence:** Loss of alveolar attachments → premature small airway collapse during expiration (dynamic hyperinflation).
* **Types:** Centrilobular (smoking-related, upper lobes); Panacinar (AAT deficiency, lower lobes).

### Inflammatory & Cellular Mechanisms
* **Predominant Cells:** CD8+ T lymphocytes, Macrophages, Neutrophils.
* **Mediators:** LTB4, IL-8, TNF-α.
* **Systemic Effects:** Chronic low-grade systemic inflammation → skeletal muscle wasting (cachexia), cardiovascular comorbidities.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Pertinent History for COPD Assessment
* **Primary Symptoms:** Insidious, progressive exertional dyspnea (breathlessness), chronic cough, chronic sputum production.
* **Pattern:** Symptoms worse in morning; daily variability is less than Asthma.
* **Risk Profiling:** Pack-years of smoking (1 pack-year = 20 cigs/day for 1 year), occupational history.
* **Exacerbation Hx:** Frequency of acute worsening (AECOPD) requiring steroids/antibiotics or hospitalization in past 12 months.

### General Physical Examination Signs
* **Inspection:** Central cyanosis, tar-stained fingers (nicotine).
* **Habitus:** "Pink Puffer" (emphysematous, thin, dyspneic) vs "Blue Bloater" (bronchitic, cyanotic, edematous) – *historical terms, often mixed clinically*.
* **Systemic Signs:** Flapping tremor (Asterixis) if severe CO2 retention (Type II Respiratory Failure).

### Respiratory System Signs
* **Inspection:** Barrel chest (↑ AP diameter), pursed-lip breathing (creates intrinsic PEEP to keep airways open), use of accessory muscles (SCM, scalenes), intercostal indrawing, Hoover’s sign (paradoxical inward movement of lower rib cage during inspiration).
* **Palpation:** ↓ chest expansion bilaterally, palpable tracheal tug.
* **Percussion:** Diffusely hyperresonant, loss of cardiac and hepatic dullness.
* **Auscultation:** Globally ↓ breath sounds, prolonged expiratory phase, early inspiratory crackles (opening of small airways), expiratory polyphonic wheeze.

### Signs of Right Heart Failure / Cor Pulmonale (肺心病)
* **Neck:** Elevated JVP (prominent 'a' wave if pulmonary HTN).
* **Precordium:** Left parasternal heave (RV hypertrophy), loud P2.
* **Abdomen/Limbs:** Tender hepatomegaly, bilateral pitting pedal edema.
* **Note:** Finger clubbing is **NOT** a sign of COPD; if present, suspect Lung Cancer, Bronchiectasis, or Fibrosis.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### COPD vs Asthma
| Feature | COPD | Asthma |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Mid-life (usually >40 yrs) | Early in life (often childhood) |
| **Symptoms** | Slowly progressive, constant | Variable daily, nocturnal awakening |
| **Smoking Hx** | Almost always | May be non-smoker |
| **Airflow Limit** | Irreversible (post-BD FEV1/FVC <0.7) | Reversible (↑ FEV1 >12% & >200mL) |
| **Inflammation** | Neutrophilic (CD8+) | Eosinophilic (CD4+, Th2) |

### Other Key Differential Diagnoses
* **Bronchiectasis (支氣管擴張):** Large volumes of purulent sputum, coarse pan-inspiratory crackles, clubbing, frequent bacterial infections.
* **Congestive Heart Failure (心力衰竭):** Orthopnea, PND, fine basal crepitations, S3 gallop, normal spirometry (or restrictive pattern).
* **Tuberculosis (肺結核):** Endemic in HK. Hemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss, apical signs.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### GOLD Diagnostic Criteria for COPD
* **Spirometry Criteria:** Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70 confirms presence of persistent airflow limitation.

### Bedside Investigations
* **Spirometry:** Reveals obstructive pattern (FEV1 ↓↓, FVC ↓ or normal, FEV1/FVC < 0.70). Concave expiratory flow-volume loop.
* **Pulse Oximetry (SpO2):** Assess for resting hypoxemia.
* **Sputum C/ST:** Pathogen identification during exacerbations (Pseudomonas, H. influenzae, S. pneumoniae).
* **ECG:** Right axis deviation, P-pulmonale (peaked P waves >2.5mm in II, III, aVF), RVH.

### Blood Investigations
* **CBC:** Secondary polycythemia (↑ Hb/Hct) due to chronic hypoxia; ↑ WBC if acute infection.
* **Blood Eosinophil Count:** Directs Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) therapy. (Eosinophils ≥ 300 cells/µL predicts ICS benefit).
* **ABG:** Crucial for staging and AECOPD. Assesses for Hypoxemia (Type I RF) or Hypercapnia (Type II RF, PaCO2 > 6.0 kPa) and acid-base status.
* **Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Level:** Indicated in young patients (<45 yrs), Caucasian descent, or lower lobe emphysema.

### Imaging Studies
* **CXR (PA & Lateral):** Hyperinflation (>6 anterior ribs visible), flattened hemidiaphragms, rapid tapering of vascular shadows, hyperlucency, bullae, narrow cardiac silhouette.
* **HRCT Thorax:** Not routine for Dx. Used to detect bronchiectasis, evaluate for lung volume reduction surgery (LVRS), or screen for lung cancer.

### Special / Lung Function Testing
* **Plethysmography:** ↑ Total Lung Capacity (TLC), ↑ Residual Volume (RV) indicating air trapping.
* **DLCO:** Decreased in emphysema (due to loss of alveolar capillary bed). Normal in pure chronic bronchitis.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD) Definition
* **Definition:** Acute worsening of respiratory symptoms resulting in additional therapy (↑ dyspnea, ↑ sputum volume, ↑ sputum purulence).

### Initial Resuscitation & Oxygenation Target
* **Controlled Oxygen Therapy:** Initiate via Venturi mask (24% or 28%) to prevent worsening hypercapnia (Haldane effect, V/Q mismatch, ↓ hypoxic drive).
* **Target SpO2:** Strictly **88% - 92%**.
* **Monitoring:** Perform ABG within 30-60 minutes of starting O2 to check pH and PaCO2.

### Pharmacological Management of AECOPD
* **Bronchodilators:** Nebulized Short-Acting Beta-Agonist (SABA) + Short-Acting Muscarinic Antagonist (SAMA) (e.g., Salbutamol 2.5mg + Ipratropium 0.5mg Q4H).
* **Systemic Corticosteroids:** Shortens recovery time, improves FEV1.
  * **Regimen:** PO Prednisolone 30-40 mg daily for 5 days.
  * **Alternative:** IV Hydrocortisone 100mg Q6H if patient cannot swallow.
* **Antibiotics:** Indicated if 3 cardinal symptoms present (Anthonisen criteria: ↑ dyspnea, ↑ sputum volume, ↑ purulence) or requires mechanical ventilation.
  * **Typical HA Regimen:** PO Amoxicillin-Clavulanate or Macrolide (e.g., Clarithromycin/Azithromycin) for 5-7 days.
  * **Pseudomonas risk:** PO Ciprofloxacin or IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam.

### Ventilatory Support Algorithm
* **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP):**
  * **Indications:** Respiratory acidosis (pH ≤ 7.35 and PaCO2 ≥ 6.0 kPa) despite optimal medical therapy, severe dyspnea with clinical signs of muscle fatigue.
  * **Contraindications:** Impaired consciousness, vomiting/high aspiration risk, facial trauma, undrained pneumothorax.
* **Invasive Mechanical Ventilation (Intubation):**
  * **Indications:** Failure of NIV, respiratory arrest, severe life-threatening hypoxemia, altered mental status preventing NIV.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Non-Pharmacological Interventions
* **Smoking Cessation (戒煙):** The ONLY intervention (alongside LTOT) proven to ↓ mortality. Provide Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT), Varenicline, Bupropion, and counseling.
* **Pulmonary Rehabilitation:** Minimum 6 weeks exercise training, education, nutritional counseling. Improves quality of life and exercise tolerance.
* **Vaccinations:** Annual Influenza vaccine, Pneumococcal vaccine (PCV13/PPSV23), COVID-19 vaccine, Tdap.

### Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT - 長期氧氣治療)
* **Survival Benefit:** Improves survival if used >15 hours/day.
* **Indications (ABG taken at rest on room air in stable state):**
  1. PaO2 ≤ 7.3 kPa (55 mmHg) or SaO2 ≤ 88%.
  2. PaO2 7.4 - 8.0 kPa (55-59 mmHg) **WITH** evidence of Cor Pulmonale, Right Heart Failure, or Polycythemia (Hematocrit > 55%).

### GOLD ABE Pharmacological Step-Up Algorithm
| GOLD Group | Characteristics (Symptoms & Exacerbations) | Initial Treatment Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group A** | Few symptoms (mMRC 0-1, CAT <10), 0-1 exacerbations/yr (no hospitalization) | **SABA or SAMA** (prn) |
| **Group B** | More symptoms (mMRC ≥2, CAT ≥10), 0-1 exacerbations/yr (no hospitalization) | **LABA + LAMA** (preferred over monotherapy) |
| **Group E** | ≥2 exacerbations/yr OR ≥1 leading to hospitalization (regardless of symptoms) | **LABA + LAMA** (Consider LABA + LAMA + **ICS** if blood Eosinophils ≥ 300) |

* **Step-Up/Step-Down Rules:** Never use ICS monotherapy in COPD (↑ pneumonia risk). Triple therapy (LABA/LAMA/ICS) for frequent exacerbators with eosinophilia. PDE4 inhibitors (Roflumilast) or Macrolides (Azithromycin) can be added for severe refractory exacerbators.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### GOLD Spirometric Severity (Based on post-BD FEV1)
* **GOLD 1 (Mild):** FEV1 ≥ 80% predicted
* **GOLD 2 (Moderate):** 50% ≤ FEV1 < 80% predicted
* **GOLD 3 (Severe):** 30% ≤ FEV1 < 50% predicted
* **GOLD 4 (Very Severe):** FEV1 < 30% predicted

### Symptom Assessment Scores
* **mMRC (Modified Medical Research Council) Dyspnea Scale:**
  * 0: Breathless only with strenuous exercise.
  * 1: Breathless when hurrying on level ground or walking up a slight hill.
  * 2: Walks slower than people of same age due to breathlessness, or has to stop for breath when walking at own pace on the level.
  * 3: Stops for breath after walking ~100 meters or after a few minutes on the level.
  * 4: Too breathless to leave the house, or breathless when dressing/undressing.
* **CAT (COPD Assessment Test):** Comprehensive score 0-40. Score ≥10 indicates significant symptom burden.

### BODE Index for Prognosis
* Predicts mortality (especially all-cause and respiratory).
* **B** = BMI
* **O** = Airflow **O**bstruction (FEV1 % predicted)
* **D** = **D**yspnea (mMRC score)
* **E** = **E**xercise capacity (6-Minute Walk Distance)

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Respiratory & Malignant Complications
* **Pneumothorax:** Rupture of apical bullae (can be fatal in severe COPD).
* **Respiratory Failure:** Progressive hypoxemia and hypercapnia.
* **Lung Cancer:** Independent risk factor for bronchogenic carcinoma (shared smoking etiology and chronic inflammation).

### Cardiovascular Complications
* **Cor Pulmonale (Right Heart Failure):** Chronic alveolar hypoxia → hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction → Pulmonary Hypertension → RV hypertrophy/failure.
* **Arrhythmias:** Atrial Fibrillation (AF), Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT) (often exacerbated by Theophylline or Hypoxia).

### Metabolic & Psychiatric Complications
* **Cachexia/Sarcopenia:** Weight loss, skeletal muscle wasting.
* **Depression & Anxiety:** Extremely common due to chronic breathlessness and immobility.
* **Osteoporosis:** Multifactorial (smoking, reduced mobility, recurrent systemic steroid use).

### Drug-Induced Adverse Effects
* **Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS):** Oral candidiasis, dysphonia, ↑ risk of pneumonia.
* **Beta-Agonists (SABA/LABA):** Fine tremor, sinus tachycardia, hypokalemia.
* **Muscarinic Antagonists (SAMA/LAMA):** Dry mouth, urinary retention (caution in BPH), acute angle-closure glaucoma.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the target SpO2 for a patient with an acute exacerbation of COPD? Answer: 88-92% to prevent worsening of Type II respiratory failure.
* Viva Question: Which two therapies have been definitively proven to improve survival in COPD patients? Answer: Smoking cessation and Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT).
* Trap: Prescribing an Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) as monotherapy for a newly diagnosed COPD patient. Pearl: ICS monotherapy is strictly contraindicated in COPD due to the increased risk of pneumonia and lack of efficacy. It must always be combined with a LABA and/or LAMA.
* Pitfall: Withholding oxygen from a severely hypoxic COPD patient for fear of causing hypercapnia. Deliver controlled oxygen (Venturi mask) to achieve SpO2 88-92% and use serial ABGs to monitor PaCO2 and pH; hypoxemia kills faster than hypercapnia.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing Cor Pulmonale based solely on bilateral pitting edema. Edema in COPD can also result from poor venous return (↑ intrathoracic pressure) or concurrent left heart failure; echocardiography is required to confirm right ventricular dysfunction.
