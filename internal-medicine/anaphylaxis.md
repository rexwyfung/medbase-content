# Anaphylaxis (過敏性休克)

## 1. Clinical Definition & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Anaphylaxis
* **Anaphylaxis (過敏性休克)**: Severe, life-threatening generalized/systemic hypersensitivity reaction.
* **Core Features**: Rapid onset (minutes to hours) involving life-threatening Airway, Breathing, or Circulation (ABC) compromise, usually associated with skin/mucosal changes.

### Epidemiology & HK Context
* **Incidence**: ↑ rapidly in HK, parallel to global trends. Food allergies represent the majority of pediatric ED admissions for anaphylaxis.
* **Mortality**: Rare but highly preventable; fatalities mostly due to delayed adrenaline administration or upright positioning during the event.

## 2. Etiologies, Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms
### Common Anaphylaxis Triggers in Hong Kong
* **Foods**:
	* Seafood/shellfish (甲殼類 - extremely common in HK adults).
	* Peanut/tree nuts (common in HK children).
	* Cow's milk.
	* Egg.
* **Drugs**:
	* Beta-lactams (e.g., Penicillin/青黴素).
	* NSAIDs, radiocontrast media.
	* Neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) used in anesthesia.
* **Venom**:
	* Bee (蜜蜂) and wasp (黃蜂) stings.
* **Other**:
	* Latex.
	* Exercise-induced.
	* Idiopathic (特發性).

### Immunologic vs. Non-Immunologic Mechanisms
* **IgE-Mediated (Type I Hypersensitivity)**:
	* Classic pathway.
	* Previous sensitization → Allergen cross-links specific IgE on **Mast Cells (肥大細胞)** & **Basophils (嗜鹼性粒細胞)**.
* **Non-IgE-Mediated (Anaphylactoid)**:
	* Direct degranulation of mast cells without IgE (e.g., opioids, radiocontrast, vancomycin).
	* Clinically indistinguishable from IgE-mediated; acute management is identical.

### Mediators of Anaphylactic Shock
* **Preformed Mediators**:
	* **Histamine (組織胺)**.
	* **Tryptase (類胰蛋白酶)**.
	* Chymase.
* **Newly Synthesized Mediators**:
	* Leukotrienes (LTC4, LTD4).
	* Prostaglandins (PGD2).
	* Platelet-activating factor (PAF).
![image|500](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/7030c753f68fb8c99c3b870d022a3bd6.png)

### Systemic Pathophysiological Effects
* **Cardiovascular**:
	* Massive vasodilation → profound ↓ SVR → Distributive Shock (分佈性休克).
	* ↑ Capillary permeability → fluid shift into extravascular space (up to 35% of intravascular volume lost in 10 mins).
* **Respiratory**:
	* Smooth muscle contraction → severe bronchospasm (支氣管痙攣).
	* Mucosal edema → laryngeal edema/upper airway obstruction.
* **Integumentary**:
	* Vasodilation & ↑ permeability → Urticaria (蕁麻疹) & Angioedema (血管性水腫).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Critical History Elements in Anaphylaxis
* **Time Course**: Exact onset time relative to suspected exposure (usually 5-30 mins for IV/stings, up to 2 hrs for oral food).
* **Progression**: Rapidity of symptom evolution (rapid progression = higher risk of fatality).
* **Past History**:
	* Previous allergic reactions (severity, triggers).
	* Asthma (major risk factor for fatal outcome).
	* Current medications (beta-blockers, ACEi).

### Dermatological & Mucosal Signs
* **Skin**:
	* Generalized **Urticaria (蕁麻疹)** (hives).
	* Severe pruritus (itching).
	* Flushing (erythema).
* **Mucosa**: **Angioedema (血管性水腫)** involving lips, tongue, uvula, or periorbital regions.
* *Note*: Up to 20% of severe cases may lack cutaneous signs (especially in profound shock).

### Respiratory Signs
* **Upper Airway**:
	* **Stridor (喘鳴)**.
	* Hoarseness.
	* Dysphonia.
	* Feeling of "throat closing".
	* Excessive drooling.
* **Lower Airway**:
	* Expiratory **wheeze (哮鳴)**.
	* Tachypnea.
	* Accessory muscle use.
	* Central cyanosis (late/pre-terminal sign).

### Cardiovascular Signs
* **Hemodynamics**:
	* Profound **hypotension (低血壓)** (systolic BP < 90 mmHg or > 30% drop from baseline).
	* Compensatory **tachycardia (心搏過速)**.
* **Perfusion**:
	* Delayed capillary refill.
	* Diaphoresis.
	* Syncope/collapse.
	* Bradycardia may occur late (imminent arrest).

### Gastrointestinal & Neurological Signs
* **GI**:
	* Crampy abdominal pain.
	* Nausea.
	* Projectile vomiting.
	* Diarrhea (often seen with food/venom triggers).
* **Neuro**:
	* Altered mental status (from cerebral hypoperfusion).
	* Extreme anxiety.
	* "Feeling of impending doom" (瀕死感).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Differentials for Anaphylaxis
| Differential Diagnosis              | Key Discriminatory Features vs. Anaphylaxis                                                                                               |
| :---------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vasovagal Syncope (血管迷走神經性暈厥)**   | - Pallor (not flushed), **bradycardia** (not tachycardia), diaphoresis, no cutaneous/respiratory signs.                                   |
| **Acute Severe Asthma (急性哮喘)**      | Wheezing/dyspnea present, but **NO** urticaria, angioedema, or profound hypotension.                                                      |
| **Septic/Cardiogenic Shock**        | Slower onset, +ve infective source/fever or +ve cardiac history/ECG changes; no hives/itching.                                            |
| **Hereditary Angioedema (遺傳性血管水腫)** | C1-inhibitor deficiency. Recurrent angioedema (usually without urticaria). GI pain common. **Unresponsive** to adrenaline/antihistamines. |
| **Systemic Mastocytosis**           | Chronic disease with baseline elevated tryptase; characteristic skin lesions (urticaria pigmentosa).                                      |
| **Panic Attack**                    | Hyperventilation, tachycardia, anxiety, but normal BP, no hypoxemia, no skin signs.                                                       |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### WAO Diagnostic Criteria for Anaphylaxis
> Professor Philip Li (HKU) does not require students to memorise the WAO definition. Students are only required to know the definition of anaphylaxis to be:
> 1. Immediate
> 2. Systemic
> 3. Life-threatening
* **Diagnosis is strictly CLINICAL**. Do NOT delay treatment for any investigations.
* Anaphylaxis is highly likely if **ANY ONE** of the following 2 criteria is met:
	* **Criterion 1**: Acute onset (mins to hrs) of illness involving skin, mucosal tissue, or both (e.g., generalized hives, pruritus/flushing, swollen lips/tongue/uvula) **AND AT LEAST ONE** of the following:
		* a) Respiratory compromise (e.g., dyspnea, wheeze-bronchospasm, stridor, hypoxemia).
		* b) Reduced BP or associated symptoms of end-organ dysfunction (e.g., hypotonia, syncope, incontinence).
	* **Criterion 2**: Acute onset of **hypotension OR bronchospasm OR laryngeal involvement** after exposure to a *known or highly probable allergen* for that patient (minutes to several hours), even in the absence of typical skin involvement.

### Bedside Monitoring
* **ECG**: Continuous cardiac monitoring (ischemia, arrhythmias).
* **Vitals**:
	* Continuous SpO2.
	* Continuous non-invasive BP (q3-5 mins initially).

### Blood Investigations (Timing Critical)
* **Serum Mast Cell Tryptase (SMCT) (血清類胰蛋白酶)**: Gold standard to retrospectively confirm mast cell degranulation.
	* *Sample 1*: Best taken **1-2 hours** post-onset of symptoms (peak level).
	* *Sample 2*: 24 hours post-resolution (or baseline at convalescence) to rule out underlying mastocytosis.
	* *Formula*: Acute tryptase > [(1.2 x baseline tryptase) + 2 mcg/L] suggests anaphylaxis.
* **Routine Labs**: CBC, LRFT, ABG (if severe resp compromise/shock).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Immediate Bedside Actions & Posture
* **Positioning (CRITICAL)**:
	* **Supine with legs elevated**. DO NOT SIT UP or STAND. (Sudden upright posture → "empty vena cava syndrome" → abrupt fatal cardiac arrest).
	* Place in recovery position if vomiting; semi-recumbent ONLY if primarily severe respiratory distress and hemodynamically stable.
* **Remove Trigger**:
	* Stop IV drugs/infusions immediately.
	* Remove insect stinger.
* **Call for Help**:
	* Trigger hospital resuscitation team / senior support.

### First-Line Pharmacotherapy: Adrenaline (腎上腺素)
* **Route & Location**: **Intramuscular (IM)** injection into the anterolateral aspect of the middle third of the thigh (vastus lateralis). *Never SC (erratic absorption)*.
* **Formulation**: 1:1,000 (1 mg/mL) adrenaline.
* **Adult Dose**: **0.5 mg (0.5 mL) IM**.
* **Pediatric Doses**:
	* \> 12 years: 0.5 mg IM
	* 6 - 12 years: 0.3 mg IM
	* < 6 years: 0.15 mg IM
* **Repetition**: Repeat every **5 to 15 minutes** if no clinical improvement or if deterioration occurs.
![image|200](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/47ca8c5123bee5f161277cf19d8bec51.png)

### Resuscitation (ABCDE Protocol)
* **Airway (A)**:
	* High-flow O2 (15L/min via non-rebreather mask).
	* Early intubation (by expert anesthetist) if stridor/impending airway loss (laryngeal edema rapidly worsens).
* **Breathing (B)**:
	* Nebulized **Salbutamol (沙丁胺醇)** 5mg
	* ± Ipratropium 0.5mg for residual bronchospasm *after* adrenaline.
* **Circulation (C)**:
	* IV access (2 large bore cannulae).
	* Give rapid IV fluid bolus (1-2 Liters Normal Saline/Hartmann's) for hypotension.
* **Refractory Shock (ICU Level)**:
	* IV Adrenaline infusion (requires central line and continuous arterial BP monitoring). Start at 0.1 mcg/kg/min and titrate.
	* If patient is on **Beta-Blockers** (unresponsive to adrenaline): Give IV **Glucagon (胰高血糖素)** 1-2mg slow IV over 5 mins, followed by infusion.

### Second-Line Adjuncts (Do Not Delay Adrenaline)
* *HK HA context dictates these are strictly 2nd line for symptomatic relief (skin) or preventing late relapse, NOT for acute life-saving.*
* **Antihistamines**:
	* H1 antagonist (e.g., IV/PO Chlorpheniramine 10mg or PO Cetirizine).
	* H2 antagonist (e.g., IV Famotidine 20mg).
* **Corticosteroids**:
	* IV Hydrocortisone (氫化可的松) 100-200mg OR PO Prednisolone 50mg. (Takes 4-6 hours to act; theoretically mitigates biphasic reaction).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Discharge & Observation Protocols
* **Observation Period**:
	* Minimum **4-6 hours** post-resolution of symptoms due to risk of biphasic reaction.
	* Observe for **12-24 hours** if: severe initial reaction requiring repeated adrenaline, history of severe asthma, late night presentation, or living far from hospital.
* **Discharge Medications**:
	* Short course (3-5 days) of oral antihistamines (e.g., Cetirizine) and oral corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone).
![image|300](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/6e4d19c013e3a950b77d83a6e2ad9309.png)

### Autoinjector Prescription (EpiPen / Jext)
* **Indication**: Absolute requirement for venom/food/idiopathic anaphylaxis.
* **Dosing (EpiPen)**:
	* Body weight ≥ 30 kg: **0.3 mg** (EpiPen)
	* Body weight 15-30 kg: **0.15 mg** (EpiPen Jr)
* **Patient Education**:
	* Must be prescribed TWO devices. Train patient/family using a trainer pen.
	* Blue to the sky, orange to the thigh (EpiPen rule).
	* Hold firmly for 3 seconds. Massage site.
	* Call 999 immediately post-use.
![image|400](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/f5adb6fd95b6ddd0a15d76583d37030d.png)

### Long-Term Specialist Referral & Prevention
* **Allergy/Immunology Referral (HK HA Centers e.g., QMH, PWH)**: For definitive identification of triggers.
* **Specific Testing**: Skin Prick Testing (SPT) or specific IgE blood tests (RAST/ImmunoCAP) performed 4-6 weeks *after* the acute event (to avoid false negatives due to mast cell depletion).
* **Desensitization (Immunotherapy)**:
	* Subcutaneous Immunotherapy (SCIT) or Sublingual Immunotherapy (SLIT).
	* Highly effective for **venom allergy** (wasp/bee).
	* Emerging protocols for peanut allergy (oral immunotherapy).
* **Lifestyle**:
	* Medical alert bracelet/necklace (MedicAlert).
	* Strict trigger avoidance.
	* Provide a written Anaphylaxis Action Plan.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### World Allergy Organization (WAO) Grading of Anaphylaxis Severity
| Grade       | Severity | Clinical Description                                                           |
| :---------- | :------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Grade 1** | Mild     | Skin/mucosal tissue involvement only (hives, itching, lip swelling).           |
| **Grade 2** | Moderate | Mild respiratory/CV symptoms + skin. (Dyspnea, wheeze, N/V, dizziness).        |
| **Grade 3** | Severe   | Hypoxia (cyanosis, SpO2 ≤92%), hypotension, neurologic compromise (confusion). |
| **Grade 4** | Arrest   | Respiratory arrest or Cardiac arrest.                                          |

### Biphasic Reactions & Prognostic Factors
* **Biphasic Reaction**:
	* Recurrence of symptoms without re-exposure to the trigger.
	* Occurs in up to **20%** of patients, usually within **8-10 hours** (range 1-72 hours).
* **Poor Prognostic Factors (↑ risk of fatality/severe course)**:
	* Co-existing poorly controlled Asthma.
	* Delayed administration of IM adrenaline.
	* Upright posture during the event.
	* Use of Beta-blockers or ACE inhibitors (blunts compensatory response and adrenaline efficacy).
	* Peanut/tree nut allergy (highest mortality in food-induced).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Ischemic & Hypoxic Complications (Post-Resuscitation)
* **Neurological**: Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) leading to permanent cognitive deficits or vegetative state post-cardiac arrest.
* **Cardiovascular**: Myocardial Infarction (MI) due to profound hypotension OR excessive exogenous adrenaline.
* **Kounis Syndrome (Allergic Angina/Allergic MI)**: Acute coronary syndrome triggered by mast cell degranulation in coronary arteries (histamine-induced coronary vasospasm or atheromatous plaque rupture).
* **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) - specifically Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) due to prolonged shock state.

### Adverse Effects of Treatment (Adrenaline)
* **IM Adrenaline Expected SEs**: Transient pallor, tremor, anxiety, palpitations, headache. (Reassure patient; indicates drug is working).
* **Overdose/Inappropriate IV Push Toxicity**: Giving undiluted 1:1,000 adrenaline via IV push can cause fatal ventricular arrhythmias (VT/VF), acute myocardial infarction, hypertensive crisis, and pulmonary edema.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Withholding IM adrenaline because the patient is elderly or has known ischemic heart disease. Pearl: There is NO absolute contraindication to IM adrenaline in life-threatening anaphylaxis. The risk of untreated anaphylaxis far outweighs the risk of adrenaline-induced ischemia.
* Pitfall: Administering the 1:1000 adrenaline ampoule intravenously (IV push) in the general ward. This will cause lethal arrhythmias. Pearl: 1:1000 is ONLY for IM use. IV adrenaline must be diluted to 1:10,000 or 1:100,000 and given strictly by critical care specialists/anesthetists with continuous cardiac monitoring.
* Viva Question: You are treating a patient with severe anaphylaxis who is unresponsive to 3 doses of IM adrenaline. They are on Metoprolol. What specific pharmacological antidote should you administer? Answer: IV Glucagon (1-2mg). It bypasses the beta-receptor to ↑ intracellular cAMP, providing inotropy/chronotropy.
* Pitfall: Allowing a patient with respiratory distress and anaphylactic shock to walk to the ambulance or sit upright on the stretcher. This causes empty vena cava syndrome. Pearl: Keep the patient strictly supine with legs elevated to maximize venous return.
* Viva Question: When is the optimal time to draw blood for Serum Mast Cell Tryptase, and why is a second sample needed? Answer: Take the first sample 1-2 hours after symptom onset to capture the peak level. A second sample is needed >24 hours later (baseline) to prove the initial level was an acute rise and rule out underlying systemic mastocytosis.
