# Bronchiectasis (支氣管擴張)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Bronchiectasis
* **Core concept:** Permanent, irreversible, abnormal dilatation of one or more bronchi → chronic airway inflammation & recurrent bacterial infections.
* **Component 1:** Structural damage (elastin/cartilage destruction).
* **Component 2:** Functional failure (impaired mucociliary clearance).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Leading cause in HK/Asia:** Post-Tuberculosis (結核病) (traction bronchiectasis/parenchymal destruction).
* **Demographics:** ↑ prevalence in elderly population; female predominance in idiopathic cases.
* **CF Prevalence:** Cystic Fibrosis (CF) is exceedingly rare in the local Chinese population.

### Etiological Classification (Non-CF Bronchiectasis)
* **Post-infective (Most Common):** Prior TB, severe childhood pneumonia (Measles, Pertussis), Adenovirus.
* **Immunodeficiency:** Hypogammaglobulinemia (Common Variable Immunodeficiency - CVID), HIV.
* **Genetic/Mucociliary:** Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia (PCD) / Kartagener's Syndrome, Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency.
* **Allergic/Immunologic:** Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis (ABPA) (過敏性支氣管肺麴菌病), Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA), Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD).
* **Airway Obstruction:** Endobronchial tumor, foreign body aspiration, hilar lymphadenopathy.
* **Idiopathic:** Up to 30-50% of cases in developed nations.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Cole's "Vicious Circle" Hypothesis
* **Initial Insult:** Genetic, infectious, or immune trigger.
* **Mechanism:** Impaired mucociliary clearance → mucus stasis.
* **Colonization:** Chronic bacterial colonization in stagnant mucus.
* **Inflammatory Response:** Intense, unchecked neutrophilic inflammation (release of neutrophil elastase, IL-8, TNF-α, matrix metalloproteinases).
* **Tissue Destruction:** Breakdown of structural airway proteins (elastin, cartilage) → progressive bronchial dilatation → further clearance impairment.

### Morphological Phenotypes (Reid Classification)
* **Cylindrical (Tubular):** Smooth, uniform dilatation (mildest form).
* **Varicose:** Irregular, beaded dilatation (alternating constriction/dilatation).
* **Cystic (Saccular):** Balloon-like sacs with air-fluid levels (most severe form).

### Microbiology of Colonization
* **Early/Mild Disease:** *Haemophilus influenzae* (most common), *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*.
* **Advanced Disease:** *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (綠膿桿菌) (associated with rapid lung function decline, ↑ exacerbations, ↑ mortality).
* **Emerging Pathogens:** Non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM), MRSA.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Presenting Illness
* **Cardinal Symptom:** Chronic, daily productive cough with copious, purulent sputum (often >50ml/day).
* **Sputum Characteristics:** May separate into 3 layers upon standing (froth, saliva, purulent sediment).
* **Hemoptysis (喀血):** Ranging from blood-streaking to massive, life-threatening hemorrhage.
* **Respiratory symptoms:** Exertional dyspnea, wheezing, pleuritic chest pain.
* **Systemic symptoms:** Chronic fatigue, weight loss, low-grade fever.

### Targeted Past Medical History
* **Prior Infections:** Hx of pulmonary TB, severe childhood chest infections.
* **Systemic Clues:** Joint pain/deformity (RA), chronic diarrhea (IBD), recurrent sinusitis/otitis media (PCD), infertility/situs inversus (Kartagener's).

### General Physical Examination Signs
* **General:** Cachexia, chronically ill appearance.
* **Hands:** Clubbing (杵狀指) (classic sign of chronic suppuration/hypoxia).
* **Breath:** Halitosis (fetor oris) from purulent sputum.
* **Hemodynamics:** Signs of Cor Pulmonale (elevated JVP, right ventricular heave, peripheral edema).

### Respiratory Chest Signs
* **Auscultation:** Coarse, pan-inspiratory crepitations (crackles).
* **Location pattern:** Usually bilateral basal (idiopathic/systemic) vs. apical (typically post-TB or ABPA).
* **Wheezing:** Common, indicating co-existing airway hyperreactivity or obstruction.
* **Consolidation signs:** Dull percussion, ↑ tactile vocal fremitus, bronchial breathing (if active pneumonia or severely compressed lung).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table for Chronic Productive Cough

| Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bronchiectasis** | Copious purulent sputum daily, significant hemoptysis, clubbing, coarse crackles. |
| **Chronic Bronchitis (COPD)** | Smoker Hx, progressive exertional dyspnea, wheeze dominant, clubbing is RARE. |
| **Asthma** | Variable airflow obstruction, prominent wheeze, night cough, atopy, diurnal variation. |
| **Lung Cancer** | New/changed cough, significant weight loss, heavy smoker, localized signs, clubbing. |
| **Pulmonary TB** | Subacute onset, night sweats, fever, apical signs, travel/contact Hx. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Sputum Culture & Microscopy:** C&S for routine bacteria, Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB) smear x3 & TB culture, Fungal culture (Aspergillus).
* **ECG:** Check for Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (RVH) (p-pulmonale, right axis deviation) if cor pulmonale suspected.

### Blood Investigations
* **Baseline:** CBC (↑WBC in exacerbation, ↑Eosinophils in ABPA), CRP/ESR.
* **Immunology Screen:** Immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM) to exclude CVID.
* **ABPA Screen:** Total IgE, Aspergillus-specific IgE & IgG (precipitins).
* **Autoimmune Screen:** Rheumatoid Factor (RF), anti-CCP, ANA, ANCA (if vasculitis suspected).
* **Genetic:** Alpha-1 antitrypsin level.

### Imaging & Diagnostic Criteria
* **High-Resolution CT (HRCT) Thorax (Gold Standard for Dx):**
  * **Signet Ring Sign:** Bronchus internal diameter > adjacent pulmonary artery.
  * **Lack of Tapering:** Bronchi fail to taper as they travel peripherally.
  * **Peripheral Visibility:** Bronchi visible within 1 cm of the costal pleura.
  * **Tram-track lines:** Parallel thickened bronchial walls.
  * **Tree-in-bud appearance:** Mucus plugging in small airways.
* **CXR (Insensitive, baseline):** Tram-track opacities, ring shadows, cystic spaces (honeycombing) with air-fluid levels, atelectasis.

### Special & Invasive Tests
* **Spirometry:** Typically reveals obstructive pattern (↓ FEV1/FVC <0.70) without significant bronchodilator reversibility; restrictive pattern in advanced destruction.
* **Bronchoscopy:** Indicated if suspecting focal obstruction (tumor, foreign body), localized lobar collapse, or to obtain BAL for NTM/TB if unable to expectorate.
* **Nasal Nitric Oxide (nNO) / Saccharin Test:** Screening for Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia (PCD).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Definition of Acute Exacerbation
* **Clinical Criteria (requires ≥3 for ≥48 hours):** ↑ cough, ↑ sputum volume/purulence, ↑ dyspnea, ↑ wheeze, ↓ exercise tolerance, ↑ fatigue, hemoptysis, AND requires change in treatment.

### Initial Resuscitation & Airway Clearance
* **Oxygenation:** Titrate SpO2 to 94-98% (or 88-92% if known hypercapnic Type 2 respiratory failure).
* **Intensified Physiotherapy:** Increase frequency of Active Cycle of Breathing Techniques (ACBT), postural drainage, and flutter valve usage.

### Acute Antimicrobial Protocol (HK HA Context)
* **Rule:** Base choice on previous sputum culture sensitivities; sputum sample MUST be taken *before* starting empirical antibiotics.
* **Mild-Moderate (Outpatient):** Oral Amoxicillin/Clavulanate (Augmentin) OR Levofloxacin (if penicillin-allergic).
* **Severe / Known *Pseudomonas* Colonization:**
  * Oral Ciprofloxacin 500-750mg BD (if susceptible).
  * IV Dual Therapy (if resistant/severe): Anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam (Piperacillin-Tazobactam or Ceftazidime) ± Aminoglycoside (Amikacin).
* **Duration:** Typically 14 days (longer than standard pneumonia).

### Management of Massive Hemoptysis (Medical Emergency)
* **Resuscitation:** ABCDE approach, large-bore IV access, group & save, correct coagulopathy.
* **Positioning:** Patient positioned in lateral decubitus with the **bleeding lung DOWN** (dependent) to protect the healthy lung from blood aspiration.
* **Intervention 1:** Bronchial Artery Embolization (BAE) via Interventional Radiology (first-line definitive Tx).
* **Intervention 2:** Rigid bronchoscopy with cold saline/epinephrine lavage or balloon tamponade.
* **Intervention 3:** Emergency surgical resection (lobectomy) if BAE fails.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Step 1: Baseline Lifestyle & Non-Pharmacological Therapy
* **Airway Clearance Techniques (ACT):** Cornerstone of Tx. Physiotherapist-taught daily ACBT, postural drainage, oscillating positive expiratory pressure (OPEP) devices (Acapella, Flutter).
* **Pulmonary Rehabilitation:** Exercise training to ↑ exercise capacity.
* **Vaccinations:** Annual Influenza, Pneumococcal vaccines (PCV13/PPSV23).
* **Lifestyle:** Absolute smoking cessation.

### Step 2: Mucoactive & Inhaled Therapies
* **Mucoactive Agents:** Nebulized hypertonic saline (3% or 7%) to hydrate secretions and induce cough. (Avoid routine recombinant human DNase outside of CF).
* **Oral Mucolytics:** Carbocysteine or Bromhexine (modest benefit, well-tolerated).
* **Bronchodilators (LABA/LAMA):** Prescribed if concurrent COPD/Asthma, or demonstrated airflow reversibility/dyspnea.
* **Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS):** STRICTLY reserved for co-existing asthma or ABPA. Routine use in bronchiectasis is contraindicated due to ↑ pneumonia/NTM risk.

### Step 3: Chronic Suppressive Antimicrobial Therapy
* **Indication:** Patients with ≥3 exacerbations/year OR chronic *P. aeruginosa* colonization.
* **Long-term Macrolides:** Oral Azithromycin (阿奇黴素) 250mg 3x/week. Possesses potent anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and anti-biofilm properties. *Must ensure negative NTM sputum before initiation to prevent macrolide-resistant NTM.*
* **Inhaled Antibiotics:** Nebulized Colistin or Tobramycin. Specifically targeted for eradicating or suppressing *P. aeruginosa*.

### Step 4: Surgical Interventions
* **Surgical Resection (Lobectomy):** Considered only for highly localized disease that is refractory to medical management, or recurrent life-threatening massive hemoptysis.
* **Lung Transplantation:** For end-stage bilateral disease (FEV1 <30% predicted, severe pulmonary hypertension).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Bronchiectasis Severity Index (BSI)
* **Purpose:** Predicts future mortality, hospital admissions, and exacerbations.
* **Parameters included:** Age, BMI, FEV1% predicted, previous hospital admissions, exacerbations in prior year, MRC dyspnea score, chronic *Pseudomonas* colonization, colonization with other organisms, radiological extent (number of lobes).
* **Scoring tiers:**
  * **Mild:** 0-4 points.
  * **Moderate:** 5-8 points.
  * **Severe:** ≥9 points (High risk of mortality/hospitalization).

### FACED Score
* **Purpose:** Easier, 5-variable clinical score predicting 5-year mortality.
* **F:** FEV1% predicted.
* **A:** Age.
* **C:** Chronic colonization by *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.
* **E:** Extension (number of lobes involved on HRCT).
* **D:** Dyspnea (MRC score).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Respiratory & Cardiac Complications
* **Massive Hemoptysis:** Secondary to hypertrophy and erosion of tortuous bronchial arteries driven by chronic inflammation.
* **Respiratory Failure:** Progressive lung destruction → Type 1 or Type 2 respiratory failure.
* **Cor Pulmonale (肺心病):** Chronic hypoxia → pulmonary vasoconstriction → pulmonary hypertension → Right Ventricular Hypertrophy & Failure.

### Systemic Complications
* **Secondary (AA) Amyloidosis:** Chronic purulent inflammation → sustained ↑ Serum Amyloid A → tissue deposition → nephrotic syndrome, hepatosplenomegaly.
* **Brain Abscess:** Rare, hematogenous spread from chronic lung suppuration.

### Iatrogenic / Drug Adverse Effects
* **Macrolides (Azithromycin):** QTc prolongation (risk of Torsades de pointes), hearing loss/tinnitus, induction of macrolide-resistant NTM.
* **Aminoglycosides (IV/Nebulized):** Nephrotoxicity, irreversible ototoxicity (vestibular and cochlear).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the most important prerequisite investigation before starting a patient on long-term prophylactic Azithromycin? Answer: Sputum cultures for Non-Tuberculous Mycobacteria (NTM). Macrolide monotherapy in the presence of NTM rapidly induces resistant NTM strains, rendering standard NTM treatment regimens useless.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate hemoptysis from hematemesis? Answer: Hemoptysis is coughed up, frothy, bright red, alkaline pH, mixed with sputum; Hematemesis is vomited, non-frothy, dark red/coffee ground, acidic pH, mixed with food particles.
* Trap: Prescribing Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) universally for bronchiectasis symptoms. Pearl: ICS only has a role if there is documented co-existing asthma, COPD, or ABPA. In pure bronchiectasis, ICS increases the risk of recurrent pneumonias and NTM infections without improving lung function.
* Trap: Confusing the arterial source of hemoptysis in bronchiectasis. Pearl: Massive hemoptysis in bronchiectasis almost always originates from the high-pressure Bronchial Artery circulation (systemic), NOT the low-pressure Pulmonary Artery circulation.
* Pitfall: Sending a sputum culture after the first dose of antibiotics during an exacerbation. Sputum yield drops drastically; always collect the sputum sample prior to administering the first dose of empirical antibiotics to accurately guide definitive therapy.
