# Drug Eruptions (藥疹)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Drug Eruptions
* **Cutaneous Adverse Drug Reactions (CADR)**: Skin manifestations resulting from systemic drug administration
* **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)**: Life-threatening CADRs requiring immediate hospitalization (SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP)
* **Body Surface Area (BSA) Classification for SJS/TEN**:
  * **SJS (史提芬強生症候群)**: <10% BSA detachment
  * **SJS/TEN Overlap**: 10–30% BSA detachment
  * **TEN (毒性表皮溶解症)**: >30% BSA detachment

### Common Etiological Agents (Culprit Drugs)
* **Antibiotics**: Penicillins (青黴素), Cephalosporins, Sulfonamides (e.g., Cotrimoxazole)
* **Anticonvulsants (Aromatic)**: Carbamazepine (卡馬西平), Phenytoin (苯妥英), Phenobarbital
* **Urate-Lowering Agents**: Allopurinol (別嘌醇)
* **NSAIDs**: Piroxicam, Meloxicam, Ibuprofen
* **Others**: Nevirapine, Lamotrigine, NSAIDs, Contrast media

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **High-Risk Population**: Han Chinese possess high allele frequencies of specific HLA variants → ↑ susceptibility to SCARs
* **Carbamazepine-induced SJS/TEN**: Strongly linked to HLA-B*15:02 (Hong Kong prevalence ~8-15%)
* **Allopurinol-induced SCARs**: Strongly linked to HLA-B*58:01 (Hong Kong prevalence ~20%)

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Gell & Coombs Classification in Drug Eruptions
* **Type I (IgE-mediated)**: Immediate (minutes-hours) → Urticaria (蕁麻疹), Anaphylaxis
* **Type II (Cytotoxic)**: IgG/IgM against cell surface → Drug-induced pemphigus
* **Type III (Immune Complex)**: IgG/IgM + Ag complexes → Serum sickness, Vasculitis
* **Type IV (Delayed T-cell Mediated)**: Main mechanism for exanthems & SCARs

### Subtypes of Type IV Hypersensitivity in CADR
* **Type IVa (Th1/IFN-γ)**: Macrophage activation → Tuberculin-like reactions, Contact dermatitis
* **Type IVb (Th2/IL-4, IL-5)**: Eosinophil predominant → DRESS Syndrome, Maculopapular eruptions
* **Type IVc (Cytotoxic T-cells/Perforin/Granulysin)**: CD8+ T-cell mediated keratinocyte apoptosis → SJS/TEN, Fixed Drug Eruption (FDE), Maculopapular eruptions
* **Type IVd (T-cells/IL-8)**: Neutrophil recruitment → AGEP

### Molecular Pathogenesis of SJS/TEN
* **Drug-Antigen Presentation**: Drug binds directly to TCR or HLA (p-i concept) or acts as hapten
* **Massive Apoptosis**: CD8+ CTLs & NK cells secrete **Granulysin** (key mediator), Perforin, Granzyme B
* **Fas-FasL Interaction**: Up-regulated on keratinocytes → triggers widespread epidermal necrolysis

### Pathophysiology of DRESS Syndrome
* **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)**
* **Viral Reactivation**: Drug triggers reactivation of latent Herpesviruses (HHV-6 most common, CMV, EBV)
* **Immune Dysregulation**: Massive expansion of drug-specific T-cells + anti-viral T-cells → profound systemic inflammation & end-organ damage (esp. liver)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Chronology of Drug Eruptions (Crucial Diagnostic Clue)
* **Anaphylaxis/Urticaria**: Minutes to <24 hours
* **Fixed Drug Eruption (FDE)**: 30 mins to 8 hours (on re-exposure)
* **AGEP**: <4 days (typically 24-48 hours)
* **Morbilliform (Maculopapular) Exanthem**: 7-14 days
* **SJS/TEN**: 1-3 weeks (usually 7-21 days)
* **DRESS Syndrome**: 2-6 weeks (delayed onset is characteristic)

### Physical Signs of Morbilliform Eruptions
* **Appearance**: Blanching erythematous macules & papules
* **Distribution**: Truncal onset → spreads symmetrically to extremities
* **Absence of Danger Signs**: No mucosal involvement, no blisters, no systemic toxicity

### Physical Signs of Fixed Drug Eruption (FDE)
* **Appearance**: Sharply demarcated, round/oval dusky erythematous plaques → evolve to targetoid/bullous
* **Location**: Glans penis, lips, hands, face
* **Hallmark**: Recurs at the **exact same anatomical site** upon drug re-exposure
* **Resolution**: Leaves distinct post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation

### Physical Signs of Erythema Multiforme (EM)
* **Typical Lesions**: "Target" lesions (3 concentric zones: dusky center, pale ring, erythematous halo)
* **Atypical Lesions**: Raised, edematous, 2 zones
* **Distribution**: Acral preference (hands, feet, face) → moves centripetally
* **Mucosal**: Usually limited (EM minor) or severe in one mucosa (EM major)

### Physical Signs of SJS/TEN (史提芬強生症候群/毒性表皮溶解症)
* **Prodrome**: Influenza-like illness (fever, sore throat, malaise) for 1-3 days
* **Skin Signs**: Atypical flat targetoid lesions → flaccid blisters → extensive epidermal sloughing (wet cigarette paper appearance)
* **Nikolsky Sign**: +ve (lateral pressure on normal skin induces epidermal detachment)
* **Asboe-Hansen Sign**: +ve (pressure on intact bulla forces fluid to spread laterally)
* **Mucosal Signs**: Severe hemorrhagic erosions in ≥2 mucosal sites (oral, conjunctival, genital)

### Physical Signs of DRESS Syndrome
* **Skin Signs**: Morbilliform rash evolving into diffuse Erythroderma (紅皮症)
* **Facial Involvement**: Severe facial edema (hallmark)
* **Systemic Signs**: High fever (>38°C), generalized tender lymphadenopathy (淋巴結腫大)

### Physical Signs of AGEP
* **Acute Generalized Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP)**
* **Appearance**: Dozens/hundreds of pinpoint, sterile, non-follicular pustules on an edematous erythematous base
* **Distribution**: Intertriginous areas (axillae, groin) → rapid systemic spread
* **Systemic Signs**: Fever

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Blistering & Desquamating Rashes
* **Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS)**: Cleavage at stratum granulosum (exfoliatin toxin); strictly SPARES mucous membranes; affects infants/children
* **Pemphigus Vulgaris**: Autoimmune (anti-desmoglein); chronic onset; +ve Nikolsky
* **Bullous Pemphigoid**: Autoimmune (anti-hemidesmosome); tense bullae; -ve Nikolsky
* **Autoimmune Blistering vs SCARs**: SCARs are acute, deeply systemic, closely tied to drug timeline

### Differentials for Maculopapular/Erythematous Rashes
* **Viral Exanthems**: Measles, EBV, CMV, HIV seroconversion
* **Kawasaki Disease**: Children, coronary aneurysms, strawberry tongue
* **Secondary Syphilis**: Palmar/plantar involvement, painless chancre history

### Diagnostic Discriminators in Drug Eruptions
| Feature | Morbilliform | DRESS | SJS/TEN | AGEP | EM |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset Timeline** | 1-2 weeks | 2-6 weeks | 1-3 weeks | <4 days | <2 weeks |
| **Fever** | Rare/Low-grade | High (>38°C) | High | High | +/- |
| **Mucosal Erosions** | -ve | Rare/Mild | +++ (Severe) | -ve | + (EM Major) |
| **Facial Edema** | -ve | +++ | -ve | -ve | -ve |
| **Epidermal Sloughing**| -ve | -ve | +++ (+ve Nikolsky)| -ve | -ve |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Laboratory Investigations for CADR
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**:
  * **Eosinophilia**: >1.5 × 10^9/L (Strong indicator of DRESS)
  * **Atypical Lymphocytes**: Present in DRESS
  * **Neutrophilia**: >7 × 10^9/L (Strong indicator of AGEP)
* **Renal & Liver Function Tests (RFT, LFT)**: Transaminitis (hepatitis in DRESS), AKI
* **Infection Screen**: Blood cultures (r/o sepsis in TEN), Mycoplasma serology (triggers EM/SJS), HHV-6 PCR (DRESS)

### Skin Biopsy & Histopathology (Definitive Dx)
* **SJS/TEN**: Full-thickness epidermal necrosis, minimal dermal inflammation, subepidermal split
* **EM**: Vacuolar interface dermatitis, scattered dyskeratotic keratinocytes, dense lymphocytic infiltrate
* **DRESS**: Spongiosis, interface dermatitis, dermal eosinophilic infiltrate
* **AGEP**: Subcorneal/intraepidermal sterile pustules, papillary dermal edema
* **Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF)**: -ve in SCARs (rules out autoimmune bullous diseases)

### RegiSCAR Diagnostic Criteria for DRESS
| Criteria (Need ≥3 for Definite Dx) | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Skin Eruption** | Maculopapular rash or Erythroderma |
| **Fever** | > 38°C |
| **Lymphadenopathy** | Enlarged nodes in ≥2 distinct sites |
| **Hematological Abnormalities** | Eosinophilia (>1.5×10^9/L) OR Atypical lymphocytes |
| **Internal Organ Involvement** | Liver (most common), kidney, lung, heart |
| **Viral Reactivation** | HHV-6 detection |

### Causality Assessment: Naranjo Algorithm
* **Purpose**: Standardized tool to determine probability of adverse drug reaction (ADR)
* **Key Questions**:
  * Did ADR appear after drug given?
  * Did ADR improve when drug discontinued?
  * Did ADR reappear upon re-challenge?
  * Are alternative causes ruled out?
* **Scoring**: ≥9 (Definite), 5-8 (Probable), 1-4 (Possible), 0 (Doubtful)

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Universal First-Line Intervention for All CADRs
* **IMMEDIATE DRUG WITHDRAWAL**: Discontinue the suspected culprit drug and all non-essential medications immediately. Early withdrawal significantly ↓ mortality in SJS/TEN.

### Acute Resuscitation Protocol for SJS/TEN
* **Disposition**: Transfer to Burns Unit or ICU (if >10% BSA)
* **Barrier Nursing**: Strict strict reverse isolation, aseptic handling
* **Fluid Management**: IV crystalloids (Parkland formula adjustments, output >0.5 mL/kg/hr)
* **Temperature Regulation**: Prevent hypothermia (ambient temp 28-32°C, warmed fluids)
* **Wound Care**: Non-adherent dressings, avoid aggressive debridement, topical antiseptics (Chlorhexidine)
* **Mucosal Care**: Daily ophthalmology review (lubricants, topical steroids), mouth washes, urology consult (prevent strictures)
* **Nutrition**: Early enteral feeding via NGT (promotes healing, prevents stress ulcers)

### Pharmacological Interventions for SJS/TEN (Controversial/HA Context)
* **Systemic Corticosteroids**: Early high-dose IV pulse (Methylprednisolone) within first 48-72h ONLY. Harmful if given late (↑ sepsis risk).
* **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: High dose (2-3 g/kg over 3-4 days). Blocks Fas-FasL interaction.
* **Cyclosporine**: Emerging as preferred agent in some centers (3-5 mg/kg/day). Strong mortality benefit via inhibition of CD8+ cell activation.

### Acute Management of DRESS
* **First-line Pharmacotherapy**: Systemic corticosteroids (Oral Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day or IV equivalent)
* **Tapering Protocol**: MUST taper very slowly over 6-8 weeks. Rapid tapering → high risk of severe relapse.
* **Second-line (Steroid-resistant/Contraindicated)**: Cyclosporine or IVIG

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Avoidance & Documentation
* **Clinical Management System (CMS) Alert**: MUST register severe allergies in HK HA CMS system immediately
* **Patient Education**: Issue MedicAlert bracelet/card explicitly naming the drug and "SJS/TEN" or "DRESS"
* **Cross-Reactivity Avoidance**:
  * **Aromatic Anticonvulsants**: If reactive to Carbamazepine → avoid Phenytoin, Phenobarbital, Oxcarbazepine. Switch to non-aromatics (Levetiracetam, Valproate, Gabapentin).
  * **Penicillins**: Avoid all Penicillins and 1st-gen Cephalosporins.
  * **NSAIDs**: Avoid all COX-1 inhibitors if generalized NSAID hypersensitivity exists.

### Hong Kong HA Pre-Prescription HLA Screening Algorithms
* **HLA-B*15:02 Screening**: Mandatory for all new prescriptions of **Carbamazepine** in Han Chinese patients in HK HA. If +ve, absolutely contraindicated.
* **HLA-B*58:01 Screening**: Strongly recommended before initiating **Allopurinol**, especially in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) who are at highest risk.

### Drug Desensitization Protocols
* **Indications**: Indispensable medications with no alternatives (e.g., Penicillin for neurosyphilis, Aspirin for stent, Allopurinol for severe gout)
* **Mechanism**: Gradual incremental dosing (starting at 1/10,000th dose) to induce temporary tolerance (Type I IgE mechanisms).
* **Absolute Contraindication**: NEVER attempt desensitization in patients with a history of SCARs (SJS, TEN, DRESS). Re-challenge is universally fatal.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### SCORTEN Scale (Mortality Risk in SJS/TEN)
* Calculated within first 24 hours and day 3 of admission. 1 point for each:

| SCORTEN Parameter | Value for 1 Point |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Age** | ≥ 40 years |
| **Malignancy** | Presence of cancer |
| **Heart Rate** | ≥ 120 bpm |
| **Initial Epidermal Detachment** | ≥ 10% BSA |
| **Serum Urea (BUN)** | > 10 mmol/L (>28 mg/dL) |
| **Serum Glucose** | > 14 mmol/L (>252 mg/dL) |
| **Serum Bicarbonate** | < 20 mmol/L |

* **Mortality Correlation**: 0-1 (3%), 2 (12%), 3 (35%), 4 (58%), ≥5 (>90%)

### ALDEN Score (Algorithm of Drug Causality for Epidermal Necrolysis)
* Explicitly stratifies causality specifically for SJS/TEN based on: Time delay (index day), presence of drug in body, prechallenge/rechallenge, Naranjo probability.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of SJS/TEN
* **Acute Complications**:
  * **Sepsis/Septic Shock**: Pseudomonas and S. aureus (leading cause of death)
  * **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)**: Sloughing of bronchial epithelium
  * **Hypovolemic Shock**: Massive transepidermal fluid loss
* **Chronic/Sequelae**:
  * **Ocular**: Symblepharon (adhesion of eyelids to eyeball), synechiae, entropion, corneal scarring → **Blindness**
  * **Urogenital**: Urethral strictures, vaginal stenosis
  * **Dermatological**: Severe scarring, dyspigmentation, anonychia (loss of nails)

### Complications of DRESS Syndrome
* **Acute Visceral Failure**: Fulminant hepatic failure (most common cause of DRESS mortality), interstitial nephritis, eosinophilic myocarditis
* **Delayed Autoimmune Sequelae**: Autoimmune disease development months/years post-recovery (Autoimmune Thyroiditis, Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus). Requires long-term endocrine follow-up.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important initial management step for a patient presenting with suspected SJS/TEN? Answer: Immediate identification and cessation of the culprit drug.
* Trap: Treating suspected DRESS syndrome with a short 1-week course of steroids. Pearl: DRESS requires a prolonged systemic steroid taper over 6-8 weeks; rapid withdrawal almost always precipitates a severe relapse.
* Pitfall: Attempting drug desensitization for a patient who had Carbamazepine-induced SJS. Re-exposure or desensitization for any SCAR (SJS/TEN/DRESS) is an absolute contraindication and can trigger a fatal recurrence.
* Viva Question: A Han Chinese patient requires Carbamazepine for trigeminal neuralgia. What mandatory test is required by HK HA protocol? Answer: HLA-B*15:02 genotyping to rule out high risk for SJS/TEN.
* Trap: Confusing Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS) with TEN in a blistering patient. Pearl: SSSS strictly spares the mucous membranes and lacks a full-thickness subepidermal split on biopsy, whereas TEN heavily involves mucosa with full-thickness necrosis.
* Pitfall: Assuming a maculopapular rash occurring 2 days after starting Penicillin is a delayed drug exanthem. Type IV exanthems typically take 7-14 days to develop on first exposure; a rapid <2 day onset suggests prior sensitization or a different etiology (like AGEP).
* Viva Question: What is the primary cause of mortality in patients surviving the initial acute phase of TEN? Answer: Cutaneous infection leading to sepsis (most commonly Staph aureus and Pseudomonas).
