# Asthma (哮喘)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Asthma
* **Asthma (哮喘)**: Chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by variable and reversible expiratory airflow limitation.
* **Key feature**: Airway hyperresponsiveness (氣道高反應性) to direct/indirect stimuli.

### Etiologies and Triggers of Asthma
* **Host Factors**: Atopy (特應性) (genetic predisposition to produce IgE), obesity.
* **Environmental Allergens**: House dust mites (Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus), animal dander, cockroach antigens, fungi.
* **Occupational Sensitizers**: Isocyanates (paint sprayers), flour (baker's asthma), colophony (soldering).
* **Common Triggers**: 
* **Viral URTI**: Rhinovirus, RSV.
* **Exercise**: Esp. in cold, dry air.
* **Drugs**: Non-selective β-blockers, Aspirin/NSAIDs.
* **Irritants**: Tobacco smoke, air pollution, strong odors.

### Hong Kong Local Epidemiology for Asthma
* **Prevalence**: ~10% in children, ~5% in adults in HK.
* **Trend**: High burden of hospitalization in Hospital Authority (HA) during winter influenza seasons and poor air quality index (AQI) days.
* **Common Local Phenotypes**: Allergic (atopic) asthma is the most prevalent phenotype in HK youth.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Immune and Inflammatory Mechanisms of Asthma
* **Type I Hypersensitivity Pathway**: 
* **Sensitization**: Allergen exposure → Dendritic cells present to Th2 cells.
* **Cytokine Release**: Th2 cells secrete IL-4 (IgE production), IL-5 (Eosinophil activation), IL-13 (Mucus hypersecretion).
* **Early Phase Reaction (Minutes)**: Cross-linking of IgE on mast cells → Degranulation of preformed mediators (Histamine, Leukotrienes C4/D4/E4, Prostaglandin D2) → Acute bronchoconstriction, ↑ vascular permeability, mucosal edema.
* **Late Phase Reaction (Hours)**: Recruitment of **Eosinophils (嗜酸性粒細胞)**, basophils, neutrophils → Release of Major Basic Protein (MBP) and Eosinophil Cationic Protein (ECP) → Epithelial damage and sustained inflammation.

### Airway Remodeling in Asthma
* **Structural Changes**: Chronic persistent inflammation → irreversible changes over years.
* **Subepithelial Fibrosis**: Thickening of the basement membrane (collagen deposition).
* **Smooth Muscle Hypertrophy/Hyperplasia**: Leads to exaggerated bronchoconstriction.
* **Goblet Cell Metaplasia**: ↑ mucus production → formation of mucus plugs.
* **Angiogenesis**: ↑ airway vascularity.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Patient History for Asthma
* **Primary Symptoms**:
* **Wheeze (喘鳴)**: High-pitched, whistling sound.
* **Dyspnea**: Shortness of breath, episodic.
* **Cough**: Often dry, prominently nocturnal or early morning.
* **Chest Tightness**: Band-like sensation.
* **Symptom Patterns**: Variable over time and intensity; worsening at night/early AM; triggered by viral infections, exercise, allergens, or stress.
* **Atopic Triad**: Personal or family history of allergic rhinitis (鼻敏感), eczema (濕疹), and asthma.
* **Occupational History**: Improvement of symptoms during weekends or holidays (suggests occupational asthma).
* **Samter's Triad (AERD)**: Asthma + Aspirin sensitivity + Nasal polyposis.

### Physical Examination Findings in Asthma
* **General Appearance**: May be completely normal between flares.
* **Chest Inspection**:
* **Hyperinflation**: Barrel chest in chronic severe cases.
* **Accessory Muscle Use**: SCM, scalenes active during exacerbations.
* **Chest Auscultation**:
* **Wheezing**: Polyphonic, primarily expiratory (widespread).
* **Prolonged Expiratory Phase**: Ratio of inspiration to expiration altered.
* **Silent Chest**: Absent breath sounds in life-threatening exacerbations (no air movement).
* **Signs of Severe Respiratory Distress**:
* **Tachypnea**: Rapid breathing.
* **Tachycardia**: ↑ heart rate.
* **Pulsus Paradoxus**: ↓ systolic BP >10 mmHg during inspiration.
* **Cyanosis**: Central cyanosis (late, dire sign).
* **Inability to Complete Sentences**: Speaking in words/phrases only.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Asthma
| Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features vs Asthma |
| :--- | :--- |
| **COPD (慢性阻塞性肺病)** | Older onset (>40y), significant smoking Hx, largely irreversible airflow limitation, progressive decline. |
| **Cardiac Asthma (Heart Failure)** | Basal crepitations, elevated JVP, peripheral edema, orthopnea, prominent cardiomegaly. |
| **Bronchiectasis (支氣管擴張)** | Chronic daily copious purulent sputum, coarse pan-inspiratory crepitations, clubbing (杵狀指). |
| **Vocal Cord Dysfunction** | Inspiratory stridor rather than expiratory wheeze, poor response to bronchodilators, throat tightness. |
| **Mechanical Obstruction (Tumor/Foreign Body)** | Monophonic (single pitch) localized wheeze, sudden onset if foreign body (children/elderly). |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Asthma
* **Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) (最高呼氣流速)**:
* **Diurnal Variation**: >20% variability between morning and evening readings over 2 weeks confirms Dx.
* **Sputum Analysis**: Curschmann spirals (mucus plugs), Charcot-Leyden crystals (eosinophil breakdown products).
* **ECG**: Rule out cardiac causes; may show sinus tachycardia in acute flare.

### Blood Tests for Asthma
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Eosinophilia (>0.3 x 10^9/L or >300/µL).
* **Total Serum IgE**: Elevated in allergic phenotypes.
* **Allergen-Specific IgE (RAST/ImmunoCAP)**: Identifies specific triggers (dust mites, pollen).
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: (For acute flares) Normal/high PaCO2 indicates impending respiratory failure (fatigue).

### Imaging for Asthma
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR)**:
* **Routine**: Often normal.
* **Acute Exacerbation**: Hyperinflation (flattened hemidiaphragms).
* **Purpose**: Rule out complications (pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum) or mimics (pneumonia, heart failure).

### Lung Function Tests (Diagnostic Criteria)
* **Spirometry (肺活量測定) (Gold Standard)**:
* **Obstructive Pattern**: FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70 (or < LLN).
* **Reversibility Testing**: ↑ FEV1 > 12% AND > 200 mL from baseline after administering a short-acting bronchodilator (SABA).
* **Bronchoprovocation Testing**: 
* **Methacholine/Histamine Challenge**: For symptomatic patients with normal baseline spirometry.
* **Positive Test**: ↓ FEV1 > 20% from baseline at low provocative concentrations (PC20 ≤ 8 mg/mL).
* **Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO)**:
* **Marker of Eosinophilic Inflammation**: > 50 ppb (+ve for eosinophilic asthma), suggests good response to Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Initial Resuscitation for Acute Asthma Exacerbation
* **Oxygen Therapy**: Target SpO2 93-95% (titrate via nasal cannula or face mask).
* **Positioning**: Sit patient upright.
* **Monitoring**: Continuous pulse oximetry, cardiac monitoring, hourly PEFR (if able).

### Pharmacological Therapy for Acute Asthma
* **First-line Bronchodilators**:
* **Salbutamol (SABA)**: 2.5–5 mg via nebulizer every 15-30 mins, or continuous nebulization for severe cases.
* **Ipratropium Bromide (SAMA)**: 0.5 mg via nebulizer Q6H (added to SABA in severe/life-threatening attacks).
* **Systemic Corticosteroids**:
* **Prednisolone**: 40-50 mg PO daily for 5-7 days (preferred if patient can swallow).
* **Hydrocortisone**: 100-200 mg IV Q6H (if unable to tolerate PO or life-threatening).
* **Second-line Therapies**:
* **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4)**: 1.2-2.0 g IV over 20 mins (for severe/life-threatening asthma not responding to initial Rx).

### Refractory/Life-Threatening Asthma Management
* **Intravenous Bronchodilators**:
* **Aminophylline**: Loading dose 5 mg/kg IV (omit if patient takes PO theophylline), followed by continuous infusion. Requires ECG and level monitoring.
* **Ventilatory Support**:
* **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV/BiPAP)**: Controversial, use cautiously in ICU/HDU to bridge to intubation.
* **Invasive Mechanical Ventilation**: Indications include coma, respiratory arrest, worsening acidosis (pH <7.3), exhausting patient (rising PaCO2). 
* **Ventilator Strategy**: Permissive hypercapnia, low tidal volumes, prolonged expiratory time to avoid dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Lifestyle and Trigger Avoidance in Asthma
* **Smoking Cessation**: Crucial to prevent accelerated lung function decline.
* **Allergen Mitigation**: Wash bedding in hot water, remove carpets, use HEPA filters, avoid furry pets.
* **Vaccination**: Annual Influenza vaccine, Pneumococcal vaccine.
* **Weight Management**: Diet and exercise for obese patients to improve asthma control.

### Chronic Pharmacological Algorithm (GINA Stepwise Approach)
| GINA Step | Preferred Reliever (Hong Kong HA Protocol) | Daily Maintenance Controller Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** (Infrequent symptoms) | Low-dose ICS-Formoterol PRN | Low-dose ICS-Formoterol taken PRN for symptom relief (MART approach). |
| **Step 2** (Symptoms >2x/mth) | Low-dose ICS-Formoterol PRN | Daily low-dose ICS OR PRN low-dose ICS-Formoterol. |
| **Step 3** (Symptoms most days) | Low-dose ICS-Formoterol PRN | Daily low-dose ICS-LABA (e.g., Budesonide/Formoterol [Symbicort]). |
| **Step 4** (Daily symptoms/awakenings) | Low-dose ICS-Formoterol PRN | Daily medium-dose ICS-LABA. |
| **Step 5** (Refractory/Severe) | Low-dose ICS-Formoterol PRN | Daily high-dose ICS-LABA + refer to respiratory physician for add-on therapies. |

### Advanced / Biologic Therapies for Severe Asthma (Step 5)
* **LAMA Addition**: Tiotropium (Spiriva) via Respimat.
* **Anti-IgE Therapy**: Omalizumab (Xolair) SC injection (for severe allergic asthma with ↑ IgE).
* **Anti-IL-5 Therapy**: Mepolizumab (Nucala), Benralizumab (for severe eosinophilic asthma).
* **Anti-IL-4/IL-13 Receptor**: Dupilumab.
* **Oral Corticosteroids (OCS)**: Low-dose maintenance Prednisolone (Last resort due to toxicity).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Acute Asthma Exacerbation Severity Scoring
| Severity Category | Clinical and Objective Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mild / Moderate** | PEF >50% best/predicted, speaking in phrases, RR < 25/min, HR < 110 bpm. |
| **Severe** | PEF 33-50% best/predicted, cannot complete sentences, RR ≥ 25/min, HR ≥ 110 bpm. |
| **Life-Threatening** | PEF <33% best/predicted, SpO2 <92%, Silent chest, cyanosis, feeble respiratory effort, bradycardia/arrhythmia, hypotension, exhaustion, confusion/coma, Normal or ↑ PaCO2. |
| **Near-Fatal** | Raised PaCO2 and/or requiring mechanical ventilation with raised inflation pressures. |

### Asthma Control Test (ACT) Scoring
| ACT Score | Interpretation for Chronic Management |
| :--- | :--- |
| **20 - 25** | Well-controlled asthma. Continue current step. |
| **16 - 19** | Not well-controlled. Consider stepping up therapy. |
| **≤ 15** | Very poorly controlled. Immediate step-up and specialist review indicated. |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications of Asthma
* **Respiratory Failure**: Type 2 (hypercapnic) in late exhaustion phase.
* **Barotrauma**: Pneumothorax (氣胸), pneumomediastinum due to air trapping and ruptured bullae.
* **Airway Remodeling**: Fixed airflow obstruction mimicking COPD over decades.
* **Cor Pulmonale**: Rare, but can occur in severe, long-standing cases with fixed obstruction.

### Adverse Effects of Asthma Medications
* **Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)**:
* **Local**: Oral candidiasis (thrush - advise rinsing mouth after use), dysphonia (vocal cord myopathy).
* **Systemic (High dose)**: Osteopenia, bruising, cataract, suppression of HPA axis.
* **Beta-2 Agonists (SABA/LABA)**:
* **Neurological**: Fine skeletal muscle tremor.
* **Cardiovascular**: Tachycardia, palpitations.
* **Metabolic**: Hypokalemia (due to intracellular K+ shift).
* **Systemic Corticosteroids (Prednisolone)**:
* **Acute**: Hyperglycemia, insomnia, psychosis, acute peptic ulcer disease.
* **Chronic**: Cushingoid facies, weight gain, osteoporosis, hypertension, immunosuppression.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the most ominous ABG finding in a patient with a severe asthma exacerbation? Answer: A normal or elevated PaCO2. Early in an attack, hyperventilation causes low PaCO2 (respiratory alkalosis). A rising PaCO2 means the patient is exhausting and impending respiratory failure is imminent.
* Trap: Assuming a "silent chest" means the asthma attack has resolved. Pearl: A silent chest indicates life-threatening bronchospasm with insufficient air movement to even generate a wheeze; this patient requires immediate escalation of treatment and possible intubation.
* Pitfall: Prescribing a beta-blocker to a patient with a history of asthma. Always check the patient's medical history before prescribing beta-blockers (even cardioselective ones like Bisoprolol can trigger fatal bronchospasm in susceptible asthmatics); use calcium channel blockers instead if rate control is needed.
* Viva Question: Why do we use ICS-Formoterol as a PRN reliever rather than Salbutamol alone in modern guidelines (GINA)? Answer: Formoterol has a rapid onset of action equivalent to Salbutamol, but combined with an ICS, it simultaneously treats the underlying airway inflammation during a flare, significantly reducing the risk of severe exacerbations compared to SABA monotherapy.
* Trap: Reassuring a patient based solely on a normal chest X-ray during an acute asthma flare. Pearl: The primary purpose of a CXR in acute asthma is NOT to diagnose asthma, but to rule out complications like pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, or consolidation that might alter your management.
* Pitfall: Discharging a patient from the A&E after an acute asthma attack without a short course of oral corticosteroids. Always prescribe a 5-7 day course of oral Prednisolone (e.g., 40mg daily) to prevent early relapse.
