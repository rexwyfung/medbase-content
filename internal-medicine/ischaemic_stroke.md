# Ischaemic Stroke & TIA (缺血性中風與短暫性腦缺血發作)

## 1. Clinical Definition & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Stroke and TIA
* **Stroke (中風)**: Sudden onset of focal (or global) neurological deficit of presumed vascular origin lasting >24 hours or leading to death.
* **TIA (短暫性腦缺血發作)**: Transient episode of neurological dysfunction caused by focal brain, spinal cord, or retinal ischaemia, without acute infarction on imaging (tissue-based definition) or lasting <24h (classic time-based definition).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Incidence**: 4th leading cause of death in HK; leading cause of adult disability.
* **Subtypes**: ~80% ischaemic, ~20% haemorrhagic (higher ICH rate in Chinese vs Western populations).
* **Prevalence**: ↑ intracranial atherosclerosis (ICAS) in Chinese populations compared to extracranial carotid atherosclerosis in Caucasians.

## 2. Etiologies, Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Etiological Classification of Ischaemic Stroke (TOAST Criteria)
* **Large Artery Atherosclerosis (大血管動脈粥樣硬化)**: >50% stenosis of major extra/intracranial artery (e.g., ICA, MCA).
* **Cardioembolism (心源性栓塞)**: Thrombus from heart (e.g., **Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動)**, prosthetic valve, recent MI, LV thrombus).
* **Small Vessel Occlusion (小血管阻塞 / 腔隙性中風)**: Lipohyalinosis of deep penetrating arteries → Lacunar infarcts.
* **Stroke of Other Determined Aetiology**: Dissection, vasculitis, hypercoagulable states, Moyamoya disease.
* **Stroke of Undetermined Aetiology**: Cryptogenic (no cause found after extensive workup) or embolic stroke of undetermined source (ESUS).

### The Ischaemic Core and Penumbra
* **Ischaemic Core**: Area of severe blood flow reduction (<10-12 mL/100g/min) → immediate irreversible necrotic cell death.
* **Ischaemic Penumbra (缺血半暗帶)**: Surrounding rim of moderate ischaemia (12-20 mL/100g/min) → electrically silent but structurally intact. 
* **Target**: Penumbra is functionally impaired but salvageable by rapid reperfusion (thrombolysis/thrombectomy).

### Cellular Ischaemic Cascade
* **Energy Failure**: ↓ ATP production → Na+/K+ ATPase pump failure.
* **Cytotoxic Oedema**: Intracellular Na+ and Ca2+ influx → cellular swelling.
* **Excitotoxicity**: Massive release of **Glutamate (穀氨酸)** → NMDA receptor activation → massive Ca2+ influx.
* **Free Radical Generation**: Activation of lipases, proteases, endonucleases → reactive oxygen species (ROS) → mitochondrial breakdown.
* **Apoptosis/Necrosis**: Irreversible membrane damage and cell death.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Critical Stroke History (Onset and Progression)
* **Time of Onset (Last Known Well Time)**:
	* Most critical question;
	* Determines thrombolysis/EVT eligibility.
* **Wake-up Stroke**: Time of onset is when patient went to sleep (unless identified by specific imaging mismatch).
* **Symptom Evolution**: Sudden maximal onset (embolic) vs stepwise progression (lacunar/atherothrombotic).
* **Associated Symptoms**:
	* Seizures (mimic vs complication).
	* Headache/vomiting (suggests ICH or ↑ ICP).

### Bamford / OCSP Clinical Classification of Stroke Syndromes
| Subtype                                 | Anatomic Territory | Clinical Criteria (MUST KNOW)                                                                                                                   |
| :-------------------------------------- | :----------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TACI** (Total Anterior Circulation)   | MCA / ICA          | All 3 of:<br>1. Higher cortical dysfunction (dysphasia/visuospatial)<br>2. Homonymous hemianopia<br>3. Contralateral hemimotor/sensory deficit. |
| **PACI** (Partial Anterior Circulation) | MCA / ACA          | - 2 of 3 TACI criteria.<br>- OR isolated higher cortical dysfunction.<br>- OR restricted motor/sensory deficit.                                 |
| **LACI** (Lacunar)                      | Deep perforators   | - Pure motor, Pure sensory, Sensorimotor, or Ataxic hemiparesis.<br>- NO cortical signs.                                                        |
| **POCI** (Posterior Circulation)        | Vertebrobasilar    | - Brainstem/cerebellar signs.<br>- OR isolated hemianopia. <br>- OR crossed deficits (ipsilateral CN palsy + contralateral motor deficit).      |

### Focused Neurological Examination Signs
* **Vital Signs**: Note extremes of BP, irregular pulse (AF).
* **Vascular**: Carotid bruits (atherosclerosis).
* **Cortical Signs**:
	* **Aphasia (失語症)** (Broca's/Wernicke's).
	* Neglect.
	* Apraxia.
	* Agnosia.
* **Visual**:
	* Visual field defects (e.g., homonymous hemianopia).
	* Gaze palsy (eyes deviate *towards* hemispheric lesion, *away* from brainstem lesion).
* **Motor/Sensory**:
	* UMN facial nerve palsy (spares forehead).
	* Hemiparesis.
	* Hemisensory loss.
* **Brainstem/Cerebellar**:
	* Nystagmus, diplopia.
	* Dysarthria, dysphagia.
	* Ataxia.
	* Horner's syndrome.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Common Stroke Mimics and Discriminators
| Mimic | Discriminatory Features (Why it's not a stroke) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypoglycaemia (低血糖)** | Sweating, tremors, rapid resolution with IV Dextrose. Must check capillary blood glucose (CBG) in ALL suspected strokes. |
| **Todd's Paresis (Seizure)** | Witnessed seizure prior to focal deficit; usually resolves <48h. |
| **Space-Occupying Lesion (Tumour/Abscess)** | Gradual, progressive onset over weeks/months; headache, papilloedema. |
| **Hemiplegic Migraine** | Gradual aura spreading over minutes, severe headache, +ve family history. |
| **Bell's Palsy (LMN CN VII)** | Weakness includes forehead/brow (UMN stroke spares upper face). |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Initial Stroke Investigations
* **Bedside**:
	* CBG (mandatory to exclude hypoG).
	* ECG (rule out AF, AMI).
* **Bloods**:
	* CBP.
	* LRFT (baseline before drugs).
	* PT/APTT/INR (rule out coagulopathy/monitor warfarin).
	* Lipid panel.
	* HbA1c.
	* Troponin.

### Acute Stroke Neuroimaging Pathways
* **Non-Contrast CT Brain (NCCT)**:
	* 1st line. Rapid.
	* Excludes haemorrhage.
	* May show early ischaemic signs (loss of insular ribbon, blurred basal ganglia, hyperdense MCA sign).
* **CT Angiogram (CTA) Head & Neck**:
	* Identifies large vessel occlusion (LVO) for thrombectomy planning.
* **CT Perfusion (CTP) / MRI Diffusion-Perfusion**:
	* Identifies core/penumbra mismatch for delayed time-window interventions (e.g., wake-up strokes).
* **MRI Brain (DWI/ADC)**:
	* Gold standard for acute ischaemia.
	* Bright on DWI, dark on ADC map within minutes of onset.

### Stroke Aetiology Screen (Post-Acute)
* **Carotid Doppler Ultrasound**: Assesses for extra-cranial ICA stenosis >50%.
* **Echocardiogram (TTE/TOE)**: Checks for structural heart disease, PFO, LA appendage thrombus.
* **24h-72h Holter Monitor**: Detects paroxysmal AF (if resting ECG is normal).
* **Young Stroke Screen (<50y)**:
	* ANA, ANCA, APLA.
	* Thrombophilia screen (Protein C/S, Factor V Leiden, ATIII).
	* Syphilis serology.
	* Toxicology.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### General Acute Stroke Resuscitation & BP Targets
* **Airway**: NBM until swallowing assessed by SLT.
* **Breathing**: O2 only if SpO2 <94%.
* **Blood Pressure Targets (Strict!)**:
	* **Not for thrombolysis**: ONLY treat if BP >220/120 mmHg (permissive hypertension to perfuse penumbra).
	* **Prior to thrombolysis**: Must lower to <185/110 mmHg (Labetalol IV / Urapidil IV).
	* **Post-thrombolysis**: Maintain <180/105 mmHg for 24h to prevent ICH.

### Acute Intravenous Thrombolysis Protocol
* **Agent**: IV Alteplase (r-tPA) 0.9 mg/kg (10% as bolus, 90% over 1h) or IV Tenecteplase.
* **Time Window**: Within **4.5 hours** of symptom onset (or last known well).
* **Absolute Contraindications**:
	* History of ICH.
	* Current SAH.
	* Active internal bleeding.
	* Recent intracranial/spinal surgery <3mo.
	* BP consistently >185/110.
	* Platelets <100.
	* Current therapeutic DOAC/Warfarin (INR >1.7).
	* Extensive infarction on CT (>1/3 MCA territory).

### Endovascular Thrombectomy (EVT) Protocol
* **Indication**: Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO) in anterior circulation (ICA, proximal MCA M1/M2).
* **Time Window**:
	* Within **6 hours** of onset (standard).
	* Can be extended up to **24 hours** if favourable perfusion mismatch on CTP/MRI (DAWN/DEFUSE-3 criteria).
* **Integration**:
	* Give IV Alteplase first if eligible.
	* Do NOT delay EVT to wait for Alteplase effect.

### Acute Antiplatelet Loading Strategy
* **Timing**: Give immediately IF neuroimaging rules out ICH AND patient is NOT receiving thrombolysis.
* **Post-Thrombolysis Timing**: Delay antiplatelets for **24 hours** post-tPA (repeat NCCT first to exclude haemorrhagic transformation).
* **Regimen**: Aspirin 300mg PO STAT, then continue daily.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Secondary Prevention: Antithrombotic Algorithms
| Stroke Aetiology                                | Chronic Antithrombotic Regimen                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| :---------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Non-Cardioembolic (Atherosclerotic/Lacunar)** | - **Single Antiplatelet**: Clopidogrel 75mg daily OR Aspirin 80mg daily.<br>- **Minor Stroke (NIHSS ≤3) or High-Risk TIA (ABCD2 ≥4)**: Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT - Aspirin + Clopidogrel) for 21 days, then switch to SAPT. |
| **Cardioembolic (e.g., Atrial Fibrillation)**   | - **Oral Anticoagulation (OAC)**: DOACs (Apixaban, Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban) preferred over Warfarin.<br>- Antiplatelets are contraindicated alongside OAC unless specific cardiac stent indications exist.                        |

### Secondary Prevention: Vascular Risk Factor Modification
* **Lipid Lowering**:
	* High-intensity Statin (e.g., Atorvastatin 40-80mg or Rosuvastatin 20-40mg) target LDL <1.8 mmol/L (or <1.4 if recurrent).
* **Hypertension**:
	* Restart/titrate antihypertensives (ACEi, Indapamide) after acute phase (usually 3-7 days post-stroke).
	* Target <130/80 mmHg.
* **Diabetes**:
	* Optimal glycaemic control (HbA1c <7%).
* **Lifestyle**:
	* Smoking cessation.
	* Diet (Mediterranean).
	* CPAP if obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA).

### Carotid Artery Revascularization Protocol
* **Indication**: Symptomatic severe (70-99%) or moderate (50-69%) extracranial ICA stenosis.
* **Contraindication**: Near-occlusion or complete 100% occlusion.
* **Procedures**:
	* Carotid Endarterectomy (CEA) is 1st line.
	* Carotid Artery Stenting (CAS) if anatomically inaccessible or high surgical risk.
* **Timing**: Perform within 2 weeks of the index event.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### ABCD2 Score for TIA Risk Stratification
* Predicts 2-day risk of stroke after TIA.

| Variable              | Criteria & Points                                                             |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A**ge               | ≥60 years (1 pt)                                                              |
| **B**P                | SBP ≥140 or DBP ≥90 mmHg (1 pt)                                               |
| **C**linical features | - Unilateral weakness (2 pts) <br>- Speech impairment without weakness (1 pt) |
| **D**uration          | - ≥60 mins (2 pts) <br>- 10-59 mins (1 pt)                                    |
| **D**iabetes          | Present (1 pt)                                                                |
* **Scoring**: 0-3 (Low risk), 4-5 (Moderate risk), 6-7 (High risk).

### National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS)
* Quantifies stroke severity (0 to 42 points).
* **Score Interpretation**:
	* 1-4: Minor stroke
	* 5-15: Moderate stroke
	* 16-20: Moderate-severe
	* \>20: Severe stroke

### Modified Rankin Scale (mRS) for Functional Prognosis
* Measures degree of disability post-stroke.
* Target for good outcome = mRS 0-2.
	* 0: No symptoms.
	* 1: Symptoms present, no significant disability.
	* 2: Slight disability (unable to do all previous activities, but independent).
	* 3: Moderate disability (needs some help, walks independently).
	* 4: Moderately severe disability (unable to walk/care for self without assistance).
	* 5: Severe disability (bedridden, incontinent).
	* 6: Dead.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological Complications of Ischaemic Stroke
* **Haemorrhagic Transformation**:
	* Especially large territorial strokes or post-thrombolysis.
* **Malignant MCA Syndrome (Cerebral Oedema)**:
	* Massive swelling causing midline shift & herniation.
	* Occurs days 2-5.
	* Requires emergent decompressive hemicraniectomy.
* **Post-Stroke Seizures**:
	* Cortical strokes.
	* Require antiepileptics (e.g., Levetiracetam).
* **Vascular Dementia**:
	* Stepwise cognitive decline with recurrent infarcts.

### Systemic & Medical Complications Post-Stroke
* **Aspiration Pneumonia**:
	* Due to dysphagia.
	* Prevent via early SLT assessment and NGT feeding if unsafe swallow.
* **VTE (DVT / PE)**:
	* High risk due to immobility.
	* Rx with intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) initially; chemical prophylaxis (LMWH) if no haemorrhage.
* **Pressure Sores / Contractures / Frozen Shoulder**:
	* Requires early physiotherapy and occupational therapy.
* **Post-Stroke Depression**:
	* Affects ~30%.
	* Rx with SSRIs.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)
* Viva Question: What is the target door-to-needle time for acute stroke thrombolysis? Answer: Less than 60 minutes (HA target is increasingly aiming for <45 mins).
* Trap: Automatically lowering blood pressure to normal levels in an acute stroke patient not receiving thrombolysis. Pearl: Permissive hypertension (up to 220/120) is required to maintain perfusion to the ischaemic penumbra; lowering it worsens infarction.
* Pitfall: Giving Aspirin immediately upon patient arrival. Always wait for NCCT brain to exclude intracerebral haemorrhage before administering any antiplatelets.
* Viva Question: A patient with AF presents with a TIA. When do you start anticoagulation? Answer: Immediately for TIA. For confirmed stroke, delay anticoagulation (e.g., 1-3-6-12 day rule) depending on infarct size to prevent haemorrhagic transformation.
* Trap: Relying solely on a normal CT brain to rule out acute ischaemic stroke. Pearl: NCCT is highly insensitive in the first few hours; its primary role is to rule out haemorrhage.
* Pitfall: Reversing Warfarin in a patient with acute ischaemic stroke to give thrombolysis. This is contraindicated; if INR >1.7, thrombolysis cannot be given.
