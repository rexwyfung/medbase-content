# Adrenal Insufficiency (腎上腺功能不全)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Adrenal Insufficiency
* **Definition**: Inadequate secretion of glucocorticoids (cortisol) ± mineralocorticoids (aldosterone) & androgens from adrenal cortex.
* **Classification**: 
  * **Primary (Addison's Disease)**: Primary adrenal gland failure (all 3 cortical layers destroyed).
  * **Secondary**: Pituitary failure (↓ ACTH).
  * **Tertiary**: Hypothalamic failure (↓ CRH).

### Etiologies of Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease)
* **Autoimmune Adrenalitis**: Most common overall (70-90%). Isolated or part of Autoimmune Polyendocrine Syndromes (APS Type 1 & 2).
* **Infectious**: Tuberculosis (結核病) (historically most common, still highly relevant in HK elderly), HIV/AIDS (CMV, MAC), Fungal (Histoplasmosis).
* **Infiltrative/Neoplastic**: Metastases (lung, breast, melanoma), Amyloidosis, Haemochromatosis.
* **Vascular**: Bilateral adrenal hemorrhage (Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome 2° to meningococcemia, anti-phospholipid syndrome, anticoagulation).
* **Genetic/Metabolic**: Adrenoleukodystrophy (X-linked).

### Etiologies of Secondary & Tertiary Adrenal Insufficiency
* **Iatrogenic**: Exogenous glucocorticoid withdrawal (most common cause of AI globally & in HK).
* **Pituitary/Hypothalamic Disease**: Pituitary macroadenomas, craniopharyngioma, apoplexy (Sheehan syndrome), irradiation, trauma, infiltrative (sarcoidosis).

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong Context)
* **Prevalence**: Rare but life-threatening. Autoimmune predominant in young females.
* **HK Specifics**: High historical prevalence of endemic TB means post-TB adrenal calcification remains a significant cause of Primary AI in older local populations. TCM (Traditional Chinese Medicine) adulterated with unlabelled steroids is a hidden cause of iatrogenic Secondary AI upon abrupt cessation.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Primary Adrenal Insufficiency
* **Zonal Destruction**: Autoimmune/infiltrative processes destroy >90% of adrenal cortex (Zona Glomerulosa, Fasciculata, Reticularis).
* **Hormonal Deficits**: 
  * ↓ Cortisol (loss of stress response, ↓ gluconeogenesis).
  * ↓ Aldosterone (↓ renal Na+ reabsorption, ↓ K+/H+ excretion).
  * ↓ Androgens (loss of DHEA).
* **Feedback Mechanism**: Loss of cortisol negative feedback → ↑ Hypothalamic CRH → ↑ Pituitary ACTH & Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) cleavage.
* **Hyperpigmentation**: POMC cleavage yields ACTH + Melanocyte-Stimulating Hormone (MSH) → ↑ melanin synthesis in skin/mucosa.

### Pathophysiology of Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency
* **Axis Suppression**: Pituitary pathology or exogenous steroids suppress ACTH production.
* **Hormonal Deficits**: 
  * ↓ ACTH → ↓ Cortisol & ↓ Androgens.
  * **NORMAL Aldosterone**: Zona glomerulosa is preserved as it is primarily regulated by the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) and serum K+, NOT ACTH.
* **Feedback Mechanism**: ↓ ACTH & POMC → ↓ MSH → NO hyperpigmentation.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Symptoms of Glucocorticoid Deficiency (Seen in 1° & 2° AI)
* **Constitutional**: Profound fatigue, lethargy, anorexia, unexplained weight loss.
* **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea, vomiting, vague abdominal pain, diarrhea/constipation.
* **Psychiatric**: Depression, apathy, cognitive impairment.
* **Musculoskeletal**: Myalgia, arthralgia.

### Symptoms of Mineralocorticoid & Androgen Deficiency
* **Mineralocorticoid Deficiency (Primary AI ONLY)**: Salt craving (desire for salty foods/fluids), postural dizziness, syncope.
* **Androgen Deficiency**: Loss of axillary/pubic hair, ↓ libido (prominent in females; males rely on testicular testosterone).

### Physical Signs of Primary Adrenal Insufficiency
* **Hyperpigmentation (色素沉著)**: Pronounced in sun-exposed areas, friction zones (palmar creases, knuckles), recent scars, and mucosal surfaces (buccal mucosa, gums).
* **Hemodynamics**: Postural hypotension (體位性低血壓) (systolic drop >20 mmHg upon standing), tachycardia.
* **Skin**: Vitiligo (白癜風) (if concurrent autoimmune etiology).

### Physical Signs of Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency
* **Skin**: Pallor (alabaster-like skin) due to lack of MSH. NO hyperpigmentation.
* **Hemodynamics**: Postural hypotension is rare (aldosterone intact).
* **Neurological/Endocrine**: Bitemporal hemianopia (pituitary mass effect), signs of other pituitary hormone deficits (e.g., absent axillary hair, ↓ testicular volume, galactorrhea).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Features: Primary vs Secondary AI
| Feature | Primary AI (Addison's) | Secondary AI (Central) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hyperpigmentation** | +ve | -ve |
| **Salt Craving** | +ve | -ve |
| **Postural Hypotension** | Prominent | Rare |
| **Associated Signs** | Vitiligo, other autoimmune signs | Visual field defects, other pituitary deficits |

### Differentials for Chronic Fatigue & Weight Loss
* **Malignancy/Occult Infection**: Differentiated by systemic symptoms, organ-specific signs, +ve inflammatory markers.
* **Anorexia Nervosa**: Distorted body image, primary amenorrhea, bradycardia (vs AI which often has tachycardia).
* **Gastrointestinal Malabsorption (e.g., Celiac)**: Chronic diarrhea, specific autoantibodies, normal adrenal axis.

### Differentials for Hyperpigmentation
* **Haemochromatosis**: "Bronze diabetes", hepatomegaly, arthropathy (MCP joints).
* **Thyrotoxicosis**: Goitre, tremor, hyperreflexia, heat intolerance.
* **Heavy Metal Toxicity (e.g., Arsenic, Silver)**: Specific exposure history, neuropathy, nail changes (Mees' lines).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG)**: Fasting hypoglycemia (↓ gluconeogenesis).
* **ECG**: Arrhythmias, signs of hyperkalemia (peaked T waves, widened QRS, loss of P waves).

### Blood Investigations for Adrenal Insufficiency
* **Basic Panel**: 
  * **U&E**: Hyponatremia (↓ Na+), Hyperkalemia (↑ K+) & Uremia (↑ Urea due to dehydration) — classic for Primary AI. Normal K+ in Secondary AI.
  * **CBP**: Normocytic normochromic anemia, eosinophilia.
* **Basal Endocrine Screen (8 AM)**:
  * **Serum Cortisol**: <100 nmol/L strongly suggests AI; >400 nmol/L excludes AI.
  * **Plasma ACTH**: ↑↑ markedly in Primary AI; ↓ or inappropriately normal in Secondary AI.
* **Mineralocorticoid Axis (Primary AI suspected)**: ↑ Plasma Renin Activity (PRA), ↓ Serum Aldosterone.
* **Etiological Markers**: Adrenal autoantibodies (21-hydroxylase Abs) +ve in autoimmune Addison's.

### Dynamic Endocrine Testing (Short Synacthen Test)
* **Short Synacthen Test (SST) (短效促腎上腺皮質激素刺激試驗)**: Gold standard diagnostic test.
| Step | Action/Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Baseline** | Draw basal serum cortisol (T=0) |
| **Stimulation** | Administer 250mcg Tetracosactide (Synacthen) IM/IV |
| **Measurement** | Draw serum cortisol at T=30 and/or T=60 mins |
| **Normal Response** | Peak cortisol ≥ 430-500 nmol/L (assay dependent) |
| **Interpretation** | Suboptimal peak confirms Adrenal Insufficiency |

### Imaging Investigations
* **CT Adrenals (Non-contrast)**: Indicated if 21-hydroxylase Abs -ve. Findings: Enlarged/calcified (TB/hemorrhage/metastases) OR small/atrophic (autoimmune).
* **MRI Pituitary (With Gadolinium)**: Indicated if Secondary AI confirmed (↓ ACTH, abnormal SST) to rule out adenomas/apoplexy.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Triggers of Adrenal Crisis (腎上腺危象)
* **Precipitating Factors**: Acute intercurrent infection (gastroenteritis, pneumonia), surgery, trauma, acute myocardial infarction, abrupt cessation of chronic steroid therapy.

### Acute Resuscitation Protocol for Adrenal Crisis
* **IMMEDIATE ACTION**: DO NOT delay treatment to perform diagnostic tests (e.g., SST). Draw baseline cortisol/ACTH if instantly available, then treat immediately.
* **1. Glucocorticoid Replacement**: IV Hydrocortisone (氫化可的松) 100 mg stat bolus, followed by 50 mg IV/IM Q6H (or 200 mg/24hr continuous IV infusion).
* **2. Fluid Resuscitation**: Rapid IV Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) infusion (e.g., 1L over 1 hour, then guided by hemodynamics/urine output) to correct hypovolemia and hyponatremia.
* **3. Hypoglycemia Correction**: Add IV Dextrose (e.g., 10% Dextrose) if CBG is low.
* **4. Hyperkalemia Management**: Usually resolves with IV fluids and hydrocortisone. Avoid aggressive insulin/dextrose unless ECG changes are severe, to prevent exacerbating hypoglycemia.
* **5. Treat Underlying Trigger**: Empirical IV antibiotics if sepsis suspected.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Glucocorticoid Replacement Therapy
* **Agent of Choice**: Oral Hydrocortisone (short-acting, closely mimics circadian rhythm).
* **Regimen**: 15–25 mg daily in divided doses (e.g., 10 mg upon waking, 5 mg at lunch, 5 mg in late afternoon). Avoid late evening doses to prevent insomnia.
* **Alternatives**: Prednisolone (3-5 mg OD) if compliance with TDS hydrocortisone is poor.
* **Monitoring**: Clinical assessment (weight, BP, energy levels, absence of Cushingoid features). Routine ACTH or cortisol levels are NOT useful for dose titration.

### Chronic Mineralocorticoid Replacement Therapy (Primary AI ONLY)
* **Agent of Choice**: Oral Fludrocortisone (氟氫可的松).
* **Regimen**: 50–200 mcg OD.
* **Monitoring**: Titrate based on clinical signs (postural BP, peripheral edema) and labs (serum Na+, K+, and keeping Plasma Renin Activity in upper half of reference range).

### Sick-Day Rules (病假日指引)
* **Rule 1 (Mild/Moderate Illness)**: Double the daily oral glucocorticoid dose for fever >38°C or intercurrent illness requiring bed rest/antibiotics. Fludrocortisone dose remains unchanged.
* **Rule 2 (Severe Illness/Vomiting/Diarrhea)**: If unable to tolerate oral meds, must administer IM Hydrocortisone 100 mg immediately and seek emergency medical care.
* **Patient Education**: All patients MUST carry an HA Steroid Alert Card (類固醇警告卡) and a Medical Alert bracelet. Issue a hydrocortisone injection kit for home emergency use.

### Steroid Tapering Protocol (For Iatrogenic Secondary AI)
* **Goal**: Allow HPA axis recovery after prolonged (>3 weeks) exogenous steroid use.
* **Algorithm**: Gradually taper long-acting steroids (e.g., Dexamethasone) → switch to physiological dose Hydrocortisone (20mg/day) → check 8 AM cortisol. If >400 nmol/L, stop. If lower, proceed to SST to confirm axis recovery before full cessation.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for Adrenal Crisis
* **High-Risk Profiles**: Patients with concomitant Type 1 Diabetes, malabsorption syndromes (Celiac), previous history of adrenal crisis, or those taking CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., Rifampicin for TB, Phenytoin) which ↑ hydrocortisone clearance.

### Prognosis & Life Expectancy
* **Overall Prognosis**: Normal life expectancy with optimal replacement therapy.
* **Mortality Risks**: ↑ Risk of sudden death due to unrecognized adrenal crisis during intercurrent illness. ↑ Risk of cardiovascular mortality linked to chronic glucocorticoid over-replacement.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Adrenal Insufficiency (Under-Replacement)
* **Cardiovascular**: Refractory vasodilatory shock, cardiac arrest (hyperkalemic arrhythmias).
* **Neurological**: Hypoglycemic coma, severe lethargy.

### Adverse Effects of Glucocorticoid Over-Replacement
* **Metabolic**: Iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome (醫源性庫欣氏症), weight gain, visceral adiposity, impaired glucose tolerance/Type 2 Diabetes.
* **Musculoskeletal**: Osteoporosis (骨質疏鬆) (consider baseline DEXA scan), avascular necrosis, proximal myopathy.
* **Ophthalmological**: Glaucoma, posterior subcapsular cataracts.

### Adverse Effects of Fludrocortisone Over-Replacement
* **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension, peripheral edema, volume overload leading to heart failure.
* **Electrolyte**: Hypokalemia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: Why do patients with Primary Adrenal Insufficiency present with hyperpigmentation while those with Secondary AI do not? Answer: In Primary AI, loss of cortisol feedback causes the pituitary to overproduce POMC, which is cleaved into both ACTH and Melanocyte-Stimulating Hormone (MSH). In Secondary AI, the pituitary fails to produce POMC, hence no MSH or hyperpigmentation.
* Viva Question: During an acute adrenal crisis, should you wait for the Short Synacthen Test results before giving IV Hydrocortisone? Answer: Absolutely not. Draw baseline bloods if immediately available, but administer IV Hydrocortisone and IV fluids instantly. Delaying treatment is fatal.
* Trap: Checking an 8 AM ACTH level to monitor whether the hydrocortisone dose is adequate. Pearl: ACTH levels are useless for titrating hydrocortisone doses in Primary AI. Titration is strictly clinical (weight, energy, BP).
* Trap: Prescribing fludrocortisone for a patient with iatrogenic secondary adrenal insufficiency. Pearl: Secondary AI patients have an intact Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System, so mineralocorticoid production is normal. They only require glucocorticoid replacement.
* Pitfall: Forgetting to advise patients on sick-day rules. You must explicitly teach patients to double their oral steroid dose during fever or infection, and to use IM hydrocortisone and attend A&E if vomiting prevents oral absorption.
