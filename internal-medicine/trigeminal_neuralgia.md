# Trigeminal Neuralgia (三叉神經痛)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Trigeminal Neuralgia
* **Trigeminal Neuralgia (TN):** A chronic pain disorder characterized by recurrent, unilateral, brief, electric shock-like pains, abrupt in onset/termination, limited to the distribution of ≥1 divisions of the Trigeminal Nerve (CN V)

### Etiologies of Trigeminal Neuralgia
* **Primary (Classical/Idiopathic) TN (85-90%):**
  * **Vascular Compression:** Microvascular compression at the root entry zone (REZ) in the prepontine cistern
  * **Offending Vessel:** Superior Cerebellar Artery (SCA) (80%) > Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (AICA) or veins
* **Secondary (Symptomatic) TN (10-15%):**
  * **Demyelinating:** Multiple Sclerosis (多發性硬化症) (strongly suspect if bilateral or young onset)
  * **Mass Effect:** Cerebellopontine angle (CPA) tumors (Vestibular Schwannoma, Meningioma, Epidermoid cyst)
  * **Vascular:** Arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), aneurysms
  * **Trauma/Infection:** Post-surgical, Herpes Zoster (Post-herpetic neuralgia)

### Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** ~0.1-0.2% in general population
* **Gender:** F > M (1.5:1)
* **Age of Onset:** Peak in 5th-6th decades (Classical). Onset <40 yrs → suspect Secondary TN
* **Anatomical Distribution:** V2 (Maxillary) & V3 (Mandibular) > V1 (Ophthalmic). Right side > Left side

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Mechanism of Primary Trigeminal Neuralgia
* **Neurovascular Conflict:** Pulsatile arterial compression at the REZ (where central/peripheral myelination transition occurs via oligodendrocytes/Schwann cells)
* **Demyelination:** Chronic compression → local ischemia → focal demyelination of nerve fibers
* **Ephaptic Transmission (Cross-talk):** Loss of myelin permits abnormal lateral electrical spread between large, myelinated Aβ fibers (light touch) and thinly myelinated/unmyelinated Aδ/C fibers (pain)
* **Hyperexcitability:** Ectopic impulse generation → hyperactive sensory nuclei → paroxysmal pain from non-noxious stimuli (Allodynia)

### Mechanism of Secondary Trigeminal Neuralgia
* **Multiple Sclerosis:** Demyelinating plaques strategically located at the trigeminal entry zone within the pons
* **Tumors/AVMs:** Direct mechanical tethering, stretch, or compression of CN V → secondary demyelination and ischemic axonal injury

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History: Pain Characteristics
* **Site:** Strictly unilateral (unless MS), localized to V2 (cheek/upper lip) and/or V3 (jaw/lower lip). V1 involvement rare (<5%)
* **Character:** Lancinating, sharp, stabbing, "electric-shock", shooting
* **Severity:** 10/10, excruciating (often described as one of the most painful conditions known)
* **Duration:** Fractions of a second to ≤2 minutes per episode
* **Frequency:** Paroxysms ranging from few/day to hundreds/day
* **Refractory Period:** Characteristic pain-free interval immediately following an attack

### History: Triggers
* **Innocuous Mechanical Stimuli:** Light touch, chewing, talking, brushing teeth, washing face, shaving, cold wind exposure
* **Trigger Zones:** Specific hypersensitive facial areas (e.g., nasolabial fold, upper lip) that provoke attacks when touched

### Physical Examination: Head & Neck Findings
* **Classical TN Findings:** Neurological examination is typically **completely normal**
* **Pain Behaviors:** Patient may flinch, stop talking mid-sentence ("tic douloureux"), or guard their face (refusing to let examiner touch trigger zones)
* **Red Flags (Suggesting Secondary TN):**
  * **Sensory Deficits:** Altered pinprick/temperature sensation in CN V distribution
  * **Motor Deficits:** Masseter/temporalis wasting, jaw deviation to ipsilateral side on opening
  * **Reflexes:** Diminished/absent corneal reflex (V1 sensory, VII motor)
  * **Other CN Palsies:** Hearing loss/tinnitus (CN VIII - CPA tumor), facial weakness (CN VII)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Facial Pain
| Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dental Pathology (Caries/Abscess)** | Dull, throbbing, continuous pain. Worse with hot/cold/sweet foods. Local tenderness, +ve percussion test |
| **Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ) Dysfunction** | Deep aching pain. Associated with jaw movement/chewing. Joint clicking/crepitus. Tenderness over TMJ |
| **Post-Herpetic Neuralgia (帶狀皰疹後遺神經痛)** | Continuous burning pain. Hx of vesicular rash. Usually V1 distribution. Allodynia + scarring |
| **Glossopharyngeal Neuralgia** | Pain similar to TN but located in tonsil, posterior pharynx, base of tongue, ear. Triggered by swallowing |
| **Temporal Arteritis (Giant Cell Arteritis)** | >50 yrs, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, visual changes. Continuous headache |
| **Cluster Headache** | Males > Females. Unilateral periorbital/temporal pain. Autonomic features (lacrimation, ptosis, rhinorrhea). Lasts 15-180 mins |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **Diagnostic Approach:** TN is primarily a *clinical diagnosis*. Tests aim to exclude secondary causes and prepare for Rx
* **Routine Bloods:** CBP, LFT, RFT, Electrolytes (baseline before initiating anticonvulsants)
* **Specific Screening (HA Mandatory):** **HLA-B*1502 allele** testing in all Han Chinese patients *prior* to starting Carbamazepine/Oxcarbazepine
* **Inflammatory Markers:** ESR/CRP (if >50 yrs to rule out Temporal Arteritis)

### Imaging for Trigeminal Neuralgia
* **MRI Brain with Contrast (Gold Standard):**
  * **Specific Sequences:** High-resolution T2-weighted sequences (e.g., **FIESTA** or **CISS**) + MRA
  * **Target:** To visualize the trigeminal root entry zone (REZ) in the prepontine cistern
  * **Findings Sought:** Vascular loop compressing the nerve, CPA tumors (e.g., meningioma), brainstem demyelinating plaques (MS)

### Diagnostic Criteria for Trigeminal Neuralgia (ICHD-3)
* **A:** ≥3 attacks of unilateral facial pain fulfilling criteria B and C
* **B:** Occurring in ≥1 divisions of trigeminal nerve, with no radiation beyond trigeminal distribution
* **C:** Pain has ≥3 of the following 4 characteristics:
  * 1. Recurring in paroxysmal attacks lasting from a fraction of a second to 2 minutes
  * 2. Severe intensity
  * 3. Electric shock-like, shooting, stabbing, or sharp in quality
  * 4. Precipitated by innocuous stimuli to the affected side of the face
* **D:** No clinically evident neurological deficit (for classical TN)
* **E:** Not better accounted for by another ICHD-3 diagnosis

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Exacerbation Stabilization Protocol
* **Triage & Assessment:** Patients in severe status neuralgicus ("trigeminal crisis") may present dehydrated/malnourished due to inability to eat/drink
* **Intravenous Hydration:** IV normal saline if oral intake severely compromised
* **Acute Pharmacological Rescue (HA Protocols):**
  * **IV Phenytoin or IV Fosphenytoin:** Loading dose can provide rapid cessation of paroxysms (ECG monitoring required)
  * **IV Lidocaine:** Continuous infusion (specialist setting, ECG monitoring)
* **Local Interventions:**
  * **Anesthetic Blocks:** Viscous lidocaine to oral mucosa trigger points or targeted nerve blocks (e.g., infraorbital/mental nerve) via Pain Team

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Pharmacological Step-Up Algorithm for Trigeminal Neuralgia
| Step | Treatment | Mechanism / Regimen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First-Line** | **Carbamazepine (卡馬西平)** | Sodium channel blocker. Start 100mg BD, titrate up by 100-200mg/day to effect (max 1200mg/day) |
| **First-Line (Alt)**| **Oxcarbazepine** | Better tolerability profile than carbamazepine, fewer drug interactions |
| **Second-Line** | **Pregabalin / Gabapentin** | Ca2+ channel α2δ-subunit ligands. Used as add-on or if 1st line not tolerated |
| **Second-Line** | **Baclofen / Lamotrigine** | GABA-B agonist / Na+ channel blocker. Often used adjunctively with carbamazepine |
| **Refractory** | **Botulinum Toxin A (Botox)** | Subcutaneous injection into trigger zones/pain distribution |

### Surgical & Interventional Management Algorithm
* **Indications:** Medically refractory TN, intolerable medication side effects, or clear neurovascular compression on MRI in surgical candidate
* **Definitive/Curative:**
  * **Microvascular Decompression (MVD):** Craniotomy → insert Teflon sponge between offending vessel (SCA) and CN V. Highest long-term success rate (~80% at 10 yrs), requires fit patient (major neurosurgery)
* **Ablative/Destructive (For frail/elderly):**
  * **Stereotactic Radiosurgery (Gamma Knife):** Targeted radiation to REZ. Delayed onset of relief (weeks-months)
  * **Percutaneous Rhizotomy (Radiofrequency/Glycerol/Balloon):** Destruction of Gasserian ganglion. Immediate relief but higher recurrence and facial numbness risk

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Barrow Neurological Institute (BNI) Pain Intensity Score
| Score | Clinical Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | No trigeminal pain |
| **II** | Occasional pain, not requiring medication |
| **III** | Some pain, adequately controlled with medication |
| **IV** | Some pain, not adequately controlled with medication |
| **V** | Severe pain/no pain relief |

### Prognostic Factors
* **Favorable Surgical Prognosis (for MVD):** Typical TN presentation, lack of sensory deficit, positive identification of venous/arterial compression on MRI, shorter symptom duration
* **Disease Course:** Characterized by exacerbations and remissions. Remissions may last months to years but tend to shorten progressively over time

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications
* **Nutritional Decline:** Severe weight loss, dehydration, and cachexia secondary to fear of triggering pain by eating/drinking
* **Psychiatric:** Severe clinical depression, anxiety, social isolation, and elevated suicide risk

### Pharmacological Adverse Effects (Highly Tested)
* **Carbamazepine Toxicity:**
  * **Dermatological:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) / Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) (Highest risk in **HLA-B*1502** positive Han Chinese)
  * **Hematological:** Aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia (Requires routine CBP monitoring)
  * **Electrolytes:** Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone (SIADH) → Hyponatremia
  * **Neurological:** Dizziness, ataxia, diplopia, drowsiness
  * **Hepatic:** Elevated transaminases (Requires LFT monitoring), Cytochrome P450 induction (reduces efficacy of OCPs, warfarin)

### Surgical Complications
* **MVD:** CSF leak, meningitis, ipsilateral hearing loss (CN VIII damage during CPA access), facial palsy (CN VII)
* **Ablative Procedures:** **Anesthesia Dolorosa** (severe, intractable deafferentation pain in a numb facial area - worse than original TN), corneal anesthesia (risk of keratitis)

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What mandatory screening must be done before starting Carbamazepine in a Hong Kong Chinese patient? Answer: Genetic screening for the HLA-B*1502 allele to prevent life-threatening Stevens-Johnson Syndrome.
* Viva Question: A 28-year-old female presents with bilateral trigeminal neuralgia. What is the most likely underlying diagnosis? Answer: Multiple Sclerosis.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with continuous, burning facial pain and sensory loss has Classical Trigeminal Neuralgia. Pearl: Classical TN has no sensory loss and consists of brief paroxysms. Continuous pain with sensory deficits implies neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia, or a mass lesion.
* Trap: Discharging a severe TN patient who has stopped drinking for 3 days due to pain with just an oral prescription. Pearl: Patients with status neuralgicus refusing oral intake risk acute kidney injury; they require admission, IV fluids, and potentially IV Phenytoin for rapid pain control.
* Pitfall: Overlooking the corneal reflex during cranial nerve examination. A diminished corneal reflex is a red flag for secondary causes (like a cerebellopontine angle tumor) compressing CN V1.
* Pitfall: Prescribing Carbamazepine without warning female patients about drug interactions. Carbamazepine is a strong CYP450 inducer and can lead to oral contraceptive pill failure and unintended pregnancies.
