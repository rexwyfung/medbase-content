# Frailty, Falls & Polypharmacy (衰弱、跌倒及多重用藥)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Frailty (衰弱)
* **Frailty**: A biological syndrome of ↓ reserve and resistance to stressors.
* **Result**: ↑ vulnerability to adverse outcomes (falls, hospitalization, institutionalization, death).
* **Two Core Models**:
  * **Phenotype Model** (Fried): Focuses on physical exhaustion and sarcopenia (肌少症).
  * **Deficit Accumulation Model** (Rockwood): Frailty as a cumulative index of diseases, signs, symptoms, and disabilities.

### Definition of Falls (跌倒) & Polypharmacy (多重用藥)
* **Falls**: Inadvertent coming to rest on the ground, floor, or other lower level, excluding intentional change in position or acute overwhelming medical event (e.g., massive stroke).
* **Polypharmacy**: 
  * **Numerical**: Routine use of ≥5 medications.
  * **Appropriate**: All drugs clinically indicated and optimized.
  * **Problematic**: Prescription of drugs where harm outweighs benefit, or dosing is incorrect.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Aging Population**: HK has one of the highest life expectancies globally; >20% population >65 years old.
* **Fall Rate**: ~20-30% of community-dwelling older adults fall annually in HK.
* **Hospital Authority (HA) Impact**: Falls are a leading cause of Emergency Department attendances and acute medical/orthopaedic admissions for the elderly.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Frailty
* **Inflammaging**: Chronic, low-grade systemic inflammation (↑ CRP, ↑ IL-6, ↑ TNF-α).
* **Endocrine Senescence**: ↓ IGF-1, ↓ DHEA, ↓ Testosterone, dysregulated HPA axis (hypercortisolemia).
* **Sarcopenia (肌少症)**: Loss of motor neurons, ↓ mitochondrial biogenesis, type II muscle fiber atrophy.
* **Immunosenescence**: Altered T-cell and B-cell function → ↓ response to vaccines, ↑ infection risk.

### Pathophysiology of Falls in the Elderly
* **Neuromuscular**: ↓ proprioception, prolonged reaction time, altered righting reflexes.
* **Cardiovascular**: Blunted baroreceptor sensitivity → impaired HR and vasomotor response to orthostasis.
* **Visual**: ↓ contrast sensitivity, ↓ depth perception, altered dark adaptation.
* **Vestibular**: Loss of vestibular hair cells → presbyastasis (age-related dysequilibrium).

### Pathophysiology of Polypharmacy Adverse Drug Events (ADEs)
* **Pharmacokinetics (PK) Changes**:
  * **Absorption**: ↓ gastric acid, delayed emptying (rarely clinically significant).
  * **Distribution**: ↑ body fat (↑ half-life of lipophilic drugs e.g., **Diazepam**), ↓ lean muscle, ↓ total body water (↑ toxicity of water-soluble drugs e.g., **Lithium**), ↓ albumin (↑ free fraction of highly protein-bound drugs e.g., **Phenytoin**).
  * **Metabolism**: ↓ hepatic blood flow, ↓ CYP450 phase I metabolism.
  * **Excretion**: ↓ GFR (major cause of drug accumulation e.g., **Digoxin**, **Renally cleared DOACs**).
* **Pharmacodynamics (PD) Changes**:
  * ↑ sensitivity to CNS depressants (**Benzodiazepines**, **Opioids**).
  * ↑ sensitivity to anticholinergics (exacerbates delirium, constipation, urinary retention).
  * ↓ beta-receptor responsiveness.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Falls History (SPLAT & FRIDs)
* **S**ymptoms before fall (Prodrome? Palpitations? Dizziness? Aura?)
* **P**revious falls (Frequency, pattern)
* **L**ocation (Indoor vs outdoor, stairs, bathroom)
* **A**ctivity at time of fall (Getting out of bed, straining, turning head)
* **T**rauma or Time (Duration on floor, "long lie", head strike)
* **Medication Hx**: Actively screen for Fall-Risk Increasing Drugs (FRIDs) (e.g., sedatives, antihypertensives, anticholinergics).

### Gait, Balance & Neurological Examination
* **Visual Assessment**: Cataracts, visual fields.
* **Cranial Nerves**: Nystagmus, hearing loss.
* **Motor**: Proximal myopathy (difficulty rising from chair), rigidity, tremor.
* **Sensory**: Joint position sense (proprioception), stocking-glove anesthesia.
* **Coordination**: Dysdiadochokinesia, past-pointing.
* **Gait Evaluation**:
  * **Wide-based**: Cerebellar or sensory ataxia.
  * **Shuffling/Festinating**: Parkinsonism.
  * **Marche à petits pas**: Vascular dementia / diffuse cerebrovascular disease.
  * **High-stepping**: Foot drop (common peroneal nerve palsy / severe neuropathy).

### Cardiovascular & Musculoskeletal Examination
* **Pulse**: Irregularly irregular (Atrial Fibrillation), bradycardia.
* **Blood Pressure**: Lying and standing BP technique (detect orthostasis).
* **Precordium**: Ejection systolic murmur radiating to carotids (Aortic Stenosis).
* **Carotid Bruit**: Cerebrovascular disease.
* **Joints**: Osteoarthritic deformities (knees, hips), rheumatoid hands.
* **Feet**: Calluses, bunions, inappropriate footwear, podiatry issues.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Features of Falls vs Syncope
| Feature | Mechanical Fall | Cardiogenic Syncope | Vasovagal Syncope | Orthostatic Syncope |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prodrome** | None | None / Palpitations | Nausea, diaphoresis, visual dimming | Lightheadedness on standing |
| **Trigger** | Environmental hazard | Exertion / Supine | Pain, emotion, standing long | Postural change (sit → stand) |
| **Recovery** | Immediate | Rapid | Slow, prolonged fatigue | Rapid upon supine positioning |

### Discriminatory Features of Syncope vs Seizure
| Feature | Syncope (暈厥) | Seizure (癲癇) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Usually gradual (except cardiogenic) | Sudden, aura may precede |
| **Motor Activity** | Brief myoclonus possible (<10 jerks) | Prolonged tonic-clonic movements |
| **Incontinence** | Rare | Common |
| **Post-ictal Phase** | Absent or brief (<5 mins) | Prolonged confusion, drowsiness |
| **Tongue Biting** | Rare | Common (lateral edge) |

### Differential Diagnoses of Cognitive Decline (Delirium vs Dementia)
* **Delirium**: Acute onset, fluctuating course, impaired attention, altered conscious level, triggered by infection/drugs/hypoxia.
* **Dementia**: Insidious onset, progressive decline, clear consciousness (until late stages), structural brain pathology.
* **Depression (Pseudodementia)**: Poor effort in testing ("don't know" answers), somatic complaints, prominent morning worsening.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Diagnostic Criteria for Fried Frailty Phenotype
* **Presence of ≥3 of the following 5 criteria = Frail** (1-2 = Pre-frail):
  * **Unintentional weight loss**: >4.5 kg or >5% of body mass in last year.
  * **Self-reported exhaustion**: Feels everything is an effort >3 days/week.
  * **Weakness**: ↓ grip strength (measured by dynamometer, adjusted for sex/BMI).
  * **Slow walking speed**: Time to walk 15 feet (4.6m) adjusted for sex/height.
  * **Low physical activity**: Kilocalories expended per week (measured via Minnesota Leisure Time Activity Questionnaire).

### Diagnostic Criteria for Orthostatic Hypotension (體位性低血壓)
* **Definition**: Measured after 5 mins supine, then 1 min and 3 mins standing.
* **Criteria**: 
  * ↓ Systolic BP ≥20 mmHg **OR**
  * ↓ Diastolic BP ≥10 mmHg.
* **Note**: In patients with baseline supine hypertension (SBP ≥160 mmHg), a drop of ≥30 mmHg systolic is required.

### Tiered Investigations for Falls & Syncope
* **Bedside**:
  * **ECG (12-lead)**: Arrhythmias, complete heart block, prolonged QTc, LVH, bundle branch blocks.
  * **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG)**: Rule out hypoglycemia.
  * **Urine Dipstick**: UTI screening (if symptomatic or delirium present).
* **Bloods**:
  * **CBC**: Anemia, macrocytosis (B12/folate), infection.
  * **U&E, Cr**: AKI, hyponatremia (SIADH from SSRIs), hypokalemia (diuretics).
  * **Calcium, Phosphate, ALP**: Osteomalacia, hyperparathyroidism.
  * **HbA1c**: Diabetic control.
  * **TSH**: Hypo/hyperthyroidism.
  * **Vitamin D, B12, Folate**: Deficiency states contributing to neuropathy/myopathy.
* **Imaging & Special Tests**:
  * **CT Brain (Non-contrast)**: If head trauma, focal neurological signs, or altered mental status (rule out Subdural Hematoma - SDH).
  * **Echocardiogram**: If structural heart disease suspected (murmur, ECG changes).
  * **Holter Monitor / Telemetry**: If intermittent arrhythmia suspected (unexplained syncope).
  * **DEXA Scan**: Bone mineral density assessment for fracture risk.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Fall with Head Injury / "Long Lie"
* **Primary Survey**: ABCDE approach.
* **C-Spine Precautions**: Immobilize if neck pain, midline tenderness, or distracting injuries.
* **Trauma Code Activation**: If severe mechanism or progressive neurological deficit.
* **Manage "Long Lie" Complications**:
  * **Hypothermia**: Active warming with Bair Hugger, warm IV fluids.
  * **Rhabdomyolysis**: Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation (Normal Saline) to maintain urine output >100ml/hr, monitor U&E (hyperkalemia), monitor CK levels.
  * **Pressure Injuries**: Prompt assessment, repositioning, pressure-relieving mattress.

### Acute Management of Syncope with Hemodynamic Instability
* **Positioning**: Trendelenburg position (head down, legs elevated) unless heart failure.
* **Bradycardia/AV Block**:
  * IV **Atropine** 0.5-1mg (max 3mg).
  * Isoprenaline infusion or external pacing if Atropine fails.
* **Hypotension**:
  * IV Fluid bolus (250-500ml Normal Saline).
  * Vasopressors if unresponsive (e.g., Noradrenaline).

### Acute Management of Drug Toxicity (Delirium/Overdose)
* **Opiates**: IV/IM **Naloxone** (titrate to respiratory rate, beware short half-life requires infusion).
* **Anticholinergic Toxicity**: Discontinue agent, supportive care.
* **Benzodiazepines**: **Flumazenil** (rarely used in elderly due to high risk of precipitating intractable withdrawal seizures; preferred Rx is supportive airway/ventilation).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA)
* **Multidisciplinary Approach**: Geriatrician, Physiotherapist (PT), Occupational Therapist (OT), Medical Social Worker (MSW), Dietitian.
* **Goal**: Optimize medical conditions, maximize functional independence, prevent institutionalization.

### Deprescribing Algorithm (Polypharmacy Management)
* **Step 1**: Compile comprehensive medication list (include OTC, Chinese Traditional Medicine).
* **Step 2**: Identify drugs without indication or where harm > benefit.
* **Step 3**: Assess for prescribing cascades (e.g., Amlodipine → edema → Furosemide).
* **Step 4**: Taper and stop offending agents.
* **High-Yield Deprescribing Targets (STOPP Criteria / Beers Criteria highlights)**:
  * **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)** (e.g., Amitriptyline): Highly anticholinergic, orthostasis, sedation. Replace with SSRI or Gabapentin/Pregabalin for neuropathy.
  * **Benzodiazepines** (e.g., Diazepam): Falls, cognitive impairment. Taper slowly.
  * **First-generation Antihistamines** (e.g., Chlorpheniramine): Highly anticholinergic. Use 2nd generation (Loratadine) if strictly needed.
  * **Alpha-blockers** (e.g., Prazosin): First-dose syncope, severe orthostasis.
  * **PPIs** (e.g., Omeprazole): If used >8 weeks without indication (↑ risk of C. diff, bone loss).

### Multifactorial Interventions (MFI) for Falls Prevention
* **Medication Review**: Withdraw FRIDs (especially psychotropics).
* **Physiotherapy (PT)**:
  * Strength and balance training.
  * **Tai Chi (太極)**: Evidence-based for ↓ falls risk in community-dwelling older adults.
  * Assistive devices (walking sticks, frames).
* **Occupational Therapy (OT)**:
  * Home hazard assessment and modification.
  * Grab rails, non-slip mats, adequate lighting, removal of loose rugs.
* **Visual/Hearing Correction**: Cataract surgery, correct refraction (caution with new multifocal lenses, which can initially ↑ fall risk).
* **Cardiovascular Optimization**: Treat symptomatic orthostasis (compression stockings, adequate hydration, occasionally **Midodrine** or **Fludrocortisone** if refractory).

### Frailty & Sarcopenia Management
* **Nutrition**: Protein supplementation (1.0-1.2 g/kg/day), Caloric optimization.
* **Vitamin D**: Supplement if <50 nmol/L (prescribe **Cholecalciferol**).
* **Exercise**: Progressive Resistance Training (PRT) is the most effective intervention for sarcopenia.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS)
* **Function**: Used widely in HK HA (especially upon admission) to stratify baseline functional status (assessed based on status 2 weeks prior to acute illness).
* **Scale**:
  * **1. Very Fit**: Robust, active, energetic.
  * **2. Well**: No active disease symptoms, exercises occasionally.
  * **3. Managing Well**: Medical problems well controlled, not regularly active.
  * **4. Vulnerable**: Not dependent, but symptoms limit activities.
  * **5. Mildly Frail**: Needs help with high-order IADLs (finances, transport, heavy housework).
  * **6. Moderately Frail**: Needs help with all outside activities and keeping house. Needs help with bathing.
  * **7. Severely Frail**: Completely dependent for personal care (ADLs) from whatever cause.
  * **8. Very Severely Frail**: Completely dependent, approaching end of life.
  * **9. Terminally Ill**: Life expectancy <6 months.

### Timed Up and Go (TUG) Test
* **Procedure**: Patient sits in standard chair, stands up, walks 3 meters, turns, walks back, and sits down.
* **Scoring**:
  * **<10 seconds**: Normal, freely mobile.
  * **>12-15 seconds**: Increased risk of falls. Need comprehensive assessment.
  * **>30 seconds**: High dependence, severe mobility impairment.

### STOPP/START Criteria (Screening Tool of Older Persons' Prescriptions)
* **STOPP**: Potentially Inappropriate Medications (PIMs) to stop (e.g., prolonged NSAIDs in heart failure, anticholinergics in dementia).
* **START**: Screening Tool to Alert to Right Treatment (e.g., initiating ACEi in HFrEF, starting bone protection in long-term steroids).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Falls
* **Musculoskeletal**: Fractures (Neck of Femur/NOF, Colles' fracture, pubic rami, vertebral compression), soft tissue injuries.
* **Neurological**: Subdural Hematoma (bridging veins sheer easily in atrophic brain).
* **Renal/Metabolic**: Rhabdomyolysis → Myoglobinuric Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) from prolonged immobilization.
* **Psychological**: Ptophobia (fear of falling) → self-restricted activity → further deconditioning → ↑ frailty.

### Complications of Polypharmacy (The "Geriatric Giants")
* **Cognitive**: Delirium, exacerbation of underlying dementia (especially with anticholinergic burden).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Constipation (Opioids, Calcium channel blockers), GI bleeding (NSAIDs + SSRIs).
* **Urological**: Urinary retention (Anticholinergics), Urinary incontinence (Diuretics, Alpha-blockers).
* **Cardiovascular**: Orthostatic hypotension, bradycardia (Beta-blockers + Non-DHP CCBs).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the defining difference between mild, moderate, and severe frailty on the Clinical Frailty Scale? Answer: Mild frailty involves dependence in Instrumental ADLs (IADLs like shopping, finances), Moderate involves dependence in Basic ADLs (bathing, dressing), and Severe involves complete dependence for all personal care.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with a normal TSH and B12 does not have a reversible cause for cognitive decline. Pearl: Always review the medication list; polypharmacy and anticholinergic burden are highly prevalent, easily reversible causes of cognitive impairment.
* Pitfall: Prescribing a purely medical intervention (e.g., Vitamin D) for falls prevention without referring to allied health. A Multifactorial Intervention (MFI), including physiotherapy and OT home modification, is mandatory and has the highest evidence for reducing fall risk.
* Viva Question: An 80-year-old falls and is found to have a drop in BP of 25/15 upon standing. What is the diagnosis and what drug classes should you review? Answer: Orthostatic hypotension. Review alpha-blockers, beta-blockers, diuretics, TCAs, and L-dopa.
* Trap: Reassuring a patient who had a fall without syncope but has a new murmur. Pearl: A fall can be the presenting symptom of critical Aortic Stenosis (AS) due to transient cerebral hypoperfusion without full loss of consciousness. Always auscultate the chest.
* Pitfall: Up-titrating Furosemide for ankle edema in an elderly patient taking Amlodipine. This is a classic prescribing cascade; the correct action is to stop or reduce Amlodipine rather than adding a diuretic.
