# Haemolytic Anaemia (溶血性貧血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Haemolytic Anaemia
* **Haemolytic Anaemia (溶血性貧血):** Premature destruction of red blood cells (RBCs) reducing their lifespan to <120 days, where bone marrow compensatory reticulocytosis is insufficient to maintain normal haemoglobin levels.

### Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Etiological Classification
* **Intrinsic / Congenital Defects (RBC Abnormalities):**
  * **Membranopathies:** Hereditary Spherocytosis (HS) (遺傳性球形紅細胞增多症), Hereditary Elliptocytosis.
  * **Enzymopathies:** G6PD Deficiency (G6PD缺乏症), Pyruvate Kinase Deficiency.
  * **Haemoglobinopathies:** Thalassaemia (地中海貧血), Sickle Cell Disease (SCD) (鐮狀細胞貧血) (Rare in HK).
  * **Acquired Intrinsic:** Paroxysmal Nocturnal Haemoglobinuria (PNH) (陣發性睡眠性血紅蛋白尿症).
* **Extrinsic / Acquired Defects (Environmental Abnormalities):**
  * **Immune-Mediated:** Autoimmune Haemolytic Anaemia (AIHA) (自身免疫性溶血性貧血) [Warm vs Cold], Alloimmune (Transfusion reactions), Drug-induced (e.g., Penicillin, Methyldopa).
  * **Microangiopathic Haemolytic Anaemia (MAHA) (微血管病性溶血性貧血):** TTP, HUS, DIC, HELLP syndrome, Malignant hypertension.
  * **Mechanical:** Prosthetic heart valves, March haemoglobinuria.
  * **Infectious/Toxic:** Malaria (瘧疾), Clostridium perfringens septicaemia, snake venom, lead poisoning.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Thalassaemia:** Highly prevalent; ~8% of HK population are carriers of α- or β-thalassaemia trait.
* **G6PD Deficiency:** Affects ~4.5% of local male population (X-linked recessive). Universal neonatal screening implemented in HK since 1984.
* **AIHA & HS:** Most common causes of acquired and congenital haemolytic anaemias (excluding thalassaemia) requiring active haematology follow-up in HA hospitals.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Extravascular Haemolysis
* **Mechanism:** Macrophage-mediated destruction of abnormal or opsonized RBCs within the Reticuloendothelial System (RES), primarily the spleen and liver.
* **Biochemical Pathway:** RBC breakdown → Heme converted to unconjugated bilirubin (UCB) → Liver conjugates to direct bilirubin → Excreted in bile. ↑ UCB overwhelms liver → Jaundice & pigmented gallstones.
* **Associated Conditions:** HS, Warm AIHA, Delayed transfusion reactions.

### Pathophysiology of Intravascular Haemolysis
* **Mechanism:** Direct destruction of RBCs within the circulation (blood vessels), releasing free haemoglobin directly into plasma.
* **Biochemical Pathway:** Free Hb binds to Haptoglobin → complex cleared by liver (↓ Haptoglobin). Excess free Hb → Haemoglobinaemia → Filtered by kidneys (Haemoglobinuria) → Proximal tubule cells absorb Hb, convert to haemosiderin (Haemosiderinuria).
* **Associated Conditions:** PNH, Cold AIHA (complement-mediated), MAHA (TTP/HUS), G6PD severe crisis, Malaria.

### Molecular Mechanisms of Specific Intrinsic Defects
* **Hereditary Spherocytosis (HS):** Mutations in cytoskeletal proteins (Ankyrin, Spectrin, Band 3) → Uncoupling of lipid bilayer → Loss of membrane microvesicles → Decreased surface area-to-volume ratio → Rigid spherocytes trapped & destroyed in spleen.
* **G6PD Deficiency:** Enzyme defect → Inability to generate NADPH → Failure to maintain reduced glutathione → RBC susceptible to oxidative stress (from drugs/fava beans) → Hb denatures into Heinz bodies → Splenic macrophages bite out Heinz bodies ("Bite cells").
* **Thalassaemia:** Unbalanced synthesis of α or β globin chains. Excess unpaired chains precipitate → Intracellular inclusions → RBC membrane damage → Premature destruction in marrow (Ineffective erythropoiesis) and spleen.

### Pathophysiology of Autoimmune & Clonal Defects
* **Warm AIHA:** IgG autoantibodies bind RBCs at 37°C → Fc receptors on splenic macrophages recognize IgG → Partial phagocytosis → Spherocyte formation → Splenic destruction (Extravascular).
* **Cold Agglutinin Disease (CAD):** IgM autoantibodies bind RBCs at <37°C (extremities) → Activates classical complement pathway (C3b deposition) → C3b-opsonized RBCs destroyed by Kupffer cells in liver (Extravascular) OR terminal complement MAC lysis (Intravascular).
* **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Haemoglobinuria (PNH):** Acquired somatic mutation in *PIGA* gene in haematopoietic stem cell → Lack of GPI anchor → Absence of CD55 (Decay Accelerating Factor) & CD59 (MIRL) on RBC surface → RBCs highly susceptible to complement-mediated intravascular lysis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Clues for Haemolytic Anaemia
* **Onset & Triggers:** Sudden onset post-drug ingestion (e.g., dapsone, primaquine, nitrofurantoin), infections, or fava bean consumption (G6PD).
* **Temperature Sensitivity:** Acrocyanosis or Raynaud's phenomenon upon cold exposure (Cold AIHA).
* **Urine Changes:** "Coca-Cola" or dark tea-coloured urine, especially morning (PNH) or during acute crises (Intravascular haemolysis).
* **Family History:** Gallstones at a young age, early cholecystectomy, chronic anaemia, splenectomy (HS, Thalassaemia).
* **Comorbidities:** History of SLE, CLL, Mycoplasma pneumonia, EBV/IM (Secondary AIHA).
* **Bleeding/Neurological Symptoms:** Fever, confusion, seizures, mucosal bleeding (TTP/MAHA).

### Physical Examination: General & Integumentary Signs
* **General:** Pallor (Anaemia), Scleral icterus & Jaundice (Hyperbilirubinaemia), Cachexia (Chronic severe thalassaemia).
* **Skin:** Petechiae/purpura/bruising (if concurrent thrombocytopenia in MAHA/Evans syndrome).
* **Extremities:** Livedo reticularis, Acrocyanosis in fingers/toes/nose/ears upon cold exposure (Cold AIHA). Chronic leg ulcers around medial malleolus (HS, severe Thalassaemia due to microvascular occlusion/poor perfusion).

### Physical Examination: Abdominal & Skeletal Signs
* **Abdomen:** Splenomegaly (Massive in chronic extravascular haemolysis like HS, Thalassaemia major; moderate in Warm AIHA). Hepatomegaly (Extramedullary haematopoiesis, right heart failure secondary to chronic anaemia, or iron overload).
* **Scars:** Right upper quadrant surgical scars (Previous open/laparoscopic cholecystectomy for pigment stones). Left upper quadrant scars (Previous splenectomy).
* **Facies & Skeleton:** "Chipmunk facies" (Frontal bossing, maxillary hyperplasia, depressed nasal bridge) due to marrow expansion in Thalassaemia Major.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Extravascular vs Intravascular Haemolysis Discriminators
| Feature | Extravascular Haemolysis | Intravascular Haemolysis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Site** | Spleen, Liver (RES) | Blood vessels |
| **Key Etiologies** | HS, Warm AIHA, Thalassaemia | PNH, MAHA, Severe G6PD, Cold AIHA |
| **Splenomegaly** | Prominent | Usually absent / mild |
| **Jaundice** | Prominent | Variable |
| **Haemoglobinaemia** | Absent | Present (Pink/Red plasma) |
| **Haemoglobinuria** | Absent | Present (Dark urine) |
| **Haemosiderinuria**| Absent | Present (Chronic state) |

### Discriminatory Differential: Causes of Microangiopathic Haemolytic Anaemia (MAHA)
| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **TTP** | FAT RN (Fever, Anaemia, Thrombocytopenia, Renal failure, Neurological deficits). Extreme thrombocytopenia. |
| **HUS** | Often post-diarrhoeal (*E. coli* O157:H7). Severe acute kidney injury. Neurological signs less common. |
| **DIC** | Underlying sepsis/malignancy. Bleeding diathesis. Abnormal coagulation profile (↑ PT/APTT). |
| **HELLP** | Pregnant (3rd trimester). Haemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets. Hypertension. |
| **Mechanical** | Mechanical heart valve click on auscultation. History of valve replacement. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Urinalysis (Dipstick):** +ve for blood but ZERO RBCs on microscopy (indicates Haemoglobinuria).
* **Urine Microscopy:** Prussian blue stain +ve for Haemosiderinuria (sloughed tubular cells containing iron).

### Tier 1: Confirming Haemolysis (The Haemolysis Screen)
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** ↓ Hb. MCV variable (↑ in marked reticulocytosis, ↓ in Thalassaemia, normal in AIHA/HS).
* **Reticulocyte Count:** ↑↑ (Bone marrow response). Reticulocyte Production Index (RPI) > 2.
* **Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH):** ↑↑ (Intracellular enzyme released from lysed RBCs).
* **Unconjugated Bilirubin (UCB):** ↑ (Breakdown product of heme).
* **Haptoglobin:** ↓↓ or undetectable (Binds free Hb, rapidly cleared). *Most sensitive marker for intravascular, but also ↓ in severe extravascular*.

### Tier 2: Identifying the Cause via Peripheral Blood Film
| Peripheral Blood Film Finding | Associated Haemolytic Condition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Spherocytes** (Loss of central pallor) | Hereditary Spherocytosis, Warm AIHA |
| **Schistocytes / Helmet cells** (Fragmented) | MAHA (TTP, HUS, DIC), Prosthetic valves |
| **Bite cells & Blister cells** | G6PD deficiency (Post-oxidative stress) |
| **Target cells & Basophilic stippling** | Thalassaemia |
| **Elliptocytes / Ovalocytes** | Hereditary Elliptocytosis |
| **Acanthocytes** (Spur cells) | Liver disease (Spur cell anaemia) |
| **RBC Agglutination** (Clumping) | Cold AIHA (CAD) |

### Tier 3: Specific Confirmatory Tests & Diagnostic Criteria
* **Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT / Coombs Test):**
  * **Warm AIHA:** DAT +ve for IgG (with or without C3d).
  * **Cold AIHA:** DAT +ve for C3d ONLY (IgG -ve).
* **Eosin-5-Maleimide (EMA) Binding Test:** Primary diagnostic test for Hereditary Spherocytosis (replaces osmotic fragility test). Shows decreased fluorescence due to ↓ Band 3 protein.
* **G6PD Quantitative Enzyme Assay:** Measures G6PD activity. *Must test baseline levels >2-3 months after acute haemolytic crisis to avoid false normal.*
* **Flow Cytometry (for PNH):** Detects absence of GPI-anchored proteins (CD55 and CD59) on RBCs and granulocytes (Gold standard).
* **Haemoglobin Pattern (Capillary Electrophoresis / HPLC):** Confirms Thalassaemia (e.g., ↑ HbA2 >3.5% in β-Thalassaemia trait).
* **ADAMTS13 Activity & Inhibitor Screen:** For suspected TTP (Activity <10% is diagnostic).
* **Cold Agglutinin Titer:** >1:64 at 4°C diagnostic for CAD.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Severe Haemolytic Crisis Resuscitation
* **Haemodynamic Support:** IV fluid resuscitation with Normal Saline if hypotensive or suspected intravascular lysis to maintain renal perfusion and prevent acute tubular necrosis (ATN) from haemoglobinuria.
* **Transfusion Support:** Packed RBCs (PRBCs). *Caveat in AIHA:* Cross-matching is notoriously difficult due to pan-agglutination. Communicate with Transfusion Medicine doctor. Give "least incompatible" blood if life-threatening anaemia (Hb < 5 g/dL with ischaemic ECG changes/heart failure).
* **Folic Acid Supplementation:** PO Folic Acid 5mg daily urgently to support massive erythropoiesis and prevent acute megaloblastic crisis.

### Acute Flare of Warm Autoimmune Haemolytic Anaemia (AIHA)
* **First Line Immunosuppression:** High-dose Corticosteroids (e.g., PO Prednisolone 1-1.5 mg/kg/day or IV Hydrocortisone equivalent).
* **Rescue Therapy (Refractory/Life-threatening):** IVIG 1g/kg for 2 days. Consider urgent Plasmapheresis if hyperacute and unresponsive to steroids/transfusion (though less effective than in TTP).

### Acute MAHA / Suspected TTP Emergency Protocol
* **Medical Emergency:** Immediate transfer to ICU/HDU.
* **Primary Intervention:** Urgent Therapeutic Plasma Exchange (PLEX / Plasmapheresis) using Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) replacement to remove ADAMTS13 inhibitors and replenish ADAMTS13 enzyme.
* **Bridging:** If PLEX delayed, infuse IV FFP immediately.
* **Immunosuppression:** IV Methylprednisolone.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of Warm AIHA
* **Step-Down Algorithm:** Taper Prednisolone slowly over months once Hb stabilizes.
* **Second-Line (Steroid-dependent/refractory):**
  * **Biologic Therapy:** IV Rituximab (Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody) - heavily utilized in HK HA protocols.
  * **Surgical:** Splenectomy (removes site of destruction and antibody production). Requires strict pre-operative vaccinations (Pneumococcus, Meningococcus, Haemophilus influenzae B).
* **Third-Line:** Immunosuppressants (Azathioprine, Mycophenolate Mofetil, Cyclosporine).

### Chronic Management of Cold Agglutinin Disease (CAD)
* **Lifestyle Modification:** Strict avoidance of cold. Warm clothing, heated gloves/socks.
* **Pharmacotherapy:** Steroids and splenectomy are INEFFECTIVE (destruction is hepatic/intravascular). First-line medical Rx is Rituximab ± Bendamustine. Sutimlimab (C1s inhibitor) for severe cases.

### Chronic Management of Hereditary Spherocytosis (HS)
* **Supplementation:** Lifelong PO Folic Acid 5mg daily.
* **Surgical Algorithm:** Splenectomy (curative for anaemia, but not the membrane defect).
  * *Timing:* Deferred until >6 years old to minimize post-splenectomy sepsis risk.
  * *Concomitant Surgery:* Laparoscopic cholecystectomy simultaneously if symptomatic gallstones present.

### Chronic Management of G6PD Deficiency
* **Trigger Avoidance (Medical Alert Card):**
  * **Foods:** Fava beans (Broad beans).
  * **Drugs:** Antimalarials (Primaquine), Sulphonamides (Sulfamethoxazole), Nitrofurantoin, Dapsone, Rasburicase.
  * **Chemicals:** Mothballs (Naphthalene).

### Chronic Management of Paroxysmal Nocturnal Haemoglobinuria (PNH)
* **Targeted Biologics:** Terminal Complement Inhibitors (Eculizumab or Ravulizumab). Blocks C5 cleavage, preventing MAC formation. Requires mandatory Meningococcal vaccination prior to initiation.
* **Supportive:** Anticoagulation if thrombotic events occur. Curative option: Allogeneic Haematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT) for severe bone marrow failure.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### PLASMIC Score for TTP Probability
* **Purpose:** Risk stratifies patients with MAHA to predict severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (<10%) and guide urgent PLEX initiation before ADAMTS13 assay results return.
* **Criteria (1 point each):**
  * **P**latelets < 30 x 10^9/L
  * **L**ysis (Hemolysis: Retic > 2.5%, Undetectable haptoglobin, or Indirect Bilirubin > 2 mg/dL)
  * **A**ctive cancer absence
  * **S**tem cell or solid organ transplant absence
  * **M**CV < 90 fL
  * **I**NR < 1.5
  * **C**reatinine < 2.0 mg/dL
* **Interpretation:** Score 6-7 = High risk (96% probability of TTP; initiate PLEX immediately). Score 0-4 = Low risk.

### AIHA Relapse Risk & Prognosis
* **Warm AIHA:** ~30% achieve long-term remission with steroids alone. High relapse rate upon tapering requires transition to Rituximab.
* **Secondary AIHA:** Prognosis dictated by underlying condition (e.g., CLL, SLE). Complete remission of secondary AIHA requires control of the primary disease.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Direct Complications of Chronic Haemolysis
* **Hepatobiliary:** Calcium bilirubinate (Pigment) gallstones → Cholecystitis, Choledocholithiasis, ascending cholangitis.
* **Haematological:**
  * **Aplastic Crisis:** Acute severe marrow failure provoked by Parvovirus B19 infection (targets erythroblasts). Dramatic drop in Reticulocytes and Hb.
  * **Megaloblastic Crisis:** Folate depletion from chronic compensatory erythropoiesis.
* **Renal:** Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) secondary to massive haemoglobinuria/iron toxicity in severe intravascular haemolysis.

### Disease-Specific Complications
* **PNH:** Unprovoked Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) in unusual sites (Hepatic vein/Budd-Chiari syndrome, cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, mesenteric veins).
* **Thalassaemia Major (Transfusion-dependent):** Secondary Haemosiderosis (Iron overload) → Restrictive cardiomyopathy (fatal arrythmias), liver cirrhosis, endocrine failure (Diabetes/Bronze diabetes, hypogonadism).

### Complications of Splenectomy (Management Sequelae)
* **Overwhelming Post-Splenectomy Infection (OPSI):** High mortality sepsis from encapsulated organisms (*S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, H. influenzae*).
* **Vascular:** Increased lifetime risk of VTE and pulmonary hypertension (due to loss of splenic clearance of procoagulant RBC microvesicles).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you interpret a Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT) showing positive C3d but negative IgG? Answer: This is classic for Cold Agglutinin Disease (IgM dissociates in the warmer core circulation leaving only C3d behind) or Paroxysmal Cold Haemoglobinuria (Donath-Landsteiner antibody).
* Viva Question: A patient with G6PD deficiency presents with acute haemolysis. FBC shows Hb 6 g/dL. You order a G6PD enzyme assay and it returns NORMAL. Why? Answer: Reticulocytes (which flood the blood during acute crisis) have high levels of G6PD enzyme, masking the deficiency of the older, lysed RBCs. Retest in 2-3 months.
* Trap: Withholding blood transfusion in life-threatening Warm AIHA because the blood bank reports "pan-agglutination / incompatible crossmatch". Pearl: In severe hypoxic AIHA, you must transfuse the "least incompatible" ABO/RhD matched blood slowly under strict monitoring; withholding blood is fatal.
* Trap: Requesting a reticulocyte count to evaluate haemolysis and ignoring the MCV. Pearl: A markedly elevated reticulocyte count artificially raises the MCV (macrocytosis), which can falsely suggest B12/Folate deficiency. Always review the blood film.
* Pitfall: Treating a patient with Cold Agglutinin Disease (CAD) using high-dose corticosteroids or splenectomy. The destruction in CAD is complement-mediated and primarily occurs in the liver (Kupffer cells), making both steroids and splenectomy totally ineffective.
* Pitfall: Failing to prescribe folic acid for a patient with chronic haemolysis. Chronic RBC turnover rapidly depletes folate stores, leading to a sudden, severe superimposed megaloblastic anaemia (megaloblastic crisis).
