# Seronegative Spondyloarthropathy (血清陰性脊柱關節病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Spondyloarthropathy
* **Seronegative Spondyloarthropathy (SpA)**: Group of overlapping inflammatory arthritides characterized by negative Rheumatoid Factor (RF) & anti-CCP, strong association with **HLA-B27**, axial skeleton involvement, and **enthesitis** (inflammation at tendon/ligament insertion sites).

### Etiologies and Subtypes of SpA
* **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS) (強直性脊柱炎)**: Prototype axial SpA; primary involvement of spine & sacroiliac joints.
* **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA) (銀屑病關節炎)**: Associated with psoriasis; mixed axial/peripheral + dactylitis.
* **Reactive Arthritis (ReA) (反應性關節炎)**: Post-infectious (GI/GU) autoimmune arthritis (formerly Reiter's syndrome); sterile joints.
* **Enteropathic Arthritis (腸病性關節炎)**: Associated with **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)** (Crohn’s/UC).
* **Undifferentiated SpA**: Meets SpA criteria but does not fit cleanly into above subtypes.

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of SpA
* **Prevalence**: ~0.2-0.5% in Hong Kong. 
* **HLA-B27 Carriage**: ~7% in healthy Southern Chinese population.
* **AS Specifics**: Male:Female ratio 3:1. Typical onset late teens to 30s. >90% of local AS patients are HLA-B27 +ve.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Immunopathogenesis and HLA-B27 Role
* **HLA-B27 Misfolding Hypothesis**: Aberrant folding of HLA-B27 heavy chains in ER → unfolded protein response (UPR) → cellular stress → ↑ IL-23 production.
* **Arthritogenic Peptide Hypothesis**: HLA-B27 cross-reacts with foreign peptides (e.g., *Chlamydia*, *Klebsiella*) → molecular mimicry initiating autoreactive T-cell response.
* **Microbiome Dysbiosis**: Gut permeability alterations ("leaky gut") → bacterial translocation → subclinical gut inflammation drives systemic immunity.

### Cytokine Axis in SpA
* **IL-23/IL-17 Axis**: Dendritic cells/macrophages secrete **IL-23** → activates Th17 cells & γδ T cells → release **IL-17** and **TNF-α**.
* **Tissue Target**: IL-17/TNF-α drive inflammation primarily at the **entheses** (enthesitis) rather than the synovium (synovitis, as in RA).

### Enthesitis to Osteoproliferation Pathway
* **Phase 1 (Inflammation)**: Macrophage/lymphocyte infiltration at enthesis → bone marrow edema (osteitis).
* **Phase 2 (Destruction)**: Osteoclast activation → cortical erosions.
* **Phase 3 (Proliferation)**: Post-inflammatory repair via Wnt/BMP signaling → osteoblast activation → pathological new bone formation (**syndesmophytes** & ankylosis).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Features of Inflammatory Back Pain (IBP)
* **Age of Onset**: Insidious onset <40 years old.
* **Duration**: Chronic (>3 months).
* **Timing**: Morning stiffness lasting >30 mins; pain wakes patient in second half of night.
* **Activity**: Improves with exercise, worsens with rest (differentiates from mechanical).
* **Location**: Alternating buttock pain (sacroiliitis).

### Peripheral Joint and Appendicular Symptoms
* **Peripheral Arthritis**: Usually asymmetrical, oligoarticular (<5 joints), predominantly lower limbs (knees, ankles).
* **Enthesitis**: Heel pain (Achilles tendonitis, plantar fasciitis), pelvic brim pain, costochondritis.
* **Dactylitis (香腸指/趾)**: "Sausage digit" – uniform swelling of entire digit (classic for PsA, ReA).

### Extra-articular History (Uveitis, GI, GU)
* **Ocular**: Unilateral red, painful, photophobic eye (Acute Anterior Uveitis).
* **Cutaneous/Nail**: Psoriatic plaques, nail pitting, onycholysis. Keratoderma blennorrhagicum / circinate balanitis (ReA).
* **GI/GU**: Preceding diarrhea or dysuria (ReA); chronic diarrhea, bloody stools (IBD).

### Axial Physical Examination (Schober's, Chest Expansion)
* **Modified Schober’s Test**: Mark 5cm below and 10cm above PSIS. Patient flexes forward. Normal increase is >5cm. <5cm indicates ↓ lumbar flexion.
* **Occiput-to-Wall Distance**: Patient stands heels/back to wall. Inability to touch occiput to wall = cervical/thoracic kyphosis.
* **Chest Expansion**: Measured at 4th intercostal space. <2.5cm expansion suggests costovertebral joint ankylosis.
* **Sacroiliac Provocation**: Direct SIJ tenderness, FABER (Patrick’s) test +ve.

### Peripheral and Extra-Articular Physical Signs
* **Joints**: Asymmetrical effusion, typically knee/ankle.
* **Digits**: Diffuse swelling (dactylitis), telescoping digits ("arthritis mutilans" in severe PsA).
* **Spine**: Loss of lumbar lordosis, exaggerated thoracic kyphosis ("question mark" posture).
* **Cardiac**: Diastolic murmur at left sternal edge (aortic regurgitation).
* **Respiratory**: Apical dullness/crepitations (apical fibrosis).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnosis for Inflammatory Back Pain
| Differential | Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanical Back Pain** | Worsens with activity, improves with rest; acute onset; no morning stiffness or systemic signs. |
| **DISH (彌漫性特發性骨質增生)** | Older age (>50); preserved SIJ; flowing anterior osteophytes; usually painless or mild stiffness; associated with DM. |
| **Infectious Sacroiliitis** | Acute severe unilateral pain; fever, ↑↑ inflammatory markers; IVDU or immunosuppressed risk. |
| **Spinal Malignancy/Mets** | Unremitting pain (especially night), weight loss, localized spinal tenderness, older age. |

### Differentiating SpA from Rheumatoid Arthritis
| Feature | SpA (PsA/ReA/AS) | Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Symmetry** | Asymmetrical | Symmetrical |
| **Joint Distribution** | Axial skeleton, lower limbs, DIP joints (PsA) | MCP, PIP, wrists, small joints of feet |
| **Primary Lesion** | Enthesitis | Synovitis |
| **Serology** | HLA-B27 (+ve), RF/anti-CCP (-ve) | RF (+ve), anti-CCP (+ve) |
| **Extra-articular** | Uveitis, psoriasis, IBD, aortic regurg | Nodules, ILD, vasculitis |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside and Laboratory Investigations for SpA
* **Bedside**: ECG (monitor for heart block / conduction delays). Urinalysis (hematuria/proteinuria rule out IgA nephropathy).
* **Bloods**: ESR & CRP (elevated in flares, though may be normal in pure axial disease).
* **Genetics**: HLA-B27 typing (high sensitivity for AS, supportive in undifferentiated cases).
* **Immunology**: RF and anti-CCP (strictly -ve in SpA).
* **Microbiology**: Stool culture, urine NAAT for *Chlamydia* (if ReA suspected).

### Imaging Modalities for Axial and Peripheral SpA
* **Plain Radiography (X-ray)**: 
  * SI Joints: Sclerosis, erosions, joint space widening → late fusion/ankylosis.
  * Spine: "Squaring" of vertebral bodies, **syndesmophytes** (vertical bony bridges), "bamboo spine" (竹節樣脊柱), "dagger sign" (ossified supraspinous ligament).
* **MRI SIJ/Spine (Gold Standard for early Dx)**: 
  * STIR/T2FS sequence: Shows **bone marrow edema** (active osteitis/sacroiliitis) *before* X-ray changes.
  * T1 sequence: Shows fat metaplasia (chronic damage).
* **Ultrasound**: Highly sensitive for peripheral enthesitis (thickening, hypervascularity on power Doppler) & dactylitis.

### ASAS Diagnostic Criteria for Axial SpA
* **Pre-requisite**: Patients with ≥3 months back pain & age of onset <45 years.
| Imaging Arm (requires 1+2) | Clinical Arm (requires 1+2) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1.** Sacroiliitis on imaging (MRI active inflammation OR definite X-ray sacroiliitis) | **1.** HLA-B27 positive |
| **2.** ≥1 SpA feature* | **2.** ≥2 other SpA features* |
* **SpA Features**: IBP, arthritis, enthesitis, uveitis, dactylitis, psoriasis, Crohn's/colitis, good response to NSAIDs, family history of SpA, elevated CRP.

### CASPAR Diagnostic Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis
* **Pre-requisite**: Inflammatory articular disease (joint, spine, or entheseal).
* **Criteria**: ≥3 points from the following:
  * Current psoriasis (2 pts) OR personal/family history of psoriasis (1 pt)
  * Typical psoriatic nail dystrophy (1 pt)
  * Negative test for RF (1 pt)
  * Current dactylitis OR history of dactylitis (1 pt)
  * Radiologic evidence of juxta-articular new bone formation (1 pt)

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Management of Acute SpA Articular Flares
* **Axial Flares**: Maximize NSAID dose (e.g., Naproxen 500mg BD or Celecoxib 200mg BD) + PPI. Short course oral steroids generally **ineffective** and not recommended for pure axial flares.
* **Peripheral Flares**: Intra-articular corticosteroid injections for mono/oligoarthritis. Systemic corticosteroids (prednisolone taper) ONLY for severe polyarticular peripheral flares.

### Acute Extra-Articular Emergencies (Acute Anterior Uveitis)
* **Ophthalmology Referral**: Urgent slit-lamp exam to prevent synechiae and vision loss.
* **Topical Therapy**: Corticosteroid drops (e.g., Prednisolone acetate 1%) + Mydriatic/cycloplegic drops (e.g., Cyclopentolate) to relieve ciliary spasm and prevent adhesions.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Non-Pharmacological Management of SpA
* **Physiotherapy (CRITICAL)**: Daily spinal mobility exercises, postural training, hydrotherapy to prevent ankylosis in kyphotic position.
* **Smoking Cessation**: Smoking accelerates syndesmophyte formation and worsens functional outcomes in AS.

### Step-Up Pharmacotherapy Algorithm for Axial SpA
| Step | Intervention | Clinical Notes & HA Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | Continuous NSAIDs | First-line disease-modifying therapy for axial SpA. E.g., Celecoxib, Indomethacin. Must give minimum 2-4 weeks at max tolerated dose. |
| **Step 2** | bDMARD: TNF Inhibitors (TNFi) | If refractory to ≥2 NSAIDs + high BASDAI/ASDAS. Agents: Adalimumab, Etanercept, Infliximab. Requires HA special funding (Samaritan/CCF) screening (Latent TB/Hep B screening mandatory). |
| **Step 3** | bDMARD: IL-17 Inhibitors | Alternative if TNFi fails or contraindicated. Agent: Secukinumab, Ixekizumab. **Do not use in IBD** (may exacerbate bowel disease). |
| **Note** | csDMARDs | **NOT EFFECTIVE** for isolated axial disease. |

### Step-Up Pharmacotherapy Algorithm for Peripheral SpA
| Step | Intervention | Clinical Notes & HA Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | NSAIDs +/- IA Steroids | First line for mild oligoarticular disease. |
| **Step 2** | csDMARDs | **Effective for peripheral joints.** Sulfasalazine (SSZ) or Methotrexate (MTX). SSZ preferred if concurrent IBD. |
| **Step 3** | bDMARD (TNFi or IL-17i) | For csDMARD-refractory peripheral SpA or severe enthesitis/dactylitis. |
| **Step 4** | tsDMARD (JAK inhibitors) | E.g., Tofacitinib, Upadacitinib. Emerging role in refractory PsA/AS. |

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Disease Activity Scoring (BASDAI and ASDAS)
* **BASDAI (Bath AS Disease Activity Index)**: Patient-reported symptom score (0-10) assessing fatigue, spinal pain, joint pain, enthesitis, morning stiffness severity/duration.
  * **Threshold**: BASDAI ≥ 4 indicates active disease requiring step-up therapy (e.g., qualifying for TNFi).
* **ASDAS (AS Disease Activity Score)**: Composite score combining patient-reported symptoms with objective inflammatory markers (CRP or ESR).
  * **Threshold**: <1.3 = Inactive, >2.1 = High activity, >3.5 = Very high activity. Preferred over BASDAI by ASAS.

### Functional Impairment Scoring (BASFI)
* **BASFI (Bath AS Functional Index)**: Patient questionnaire assessing ability to perform daily tasks (e.g., putting on socks, picking up a pen from the floor, reaching high shelves). Higher score = worse physical function and structural damage.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Musculoskeletal Complications of SpA
* **Spinal Fractures**: Ankylosed "bamboo spine" is highly osteoporotic and rigid. Minimal trauma can cause unstable transverse fractures (typically cervical/cervicothoracic junction) → high risk of cord injury.
* **Cauda Equina Syndrome**: Rare late complication of chronic AS, insidious onset of neurogenic bladder and saddle anesthesia.
* **Atlanto-Axial Subluxation**: Can occur in severe disease, risking medullary compression.

### Systemic and End-Organ Complications
* **Cardiac**: Aortitis leading to aortic root dilation, **Aortic Regurgitation**, and conduction defects (AV blocks).
* **Respiratory**: **Apical pulmonary fibrosis**; rigid chest wall (costovertebral ankylosis) causing restrictive lung defect and recurrent infections.
* **Renal**: **IgA Nephropathy**; Secondary **AA Amyloidosis** due to chronic inflammation (presents as nephrotic syndrome).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate the back pain of AS from a prolapsed intervertebral disc?
  Answer: AS causes inflammatory back pain (insidious, waking at night, >30 min morning stiffness, improves with exercise). Disc prolapse causes mechanical/radicular pain (acute, electrical radiation down leg, worsened by movement, improves with rest).
* Trap: Assuming normal X-rays of the sacroiliac joint exclude Ankylosing Spondylitis in a young patient with inflammatory back pain.
  Pearl: Plain X-rays can take 5-10 years to show definitive structural sacroiliitis. You must order an MRI of the SI joints (STIR sequence) to detect early active osteitis/bone marrow edema.
* Pitfall: Prescribing Sulfasalazine or Methotrexate for a patient with purely axial Ankylosing Spondylitis. Conventional synthetic DMARDs (csDMARDs) have zero efficacy for spinal/axial disease; they are strictly indicated only if there is peripheral joint involvement.
* Viva Question: Before starting a patient on Infliximab or Adalimumab for severe AS, what mandatory screening must be performed?
  Answer: Screening for latent infections, crucially Latent Tuberculosis (Quantiferon-TB Gold / CXR) and Hepatitis B surface antigen, as TNF inhibitors can cause fatal reactivation.
* Trap: Treating a patient with concurrent Ankylosing Spondylitis and Crohn's disease with an IL-17 inhibitor (Secukinumab).
  Pearl: IL-17 inhibitors can exacerbate or trigger Inflammatory Bowel Disease. You must use a TNF inhibitor (like Adalimumab or Infliximab) for a patient with SpA and IBD.
