# Lymphoma (淋巴瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Lymphoma
* **Lymphoma (淋巴瘤)**: A heterogeneous group of clonal malignant proliferations arising from lymphocytes (B-cells, T-cells, or NK cells) primarily involving lymph nodes, spleen, or extranodal lymphoid tissues.
* **Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) (霍奇金淋巴瘤)**: Characterized by the presence of multinucleated giant cells called **Reed-Sternberg (RS) cells** within a rich inflammatory background.
* **Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) (非霍奇金淋巴瘤)**: All other lymphomas without RS cells; categorized clinically into indolent (slow-growing) and aggressive (fast-growing) subtypes.

### Etiologies and Risk Factors for Lymphoma
* **Viral Infections**:
  * **Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)**: Strongly associated with Burkitt lymphoma (African endemic), post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD), extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (nasal type), and classical HL.
  * **Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)**: ↑ risk of aggressive NHL (e.g., DLBCL, Burkitt, primary CNS lymphoma).
  * **Human T-lymphotropic Virus 1 (HTLV-1)**: Adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma.
* **Bacterial Infections**:
  * **Helicobacter pylori**: Gastric MALT lymphoma.
* **Autoimmune & Chronic Inflammatory Disorders**:
  * Hashimoto thyroiditis (→ Thyroid lymphoma), Sjögren's syndrome (→ Salivary gland MALT).
  * Coeliac disease (→ Enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma).
* **Immunosuppression**: Solid organ transplant recipients (PTLD), autoimmune therapies.

### Local Epidemiology of Lymphoma in Hong Kong
* **NHL**: Consistently among the top 10 most common cancers and top 10 causes of cancer deaths in HK.
* **HL**: Rare in HK compared to Western populations. The classic "bimodal age distribution" (peaks at 20s and >55 years) is less pronounced in Asian populations.
* **Subtype Prevalence in HK**:
  * **Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)**: Most common NHL globally and locally (~30-40%).
  * **Follicular Lymphoma (FL)**: Less common in HK/Asia compared to the West.
  * **Extranodal NK/T-cell Lymphoma, nasal type**: Higher incidence in Asian populations (strong EBV link).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL)
* **Reed-Sternberg (RS) Cells**: Neoplastic B-cells (mostly) that have lost normal B-cell receptor expression. Characterized by "owl-eye" appearance (bi-lobed nucleus with prominent eosinophilic nucleoli).
* **Immunophenotype**: CD15+ve, CD30+ve, CD20-ve, CD45-ve.
* **Tumor Microenvironment**: RS cells constitute only 1-5% of the tumor mass; they secrete cytokines (e.g., IL-5, TGF-β) that attract a massive reactive infiltrate of T-cells, eosinophils, macrophages, and plasma cells, causing the clinical "B symptoms".
* **Subtypes**: Nodular Sclerosing (most common, broad collagen bands), Mixed Cellularity (EBV associated), Lymphocyte Rich (best prognosis), Lymphocyte Depleted (worst prognosis, HIV associated). Nodular Lymphocyte Predominant HL (NLPHL) is a distinct entity (CD20+ve, CD30-ve).

### Pathophysiology of Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) - Aggressive Subtypes
* **Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)**:
  * **Mechanism**: Rapid proliferation of large neoplastic B-cells effacing nodal architecture.
  * **Genetics**: Translocations involving **BCL6** (3q27), **BCL2**, or **c-MYC**.
  * **Double-hit/Triple-hit**: Rearrangements of MYC plus BCL2 and/or BCL6 → highly aggressive, poor prognosis.
* **Burkitt Lymphoma**:
  * **Mechanism**: Ultra-fast growing tumor of mature B-cells. Histology shows classic **"starry-sky" appearance** (macrophages ingesting apoptotic debris against a dark background of malignant lymphocytes).
  * **Genetics**: **t(8;14)** translocation → **c-MYC** overexpression → uninhibited cellular proliferation. Ki-67 proliferation index is nearly 100%.

### Pathophysiology of Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) - Indolent Subtypes
* **Follicular Lymphoma (FL)**:
  * **Mechanism**: Originates from germinal center B-cells, retaining a nodular/follicular growth pattern.
  * **Genetics**: **t(14;18)** translocation → **BCL2** overexpression → inhibition of apoptosis (immortal cells, slow accumulation).
* **MALT Lymphoma (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue)**:
  * **Mechanism**: Arises in extranodal sites due to chronic antigenic stimulation (e.g., *H. pylori* in stomach). B-cells proliferate dependently on T-cell signals initially, eventually acquiring mutations (e.g., t(11;18)) enabling autonomous growth.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### History and Symptoms of Lymphoma
* **Painless Lymphadenopathy**: Insidious onset, progressively enlarging lumps in neck, axilla, or groin.
* **"B" Symptoms (Systemic)**:
  * Unexplained fever (>38°C).
  * Drenching night sweats (requiring change of clothes/bed sheets).
  * Unintentional weight loss (>10% of body weight over 6 months).
* **HL-Specific Symptoms**:
  * **Alcohol-induced pain**: Severe pain in lymph nodes minutes after consuming alcohol (highly specific but rare).
  * Severe generalized pruritus.
  * Pel-Ebstein fever: Cyclical fever peaking over 1-2 weeks followed by afebrile periods.
* **Extranodal Symptoms (More common in NHL)**:
  * Gastrointestinal: Dyspepsia, hematemesis, melena, abdominal pain/mass (Gastric MALT, DLBCL).
  * Chest/Mediastinal: Dyspnea, cough, facial swelling (SVC obstruction).
  * CNS: Headache, confusion, focal neurology, personality changes.

### Physical Examination Findings in Lymphoma
* **Lymph Nodes**: Usually firm/rubbery, non-tender, discrete initially, becoming matted or fixed if aggressive. Assess cervical, supraclavicular (Virchow's node), axillary, epitrochlear, and inguinal chains.
* **Waldeyer's Ring**: Asymmetric tonsillar enlargement (common in NHL).
* **Abdomen**:
  * **Splenomegaly**: May indicate splenic involvement or portal hypertension.
  * **Hepatomegaly**: May indicate liver infiltration.
  * Palpable abdominal mass (mesenteric/retroperitoneal nodes).
* **Skin/Soft Tissue**: Nodules or plaques (Cutaneous T-cell lymphoma/Mycosis Fungoides).
* **Cardiorespiratory**: Pleural effusion (stony dullness), superior vena cava (SVC) syndrome (facial plethora, engorged neck veins, positive Pemberton's sign).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Lymphadenopathy
| Category | Differential Diagnosis | Discriminatory Features vs Lymphoma |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | **Tuberculosis (結核病)** | Tender/matting nodes (scrofula), caseating granulomas on FNA, +ve AFB/GeneXpert. |
| | **Infectious Mononucleosis (EBV/CMV)** | Acute onset, tender nodes, severe pharyngitis, +ve Monospot test, atypical lymphocytes. |
| | **HIV Infection** | Generalized lymphadenopathy, opportunistic infections, +ve HIV serology. |
| **Autoimmune** | **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)** | Malar rash, arthritis, +ve ANA/dsDNA, cytopenias. |
| | **Sarcoidosis (結節病)** | Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy on CXR, non-caseating granulomas, ↑ ACE levels. |
| **Malignancies** | **Metastatic Carcinoma** | Hard, craggy, fixed nodes. Nasopharyngeal Ca (cervical), Breast Ca (axillary), Lung Ca/GI (supraclavicular). |
| | **Leukaemia (CLL/ALL)** | Pronounced leukocytosis on CBC, predominantly bone marrow/blood involvement (flow cytometry diagnostic). |

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Bedside and Blood Investigations for Lymphoma
* **Bedside Tests**:
  * **ECG**: Baseline assessment before giving cardiotoxic anthracyclines (Doxorubicin).
* **Baseline Bloods**:
  * **CBC & Peripheral Blood Smear**: To check for marrow infiltration (cytopenias) or leukemic phase.
  * **Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)**: ↑ levels indicate high tumor burden/turnover; critical for IPI prognostic scoring.
  * **Urate**: ↑ levels indicate spontaneous tumor lysis.
  * **ESR**: ↑ in classical HL (used for risk stratification in early-stage disease).
  * **Renal & Liver Function (RFT/LFT)**: Pre-chemotherapy baseline, evaluate organ infiltration.
* **Infectious Disease Screening (CRITICAL prior to immunosuppression)**:
  * **Hepatitis B & C Serology** (HBsAg, anti-HBs, anti-HBc): HK is endemic. Rituximab can cause fatal HBV reactivation.
  * **HIV Serology**: Changes management and prognosis.

### Imaging Studies for Lymphoma
* **FDG PET-CT (Positron Emission Tomography-Computed Tomography)**:
  * **Gold standard** for initial staging and end-of-treatment response assessment (Deauville criteria).
  * Identifies nodal and extranodal FDG-avid lesions.
* **CT Neck/Thorax/Abdomen/Pelvis with Contrast**:
  * Alternative staging tool if PET-CT is unavailable, or for non-FDG-avid histologies (e.g., some low-grade lymphomas).
* **Echocardiogram (Echo) / MUGA Scan**:
  * Mandatory to assess baseline Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) prior to Anthracycline therapy.

### Biopsy and Histopathological Criteria for Lymphoma
* **Excisional Lymph Node Biopsy**:
  * **Gold standard diagnostic test**. Requires intact nodal architecture to grade the follicle patterns and identify cellular background.
  * *Note: Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) is generally INADEQUATE for initial diagnosis.*
* **Immunohistochemistry (IHC) / Flow Cytometry**:
  * Differentiates subtypes (e.g., B-cell: CD19/CD20+; T-cell: CD3/CD5+; HL: CD15/CD30+).
* **Bone Marrow Aspirate & Trephine Biopsy (BMAT)**:
  * Used for staging to detect marrow involvement (Ann Arbor Stage IV).
  * *Modern practice*: May be omitted in HL and DLBCL if PET-CT clearly shows no marrow involvement or if clinical management will not change. Still required for indolent lymphomas.
* **Lumbar Puncture (LP)**:
  * CSF analysis/flow cytometry for high-risk DLBCL (high IPI, multi-extranodal, testicular involvement) or Burkitt to check CNS involvement.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Management of Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS)
* **Risk**: High tumor burden, high-grade lymphomas (Burkitt, DLBCL) post-initiation of chemotherapy.
* **Signs**: ↑ K+, ↑ PO4, ↑ Urate, ↓ Ca2+, acute kidney injury.
* **Resuscitation Protocol**:
  * Aggressive IV hydration (Normal Saline) to maintain high urine output.
  * Prophylaxis/Treatment: **Allopurinol** (xanthine oxidase inhibitor) for low/intermediate risk; **Rasburicase** (recombinant urate oxidase) for high risk or established TLS.
  * Correct hyperkalemia (IV Calcium Gluconate, Insulin+Dextrose) and hypocalcemia (only if symptomatic, to avoid calcium-phosphate precipitation).

### Management of Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Obstruction
* **Risk**: Large anterior mediastinal mass (e.g., primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma, T-LBL).
* **Resuscitation Protocol**:
  * Sit patient upright, provide supplemental oxygen.
  * **High-dose Dexamethasone** (reduces tumor edema and lymphotoxic effect).
  * Endovascular stenting for rapid symptomatic relief if severely compromised.
  * Urgent definitive treatment with chemotherapy or radiotherapy once biopsy is secured.

### Management of Spinal Cord Compression
* **Signs**: Back pain, lower limb weakness, sensory level, bowel/bladder dysfunction.
* **Resuscitation Protocol**:
  * Urgent whole spine MRI.
  * Immediate **IV Dexamethasone** (typically 16mg daily in divided doses).
  * Urgent decompressive surgery or radiotherapy/chemotherapy depending on the radio/chemo-sensitivity of the tumor.

### Management of Neutropenic Sepsis
* **Definition**: Neutrophils <0.5 x 10^9/L + Fever >38.3°C or sustained >38°C (usually 7-14 days post-chemo).
* **Resuscitation Protocol**:
  * Draw blood cultures (central and peripheral) IMMEDIATELY. Do not wait for temperature spikes.
  * Empirical broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour (e.g., **Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin)** or Cefepime). Add Vancomycin if line infection or MRSA suspected.
  * Source isolation (barrier nursing). Consider G-CSF if profound/prolonged.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Treatment Algorithm for Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL)
* **Standard First-line**: **ABVD Regimen** (Adriamycin [Doxorubicin], Bleomycin, Vinblastine, Dacarbazine).
* **PET-Adapted Therapy**:
  * Perform interim PET-CT after 2 cycles (PET-2).
  * If PET-2 is negative: De-escalate (drop Bleomycin → continue AVD) to avoid pulmonary toxicity.
  * If PET-2 is positive: Escalate to more intensive regimen (e.g., BEACOPP).
* **Radiotherapy (RT)**: Involved-Site Radiotherapy (ISRT) used for early-stage (I/II) disease or bulky disease to minimize late toxicities.
* **Relapsed/Refractory HL**:
  * Salvage chemotherapy (e.g., ICE or DHAP) followed by **Autologous Stem Cell Transplant (ASCT)**.
  * Targeted agents: **Brentuximab vedotin** (anti-CD30 antibody-drug conjugate), **Pembrolizumab/Nivolumab** (PD-1 inhibitors).

### Treatment Algorithm for Aggressive NHL (e.g., DLBCL)
* **Standard First-line**: **R-CHOP Regimen** (Rituximab [anti-CD20], Cyclophosphamide, Hydroxydaunorubicin [Doxorubicin], Oncovin [Vincristine], Prednisolone) every 21 days for 6 cycles.
* **CNS Prophylaxis**: For high-risk DLBCL (high IPI, dual expressors), add Intrathecal Methotrexate or high-dose systemic IV Methotrexate.
* **Relapsed/Refractory DLBCL**:
  * Salvage chemotherapy (e.g., R-ICE) → ASCT if chemosensitive.
  * **CAR-T Cell Therapy** (Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-cell, e.g., Tisagenlecleucel) for ASCT failures.
* **Burkitt Lymphoma**:
  * Highly intensive, short-course regimens (e.g., CODOX-M/IVAC) + mandatory CNS prophylaxis. R-CHOP is INADEQUATE.

### Treatment Algorithm for Indolent NHL (e.g., Follicular & MALT)
* **Follicular Lymphoma**:
  * **Asymptomatic / Low tumor burden**: **"Watch and Wait"** (observation). Early treatment does not improve overall survival.
  * **Symptomatic / High tumor burden**: **R-Bendamustine** or R-CHOP, followed by Rituximab maintenance (every 2 months for 2 years).
* **Gastric MALT Lymphoma**:
  * First step: **H. pylori eradication therapy** (PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin). This cures ~70-80% of localized gastric MALT.
  * If refractory or H. pylori negative: Involved-site Radiotherapy or Rituximab.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Ann Arbor Staging System for Lymphoma
| Stage | Description | Modifier Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage I** | Involvement of a single lymph node region (I) or single extranodal site (IE). | **A**: Absence of B symptoms. |
| **Stage II** | Two or more lymph node regions on the SAME side of the diaphragm. | **B**: Presence of B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss). |
| **Stage III** | Lymph node regions on BOTH sides of the diaphragm. | **E**: Extranodal extension from an adjacent node. |
| **Stage IV** | Diffuse/disseminated involvement of one or more extralymphatic organs (e.g., bone marrow, liver, lung parenchyma), with or without node involvement. | **X**: Bulky disease (>10 cm mass or >1/3 of transthoracic diameter). |

### International Prognostic Index (IPI) for Aggressive NHL
* **Purpose**: Predicts overall survival in DLBCL.
* **Criteria (APLES)** (1 point each; higher score = worse prognosis):
  * **A**ge: > 60 years.
  * **P**erformance Status (ECOG): ≥ 2 (symptomatic, in bed >50% of the day).
  * **L**DH: Elevated above normal.
  * **E**xtranodal sites: > 1 site involved.
  * **S**tage: Ann Arbor Stage III or IV.

### Hasenclever Index (IPS) for Advanced Hodgkin Lymphoma
* **Purpose**: Prognostic score for advanced (Stage III/IV) HL.
* **Criteria (7 factors, 1 point each)**:
  * Serum Albumin < 40 g/L.
  * Hemoglobin < 10.5 g/dL.
  * Male sex.
  * Age ≥ 45 years.
  * Stage IV disease.
  * Leukocytosis (WBC ≥ 15 x 10^9/L).
  * Lymphopenia (Lymphocytes < 0.6 x 10^9/L or < 8% of WBC).

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### Direct Disease Complications of Lymphoma
* **Hematological**: Bone marrow failure (anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia) due to malignant infiltration.
* **Immunological**: Secondary immunodeficiency (hypogammaglobulinemia in CLL/small lymphocytic lymphoma), Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA), Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP).
* **Mechanical**: Airway/SVC obstruction, ureteric obstruction (retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy → hydronephrosis), biliary obstruction.

### Chemotherapy and Radiotherapy Adverse Effects
* **Anthracyclines (Doxorubicin)**: Irreversible dilated cardiomyopathy / heart failure (dose-dependent limit).
* **Bleomycin**: Interstitial pulmonary fibrosis (exacerbated by high FiO2; strict restriction of oxygen during subsequent surgeries).
* **Vincristine**: Peripheral sensory-motor neuropathy (glove-and-stocking), autonomic neuropathy (paralytic ileus, constipation).
* **Cyclophosphamide**: Hemorrhagic cystitis (prevented by Mesna and hydration), secondary leukemia, infertility.
* **Radiotherapy (Mantle Field/Mediastinal)**:
  * Early: Mucositis, dysphagia, radiation pneumonitis.
  * Late (years later): **Secondary malignancies** (breast cancer, lung cancer, thyroid cancer), radiation-induced accelerated coronary artery disease, hypothyroidism.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the significance of the "B" classification in the Ann Arbor staging system? Answer: It indicates the presence of systemic B symptoms (unexplained fever >38°C, drenching night sweats, weight loss >10% over 6 months), which mandates more aggressive treatment and confers a worse prognosis.
* Trap: Relying on Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC) to definitively diagnose lymphoma. Pearl: FNAC destroys the tissue architecture. An excisional lymph node biopsy is the gold standard required to evaluate nodal architecture, confirm follicular patterns, and assess the cellular microenvironment (vital for HL).
* Pitfall: Starting a patient on R-CHOP for DLBCL without checking Hepatitis B serology in Hong Kong. This can lead to fulminant hepatic failure from HBV reactivation. Always screen (HBsAg, anti-HBc) and start antiviral prophylaxis (e.g., Entecavir) before giving Rituximab.
* Viva Question: A patient with gastric MALT lymphoma tests positive for Helicobacter pylori. What is the first-line treatment? Answer: Triple therapy for H. pylori eradication (PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin). Eradication alone induces complete lymphoma remission in the majority of early-stage MALT cases.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with rapidly enlarging lymph nodes and a massive LDH elevation simply needs standard chemotherapy. Pearl: This profile (especially Burkitt or DLBCL) implies a massive tumor burden at high risk for Tumor Lysis Syndrome upon starting treatment. Pre-hydrate aggressively and start Allopurinol or Rasburicase before initiating chemo.
* Pitfall: Giving high-flow oxygen unnecessarily to a patient who previously received ABVD for Hodgkin Lymphoma. Bleomycin causes lung toxicity, and exposure to high fractional inspired oxygen (FiO2) even years later can precipitate fatal acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) or pulmonary fibrosis. Maintain oxygen saturations >90% but avoid excessive FiO2.
