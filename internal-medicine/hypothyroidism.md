# Hypothyroidism (甲狀腺功能減退症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Primary Hypothyroidism
* **Clinical Definition:** A clinical syndrome resulting from ↓ production and secretion of thyroid hormones (T3, T4) from the thyroid gland, leading to a generalized slowing of metabolic processes.

### Etiologies of Primary Hypothyroidism
* **Autoimmune:** **Hashimoto's Thyroiditis (橋本氏甲狀腺炎)** (most common in iodine-sufficient regions like HK).
* **Iatrogenic:** 
  * Post-ablative [Radioactive Iodine (RAI) therapy for thyrotoxicosis].
  * Post-surgical (Total/subtotal thyroidectomy).
  * External beam radiation therapy to neck (e.g., for Head & Neck cancers, Lymphoma).
* **Drug-Induced:**
  * **Amiodarone (胺碘酮):** via Wolff-Chaikoff effect (high iodine load).
  * **Lithium (鋰):** inhibits hormone release.
  * **Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors:** e.g., Sunitinib.
  * **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors:** e.g., Pembrolizumab.
* **Nutritional:** Severe iodine deficiency (rare in HK, common in developing nations).
* **Infiltrative:** Riedel's thyroiditis, Amyloidosis, Sarcoidosis.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** Affects ~5-10% of women and ~1-2% of men globally; highly prevalent in HK HA primary care clinics.
* **Demographics:** Strong female predominance (F:M = 5:1 to 10:1); incidence ↑ with age.
* **Context:** HK is iodine-replete; therefore, Hashimoto's thyroiditis accounts for >90% of spontaneous cases.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Hashimoto's Thyroiditis
* **Mechanism:** Autoimmune destruction of thyroid follicles mediated by CD8+ cytotoxic T-cells and autoantibodies.
* **Key Autoantibodies:** 
  * **Anti-Thyroid Peroxidase (Anti-TPO)**: inhibits organification of iodine.
  * **Anti-Thyroglobulin (Anti-Tg)**.
* **Histology:** Extensive lymphocytic infiltration with germinal center formation and metaplastic **Hürthle cells**.
* **Progression:** Initial transient thyrotoxicosis ("Hashitoxicosis") from follicle destruction → progressive fibrosis → permanent hypothyroidism.

### Mechanisms of Drug-Induced Hypothyroidism
* **Amiodarone:** Contains ~37% iodine by weight. Massive iodine load triggers the **Wolff-Chaikoff effect** (autoregulatory inhibition of iodine organification and thyroid hormone synthesis). Gland fails to "escape" this block in susceptible individuals.
* **Lithium:** Concentrates in thyroid, directly inhibiting colloid pinocytosis and release of T3/T4 into circulation.

### Pathophysiology of Myxoedema (黏液性水腫)
* **Mechanism:** ↓ thyroid hormone leads to ↑ accumulation of hydrophilic glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) (e.g., hyaluronic acid) in interstitial spaces.
* **Effect:** Traps water, leading to classic non-pitting edema, thickened skin, macroglossia, and effusions.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Features of Hypothyroidism
* **Constitutional:** Severe fatigue, lethargy, cold intolerance, unexplained weight gain (despite poor appetite).
* **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Poor memory, slowed thinking, depression, paresthesia.
* **Gastrointestinal:** Severe constipation.
* **Reproductive:** Menorrhagia (early), oligomenorrhea/amenorrhea (late), ↓ libido, infertility.
* **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle cramps, arthralgia, weakness.

### General and Facies Physical Signs of Hypothyroidism
* **Myxoedema Facies:** Dull, expressionless, puffy face with periorbital edema.
* **Queen Anne's Sign:** Loss of the outer 1/3 of the eyebrows.
* **Oropharynx:** **Macroglossia (巨舌)**, hoarse/croaky voice.
* **Vitals:** Hypothermia, weight gain.

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Hypothyroidism
* **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, faint heart sounds (if pericardial effusion), mild diastolic hypertension.
* **Neurological:** **Delayed relaxation phase of deep tendon reflexes (Woltman's sign)**, proximal myopathy, bilateral Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (正中神經受壓), cerebellar ataxia (rare).
* **Dermatological:** Dry/coarse/cold skin, brittle nails, coarse/thinning hair, generalized non-pitting edema.
* **Endocrine:** +/- Goitre (firm, rubbery in Hashimoto's; absent in post-ablative).

### Presentation of Myxoedema Coma (黏液性水腫昏迷)
* **Neurological:** Profound altered mental status, stupor, coma, seizures.
* **Vitals:** Severe hypothermia (< 35.5°C), severe bradycardia, hypoventilation (type 2 respiratory failure), hypotension.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Hypothyroidism Symptoms
| Condition | Key Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Euthyroid Sick Syndrome (正常甲狀腺病態綜合徵)** | Patient is critically ill (ICU/sepsis). Symptoms masked by primary severe illness. |
| **Depression** | Lacks physical myxoedema signs (no delayed reflexes, normal skin/hair); isolated psychomotor retardation. |
| **Chronic Fatigue Syndrome** | Normal physical exam; profound post-exertional malaise; normal reflexes. |
| **Nephrotic Syndrome** | Edema is **pitting**; profound proteinuria; normal core temperature/heart rate. |
| **Secondary Hypothyroidism (Pituitary)** | Lack of goitre; often accompanied by signs of other pituitary hormone deficits (e.g., amenorrhea, pallor, visual field defects). |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Hypothyroidism
* **ECG:** **Sinus bradycardia**, **low voltage QRS complexes**, flattened or inverted T waves (can indicate concurrent pericardial effusion or diffuse myocardial infiltration).
* **Urinalysis:** R/o proteinuria (differentiating from nephrotic syndrome).

### Biochemical Blood Investigations for Hypothyroidism
* **Thyroid Function Tests (TFTs):** Primary test. ↑ TSH, ↓ Free T4.
* **Autoantibody Screen:** **Anti-TPO** (+ve in 90% Hashimoto's), **Anti-Tg**.
* **Lipid Profile:** Hypercholesterolemia (↓ LDL receptor clearance).
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Normocytic or macrocytic anemia (due to ↓ B12 absorption in associated pernicious anemia, or direct effect of ↓ T4).
* **Renal/Electrolytes:** Hyponatremia (dilutional from ↓ free water clearance), ↑ Creatine Kinase (CK) (myopathy).

### Imaging Studies for Hypothyroidism
* **Ultrasound Neck:** Indicated only if nodular goitre is palpated or rapid enlargement occurs (r/o thyroid lymphoma). Shows heterogeneous, hypoechoic parenchyma in Hashimoto's.
* **Echocardiogram:** Indicated if cardiomegaly on CXR or low voltage ECG to detect **Pericardial Effusion**.

### Diagnostic Criteria for Thyroid States
| Thyroid State | TSH Level | Free T4 Level | Free T3 Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Overt Primary Hypothyroidism** | ↑↑ (Usually >10 mIU/L) | ↓ | ↓ or Normal |
| **Subclinical Hypothyroidism** | ↑ (Usually 4.5 - 10 mIU/L) | Normal | Normal |
| **Sick Euthyroid Syndrome** | Normal or ↓ | Normal or ↓ | **↓↓ (↑ Reverse T3)** |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Management Protocol for Myxoedema Coma (黏液性水腫昏迷)
* **Setting:** Immediate ICU admission.
* **Airway/Breathing:** Intubation/mechanical ventilation often required for hypercapnic respiratory failure.
* **Steroid Administration (CRITICAL FIRST STEP):** Give **IV Hydrocortisone 100mg Q8H** *BEFORE* starting thyroid hormone replacement to prevent precipitating an acute Addisonian crisis (coexisting autoimmune adrenal insufficiency is common).
* **Thyroid Replacement:** 
  * **IV Levothyroxine (T4):** Loading dose 200-400 mcg IV, followed by 50-100 mcg IV daily until oral route tolerated.
  * +/- **IV Liothyronine (T3):** (Optional/adjunct) for rapid onset, but high risk of arrhythmias; use caution in elderly/IHD.
* **Supportive Care:** 
  * **Passive rewarming** only (active rewarming causes vasodilation and severe hypotension).
  * Cautious IV crystalloids for hypotension (avoid fluid overload).
  * Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (sepsis is a common precipitant).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Levothyroxine (左旋甲狀腺素) Initiation and Titration Algorithm
* **Standard Adult Dosing:** Start at **1.6 mcg/kg/day** PO (usually 50-100 mcg daily).
* **Elderly / Ischaemic Heart Disease (IHD):** 
  * Start low at **12.5 - 25 mcg/day** to prevent precipitating angina, arrhythmias, or myocardial infarction.
  * Titrate slowly by 25 mcg increments every 4-6 weeks.
* **Monitoring:** Check TSH every 6-8 weeks after any dose adjustment. Once stable, check TFTs annually.
* **Therapeutic Target:** TSH in lower half of reference range (typically 0.5 - 2.5 mIU/L).

### Levothyroxine Absorption Issues and Counseling
* **Timing:** Must be taken on an **empty stomach** (fasting), typically 30-60 mins before breakfast.
* **Interactions (The "4-Hour Rule"):** Must separate Levothyroxine administration by at least 4 hours from:
  * Iron supplements (Ferrous sulphate).
  * Calcium supplements (Calcium carbonate).
  * Antacids (Aluminium hydroxide).
  * Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs).

### Management of Hypothyroidism in Pregnancy
* **Pathophysiology:** ↑ estrogen ↑ Thyroid Binding Globulin (TBG) → ↓ Free T4. Also ↑ fetal demand.
* **Action upon Pregnancy Confirmation:** Immediately **↑ Levothyroxine dose by 30-50%** (e.g., take 2 extra doses per week).
* **Strict Trimester-Specific TSH Targets:** To prevent fetal neurodevelopmental defects and miscarriage.
  * 1st Trimester: < 2.5 mIU/L
  * 2nd/3rd Trimester: < 3.0 mIU/L
* **Monitoring:** Check TFTs every 4 weeks during the first half of pregnancy.

### Management Algorithm for Subclinical Hypothyroidism (亞臨床甲狀腺功能減退症)
* **Definition:** ↑ TSH with normal Free T4.
* **Treat with Levothyroxine IF:**
  * TSH > 10 mIU/L (high risk of progression to overt hypothyroidism and heart failure).
  * TSH is 4.5 - 10 mIU/L **AND** any of the following are present:
    * Highly symptomatic.
    * Positive Anti-TPO antibodies.
    * Pregnancy or actively planning pregnancy.
    * Goitre present.
* **No Treatment:** If elderly (>70 yrs) with mild ↑ TSH (4.5-7.0), treatment may cause harm (AF, osteoporosis); observe and monitor TFTs every 6-12 months.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Diagnostic Scoring for Myxoedema Coma (Popoviciu's Scoring System)
* **Purpose:** Quantifies probability of Myxoedema Coma in severe hypothyroid patients.
* **Parameters Evaluated:**
  * Hypothermia severity.
  * CNS disturbance (lethargy vs coma).
  * GI issues (decreased motility/ileus).
  * Precipitating event present.
  * Cardiovascular (bradycardia, ECG changes).
* **Interpretation:** Score >60 = diagnostic; 25-59 = at risk; <25 = unlikely.

### Prognosis of Hypothyroidism
* **Autoimmune (Hashimoto's):** Irreversible; requires lifelong Levothyroxine replacement. Excellent prognosis with adherence.
* **Drug-Induced (e.g., Amiodarone):** Often reversible upon discontinuation of offending agent, though Amiodarone's long half-life means recovery takes months.
* **Myxoedema Coma:** Highly lethal; mortality rate 20-60% even with aggressive ICU management.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Untreated Hypothyroidism
* **Cardiovascular:** **Pericardial effusion**, accelerated atherosclerosis (due to hyperlipidemia), worsening of heart failure, bradyarrhythmias.
* **Neurological:** Peripheral neuropathy, irreversible cognitive decline/dementia (if prolonged untreated).
* **Reproductive:** Infertility, high risk of miscarriage, pre-eclampsia, congenital cognitive impairment in offspring (cretinism).
* **Hematological:** Anemia.
* **Metabolic:** Severe hyponatremia.

### Adverse Effects of Levothyroxine Over-Replacement (Iatrogenic Thyrotoxicosis)
* **Cardiovascular:** **Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動)**, exacerbation of angina, palpitations.
* **Musculoskeletal:** Accelerated bone resorption leading to **Osteoporosis (骨質疏鬆症)**.
* **Systemic:** Weight loss, heat intolerance, anxiety, tremors.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic TFT pattern in Sick Euthyroid Syndrome and why does it occur? Answer: Low/normal TSH, low Free T3, and elevated Reverse T3 (rT3). It occurs because severe illness alters peripheral deiodinase activity, converting T4 to inactive rT3 instead of active T3 to conserve energy.
* Trap: Prescribing standard Levothyroxine dose (100mcg) immediately for an 80-year-old newly diagnosed hypothyroid patient. Pearl: Always start low (12.5 - 25mcg) and titrate slowly in the elderly or those with IHD to avoid precipitating acute myocardial infarction or arrhythmias.
* Trap: Administering IV Levothyroxine as the very first medication in a patient presenting with Myxoedema Coma. Pearl: Always administer IV Hydrocortisone BEFORE IV Levothyroxine. Thyroid replacement increases basal metabolic rate and steroid clearance, which will precipitate a fatal Addisonian crisis if co-existing adrenal insufficiency is present (e.g., Schmidt syndrome/Polyglandular Autoimmune Syndrome type 2).
* Pitfall: Patient complains their Levothyroxine "isn't working" and TSH remains high despite good compliance. Check their concurrent medications—they are likely taking it simultaneously with morning iron, calcium, or PPIs, which severely impairs absorption.
* Viva Question: A pregnant patient with Hashimoto's has a positive pregnancy test today. What is your immediate action regarding her thyroid medication? Answer: Empirically increase her Levothyroxine dose by approximately 30% (e.g., adding two extra doses per week) immediately to meet the increased maternal and fetal demands, then check TFTs to titrate.
* Viva Question: What is the Wolff-Chaikoff effect in the context of Amiodarone therapy? Answer: Amiodarone contains a massive iodine load. The Wolff-Chaikoff effect is a physiological autoregulatory mechanism where acute high intracellular iodine transiently inhibits thyroid peroxidase, thereby halting thyroid hormone synthesis to prevent hyperthyroidism.
