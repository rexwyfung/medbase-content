# Acute Kidney Injury (急性腎損傷)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Acute Kidney Injury (KDIGO Criteria)
* **Definition**: Abrupt ↓ in kidney function leading to retention of urea/nitrogenous waste & dysregulation of extracellular volume/electrolytes.
* **Diagnosis (requires ≥1)**:
  * ↑ Serum Creatinine (**Cr**) by ≥26.5 µmol/L within 48 hrs.
  * ↑ Serum **Cr** to ≥1.5x baseline within prior 7 days.
  * Urine Output (**UO**) <0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6 hrs.

### Etiologies of Pre-Renal AKI (腎前性)
* **Hypovolemia**: Hemorrhage, GI loss (diarrhea/vomiting), renal loss (diuretics), skin loss (burns).
* **↓ Effective Arterial Blood Volume (EABV)**: Heart failure (Cardiorenal syndrome), sepsis, anaphylaxis.
* **Altered Renal Hemodynamics**: NSAIDs (afferent arteriole constriction), ACEi/ARB (efferent arteriole dilation), renal artery stenosis.

### Etiologies of Intrinsic Renal AKI (腎實質性)
* **Glomerular**: Glomerulonephritis (GN), Goodpasture's, lupus nephritis.
* **Tubular**: Ischemic or Nephrotoxic [contrast, aminoglycosides, myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis)].
* **Interstitial**: Drugs (penicillins, PPIs, NSAIDs), infections.
* **Vascular**: Vasculitis, TTP/HUS, atheroembolic disease, malignant hypertension.

### Etiologies of Post-Renal AKI (腎後性)
* **Luminal Obstruction**: Renal calculi, blood clots.
* **Mural Obstruction**: Urothelial carcinoma, strictures.
* **Extrinsic Compression**: Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (**BPH** - 前列腺肥大), prostate/cervical cancer, retroperitoneal fibrosis.

### Local Epidemiology of AKI in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: Complicates ~10-15% of all Hospital Authority (HA) admissions; mostly elderly (人口老化).
* **Common Subtypes**: Pre-renal & ischemic tubular injury account for >70% of ward AKI.
* **High-Risk Groups**: Diabetics, underlying Chronic Kidney Disease (**CKD** - 慢性腎病), heart failure patients.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Pre-Renal AKI
* **Hemodynamic Compensation**: ↓ Renal perfusion → activation of **RAAS** & SNS → Angiotensin II constricts efferent arteriole (maintains GFR) + Prostaglandins dilate afferent arteriole.
* **Failure of Autoregulation**: Severe/prolonged ↓ perfusion limits compensatory mechanisms → sharp ↓ GFR without structural tubular damage (initially reversible).

### Pathophysiology of Intrinsic AKI (Tubuloglomerular Feedback)
* **Cellular Hypoxia**: Proximal straight tubule & medullary thick ascending limb are highly metabolically active & susceptible to ischemia.
* **Tubular Damage**: ATP depletion → loss of cytoskeletal polarity → cellular detachment & necrosis.
* **Cast Formation**: Sloughed cells form casts with Tamm-Horsfall protein → luminal obstruction → ↑ intratubular pressure → ↓ GFR.
* **Tubular Backleak**: Damaged epithelium allows ultrafiltrate to leak back into peritubular capillaries.

### Pathophysiology of Post-Renal AKI
* **Upstream Pressure**: Obstruction → ↑ retrograde hydrostatic pressure in ureter, renal pelvis & Bowman's space.
* **Filtration Failure**: Bowman's space pressure exceeds net glomerular filtration pressure → abrupt ↓ GFR.
* **Compensatory Vasodilation**: Initial transient afferent vasodilation (via prostaglandins) followed by sustained vasoconstriction (via Thromboxane A2/Angiotensin II).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for AKI Causes
* **Volume Depletion Hx**: Vomiting, diarrhea, poor PO intake, recent major hemorrhage/surgery.
* **Medication Hx**: Recent initiation of ACEi/ARB, NSAIDs (incl. OTC pain patches), diuretics, antibiotics, Chinese herbal medicine (aristolochic acid).
* **Obstructive Hx**: Hesitancy, dribbling, poor stream, nocturia (BPH), visible hematuria, flank colicky pain (stones).
* **Systemic/Vasculitis Hx**: Joint pain, novel rashes, hemoptysis, chronic fever, weight loss.

### Volume Status Examination in AKI
* **Hypovolemia (Pre-renal)**: Dry mucous membranes, ↓ skin turgor, sunken eyes, postural hypotension, resting tachycardia, flat **JVP**.
* **Hypervolemia (AKI complication or Cardiorenal)**: ↑ **JVP**, bilateral basal crepitations, peripheral pitting edema, S3 gallop, ascites.

### System-Specific Physical Signs in AKI
* **Abdominal/Pelvic**: Palpable distended urinary bladder (dull to percussion - post-renal), ballotable kidneys (polycystic), renal bruit (renal artery stenosis).
* **Dermatological**: Maculopapular rash (allergic interstitial nephritis), livedo reticularis / blue toes (atheroembolic disease), palpable purpura (vasculitis).
* **Uremic Signs**: Uremic fetor (ammonia breath), excoriations (pruritus), asterixis (flapping tremor), pericardial rub (uremic pericarditis).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Pre-Renal vs Intrinsic AKI (Clinical Features)
* **Pre-Renal**: History of fluid loss, clinically dehydrated, rapid improvement of Cr within 24-48 hrs after IV fluid challenge.
* **Intrinsic**: History of nephrotoxins/sepsis, euvolemic or fluid overloaded, no Cr improvement with fluid challenge, systemic signs of GN.

### Differentiating AKI vs Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
* **AKI**: No prior renal history, normal baseline Cr, sudden oliguria, normal hemoglobin, normal-sized kidneys on USG (>10 cm).
* **CKD (慢性腎病)**: Known elevated baseline Cr, chronic anemia (↓ EPO), renal osteodystrophy (secondary hyperparathyroidism), small/echogenic kidneys on USG (<9 cm).

### Identifying AKI on CKD (Acute-on-Chronic Renal Failure)
* **Clinical Picture**: Patient with known CKD (e.g., baseline Cr 150 µmol/L) presents with sudden spike (e.g., Cr 350 µmol/L).
* **Discriminator**: Extreme susceptibility; even minor insults (mild dehydration, low-dose NSAID) precipitate severe AKI in CKD patients.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Urine Investigations for AKI
* **Urine Dipstick**: +ve Blood/Protein (GN), +ve Leucocytes/Nitrites (UTI), normal (Pre-renal or Post-renal).
* **Urine Microscopy**:
  * Muddy brown granular casts (Tubular necrosis).
  * Red blood cell (RBC) casts (Glomerulonephritis).
  * White blood cell (WBC) casts (Interstitial nephritis / Pyelonephritis).
* **Post-Void Residual (PVR) Volume**: via bladder scanner (>100-200 mL suggests urinary retention).

### Blood Investigations for AKI
* **Renal Function Test (RFT)**: ↑ Cr, ↑ Urea, ↑/↓ K+, ↓ Na+ (dilutional).
* **Venous Blood Gas (VBG)**: Metabolic acidosis (↓ pH, ↓ HCO3-).
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: ↑ WBC (sepsis), eosinophilia (allergic interstitial nephritis), schistocytes on smear (TTP/HUS).
* **Special Autoimmune Panel (if GN suspected)**: ANA, Anti-dsDNA, ANCA, Anti-GBM, C3/C4, Serum free light chains (myeloma).

### Urinary Indices: Pre-Renal vs Intrinsic AKI
| Index | Pre-Renal AKI | Intrinsic AKI |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fractional Excretion of Na (FENa)** | < 1% (retaining Na to ↑ volume) | > 2% (tubules damaged, wasting Na) |
| **Fractional Excretion of Urea (FEUrea)** | < 35% (useful if on diuretics) | > 50% |
| **Urine Osmolality (mOsm/kg)** | > 500 (concentrated urine) | < 350 (isosthenuria - cannot concentrate) |
| **Urine Sodium (mmol/L)** | < 20 | > 40 |
| **Urea : Cr Ratio** | > 100:1 (disproportionate ↑ Urea) | < 40:1 (proportionate ↑) |

### Imaging & Biopsy in AKI
* **USG Kidneys, Ureters, Bladder (KUB)**: 1st-line imaging to r/o obstructive uropathy (hydronephrosis/hydroureter); assesses renal size/echogenicity.
* **Renal Biopsy Indications**: Unexplained intrinsic AKI, suspected rapid progressive glomerulonephritis (RPGN), delayed recovery (>4 weeks) of suspected tubular injury, systemic autoimmune disease.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Resuscitation & Fluid Management in AKI
* **Fluid Challenge Protocol (for Pre-renal)**: IV Normal Saline 250-500 mL stat over 15-30 mins.
* **Reassessment**: Strictly monitor JVP, lung bases, and UO (aim >0.5 mL/kg/hr).
* **Caution**: Stop fluids immediately if signs of volume overload (crepitations, ↑ JVP) develop.

### Hyperkalemia Acute Stabilization (Ward Protocol)
* **Stabilize Myocardium**: IV Calcium Gluconate 10% (10 mL) stat over 3-5 mins (does not ↓ K+, protects heart).
* **Shift K+ Intracellularly**: IV Insulin/Dextrose (e.g., 10 units Actrapid + 50 mL D50 stat).
* **Excrete K+**: Resonium A (PO/PR) or IV Frusemide (if euvolemic/hypervolemic & producing urine).

### Indications for Acute Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT - AEIOU)
* **A - Acidosis**: Severe metabolic acidosis (pH < 7.1) refractory to medical Rx.
* **E - Electrolytes**: Severe hyperkalemia (K+ > 6.5 mmol/L) with ECG changes refractory to medical Rx.
* **I - Intoxications**: SLIME (Salicylates, Lithium, Isopropanol, Methanol, Ethylene glycol).
* **O - Overload**: Severe pulmonary edema refractory to high-dose IV diuretics.
* **U - Uremia**: Uremic pericarditis, uremic encephalopathy, severe uremic bleeding.

### Nephrotoxin Avoidance & Adjustments
* **STOP "DAMN" Drugs**: **D**iuretics (if hypovolemic), **A**CEi/ARB, **M**etformin (risk of lactic acidosis), **N**SAIDs.
* **Contrast Minimization**: Withhold non-urgent contrast CT scans (Contrast-induced nephropathy risk).
* **Dose Adjustments**: Renally adjust all required medications (e.g., antibiotics, NOACs) based on estimated GFR.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Monitoring Post-AKI
* **Renal Recovery Tracking**: Regular outpatient monitoring of RFTs & urine protein at 3 months post-discharge (AKI heavily ↑ risk of future incident CKD).
* **BP Optimization**: Maintain tight BP control (< 130/80 mmHg) using alternative antihypertensives (CCBs, beta-blockers) until renal function stabilizes.

### Outpatient Medication Re-Introduction Post-AKI
* **ACEi/ARB Re-initiation**: Carefully reintroduce only after Cr returns to baseline/stabilizes, to manage proteinuria/hypertension; requires RFT check 1-2 weeks post-restart.
* **Metformin**: Re-initiate only if eGFR recovers to > 30 mL/min/1.73m² (HK HA guideline threshold).

### Lifestyle Modifications Post-AKI
* **Hydration Education**: Advise adequate PO fluid intake during intercurrent illnesses (e.g., gastroenteritis) to prevent recurrent pre-renal hits (Sick Day Rules).
* **Avoidance of OTC Nephrotoxins**: Counsel strictly against OTC NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Diclofenac, Ponstan) and unidentified Chinese proprietary medicines.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### KDIGO Staging System for AKI (Scoring & Severity)
| KDIGO Stage | Serum Creatinine (Cr) Criteria | Urine Output (UO) Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1** | ↑ Cr ≥26.5 µmol/L OR 1.5 - 1.9x baseline | < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6–12 hrs |
| **Stage 2** | ↑ Cr 2.0 - 2.9x baseline | < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for ≥12 hrs |
| **Stage 3** | ↑ Cr ≥3.0x baseline OR Cr ≥353.6 µmol/L OR RRT needed | < 0.3 mL/kg/hr for ≥24 hrs OR Anuria for ≥12 hrs |

### Prognosis & Phases of Renal Recovery
* **Oliguric Phase**: 1-2 weeks; high risk of volume overload and hyperkalemia.
* **Polyuric (Diuretic) Phase**: GFR recovers before tubular reabsorptive capacity → massive dilute urine output → high risk of hypovolemia and hypokalemia.
* **Recovery Phase**: Weeks to months; Cr normalizes, but up to 30% suffer permanent structural damage leading to long-term CKD.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Cardiovascular & Pulmonary Complications of AKI
* **Fluid Overload**: Acute pulmonary edema, peripheral edema, hypertension.
* **Arrhythmias**: Ventricular fibrillation or asystole secondary to severe hyperkalemia.

### Neurological & GI Complications of AKI (Uremic Syndrome)
* **Neurological**: Uremic encephalopathy (confusion, seizures, coma).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Uremic gastritis, nausea/vomiting, mucosal GI bleeding secondary to uremic platelet dysfunction (impaired aggregation).

### Iatrogenic & Treatment-Related Complications in AKI
* **Central Venous Catheter (CVC) Complications**: Catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSI), central vein stenosis, pneumothorax during insertion for RRT.
* **Dialysis Disequilibrium Syndrome**: Rapid removal of urea during RRT causes osmotic fluid shifts into the brain → cerebral edema & seizures.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate pre-renal AKI from intrinsic renal AKI using urinary sodium? Answer: Pre-renal AKI has intact tubules that aggressively reabsorb sodium to restore volume, yielding Urine Na < 20 mmol/L and FENa < 1%. In intrinsic AKI, damaged tubules cannot reabsorb sodium, yielding Urine Na > 40 mmol/L and FENa > 2%.
* Trap: Prescribing a standard fluid challenge (500ml NS) blindly to any patient with a rising Creatinine. Pearl: Always evaluate volume status clinically first. Giving fluids to a patient with post-renal obstruction or cardiorenal syndrome with pulmonary edema will precipitate respiratory failure.
* Pitfall: Trusting FENa in a patient currently taking loop diuretics. Diuretics force sodium excretion, artificially raising FENa even in true pre-renal states. Use Fractional Excretion of Urea (FEUrea < 35%) instead.
* Trap: Believing an elevated Urea automatically implies intrinsic kidney damage. Pearl: Disproportionately high Urea vs Creatinine (Urea:Cr > 100:1) is classically pre-renal, often exacerbated by upper GI bleeding (blood digestion) or high protein intake.
* Viva Question: What are the "AEIOU" indications for urgent dialysis in AKI? Answer: Acidosis (pH <7.1), Electrolytes (refractory hyperkalemia), Intoxications (SLIME), Overload (refractory pulmonary edema), Uremia (encephalopathy/pericarditis).
