# Pulmonary Hypertension & Cor Pulmonale (肺動脈高壓與肺心病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Pulmonary Hypertension (肺動脈高壓)
* **Pulmonary Hypertension (PH):** Mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) >20 mmHg at rest via Right Heart Catheterization (RHC).
* **Pre-capillary PH:** mPAP >20 mmHg, Pulmonary Artery Wedge Pressure (PAWP) ≤15 mmHg, Pulmonary Vascular Resistance (PVR) ≥3 Wood Units (WU).
* **Post-capillary PH:** mPAP >20 mmHg, PAWP >15 mmHg, PVR <3 WU.
* **Cor Pulmonale (肺心病):** Right ventricular (RV) hypertrophy/dilation ± failure 2° to primary lung parenchyma/vascular disease (excludes left-sided heart disease or congenital heart lesions).

### WHO Clinical Classification of Pulmonary Hypertension
| WHO Group | Etiology Category | Specific Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group 1** | **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)** | Idiopathic (IPAH), Heritable (BMPR2), Connective Tissue Disease (CTD), Congenital Heart Disease (CHD), HIV, Portal HTN. |
| **Group 2** | **PH due to Left Heart Disease** | HFrEF, HFpEF, Valvular disease (Mitral stenosis/regurgitation). |
| **Group 3** | **PH due to Lung Disease / Hypoxia** | COPD, Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD), Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA). |
| **Group 4** | **Chronic Thromboembolic PH (CTEPH)** | Recurrent Pulmonary Embolism (PE). |
| **Group 5** | **PH with Unclear / Multifactorial Mechanisms** | Hematologic (sickle cell), Systemic (sarcoidosis), Metabolic, CKD on dialysis. |

### Local Epidemiology & HA Context in Hong Kong
* **Most Common:** Group 2 (Left heart disease) & Group 3 (COPD/OSA).
* **Group 1 (PAH):** Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) (系統性紅斑狼瘡) is the leading cause of CTD-associated PAH in HK (predominantly young females).
* **Schistosomiasis:** Major global cause of PAH, but extremely rare in HK (except imported cases).
* **Group 4 (CTEPH):** Increasing recognition in HA; screening post-PE is essential.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (Group 1)
* **Endothelial Dysfunction:** Primary initiating event → imbalance of vasoactive mediators.
* **Prostacyclin Pathway:** ↓ Prostacyclin synthesis (PGI2) → loss of vasodilation & anti-proliferation.
* **Nitric Oxide Pathway:** ↓ Nitric oxide (NO) synthase → ↓ cGMP → impaired vasodilation.
* **Endothelin Pathway:** ↑ Endothelin-1 (ET-1) production → potent vasoconstriction & smooth muscle proliferation.
* **Structural Remodeling:** Intimal hyperplasia, medial hypertrophy, adventitial fibrosis → formation of **plexiform lesions** (pathognomonic vascular tufts) → irreversible ↑ PVR.

### Pathophysiology of Cor Pulmonale & RV Failure (Groups 3 & 4)
* **Hypoxic Pulmonary Vasoconstriction:** Chronic hypoxia (COPD/OSA) → diffuse pulmonary arterial constriction (physiological attempt to optimize V/Q match) → pathological structural remodeling.
* **Vascular Bed Destruction:** Emphysema or recurrent PE physically obliterates pulmonary capillary beds → ↑ resistance.
* **RV Mechanics:** RV is designed for volume load, not pressure load.
* **Compensatory Phase:** Chronic ↑ afterload → RV concentric hypertrophy.
* **Decompensation Phase:** RV dilation → tricuspid annular dilation → tricuspid regurgitation (TR) → RV volume + pressure overload → ↓ RV stroke volume → ↓ LV preload → systemic hypotension.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Symptomatic History of Pulmonary Hypertension
* **Early:** Exertional dyspnea (氣促) (often insidious, out of proportion to lung function tests), profound fatigue.
* **Late (RV Failure):** Exertional syncope (暈厥) (fixed cardiac output unable to meet cerebral demand), exertional angina (RV subendocardial ischemia).
* **Congestive Symptoms:** Peripheral edema, abdominal fullness, right upper quadrant pain (hepatic congestion).
* **Hoarseness:** **Ortner's Syndrome** (enlarged pulmonary artery compressing left recurrent laryngeal nerve).
* **Underlying Disease Clues:** Raynaud's phenomenon/arthralgia (CTD), loud snoring (OSA), chronic cough (COPD), prior DVT (CTEPH).

### Cardiovascular Physical Examination Findings
* **Palpation:** Left parasternal heave (RV hypertrophy), palpable P2 at 2nd left intercostal space (pulmonary HTN).
* **Auscultation:** 
  * Loud, ringing **P2** (pulmonary component of 2nd heart sound).
  * Narrow/paradoxical split S2 (if severe RV failure/RBBB).
  * **Pansystolic murmur:** Tricuspid Regurgitation (lower left sternal edge, ↑ on inspiration - Carvallo's sign).
  * **Early diastolic murmur:** Pulmonary Regurgitation (**Graham Steell murmur**).
  * RV S3/S4 gallops.

### Physical Signs of Right Heart Failure & Systemic Congestion
* **Neck:** Elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP) with prominent **'a' wave** (RV hypertrophy/stiff RV) or prominent **'v' wave / absent 'x' descent** (severe TR).
* **Abdomen:** Tender, pulsatile hepatomegaly, ascites.
* **Extremities:** Pitting lower limb edema, peripheral cyanosis.
* **Skin (if SLE/SSc related):** Malar rash, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia, calcinosis.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses of Right Ventricular Failure
| Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features (Clinical) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cor Pulmonale (Group 3/4 PH)** | Prominent respiratory Hx (smoking, chronic cough, snoring) or previous DVT. Lung signs (wheeze, crackles) dominate. |
| **Left-Sided Heart Failure (Group 2 PH)** | Orthopnea, Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND), displaced apex beat, pan-systolic murmur radiating to axilla (MR). |
| **Constrictive Pericarditis** | Hx of TB or radiation. JVP shows prominent 'x' and 'y' descents (Friedreich's sign), Kussmaul's sign +ve, pericardial knock. |
| **Right Ventricular Infarction** | Acute onset chest pain, hypotension, clear lung fields, JVP ↑. |
| **Tricuspid Valve Endocarditis** | IV drug user Hx, fever, splinter hemorrhages, track marks, new TR murmur. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations for Pulmonary Hypertension
* **ECG:** Right axis deviation (>90°), RV hypertrophy (Tall R in V1-V2, deep S in V5-V6), Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB), P pulmonale (peaked P waves >2.5mm in II, III, aVF indicating RA enlargement).
* **Bloods:** NT-proBNP/BNP (marker of RV strain), Liver Function Tests (congestive hepatopathy).
* **Autoimmune Screen:** ANA, Anti-dsDNA (SLE), Anti-centromere / Anti-Scl-70 (Systemic Sclerosis), Rheumatoid Factor.
* **Infection Screen:** HIV, Hepatitis B/C.

### Imaging & Echocardiography for Pulmonary Hypertension
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Enlarged main pulmonary arteries, peripheral "pruning" (oligemia), RV enlargement (loss of retrosternal airspace on lateral view).
* **Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE):** *First-line screening tool*.
  * Estimates RV systolic pressure (RVSP) via TR jet velocity (TRV). 
  * Identifies RV dilation, septal flattening ("D-shaped" left ventricle), RA enlargement.
  * Rules out left heart disease (Group 2) and congenital shunts.
* **V/Q Scan (Ventilation-Perfusion):** Mandatory to rule out CTEPH (mismatched perfusion defects).

### Gold Standard Diagnostic Criteria & Invasive Tests
* **Right Heart Catheterization (RHC):** *Mandatory for confirming Group 1, 3, 4, 5 PH before targeted therapy*.
  * Hemodynamic Criteria: mPAP >20 mmHg. Pre-capillary: PAWP ≤15 mmHg. Post-capillary: PAWP >15 mmHg.
* **Acute Vasoreactivity Testing:** Done during RHC using inhaled NO, IV epoprostenol, or IV adenosine.
  * *Positive response:* Drop in mPAP ≥10 mmHg to an absolute value ≤40 mmHg with preserved/increased cardiac output. Identifies candidates for high-dose Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs).
* **High-Resolution CT (HRCT) Thorax:** For evaluating Group 3 (ILD, emphysema).
* **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA):** For evaluating acute PE / assessing CTEPH clot burden.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Resuscitation of Acute Right Heart Failure (急性右心衰竭)
* **Preload Optimization:**
  * If fluid overloaded (congested): IV Furosemide (frequent boluses or continuous infusion) ± ultrafiltration.
  * If volume depleted (hypotensive, clear lungs): Cautious small fluid boluses (250ml) – *warning: aggressive fluids will rapidly worsen RV bowing and crash LV output*.
* **Afterload Reduction (Pulmonary Vasodilators):**
  * Inhaled Nitric Oxide (iNO) or Inhaled Prostacyclin (Iloprost).
  * Avoid systemic vasodilators which may cause severe systemic hypotension and ↓ RV coronary perfusion.
* **Inotropic Support (To maintain systemic BP and RV output):**
  * **Dobutamine:** Improves RV contractility.
  * **Milrinone:** Inotrope + pulmonary vasodilator (caution: systemic hypotension).
  * **Norepinephrine/Vasopressin:** First-line vasopressors to maintain mean arterial pressure (MAP) > systemic venous pressure (maintains RV coronary perfusion gradient).
* **Oxygenation:** Correct hypoxia (hypoxia triggers pulmonary vasoconstriction). High-flow O2/NIV/Intubation (avoid hypercapnia).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### General Lifestyle & Supportive Management
* **Avoidance:** Strict avoidance of pregnancy (extremely high maternal mortality, >30%), avoid high altitudes, pneumococcal/influenza vaccinations.
* **Supportive Rx:** Long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT) if hypoxemic, careful oral diuretics for fluid balance.
* **Anticoagulation:** Lifelong Warfarin (華法林) indicated for CTEPH (Group 4) and considered in IPAH.

### Pharmacological Algorithm for Group 1 PAH (Targeted Therapies)
* *Note: Requires confirmation by RHC.*
| Pathway Targeted | Drug Class | Specific HA Formulary Agents |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Calcium Channels** | CCBs (Only if Vasoreactive +ve) | High-dose Amlodipine, Diltiazem. |
| **Endothelin** | Endothelin Receptor Antagonists (ERA) | **Bosentan** (non-selective), **Macitentan**, Ambrisentan. |
| **Nitric Oxide** | Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) Inhibitors | **Sildenafil** (Revatio), Tadalafil. |
| **Nitric Oxide** | Soluble Guanylate Cyclase (sGC) Stimulators | **Riociguat** (Cannot combine with PDE-5i). |
| **Prostacyclin** | Prostacyclin Analogues | **Epoprostenol** (continuous IV), **Iloprost** (inhaled), Selexipag (oral IP receptor agonist). |
* **Treatment Strategy:** Initial combination therapy (e.g., ERA + PDE5i) is standard of care for low/intermediate risk. Escalate to IV prostanoids for high risk / clinical worsening.

### Management of Groups 2, 3, 4, 5 PH
* **Group 2 (Left Heart):** Optimize HF therapy (ARNI, SGLT2i, Beta-blockers, Diuretics, MRA). *PAH-targeted therapies are contraindicated.*
* **Group 3 (Lung):** Treat underlying lung disease (Inhalers, LTOT, CPAP/BiPAP).
* **Group 4 (CTEPH):** **Pulmonary Endarterectomy (PEA)** is the definitive surgical cure. Balloon pulmonary angioplasty or medical therapy (Riociguat) if inoperable.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### WHO Functional Classification for Pulmonary Hypertension
| Class | Clinical Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Class I** | No limitation of physical activity. No symptoms on ordinary exertion. |
| **Class II** | Slight limitation. Comfortable at rest, but ordinary activity causes symptoms. |
| **Class III** | Marked limitation. Comfortable at rest, but less-than-ordinary activity causes symptoms. |
| **Class IV** | Inability to carry out physical activity without symptoms. Symptoms at rest or signs of RV failure. |

### ESC/ERS Risk Stratification for PAH (Mortality Prediction)
* Stratifies 1-year mortality risk into **Low (<5%)**, **Intermediate (5-10%)**, or **High (>10%)**.
* **Key Variables assessed:**
  * Clinical signs of RV failure.
  * Progression of symptoms.
  * Syncope.
  * WHO Functional Class.
  * 6-Minute Walk Distance (6MWD) (e.g., Low >440m, High <165m).
  * Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing (CPET) parameters.
  * NT-proBNP levels.
  * Imaging (RA area, Pericardial effusion).
  * Hemodynamics (Right Atrial Pressure, Cardiac Index).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Cor Pulmonale
* **Cardiac Cirrhosis:** Chronic hepatic venous congestion → hepatomegaly → fibrosis → cirrhosis and liver failure.
* **Cardiorenal Syndrome:** Elevated central venous pressure transmitted back to renal veins → ↓ GFR, worsening fluid overload.
* **Arrhythmias:** Atrial flutter and Atrial Fibrillation (AF) secondary to RA stretch (poorly tolerated, leads to rapid hemodynamic collapse).
* **Pulmonary Artery Rupture / Dissection:** Rare but catastrophic complication of severe main PA dilation.

### Adverse Effects of PAH Targeted Therapies
* **PDE-5 Inhibitors (Sildenafil):** Flushing, severe headache, epistaxis, visual changes. *Absolute contraindication with Nitrates*.
* **ERAs (Bosentan):** **Hepatotoxicity** (requires monthly LFT monitoring), peripheral edema, teratogenicity (requires dual contraception).
* **Prostacyclin Analogues (Epoprostenol):** Jaw pain (jaw claudication), severe flushing, diarrhea, line infections (due to central venous catheter).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the most definitive imaging required to rule out Group 4 CTEPH before diagnosing Group 1 PAH? Answer: Ventilation-Perfusion (V/Q) Scan. CTPA can miss chronic distal micro-thrombi.
* Viva Question: Why are pure systemic vasodilators or nitrates dangerous in acute right heart failure? Answer: They decrease systemic vascular resistance (SVR) and lower aortic root pressure, which destroys the driving gradient for right coronary artery perfusion, leading to sudden RV ischemia and cardiovascular collapse.
* Trap: Prescribing PAH-targeted therapies (like Sildenafil or Bosentan) to patients with Group 2 (Left Heart Disease) PH. Pearl: These drugs are contraindicated in Group 2 PH as they increase pulmonary blood flow into a non-compliant left atrium, potentially precipitating acute pulmonary edema.
* Trap: Assuming every patient with pulmonary hypertension gets a Calcium Channel Blocker. Pearl: CCBs are ONLY indicated for the small minority (<10%) of Group 1 PAH patients who have a positive acute vasoreactivity test during RHC.
* Pitfall: Over-diuresing a patient with Cor Pulmonale. The RV relies on adequate preload to push blood against a high pulmonary vascular resistance; excessive fluid removal will severely drop cardiac output and cause hypotension.
* Pitfall: Recommending pregnancy to a female with established PAH. Maternal mortality during labor/post-partum shifts is extraordinarily high (up to 30-50%); pregnancy is contraindicated and safe termination is often advised.
