# Sepsis & Septic Shock (敗血症與感染性休克)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Sepsis-3 Consensus Definitions
* **Sepsis (敗血症):** Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.
    * Clinically: Suspected/documented infection + acute increase in **SOFA score ≥2**.
* **Septic Shock (感染性休克):** Subset of sepsis with profound circulatory, cellular, and metabolic abnormalities.
    * Clinically: Sepsis + vasopressors needed to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg + Serum lactate >2 mmol/L (despite adequate fluid resuscitation).

### Common Pathogens & Etiologies in Hong Kong
* **Gram-Negative Bacilli (GNB):**
    * *Escherichia coli* (Urinary, biliary, intra-abdominal) → High ESBL prevalence in HK.
    * *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (Respiratory, liver abscess, biliary).
    * *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (Nosocomial, neutropenic hosts, bronchiectasis).
* **Gram-Positive Cocci (GPC):**
    * *Staphylococcus aureus* (Skin/soft tissue, line infections, endocarditis) → MRSA endemic in HA hospitals.
    * *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (CAP, meningitis).
    * *Streptococcus suis* (Zoonotic in HK, pork handlers → meningitis, sepsis).
* **Fungal:**
    * *Candida* spp. (ICU patients, central lines, immunocompromised).

### Local Epidemiology & High-Risk Groups
* **Prevalence:** Major cause of ICU admission & in-hospital mortality in HK HA hospitals.
* **High-Risk Cohorts:**
    * Age >65 years.
    * Immunocompromised (Solid organ transplant, active malignancy, neutropenia).
    * Poorly controlled Diabetes Mellitus (糖尿病) → Susceptible to occult abscesses (e.g., Klebsiella).
    * Indwelling medical devices (CVCs, urinary catheters, pacing wires).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogen Recognition & Immune Activation
* **PAMPs & DAMPs:** Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (e.g., LPS in GNB, peptidoglycan in GPC) & Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns.
* **Receptor Binding:** Bind to Toll-Like Receptors (**TLRs**) on macrophages/monocytes.
* **Nuclear Transcription:** Activation of **NF-κB** → Massive transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines.

### Cytokine Storm & Vasoplegia
* **Mediators:** Torrential release of TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6.
* **Endothelial Dysfunction:**
    * ↑ Inducible Nitric Oxide Synthase (**iNOS**) → Massive NO production → Refractory vasodilation (Distributive Shock).
    * Disruption of endothelial tight junctions → Capillary leak syndrome → Intravascular hypovolemia & tissue edema.

### Coagulopathy & Microvascular Thrombosis
* **Procoagulant State:** Cytokines stimulate Tissue Factor (**TF**) expression on endothelium/monocytes.
* **Anticoagulant Suppression:** ↓ Antithrombin III, ↓ Protein C, ↓ Tissue Factor Pathway Inhibitor.
* **Fibrinolysis Inhibition:** ↑ PAI-1 (Plasminogen Activator Inhibitor-1).
* **Result:** Microvascular thrombosis → Impaired tissue perfusion → Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (**DIC**).

### Cellular Hypoxia & Organ Dysfunction
* **Macro-hemodynamic:** Hypotension + hypovolemia → ↓ DO2 (Oxygen delivery).
* **Micro-hemodynamic:** Micro-thrombi + impaired RBC deformability → Capillary shunting.
* **Cytopathic Hypoxia:** Mitochondrial dysfunction (inability to utilize O2 despite adequate delivery) → Anaerobic metabolism → ↑ Lactate production.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History & Infectious Source Screening
* **General Symptoms:** Fevers, rigors, profound lethargy, confusion.
* **Respiratory:** Productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea.
* **Genitourinary:** Dysuria, flank pain, hematuria.
* **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice.
* **Neurological:** Headache, photophobia, altered mental status.
* **Skin/Soft Tissue:** Redness, swelling, pain over wounds/lines.

### Hemodynamic & Vital Signs Assessment
* **Temperature:** Hyperthermia (>38.3°C) or Hypothermia (<36.0°C) → Hypothermia implies poor prognosis.
* **Heart Rate:** Tachycardia (>90 bpm), bounding pulse initially, weak/thready pulse later.
* **Blood Pressure:** Hypotension, widened pulse pressure (early hyperdynamic phase), narrowed pulse pressure (late decompensation).
* **Respiratory Rate:** Tachypnea (>20 breaths/min) → Often the earliest warning sign.

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Sepsis
* **Perfusion Signs:**
    * Early (Warm Shock): Flushed, warm peripheries, brisk capillary refill (<2s).
    * Late (Cold Shock): Mottled skin, cold/clammy peripheries, delayed capillary refill (>3s), cyanosis.
* **Dermatological Signs:** Petechiae, purpura fulminans (suggests meningococcemia or DIC), necrotizing fasciitis, cellulitis, Janeway lesions/Osler nodes (Endocarditis).
* **Respiratory Signs:** Basal crepitations, bronchial breathing, dullness to percussion.
* **Abdominal Signs:** RUQ tenderness (Cholangitis), renal angle percussion tenderness (Pyelonephritis), generalized guarding/rigidity (Peritonitis).
* **Neurological Signs:** Altered GCS, meningism (neck stiffness, Kernig's/Brudzinski's sign).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Table of Shock Differentials & Discriminators
| Condition | Primary Mechanism | Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Septic Shock** | Distributive (Vasodilation, capillary leak) | Fever/hypothermia, known infection source, warm peripheries early → cold later, ↑ Lactate. |
| **Cardiogenic Shock** | Pump failure (AMI, Arrhythmia) | ↑ JVP, S3 gallop, bilateral pulmonary crackles, cold/clammy extremities, chest pain. |
| **Hypovolemic Shock** | ↓ Intravascular volume (Bleed, dehydration) | Flat JVP, dry mucous membranes, melena/hematemesis, clear lungs, cold extremities. |
| **Anaphylactic Shock** | Distributive (IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation) | Urticaria, angioedema, wheeze, stridor, immediate exposure to allergen/drug. |
| **Adrenal Crisis** | Endocrine (Cortisol/Aldosterone deficiency) | History of steroid withdrawal, hyperpigmentation, severe hypoglycemia. |
| **Thyroid Storm** | Endocrine (Severe hyperthyroidism) | Extreme tachycardia out of proportion to fever, goiter, exophthalmos, hyperreflexia. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **ECG:** Rule out AMI as cause of shock, assess for AF/arrhythmias triggered by sepsis.
* **Arterial/Venous Blood Gas (ABG/VBG):**
    * Metabolic acidosis (↓ pH, ↓ HCO3-).
    * Hyperlactatemia (>2 mmol/L indicates tissue hypoperfusion).
* **POCT Glucose:** Hypoglycemia (depleted stores) or Hyperglycemia (stress response).
* **Urine Dipstick:** +ve Nitrites, +ve Leukocyte esterase.

### Blood Investigations & Biomarkers
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Leukocytosis or Leukopenia (poor prognostic sign), Thrombocytopenia (DIC consumption).
* **Renal Function Test (RFT):** ↑ Creatinine, ↑ Urea, electrolyte derangements.
* **Liver Function Test (LFT):** ↑ Bilirubin, ↑ ALT/AST, ↓ Albumin.
* **Coagulation Profile:** ↑ PT/APTT, ↓ Fibrinogen, ↑ D-Dimer (DIC panel).
* **Biomarkers:**
    * **C-Reactive Protein (CRP):** Non-specific acute phase reactant.
    * **Procalcitonin (PCT):** More specific for bacterial infections; used for antibiotic de-escalation.
* **Microbiology:**
    * **Blood Cultures:** Minimum 2 sets (aerobic + anaerobic) from different sites **BEFORE** starting antibiotics.

### Source-Specific Imaging & Sampling
* **CXR:** Consolidations, pleural effusions, free gas under diaphragm (perforated viscus).
* **KUB / Renal US:** Renal calculi, hydronephrosis, emphysematous pyelonephritis.
* **CT Scan (with IV Contrast):** Gold standard for identifying intra-abdominal/pelvic abscesses, bowel ischemia, or necrotizing pancreatitis.
* **Fluid Sampling (if clinically indicated):**
    * Urinalysis & Urine Culture.
    * Sputum Culture & AFB smear.
    * Lumbar Puncture (CSF analysis for suspected meningitis).
    * Ascitic/Pleural/Joint fluid tap for Gram stain, biochemistry, and culture.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### The Sepsis Six Bundle (Complete within 1 Hour)
* **3 IN (Treatments):**
    * High-flow Oxygen (target SpO2 ≥94%).
    * Intravenous fluid resuscitation.
    * Intravenous broad-spectrum antibiotics.
* **3 OUT (Measurements):**
    * Blood cultures (prior to Abx).
    * Serum lactate & Hemoglobin.
    * Urine output monitoring (insert Foley catheter, target >0.5 mL/kg/hr).

### Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation Protocol
* **Agent:** Isotonic crystalloids (Normal Saline or Plasmalyte/Hartmann's).
* **Dose:** **30 mL/kg** administered within the first 3 hours.
* **Monitoring:** Reassess fluid responsiveness dynamically (passive leg raise, IVC ultrasound, pulse pressure variation) to avoid pulmonary edema.

### Vasopressor Therapy (If hypotensive despite fluids)
* **1st Line:** **Noradrenaline (去甲腎上腺素)** via central venous catheter (CVC). Target MAP ≥65 mmHg.
* **2nd Line:** Add **Vasopressin** (up to 0.03 U/min) or **Adrenaline** to ↓ Noradrenaline dosage requirements.
* **Inotropic Support:** Add **Dobutamine** in the presence of myocardial dysfunction (low cardiac output with adequate filling pressures).

### Refractory Septic Shock Management
* **Corticosteroids:** IV **Hydrocortisone** 200mg/day (given as 50mg Q6H) indicated ONLY if hemodynamic stability is not achieved with adequate fluids and high-dose vasopressors.

### Source Control Protocol
* **Timing:** Must occur as rapidly as possible, optimally within **6-12 hours** of diagnosis.
* **Actions:** Abscess drainage, removal of infected necrotic tissue (debridement), removal of potentially infected central lines/catheters, relief of biliary/urinary obstruction.

### Empirical Antimicrobial Guidelines (Hong Kong HA Context)
* **Community-Acquired Sepsis (Unknown Source):** IV Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) + IV Gentamicin OR IV Ceftriaxone.
* **Hospital-Acquired Sepsis / High ESBL Risk:** IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin).
* **Neutropenic Sepsis (High Risk / Pseudomonal cover needed):** IV Meropenem OR IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam.
* **MRSA Risk (Known carrier, line infection, IVDU):** Add IV Vancomycin OR IV Linezolid.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Antimicrobial Stewardship & De-escalation
* **Culture-Guided:** Narrow antibiotic spectrum as soon as culture & sensitivity (C&S) results are available (usually 48-72 hours).
* **Biomarker-Guided:** Use serial Procalcitonin (PCT) levels to guide early cessation of antibiotics.
* **IV to PO Switch:** Transition to oral antibiotics once hemodynamically stable, afebrile for 24h, and absorbing orally.

### Post-Sepsis Syndrome (PSS) Management
* **Definition:** Long-term physical, cognitive, and psychological sequelae affecting sepsis survivors.
* **Rehabilitation:** Early mobilization in ICU, prolonged physical therapy to combat Critical Illness Polyneuropathy/Myopathy.
* **Cognitive/Psychological:** Screen for PTSD, depression, and cognitive impairment; refer to clinical psychology/occupational therapy.

### Long-Term Infection Prevention
* **Vaccination:** Pneumococcal vaccine (PCV13/PPSV23), annual Influenza vaccine, COVID-19 boosters.
* **Comorbidity Optimization:** Strict glycemic control in DM, smoking cessation, optimization of immunosuppressive regimens (e.g., tapering chronic steroids if possible).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### qSOFA Score (Quick SOFA)
* **Utility:** Screening tool outside of the ICU to identify patients at high risk of poor outcomes. Does NOT define sepsis.
* **Criteria (1 point each, ≥2 = High Risk):**
    | Parameter | Criteria for 1 Point |
    | :--- | :--- |
    | **Respiratory Rate** | ≥ 22 breaths/min |
    | **Mentation** | Altered mentation (GCS < 15) |
    | **Systolic BP** | ≤ 100 mmHg |

### SOFA Score (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment)
* **Utility:** Used in ICU settings; ↑ in score of ≥2 defines Sepsis-3. Predicts ICU mortality based on extent of organ dysfunction.
* **Parameters Evaluated (Scored 0-4 per system):**
    | System | Variable Measured |
    | :--- | :--- |
    | **Respiration** | PaO2/FiO2 ratio (needs mechanical ventilation status) |
    | **Coagulation** | Platelet count |
    | **Liver** | Serum Bilirubin |
    | **Cardiovascular** | MAP & Dose of vasopressors required |
    | **Central Nervous System** | Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) |
    | **Renal** | Serum Creatinine or Urine Output |

### Prognostic Indicators
* **Lactate Clearance:** Failure to clear lactate by ≥10% within 2-6 hours correlates strongly with mortality.
* **High Mortality Predictors:** Refractory shock, profound hypothermia, severe comorbidities (cirrhosis, heart failure), advanced age.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Cardiopulmonary Complications
* **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) (急性呼吸窘迫綜合症):** Bilateral pulmonary infiltrates, severe hypoxemia requiring mechanical ventilation.
* **Septic Cardiomyopathy:** Reversible global biventricular dysfunction; ↓ Ejection Fraction with ventricular dilatation.

### Renal & Hepatic Complications
* **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) due to hypotension and microthrombi → Often requires Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT).
* **Shock Liver (Ischemic Hepatitis):** Massive acute elevation of transaminases (AST/ALT >1000) due to severe hypoperfusion.

### Hematological & Neurological Complications
* **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) (彌散性血管內凝血):** Simultaneous bleeding and thrombosis.
* **Sepsis-Associated Encephalopathy:** Delirium/coma not explained by direct CNS infection.
* **Critical Illness Polyneuropathy (CIP):** Flaccid symmetric paralysis, failure to wean off ventilator.

### Iatrogenic / Treatment-Related Complications
* **High-Dose Vasopressors:** Peripheral limb/digital ischemia (necrosis), gut ischemia.
* **Antibiotic Complications:** *Clostridioides difficile* infection, acute interstitial nephritis, nephrotoxicity (Vancomycin, Aminoglycosides).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the Sepsis-3 definition of septic shock? Answer: Sepsis with persisting hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥ 65 mmHg AND serum lactate > 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation.
* Trap: Delaying empirical antibiotics until complex diagnostic imaging or invasive procedures (e.g., LP) are completed. Pearl: Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics must be administered within 1 hour of recognizing sepsis/septic shock; draw blood cultures first, but do not delay Abx if culture extraction is difficult.
* Pitfall: Resuscitating blindly with large volumes of fluid without reassessment. Fluid overload causes pulmonary edema, visceral edema, and worsening mortality. Assess fluid responsiveness dynamically (e.g., passive leg raise test) after the initial 30 mL/kg bolus.
* Viva Question: What is the role of Hydrocortisone in septic shock? Answer: It is indicated only in refractory septic shock where adequate fluid resuscitation and high-dose vasopressor therapy fail to restore hemodynamic stability.
* Trap: Confusing qSOFA and SOFA. Pearl: qSOFA (Respiratory rate, Altered GCS, Systolic BP) is a rapid bedside screening tool for non-ICU settings. SOFA is a comprehensive lab-based organ failure score; an acute change of ≥2 points actually defines sepsis.
