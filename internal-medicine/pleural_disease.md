# Pleural Disease (胸膜疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Pleural Diseases
* **Pleural Effusion (胸腔積液)**: Abnormal accumulation of fluid in the pleural space between parietal and visceral pleura.
* **Pneumothorax (氣胸)**: Presence of air in the pleural space leading to partial or complete lung collapse.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Tuberculosis (結核病)**: Endemic in HK; tuberculous pleurisy is a leading cause of exudative effusions in young-to-middle-aged patients.
* **Malignancy (惡性腫瘤)**: Lung cancer (肺癌) is the most common cancer in HK; frequently presents with malignant pleural effusion (MPE).
* **Primary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (PSP)**: High incidence in young, tall, thin males in HK.

### Etiologies of Transudative Pleural Effusion (漏出液)
* **Congestive Heart Failure (心衰竭)** (Most common)
* **Liver Cirrhosis (肝硬化)** (Hepatic hydrothorax)
* **Nephrotic Syndrome (腎病綜合症)**
* **Hypoalbuminemia (低白蛋白血症)** (e.g., severe malnutrition)
* **Peritoneal Dialysis (腹膜透析)**

### Etiologies of Exudative Pleural Effusion (滲出液)
* **Infective**: Parapneumonic (bacterial pneumonia), TB pleurisy.
* **Malignant**: Primary (Mesothelioma), Secondary (Lung, Breast, Lymphoma).
* **Inflammatory**: Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA).
* **Pulmonary Embolism (肺栓塞)** (Can be transudative or exudative, typically exudative).
* **GI Disease**: Pancreatitis (left-sided), esophageal rupture (Boerhaave syndrome).

### Etiologies of Pneumothorax
* **Primary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (PSP)**: No underlying lung disease; subpleural apical bleb rupture.
* **Secondary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (SSP)**: Underlying lung disease [COPD (慢性阻塞性肺病), TB, Asthma, Cystic Fibrosis, Interstitial Lung Disease].
* **Traumatic/Iatrogenic**: Central line insertion, thoracentesis, mechanical ventilation (barotrauma), rib fractures.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Transudative Effusions
* ↑ Capillary hydrostatic pressure (e.g., Heart Failure).
* ↓ Capillary oncotic pressure (e.g., Nephrotic syndrome, Cirrhosis).
* Pleural membrane remains intact; fluid is ultrafiltrate of plasma.

### Pathophysiology of Exudative Effusions
* ↑ Capillary permeability due to local inflammation (e.g., pneumonia, TB, autoimmune).
* ↓ Lymphatic drainage from the pleural space (e.g., malignant lymphatic blockage).
* Extravasation of protein-rich fluid and inflammatory cells into pleural space.

### Pathophysiology of Spontaneous Pneumothorax
* Rupture of subpleural blebs/bullae → air enters pleural cavity.
* Loss of negative intrapleural pressure → lung elastic recoil causes ipsilateral lung collapse.
* ↓ Vital capacity (VC) and ↓ PaO2 (V/Q mismatch).

### Pathophysiology of Tension Pneumothorax (張力性氣胸)
* One-way valve mechanism created by pleural tear.
* Air enters during inspiration but cannot exit during expiration.
* Rapid ↑ in intrapleural pressure → massive mediastinal shift to contralateral side.
* Compression of superior/inferior vena cava → severely ↓ venous return → obstructive shock and cardiac arrest.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History in Pleural Effusion
* **Symptoms**: Progressive dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, dry cough.
* **Infective clues**: Fever, chills, productive cough, night sweats, weight loss (TB).
* **Malignant clues**: Hemoptysis, significant weight loss, smoking history, asbestos exposure (Mesothelioma).
* **Cardiovascular clues**: Orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND), bilateral lower limb swelling.

### History in Pneumothorax
* **Symptoms**: Sudden-onset unilateral pleuritic chest pain, acute dyspnea.
* **Triggers**: Occurs at rest or during physical exertion/straining.
* **Background**: Smoking history, known COPD/Asthma/TB.

### Physical Examination Signs of Pleural Effusion
* **Inspection**: Asymmetrical chest expansion (↓ on affected side).
* **Palpation**: ↓ Vocal fremitus, apex beat displaced away from massive effusion.
* **Percussion**: **Stony dullness (堅實濁音)** over the effusion.
* **Auscultation**: ↓ or absent vesicular breath sounds, ↓ vocal resonance, aegophony at the upper border of effusion, pleural rub.

### Physical Examination Signs of Pneumothorax
* **Inspection**: ↓ Chest expansion on affected side.
* **Palpation**: ↓ Vocal fremitus.
* **Percussion**: **Hyperresonant (過度反響)**.
* **Auscultation**: ↓ or absent breath sounds, ↓ vocal resonance.

### Physical Examination Signs of Tension Pneumothorax
* **Hemodynamic collapse**: Tachycardia, severe hypotension, cyanosis.
* **Neck**: Elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP), **tracheal deviation** away from the affected side.
* **Chest**: Hyperexpanded hemithorax, absent breath sounds, hyperresonant percussion.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Pleural Effusion vs. Consolidation
| Feature | Pleural Effusion | Consolidation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tracheal Deviation** | Away (if massive) | Central |
| **Percussion** | Stony dull | Dull |
| **Breath Sounds** | Decreased/Absent | Bronchial |
| **Vocal Resonance** | Decreased | Increased (Whispering pectoriloquy) |

### Differentiating Causes of Unilateral vs. Bilateral Effusions
| Type | Typical Etiologies |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Unilateral** | Malignancy, Parapneumonic/Empyema, TB, Pulmonary Embolism |
| **Bilateral** | Heart failure, Hypoalbuminemia, Cirrhosis, Nephrotic syndrome |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Light's Criteria for Exudative Pleural Effusion
* *Criteria:* Exudate if **ONE or more** of the following are met (Specificity ~70-80%, Sensitivity ~98%):
  * Pleural fluid protein / Serum protein > 0.5
  * Pleural fluid LDH / Serum LDH > 0.6
  * Pleural fluid LDH > 2/3 of the upper limit of normal for serum LDH

### Routine Pleural Fluid Analysis Tests
* **Biochemistry**: Protein, LDH, Glucose.
* **Microbiology**: Gram stain, bacterial culture, Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB) smear, mycobacterial culture.
* **Cytology**: Malignant cells, differential white cell count.
  * Lymphocyte-predominant: TB, Malignancy, Lymphoma.
  * Neutrophil-predominant: Parapneumonic, Empyema, Pulmonary Embolism.

### Special Pleural Fluid Tests
* **Adenosine Deaminase (ADA)**: >40 U/L highly suggestive of TB pleurisy.
* **pH Level**: <7.2 indicates complicated parapneumonic effusion or empyema.
* **Triglycerides**: >1.24 mmol/L confirms Chylothorax (乳糜胸).
* **Amylase**: Elevated in pancreatic effusion or esophageal rupture.
* **Rheumatoid Factor (RF) / ANA**: For suspected autoimmune connective tissue diseases.

### Imaging Modalities for Pleural Disease
* **CXR (Erect PA)**:
  * *Effusion*: Blunting of costophrenic angle, homogenous opacity with a meniscus sign (meniscus higher laterally).
  * *Pneumothorax*: Visible visceral pleural edge, absent lung markings peripheral to the edge.
* **Bedside Thoracic USG**: Highly sensitive for small effusions; mandatory for guiding thoracentesis to prevent iatrogenic PTX.
* **CT Thorax with Contrast**: Evaluates underlying lung parenchyma (malignancy, thickened pleura, loculations, empyema split-pleura sign).

### Advanced Diagnostics
* **Pleural Biopsy**:
  * **Image-guided (CT/USG) core biopsy**: Preferred for suspected malignancy (mesothelioma).
  * **Medical Thoracoscopy (VATS)**: Gold standard for undiagnosed exudative effusions; allows direct visualization and biopsy of parietal pleura.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation of Tension Pneumothorax
* **Immediate Needle Decompression**: Large-bore cannula (14G or 16G) inserted into the 2nd intercostal space, mid-clavicular line (or 5th intercostal space, mid-axillary line) *before* CXR.
* **Definitive Rx**: Followed immediately by wide-bore intercostal chest drain (ICD) insertion with an underwater seal.

### Acute Management of Primary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (PSP)
* **Small (<2cm rim at hilum on CXR) & Minimal Symptoms**:
  * Observation + High-flow oxygen (accelerates nitrogen reabsorption).
  * Discharge with early outpatient clinic follow-up.
* **Large (>2cm) OR Symptomatic**:
  * **1st Line**: Needle aspiration (16G cannula via 2nd ICS MCL).
  * **2nd Line**: If aspiration fails (>2.5L aspirated or no resistance felt) → insert small-bore ICD (8-14 Fr) on underwater seal.

### Acute Management of Secondary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (SSP)
* *Higher risk of respiratory failure due to underlying lung disease.*
* **Small (<1cm) & Asymptomatic**: Observe with high-flow O2, admit for 24h.
* **Size 1-2cm**: Needle aspiration. If fails → ICD.
* **Large (>2cm) OR Symptomatic**: Immediate small-bore ICD insertion.

### Acute Management of Parapneumonic Effusion & Empyema (膿胸)
* **Indications for Chest Drain (Complicated Parapneumonic / Empyema)**:
  * Frank purulent fluid (pus) aspirated.
  * Pleural fluid pH < 7.2.
  * Positive Gram stain or culture from pleural fluid.
  * Loculated effusion on USG.
* **Action**: Insert intercostal chest drain + administer broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g., Amoxicillin-Clavulanate) targeting respiratory/anaerobic organisms.
* Intrapleural fibrinolytics (tPA + DNase) if loculated and failing to drain (specialist input).

### Therapeutic Thoracentesis for Symptomatic Effusion
* Drain fluid slowly (maximum 1.5L per session) to relieve severe dyspnea.
* Prevents potentially fatal **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (REPE)**.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of Malignant Pleural Effusion (MPE)
* **Symptomatic Relief Algorithms**:
  * **Chemical Pleurodesis (胸膜固定術)**: Instillation of sterile talc (slurry or via poudrage during VATS) to fuse visceral and parietal pleura. Prevents fluid re-accumulation. Requires expandable lung.
  * **Indwelling Pleural Catheter (IPC)**: Silicone tube placed for intermittent home drainage. Preferred if trapped lung (lung cannot expand) or failed pleurodesis.
* **Systemic Therapy**: Chemotherapy/Targeted therapy directed at the primary malignancy.

### Recurrent Pneumothorax Prevention
* **Indications for Surgical Intervention**:
  * Second ipsilateral PTX.
  * First contralateral PTX.
  * High-risk professions (e.g., pilots, divers).
  * Persistent air leak > 3-5 days despite chest drain.
* **Definitive Rx**: VATS (Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery) with mechanical/chemical pleurodesis and bullectomy.

### Chronic Management of Tuberculous Pleurisy
* **Pharmacological Rx**: Standard DOTS anti-TB regimen.
  * Intensive phase: 2 months of HRZE (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol).
  * Continuation phase: 4 months of HR (Isoniazid, Rifampicin).
* Adjuvant corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone) are *not* routinely recommended unless severe systemic symptoms/massive effusion.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### BTS Size Stratification for Pneumothorax
* Measure the visible rim of air between lung margin and chest wall at the **level of the hilum**.
* **<2 cm**: Small.
* **≥2 cm**: Large (indicates ~50% loss of lung volume).

### RAPID Score for Pleural Infection Prognosis
* Used to predict mortality in pleural infection.
* **R**enal (Urea >5 mmol/L)
* **A**ge (≥65, <50, 50-64)
* **P**urulence (Purulent vs non-purulent)
* **I**nfection source (Hospital-acquired vs Community-acquired)
* **D**ietary/Albumin (<25 g/L)

### LENT Score for Malignant Pleural Effusion
* Predicts survival to guide choice of palliation (IPC vs Pleurodesis).
* **L**DH (Pleural fluid)
* **E**COG performance status
* **N**eutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (Serum)
* **T**umor type (High risk vs Low risk cancers)

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Thoracentesis and Intercostal Chest Drains
* **Iatrogenic Pneumothorax**: Puncturing the visceral pleura during tap.
* **Hemothorax**: Laceration of intercostal artery (always insert needle directly *above* the rib to avoid the neurovascular bundle).
* **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (REPE)**: Rapid drainage of large chronic volumes (>1.5L); presents with severe cough, hypoxia, and frothy sputum post-drain.
* **Visceral Injury**: Liver, spleen, or diaphragmatic puncture if inserted too low.
* **Infection**: Empyema or cellulitis at the insertion site.

### Complications of Untreated Empyema
* **Empyema Necessitatis**: Pus tracking through the parietal pleura and erupting through the chest wall skin.
* **Bronchopleural Fistula**: Rupture into the bronchial tree causing purulent expectoration.
* **Fibrothorax**: Extensive pleural fibrosis and calcification causing chronic restrictive lung defect (trapped lung).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most reliable biochemical marker to differentiate a pleural exudate from a transudate if the patient has been receiving diuretics? Answer: Light's criteria can falsely categorize transudates as exudates in diuretic use; check the serum-pleural fluid albumin gradient (>12 g/L indicates transudate).
* Trap: Performing a diagnostic thoracentesis blindly based on percussion dullness. Pearl: Always use bedside ultrasound to mark the site and confirm fluid presence to avoid iatrogenic organ puncture.
* Pitfall: Waiting for a chest X-ray to confirm a tension pneumothorax. A tension PTX is a clinical diagnosis; awaiting imaging delays life-saving needle decompression and leads to cardiac arrest.
* Viva Question: What are the key pleural fluid criteria mandating chest tube insertion in parapneumonic effusion? Answer: pH < 7.2, presence of frank pus, or positive Gram stain/culture.
* Trap: Discharging a patient immediately after a successful needle aspiration for a primary spontaneous pneumothorax without follow-up. Pearl: Always obtain a post-aspiration CXR and observe for at least a few hours, then arrange early outpatient review to ensure no recurrence.
