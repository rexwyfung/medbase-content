# Cushing's Syndrome (庫欣綜合症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Cushing's Syndrome
* **Cushing's Syndrome (CS):** Clinical state resulting from chronic exposure to excessive glucocorticoids (endogenous or exogenous).
* **Cushing's Disease (CD):** A specific subtype of CS caused by an ACTH-secreting pituitary adenoma (腦垂體腺瘤).

### Epidemiology of Cushing's Syndrome in Hong Kong
* F>M (ratio ~3:1).
* Peak incidence: 20-50 years.
* **Exogenous CS** is the most common cause overall in HK, heavily driven by indiscriminate use of systemic steroids or adulterated Traditional Chinese Medicine (中藥).
* **Endogenous CS** incidence: ~2-3 per million per year.

### Etiologies of Cushing's Syndrome
| Category | Mechanism | Specific Causes | Frequency (Endogenous) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exogenous** | Iatrogenic/Adulterated | Oral/inhaled/topical steroids, adulterated TCM | Most common overall |
| **ACTH-Dependent** | Excess ACTH → ↑ Cortisol | Pituitary Adenoma (Cushing's Disease) | ~70% |
| | | Ectopic ACTH (SCLC, bronchial carcinoid) | ~10-15% |
| **ACTH-Independent** | Autonomous Cortisol secretion | Adrenal Adenoma (腎上腺腺瘤) | ~10% |
| | | Adrenal Carcinoma (腎上腺癌) | <5% |
| | | Macronodular/Micronodular Hyperplasia | Rare |

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Normal HPA Axis Physiology
* Hypothalamus secretes **CRH** → Anterior Pituitary secretes **ACTH** → Adrenal Cortex (Zona Fasciculata) secretes **Cortisol**.
* Cortisol exerts **negative feedback** on CRH and ACTH.

### Pathophysiology of ACTH-Dependent Cushing's
* **Pituitary Adenoma (Cushing's Disease):** Benign corticotroph tumor. Retains *some* sensitivity to negative feedback (suppresses at very high steroid doses). ↑ ACTH → Bilateral adrenal hyperplasia → ↑ Cortisol.
* **Ectopic ACTH Syndrome:** Malignant/neuroendocrine tumor secretes ACTH autonomously. Completely resistant to negative feedback. Profound ↑ ACTH → Massive ↑ Cortisol & ↑ Mineralocorticoid effects (hypokalemia).

### Pathophysiology of ACTH-Independent Cushing's
* **Adrenal Adenoma/Carcinoma:** Autonomous cortisol hypersecretion from adrenal mass.
* ↑ Cortisol causes intense negative feedback → **Profound ↓ ACTH** (Suppressed).
* Contralateral adrenal gland undergoes severe atrophy.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History and Symptoms of Cushing's Syndrome
* **General:** Progressive central weight gain, fatigue, poor wound healing.
* **Psychiatric:** Depression, emotional lability, acute psychosis, insomnia.
* **Reproductive:** Oligomenorrhea/amenorrhea, decreased libido, erectile dysfunction.
* **Exogenous Clues:** History of asthma, autoimmune disease, frequent intra-articular injections, or proprietary/unlabeled TCM pills (黑藥丸).

### Physical Examination: General and Facial Signs
* **General:** **Centripetal obesity** (向心性肥胖) with relative sparing of limbs.
* **Facies:** **Moon facies** (滿月臉), facial plethora (red cheeks due to thin skin/polycythemia).
* **Fat Deposition:** **Buffalo hump** (水牛肩 - dorsocervical fat pad), prominent supraclavicular fat pads.

### Physical Examination: Dermatological Signs
* **Striae:** **Broad purple striae** (紫紋) >1cm wide, typically on abdomen, thighs, breasts, axillae (due to collagen degradation).
* **Skin Atrophy:** Paper-thin skin, easy bruising (senile purpura-like).
* **Androgen Excess:** Hirsutism, acne, male-pattern balding (especially in adrenal carcinoma).
* **Hyperpigmentation:** Found ONLY in ACTH-dependent causes (ACTH co-secreted with MSH); affects palmar creases, buccal mucosa, scars.

### Physical Examination: Musculoskeletal and Cardiovascular Signs
* **MSK:** **Proximal myopathy** (近端肌肉病變) - patient cannot rise from a deep squat or chair without using arms.
* **Cardiovascular:** Elevated blood pressure (Hypertension), peripheral edema.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Pseudo-Cushing's Syndrome
* Conditions causing mild/moderate biochemical hypercortisolism without true Cushing's pathophysiology.
* **Causes:** Severe obesity, Major Depressive Disorder, Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS), Alcohol dependence, poorly controlled Diabetes Mellitus.
* **Discriminatory Features:** Lacks specific discriminatory signs (e.g., broad striae, severe proximal myopathy, unexplained osteoporosis). Biochemistry usually normalizes after treating the underlying cause (e.g., alcohol abstinence).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### First-Line Screening Tests for Cushing's Syndrome
* *Requirement:* Need at least 2 abnormal tests from the following to establish diagnosis. Do not test during acute illness (stress ↑ cortisol).
* **1mg Overnight Dexamethasone Suppression Test (ONDST):** Give 1mg Dexamethasone PO at 23:00. Check serum cortisol at 08:00.
    * *Normal:* Cortisol suppresses to <50 nmol/L.
    * *Cushing's:* Failure to suppress.
* **24-hour Urine Free Cortisol (UFC):** Requires ≥2 adequate collections (check urinary creatinine to ensure adequacy). +ve if > upper limit of normal.
* **Late-night Salivary Cortisol:** Assesses loss of normal diurnal rhythm. Sampled at 23:00-00:00. Needs ≥2 samples.

### Step 2: Plasma ACTH Measurement for Localization
* Once hypercortisolism is confirmed, check 09:00 plasma ACTH.
* **ACTH <1.1 pmol/L (Suppressed):** **ACTH-Independent** (Adrenal source or Exogenous).
* **ACTH >3.3 pmol/L (Normal/Elevated):** **ACTH-Dependent** (Pituitary or Ectopic).
* **ACTH 1.1-3.3 pmol/L (Indeterminate):** Proceed to CRH stimulation test.

### Step 3: Differentiating ACTH-Dependent Causes (Pituitary vs Ectopic)
| Test | Cushing's Disease (Pituitary) | Ectopic ACTH Syndrome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High-Dose Dexamethasone Test (HDDST - 8mg)** | Cortisol suppresses >50% | No suppression |
| **CRH Stimulation Test** | Exaggerated ↑ ACTH & Cortisol | No response |
| **Inferior Petrosal Sinus Sampling (IPSS)** | Central:Peripheral ACTH ratio >2 (basal) or >3 (post-CRH) | Central:Peripheral ACTH ratio <2 |
* *Note:* IPSS is the gold standard invasive test.

### Imaging Modalities for Cushing's Syndrome
* **ACTH-Independent:** **CT Adrenal** with contrast (assess for adenoma, carcinoma characteristics).
* **ACTH-Dependent (Pituitary):** **MRI Pituitary** with Gadolinium contrast (look for microadenoma <1cm).
* **ACTH-Dependent (Ectopic):** **CT Thorax/Abdomen/Pelvis** (look for SCLC, carcinoid). If CT negative, use **Gallium-68 DOTATATE PET-CT** (detects somatostatin receptors on neuroendocrine tumors).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Life-Threatening Hypercortisolism
* Most often seen in Ectopic ACTH (e.g., SCLC) presenting with severe psychosis, severe myopathy, or opportunistic sepsis.
* **Severe Hypokalemia:** Aggressive **IV Potassium Chloride** replacement (via central line if >10mmol/hr), PO Spironolactone.
* **Rapid Chemical Adrenalectomy:** **IV Etomidate** (imidazole derivative) administered in ICU. Rapidly blocks 11β-hydroxylase.
* Treat concomitant opportunistic infections empirically if septic (e.g., broad-spectrum antibiotics, antifungals).

### Acute Post-Operative Management (Adrenal Crisis Prevention)
* Post-unilateral adrenalectomy or transsphenoidal surgery (TSS), the remaining HPA axis is profoundly suppressed.
* High risk of **Addisonian Crisis** (Hypotension, hypoglycemia, shock).
* **Intra-operative/Immediate Post-op:** Start **IV Hydrocortisone** (e.g., 50-100mg q6h).
* **Step-down:** Taper rapidly over days to maintenance PO replacement (e.g., Hydrocortisone 15mg AM, 5mg PM) until HPA axis recovers (takes months to years).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Definitive Surgical Management
* **Cushing's Disease:** **Transsphenoidal Surgery (TSS)** (經蝶竇腦垂體切除術) by neurosurgery.
* **Adrenal Adenoma:** Laparoscopic unilateral adrenalectomy.
* **Ectopic ACTH:** Resection of primary tumor (e.g., lobectomy for bronchial carcinoid).
* **Bilateral Adrenalectomy:** Last resort for refractory cases. Requires lifelong glucocorticoid (Hydrocortisone) and mineralocorticoid (Fludrocortisone) replacement.

### Medical Therapy (Steroidogenesis Inhibitors)
* Indicated as pre-operative bridge, post-surgical failure, or in occult ectopic ACTH.
* **Metyrapone (甲吡酮):** Blocks 11β-hydroxylase. Rapid onset. Can cause hirsutism (androgen shunting).
* **Ketoconazole (酮康唑):** Blocks 11β-hydroxylase & 17α-hydroxylase. Major risk of severe hepatotoxicity (requires baseline and regular LFTs).
* **Mitotane:** Adrenolytic agent. Primary medical Rx for Adrenocortical Carcinoma (ACC).

### Pituitary-Directed and Receptor-Directed Therapies
* **Pasireotide:** Somatostatin receptor type 5 (SSTR5) analogue. ↓ ACTH secretion from pituitary. Major side effect: Severe hyperglycemia.
* **Cabergoline:** Dopamine agonist (off-label for corticotroph adenomas).
* **Mifepristone:** Glucocorticoid receptor antagonist. Does NOT lower cortisol levels, but blocks peripheral effects. Used for rapid control of severe hyperglycemia/psychosis in CS.

### Radiotherapy
* **Stereotactic Radiosurgery (Gamma Knife):** For residual, recurrent, or unresectable pituitary adenomas.
* Slow onset of action (months to years). Requires bridging medical therapy (e.g., Metyrapone) while awaiting effect. High risk of eventual panhypopituitarism.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Pre-Operative Risk Stratification
* **Thrombotic Risk:** CS creates a severe hypercoagulable state (↑ Factor VIII, vWF, ↓ fibrinolysis). Extremely high risk for DVT/PE peri-operatively.
* **Requirement:** Mandatory DVT prophylaxis (e.g., LMWH Enoxaparin SC) pre- and post-operatively.

### Post-Operative Prognostic Indicators
* **Morning Serum Cortisol (Day 2-3 post-op):**
    * Cortisol <50 nmol/L → Indicates successful resection and severe suppression of normal tissue. High likelihood of long-term remission.
    * Cortisol >138 nmol/L → High risk of residual disease/early relapse.
* **Untreated Cushing's Prognosis:** 50% 5-year mortality, driven primarily by myocardial infarction, stroke, and opportunistic infections.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Hypercortisolism
* **Metabolic:** Secondary Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (severe insulin resistance), profound central obesity, dyslipidemia.
* **Cardiovascular:** Refractory hypertension, early-onset atherosclerosis, venous thromboembolism (VTE).
* **Musculoskeletal:** Osteoporosis (骨質疏鬆症) leading to vertebral crush fractures, **Avascular Necrosis (AVN)** of the femoral head, severe myopathy.
* **Infectious:** Immunosuppression → Opportunistic infections (Pneumocystis jirovecii, invasive Aspergillosis, disseminated TB, candidiasis).
* **Ophthalmological:** Posterior subcapsular cataracts, secondary glaucoma.

### Drug and Treatment-Specific Complications
* **Nelson's Syndrome:** Occurs after bilateral adrenalectomy. Loss of negative feedback leads to aggressive, rapid growth of a residual corticotroph pituitary microadenoma → visual field defects (bitemporal hemianopsia) and severe hyperpigmentation.
* **Ketoconazole:** Fulminant hepatic failure.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate between ACTH-dependent and ACTH-independent Cushing's purely on physical examination? Answer: Look for hyperpigmentation (palmar creases, buccal mucosa). It is only present in ACTH-dependent causes due to co-secretion of Melanocyte-Stimulating Hormone (MSH) with ACTH from the POMC precursor.
* Trap: Relying on random daytime cortisol levels to diagnose Cushing's. Pearl: Cortisol is pulsatile and stress-responsive; random levels are useless. You must use tests that assess normal physiology loss (1mg ONDST, 24h UFC, late-night salivary cortisol).
* Pitfall: Forgetting to take a thorough drug history, including alternative medicines. Always explicitly ask about over-the-counter pain relievers, asthma inhalers, joint injections, and Traditional Chinese Medicine, as exogenous steroid use is the most common cause of Cushing's syndrome in Hong Kong.
* Viva Question: A patient with confirmed Cushing's Disease undergoes successful transsphenoidal surgery. Post-op day 2, they develop profound hypotension and hypoglycemia. What happened and how do you treat it? Answer: The patient is in an Addisonian crisis because the remaining normal pituitary tissue is chronically suppressed. Treat immediately with IV Hydrocortisone and aggressive IV fluids.
* Trap: Assuming pseudo-Cushing's requires surgical intervention. Pearl: Treat the underlying psychiatric, metabolic, or alcohol-use disorder; the HPA axis dysfunction in pseudo-Cushing's is reversible.
