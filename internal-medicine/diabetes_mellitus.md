# Diabetes Mellitus (糖尿病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Diabetes Mellitus
* **Definition:** Chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycemia (高血糖) secondary to absolute or relative insulin deficiency, or insulin resistance.

### Etiologies of Type 1 Diabetes (T1DM)
* **Autoimmune (Type 1A):** Cell-mediated destruction of pancreatic β-cells.
* **Idiopathic (Type 1B):** β-cell destruction without autoimmune markers, prone to ketoacidosis.

### Etiologies of Type 2 Diabetes (T2DM)
* **Insulin Resistance:** ↓ tissue sensitivity to insulin (muscle, liver, adipose).
* **Relative Insulin Deficiency:** Progressive β-cell exhaustion and failure over time.

### Other Specific Types of Diabetes (Secondary DM)
* **Genetic Defects (β-cell):** MODY (Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young).
* **Exocrine Pancreas Disease:** Chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, pancreatectomy.
* **Endocrinopathies:** Cushing's syndrome (庫茲氏症候群), Acromegaly (肢端肥大症), Pheochromocytoma.
* **Drug-induced:** Glucocorticoids, calcineurin inhibitors (Tacrolimus), atypical antipsychotics, thiazides.

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of Diabetes Mellitus
* **Prevalence:** ~10% of adults in Hong Kong.
* **Proportion:** T2DM accounts for >90% of all DM cases.
* **Trend:** ↑ incidence of young-onset T2DM (<40 years old) in HK.
* **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** DM is the leading cause of new ESRD requiring dialysis in HK HA system.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Type 1 Diabetes
* **Immune Dysregulation:** Failure of self-tolerance → autoreactive T-lymphocytes (Type IV hypersensitivity) infiltrate Islets of Langerhans (insulitis).
* **Autoantibodies:** Formation of Islet Cell Autoantibodies (ICA), Glutamic Acid Decarboxylase (GAD65), Insulin Autoantibodies (IAA).
* **Absolute Deficiency:** >80% β-cell destruction before clinical onset → loss of insulin → ↑ lipolysis → unchecked ketogenesis (DKA risk).

### Pathophysiology of Type 2 Diabetes (The Ominous Octet)
* **Pancreas:** ↓ β-cell insulin secretion, ↑ α-cell glucagon secretion.
* **Liver:** ↑ hepatic gluconeogenesis (insulin resistance).
* **Skeletal Muscle:** ↓ peripheral glucose uptake (GLUT4 dysfunction).
* **Adipose Tissue:** ↑ lipolysis → ↑ free fatty acids (lipotoxicity).
* **Incretin Effect:** ↓ GLP-1/GIP secretion from gut → ↓ postprandial insulin release.
* **Kidneys:** ↑ renal glucose reabsorption (via SGLT2 upregulation).
* **Brain:** Neurotransmitter dysfunction → ↑ appetite/polyphagia.

### Pathophysiology of Microvascular Complications
* **Advanced Glycation End-products (AGEs):** Non-enzymatic glycosylation of proteins → cross-linking of collagen → vascular stiffness.
* **Polyol Pathway Activation:** Intracellular hyperglycemia → aldose reductase converts glucose to sorbitol → osmotic damage + oxidative stress.
* **Protein Kinase C (PKC) Activation:** Diacylglycerol (DAG) pathway → ↑ VEGF, ↑ TGF-β → neovascularization and fibrosis.
* **Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS):** Mitochondrial dysfunction → ↑ superoxide production → endothelial dysfunction.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History: Classic Hyperglycemic Symptoms
* **Polyuria (多尿):** Osmotic diuresis from glycosuria.
* **Polydipsia (多飲):** Compensatory thirst response.
* **Polyphagia (多食):** Cellular starvation despite hyperglycemia (especially T1DM).
* **Weight Loss:** Severe catabolic state (lipolysis/muscle breakdown).

### History: Complication-Specific Symptoms
* **Neurologic:** Glove-and-stocking paresthesia (numbness/tingling), erectile dysfunction, nocturnal diarrhea, postural dizziness.
* **Infectious:** Recurrent candidiasis (vaginal/balanitis), slow-healing skin wounds, recurrent UTIs.
* **Macrovascular:** Intermittent claudication, exertional angina, TIA/stroke symptoms.

### Physical Examination: General Appearance & Vitals
* **Habitus:** Central obesity (T2DM); Cachexia (severe untreated T1DM/late T2DM).
* **Vitals:** Postural drop >20/10 mmHg (autonomic neuropathy).

### Physical Examination: Dermatological Signs
* **Acanthosis Nigricans (黑棘皮症):** Velvety, hyperpigmented plaques on neck/axilla (hallmark of severe insulin resistance).
* **Necrobiosis Lipoidica Diabeticorum:** Yellow-brown, atrophic, telangiectatic plaques on shins.
* **Diabetic Dermopathy:** "Shin spots" (hyperpigmented atrophic macules).
* **Lipohypertrophy:** Lumps at repeated insulin injection sites (abdomen/thighs).

### Physical Examination: Diabetic Foot (Neuropathic & Ischemic)
* **Neuropathic Signs:** ↓ sensation to 10g monofilament (distal-to-proximal gradient), absent ankle reflexes, impaired vibration sense (tuning fork 128Hz).
* **Deformities:** Charcot arthropathy (Rocker-bottom foot), claw toes, pes cavus.
* **Ulceration:** Callus formation and painless ulcers at pressure points (metatarsal heads, heel).
* **Vascular Signs:** Absent/weak dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses, cold/pale extremities, hair loss, arterial ulcers (tips of toes).

### Physical Examination: Cranial Nerves
* **Diabetic CN III Palsy:** "Down and out" eye, ptosis, BUT **pupil-sparing** (parasympathetic fibers run peripherally, ischemia affects central core).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses of Polyuria/Polydipsia
| Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Diabetes Mellitus** | Glycosuria +ve, High CBG, Polyphagia. |
| **Diabetes Insipidus (尿崩症)** | Normoglycemic, hypernatremia, dilute urine (low specific gravity). |
| **Psychogenic Polydipsia** | Normoglycemic, hyponatremia, psychiatric history. |

### Differentiating DM Subtypes
| Feature | Type 1 DM | Type 2 DM | LADA | MODY |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset Age** | <30 years | >40 years (shifting ↓) | >30 years | <25 years |
| **Body Habitus** | Lean | Obese/Overweight | Lean | Lean/Normal |
| **Ketosis Risk** | High (DKA) | Low (HHS > DKA) | Variable | Rare |
| **Autoantibodies** | Positive | Negative | Positive | Negative |
| **C-peptide** | Low/Undetectable | Normal/High | Low (slow decline) | Normal/Low |
| **Family History** | Polygenic (<10-15%) | Polygenic (Strong) | Polygenic | Autosomal Dominant (Strong) |

### Differential Diagnoses of Transient Hyperglycemia
* **Stress Hyperglycemia:** Acute illness (Sepsis, MI, stroke, major surgery) → ↑ cortisol/catecholamines. Resolves post-illness.
* **Steroid-induced DM:** Exogenous glucocorticoids (e.g., Prednisolone for SLE) → insulin resistance + ↑ gluconeogenesis. Usually self-limiting if steroids tapered.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### WHO/HK HA Diagnostic Criteria for Diabetes Mellitus
* **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):** ≥7.0 mmol/L (≥126 mg/dL). *(Requires 8-hr fast)*
* **2-Hour Post-Load Glucose (OGTT):** ≥11.1 mmol/L (≥200 mg/dL). *(75g anhydrous glucose load)*
* **HbA1c:** ≥6.5% (48 mmol/mol). *(Lab standard NGSP/DCCT calibrated)*
* **Random Plasma Glucose:** ≥11.1 mmol/L (≥200 mg/dL) **WITH** classic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss) or hyperglycemic crisis.
* *(Note: In absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia/crisis, criteria 1-3 require confirmation by repeat testing).*

### Prediabetes Diagnostic Criteria (HK HA Standards)
* **Impaired Fasting Glucose (IFG):** FPG 6.1 - 6.9 mmol/L (WHO); 5.6 - 6.9 mmol/L (ADA).
* **Impaired Glucose Tolerance (IGT):** 2-hour OGTT 7.8 - 11.0 mmol/L.
* **HbA1c Prediabetes range:** 5.7 - 6.4% (ADA); HK HA utilizes this to flag high-risk individuals.

### Bedside Investigations
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG):** Instant screening.
* **Urine Dipstick:** Glycosuria, ketonuria (critical for DKA triage).
* **Urine Albumin/Creatinine Ratio (ACR):** Screening for microalbuminuria (spot urine).
* **ECG:** Screen for silent MI (asymptomatic due to autonomic neuropathy).

### Laboratory Blood Investigations
* **Glycemic Control:** HbA1c (assesses average glucose over past 2-3 months via RBC lifespan).
* **Subtype Discrimination:**
  * Fasting C-peptide (Endogenous insulin production marker).
  * Autoantibodies: Anti-GAD65, Anti-IA-2, ICA, IAA, ZnT8 (confirms T1DM/LADA).
* **Complication & Comorbidity Screening:**
  * Fasting Lipid Profile (LDL, HDL, Triglycerides, Total Cholesterol).
  * Renal Function Test (U&E/Cr, eGFR).
  * Liver Function Test (R/O MASLD / NAFLD).
  * TFT (Co-existing autoimmune thyroid disease in T1DM).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) Management Protocol (HK HA)
* **Step 1: Fluid Resuscitation (Most Critical):** 0.9% NaCl. Typical: 1L in 1st hr, 1L over next 2 hrs, 1L over next 4 hrs, 1L over next 8 hrs. (Adjust based on hydration/cardiac status).
* **Step 2: Potassium Replacement:** Insulin shifts K+ intracellularly. If K+ <5.5 mmol/L AND urine output adequate, add KCl to IV fluids. (If K+ <3.3, hold insulin until replaced).
* **Step 3: Fixed-Rate Intravenous Insulin Infusion (FRIII):** Actrapid 0.1 units/kg/hr. DO NOT stop until ketonemia clears (blood ketones <0.6 mmol/L or pH >7.3).
* **Step 4: Glucose Management:** When CBG drops <14.0 mmol/L, start IV 10% Dextrose concurrently with insulin to prevent hypoglycemia while clearing ketones.

### Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS) Management Protocol
* **Core Principle:** Profound fluid deficit (>9-10L). Osmolality strictly managed to prevent central pontine myelinolysis / cerebral edema.
* **Fluid Resuscitation:** 0.9% NaCl slowly. Monitor serum osmolality and Na+ frequently. Target Na+ reduction of 0.5 mmol/L/hr.
* **Insulin Therapy:** DO NOT start IV insulin immediately unless ketones +ve or glucose falling <5 mmol/L/hr with fluids alone. Insulin shifts water intracellularly → vascular collapse. If used, start low (0.05 U/kg/hr).
* **Thromboprophylaxis:** High risk of thrombosis. Prophylactic LMWH (Enoxaparin) mandatory unless contraindicated.

### Acute Hypoglycemia Management Protocol
* **Conscious & Swallow Safe:** 15-20g fast-acting oral carbohydrates (e.g., 3-4 glucose tablets, 150ml fruit juice). Recheck CBG in 15 mins. Repeat if <4.0 mmol/L. Follow up with complex carbs (e.g., biscuits/bread).
* **Unconscious / Nil By Mouth (NBM) WITH IV Access:** 50ml of 20% Dextrose IV stat (or HA preferred D10% 100ml bolus).
* **Unconscious / NBM WITHOUT IV Access:** 1mg Glucagon IM (Ineffective in severe hepatic disease, starvation, or sulfonylurea overdose).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Non-Pharmacological Management & Lifestyle Modifications
* **Medical Nutrition Therapy:** Dietitian referral. ↓ simple sugars, ↑ complex carbs, Mediterranean diet pattern.
* **Weight Management:** Target 5-10% body weight loss in overweight T2DM patients.
* **Exercise:** 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise/week + resistance training twice weekly.
* **Smoking Cessation:** Crucial for micro/macrovascular risk reduction.
* **Multidisciplinary:** Podiatrist (foot care), Ophthalmologist/Optometrist (retinal screening).

### HK HA Tiered Pharmacological Algorithm for T2DM
| Tier | Clinical Scenario | Preferred Agent(s) | Key Mechanisms & Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Line** | All newly diagnosed T2DM (unless contraindicated) | **Metformin (二甲雙胍)** | ↓ Hepatic gluconeogenesis. Weight neutral. Risk: Lactic acidosis, B12 deficiency. |
| **2nd Line** | High ASCVD risk, Heart Failure, or CKD | **SGLT2 Inhibitors** (e.g., Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin) | ↑ Glycosuria. Benefits: HF hospitalizations ↓, renoprotective. Risk: GU infections, euglycemic DKA. |
| **2nd Line** | High ASCVD risk, Obesity prominent | **GLP-1 Receptor Agonists** (e.g., Liraglutide, Semaglutide) | ↑ Incretin effect, delays gastric emptying. Weight loss. Risk: Pancreatitis. (Injectable/Oral). |
| **Alternative 2nd Line** | Frail elderly, CKD (where SGLT2i/GLP1 unsuited), Low Cost needed | **DPP-4 Inhibitors** (e.g., Sitagliptin, Linagliptin) | Prolongs endogenous GLP-1. Weight neutral, low hypo risk. Linagliptin safe in severe CKD. |
| **Alternative 2nd Line** | Need rapid HbA1c drop, low cost needed | **Sulfonylureas (SU)** (e.g., Gliclazide, Glimepiride) | ↑ Pancreatic insulin release (K-ATP channel block). Risk: Hypoglycemia, weight gain. |
| **3rd Line / Advanced** | Severe symptomatic hyperglycemia, HbA1c >9-10% | **Insulin Therapy** (Basal +/- Bolus) | Glargine (Long-acting), NPH (Intermediate). Actrapid (Short), Aspart/Lispro (Rapid). |

### Type 1 Diabetes Pharmacotherapy
* **Basal-Bolus Regimen (MDI):** 1x Long-acting basal insulin (Glargine) + 3x Rapid-acting bolus insulin (Aspart) before meals.
* **Continuous Subcutaneous Insulin Infusion (CSII):** Insulin pump therapy for selected brittle T1DM patients.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Glycemic Targets (HK HA Guidelines)
* **General Target:** HbA1c <7.0% (<53 mmol/mol).
* **Stringent Target:** HbA1c ≤6.5% (Young, short disease duration, no CVD, low hypo risk).
* **Relaxed Target:** HbA1c 7.5 - 8.0% (Elderly, frail, history of severe hypoglycemia, limited life expectancy).

### Cardiovascular Risk Stratification & Targets
* **Cardiovascular Risk Score:** Localized SCORE2-Asia or WHO risk charts to estimate 10-year fatal/non-fatal CVD risk.
* **Blood Pressure Targets:**
  * General DM: <130/80 mmHg (HA hypertension guidelines updated).
  * Drug of choice: ACEi or ARB (especially if microalbuminuria present).
* **Lipid Targets:**
  * DM without established CVD: LDL <1.8 mmol/L.
  * DM with established CVD: LDL <1.4 mmol/L.
  * Drug of choice: High-intensity statin (Atorvastatin/Rosuvastatin).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Macrovascular Complications (Accelerated Atherosclerosis)
* **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD):** Ischemic heart disease, frequently atypical/silent myocardial infarctions.
* **Cerebrovascular Disease:** Ischemic stroke, transient ischemic attack (TIA).
* **Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD):** Claudication, critical limb ischemia, gangrene.

### Microvascular Complications
* **Diabetic Neuropathy:**
  * Sensory: Distal symmetric polyneuropathy (glove/stocking).
  * Motor: Muscle atrophy (interossei), pes cavus.
  * Autonomic: Gastroparesis, resting tachycardia, postural hypotension, erectile dysfunction, neurogenic bladder.
  * Mononeuritis Multiplex: CN III, IV, VI palsies.
* **Diabetic Nephropathy:** (Excluded per instructions - leads to CKD).
* **Diabetic Retinopathy:** (Excluded per instructions - leads to visual loss).

### Endocrine & Infectious Complications
* **Infections:** Fungal (Candidiasis, Mucormycosis), Bacterial (Tuberculosis, severe UTIs/pyelonephritis, Emphysematous cholecystitis).
* **HK Endemic Specific:** Severe *Burkholderia pseudomallei* (Melioidosis) infections are highly associated with poorly controlled DM in HK.

### Key Pharmacological Adverse Effects
* **Metformin:** GI intolerance, Lactic acidosis (avoid in acute kidney injury/sepsis), Vitamin B12 deficiency.
* **Sulfonylureas:** Hypoglycemia, Weight gain.
* **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Euglycemic DKA (ketosis with normal/mildly ↑ glucose), genital mycotic infections, Fournier's gangrene, volume depletion.
* **GLP-1 RAs:** Nausea/Vomiting, Pancreatitis, Biliary disease.
* **Insulin:** Hypoglycemia, Weight gain, Lipodystrophy at injection sites.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a CN III palsy caused by diabetes from one caused by a posterior communicating artery (PCoA) aneurysm? Answer: Diabetic CN III palsy is classically "pupil-sparing" due to microvascular ischemia of the central nerve core. PCoA aneurysms compress the outside of the nerve, hitting the parasympathetic pupilloconstrictor fibers first, causing a dilated, unreactive pupil.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with DKA and a "normal" serum potassium level of 4.5 mmol/L does not need potassium replacement. Pearl: Total body potassium is profoundly depleted in DKA. Once insulin is given, potassium rapidly shifts intracellularly, risking fatal arrhythmias. Always add KCl to IV fluids once urine output is confirmed, unless serum K+ is >5.5 mmol/L.
* Pitfall: Prescribing SGLT2 inhibitors and advising the patient to continue taking them during an acute illness or fasting. You must advise patients on "Sick Day Rules" to withhold SGLT2i during illness, fasting, or perioperatively to prevent the rare but severe risk of Euglycemic DKA.
* Viva Question: Why do we use IV Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) initially for HHS instead of hypotonic solutions, despite the hyperosmolar state? Answer: Rapid shifts in osmolality with hypotonic fluids can drive water into neurons, causing devastating cerebral edema. Saline allows gradual osmolality reduction while expanding the severely depleted intravascular volume.
* Trap: Using HbA1c to diagnose or monitor diabetes in a patient with Thalassemia or recent blood transfusions. Pearl: HbA1c relies on a normal 120-day RBC lifespan. Hemoglobinopathies (highly prevalent in HK), hemolysis, or transfusions alter RBC turnover, rendering HbA1c inaccurate. Use FPG, OGTT, or fructosamine instead.
* Pitfall: Stopping the fixed-rate IV insulin infusion in DKA as soon as the capillary blood glucose drops below 14.0 mmol/L. The insulin is suppressing ketogenesis; stopping it will cause relapse. You must add IV Dextrose to maintain euglycemia while continuing the insulin infusion until blood ketones clear (<0.6 mmol/L).
