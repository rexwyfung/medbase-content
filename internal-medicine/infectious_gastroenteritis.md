# Infectious Gastroenteritis (感染性胃腸炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Infectious Gastroenteritis
* **Definition:** Inflammation of the GI tract secondary to infection → acute diarrhoea (腹瀉) (≥3 loose/watery stools/24h) ± nausea, vomiting (嘔吐), abdominal pain (腹痛), and fever.
* **Duration:** Acute (<14 days), Persistent (14-30 days), Chronic (>30 days).

### Viral Etiologies (Local Epidemiology)
* **Norovirus (諾如病毒):** Most common cause of adult viral GE & outbreaks in HK (care homes, hospitals). Peaks in winter. Highly contagious.
* **Rotavirus (輪狀病毒):** Historically most common paediatric cause; incidence ↓ sharply post-vaccination.
* **Adenovirus/Astrovirus:** Common in paediatric populations.

### Bacterial & Parasitic Etiologies (Local Epidemiology)
* **Campylobacter jejuni:** Most common bacterial cause worldwide. Associated with undercooked poultry.
* **Salmonella spp. (沙門氏菌):** Eggs, poultry. High incidence in HK.
* **Vibrio parahaemolyticus (副溶血性弧菌):** Endemic in HK coastal waters; classic post-consumption of raw seafood/oysters. Peaks in summer.
* **Clostridioides difficile (艱難梭菌):** Major nosocomial pathogen in HK Hospital Authority (HA) network; triggered by broad-spectrum antibiotic use.
* **Parasites:** *Giardia lamblia* (賈第鞭毛蟲), *Entamoeba histolytica* (溶組織內阿米巴). Mostly travel-related in HK.

### Food-Borne Gastroenteritis Syndromes Table
| Incubation Period | Likely Organism | Classic Food Source | Pathogenesis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1-6 hours** | *Staphylococcus aureus* | Mayo, creams, cold meats | Preformed enterotoxin |

### HK Statutory Notification
* **Notifiable Diseases (須呈報傳染病):** Food poisoning outbreaks (≥2 linked cases), Cholera, Bacillary dysentery, Typhoid/Paratyphoid must be reported to the Centre for Health Protection (CHP) (衞生防護中心).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Secretory Diarrhoea Mechanisms
* **Pathophysiology:** Enterotoxins (e.g., Cholera toxin, ETEC heat-labile toxin) bind enterocyte receptors → ↑ intracellular cAMP or cGMP.
* **Effect:** Activation of CFTR channel → massive active Cl- secretion into lumen → Na+ and H2O passively follow osmotic gradient.
* **Result:** Copious, watery diarrhoea without mucosal destruction or profound inflammation.

### Inflammatory & Invasive Diarrhoea Mechanisms
* **Pathophysiology:** Organisms (e.g., *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*, *Salmonella*, EIEC) directly invade mucosa or release cytotoxins (e.g., Shiga toxin, *C. difficile* Toxin A/B).
* **Effect:** Cell death, intense local inflammatory response, loss of mucosal integrity, and micro-ulceration.
* **Result:** Exudation of mucus, blood, and leukocytes → Dysentery (bloody, small-volume diarrhoea with tenesmus).

### Osmotic Diarrhoea Mechanisms
* **Pathophysiology:** Viral pathogens (e.g., Norovirus) replicate in and destroy mature enterocytes on intestinal villi.
* **Effect:** Transient lactase (and other brush border enzyme) deficiency → unabsorbed carbohydrates accumulate in lumen → exert osmotic force → draw H2O into lumen.
* **Result:** Watery diarrhoea, often exacerbated by dairy intake.

### Clostridioides difficile Pathogenesis
* **Trigger:** Broad-spectrum Abx (Clindamycin, Cephalosporins, Fluoroquinolones) → disruption of normal colonic microbiome.
* **Mechanism:** *C. difficile* spores germinate/overgrow → release Toxin A (enterotoxin; causes fluid secretion) and Toxin B (cytotoxin; disrupts actin cytoskeleton).
* **Result:** Apoptosis of colonocytes → formation of fibrinous exudate → **Pseudomembranous colitis (偽膜性結腸炎)**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Gastroenteritis History Taking
* **Onset & Duration:** Exact timeline (differentiates pre-formed toxin vs invasive).
* **Stool Characteristics:** Volume, frequency, presence of visible blood or mucus (dysentery vs watery).
* **Associated Symptoms:** Nausea, profound vomiting (points to viral or pre-formed toxin), severe abdominal cramps, fever, tenesmus.
* **Epidemiological Clues:** Recent travel (Travellers' diarrhoea), dietary history (raw seafood, buffets, reheated rice), sick contacts/outbreaks, recent hospitalisation or antibiotic use within 3 months.

### Physical Signs of Systemic Dehydration
* **Mild/Moderate:** Dry mucous membranes, ↓ skin turgor, sunken eyes, absence of tears, tachycardia.
* **Severe (Shock):** Postural hypotension, resting hypotension, delayed capillary refill time (>2s), oliguria/anuria, altered mental status/lethargy.

### Abdominal & Gastrointestinal Examination
* **Inspection:** Scaphoid or distended abdomen.
* **Auscultation:** Hyperactive, loud bowel sounds (borborygmi).
* **Palpation:** Diffuse, mild tenderness.
* **Red Flag Signs:** Localised peritonism, rebound tenderness, rigid guarding, or palpable mass (suggests perforation, toxic megacolon, or surgical abdomen).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Surgical & Acute Abdomen Differentials
* **Acute Appendicitis:** Initial periumbilical pain migrating to right iliac fossa (RIF) + localised peritonism.
* **Ischemic Colitis/Mesenteric Ischaemia:** Pain entirely out of proportion to physical signs, severe vascular risk factors (AF, atherosclerosis), high serum lactate.
* **Diverticulitis:** Left lower quadrant (LLQ) pain, localised guarding, older age.

### Non-Infectious Gastrointestinal Differentials
* **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) (炎症性腸病):** Gradual onset, chronic/relapsing course, weight loss, anaemia, extra-intestinal manifestations (erythema nodosum, uveitis).
* **Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS):** Chronic alternating bowel habits (>6 months), pain classically relieved by defecation, no nocturnal symptoms, normal inflammatory markers.
* **Microscopic Colitis:** Chronic, watery, painless diarrhoea in older females; normal macroscopic endoscopy.

### Systemic & Endocrine Differentials
* **Thyrotoxicosis (甲狀腺毒症):** Tachycardia, tremors, weight loss despite ↑ appetite, heat intolerance.
* **Carcinoid Syndrome:** Intermittent secretory diarrhoea, facial flushing, wheezing, right-sided heart murmurs.
* **Medication-Induced:** Laxative abuse, Metformin (common dose-dependent side effect), Colchicine.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Indications for Stool Testing in HK HA
* Routine stool testing is NOT required for uncomplicated, self-limiting diarrhoea.
* **Indications:** Severe dehydration, prolonged symptoms (>7 days), bloody stools/dysentery, fever >38.5°C, immunocompromised state, recent antibiotic use, or nosocomial acquisition.

### Bedside & Basic Investigations
* **Vitals & ECG:** Check for haemodynamic instability, prolonged QTc (due to ↓ K+, ↓ Ca2+), or arrhythmias.
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG):** Exclude hypoglycaemia (especially in elderly/paediatrics).
* **Bloods:**
  * **CBP:** ↑ WBC (suggests invasive/bacterial infection or *C. diff*).
  * **LRFT:** ↑ Urea & ↑ Cr (pre-renal AKI), ↓ K+, ↓ Na+ or ↑ Na+ (hypo/hypernatraemic dehydration), ↓ HCO3- (metabolic acidosis from stool base loss).
  * **Blood Cultures:** Indicated if septic or high fever.

### Specific Stool Investigations
* **Stool Routine & Microscopy (R&M):** Check for WBCs, RBCs, cysts, ova, and parasites (requires 3 consecutive samples for O&P sensitivity).
* **Stool Culture & Sensitivity (C&S):** Detects *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Campylobacter* (requires special media).
* **Multiplex PCR (e.g., BioFire FilmArray):** Rapidly detects >20 GI pathogens. Increasingly available in HK HA for severe/refractory cases.
* **C. difficile Testing Protocol:** 
  * 2-step algorithm: GDH (Glutamate Dehydrogenase) antigen EIA + *C. diff* Toxin A/B EIA.
  * If discordant (GDH +ve / Toxin -ve): Confirm with NAAT/PCR for toxigenic gene.

### Imaging & Endoscopic Investigations
* **AXR (Erect & Supine):** Rule out toxic megacolon (colonic dilation >6cm) or bowel perforation (pneumoperitoneum).
* **CT Abdomen & Pelvis (with contrast):** If high suspicion of surgical abdomen, ischemia, or severe colitis (shows bowel wall thickening/thumbprinting).
* **Flexible Sigmoidoscopy/Colonoscopy:** Differentiates IBD vs infectious colitis. Essential for diagnosing pseudomembranous colitis in indeterminate CDI.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation & Fluid Management
* **Severe Dehydration / Shock:** Prompt IV fluid resuscitation.
  * Give 500ml-1L boluses of **0.9% Normal Saline (NS)** or **Ringer's Lactate** (Hartmann's).
  * Reassess haemodynamics continuously.
* **Electrolyte Correction:** IV/PO Potassium replacement for hypokalaemia; correct metabolic acidosis via adequate hydration.

### Acute Management of Severe C. difficile Infection (CDI)
* **Immediate Actions:** Cease offending antibiotic IMMEDIATELY if clinically feasible. Isolate patient (Contact Precautions - use soap & water; alcohol rubs DO NOT kill spores).
* **Fulminant CDI Protocol (Shock, Ileus, Toxic Megacolon):**
  * **PO Vancomycin 500mg QID** (via NGT if PO not tolerated; use Vancomycin enema if complete ileus).
  * PLUS **IV Metronidazole 500mg Q8H**.
  * Early surgical consultation for urgent subtotal colectomy.

### Avoidance of Anti-Motility Agents in Emergencies
* Strict absolute contraindication for Loperamide or Diphenoxylate in the presence of:
  * Bloody diarrhoea / Dysentery.
  * High fever.
  * Suspected *C. difficile* infection.
  * *Reason:* ↑ risk of toxic megacolon and prolonged pathogen mucosal contact.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Step-Up Rehydration Strategies
* **Step 1 (Mild-Moderate):** Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) is first-line. Superior to plain water/sports drinks as it leverages the Na+/glucose co-transporter to maximally absorb water.
* **Step 2 (Intolerance/Severe):** IV fluid replacement. Early transition to oral fluids once vomiting resolves.

### Empiric Antimicrobial Therapy (Travellers' Diarrhoea & Dysentery)
* Most viral/food poisoning is self-limiting (supportive care only).
* **Indications for Empiric Abx:** Severe travellers' diarrhoea, dysentery, immunocompromised.
* **HK HA Formulary Choices:**
  * **Azithromycin** (Preferred due to very high local fluoroquinolone resistance in *Campylobacter* and *Salmonella*).
  * Alternative: **Ciprofloxacin** (if known sensitivity).

### C. difficile Infection (CDI) Treatment Algorithm
| CDI Episode | Preferred Regimen | Alternative Regimen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Initial Episode** | PO Fidaxomicin 200mg BD x 10d | PO Vancomycin 125mg QID x 10d |
| **1st Recurrence** | PO Fidaxomicin (if Vanc used 1st) | Tapered/Pulsed PO Vancomycin |
| **≥2nd Recurrence** | Faecal Microbiota Transplant (FMT) | Bezlotoxumab (Anti-Toxin B mAb) |

### Faecal Microbiota Transplantation (FMT) (腸道微生態移植)
* **Indication:** Highly effective (>90%) for multiple recurrent CDI refractory to antibiotics.
* **Mechanism:** Restores healthy gut microbiome diversity, outcompeting *C. diff* spores.
* **Delivery:** Via colonoscopy, nasojejunal tube, or oral capsules. (Available in selected HK HA clusters).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Severity Criteria for C. difficile Infection (IDSA/SHEA Guidelines)
* **Non-Severe:** WBC ≤ 15 x 10^9/L AND Serum Creatinine < 132 µmol/L (<1.5 mg/dL).
* **Severe:** WBC > 15 x 10^9/L OR Serum Creatinine ≥ 132 µmol/L (≥1.5 mg/dL).
* **Fulminant:** Presence of hypotension, shock, ileus, or toxic megacolon.

### WHO Dehydration Risk Stratification
| Severity | % Body Weight Loss | Clinical Signs | Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | 3-5% | Normal vitals, thirsty, restless | ORS |
| **Moderate** | 6-9% | Tachycardia, ↓ turgor, sunken eyes | ORS ± IV |
| **Severe** | ≥10% | Hypotension, lethargy, weak pulse, anuria | Immediate IV Resus |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Renal & Haemodynamic Complications
* **Pre-Renal AKI (急性腎損傷):** Due to profound volume depletion. Reversible with fluid challenge.
* **Electrolyte Emergencies:** Severe hypokalaemia → cardiac arrhythmias, ileus, muscle weakness.

### Haematological Complications
* **Haemolytic Uraemic Syndrome (HUS) (溶血性尿毒症綜合症):**
  * Classic triad: Microangiopathic Haemolytic Anaemia (MAHA), Thrombocytopenia, AKI.
  * Triggered by Shiga-toxin producing *E. coli* (STEC O157:H7) or *Shigella dysenteriae*.
  * Avoid antibiotics in STEC as it ↑ toxin release and ↑ HUS risk.

### Neurological Complications
* **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) (吉巴氏綜合症):** Acute, ascending, symmetrical flaccid paralysis developing 1-3 weeks post-*Campylobacter jejuni* infection (molecular mimicry affecting peripheral nerves).

### Rheumatological Complications
* **Reactive Arthritis (Reiter's Syndrome):**
  * Classic triad: Urethritis, Conjunctivitis, Arthritis ("Can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree").
  * Occurs 1-4 weeks post-infection (e.g., *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*). Strong HLA-B27 association.

### Gastrointestinal Complications
* **Toxic Megacolon (中毒性巨結腸):** Life-threatening colonic dilation (>6cm) with systemic toxicity. High risk of perforation and faecal peritonitis.
* **Post-Infectious IBS:** Persistent functional bowel symptoms long after acute pathogen clearance.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of traveller's diarrhoea and how is it acquired? Answer: Enterotoxigenic *E. coli* (ETEC), acquired via faecal-oral route from contaminated food and water.
* Viva Question: A patient develops bloody diarrhoea and acute kidney injury 5 days after eating an undercooked beef burger. What is the diagnosis? Answer: Haemolytic Uraemic Syndrome (HUS) secondary to Shiga-toxin producing *E. coli* (O157:H7).
* Trap: Prescribing Metronidazole as first-line therapy for severe initial *C. difficile* infection. Pearl: Current guidelines strictly recommend PO Vancomycin or PO Fidaxomicin for initial episodes due to superior efficacy; Metronidazole is relegated to mild cases when alternatives are unavailable or IV adjunct for fulminant cases.
* Trap: Advising healthcare workers to use alcohol-based hand rub after treating a *C. difficile* patient. Pearl: Alcohol does NOT kill *C. difficile* spores; strict handwashing with soap and water is mandatory.
* Pitfall: Giving anti-motility agents (Loperamide) to a patient with bloody diarrhoea and fever. This precipitates toxic megacolon by trapping the invasive pathogen and toxins within the bowel lumen.
* Pitfall: Treating suspected STEC (*E. coli* O157:H7) gastroenteritis with empirical antibiotics. This increases bacterial cell lysis, massively increasing Shiga-toxin release and significantly precipitating the risk of HUS.
