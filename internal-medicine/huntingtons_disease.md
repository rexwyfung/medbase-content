# Huntington's Disease (亨廷頓舞蹈症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Huntington's Disease
* **Huntington's Disease (HD)**: Autosomal dominant (AD) fatal neurodegenerative disorder characterized by a classic clinical triad: choreiform movements, progressive cognitive decline, and psychiatric disturbances.

### Etiology and Genetics of Huntington's Disease
* **Genetic Locus**: Mutation in the **HTT gene (Huntingtin)** on **Chromosome 4p16.3**.
* **Mutation Type**: Expansion of trinucleotide **CAG (Cytosine-Adenine-Guanine)** repeats.
* **Genetic Anticipation (遺傳早現)**: Disease onset occurs at an earlier age and with ↑ severity in successive generations.
* **Parent of Origin Effect**: Anticipation is most prominent with **paternal transmission** due to instability of CAG repeats during spermatogenesis.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: Rare in Asian populations (~0.4–0.5 per 100,000 in HK/Asia) compared to Caucasian populations (~4–10 per 100,000).
* **Onset**: Typically manifests between **30–50 years of age**.
* **Juvenile HD (Westphal Variant)**: Accounts for ~5–10% of cases; onset <20 years of age.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Molecular Pathogenesis of Mutant Huntingtin
* **CAG Expansion** → Encodes a polyglutamine (polyQ) tract → formation of **mutant Huntingtin (mHTT)** protein.
* **Toxic Gain-of-Function**: mHTT misfolds, evades the ubiquitin-proteasome degradation system, and forms intracellular inclusion bodies.
* **Cellular Toxicity**: Leads to transcriptional dysregulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, ferroptosis, and ultimately neuronal apoptosis.

### Striatal Neurodegeneration Mechanism
* **Selective Vulnerability**: Massive loss of **GABAergic Medium Spiny Neurons (MSNs)** in the **Striatum (Caudate Nucleus & Putamen)**.
* **Neurotransmitter Imbalance**: Loss of inhibitory GABAergic efferents to the globus pallidus externus (GPe) → relative overactivity of dopaminergic pathways → hyperkinetic movements (**Chorea**).
* **Cortical Involvement**: Diffuse cerebral cortical atrophy occurs later → profound frontostriatal dementia.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Detailed History for Huntington's Disease
* **Family History**: Establish 3-generation pedigree assessing for AD inheritance, early-onset dementia, or unexplained suicides.
* **Motor Symptoms**: Clumsiness, dropping objects, unexplained falls, continuous "fidgeting".
* **Psychiatric Symptoms**: Personality changes, irritability, apathy, severe depression, psychotic features (paranoia).
* **Cognitive Symptoms**: ↓ executive function, slowed processing, lack of insight (anosognosia).

### Neurological Physical Signs in Huntington's Disease
* **Chorea (舞蹈症)**: Involuntary, abrupt, irregular, purposeless dance-like movements flowing from one body part to another.
* **Motor Impersistence**: Inability to maintain constant voluntary muscle contraction.
  * **Milkmaid's Grip**: Fluctuating grip strength when holding the examiner's fingers.
  * **Tongue Darting (Jack-in-the-box tongue)**: Inability to keep tongue protruded for >10 seconds.
* **Oculomotor Abnormalities**: Delayed initiation of saccades, slow saccadic velocity, interrupted smooth pursuit.
* **Gait Abnormalities**: Broad-based, staggering, "drunken" choreiform gait.
* **Late-Stage Motor Signs**: Chorea often replaced by dystonia, bradykinesia, and marked rigidity.

### Juvenile Huntington's Disease (Westphal Variant) Signs
* **Atypical Presentation**: Chorea is minimal or absent.
* **Dominant Signs**: Severe Parkinsonism (rigidity, severe bradykinesia), dystonia, and rapid cognitive decline.
* **Seizures**: Present in 30–50% of juvenile HD cases (rare in adult-onset HD).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Choreiform Syndromes
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sydenham's Chorea** | Post-streptococcal (pediatric onset), associated with Rheumatic Fever carditis, self-limiting. |
| **Wilson's Disease (威爾遜症)** | Kayser-Fleischer rings, abnormal LFTs, ↓ serum ceruloplasmin, ↑ 24h urinary copper. |
| **Tardive Dyskinesia** | Clear Hx of chronic dopamine antagonist (antipsychotic/antiemetic) exposure; prominent oro-buccal-lingual movements. |
| **Neuroacanthocytosis** | Prominent self-mutilation (lip/tongue biting), peripheral neuropathy, acanthocytes on blood smear. |
| **Dentatorubral-Pallidoluysian Atrophy (DRPLA)** | Highly prevalent in Japan; prominent ataxia, myoclonus, and seizures. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Huntington's Disease
* **Cognitive Assessment**: Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) → detects early executive/visuospatial deficits (subcortical dementia) better than MMSE.
* **Swallow Assessment**: Urgent bedside assessment by Speech & Language Therapist (SLT) to evaluate aspiration risk.

### Blood and Genetic Investigations
* **Diagnostic Genetic Testing (HTT Gene)**: Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) determining precise CAG repeat length. Requires mandatory pre-test and post-test genetic counseling.
* **Basic Bloods**: LFTs, Ceruloplasmin, Thyroid function, Treponemal serology (to exclude treatable mimics).
* **Peripheral Blood Smear**: To exclude acanthocytosis.

### Neuroimaging Findings in Huntington's Disease
* **MRI Brain**: 
  * **Caudate Nucleus Atrophy (尾狀核萎縮)**: Loss of normal convex bulge into the lateral ventricles.
  * **"Boxcar Ventricles"**: Striking enlargement of the frontal horns of the lateral ventricles secondary to caudate volume loss.
  * Generalized cortical atrophy (late stage).
* **PET Scan (Research/Atypical cases)**: ↓ glucose metabolism in the striatum (often precedes structural atrophy).

### Diagnostic Criteria for Huntington's Disease (CAG Repeat Thresholds)
| CAG Repeat Length | Clinical Penetrance | Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **< 26** | Normal | No HD; no risk to offspring. |
| **27 – 35** | Intermediate | No HD; but mutable (may expand in offspring, posing risk to next generation). |
| **36 – 39** | Reduced Penetrance | May or may not develop HD symptoms; risk to offspring. |
| **≥ 40** | Full Penetrance | **Definitive diagnosis**; individual will develop HD if they live long enough. |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Agitation and Psychosis Management
* **Indication**: Severe aggression, active psychosis, or danger to self/others.
* **Sedation/Antipsychotic Protocol**:
  * **Haloperidol** 1-2 mg PO/IM OR **Olanzapine** 5-10 mg PO/IM.
  * Avoid physical restraints if possible (exacerbates chorea and injury risk).
* **Suicide Risk**: Acute admission to psychiatric ward with 1-to-1 nursing observation if active suicidal intent is present.

### Acute Aspiration Pneumonia Protocol
* **Resuscitation**: O2 supplementation, IV fluid rehydration.
* **Airway/Feeding**: Strict Nil By Mouth (NBM) until formal SLT assessment; insert Nasogastric (NG) tube for feeding and medications.
* **Antibiosis (HA Protocol)**: IV Amoxicillin-Clavulanate 1.2g Q8H (coverage for oral anaerobes) ± IV Metronidazole if severe/cavitary.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Pharmacological Management Algorithm for Chorea
| Step | Pharmacological Class | Local Formulary Options & Clinical Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Line** | **VMAT2 Inhibitors** | **Tetrabenazine (TBZ)** or Deutetrabenazine. Depletes presynaptic dopamine. *Crucial: Avoid if poorly controlled depression or active suicidality.* |
| **Alternative 1st Line** | **Atypical Antipsychotics** | **Olanzapine**, **Quetiapine**, or **Risperidone**. Preferred if concurrent psychosis, severe irritability, or significant weight loss (Olanzapine ↑ appetite). |
| **Refractory / Rescue** | **Typical Antipsychotics** | **Haloperidol**. Strong D2 blockade. High risk of Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS) and tardive dyskinesia. |

### Chronic Psychiatric Management in HD
* **Depression**: **SSRIs** (e.g., Sertraline, Citalopram) are 1st line. Monitor closely for activation/suicidality upon initiation.
* **Irritability/Obsessive-Compulsive Behaviors**: SSRIs or low-dose Atypical Antipsychotics.

### Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) and Supportive Care
* **Dietitian**: Chorea causes massive energy expenditure → requires high-caloric, high-protein diet (often >3000 kcal/day) ± PEG tube feeding in late stages.
* **Speech & Language Therapy (SLT)**: Modifying diet texture (thickened fluids, pureed diet), swallowing techniques, augmentative communication devices.
* **Physiotherapy & OT**: Gait training, fall prevention strategies, home environment modification, specialized seating.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Clinical Scoring Systems for HD (UHDRS)
* **Unified Huntington's Disease Rating Scale (UHDRS)**: Gold-standard comprehensive assessment tool.
  * Evaluates 4 domains: Motor, Cognitive, Behavioral, and Functional Capacity.
* **Total Functional Capacity (TFC)**: Sub-score of UHDRS used to stage disease (Stages I to V) and predict care requirements.

### Prognostic Trajectory and Mortality
* **Disease Progression**: Relentlessly progressive over **10 to 20 years** from symptom onset.
* **Juvenile HD Prognosis**: Faster progression, death typically within 10 years of onset.
* **Primary Causes of Death**:
  * Respiratory failure secondary to **Aspiration Pneumonia** (most common).
  * Cardiovascular disease.
  * **Suicide** (highest risk immediately before/after genetic testing and at onset of loss of independence).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Systemic Complications
* **Nutritional**: Severe cachexia and progressive weight loss (despite adequate caloric intake).
* **Musculoskeletal**: Recurrent fractures and subdural hematomas from chorea-induced falls.
* **Psychiatric**: Extremely high rate of completed suicide (up to 4x general population).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Severe dysphagia and recurrent choking episodes.

### Pharmacological Adverse Effects
* **VMAT2 Inhibitors (Tetrabenazine)**: Severe worsening of depression, precipitation of suicidal ideation, drug-induced parkinsonism, akathisia.
* **Antipsychotics**: QTc prolongation, Metabolic syndrome (weight gain, dyslipidemia, diabetes with Olanzapine), Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the classic clinical triad of Huntington's Disease? Answer: Choreiform movements, progressive cognitive decline (frontostriatal dementia), and psychiatric disturbances.
* Viva Question: What is the genetic mechanism explaining why successive generations develop HD earlier and more severely? Answer: Genetic Anticipation, driven by the expansion of CAG trinucleotide repeats, which is particularly unstable during spermatogenesis (paternal transmission).
* Trap: Prescribing Tetrabenazine as first-line for chorea in an HD patient with active, poorly controlled depression. Pearl: Tetrabenazine is a VMAT2 inhibitor that rapidly depletes central monoamines (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine), which can fatally exacerbate depression and trigger suicide. Use an atypical antipsychotic like Olanzapine instead.
* Trap: Assuming the dementia in HD presents similarly to Alzheimer's Disease. Pearl: HD causes a subcortical dementia characterized by severe executive dysfunction, slowed processing speed, and apathy, but often spares cortical functions like language (no aphasia) and recognition (no agnosia) until very late stages.
* Pitfall: Sending blood for HTT CAG repeat genetic testing in an asymptomatic relative without formal preparation. You must never test asymptomatic at-risk individuals without a rigorous, multi-session pre-test genetic counseling protocol, as a positive result carries a massive psychological burden and acute suicide risk.
