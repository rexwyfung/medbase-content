# Myopericardial Disease (心肌心包疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Myopericardial Disease
* **Myopericarditis (心肌心包炎)**: Inflammatory syndrome of both the **myocardium (心肌)** and **pericardium (心包)**.
* **Dominant Feature**: Diagnosed based on the predominantly affected layer (Myocarditis vs. Pericarditis).
* **Clinical Presentation Spectrum**: Ranges from asymptomatic → self-limiting chest pain → fulminant cardiogenic shock.

### Viral & Infectious Etiologies
* **Viral (Most Common in HK/Global)**: **Coxsackievirus B**, Adenovirus, Parvovirus B19, HHV-6, Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV), SARS-CoV-2.
* **Bacterial**: **Mycobacterium tuberculosis (結核菌)** (Highly relevant in Hong Kong → classic cause of constrictive pericarditis), Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, Pneumococcus.
* **Fungal/Parasitic**: Histoplasmosis, Toxoplasmosis, Chagas disease (Trypanosoma cruzi - rare in HK).

### Non-Infectious Etiologies
* **Autoimmune/Systemic**: **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) (系統性紅斑狼瘡)**, Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA), Scleroderma, Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA).
* **Post-Cardiac Injury Syndrome**: **Dressler syndrome** (weeks post-MI), post-pericardiotomy.
* **Metabolic**: **Uremia (尿毒症)** (severe renal failure), Hypothyroidism (myxedema).
* **Neoplastic**: Lung cancer, Breast cancer, Lymphoma, Leukemia (direct invasion or paraneoplastic).
* **Toxic/Iatrogenic**: Radiation, Anthracyclines (Doxorubicin), Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), Clozapine.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Viral Myocarditis
* **Phase 1 (Viremia/Direct Cytotoxicity)**: Viral entry via **Coxsackie-Adenovirus Receptor (CAR)** → viral replication → direct myocyte lysis & necrosis (Day 1-7).
* **Phase 2 (Immune-Mediated Damage)**: T-cell activation, molecular mimicry (cross-reaction between viral antigens and cardiac myosin) → cytokine storm (TNF-α, IL-6) → secondary myocyte damage (Weeks 1-4).
* **Phase 3 (Dilated Cardiomyopathy)**: Defective viral clearance & chronic autoimmunity → fibrotic remodeling → **Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) (擴張型心肌病)**.

### Pathophysiology of Pericarditis & Tamponade
* **Pericardial Inflammation**: Serous/fibrinous exudate forms between visceral and parietal pericardium → generates pain and friction.
* **Pericardial Effusion (心包積液)**: Accumulation of transudate, exudate, or blood in the pericardial space.
* **Cardiac Tamponade (心包填塞) Physiology**: Fluid accumulation exceeds pericardial compliance stretch limit → intrapericardial pressure > right-sided diastolic pressures → compression of right atrium/ventricle → impaired diastolic filling → ↓ stroke volume → ↓ cardiac output.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History & Symptomatology of Myopericarditis
* **Pain Characteristics**: Sharp, **pleuritic chest pain** (worse on inspiration/coughing).
* **Postural Relief**: Pain classically **relieved by sitting up and leaning forward**; exacerbated by lying supine.
* **Prodrome**: Recent history (1-2 weeks prior) of fever, myalgia, respiratory/GI symptoms.
* **Myocardial Involvement Symptoms**: Dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, palpitations, syncope.

### Cardiovascular Physical Signs of Pericardial Disease
* **Pericardial Friction Rub (心包摩擦音)**: High-pitched, scratchy, leathery sound. Best heard at left lower sternal border with patient leaning forward in end-expiration. Has up to 3 components (atrial systole, ventricular systole, rapid ventricular filling).
* **Ewart's Sign**: Dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, and egophony below the angle of the left scapula (due to large effusion compressing the left lower lung lobe).

### Hemodynamic Signs of Tamponade & Heart Failure
* **Beck's Triad (for Tamponade)**: Hypotension, Muffled heart sounds, Jugular Venous Distention (JVD).
* **Pulsus Paradoxus**: Abnormally large ↓ in systolic BP (**>10 mmHg**) during inspiration.
* **Kussmaul's Sign**: ↑ JVP with inspiration (typically absent in uncomplicated tamponade, classic for Constrictive Pericarditis/RV failure).
* **Signs of Myocardial Dysfunction**: S3 gallop, laterally displaced apex beat, fine bibasal lung crepitations, peripheral edema.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Critical Differentials for Acute Chest Pain

| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features (Hx & Ex) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI)** | Crushing/pressure-like pain, radiating to jaw/arm. NOT positional or pleuritic. Accompanied by diaphoresis, nausea. |
| **Pulmonary Embolism (PE)** | Sudden onset pleuritic pain + profound dyspnea, tachycardia, hemoptysis. DVT risk factors (immobilization, malignancy). |
| **Aortic Dissection** | Tearing/ripping pain radiating to the back. Asymmetrical pulses/blood pressures between arms. Aortic regurgitation murmur. |
| **Pneumothorax** | Sudden onset pleuritic pain + dyspnea. Hyperresonant percussion, absent breath sounds on affected side. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Diagnostic Criteria for Acute Pericarditis
* Requires **≥2 of the following 4** criteria:
  * 1. Characteristic chest pain (pleuritic, positional).
  * 2. Pericardial friction rub.
  * 3. Typical ECG changes (widespread ST elevation/PR depression).
  * 4. New or worsening pericardial effusion.

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **ECG (Classic 4 Stages of Pericarditis)**:
  * **Stage 1**: Widespread, diffuse **concave-up ST-segment elevation** & **PR-segment depression** (except in aVR and V1 which show PR elevation/ST depression).
  * **Stage 2**: Normalization of ST and PR segments.
  * **Stage 3**: Widespread T-wave inversion.
  * **Stage 4**: Normalization of T-waves.
* **ECG (Myocarditis)**: Sinus tachycardia (out of proportion to fever), ventricular arrhythmias, AV blocks, localized ST-T changes.
* **Bloods**: 
  * **Biomarkers**: ↑ Troponin I/T (indicates myocardial necrosis), ↑ CK-MB.
  * **Inflammatory markers**: ↑ CRP, ↑ ESR, ↑ WBC.
  * **Etiological screen**: Viral serology/PCR, Blood cultures, Autoimmune panel (ANA, ENA, RF), Quantiferon-TB/T-SPOT.

### Imaging Modalities (ECHO)
* **Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE)**: 
  * **Pericardial disease**: Echo-free space (effusion). Tamponade signs: Right Atrial (RA) systolic collapse, Right Ventricular (RV) diastolic collapse, plethoric IVC with loss of respiratory variation.
  * **Myocarditis**: Global or regional wall motion abnormalities, ↓ Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF), increased ventricular wall thickness (myocardial edema).

### Lake Louise Criteria for Myocarditis (Cardiac MRI)
* **Definitive Non-Invasive Diagnosis**: Requires ≥1 criteria from both Category A and B.

| Category | CMR Finding | Pathophysiological Correlate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A: Myocardial Edema** | ↑ T2 relaxation time OR ↑ Signal intensity on T2-weighted imaging | Active tissue inflammation & edema |
| **B: Non-Ischemic Injury** | ↑ T1, ↑ Extracellular volume (ECV), or **Late Gadolinium Enhancement (LGE)** | Necrosis, fibrosis, or capillary leak |

### Endomyocardial Biopsy (EMB) & Dallas Criteria
* **Indications**: Fulminant heart failure of unknown cause, suspected Giant Cell Myocarditis, suspected Eosinophilic Myocarditis.
* **Dallas Criteria**: Active myocarditis = Inflammatory cellular infiltrate + myocyte necrosis (not typical of ischemic damage).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Medical Therapy for Viral/Idiopathic Pericarditis
* **First-line dual therapy**:
  * **NSAIDs**: Ibuprofen 600-800mg TDS OR Aspirin 750-1000mg TDS (First choice if post-MI). Give with PPI for gastroprotection.
  * *PLUS* **Colchicine (秋水仙鹼)**: 0.5mg OD/BD (weight-dependent) for 3 months (reduces recurrence rate).
* **Refractory/Contraindicated cases**: Low-dose systemic Corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone) used ONLY if NSAIDs/Colchicine are contraindicated (e.g., pregnancy, severe renal failure) or in immune-mediated etiologies.

### Hemodynamic Resuscitation in Fulminant Myocarditis
* **Cardiogenic Shock Protocol**:
  * Inotropic support: Dobutamine or Milrinone.
  * Vasopressor support: Norepinephrine if profoundly hypotensive.
* **Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS)**:
  * Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP), Impella, or **Veno-Arterial Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (VA-ECMO)** as a bridge to recovery or transplant.

### Emergency Management of Cardiac Tamponade
* **Definitive Rx**: **Urgent Echocardiography-guided Pericardiocentesis (心包穿刺術)**.
* **Alternative**: Surgical pericardial window (if complex loculations, recurrent, or traumatic).
* **Fluid Resuscitation**: IV crystalloid bolus to maintain right-sided filling pressures until drainage can be performed.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Recurrent Pericarditis Pharmacological Step-Up Algorithm

| Step | Therapy | Indication/Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | NSAIDs + Colchicine | Acute flare-up. Colchicine duration extended to >6 months. |
| **Step 2** | Low-dose Corticosteroids | Refractory to Step 1, or autoimmune etiology. Require slow taper. |
| **Step 3** | IL-1 Receptor Antagonists | **Anakinra**, Rilonacept. Used for steroid-dependent/colchicine-resistant disease. |
| **Step 4** | Immunomodulators | Azathioprine, IVIG. |
| **Step 5** | Surgical Pericardiectomy | Intractable pain or progression to constrictive pericarditis. |

### Chronic Heart Failure Management post-Myocarditis
* If persistent LVEF depression, initiate Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT):
  * **ARNI** (Sacubitril/Valsartan) or ACE-i/ARB.
  * **Beta-blockers** (Bisoprolol, Carvedilol - initiate *only* when euvolemic).
  * **MRAs** (Spironolactone, Eplerenone).
  * **SGLT2 inhibitors** (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin).

### Lifestyle Modifications & Sports Restriction
* **Physical Activity**: Strict avoidance of competitive sports/intense aerobic exercise for **3 to 6 months** after resolution of acute myopericarditis.
* **Clearance**: Requires normalization of LVEF, absence of arrhythmias on Holter, and normalization of biomarkers/ECG before returning to play.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### High-Risk Indicators in Acute Pericarditis (Indications for Admission)
* Major Predictors of poor prognosis/specific etiology:
  * Fever > 38°C.
  * Subacute onset.
  * Large pericardial effusion (diastolic echo-free space >20mm).
  * Evidence of Cardiac Tamponade.
  * Lack of response to 1 week of NSAID therapy.

### Prognosis of Myocarditis
* **Viral**: Usually self-limiting with complete recovery (50%). ~25% develop persistent cardiac dysfunction.
* **Giant Cell Myocarditis**: Extremely poor prognosis, rapid progression to cardiogenic shock and death without early transplant or aggressive immunosuppression.
* **Peripartum Cardiomyopathy**: High risk of recurrence in subsequent pregnancies if LVEF does not fully recover.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Cardiac & Hemodynamic Complications
* **Constrictive Pericarditis (縮窄性心包炎)**: Fibrotic, calcified, rigid pericardium restricting diastolic filling. Presents with Kussmaul's sign, pericardial knock, prominent y-descent on JVP. Requires surgical pericardiectomy.
* **Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)**: Chronic sequela of myocarditis leading to structural dilation and systolic heart failure.
* **Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD)**: Due to malignant ventricular arrhythmias (VT/VF) triggered by active myocardial inflammation or chronic fibrotic scar (LGE on MRI is highly arrhythmogenic).

### Iatrogenic Adverse Effects
* **Colchicine**: Diarrhea (most common dose-limiting toxicity), hepatotoxicity, bone marrow suppression.
* **NSAIDs**: Peptic ulcer disease/GI bleed, Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), fluid retention.
* **Corticosteroids**: ↑ risk of recurrent pericarditis (if tapered too rapidly), osteoporosis, immunosuppression, diabetes mellitus.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate the ST elevation in acute pericarditis from an acute STEMI? Answer: Pericarditis causes diffuse, widespread ST elevation (concave-up) not corresponding to a single coronary territory, usually accompanied by PR segment depression. It lacks reciprocal ST depression (except in aVR/V1) and Q waves.
* Trap: Prescribing corticosteroids as first-line therapy for acute idiopathic pericarditis. Pearl: Corticosteroids should be avoided initially as they significantly increase the rate of recurrent pericarditis. Use high-dose NSAIDs + Colchicine first.
* Pitfall: Withholding fluids in a hypotensive patient with cardiac tamponade. A patient with tamponade is highly dependent on high right-sided venous pressures to maintain cardiac filling against the intrapericardial pressure. Give IV fluids immediately to augment preload while preparing for definitive pericardiocentesis.
* Viva Question: What is the classic finding of Constrictive Pericarditis on JVP waveform? Answer: Prominent x and y descents (W-shaped).
* Trap: Relying solely on a negative troponin to rule out myocarditis. Pearl: Troponin can be normal in biopsy-proven myocarditis. If clinical suspicion is high, proceed to Cardiac MRI (CMR) to assess for edema and late gadolinium enhancement.
* Pitfall: Discharging a myocarditis patient without specific lifestyle advice. You must explicitly advise strict abstention from intense physical exercise for 3-6 months to prevent sudden cardiac death from exercise-induced arrhythmias.
