# Respiratory Viral Infections (呼吸道病毒感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Respiratory Viral Infections
* **Def:** Acute viral invasion of mucosal epithelium from upper (URTI) to lower (LRTI) respiratory tract
* **URTI:** Rhinitis, pharyngitis, laryngitis
* **LRTI:** Tracheobronchitis, bronchiolitis, viral pneumonia

### Key Viral Etiologies
* **Influenza (流行性感冒):** Orthomyxoviridae (Type A, B, C); Subtypes based on Hemagglutinin (H) & Neuraminidase (N) antigens
* **COVID-19 (新型冠狀病毒):** Coronaviridae (SARS-CoV-2)
* **RSV (呼吸道合胞病毒):** Paramyxoviridae
* **Others:** Adenovirus, Rhinovirus, Parainfluenza, Human Metapneumovirus (hMPV)

### Hong Kong Epidemiology
* **Influenza:** Bimodal peaks (Winter: Jan-Mar, Summer: Jul-Aug)
* **COVID-19:** Endemic, all-year circulation; periodic variant waves
* **RSV:** Peak in spring/summer; major cause of COPD/Asthma exacerbations & elderly pneumonia in HA wards

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Influenza
* **Hemagglutinin (HA):** Binds to host sialic acid receptors → virion cellular entry
* **Neuraminidase (NA):** Cleaves sialic acid → releases progeny virions (Target of Oseltamivir)
* **Antigenic Drift:** Minor point mutations (HA/NA) → seasonal epidemics
* **Antigenic Shift:** Major RNA segment reassortment (Type A only) → global pandemics

### Pathophysiology of SARS-CoV-2
* **Receptor:** Viral Spike (S) protein binds **ACE2** on alveolar type II cells
* **Cleavage:** TMPRSS2 protease primes S protein → membrane fusion
* **Cytokine Storm:** Macrophage/T-cell hyperactivation → massive IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α release → ARDS & endothelial injury

### Pathophysiology of RSV
* **F (Fusion) Protein:** Mediates merging of host cell membranes → **Syncytia** (multinucleated giant cells) formation
* **Mechanism:** Sloughed necrotic epithelium + dense mucus → small airway plugging (bronchiolitis)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Detailed History (TOCC)
* **Constitutional:** Sudden onset high fever, severe myalgia, profound fatigue, arthralgia
* **Respiratory:** Dry non-productive cough, coryza, sore throat, dyspnea
* **COVID-19 Specific:** Anosmia (loss of smell), ageusia (loss of taste)
* **TOCC Hx:** Travel, Occupation, Contact, Cluster (Crucial in HA A&E triage)

### System-Specific Physical Signs
* **Vitals:** Fever (>38.5°C), tachypnea, tachycardia, hypoxia (↓SpO2)
* **URTI Exam:** Pharyngeal erythema, rhinorrhea (usually -ve purulent exudates)
* **LRTI/Pneumonia Exam:** Bilateral fine basal crepitations, ↓ air entry
* **Bronchiolitis/Reactive Airway Exam:** Expiratory wheeze, prolonged expiratory phase, accessory muscle use

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Acute Viral Respiratory Presentation
| Differential | Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bacterial Pneumonia (細菌性肺炎)** | Productive cough with purulent/rust-colored sputum, focal bronchial breathing, lobar consolidation |
| **Atypical Pneumonia (非典型肺炎)** | Insidious onset, extrapulmonary features (erythema multiforme in *Mycoplasma*, diarrhea in *Legionella*) |
| **Acute Exacerbation of COPD** | Known heavy smoker, hyperinflated chest, triggers may be non-viral |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | Pleuritic chest pain, hemoptysis, +ve D-dimer, sudden severe dyspnea |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Laboratory Bloods
* **Bedside:** SpO2, ECG (exclude ACS/myocarditis)
* **CBP:** Leukopenia, severe **lymphopenia** (classic in COVID-19/Flu), normal or mildly ↑ WCC
* **Inflammatory Markers:** ↑ CRP; **Procalcitonin (PCT) usually NORMAL** (<0.25 mcg/L) → if PCT markedly ↑, highly suggests bacterial superinfection
* **Organ Fn:** LFT (mild transaminitis), RFT (AKI from dehydration/sepsis)

### Microbiological Investigations
* **Specimen:** Deep Throat Saliva (DTS), Nasopharyngeal Aspirate (NPA), or Nasal/Throat Swab (NTS)
* **Multiplex PCR Panel:** BioFire/FilmArray (Detects Flu A/B, RSV, Rhino, Adeno, SARS-CoV-2 simultaneously)
* **RAT (Rapid Antigen Test):** Point-of-care screening; high specificity, lower sensitivity than PCR

### Imaging Findings
* **CXR:** Often normal early; classic viral pneumonia shows bilateral, patchy interstitial infiltrates
* **COVID-19 CXR:** Peripheral, lower-zone predominant patchy ground-glass opacities (GGO)
* **HRCT Thorax:** Reserved for severe/atypical cases; shows bilateral GGOs with "crazy paving" pattern

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation & Oxygenation
* **Hypoxia Protocol:** Titrate O2 to target SpO2 94-98% (88-92% if Type II Respiratory Failure risk)
* **Severe ARDS Escalation:** High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) → Non-Invasive Ventilation (CPAP/BiPAP) → Intubation (Low tidal volume strategy)
* **Steroids:** **IV/PO Dexamethasone 6mg OD x 10d** (ONLY for COVID-19 patients requiring supplemental O2)

### Acute Antiviral Therapy (HA Protocols)
* **Influenza:**
 * **Oseltamivir (特敏福) PO:** 75mg BD x 5d (Start <48hrs, but give anyway in severe/hospitalized cases)
 * **Peramivir IV:** 600mg single dose (If PO contraindicated/severe malabsorption)
* **COVID-19:**
 * **Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir (Paxlovid) PO:** For mild/mod disease with high risk of progression (<5d of onset)
 * **Molnupiravir PO:** Alternative if Paxlovid contraindicated
 * **Remdesivir IV:** For severe hospitalized COVID-19 pneumonia
* **RSV:** Supportive; Ribavirin inhalation strictly reserved for severe immunocompromised (e.g., HSCT patients)

### Immunomodulators (Severe COVID-19)
* **Baricitinib (PO) / Tocilizumab (IV):** IL-6/JAK inhibitors added if rapid respiratory deterioration despite Dexamethasone + CRP >75mg/L

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Primary Prevention & Vaccinations
* **Influenza Vaccine:** Annual Quadrivalent Influenza Vaccine (QIV); mandatory for HA HCWs & high-risk groups
* **COVID-19 Vaccine:** mRNA (Comirnaty) boosters recommended for elderly/immunosuppressed based on variants
* **RSV Vaccine:** Recombinant protein vaccines (Arexvy, Abrysvo) indicated for adults >60 yrs with cardiopulmonary disease

### Post-Viral Syndrome & Long COVID Management
* **Long COVID (長新冠):** Symptoms persisting >12 weeks post-infection
* **Pulmonary Rehab:** Graded exercise therapy, spirometry monitoring for fibrotic sequelae
* **Chronic Cough Rx:** Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) or ipratropium bromide for post-viral bronchial hyperreactivity
* **Infection Control:** Strict hand hygiene, universal masking in clinical areas during peak seasons

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Factors for Severe Disease & Mortality
* Extreme of ages (<2 yrs, >65 yrs)
* Pregnancy (Especially 3rd trimester for Flu)
* Comorbidities: COPD, Asthma, CHF, DM, ESRF
* Immunocompromised: Post-transplant, HIV, active chemotherapy

### Pneumonia Severity Scoring (CURB-65)
| Parameter (1 point each) | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **C**onfusion | AMTS <= 8 |
| **U**rea | > 7 mmol/L |
| **R**espiratory Rate | >= 30 breaths/min |
| **B**lood Pressure | SBP < 90 mmHg or DBP <= 60 mmHg |
| **65** | Age >= 65 years |

* **Score 0-1:** Outpatient Rx; **Score 2:** Ward admission; **Score 3-5:** ICU admission

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease Complications
* **Pulmonary:** Secondary bacterial pneumonia, ARDS, organizing pneumonia
* **Cardiac:** Acute myocarditis, pericarditis, precipitation of Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI)
* **Neurological:** Post-infectious Encephalitis, Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)
* **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis → Myoglobinuria → AKI (especially Flu A)

### Drug Adverse Effects
* **Oseltamivir:** Nausea/vomiting (most common), rare neuropsychiatric events (delirium, self-injury) in adolescents
* **Paxlovid (Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir):** Severe **CYP3A4 interactions**; requires withholding Statins, adjusting DOACs/calcineurin inhibitors; Dysgeusia (metallic taste)

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the classic secondary bacterial pneumonia following an influenza infection? Answer: Staphylococcus aureus pneumonia (often severe, necrotizing, and cavitating).
* Trap: Prescribing antibiotics for an uncomplicated viral URTI to prevent secondary infection. Pearl: Antibiotics do not prevent secondary bacterial infections in viral RTIs and only promote antimicrobial resistance; reserve antibiotics for confirmed or highly suspected bacterial superinfections (e.g., purulent sputum, rising Procalcitonin).
* Pitfall: Prescribing Paxlovid without checking the patient's existing medication list. Ritonavir is a potent CYP3A4 inhibitor and can cause fatal toxicity when combined with drugs like Rivaroxaban, Tacrolimus, or Simvastatin.
