# Urticaria & Angioedema (蕁麻疹及血管性水腫)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Urticaria & Angioedema
* **Urticaria (蕁麻疹 / 風癩)**: Pruritic wheals (hives) ± surrounding erythema caused by superficial dermal edema; lesions typically last <24h without scarring.
* **Angioedema (血管性水腫)**: Swelling of deep dermis, subcutaneous, or submucosal tissues; often non-pruritic, can be painful, resolving in up to 72h.

### Classification of Urticaria
* **Acute Urticaria**: Duration <6 weeks.
* **Chronic Urticaria (慢性蕁麻疹)**: Duration >6 weeks, occurring most days of the week.

### Etiologies of Acute Urticaria
* **Infections**: Viral (URI, Hepatitis, EBV, Coxsackie), parasitic, bacterial.
* **Drugs**: Penicillins, NSAIDs, opiates, radiocontrast media.
* **Foods/Allergens**: Shellfish, peanuts, latex, insect stings.
* **Idiopathic**: ~50% of acute cases.

### Etiologies of Chronic Urticaria
* **Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria (CSU) (慢性自發性蕁麻疹)**: Idiopathic or autoimmune (associated with thyroid disease).
* **Chronic Inducible Urticaria (CINDU) (慢性誘發性蕁麻疹)**: Triggered by physical stimuli (cold, heat, solar, pressure, vibration, cholinergic/sweat, aquagenic).

### Etiologies of Angioedema Without Urticaria
* **Drug-induced**: ACE-inhibitors (ACEi) (most common locally in HK HA clinics), ARBs, NSAIDs, gliptins (DPP-4 inhibitors).
* **Hereditary Angioedema (HAE) (遺傳性血管水腫)**: Autosomal dominant mutation (SERPING1 gene) → C1-inhibitor (C1-INH) deficiency/dysfunction.
* **Acquired Angioedema (AAE)**: Associated with lymphoproliferative disorders (B-cell lymphomas) or autoimmune diseases → autoantibodies against C1-INH.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Histamine-Mediated Urticaria & Angioedema
* **IgE-Mediated (Type 1 Hypersensitivity)**: Allergen cross-links IgE bound to FcεRI on mast cells/basophils → rapid degranulation.
* **Non-IgE-Mediated**: Direct mast cell degranulation by drugs (opiates, vancomycin) or physical stimuli.
* **Autoimmune (CSU)**: IgG autoantibodies against IgE or the high-affinity IgE receptor (FcεRI) → spontaneous mast cell activation.
* **Mediator Release**: Histamine, prostaglandins, leukotrienes release → bind H1/H2 receptors on venules → ↑ capillary permeability, vasodilation, stimulation of unmyelinated C-fibers (pruritus).

### Pathophysiology of Bradykinin-Mediated Angioedema (ACEi & HAE)
* **Bradykinin Pathway**: Kininogen → (via Kallikrein) → Bradykinin → binds B2 receptors → profound vasodilation & ↑ vascular permeability (NO pruritus).
* **HAE/AAE Mechanism**: Defective/absent C1-INH → unchecked activation of the contact/kallikrein-kinin system → massive bradykinin overproduction.
* **ACEi Mechanism**: ACE normally degrades bradykinin; ACE inhibition → ↑ bradykinin accumulation (can occur after years of stable ACEi use).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for Urticaria & Angioedema
* **Chronology**: Onset, duration of individual lesions (<24h vs >24h), total duration of disease (<6 wks vs >6 wks).
* **Precipitants**: New medications (especially antibiotics, NSAIDs, ACEi), recent viral illnesses, specific foods, physical triggers (heat, cold, stress, exercise, tight clothing).
* **Associated Symptoms**: Dyspnea, dysphagia, voice change (airway compromise); abdominal pain/diarrhea (bowel edema).
* **Family History**: Unexplained sudden deaths, recurrent abdominal pain, or angioedema (HAE).

### Physical Signs of Urticaria (風癩)
* **Wheals**: Circumscribed, raised, erythematous/pale plaques with central pallor.
* **Size/Shape**: Millimeters to giant confluent plaques; annular, polycyclic, or serpiginous.
* **Evanescent Nature**: Individual lesions resolve within 24 hours without leaving bruising or hyperpigmentation.
* **Dermographism (皮膚劃痕症)**: Linear wheal formation strictly occurring along the line of skin scratching/stroking.

### Physical Signs of Angioedema (血管性水腫)
* **Distribution**: Predominantly involves areas with loose connective tissue (lips, eyelids, tongue, genitalia, extremities).
* **Appearance**: Non-pitting, asymmetric, ill-defined swelling.
* **Mucosal Involvement**: Stridor, hoarseness, uvular swelling, macroglossia.
* **Abdominal Signs**: Diffuse tenderness, distension, absent/hyperactive bowel sounds (signs of visceral angioedema).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Urticaria from Urticarial Vasculitis
* **Urticaria**: Lesions last <24 hours, extremely pruritic, resolve completely.
* **Urticarial Vasculitis**: Lesions last >24 hours, described as burning/painful rather than itchy, resolve with residual bruising (purpura) or hyperpigmentation.

### Differentiating Bradykinin vs Histamine Angioedema
* **Histamine-Mediated**: Accompanying urticaria common, severe pruritus, short duration (hours), rapidly responds to antihistamines/corticosteroids.
* **Bradykinin-Mediated**: Urticaria ALWAYS ABSENT, painful rather than pruritic, prominent GI involvement (severe abdominal pain), unresponsive to antihistamines/corticosteroids/adrenaline.

### Other Rash Differentials
* **Erythema Multiforme**: Target lesions with 3 distinct zones, often acral distribution.
* **Bullous Pemphigoid (Early Prodrome)**: Urticarial plaques pre-blistering, highly pruritic, common in elderly.
* **Mastocytosis (Urticaria Pigmentosa)**: Brown macules that wheal upon rubbing (Darier's sign).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Clinical Provocation Tests for Inducible Urticaria
* **Symptomatic Dermographism**: Firmly stroke skin with a blunt object (e.g., pen cap) → evaluate for wheal at 10 mins.
* **Cold Urticaria**: Ice cube test on forearm for 5 mins → evaluate for wheal upon rewarming.
* **Cholinergic Urticaria**: Exercise or hot bath challenge.
* **Delayed Pressure Urticaria**: Suspend weights over shoulder/thigh for 15 mins → evaluate at 6-8 hours.

### Blood Investigations for Chronic Urticaria (CSU)
* **Routine Labs (Usually Normal)**: CBC with differential (eosinophilia suggests parasitic cause), ESR/CRP (normal in CSU; ↑ in vasculitis/systemic disease).
* **Targeted Labs**: TFTs and Anti-TPO antibodies (frequent association with autoimmune thyroiditis).
* **Note**: Routine allergy testing (IgE/Skin Prick) is NOT recommended for CSU unless a specific trigger is highly suspected from history.

### Diagnostic Workup for Isolated Angioedema (HAE vs AAE)
* **Screening Test**: Serum C4 level (low during attacks and often between attacks in both HAE and AAE).
* **Confirmatory Tests**: C1-INH Antigen Level, C1-INH Functional Assay, C1q Level.

| Type | C4 Level | C1-INH Antigen Level | C1-INH Function | C1q Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HAE Type I (85%)** | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | Normal |
| **HAE Type II (15%)** | ↓ | Normal / ↑ | ↓ | Normal |
| **Acquired (AAE)** | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | **↓ (Key Differentiator)** |
| **ACEi Angioedema**| Normal | Normal | Normal | Normal |

### Skin Biopsy Indications
* Indicated ONLY if Urticarial Vasculitis is suspected (lesions >24h, painful, residual bruising).
* **Findings**: Leukocytoclasis, fibrinoid necrosis of vessel walls, erythrocyte extravasation.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Airway Angioedema Resuscitation
* **Immediate Priority**: Assess airway patency (stridor, drooling).
* **Intervention**: Early call for Anesthetist/ENT. Prepare for fiberoptic intubation or emergency cricothyroidotomy/tracheostomy.

### Acute Management of Histamine-Mediated Angioedema & Severe Urticaria
* **First Line**: IV/IM Chlorpheniramine (1st Gen H1-antihistamine) or PO 2nd Gen H1-antihistamines.
* **Adjuncts**: IV Hydrocortisone (to prevent late-phase reaction) + IV Famotidine (H2-antihistamine).
* **Anaphylaxis Overlap**: If airway/breathing compromised or hypotension exists → IM Adrenaline (0.5mg of 1:1000) immediately.

### Acute Management of Bradykinin-Mediated Angioedema (HAE/ACEi)
* **CRITICAL RULE**: Adrenaline, antihistamines, and corticosteroids are INEFFECTIVE. Do not delay specific therapy.
* **Targeted Therapy (HK HA protocol)**:
  * **Icatibant (Firazyr)**: Selective Bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist (SC injection).
  * **C1-INH Concentrate (Berinert/Cinryze)**: IV infusion to replace missing enzyme.
  * **Rescue/Resource Poor**: Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) if specific agents are unavailable (contains C1-INH, but theoretically contains kininogen substrate which can transiently worsen symptoms).
* **ACEi Angioedema**: Immediately discontinue ACEi. Airway protection is primary; Icatibant used off-label in severe cases.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Lifestyle & Trigger Avoidance
* Cease suspected medications (NSAIDs exacerbate ~30% of chronic urticaria).
* Avoid tight clothing, extreme temperatures, hot showers, and excessive alcohol (vasodilators).

### Stepwise Pharmacotherapy Algorithm for Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria (CSU)
* **Step 1**: Second-generation H1-antihistamine at standard dose (e.g., Loratadine 10mg OM, Cetirizine 10mg OM, Fexofenadine 180mg OM). Avoid 1st Gen due to sedation/anticholinergic effects.
* **Step 2 (If symptoms persist >2-4 weeks)**: Up-dose the 2nd Gen H1-antihistamine up to 4-fold the standard licensed dose (e.g., Cetirizine 40mg/day).
* **Step 3 (If inadequate control on max dose antihistamine)**: Add **Omalizumab (奧馬珠單抗)** (Subcutaneous Anti-IgE monoclonal antibody every 4 weeks).
* **Step 4 (If Omalizumab fails/unavailable)**: Add **Cyclosporine A (環孢素)** (calcineurin inhibitor).
* **Flare Management**: Short course of oral Prednisolone (e.g., 20-50mg/day for max 10 days) to regain control; strictly avoid long-term chronic corticosteroids.

### Prophylactic Management for Hereditary Angioedema (HAE)
* **Attenuated Androgens**: Danazol (stimulates hepatic synthesis of C1-INH).
* **Antifibrinolytics**: Tranexamic acid (less effective, used in pediatrics/pregnancy where Danazol is contraindicated).
* **Modern Biologics/Prophylaxis**: Lanadelumab (Plasma kallikrein inhibitor mAb) or regular SC C1-INH concentrate.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Urticaria Activity Score over 7 days (UAS7)
* Standardized tool to assess CSU disease activity and guide therapy step-up.
* Patient scores daily for 7 consecutive days (Max daily score = 6, Max weekly UAS7 = 42).

| Score (Daily) | Number of Wheals | Pruritus Intensity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | None | None |
| **1 (Mild)** | <20 wheals / 24h | Present but not bothersome |
| **2 (Moderate)** | 20-50 wheals / 24h | Bothersome, does not interfere with sleep/activities |
| **3 (Severe)** | >50 wheals / 24h or large confluent | Severe, interferes with sleep/daily activities |

* **Interpretation**: UAS7 ≤6 indicates well-controlled disease; UAS7 ≥16 indicates severe disease.

### Prognosis of Chronic Urticaria
* Highly variable but generally self-limiting over years.
* ~50% of CSU cases achieve spontaneous remission within 1 year; ~80% within 5 years.
* Concomitant angioedema or severe initial disease predicts longer duration.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Urticaria and Angioedema
* **Airway Asphyxiation**: Fatal upper airway obstruction (highest risk in laryngeal HAE/ACEi angioedema).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Severe bowel edema mimicking acute surgical abdomen → unnecessary laparotomies; hypovolemic shock due to massive fluid shift into bowel.
* **Psychiatric**: Severe anxiety, depression, and sleep deprivation (from intense pruritus).

### Adverse Effects of CSU Therapies
* **1st Gen H1-Antihistamines (Chlorpheniramine/Diphenhydramine)**: Blood-brain barrier penetration → severe sedation, impaired cognition. Antimuscarinic effects → dry mouth, urinary retention, narrow-angle glaucoma exacerbation.
* **Omalizumab**: Injection site reactions, rare risk of anaphylaxis (requires observation post-injection).
* **Cyclosporine**: Nephrotoxicity, hypertension, gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, immunosuppression.

### Adverse Effects of HAE Therapies
* **Danazol (Androgen)**: Virilization in females, weight gain, hepatotoxicity, dyslipidemia, contraindicated in pregnancy.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate clinically between histamine-mediated and bradykinin-mediated angioedema? Answer: Histamine-mediated typically presents with concurrent urticaria and intense pruritus. Bradykinin-mediated (e.g., ACEi-induced, HAE) never has urticaria, is non-pruritic, and often features severe abdominal pain due to bowel edema.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing a patient with an acute urticarial flare and dyspnea as just "angioedema" without checking blood pressure. Anaphylaxis is a systemic IgE response presenting with hypotension and bronchospasm; urticaria/angioedema are purely dermatological/mucosal. Always check for shock and treat with IM adrenaline if present.
* Trap: Assuming a patient cannot have ACE-inhibitor-induced angioedema because they have been on Lisinopril safely for 5 years. Pearl: ACEi-induced angioedema can occur at ANY TIME during treatment, even after years of uncomplicated use, and is the most common cause of non-histaminergic angioedema in adults.
* Viva Question: What is the most definitive screening blood test for Hereditary Angioedema (HAE) during and between attacks? Answer: Serum C4 level, which is persistently low due to continuous consumption by the overactive classical complement pathway.
* Trap: Administering IM adrenaline, IV hydrocortisone, and IV chlorpheniramine to a patient with known Hereditary Angioedema presenting with laryngeal swelling. Pearl: Bradykinin-mediated angioedema is completely unresponsive to adrenaline, steroids, and antihistamines. You must administer specific agents like Icatibant or C1-INH concentrate immediately.
* Pitfall: Using routine serum IgE levels or skin prick testing to investigate Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria (CSU). CSU is rarely caused by true exogenous allergens; indiscriminate allergy testing yields false positives leading to unnecessary dietary restrictions. Diagnosis is clinical.
