# Bleeding Disorders (出血性疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Primary vs Secondary Hemostasis
* **Primary Hemostasis (初級止血)**: Defect in platelet plug formation at site of endothelial injury (platelet quantity/quality or vessel wall defect)
* **Secondary Hemostasis (次級止血)**: Defect in coagulation cascade preventing stable fibrin clot formation (coagulation factor deficiencies/inhibitors)
* **Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA, 微血管病性溶血性貧血)**: Syndromes with microthrombi causing consumptive thrombocytopenia and RBC shearing [e.g., TTP, HUS, DIC]

### Etiologies of Immune & Consumptive Thrombocytopenias
* **Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP, 免疫性血小板減少症)**: Primary (idiopathic) or Secondary (SLE, HIV, HCV, H. pylori, CLL)
* **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP, 血栓性血小板減少性紫癜)**: Acquired autoantibody or inherited (Upshaw-Schulman)
* **Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS, 溶血尿毒症綜合症)**: Typical (Shiga-toxin E. coli O157:H7) or Atypical (Complement dysregulation)
* **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC, 彌散性血管內凝血)**: Sepsis, trauma, obstetric emergencies, malignancy (APML, mucin-secreting adenocarcinomas)
* **Drug-Induced Thrombocytopenia (DITP, 藥物誘發性血小板減少症)**: Heparin (HIT), Quinine, Valproate, Beta-lactams, Linezolid

### Etiologies of Coagulation Factor Deficiencies
* **Inherited**:
* **Hemophilia A (甲型血友病)**: Factor VIII deficiency (X-linked recessive)
* **Hemophilia B (乙型血友病)**: Factor IX deficiency (X-linked recessive)
* **Von Willebrand Disease (vWD, 血管性血友病)**: Most common inherited bleeding disorder (Autosomal dominant/recessive)
* **Acquired**:
* **Liver Cirrhosis (肝硬化)**: ↓ synthesis of all factors except VIII and vWF
* **Vitamin K Deficiency**: Malabsorption, malnutrition, biliary obstruction (affects II, VII, IX, X, Protein C/S)

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Hemophilia**: ~250-300 patients managed under Hong Kong Hospital Authority (HA) specialized hemophilia centers (e.g., PWH, QMH)
* **ITP**: High prevalence of secondary ITP linked to endemic HBV/HCV and rheumatological diseases in Southern Chinese populations

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)
* **IgG Autoantibodies**: Target platelet surface glycoproteins (mostly GpIIb/IIIa or GpIb/IX)
* **Destruction**: Sensitized platelets destroyed by splenic macrophages via Fc-receptors
* **Production**: Autoantibodies also attack megakaryocytes → ↓ thrombopoiesis

### Pathophysiology of Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP)
* **Enzyme Deficiency**: Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (<10% activity) due to IgG autoantibodies
* **Mechanism**: Inability to cleave ultra-large von Willebrand factor (ULVWF) multimers
* **Consequence**: Unregulated platelet adhesion → microthrombi in small vessels → organ ischemia + mechanical hemolysis

### Pathophysiology of Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS)
* **Typical HUS**: Shiga toxin (enterohemorrhagic E. coli) damages renal endothelial cells → local microthrombi (predominantly in kidneys)
* **Atypical HUS**: Genetic mutations in Alternative Complement Pathway (e.g., Factor H) → unchecked complement activation on endothelium

### Pathophysiology of Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)
* **Trigger**: Massive systemic release of Tissue Factor (Factor III) or endotoxins
* **Hypercoagulation Phase**: Widespread microvascular thrombosis causing multi-organ failure
* **Consumption Phase**: Depletion of platelets, coagulation factors, and fibrinogen → paradoxical severe bleeding
* **Fibrinolysis**: Secondary activation of plasmin → ↑ Fibrin Degradation Products (FDPs) / D-dimers

### Pathophysiology of Hemophilia A and B
* **Hemophilia A**: FVIII deficiency interrupts the intrinsic tenase complex (FIXa-FVIIIa)
* **Hemophilia B**: FIX deficiency impairs conversion of FX to FXa
* **Result**: Failure to generate a sufficient thrombin burst to form a stable fibrin clot

### Pathophysiology of Von Willebrand Disease (vWD)
* **vWF Function 1**: Mediates initial platelet adhesion to subendothelial collagen via GpIb
* **vWF Function 2**: Carrier protein that binds and stabilizes Factor VIII in circulation (prevents rapid degradation)
* **Consequence**: Defect causes mixed primary (platelet-type) and secondary (coagulation-type) bleeding patterns

### Pathophysiology of Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT)
* **IgG Formation**: Antibodies against Heparin-Platelet Factor 4 (PF4) complexes
* **Activation**: Immune complex binds platelet FcγIIA receptors → massive platelet activation and consumption → paradoxical arterial/venous thrombosis

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History in Primary vs Secondary Bleeding Disorders
* **Primary Hemostasis Defect History**:
* Immediate bleeding post-trauma/surgery
* Epistaxis, gingival bleeding, menorrhagia
* Easy bruising from minimal contact
* **Secondary Hemostasis Defect History**:
* Delayed bleeding (hours to days post-trauma)
* Deep muscle hematomas
* Spontaneous joint bleeds (hemarthrosis)

### System-Specific Physical Signs in Bleeding Disorders
* **Dermatological**:
* **Petechiae**: Pinpoint (<2mm) non-blanching hemorrhages (hallmark of severe thrombocytopenia)
* **Purpura**: Confluent petechiae (2-10mm)
* **Ecchymoses**: Large bruises (>10mm)
* **Musculoskeletal**:
* **Hemarthrosis**: Warm, swollen, painful, restricted joints (knees, elbows, ankles in Hemophilia)
* **Target Joint**: Deformed, chronically swollen joint from repeated bleeds
* **Gastrointestinal**:
* **Hepatomegaly/Splenomegaly**: Points away from primary ITP; suggests cirrhosis, portal hypertension, or hematological malignancy
* **Neurological**:
* Fluctuating conscious level, focal deficits, seizures (classic in TTP, intracranial hemorrhage in Hemophilia/ITP)

### Physical Signs in TTP (Classic Pentad)
* **Fever**: Unexplained pyrexia
* **Neurological**: Confusion, transient ischemic attacks, seizures
* **Renal**: Oliguria, edema (less prominent than HUS)
* **Dermatological**: Petechiae/purpura (thrombocytopenia)
* **General**: Pallor, jaundice (MAHA)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Primary vs Secondary Hemostasis Defects
| Feature | Primary Hemostasis (Platelet/vWD) | Secondary Hemostasis (Factors) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Site of Bleeding** | Mucous membranes, skin | Deep tissues, joints, muscles |
| **Type of Lesion** | Petechiae, purpura, superficial ecchymoses | Large spreading hematomas, hemarthrosis |
| **Timing post-injury** | Immediate | Delayed (hours to days) |
| **Response to Pressure** | Often stops bleeding | Often continues or restarts |
| **Gender bias** | Females (heavy menses) | Males (X-linked Hemophilias) |

### Differentiating TTP, HUS, and DIC
| Feature | TTP | HUS | DIC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Trigger** | Autoimmune (ADAMTS13) | Infection (E. coli O157:H7) | Sepsis, Trauma, Malignancy |
| **Key Organ Involvement** | Brain (Neuro symptoms) | Kidneys (Renal failure) | Multi-organ + Systemic bleeding |
| **Coagulation Profile** | Normal PT/aPTT | Normal PT/aPTT | Prolonged PT/aPTT |
| **Fibrinogen** | Normal | Normal | Consumed (Low) |

### Differentiating Inherited vs Acquired Bleeding Disorders
* **Inherited**: Lifelong history, positive family history, unprovoked severe bleeds since childhood, isolated factor deficiencies.
* **Acquired**: Adult onset, negative family history, systemic illness (liver disease, autoimmune), multiple factor deficiencies (DIC) or presence of inhibitors (acquired hemophilia).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Initial Investigations
* **Urinalysis**: Hematuria (vWD, Hemophilia, HUS)
* **Stool Occult Blood**: +ve in GI bleeding
* **ECG**: Rule out myocardial ischemia if severe anemia/hemorrhage

### Blood Tests: Complete Blood Count & Smear
* **CBC**:
* ↓ Platelets (<150 x 10^9/L indicates thrombocytopenia; <20 implies high spontaneous bleed risk)
* ↓ Hb (Anemia from hemorrhage or hemolysis)
* **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**:
* **Schistocytes (Fragmented RBCs)**: Diagnostic of MAHA (TTP, HUS, DIC)
* **Large Platelets**: Suggests rapid marrow turnover (ITP)
* **Absence of Blasts/Dysplasia**: Rules out leukemia/MDS

### Blood Tests: PT/aPTT Interpretation Algorithm
| Lab Finding | Interpretation | Key Differential Diagnoses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal PT & aPTT** | Platelet defect, mild factor def. | ITP, mild vWD, Factor XIII deficiency |
| **Prolonged PT, Normal aPTT** | Extrinsic pathway defect (Factor VII) | Early Warfarin, Early Liver Disease, Vit K def. |
| **Normal PT, Prolonged aPTT** | Intrinsic pathway defect (VIII, IX, XI) | Hemophilia A/B, vWD (if FVIII low), Lupus Anticoagulant, Heparin |
| **Prolonged PT & aPTT** | Common pathway / Multiple defects | DIC, Severe Liver Disease, Supratherapeutic Warfarin/DOACs |

### Blood Tests: DIC and Microangiopathy Screen
* **D-Dimer / FDPs**: Markedly ↑ in DIC (fibrinolysis)
* **Fibrinogen**: ↓ in DIC (consumed); Normal/↑ in TTP/HUS
* **Hemolysis Screen**: ↑ LDH, ↑ Indirect Bilirubin, ↓ Haptoglobin, +ve Schistocytes (TTP/HUS/DIC)
* **ADAMTS13 Activity**: <10% confirms TTP
* **Coombs Test**: Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT) to rule out autoimmune hemolytic anemia (Evans Syndrome if ITP + AIHA)
* **Mixing Study**: Normalizes prolonged aPTT in factor deficiency; fails to normalize if inhibitor present (e.g., Lupus Anticoagulant, Acquired Hemophilia)

### Blood Tests: Specific Factor Assays & vWD Panel
* **vWF Antigen (vWF:Ag)**: Quantitative level of vWF
* **Ristocetin Cofactor Activity (vWF:RCo)**: Qualitative function of vWF
* **Factor VIII Assay**: ↓ in Hemophilia A and vWD
* **Factor IX Assay**: ↓ in Hemophilia B

### Diagnostic Criteria for ITP
* Diagnosis of **exclusion**.
* Isolated thrombocytopenia (Platelets <100 x 10^9/L).
* Absence of systemic illness (normal PT/aPTT, normal smear except large platelets, no hepatosplenomegaly).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation in Severe Bleeding (General)
* **ABCDE Approach**: Secure airway (especially in neck/oral bleeds), large-bore IV access, group & crossmatch.
* **Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)**: Activate for unremitting hemorrhage (Ratio 1:1:1 of PRBCs:FFP:Platelets).
* **Local HA Guidelines**: Reversal of DOACs (Idarucizumab for Dabigatran, Andexanet alfa for Apixaban/Rivaroxaban, or Prothrombin Complex Concentrate [PCC] if antidotes unavailable).

### Acute Management of ITP Emergencies (Platelets <30 or Active Bleed)
* **First-line Medical Rescue**:
* **High-dose Corticosteroids**: IV Methylprednisolone (1g/day for 3 days) or PO Dexamethasone (40mg/day for 4 days).
* **IV Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: 1g/kg for 1-2 days (blocks splenic macrophage Fc-receptors; rapid ↑ platelets in 24-48h).
* **Platelet Transfusion**: ONLY if life-threatening hemorrhage (e.g., intracranial). In ITP, transfused platelets are destroyed rapidly; must give alongside IVIG/Steroids.

### Acute Management of TTP (Medical Emergency)
* **Plasma Exchange (PLEX)**: Gold standard (removes autoantibody, replaces ADAMTS13). Initiate immediately.
* **Immunosuppression**: High-dose IV Corticosteroids.
* **Caplacizumab**: Anti-vWF nanobody (inhibits platelet aggregation).
* **Contraindication**: NEVER transfuse platelets (fuels the microvascular fire → worsens thrombosis).

### Acute Management of DIC
* **Primary Objective**: Treat the underlying cause (IV antibiotics for sepsis, deliver placenta in ob-gyn, anti-leukemic therapy for APML).
* **Blood Product Support (Only if actively bleeding or high-risk surgery)**:
* **Platelets**: Maintain >50 x 10^9/L.
* **FFP**: If PT/aPTT significantly prolonged.
* **Cryoprecipitate**: Target Fibrinogen >1.5 g/L.

### Acute Management of Hemophilia Bleeds
* **Immediate Factor Replacement**:
* **Major bleeds (CNS, GI, trauma)**: Target 80-100% factor level.
* **Minor bleeds (joints, muscles)**: Target 40-50% factor level.
* **Bypass Agents**: Recombinant Factor VIIa (rFVIIa) or activated PCC (FEIBA) if patient has developed high-titer inhibitors.

### Transfusion Guidelines & Adjuncts (Hong Kong HA Protocol)
* **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP, 新鮮冷凍血漿)**: Contains all coagulation factors. Dose: 15ml/kg. Used for multiple factor deficiencies (liver disease, DIC, massive hemorrhage).
* **Cryoprecipitate (冷凍沉澱品)**: Concentrated Fibrinogen, FVIII, vWF, FXIII. Used for hypofibrinogenemia (DIC) or uremic bleeding.
* **Tranexamic Acid (TXA, 氨甲環酸)**: Antifibrinolytic (binds plasminogen). Given IV/PO for mucosal bleeding, trauma, menorrhagia. *Avoid in upper urinary tract bleeding (clot retention).*
* **Desmopressin (DDAVP)**: Induces endothelial release of stored vWF and FVIII. Used in mild Hemophilia A and Type 1 vWD.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Chronic Management of Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)
* **Step 1: First-line Therapy**:
* PO Prednisolone (1 mg/kg/day) tapering over weeks.
* **Step 2: Second-line Therapy (Steroid-dependent / Relapsing)**:
* **TPO-Receptor Agonists (TPO-RAs)**: Eltrombopag (PO) or Romiplostim (SC) to stimulate megakaryocytes. *First choice in HK HA due to efficacy.*
* **Rituximab**: Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (depletes B-cells producing autoantibodies).
* **Step 3: Surgical Option**:
* **Splenectomy**: High cure rate but lifelong sepsis risk (requires pneumococcal, meningococcal, Hib vaccines 2 weeks prior).

### Chronic Management of Hemophilia A and B
* **Prophylactic Factor Replacement**: Routine IV infusions (2-3x/week) to maintain trough levels >1% (converts severe phenotype to moderate).
* **Non-Factor Prophylaxis (Hemophilia A)**:
* **Emicizumab**: Bispecific monoclonal antibody mimicking FVIII (bridges FIXa and FX). Administered SubQ every 1-4 weeks. Highly effective, even with inhibitors.
* **Gene Therapy**: Emerging therapies (Valoctocogene roxaparvovec for A, Etranacogene dezaparvovec for B) for single-dose cure.

### Chronic Management of Von Willebrand Disease (vWD)
* **Type 1 (Quantitative defect)**: DDAVP (nasal spray/IV) prior to minor procedures.
* **Type 2 & 3 (Qualitative / Severe defect)**: vWF/FVIII concentrates (e.g., Haemate P).
* **Menorrhagia control**: Oral Contraceptive Pills (OCPs), Levonorgestrel IUD (Mirena), or PO Tranexamic Acid.

### Lifestyle Modifications for Bleeding Disorders
* Avoid NSAIDs and Aspirin (impair platelet function). Use Paracetamol for pain.
* Avoid intramuscular (IM) injections; use Subcutaneous (SC) where possible.
* Maintain dental hygiene to prevent invasive dental procedures.
* Avoid high-impact contact sports.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification in DIC (ISTH Scoring System)
| Parameter | Value | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Platelet Count (x 10^9/L)** | >100 / 50-100 / <50 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| **D-Dimer / FDP** | No ↑ / Mod ↑ / Strong ↑ | 0 / 2 / 3 |
| **Prolonged PT (seconds)** | <3 / 3-6 / >6 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| **Fibrinogen (g/L)** | >1.0 / <1.0 | 0 / 1 |
* **Interpretation**: Score ≥ 5 compatible with overt DIC; Score < 5 suggestive of non-overt DIC.

### Risk Stratification in TTP (PLASMIC Score)
| Parameter | Point (if present) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **P**latelet count < 30 x 10^9/L | 1 |
| Hemolysis (Retics, LDH, Ind. Bilirubin) | 1 |
| **A**bsence of active cancer | 1 |
| **S**tem-cell/solid-organ transplant absence | 1 |
| **M**CV < 90 fL | 1 |
| **I**NR < 1.5 | 1 |
| **C**reatinine < 176 µmol/L (2.0 mg/dL) | 1 |
* **Interpretation**: Score 6-7 = High risk (90% predictive of ADAMTS13 <10%). Initiate PLEX immediately without waiting for lab confirmation.

### Severity Classification of Hemophilia (Based on Factor Level)
* **Severe**: <1% of normal (Frequent spontaneous bleeds, severe hemarthrosis).
* **Moderate**: 1-5% of normal (Bleeding with minor trauma, rare spontaneous bleeds).
* **Mild**: 5-40% of normal (Bleeding only with surgery or major trauma).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Hemophilia
* **Hemophilic Arthropathy**: Recurrent hemarthrosis → synovial hypertrophy, hemosiderin deposition, cartilage destruction → severe crippling osteoarthritis.
* **Inhibitor Development**: Immune system produces neutralizing IgG antibodies against exogenous Factor VIII (occurs in ~30% of severe Hemophilia A), rendering standard factor replacement ineffective.
* **Transfusion-Transmitted Infections**: Historical risk of HIV and Hepatitis C (major cohort in 1980s-90s HK HA patients; now obsolete with recombinant products).

### Complications of MAHA & Consumptive Coagulopathy
* **TTP/HUS**: Irreversible renal failure (ESRD requiring dialysis, especially HUS), permanent neurological deficits, cardiac ischemia.
* **DIC**: Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), peripheral gangrene (purpura fulminans requiring amputation), multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS).

### Adverse Effects of Bleeding Disorder Treatments
* **TPO-RAs (Eltrombopag/Romiplostim)**: Rebound thrombocytopenia upon cessation, hepatotoxicity, marrow reticulin fibrosis, ↑ risk of venous thromboembolism.
* **High-dose Steroids**: Iatrogenic Cushing's, avascular necrosis of femoral head, osteoporosis, steroid-induced diabetes.
* **Tranexamic Acid**: Seizures (at high IV doses), thromboembolism, renal ureteric obstruction (if used in hematuria).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the most feared and immediate complication of treating TTP incorrectly? Answer: Transfusing platelets in TTP fuels the microthrombi formation, leading to immediate neurological collapse and death.
* Trap: Assuming a prolonged aPTT always means a bleeding tendency. Pearl: Lupus Anticoagulant prolongs the aPTT in vitro but causes a severe prothrombotic state in vivo.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a factor deficiency from an inhibitor based on a mixing study? Answer: Mix patient plasma 1:1 with normal plasma. If aPTT normalizes, it is a factor deficiency. If aPTT remains prolonged, an inhibitor (autoantibody) is present.
* Pitfall: Giving Tranexamic Acid for massive hematuria. It can cause solid clot formation in the renal pelvis or ureters, leading to acute obstructive uropathy.
* Trap: Diagnosing ITP based solely on low platelets without checking a blood smear. Pearl: You must review the peripheral smear to rule out pseudothrombocytopenia (platelet clumping in EDTA tubes) and MAHA (schistocytes).
* Viva Question: Why do patients with severe liver cirrhosis have both a bleeding and clotting risk? Answer: Cirrhosis causes decreased synthesis of pro-coagulant factors (leading to high PT/INR) but also decreased synthesis of anti-coagulants like Protein C, Protein S, and Antithrombin, creating a fragile, rebalanced hemostatic state.
