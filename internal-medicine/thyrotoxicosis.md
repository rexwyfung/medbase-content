# Thyrotoxicosis (甲狀腺毒症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Thyrotoxicosis
* **Thyrotoxicosis (甲狀腺毒症):** Clinical syndrome of hypermetabolism resulting from ↑ circulating free T4 (fT4) and/or free T3 (fT3).
* **Hyperthyroidism (甲狀腺機能亢進):** A specific subset of thyrotoxicosis caused by ↑ endogenous synthesis and release of thyroid hormones from the thyroid gland.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* Highly prevalent in HK; affects ~1-2% of women.
* F:M ratio ≈ 5:1.
* Graves' disease peaks at 30-50 years of age.

### Primary Etiologies of Hyperthyroidism (↑ Synthesis)
* **Graves' Disease (格雷夫斯病):** Most common (70-80%). Autoimmune.
* **Toxic Multinodular Goitre (TMNG):** 15%. Common in older patients; often arises from long-standing non-toxic goitres.
* **Toxic Adenoma:** 5%. Solitary autonomous hyperfunctioning nodule.
* **TSH-secreting Pituitary Adenoma:** Rare central cause.
* **Trophoblastic Tumors:** Choriocarcinoma / hydatidiform mole (↑ hCG cross-reacts with TSH receptor).

### Primary Etiologies of Thyrotoxicosis Without Hyperthyroidism (Destruction / Exogenous)
* **Subacute Granulomatous Thyroiditis (De Quervain's):** Post-viral.
* **Postpartum Thyroiditis:** Autoimmune destruction within 1 year post-delivery.
* **Painless (Silent) Thyroiditis:** Variant of Hashimoto's.
* **Drug-Induced:** Amiodarone (Type 2 AIT), Lithium, Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors, Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors.
* **Factitious Thyrotoxicosis:** Exogenous thyroxine (e.g., weight loss attempts).
* **Struma Ovarii:** Ectopic thyroid tissue in ovarian teratoma.


## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Graves' Disease
* B-cell/T-cell mediated autoimmune disorder.
* Autoantibodies (IgG) directly stimulate the TSH receptor (**TRAb / TSI**).
* → Uncoupled receptor activation → continuous ↑ cAMP cascade.
* → Diffuse follicular cell hypertrophy, hyperplasia, and ↑ synthesis of T3/T4 independent of pituitary feedback.

### Pathophysiology of Graves' Ophthalmopathy (甲狀腺眼病)
* TRAb targets shared antigens (TSH receptors) on orbital fibroblasts and preadipocytes.
* → T-cell infiltration (Th1 cytokines: IFN-γ, TNF-α).
* → ↑ Secretion of hydrophilic glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) by fibroblasts + ↑ adipogenesis.
* → Osmotic water retention, retro-orbital edema, muscle swelling, and subsequent fibrosis → proptosis and restrictive strabismus.

### Pathophysiology of Toxic Multinodular Goitre (TMNG)
* Somatic mutations in the TSH receptor gene or Gs-alpha protein.
* → Constitutive activation of cAMP pathway in focal clusters of thyroid cells.
* → Autonomous clonal expansion forming multiple nodules that ignore negative feedback.

### Pathophysiology of Amiodarone-Induced Thyrotoxicosis (AIT)
* **Type 1 AIT:** Iodine-induced (Jod-Basedow phenomenon). Amiodarone is 37% iodine by weight. Iodine load fuels excessive hormone synthesis in pre-existing abnormal glands (TMNG or subclinical Graves').
* **Type 2 AIT:** Direct drug-induced destructive thyroiditis. Toxic effect on follicles causes transient leakage of preformed T3/T4.


## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Endocrine & Metabolic History
* Weight loss despite ↑ appetite (hyperphagia).
* Severe heat intolerance and excessive sweating.
* Polydipsia.

### Neurological & Psychiatric History
* Restlessness, irritability, anxiety, emotional lability.
* Fine tremors (especially when attempting precise tasks).
* Insomnia.

### Cardiovascular History
* Palpitations.
* Dyspnea on exertion.
* Orthopnea / ankle swelling (if progressing to heart failure).

### Gastrointestinal & Genitourinary History
* Frequent, loose bowel movements (hyperdefecation).
* Oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea in women.
* Erectile dysfunction / gynecomastia in men.

### General Physical Signs
* **Vitals:** Sinus tachycardia, Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動), widened pulse pressure (systolic HTN).
* **Skin:** Warm, moist, velvety skin; palmar erythema; premature graying of hair.
* **Neuromuscular:** Fine postural tremor (assess with paper on outstretched hands), proximal myopathy (difficulty standing from a squat), hyperreflexia.

### Neck Examination (Goitre Assessment)
* **Graves' Disease:** Diffuse, smooth, symmetrical goitre. Audible thyroid bruit / palpable thrill (due to ↑ vascularity).
* **TMNG:** Palpably nodular, asymmetrical, irregular goitre. No bruit.
* **Toxic Adenoma:** Solitary palpable nodule.
* **Subacute Thyroiditis:** Exquisitely tender, firm thyroid.

### Extrathyroidal Manifestations of Graves' Disease
* **Graves' Ophthalmopathy (TED):** Exophthalmos (proptosis), lid retraction (Dalrymple's sign), lid lag (von Graefe's sign), periorbital edema, chemosis, extraocular muscle restriction (inferior rectus most commonly involved → defect in upward gaze).
* **Pretibial Myxedema (Dermopathy):** Non-pitting, indurated, *peau d'orange* plaques on anterior shins.
* **Thyroid Acropachy:** Clubbing and periosteal new bone formation (rare).


## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Diagnostic Differentiation of Common Thyrotoxicosis Causes
| Feature | Graves' Disease | TMNG | Subacute Thyroiditis | Factitious |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Goitre Type** | Diffuse, smooth, bruit | Multinodular | Firm, very tender | Absent |
| **Eye/Skin Signs** | +ve (Exophthalmos) | -ve | -ve | -ve |
| **TRAb Status** | High +ve | -ve | -ve | -ve |
| **Thyroglobulin** | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ | **↓** |
| **Radionuclide Scan** | Diffuse homogeneous ↑ | Patchy uneven ↑ | Diffuse ↓ (Absent) | Diffuse ↓ (Absent) |
| **ESR / CRP** | Normal | Normal | **Significantly ↑** | Normal |

### Type 1 vs Type 2 Amiodarone-Induced Thyrotoxicosis (AIT)
| Feature | Type 1 AIT (Hyperthyroidism) | Type 2 AIT (Thyroiditis) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Underlying Thyroid** | Abnormal (Goitre/Graves) | Normal |
| **Pathogenesis** | Iodine load (Jod-Basedow) | Direct drug destruction |
| **Thyroid USG** | ↑ Vascularity (Doppler) | ↓ or Normal Vascularity |
| **Management** | High-dose ATD + KClO4 | High-dose Corticosteroids |


## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Bedside Investigations
* **ECG:** Mandatory to check for Atrial Fibrillation (AF) or sinus tachycardia with ischemic changes.

### Blood Investigations (Biochemistry & Immunology)
* **Thyroid Function Tests (TFTs):**
  * **Overt Thyrotoxicosis:** ↓ TSH (<0.05 mIU/L), ↑ fT4, ↑ fT3.
  * **T3 Toxicosis:** ↓ TSH, Normal fT4, ↑ fT3 (early Graves/TMNG).
  * **Subclinical Hyperthyroidism:** ↓ TSH, Normal fT4, Normal fT3.
* **Autoantibodies:**
  * **TRAb (TSH-Receptor Antibody):** Diagnostic for Graves' disease. Indicates risk of relapse and guides pregnancy counseling.
  * **TPOAb (Anti-Thyroid Peroxidase):** +ve in ~80% of Graves; non-specific (also high in Hashimoto's).
* **Inflammatory Markers:** ↑ ESR / CRP highly specific for Subacute (De Quervain's) Thyroiditis.
* **Serum Thyroglobulin (Tg):** Differentiates endogenous hyperthyroidism (↑ Tg) from factitious thyrotoxicosis (↓ Tg due to negative feedback on endogenous synthesis).

### Imaging & Nuclear Medicine Investigations
* **Radionuclide Thyroid Uptake Scan (Tc-99m or I-131):**
  * Essential to differentiate ↑ synthesis vs destruction/exogenous.
  * **Criteria:** Diffuse high uptake (Graves), Hot nodule with suppressed remainder (Toxic Adenoma), Multiple hot/cold patches (TMNG), Near-zero uptake (Thyroiditis/Factitious).
* **Doppler Ultrasound Thyroid:**
  * Use if uptake scan is contraindicated (e.g., pregnancy, amiodarone use).
  * **Criteria:** Graves shows diffuse enlargement, hypoechogenicity, and grossly ↑ vascularity ("thyroid inferno"). Thyroiditis shows ↓ vascularity.


## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Thyroid Storm (甲狀腺風暴) Resuscitation Protocol
* **Definition:** Life-threatening hypermetabolic crisis triggered by stress (infection, surgery, trauma, abrupt withdrawal of ATDs) in a thyrotoxic patient.
* **Step 1: Peripheral Beta-Blockade (Immediate)**
  * **Propranolol** (PO/IV): Controls HR and inhibits peripheral T4 → T3 conversion.
  * Esmolol IV infusion if cardiovascular collapse/asthma.
* **Step 2: Block New Synthesis (Thionamides)**
  * **Propylthiouracil (PTU)** preferred acutely: Blocks synthesis AND blocks peripheral T4 → T3 conversion. High dose (e.g., 200mg Q4H).
  * Alternative: Carbimazole (rectal route possible if intubated).
* **Step 3: Block Hormone Release (Iodine)**
  * **Lugol's Iodine** or SSKI PO.
  * *CRITICAL:* Must be given **at least 1 hour AFTER** thionamides. Giving it before fuels new synthesis. Given after thionamides, it utilizes the Wolff-Chaikoff effect to arrest preformed hormone release.
* **Step 4: Corticosteroids (Inhibit Conversion & Autoimmunity)**
  * **IV Hydrocortisone**: Treats relative adrenal insufficiency, ↓ T4 → T3 conversion, and modulates autoimmune response.
* **Step 5: Supportive & Precipitant Control**
  * Aggressive cooling (paracetamol, cooling blankets). *Avoid aspirin* (displaces T4 from TBG, worsening crisis).
  * IV fluids for volume resuscitation.
  * Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics if infection suspected.
  * Cholestyramine (binds enterophepatic T4).

### Acute Management of Thyrotoxic Atrial Fibrillation
* Rate control primarily with Beta-blockers (Propranolol / Atenolol).
* Avoid Amiodarone (contains iodine, worsens thyrotoxicosis).
* Anticoagulation (LMWH or NOAC/Warfarin) based on CHA2DS2-VASc, regardless of whether euthyroid state is achieved yet.


## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### 1st Line: Antithyroid Drugs (ATD - 抗甲狀腺藥物)
* **Carbimazole (CBZ):** 1st-line agent in HK. Prodrug of methimazole. Dosed once daily.
* **Propylthiouracil (PTU):** 2nd-line. Preferred ONLY in: 1st trimester of pregnancy (CBZ teratogenic), thyroid storm, or minor allergy to CBZ.
* **Titration Regimen:** Start high dose (CBZ 15-30mg/day) → check TFTs every 4-6 weeks → gradually taper to maintenance dose (5-10mg/day) for 12-18 months.
* **Block and Replace Regimen:** High-dose CBZ continuously (to completely block thyroid) + Levothyroxine (to maintain euthyroidism). Contraindicated in pregnancy.

### ATD Adverse Effects & Monitoring Protocol
* **Agranulocytosis:** (<0.5%). Sudden onset fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers.
  * *Protocol:* Counsel patient to stop drug immediately and attend A&E for Urgent Complete Blood Picture (CBP).
* **Hepatotoxicity:** PTU causes fulminant hepatic necrosis. CBZ causes cholestatic jaundice.
* **Minor:** Rash, pruritus, arthralgia (can often switch ATDs or treat with antihistamines).

### 2nd Line: Radioactive Iodine (RAI / I-131)
* **Indications:** Definitive treatment of choice for TMNG / Toxic Adenoma. Relapsed Graves' post-ATD course. Patients intolerant to ATDs.
* **Contraindications:** Pregnancy, breastfeeding (delay pregnancy for ≥6 months post-RAI), active moderate/severe Graves' Ophthalmopathy (RAI worsens eye disease).
* **Pre-treatment:** Stop ATDs 3-7 days before RAI to allow I-131 uptake.
* **Complication:** Permanent hypothyroidism (requires lifelong thyroxine replacement).

### 3rd Line: Surgical Management (Total / Subtotal Thyroidectomy)
* **Indications:** Massive goitre with compressive symptoms (stridor, dysphagia), suspected concurrent malignancy, severe active ophthalmopathy (avoiding RAI), pregnant women uncontrolled on PTU/CBZ (operate in 2nd trimester).
* **Pre-operative Preparation:**
  * Must render patient euthyroid with ATDs for 6-8 weeks prior to prevent intraoperative thyroid storm.
  * Give Lugol's iodine for 10-14 days pre-op to ↓ thyroid vascularity and blood loss.

### Subclinical Hyperthyroidism Management
* **Definition:** ↓ TSH, Normal fT4/fT3.
* **Treat if:** Age >65, osteoporosis, cardiac disease/AF, or TSH <0.1 mIU/L with symptoms.

  * **≥ 45:** Highly likely thyroid storm (Initiate immediate aggressive Rx).


## 9. Multi-System Complications

### Cardiovascular Complications
* **High-Output Cardiac Failure:** Due to ↑ resting HR, ↑ myocardial contractility, and ↓ peripheral vascular resistance.
* **Atrial Fibrillation:** Risk of ischemic stroke and systemic thromboembolism.
* **Thyrotoxic Cardiomyopathy:** Reversible LV dysfunction.

### Musculoskeletal Complications
* **Thyrotoxic Periodic Paralysis:** Predominantly in Asian males. Triad of acute hypokalemia, weakness (paraparesis), and thyrotoxicosis. Shifts K+ intracellularly.
* **Secondary Osteoporosis:** Accelerated bone turnover (↑ osteoclast activity) → osteopenia and fragility fractures.

### Ophthalmic Complications (Graves' only)
* **Dysthyroid Optic Neuropathy (DON):** Compression of the optic nerve at the orbital apex → loss of color vision, ↓ visual acuity (Medical Emergency → IV Methylprednisolone).
* **Corneal Ulceration:** Due to extreme exophthalmos and inability to close eyelids (lagophthalmos).

### Endocrine / Metabolic Complications
* **Glycemic Dysregulation:** Worsens pre-existing diabetes due to ↑ hepatic gluconeogenesis and rapid enteral glucose absorption.


## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you advise a newly diagnosed Graves' patient starting Carbimazole? Answer: Emphasize the risk of agranulocytosis. Tell them: "If you develop a sudden fever, sore throat, or mouth ulcers, stop the medication immediately and go to the A&E for a white cell count check."
* Trap: Confusing the Jod-Basedow effect with the Wolff-Chaikoff effect. Pearl: Jod-Basedow is hyperthyroidism *caused* by an iodine load (fueling the fire). Wolff-Chaikoff is the transient *inhibition* of thyroid hormone release by massive doses of iodine (putting a lid on the fire, used therapeutically in thyroid storm).
* Pitfall: Prescribing Lugol's Iodine at the same time as PTU in a thyroid storm. If iodine is given before thionamides have blocked synthesis, the gland will use the iodine to synthesize massive amounts of new hormone, exacerbating the storm.
* Viva Question: Which antithyroid drug is preferred in pregnancy? Answer: Propylthiouracil (PTU) in the 1st trimester to avoid Carbimazole-associated embryopathy (aplasia cutis, choanal atresia). Switch back to Carbimazole in the 2nd/3rd trimester to minimize PTU-induced hepatotoxicity risk.
* Pitfall: Treating thyrotoxic atrial fibrillation with Aspirin for stroke prevention. Aspirin displaces T3/T4 from Thyroxine-Binding Globulin (TBG), artificially spiking free hormone levels.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with proptosis and high T4 definitely needs Radioactive Iodine. Pearl: RAI can severely exacerbate active Graves' Ophthalmopathy. If mild, give prophylactic prednisolone with RAI; if moderate-to-severe, RAI is contraindicated.
