# Skin Cancer (皮膚癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology
### Definition of Skin Cancers
* **Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC) (基底細胞癌):** Slow-growing, locally destructive malignant tumor arising from epidermal basal cells.
* **Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) (鱗狀細胞癌):** Malignant tumor of keratinizing epidermal cells with metastatic potential.
* **Melanoma (黑色素瘤):** Highly malignant neoplasm of melanocytes (neural crest origin).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Incidence:** Top 10 common cancers in HK; incidence steadily ↑.
* **Distribution:** BCC most common (~60%), followed by SCC (~30%), Melanoma (<10%).
* **Mortality:** Melanoma accounts for >75% of skin cancer deaths.
* **Asian Phenotype:** **Acral Lentiginous Melanoma (肢端黑色素瘤)** is the most common melanoma subtype in HK Chinese (unlike Caucasians where superficial spreading is most common).

### Etiologies & Risk Factors
* **Environmental:** UV radiation (UVA & UVB) → DNA damage; severe childhood sunburns (Melanoma/BCC); cumulative lifetime sun exposure (SCC).
* **Host Factors:** Fitzpatrick skin types I/II (fair skin, red hair), multiple atypical nevi, +ve family history.
* **Immunosuppression:** Solid organ transplant recipients (e.g., renal transplant) → SCC risk ↑ 65-250x.
* **Viral:** High-risk HPV (16, 18) linked to some anogenital and periungual SCCs.
* **Chemical/Physical:** Chronic arsenic exposure, prior ionizing radiotherapy, chronic scars/ulcers (Marjolin's ulcer).
* **Genodermatoses:** Xeroderma Pigmentosum, Gorlin Syndrome (Nevoid BCC syndrome), Albinism.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms
### Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC) Pathophysiology
* **Origin:** Basal layer of epidermis / outer root sheath of hair follicle.
* **Mutation:** **PTCH1** gene mutation (tumor suppressor) → Unregulated activation of **Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) signaling pathway** → continuous cell proliferation.
* **Behavior:** Highly stroma-dependent → practically never metastasizes via blood/lymph, but invades local tissues extensively (bone, cartilage, nerves).

### Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) & Precursors
* **Origin:** Suprabasal epidermal keratinocytes.
* **Mutation:** UV-induced **TP53** (p53) tumor suppressor gene mutations.
* **Progression Spectrum:**
  * **Actinic Keratosis (AK) (日光性角化病):** Dysplastic keratinocytes restricted to lower epidermis (pre-malignant).
  * **Bowen's Disease (原位癌):** SCC *in situ*; full-thickness epidermal dysplasia with intact basement membrane.
  * **Invasive SCC:** Breaches basement membrane → dermal invasion → lymphatic metastasis potential.

### Melanoma Pathophysiology
* **Origin:** Melanocytes at the dermo-epidermal junction.
* **Key Mutations:** 
  * **BRAF V600E** (~50% of cutaneous melanomas) → Constitutive activation of MAPK pathway.
  * **NRAS, c-KIT** (More frequent in mucosal and acral subtypes).
* **Growth Phases:**
  * **Radial Growth Phase:** Intraepidermal horizontal spread (low metastatic potential).
  * **Vertical Growth Phase:** Dermal invasion → gains access to lymphovascular networks → high metastatic potential.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)
### History Taking for Skin Lesions
* **Evolution:** Rate of growth, changes in color/shape/size, spontaneous bleeding, pruritus, pain.
* **Exposures:** Occupational/recreational sun exposure, tanning beds, prior PUVA therapy for psoriasis, arsenic exposure.
* **Medical Hx:** Immunosuppressive drug use (calcineurin inhibitors), prior skin cancers, solid organ transplant.
* **Family Hx:** Melanoma in 1st-degree relatives.

### Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC) Clinical Signs
* **Nodular BCC (Most common):** Pearly/translucent nodule, rolled edges, surface telangiectasia, central ulceration (rodent ulcer).
* **Superficial BCC:** Erythematous, slightly scaly plaque with a thin thread-like pearly border (often on trunk).
* **Morpheaform/Sclerosing BCC:** Ill-defined, ivory-colored, scar-like waxy plaque (aggressive, deeply infiltrative).
* **Pigmented BCC:** Contains melanin, clinically mimics melanoma (dark brown/black nodule).

### Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) Clinical Signs
* **Actinic Keratosis (AK):** <1cm, rough, sandpaper-like, hyperkeratotic papules on sun-damaged skin (face, bald scalp, dorsal hands).
* **Bowen’s Disease:** Well-demarcated, persistent, erythematous scaly plaque (mimics eczema/psoriasis but does not respond to topical steroids).
* **Invasive SCC:** Indurated, fleshy, erythematous nodule with hyperkeratosis/crusting; often ulcerated with everted edges; bleeds easily on contact.

### Melanoma ABCDE Signs
* **A**symmetry: One half does not match the other.
* **B**order: Irregular, notched, or blurred edges.
* **C**olor: Variegated (shades of brown, black, blue, red, white regression zones).
* **D**iameter: >6mm (size of a pencil eraser).
* **E**volution: Any morphological change over time (most sensitive sign).

### Melanoma Subtype-Specific Signs
* **Superficial Spreading:** Slowly enlarging flat/slightly raised irregular plaque.
* **Nodular:** Rapidly growing, blue-black or amelanotic (flesh-colored) dome-shaped nodule.
* **Acral Lentiginous:** Irregular pigmented macule/patch on palms, soles, or subungual (Hutchinson's sign: pigment spreading to proximal nail fold).
* **Lentigo Maligna:** Chronically enlarging, flat, freckle-like patch on sun-damaged face of elderly.

### Regional Lymph Node Assessment
* **Mandatory Palpation:** Cervical, axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal, and popliteal lymphadenopathy (hard, fixed nodes indicate regional mets).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features
### Differentials for Pigmented Lesions (Melanoma Suspects)
* **Seborrheic Keratosis:** "Stuck-on" appearance, warty/waxy surface, horn cysts (keratin plugs).
* **Pigmented BCC:** Pearly border, telangiectasia.
* **Blue Nevus:** Symmetrical, uniform slate-blue nodule (Tyndall effect).
* **Spitz Nevus:** Rapidly growing pink/red nodule in children/young adults (can mimic amelanotic melanoma).
* **Talon Noir (Black Heel):** Calcaneal petechiae/intraepidermal hemorrhage (sports-related, paring reveals blood).

### Differentials for Non-Melanoma Skin Cancers
* **Keratoacanthoma:** Symmetrical dome-shaped nodule with central keratinous crater; rapid growth (weeks) followed by spontaneous involution (months).
* **Sebaceous Hyperplasia:** Yellowish papules with central umbilication (mimics BCC, but softer, multiple).
* **Discoid Lupus Erythematosus (DLE):** Erythematous plaques with follicular plugging, scarring, alopecia (mimics SCC/AK).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)
### Bedside Investigations: Dermoscopy (Skin Surface Microscopy)
| Lesion Type | Characteristic Dermoscopic Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **BCC** | Arborizing vessels, maple-leaf-like areas, blue-gray ovoid nests, ulceration. |
| **SCC / AK** | Strawberry pattern (AK), keratin mass, hairpin/dotted vessels, targetoid hair follicles. |
| **Melanoma** | Atypical pigment network, irregular dots/globules, blue-white veil (regression), atypical vascular patterns. |
| **Benign Nevi** | Typical uniform pigment network, homogeneous pattern. |
| **Seborrheic Keratosis** | Milia-like cysts, comedo-like openings, brain-like/cerebriform pattern. |

### Blood Tests & Biomarkers
* **Routine:** CBC, LFT, RFT (baseline prior to systemic therapies).
* **Serum LDH:** Prognostic tumor marker in Stage IV Melanoma (elevated LDH = worse prognosis).

### Histopathological Investigations (Biopsy)
* **Excisional Biopsy (Gold Standard for Melanoma):** Complete removal with 2mm clinical margins down to deep subcutaneous fat. (DO NOT perform punch/incisional biopsy if melanoma suspected, as it prevents accurate Breslow thickness measurement).
* **Punch Biopsy:** For diagnosing inflammatory dermatoses or large un-resectable tumors.
* **Shave Biopsy:** Adequate for superficial BCC/AK, contraindicated for melanoma/deep tumors.
* **Immunohistochemistry (IHC):** 
  * Melanoma: S100, HMB-45, Melan-A, SOX10 (+ve).
  * SCC: Cytokeratin (+ve).
* **Molecular Testing:** **BRAF V600E** PCR/sequencing (mandatory for all unresectable Stage III/IV melanomas to guide targeted therapy).

### Imaging Studies (Staging)
* **Ultrasound Regional Lymph Nodes:** Detects non-palpable regional metastases (followed by US-guided FNA if suspicious).
* **PET-CT / CT Brain/Thorax/Abdo/Pelvis:** For staging high-risk SCC (e.g., deep invasion, recurrent) and Stage III/IV Melanoma to identify distant mets.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)
### Acute Management of Fungating / Bleeding Tumors
* **Hemostasis:** Direct sustained pressure → topical adrenaline (1:1000) soaked gauze → application of silver nitrate sticks or Monsel's solution (ferric subsulfate).
* **Infection Control:** Swab for C&S if discharging/malodorous; commence oral antibiotics (e.g., Amoxicillin-Clavulanate) targeting skin flora/anaerobes.
* **Analgesia:** WHO pain ladder; palliative radiotherapy for intractable pain/bleeding in end-stage disease.

### Immunotherapy-Related Adverse Events (irAE) Emergencies
* **Context:** Metastatic melanoma patients on PD-1 (Pembrolizumab) or CTLA-4 (Ipilimumab) inhibitors presenting with acute severe colitis, pneumonitis, or myocarditis.
* **Management:** Immediate cessation of immunotherapy → IV Methylprednisolone (1-2 mg/kg/day) → If refractory after 48-72h, escalate to Infliximab (for colitis) or Mycophenolate Mofetil (for pneumonitis/hepatitis).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)
### Management Algorithm for Actinic Keratosis (AK) & Bowen's Disease
* **Lesion-Targeted Therapy (Isolated lesions):** Cryotherapy (Liquid Nitrogen), Curettage and Electrodessication (C&E).
* **Field-Directed Therapy (Multiple lesions/Field cancerization):**
  * **Topical 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) 5% cream:** 1-2x daily for 2-4 weeks.
  * **Topical Imiquimod 5% cream:** TLR7 agonist; applied 2-3x/week for 4-16 weeks.
  * **Photodynamic Therapy (PDT):** Topical aminolevulinic acid (ALA) + red/blue light irradiation.

### Pharmacological Therapy for Advanced/Unresectable BCC & SCC
* **BCC (Targeted Therapy):** Hedgehog pathway inhibitors (**Vismodegib, Sonidegib**) for locally advanced or metastatic BCC not amenable to surgery/radiotherapy.
* **SCC (Immunotherapy):** Anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibodies (**Cemiplimab, Pembrolizumab**) for metastatic or locally advanced SCC.

### Pharmacological Therapy for Advanced Melanoma (Stage III/IV)
* **Adjuvant Therapy (Post-resection of Stage III):** Nivolumab or Pembrolizumab (Anti-PD-1) to ↓ recurrence risk.
* **Targeted Therapy (BRAF V600 Mutated):** Combination BRAF + MEK inhibitors (e.g., **Dabrafenib + Trametinib** or **Vemurafenib + Cobimetinib**). Rapid tumor shrinkage but high relapse rate.
* **Immunotherapy (BRAF Wild-Type or Mutated):** Anti-PD-1 (**Nivolumab/Pembrolizumab**) alone OR combined with Anti-CTLA-4 (**Ipilimumab**). Durable response but high toxicity.

### Lifestyle Modifications & Surveillance
* **Photoprotection:** Broad-spectrum sunscreen (UVA/UVB, SPF ≥30), UPF 50+ clothing, wide-brimmed hats, strict avoidance of sun between 10 AM - 4 PM.
* **Dermatologic Surveillance:** Total Body Skin Examination (TBSE) and lymph node palpation every 3-6 months for patients with a history of melanoma or multiple NMSCs.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis
### Melanoma Risk Stratification: Breslow Thickness vs. Clark Level
| Parameter | Description | Clinical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Breslow Thickness** | Absolute depth measured in mm from the granular layer to the deepest tumor cell. | **Most important independent prognostic factor.** Determines surgical margins and indication for Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB). |
| **Clark Level** | Anatomic level of invasion. (I: In situ, II: Papillary dermis, III: Fills papillary dermis, IV: Reticular dermis, V: Subcutaneous fat). | Replaced by Breslow in AJCC guidelines. Historically used to predict metastatic risk. |

### AJCC Melanoma Staging System (Simplified)
* **Stage I:** Localized disease, Breslow ≤2.0mm without ulceration, or ≤1.0mm with ulceration. (Excellent prognosis, >95% 5YSR).
* **Stage II:** Localized disease, Breslow >1.0mm with ulceration, or >2.0mm regardless of ulceration. (Moderate prognosis).
* **Stage III:** Regional lymph node metastasis (Microscopic +ve SLNB or macroscopic palpable nodes), or in-transit/satellite metastases.
* **Stage IV:** Distant metastasis (Lung, liver, brain, bone). (Poor prognosis, heavily reliant on systemic therapy).

### High-Risk Features in Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)
* **Tumor Characteristics:** Size >2cm, Breslow depth >2mm, Clark level IV/V, poor differentiation.
* **Clinical Setting:** Immunosuppressed patient, arising in a chronic scar/burn (Marjolin's ulcer).
* **Anatomic Location:** Ear, lip, genitalia, mucosal surfaces (higher recurrence and metastatic rates).
* **Histology:** Perineural invasion, lymphovascular invasion.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)
### Complications of Local Invasion & Metastasis
* **Perineural Spread (esp. SCC):** Tumor tracking along nerves → Trigeminal (CN V) or Facial (CN VII) nerve palsies, intractable dysesthesia/pain.
* **Melanoma Distant Metastasis:**
  * **Brain:** Hemorrhagic metastases, seizures, raised ICP (major cause of mortality).
  * **Gastrointestinal:** Intussusception, acute GI bleeding, obstruction.
  * **Lungs/Liver:** Malignant effusions, hepatic failure.

### Complications of Pharmacotherapy (Targeted Therapies & Immunotherapy)
* **Hedgehog Inhibitors (Vismodegib):** Severe muscle spasms, alopecia, dysgeusia (loss of taste), weight loss (often leads to drug discontinuation).
* **BRAF Inhibitors (Vemurafenib/Dabrafenib):** Paradoxical MAPK pathway activation in BRAF wild-type cells → Induction of secondary SCCs and keratoacanthomas, severe photosensitivity, pyrexia.
* **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (Anti-PD-1/CTLA-4):** Widespread autoimmune toxicity (irAEs) → Vitiligo (actually a good prognostic sign in melanoma), thyroiditis, hypophysitis, hepatitis, fatal myocarditis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)
* Viva Question: What is the most common subtype of melanoma in the local Hong Kong Chinese population? Answer: Acral Lentiginous Melanoma.
* Viva Question: A patient presents with a suspicious black skin lesion. Why is a punch or shave biopsy contraindicated? Answer: It transects the tumor and prevents accurate histological measurement of Breslow thickness, which dictates staging and subsequent surgical margins.
* Trap: Assuming a symmetric, dome-shaped skin nodule growing rapidly over 3 weeks is an aggressive malignancy. Pearl: Keratoacanthoma grows extremely rapidly but is generally considered a benign or low-grade SCC variant that often spontaneously involutes.
* Pitfall: Reassuring a patient with a subungual pigmented streak without thorough evaluation. Always consider Acral Lentiginous Melanoma (Hutchinson's sign) and biopsy if it involves the proximal nail fold or is widening, especially in Asian patients.
* Trap: Treating a non-healing, persistent "eczema" or "psoriasis" patch on a sun-exposed area with repeated courses of topical steroids. Pearl: Always biopsy persistent solitary scaly plaques to rule out Bowen’s disease (SCC in situ).
* Pitfall: Examining only the primary skin lesion during a skin cancer check. Always systematically palpate regional lymph node basins (cervical, axillary, inguinal) to rule out Stage III disease.
