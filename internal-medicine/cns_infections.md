# CNS Infections (中樞神經系統感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definitions of CNS Infections
* **Meningitis (腦膜炎):** Inflammation of the meninges (pia, arachnoid) and subarachnoid space (SAS) CSF.
* **Encephalitis (腦炎):** Inflammation of the brain parenchyma, resulting in neuronal dysfunction (altered mentation, seizures).
* **Meningoencephalitis (腦膜腦炎):** Combined meningeal and parenchymal inflammation.
* **Brain Abscess (腦膿腫):** Focal, encapsulated purulent infection within brain parenchyma.

### Bacterial Etiologies by Age Group (Hong Kong Epidemiology)
* **< 1 month:** *Streptococcus agalactiae* (Group B Strep), *E. coli*, *Listeria monocytogenes*.
* **1 month - 2 years:** *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (↓ incidence post-Hib vaccine in HK).
* **2 - 50 years:** *S. pneumoniae* (most common adult cause), *N. meningitidis*.
* **> 50 years / Immunocompromised:** *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*, *L. monocytogenes*, Gram-negative bacilli.
* **Post-neurosurgery / Trauma:** *Staphylococcus aureus*, Coagulase-negative staphylococci, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.

### Viral, Tuberculous & Fungal Etiologies
* **Viral:** Herpes Simplex Virus 1 (HSV-1, leading cause of fatal sporadic encephalitis), Enteroviruses (EV71 common in HK pediatric outbreaks), Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV), Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV).
* **Tuberculous:** *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (endemic in HK, often subacute presentation with profound basal meningeal inflammation).
* **Fungal:** *Cryptococcus neoformans* (primarily in HIV/AIDS, CD4 <100, or transplant patients).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Bacterial Meningitis
* **Nasopharyngeal Colonization & Invasion:** Pathogens attach to mucosal epithelium via pili/fimbriae → IgA proteases evade mucosal immunity → transmigration into bloodstream.
* **Meningeal Invasion:** High-grade bacteremia → pathogens cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) via choroid plexus endothelium into CSF.
* **Subarachnoid Space Multiplication:** CSF lacks complement and immunoglobulins → unchecked bacterial replication.
* **Inflammatory Cascade:** Bacterial lysis (via autolysis or antibiotics) releases peptidoglycan/teichoic acid (Gram +ve) or endotoxin/LPS (Gram -ve).
* **Cytokine Release:** Macrophages release TNF-α and IL-1 → ↑ BBB permeability (leaky tight junctions).

### Mechanisms of Cerebral Edema & Elevated ICP
* **Vasogenic Edema:** BBB disruption allows intravascular fluid/proteins to leak into extracellular space.
* **Cytotoxic Edema:** Bacterial toxins and hypoxia-ischemia impair neuronal/glial Na+/K+-ATPase pumps → intracellular swelling.
* **Interstitial Edema:** Exudate obstructs arachnoid granulations → ↓ CSF absorption → hydrocephalus.
* **Consequence:** ↑ Intracranial pressure (ICP) → ↓ cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP = MAP - ICP) → global cerebral ischemia and risk of herniation.

### Pathogenesis of HSV Encephalitis
* **Viral Tropism:** Retrograde transport via olfactory tract or trigeminal nerve directly to the brain.
* **Lobe Specificity:** Strong tropism for medial temporal and inferior frontal lobes → hemorrhagic necrosis and edema.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Clues in CNS Infections
* **Classic Triad:** Fever, headache, neck stiffness (note: all 3 present in <45% of adults).
* **Encephalitic Symptoms:** Altered mental status, confusion, personality changes, new-onset seizures, focal weakness, dysphasia.
* **Temporal Profile:** Acute (<24h, bacterial/viral), Subacute (days-weeks, TB/fungal/syphilis), Chronic (>4 weeks).
* **Epidemiological Risk Factors:** Recent travel, sick contacts, close-quarter living (dorms/camps → meningococcus), unprotected sex/HIV risk, raw food ingestion (*Listeria*).

### Physical Examination: Meningism & Raised ICP Signs
* **Nuchal Rigidity:** Resistance to passive neck flexion.
* **Kernig's Sign:** Resistance and pain with passive knee extension when hip is flexed at 90°.
* **Brudzinski's Sign:** Involuntary flexion of hips/knees upon passive neck flexion.
* **Fundoscopy:** Papilledema (blurring of optic disc margins, loss of venous pulsations) → signals ↑ ICP.

### Physical Examination: Systemic & Focal Neurological Signs
* **Dermatology:** Non-blanching petechial/purpuric rash (Meningococcemia).
* **Neurology:** Depressed GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale), Cranial Nerve palsies (CN III, IV, VI due to ↑ ICP; CN VIII in pneumococcal), dysphasia, hemiparesis.
* **Otic/Nasal:** Check for otitis media, mastoiditis, or CSF rhinorrhea/otorrhea (base of skull fracture).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Non-Infectious CNS Mimics
* **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):** "Thunderclap" headache, no preceding fever (though chemical meningitis may cause delayed fever/stiffness).
* **Autoimmune Encephalitis (e.g., Anti-NMDA receptor):** Prominent psychiatric features, movement disorders (orofacial dyskinesia), autonomic instability. Often paraneoplastic (ovarian teratoma).
* **Malignancy (Carcinomatous/Lymphomatous Meningitis):** Indolent course, weight loss, multiple cranial neuropathies, +ve malignant cells in CSF.

### Drug-Induced & Systemic Mimics
* **Drug-Induced Aseptic Meningitis (DIAM):** NSAIDs, IVIG, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX). Diagnosis of exclusion.
* **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome / Serotonin Syndrome:** Altered GCS and fever, but rigidity is lead-pipe/clonus rather than pure meningism; related to recent medication changes.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **Bedside:** Point-of-care glucose (compare with CSF).
* **Bloods:** CBC (leukocytosis), CRP, ESR, Procalcitonin.
* **Renal/Liver:** U&Es (monitor for SIADH), LFTs.
* **Microbiology:** Blood cultures (×2 sets) MUST be drawn before antibiotics (yield is 50-70% in bacterial meningitis).
* **Coagulation:** PT/APTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer (check for DIC if meningococcemia suspected).

### Neuroimaging: CT Brain Indications
* **Rule:** LP is contraindicated without prior CT Brain if signs of mass effect/↑ ICP are present (risk of uncal/tonsillar herniation).
* **"SHIFT" Criteria for CT before LP:**
  * **S**eizures (new onset).
  * **H**istory of CNS disease (mass lesion, stroke, focal infection).
  * **I**mmunocompromised state (HIV, immunosuppressants, transplant).
  * **F**ocal neurological deficit (including papilledema on exam).
  * **T**hinking abnormal (Altered mental status / GCS <15).
* **MRI Brain (Encephalitis/Abscess):** Investigation of choice for HSV (hyperintensity in temporal/frontal lobes on T2/FLAIR) and abscess (ring-enhancing lesion).

### Lumbar Puncture (LP) & CSF Analysis Parameters
* **Opening Pressure:** Normal <20 cmH2O. Measured in lateral decubitus position.
* **Biochemistry:** CSF protein, CSF glucose, CSF-to-serum glucose ratio.
* **Microscopy:** Cell count & differential (polymorphs vs lymphocytes).
* **Microbiology:** Gram stain, Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB) smear, India Ink (Cryptococcus). Bacterial, Mycobacterial, and Fungal cultures.
* **Molecular Diagnostics:** BioFire FilmArray Meningitis/Encephalitis (ME) Panel (multiplex PCR for 14 pathogens), HSV/VZV/Enterovirus PCR, TB PCR.
* **Immunology:** CSF Cryptococcal antigen (CrAg).

### Table: Diagnostic Interpretation of CSF Profiles

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Immediate Resuscitation & Initial Actions
* **Golden Hour:** Do NOT delay empirical antibiotics for imaging or LP. If CT is required or LP delayed, draw blood cultures and administer antibiotics immediately.
* **Airway/Breathing:** Intubation if GCS ≤8 or recurrent seizures.
* **Circulation:** IV fluid resuscitation for septic shock (vasopressors if non-responsive).
* **Infection Control:** Droplet isolation for suspected meningococcal or *H. influenzae* meningitis for the first 24 hours of appropriate Abx.

### Table: Empirical Antibiotic Therapy by Patient Profile (HK HA Formulary)
| Patient Group / Risk Factor | Empirical Antibiotic Regimen | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 month - 50 years (Normal)** | IV Ceftriaxone 2g Q12H | Covers *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis* |
| **> 50 years / Alcoholism** | IV Ceftriaxone + IV Ampicillin 2g Q4H | Adds *Listeria monocytogenes* coverage |
| **Immunocompromised** | IV Ceftriaxone + IV Ampicillin + IV Vancomycin | Adds *Listeria* and MRSA/resistant Pneumococcus |
| **Post-neurosurgery / Trauma** | IV Vancomycin + IV Ceftazidime (or Meropenem) | Covers MRSA and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* |
| **Severe Penicillin Allergy** | IV Vancomycin + IV Moxifloxacin +/- IV TMP-SMX | TMP-SMX covers *Listeria* if Ampicillin contraindicated |

### Adjunctive Corticosteroid Therapy (Dexamethasone)
* **Indication:** Proven mortality/morbidity benefit primarily in *S. pneumoniae* meningitis (↓ hearing loss and neurological sequelae).
* **Dosing:** IV Dexamethasone 10mg Q6H for 4 days.
* **Timing (CRITICAL):** Administer 15-20 minutes BEFORE, or concurrently with, the first dose of antibiotics. (Ineffective if given after Abx have caused bacterial lysis).
* **Cessation:** Stop if CSF Gram stain or culture reveals a non-pneumococcal bacterial cause (except H. influenzae in children).

### Empirical Treatment for Encephalitis & Abscess
* **Suspected Viral Encephalitis:** IV Aciclovir 10mg/kg Q8H immediately (covers HSV and VZV). Maintain aggressive IV hydration to prevent crystal nephropathy.
* **Suspected Brain Abscess:** IV Ceftriaxone + IV Metronidazole (adds strict anaerobe coverage).

### Management of Markedly Elevated ICP
* **Positioning:** Elevate head of bed to 30°, maintain neck in neutral alignment to facilitate venous drainage.
* **Osmotic Therapy:** IV Mannitol 20% or Hypertonic Saline (3%).
* **Ventilation:** Brief, targeted hyperventilation (PaCO2 30-35 mmHg) as a temporizing measure for impending herniation.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Disease-Specific Definitive Pathogen-Directed Therapy
* **Meningococcal (*N. meningitidis*):** Transition to IV Benzylpenicillin or Ceftriaxone for 7 days total.
* **Pneumococcal (*S. pneumoniae*):** IV Ceftriaxone (if susceptible) for 10-14 days.
* **Listeria:** IV Ampicillin for ≥21 days.
* **HSV Encephalitis:** IV Aciclovir for 14-21 days (repeat LP at end of therapy; ensure HSV PCR is negative before stopping).

### Tuberculous Meningitis Regimen
* **Induction Phase (2 months):** Isoniazid (INH), Rifampicin (RIF), Pyrazinamide (PZA), Ethambutol (EMB) (RIPE therapy).
* **Consolidation Phase (7-10 months):** INH and RIF. Total duration is 9-12 months.
* **Adjunctive Therapy:** Oral Prednisolone or IV Dexamethasone tapered over 6-8 weeks (strongly recommended for all TB meningitis to ↓ mortality and ↓ arachnoiditis).

### Cryptococcal Meningitis Algorithm
* **Induction Phase (2 weeks):** IV Liposomal Amphotericin B + PO Flucytosine. (Monitor K+, Mg2+, Cr, and CBC).
* **Consolidation Phase (8 weeks):** PO Fluconazole (high dose: 400-800mg/day).
* **Maintenance Phase (Secondary Prophylaxis):** PO Fluconazole 200mg/day until CD4 > 100 for ≥6 months.
* **ICP Management:** Daily therapeutic LPs or placement of lumbar drain/VP shunt if opening pressure persistently high. (DO NOT use dexamethasone in cryptococcal meningitis).

### Public Health, Contact Tracing & Prophylaxis
* **Notification:** Meningococcal, TB, and invasive Pneumococcal/Hib diseases are statutorily notifiable to the Centre for Health Protection (CHP) in Hong Kong.
* **Meningococcal Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP):** Indicated for close contacts (household, childcare, kissing, or healthcare workers intubating without PPE).
* **PEP Regimens:**
  * PO Ciprofloxacin 500mg single dose (treatment of choice in adults).
  * PO Rifampicin 600mg BD for 2 days (avoid in pregnancy/OCP users).
  * IM Ceftriaxone 250mg single dose (preferred in pregnancy).
* **Index Patient Eradication:** If the index patient was treated with Benzylpenicillin (which does not clear nasopharyngeal carriage), they must receive a dose of Ciprofloxacin or Ceftriaxone before hospital discharge.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Predictors of Poor Prognosis in Adult Bacterial Meningitis
* Advanced age (>60 years).
* Low GCS on admission (GCS <10).
* Seizures within 24 hours of presentation.
* Delay in initiation of antibiotic therapy.
* Infection caused by *S. pneumoniae*.

### Modified Medical Research Council (MRC) Staging for TB Meningitis
* **Stage I (Early):** GCS 15, no focal neurological deficits. (Good prognosis).
* **Stage II (Intermediate):** GCS 11-14, or GCS 15 with focal neurological deficits (e.g., cranial nerve palsies).
* **Stage III (Advanced):** GCS ≤10, or severe neurological deficits (e.g., dense hemiplegia, paraplegia). (High mortality/morbidity).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological Complications
* **Sensorineural Hearing Loss (Deafness):** Most common complication of *S. pneumoniae* (due to CN VIII inflammation or cochlear ossification).
* **Seizures/Epilepsy:** 15-30% of patients; structural damage may lead to chronic epilepsy.
* **Hydrocephalus:** Communicating (arachnoid granulations blocked) or non-communicating. Common in TB/Fungal.
* **Cerebrovascular Complications:** Vasculitis leading to ischemic strokes (especially in basal ganglia with TB meningitis) or venous sinus thrombosis.
* **Subdural Effusions:** More common in pediatric *H. influenzae* meningitis.

### Systemic Complications
* **Septic Shock & Multiorgan Failure:** Particularly in fulminant meningococcemia.
* **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):** Due to massive endotoxin release.
* **Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome:** Bilateral adrenal hemorrhage leading to acute adrenal insufficiency (classic in meningococcemia).
* **SIADH:** Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone secretion → euvolemic hyponatremia.

### Iatrogenic / Pharmacological Adverse Effects
* **Aciclovir:** Renal toxicity (crystalluria) → requires aggressive IV pre-hydration.
* **Aminoglycosides / Vancomycin:** Nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity (monitor serum trough levels).
* **Rifampicin:** Hepatotoxicity, orange/red discoloration of bodily fluids (tears/urine), strong CYP450 inducer (↓ efficacy of OCPs, warfarin).
* **Amphotericin B:** Severe nephrotoxicity, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, infusion-related chills/rigors ("shake and bake").

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Waiting for a CT Brain to be completed before giving antibiotics in a patient with an altered GCS and fever. Pearl: Never delay empirical antibiotics in suspected bacterial meningitis. Draw blood cultures and push IV antibiotics immediately; then send for CT.
* Viva Question: What is the specific timing for administering Dexamethasone in bacterial meningitis, and why? Answer: It must be given 15-20 minutes BEFORE or exactly WITH the first dose of antibiotics. It attenuates the severe inflammatory response triggered by the antibiotic-induced lysis of bacterial cell walls, specifically reducing sensorineural hearing loss in *S. pneumoniae*.
* Pitfall: Assuming a lymphocyte-predominant CSF solely indicates viral encephalitis. **Explanation of how to avoid:** A lymphocytic CSF with a LOW glucose level (<0.4 ratio) points to Tuberculous, Fungal, or Listeria meningitis, not viral (which classically has normal glucose).
* Viva Question: A patient with HIV presents with headache. India ink preparation of CSF is positive. What is the diagnosis and standard induction therapy? Answer: Cryptococcal meningitis. Induction therapy is IV Liposomal Amphotericin B + PO Flucytosine for 2 weeks.
* Trap: Treating a patient with Meningococcal meningitis with 7 days of IV Benzylpenicillin and discharging them without further action. Pearl: Benzylpenicillin does NOT eradicate nasopharyngeal carriage. The index patient must receive a dose of PO Ciprofloxacin or IM Ceftriaxone prior to discharge to prevent transmission to the community.
* Viva Question: What are the characteristic MRI brain findings in Herpes Simplex Encephalitis? Answer: Hyperintensity on T2 and FLAIR sequences localized asymmetrically to the medial temporal and inferior frontal lobes.
