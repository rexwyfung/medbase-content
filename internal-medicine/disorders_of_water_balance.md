# Disorders of Water Balance (水代謝紊亂)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Physiology of Normal Osmoregulation (滲透壓調節)
* **Receptors:** Hypothalamic osmoreceptors sense ↑ plasma osmolality (P_osm); carotid/aortic baroreceptors sense ↓ effective arterial blood volume (EABV).
* **Effector:** Antidiuretic Hormone [ADH/Arginine Vasopressin (精氨酸血管加壓素)] released from posterior pituitary.
* **Action:** Binds V2 receptors in renal collecting duct → ↑ Aquaporin-2 insertion → ↑ water reabsorption.

### Clinical Definition and Etiologies of SIADH (抗利尿激素分泌失調綜合症)
* **Definition:** Non-physiologic excess ADH release despite ↓ P_osm and normal/expanded EABV.
* **Malignancy:** Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC) [most common paraneoplastic], GI/GU tumours.
* **CNS Disorders:** Stroke, Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH), meningitis, encephalitis, head trauma.
* **Pulmonary Disease:** Tuberculosis (TB) [highly relevant in HK], severe pneumonia.
* **Drugs:** SSRIs, Carbamazepine, Sodium Valproate, PPIs, Amiodarone.

### Clinical Definition and Etiologies of Diabetes Insipidus (尿崩症)
* **Definition:** Passage of >3L/day of dilute urine (polyuria) due to ADH deficiency or resistance.
* **Central DI (ADH Deficiency):** Idiopathic (autoimmune), pituitary macroadenoma/craniopharyngioma, post-hypophysectomy, infiltrative (sarcoidosis, histiocytosis), trauma.
* **Nephrogenic DI (ADH Resistance):** 
* **Acquired:** Lithium toxicity, Hypercalcemia, Hypokalemia, chronic kidney disease.
* **Hereditary:** X-linked AVPR2 (V2 receptor) mutation, autosomal AQP2 (Aquaporin-2) mutation.

### Clinical Definition and Etiologies of Cerebral Salt Wasting (腦性耗鹽症候群)
* **Definition:** Primary renal Na+ wasting resulting in hypovolemia and hyponatremia.
* **Etiologies:** SAH, severe traumatic brain injury, post-neurosurgery.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of SIADH
* **Initial insult:** ↑ ADH → V2 receptor activation → ↑ Aquaporin-2 in collecting duct → ↑ free water reabsorption.
* **Dilution & Expansion:** → Dilutional hyponatremia + mild EABV expansion.
* **Secondary Natriuresis:** EABV expansion → ↑ ANP/BNP release & ↓ RAAS → ↑ renal Na+ excretion (urine Na+ >30 mmol/L).
* **Net Result:** Restores euvolemia but worsens/maintains hyponatremia (euvolemic hyponatremia).

### Pathophysiology of Central Diabetes Insipidus
* **Mechanism:** Destruction of magnocellular neurons in supraoptic/paraventricular nuclei OR damage to pituitary stalk → absent ADH.
* **Consequence:** Collecting duct impermeable to water → massive water diuresis → ↑ P_osm (hypernatremia if thirst mechanism impaired/no water access).

### Pathophysiology of Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus
* **Mechanism:** Receptor/channel defect (e.g., Lithium enters via ENaC, interferes with cAMP cascade) → collecting duct completely unresponsive to endogenous/exogenous ADH.
* **Consequence:** Identical water diuresis as Central DI, but with ↑ endogenous ADH levels.

### Pathophysiology of Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW)
* **Mechanism:** Brain injury → central sympathetic surge OR massive release of brain natriuretic peptide (BNP).
* **Consequence:** Primary suppression of proximal tubular Na+ reabsorption → massive natriuresis → obligate water loss → **hypovolemic** hyponatremia.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History in Polyuria Syndromes (Diabetes Insipidus vs Polydipsia)
* **Onset:** Sudden onset favours Central DI; insidious onset favours Nephrogenic DI or Primary Polydipsia.
* **Fluid Preference:** Strong craving for ice-cold water classically highly specific for Central DI.
* **Nocturia:** Severe in DI (disturbs sleep); usually absent in Primary Polydipsia (stops drinking at night).
* **Psychiatric Hx:** Schizophrenia/affective disorders suggest Primary Polydipsia.
* **Medication Hx:** Elicit Lithium use, recent contrast, steroids.

### History in Hyponatremic Syndromes (SIADH & CSW)
* **Neurological Sx (dependent on rate of Na+ drop):** Headache, nausea, vomiting, lethargy, muscle cramps → seizures, coma, respiratory arrest.
* **Localizing Sx:** Chronic cough, hemoptysis, weight loss (Lung Ca/TB).
* **Drug Hx:** Elicit recent start of SSRI or antiepileptics.

### Volume Status Examination in Water Balance Disorders
* **SIADH:** Clinically **Euvolemic** (moist mucous membranes, normal skin turgor, no edema, normal JVP, normal BP).
* **CSW:** Clinically **Hypovolemic** (dry mucous membranes, ↓ skin turgor, flat JVP, resting tachycardia, postural hypotension).
* **Diabetes Insipidus:** Usually Euvolemic (intact thirst mechanism compensates for loss); Hypovolemic/shock ONLY if obtunded or denied water.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Causes of Polyuria
* **Solute Diuresis:** Diabetes Mellitus (glycosuria), resolving acute tubular necrosis (urea), post-obstructive diuresis.
* **Water Diuresis:** Central DI, Nephrogenic DI, Primary Polydipsia.

### Differentiating Euvolemic Hyponatremia
* **SIADH:** Diagnosis of exclusion.
* **Hypothyroidism:** Severe myxedema impairs free water excretion.
* **Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency:** Glucocorticoid deficiency removes negative feedback on ADH release.

### Differentiating SIADH vs Cerebral Salt Wasting
| Feature | SIADH | Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Volume Status** | Euvolemic | Hypovolemic |
| **Body Weight** | Normal / Slight ↑ | ↓ (due to fluid loss) |
| **Urine Output** | Normal / ↓ | Polyuria |
| **Thirst** | Normal | ↑ |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Basic Investigations for Polyuria & Hyponatremia
* **Bedside:** Fluid balance chart, strict daily weights.
* **Bloods:** Paired Serum Osmolality & Na+, U&Es (K+, Ca2+ for nephrogenic DI), TFTs (exclude hypothyroidism), Short Synacthen Test (exclude Addison's).
* **Urine:** Paired Urine Osmolality & Na+.

### Diagnostic Criteria for SIADH (Bartter-Schwartz Criteria)
| Criteria Category | Required Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Serum/Plasma** | True hypotonic hyponatremia: P_osm <275 mOsm/kg |
| **Urine** | Inappropriately concentrated: U_osm >100 mOsm/kg |
| **Renal Na+ Handling** | Elevated urine Na+: >30 mmol/L (reflects natriuresis) |
| **Clinical Status** | Clinically euvolemic |
| **Exclusions** | Normal thyroid, adrenal, and renal function; no recent diuretic use |

### Water Deprivation Test Protocol and Interpretation
* **Protocol Phase 1 (Deprivation):** Withhold fluids. Measure U_osm hourly. Stop if weight drops >3% or U_osm plateaus.
* **Protocol Phase 2 (Desmopressin):** Administer IM/SC DDAVP. Measure U_osm after 1-2 hours.

| Diagnosis | Baseline U_osm | Post-Deprivation U_osm | Post-DDAVP U_osm |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | High | >600 mOsm/kg | No significant change |
| **Primary Polydipsia** | Low | >600 mOsm/kg | No significant change |
| **Central DI** | Low (<300) | Remains <300 mOsm/kg | **↑ >50%** (Often >600) |
| **Nephrogenic DI** | Low (<300) | Remains <300 mOsm/kg | No significant change (<10% ↑) |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Cerebral Salt Wasting
* **Primary Goal:** Restore intravascular volume.
* **Intervention:** Aggressive IV 0.9% NaCl (Normal Saline). 
* **Refractory Cases:** IV Fludrocortisone to promote renal Na+ reabsorption.

### Acute Post-Operative Management of Central DI (e.g., Post-Pituitary Surgery)
* **Hemodynamic Stabilization:** Replace hourly urine output strictly with IV 0.45% NaCl or 5% Dextrose (if nil by mouth).
* **Pharmacotherapy:** PRN SC/IV Desmopressin (short-acting preferred acutely to prevent iatrogenic water intoxication during recovery phase).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management Algorithm for SIADH
* **First-line:** Strict fluid restriction (usually 800mL - 1L/day).
* **Second-line:** Oral Urea packets (increases solute load to induce osmotic diuresis) OR Demeclocycline (tetracycline antibiotic that induces mild nephrogenic DI).
* **Third-line:** Tolvaptan (Oral selective V2-receptor antagonist). 
* **HK HA Context:** Tolvaptan is an expensive specialty drug; strict prescribing criteria apply, usually reserved for severe symptomatic euvolemic/hypervolemic hyponatremia failing fluid restriction.

### Chronic Management of Central Diabetes Insipidus
* **Pharmacotherapy:** Desmopressin (DDAVP).
* **Formulations:** Oral melt (sublingual), intranasal spray, or tablets.
* **Dosing Strategy:** Titrate to allow mild polyuria prior to next dose to prevent continuous antidiuresis and resultant hyponatremia.

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus
* **Step 1:** Discontinue offending agent (e.g., stop Lithium if psychiatrically stable).
* **Step 2:** Low sodium and low protein diet (↓ renal solute load → ↓ obligate water excretion).
* **Step 3 (Paradoxical Diuretic Rx):** Thiazide diuretics (e.g., Hydrochlorothiazide). Induces mild hypovolemia → ↑ proximal tubule reabsorption of Na+ and water → ↓ delivery to collecting duct → ↓ polyuria.
* **Step 4:** NSAIDs (e.g., Indomethacin). Inhibits renal prostaglandins (which normally antagonize ADH action).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Indicators in Central DI (Post-Surgical Triphasic Response)
* Following pituitary stalk transection, observe for classic 3 phases:
* **Phase 1 (Transient DI):** Axonal shock → no ADH release (Days 1-4).
* **Phase 2 (SIADH):** Degeneration of severed neurons → massive unregulated release of pre-formed ADH → hyponatremia (Days 5-14).
* **Phase 3 (Permanent DI):** Complete depletion of ADH stores.

### Risk Stratification for Severe Hyponatremia Complications
* **High Risk for Osmotic Demyelination:** Chronic hyponatremia (>48 hrs), concurrent hypokalemia, alcoholism, malnutrition, advanced liver disease.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of SIADH Pharmacotherapy
* **Tolvaptan Adverse Effects:** Hepatotoxicity (requires baseline and periodic LFT monitoring), extreme thirst, polyuria, rapid overcorrection of sodium.
* **Demeclocycline Adverse Effects:** Photosensitivity, acute kidney injury (especially in cirrhotics), dental staining.

### Complications of Diabetes Insipidus Therapies
* **Desmopressin Adverse Effects:** Water intoxication (iatrogenic severe hyponatremia) if fluid intake is not self-regulated.
* **Chronic Uncontrolled DI Complications:** Megacystis (massively dilated bladder from constant huge volumes), hydroureter, hydronephrosis, eventual chronic kidney disease.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do thiazide diuretics, which normally increase urine output, treat Nephrogenic DI? Answer: They cause mild volume depletion, triggering a compensatory increase in proximal tubular reabsorption of sodium and water, thus decreasing the amount of water reaching the defective collecting ducts.
* Viva Question: A patient with a subarachnoid hemorrhage develops hyponatremia. Are you going to fluid restrict them? Answer: No, you must differentiate between SIADH and CSW. If it is CSW, fluid restriction will worsen hypovolemia, precipitate cerebral vasospasm, and cause ischemic stroke. Give Normal Saline instead.
* Trap: Diagnosing SIADH simply because sodium is low and urine osmolality is high. Pearl: SIADH is strictly a diagnosis of exclusion. You MUST biochemically prove normal thyroid (TFTs) and normal adrenal (Short Synacthen Test) function first.
* Pitfall: Prescribing a fixed, round-the-clock dosing regimen for chronic Desmopressin. It inevitably leads to water retention and hyponatremia. Allow the patient to wait for the "breakthrough" polyuria/thirst before taking the next dose.
* Trap: Assuming hypernatremia is always present in Diabetes Insipidus. Pearl: DI patients maintain normal sodium levels as long as their thirst mechanism is intact and they have free access to water. Hypernatremia only develops if they cannot drink (e.g., intubated, unconscious, or frail).
