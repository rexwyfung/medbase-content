# Cardiomyopathy (心肌病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Cardiomyopathy
* **Cardiomyopathy (心肌病):** Heterogeneous group of myocardial diseases associated with mechanical and/or electrical dysfunction.
* **Exclusion Criteria:** Must NOT be secondary to Coronary Artery Disease (CAD), Hypertension (HTN), Valvular Heart Disease, or Congenital defects.

### Etiologies of Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) (擴張型心肌病)
* **Idiopathic:** Most common (~50%).
* **Genetic:** TTN (Titin) mutations (Familial).
* **Infectious:** Viral myocarditis (Coxsackie B, Parvovirus B19, HIV, Chagas disease).
* **Toxins:** Alcohol (酒精), Anthracyclines (Doxorubicin), Cocaine, Trastuzumab.
* **Metabolic/Endocrine:** Thyroid dysfunction, Thiamine deficiency (Beriberi), Pheochromocytoma.
* **Other:** Peripartum cardiomyopathy (圍產期心肌病), Tachycardia-induced.

### Etiologies of Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) (肥厚型心肌病)
* **Genetics:** Autosomal dominant (AD). Mutations in sarcomere proteins.
* **Specific Genes:** MYBPC3 (Myosin-binding protein C), MYH7 (β-myosin heavy chain).

### Etiologies of Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM) (限制型心肌病)
* **Infiltrative:** Amyloidosis (澱粉樣變性) (AL or ATTR), Sarcoidosis (結節病).
* **Storage Diseases:** Hemochromatosis (血色病), Fabry disease.
* **Endomyocardial:** Endomyocardial fibrosis, Radiation-induced fibrosis.

### Etiologies of Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)
* **Genetics:** AD mutations in desmosomal proteins (Plakoglobin, Desmoplakin).

### Local Hong Kong Epidemiology
* **DCM:** Most common cardiomyopathy in HK Hospital Authority (HA) admissions.
* **HCM:** Prevalence ~1 in 500; leading cause of Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD) (猝死) in young HK athletes.
* **RCM:** ATTR amyloidosis increasingly recognized in HK elderly population (Wild-type).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)
* **Mechanism:** Cytoskeletal/contractile defect → Myocyte death & fibrosis.
* **Remodeling:** Eccentric hypertrophy (volume overload) → Ventricular dilation.
* **Hemodynamics:** ↓ Contractility → ↓ Ejection Fraction (EF) → Systolic dysfunction (HFrEF).

### Pathophysiology of Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)
* **Mechanism:** Sarcomere disarray & myocardial fibrosis.
* **Remodeling:** Asymmetric septal hypertrophy (ASH).
* **Hemodynamics:** Stiff ventricle → ↓ Compliance → Diastolic dysfunction (HFpEF).
* **Outflow Obstruction (LVOTO):** Hypertrophied septum + Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM) of anterior mitral valve leaflet → Left Ventricular Outflow Tract Obstruction.

### Pathophysiology of Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM)
* **Mechanism:** Interstitial infiltration or fibrosis.
* **Remodeling:** Non-dilated, rigid ventricles with massive biatrial enlargement.
* **Hemodynamics:** Impaired ventricular filling → Severe diastolic dysfunction (HFpEF) with normal/near-normal systolic function.

### Pathophysiology of Arrhythmogenic RV Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)
* **Mechanism:** Desmosome dysfunction → Myocyte detachment under mechanical stress.
* **Remodeling:** Fibrofatty replacement of RV myocardium (typically RV free wall).
* **Consequence:** Re-entrant arrhythmias (VT/VF) & subsequent RV failure.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Features of Cardiomyopathy
* **DCM:** Progressive dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, peripheral edema.
* **HCM:** Exertional syncope (暈厥), exertional angina, palpitations. Family Hx of SCD.
* **RCM:** Right-sided HF symptoms (ascites, pedal edema) > Left-sided HF symptoms.
* **ARVC:** Palpitations, presyncope/syncope during exercise.

### Physical Examination Findings in DCM
* **Inspection:** Displaced, diffuse apex beat.
* **Palpation:** Weak peripheral pulses, parasternal heave (RV overload).
* **Auscultation:** S3 gallop (volume overload), pansystolic murmur (functional Mitral/Tricuspid Regurgitation due to annular dilation).

### Physical Examination Findings in HCM
* **Palpation:** Jerky (bisferiens) carotid pulse, double/triple apical impulse.
* **Auscultation:** S4 gallop (stiff LV). Harsh ejection systolic murmur (ESM) at left lower sternal border.
* **Dynamic Murmur Changes:**
  * **↑ Intensity:** Valsalva maneuver, standing (↓ preload → ↓ LV volume → ↑ LVOTO).
  * **↓ Intensity:** Squatting, passive leg raise (↑ preload), sustained handgrip (↑ afterload).

### Physical Examination Findings in RCM
* **JVP:** Elevated JVP with prominent X & Y descents. Kussmaul's sign +ve (↑ JVP on inspiration).
* **Palpation:** Hepatomegaly (pulsatile if severe TR), ascites.
* **Auscultation:** S4 (commonly), ± S3.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Diagnostic Differentials for Cardiomyopathy
| Disease Entity | Key Discriminatory Features vs True Cardiomyopathy |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ischemic Cardiomyopathy** | Hx of MI/angina. Regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA) corresponding to coronary territories on Echo. Coronary angiography shows severe CAD. |
| **Hypertensive Heart Disease** | Long-standing poorly controlled HTN. Symmetric concentric LVH (unlike asymmetric septal hypertrophy in HCM). |
| **Athlete's Heart** | Physiologic LVH. Reverses with detraining (3-6 months). Normal diastolic function. No SAM or LVOTO. |
| **Constrictive Pericarditis** | Hx of TB/radiation. Pericardial calcification on CXR. Equalization of end-diastolic pressures in all 4 chambers. Septal bounce on Echo. |
| **Aortic Stenosis** | ESM radiating to carotids. Slow-rising pulse (parvus et tardus) vs jerky pulse in HCM. Murmur ↓ with Valsalva. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations & ECG Features
* **ECG in DCM:** Non-specific ST-T changes, LBBB (poor prognostic marker), AF.
* **ECG in HCM:** Massive LVH, deep narrow "dagger-like" Q waves (inferolateral leads).
* **ECG in RCM:** Low QRS voltage (despite thick walls on echo) + pseudo-infarct pattern (especially in cardiac amyloidosis).
* **ECG in ARVC:** Epsilon waves (terminal notch in QRS in V1-V3), T-wave inversions in V1-V3.

### Blood Investigations for Cardiomyopathy
* **Biomarkers:** ↑ NT-proBNP / BNP (stretching/stress), High-sensitivity Troponin (myocardial injury).
* **Specific Etiology Screen:** 
  * Iron studies (Ferritin, Transferrin saturation) for Hemochromatosis.
  * Serum/Urine Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP/UPEP) & Serum Free Light Chains (sFLC) for AL Amyloid.
  * ACE level for Sarcoidosis.
* **Genetics:** Targeted gene panels for familial HCM, DCM, ARVC.

### Imaging Investigations
* **CXR:** Cardiomegaly (DCM), Pulmonary congestion, usually normal heart size in HCM/RCM.
* **Echocardiogram (1st Line Dx):**
  * **DCM:** Dilated spherical LV, LVEF <40%, functional MR.
  * **HCM:** Asymmetric Septal Hypertrophy (ASH, ratio >1.3), LV wall thickness ≥15mm, SAM of mitral valve, LVOT gradient >30 mmHg.
  * **RCM:** "Sparkling/granular" myocardium (Amyloid), biatrial enlargement, severely impaired E/A ratio (restrictive filling).
  * **ARVC:** Dilated, hypokinetic RV with structural aneurysms.
* **Cardiac MRI (CMR) with Late Gadolinium Enhancement (LGE):**
  * **DCM:** Mid-wall LGE.
  * **HCM:** Patchy LGE at RV insertion points.
  * **RCM (Amyloid):** Global subendocardial LGE.
  * **ARVC:** Fibrofatty infiltration of RV free wall.
* **Nuclear Medicine:** 99mTc-PYP or DPD scan (Differs AL vs ATTR amyloid; strongly +ve in ATTR).

### Invasive Investigations & Endomyocardial Biopsy (EMB)
* **Left/Right Heart Catheterization:** Rule out CAD; assess hemodynamics.
* **EMB Indications:** Unexplained new-onset HF <2 weeks with hemodynamic compromise, suspected myocarditis, or specific infiltrative disease.
* **EMB Findings:** Congo red stain +ve with apple-green birefringence (Amyloid), non-caseating granulomas (Sarcoidosis).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Decompensated Heart Failure (ADHF) Management (DCM/RCM)
* **Airway/Breathing:** Upright posture, O2 supplementation, Non-Invasive Ventilation (CPAP/BiPAP) if hypoxemic/pulmonary edema.
* **Circulation (LMNOP protocol):**
  * **L**asix (Furosemide): IV bolus (e.g., 40-80mg) to ↓ volume overload.
  * **M**orphine: (Rarely used in HK HA now due to adverse effects, only for severe distress/palliation).
  * **N**itrates: IV GTN if SBP >110 mmHg (↓ preload).
  * **O**xygen: Target SpO2 >94%.
  * **P**osition: Sit patient up.
* **Cardiogenic Shock:** Inotropes (Dobutamine, Milrinone). Avoid BBs acutely.

### Acute LVOTO Management (HCM with Hemodynamic Compromise)
* **Resuscitation:** IV Fluid bolus (maintain preload to open LVOT).
* **Pharmacology:** IV Beta-blockers (e.g., Metoprolol) to ↓ HR and ↑ diastolic filling.
* **CONTRAINDICATED Drugs:**
  * **Nitrates & Diuretics:** ↓ Preload → worsens LVOTO.
  * **Inotropes (Digoxin, Dobutamine):** ↑ Contractility → worsens LVOTO.
  * **Vasodilators (ACEI, DHP-CCBs):** ↓ Afterload → worsens LVOTO.

### Acute Arrhythmia / SCD Resuscitation
* **Sustained VT / VF:** Immediate ACLS protocol (CPR, Defibrillation).
* **Stable VT:** IV Amiodarone or synchronized DC cardioversion.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of Dilated Cardiomyopathy (HFrEF GDMT)
* **Step 1 (Foundational "Fantastic Four"):**
  * **ARNI/ACEI/ARB:** Sacubitril/Valsartan (Entresto) preferred.
  * **Beta-Blockers:** Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol succinate.
  * **MRA:** Spironolactone, Eplerenone.
  * **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin.
* **Step 2 (Device Therapy):**
  * **ICD (Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator):** Primary prevention if LVEF ≤35% despite ≥3 months of GDMT.
  * **CRT (Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy):** If LVEF ≤35% + LBBB with QRS >150ms.
* **Step 3 (Advanced/End-Stage):** LVAD (Left Ventricular Assist Device), Heart Transplant.

### Chronic Management of Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy
* **Pharmacology (Symptomatic Relief):**
  * 1st Line: Non-vasodilating Beta-blockers (Bisoprolol).
  * 2nd Line: Non-DHP CCB (Verapamil, Diltiazem).
  * 3rd Line: Disopyramide (Negative inotrope) + BB/CCB.
  * Emerging: Mavacamten (Cardiac myosin inhibitor).
* **Invasive Septal Reduction Therapy:** For drug-refractory severe symptoms + LVOT gradient >50 mmHg.
  * Surgical Septal Myectomy (Gold standard).
  * Alcohol Septal Ablation (Catheter-based).
* **SCD Prevention:** ICD insertion based on Risk-SCD score.

### Chronic Management of Restrictive Cardiomyopathy
* **General:** Very cautious use of Diuretics (avoid ↓ preload too much as stiff ventricles require high filling pressures). Maintain sinus rhythm (AF is poorly tolerated).
* **Disease-Specific Rx:**
  * **ATTR Amyloidosis:** Tafamidis (TTR stabilizer).
  * **Sarcoidosis:** Corticosteroids (Prednisolone) ± Immunosuppressants.
  * **Hemochromatosis:** Phlebotomy, Deferoxamine.

### Chronic Management of ARVC
* **Lifestyle:** Strict avoidance of competitive/endurance sports (accelerates disease progression).
* **Pharmacology:** Beta-blockers (Sotalol often used).
* **Device:** ICD implantation for high risk of VT/VF.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### HCM Risk-SCD Calculator (ESC Guidelines)
* **Purpose:** Estimates 5-year risk of sudden cardiac death to guide ICD insertion.
* **Variables Evaluated:**
  * Age
  * Family Hx of SCD
  * Unexplained syncope
  * Maximum LV wall thickness (Echo)
  * Maximum LVOT gradient (Echo)
  * Left Atrial diameter
  * Non-sustained VT on Holter
* **Stratification:**
  * <4%: Low risk (ICD not indicated).
  * 4-6%: Intermediate risk (Consider ICD).
  * ≥6%: High risk (ICD Recommended).

### NYHA Functional Classification for Heart Failure Progression
| Class | Symptom Severity |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Class I** | No limitation of physical activity. |
| **Class II** | Slight limitation. Comfortable at rest, but ordinary activity causes symptoms. |
| **Class III** | Marked limitation. Comfortable at rest, but less than ordinary activity causes symptoms. |
| **Class IV** | Severe limitation. Symptoms present even at rest. |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Direct Cardiac Complications
* **Heart Failure (心臟衰竭):** HFrEF (DCM) or HFpEF (HCM, RCM).
* **Arrhythmias:** Atrial Fibrillation (AF) (心房顫動) secondary to atrial dilation; VT/VF secondary to myocardial scarring.
* **Sudden Cardiac Death (猝死):** Major risk in HCM (young athletes) and ARVC.
* **Valvular Dysfunction:** Functional MR/TR due to annular dilation (DCM) or SAM (HCM).

### Systemic & Thromboembolic Complications
* **Mural Thrombus:** Blood stasis in dilated hypocontractile LV (DCM) or dilated atria (AF) → Cardioembolic Ischemic Stroke (中風).
* **Cardiorenal Syndrome:** Forward failure (↓ perfusion) or backward failure (venous congestion) → Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) / CKD progression.
* **Hepatic Congestion:** "Nutmeg liver" (Cardiac cirrhosis) due to severe right-sided heart failure.

### Drug Therapy Adverse Effects
* **ACEI/ARNI:** Hyperkalemia, angioedema, hypotension, cough (ACEI).
* **SGLT2i:** Euglycemic DKA, genital mycotic infections (candidiasis), UTI.
* **MRA (Spironolactone):** Gynecomastia, hyperkalemia.
* **Amiodarone:** Pulmonary fibrosis, thyroid dysfunction, hepatotoxicity, corneal microdeposits.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the classic murmur of HCM and how do bedside maneuvers differentiate it from Aortic Stenosis? Answer: HCM causes a harsh ejection systolic murmur at the left lower sternal edge. Valsalva and standing decrease preload, shrinking the LV and worsening the obstruction, thus INCREASING the HCM murmur. In contrast, Aortic Stenosis murmur DECREASES with Valsalva.
* Viva Question: A young athlete presents with unexplained syncope during a marathon. ECG shows dagger-like Q waves. What is the diagnosis and pathophysiology? Answer: Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM). Pathophysiology is an autosomal dominant sarcomere mutation causing asymmetric septal hypertrophy, leading to Left Ventricular Outflow Tract Obstruction (LVOTO) and diastolic dysfunction.
* Trap: Treating acute LVOTO hemodynamic compromise with standard heart failure medications like Furosemide, GTN, or Digoxin. Pearl: Never use preload reducers (diuretics, nitrates) or positive inotropes (digoxin, dobutamine) in HCM with LVOTO. They collapse the LV volume and worsen the obstruction. Treat with IV fluids to increase preload and Beta-blockers to increase filling time.
* Pitfall: Assuming low voltage on an ECG always means a pericardial effusion. If the Echo shows massively thickened ventricular walls but the ECG shows low QRS voltages, this pseudo-discordance is highly specific for Cardiac Amyloidosis (Restrictive Cardiomyopathy).
* Viva Question: What is the most important lifestyle advice to give a patient newly diagnosed with ARVC? Answer: Absolute abstinence from competitive and endurance sports, as mechanical stress on the defective desmosomes accelerates fibrofatty replacement and triggers fatal ventricular arrhythmias.
* Trap: Delaying foundational GDMT in Dilated Cardiomyopathy because blood pressure is "borderline". Pearl: Start GDMT agents (like Beta-blockers and ACEI/ARNI) at the lowest possible doses and titrate up very slowly. SGLT2 inhibitors have minimal hemodynamic effects on BP and should be initiated early.
