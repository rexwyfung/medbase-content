# Pituitary Disorders (腦下垂體疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition & Classification of Pituitary Adenomas (腦下垂體腺瘤)
* **Definition:** Benign tumors arising from anterior pituitary cells (adenohypophysis). 
* **Functional Classification:**
  * **Functioning (Hypersecretory) (~70%):** Prolactinoma (most common), Somatotroph (GH), Corticotroph (ACTH), Thyrotroph (TSH), Gonadotroph (LH/FSH - rare).
  * **Non-Functioning (NFPA) (~30%):** Silent adenomas presenting with mass effect or hypopituitarism.
* **Size Classification:**
  * **Microadenoma:** <10mm (typically functioning).
  * **Macroadenoma:** ≥10mm (typically NFPA, often causes mass effect).

### Etiologies of Hypopituitarism (腦下垂體功能減退)
* **Neoplastic:** Pituitary adenoma, Craniopharyngioma (顱咽管瘤), Meningioma, Mets.
* **Vascular:**
  * **Sheehan's Syndrome (席漢氏症候群):** Postpartum ischemic necrosis.
  * **Pituitary Apoplexy (腦下垂體中風):** Acute hemorrhage/infarction of an adenoma.
* **Inflammatory/Infiltrative:** Lymphocytic hypophysitis, Sarcoidosis, Hemochromatosis, Histiocytosis X.
* **Iatrogenic:** Post-neurosurgery (Transsphenoidal), Cranial radiotherapy.
* **Congenital:** Kallmann syndrome (GnRH deficiency + anosmia).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** Prolactinomas account for ~40-50% of all pituitary adenomas in HK.
* **Demographics:** 
  * Prolactinomas: Peak incidence in females aged 20-40.
  * Acromegaly: Insidious onset, average diagnostic delay of 5-10 years in HA clinics.
  * Craniopharyngioma: Bimodal distribution (children 5-14 yrs, adults 50-74 yrs).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Pituitary Mass Effect
* **Suprasellar Extension:** Compression of optic chiasm → decussating nasal retinal fibers compromised.
* **Lateral Extension:** Invasion of cavernous sinus → compression of CN III, IV, V1, V2, VI (internal carotid artery displacement).
* **Pituitary Stalk Effect:** Compression of infundibular stalk → physical block of hypothalamic dopamine delivery to anterior pituitary → disinhibition of lactotrophs → mild/moderate ↑ Prolactin.

### Molecular Mechanisms of Hormone Excess Syndromes
* **Prolactinoma:** Autonomous lactotroph proliferation → ↑ PRL → inhibits hypothalamic GnRH pulsatility → ↓ LH/FSH (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism).
* **Acromegaly (肢端肥大症):** Somatotroph adenoma → continuous GH secretion → stimulates hepatic synthesis of Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1) → somatic overgrowth & insulin resistance.
* **Cushing's Disease (庫茲氏症):** Corticotroph adenoma → ↑ ACTH (resistant to negative feedback) → bilateral adrenal cortical hyperplasia → chronic ↑ cortisol.

### Pathophysiology of Sheehan's Syndrome (席漢氏症候群)
* **Pregnancy:** Physiological lactotroph hyperplasia → pituitary gland enlarges by ~120%, increasing metabolic demand without proportional ↑ in blood supply.
* **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH):** Severe hypovolemia/shock → vasospasm & hypotension → ischemic necrosis of highly susceptible enlarged anterior pituitary → failure to lactate (1st sign) & amenorrhea.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Assessment of Pituitary Mass Effect
* **Visual Field Defect:** Bitemporal superior quadrantanopia (early) → bitemporal hemianopia (late).
* **Cranial Nerve Deficits:** Diplopia, ptosis, anisocoria (CN III, IV, VI palsies due to cavernous sinus invasion).
* **Neurological Signs:** Frontal headache, papilledema (if ↑ ICP).

### Clinical Signs of Acromegaly (肢端肥大症)
* **General:** Coarse facial features, macroglossia, wide-spaced teeth, prognathism (prominent jaw).
* **Extremities:** Spade-like hands, thick doughy skin, ↑ shoe/ring size.
* **Neurological:** Bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome (median nerve entrapment), proximal myopathy.
* **Cardiorespiratory:** Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), clinical signs of cardiomegaly/heart failure.
* **Endocrine:** Goiter (GH stimulates thyroid hyperplasia).

### Clinical Signs of Cushing's Disease (庫茲氏症)
* **Facies:** Moon facies, facial plethora, acne, hirsutism (in females).
* **Skin/Fat:** Centripetal obesity, buffalo hump, supraclavicular fat pads, broad purple abdominal striae (>1cm wide), thin skin, easy bruising.
* **Musculoskeletal:** Proximal muscle wasting and weakness (inability to rise from squat).

### Clinical Signs of Prolactinoma & Hypopituitarism
* **Hyperprolactinemia:** Galactorrhea (expressible milky nipple discharge), gynecomastia (males), vaginal atrophy (due to estrogen deficiency).
* **Hypogonadism:** Absent/sparse axillary & pubic hair, testicular atrophy.
* **Hypothyroidism:** Waxy pale skin (alabaster skin), delayed relaxation of ankle jerks.
* **Adrenal Insufficiency (Secondary):** Postural hypotension, pallor (NO hyperpigmentation, unlike primary Addison's where POMC/ACTH is ↑).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Hyperprolactinemia (高泌乳激素血症)
| Category | Causes / Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Prolactinoma** | PRL levels usually >5000 mU/L. Macroadenomas often >10,000 mU/L. |
| **Stalk Compression Effect** | Mass (e.g., NFPA) blocks dopamine. PRL usually modestly ↑ (1000-2000 mU/L). |
| **Drug-Induced** | Dopamine antagonists (Haloperidol, Metoclopramide, Risperidone). |
| **Systemic Disease** | Primary hypothyroidism (↑ TRH stimulates PRL release), CKD (↓ clearance). |
| **Physiological** | Pregnancy, stress, lactation, extreme exercise. |

### Differentials for Cushing's Syndrome 
| Diagnosis | ACTH Level | High-Dose Dexamethasone Suppression Test (HDDST) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pituitary (Cushing's Disease)** | Normal / ↑ | Cortisol suppresses >50% |
| **Adrenal Adenoma/Carcinoma** | Undetectable (↓) | No suppression |
| **Ectopic ACTH (e.g., SCLC)** | Markedly ↑ | No suppression |

### Differentials for Sellar / Suprasellar Masses
* **Pituitary Adenoma:** Intrasellar mass with possible suprasellar extension, uniform enhancement.
* **Craniopharyngioma:** Suprasellar, cystic components, characteristic **calcifications** on imaging.
* **Rathke's Cleft Cyst:** Benign asymptomatic cystic lesion, no solid enhancing component.
* **Meningioma:** Dural tail sign, extra-axial.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Initial Bedside & Anatomical Investigations
* **Bedside:** Formal visual field testing (Goldmann perimetry), fundoscopy (optic atrophy/papilledema).
* **Imaging:** MRI Brain/Pituitary with Gadolinium contrast (Gold standard).
  * Dynamic contrast sequence distinguishes microadenoma (hypointense/delayed enhancement) from normal gland.

### Diagnostic Testing for Acromegaly
* **Screening:** Serum IGF-1 (adjusted for age/sex) → ↑ levels warrant dynamic testing.
* **Confirmatory (Dynamic):** 75g Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) with GH measurement.
  * **Positive Dx Criteria:** Failure to suppress GH to <1 mcg/L (or <0.3 mcg/L via ultra-sensitive assay) at 2 hours post-glucose load.

### Diagnostic Testing for Cushing's Disease
* **Screening (Need 2 abnormal tests):** 
  * 24-hour Urinary Free Cortisol (UFC) x 2.
  * 1mg Overnight Dexamethasone Suppression Test (ODST) → Positive if morning cortisol >50 nmol/L.
  * Late-night salivary cortisol.
* **Localization (Source of ↑ ACTH):** 
  * 8mg High-Dose Dexamethasone Suppression Test (HDDST).
  * CRH stimulation test.
  * **Invasive (Gold Standard for Pituitary vs Ectopic):** Inferior Petrosal Sinus Sampling (IPSS) → Central-to-peripheral ACTH ratio >2 (basal) or >3 (post-CRH) confirms pituitary source.

### Diagnostic Testing for Hypopituitarism
* **Basal Bloods:** Early morning Cortisol, fT4, TSH, LH, FSH, Testosterone/Estradiol, Prolactin, IGF-1.
* **Dynamic Cortisol Testing:** 
  * **Insulin Tolerance Test (ITT):** Gold standard for ACTH & GH reserve. Induce hypoglycemia (glucose <2.2 mmol/L). Normal: Cortisol rises >500 nmol/L. (Contraindicated in ischemic heart disease or epilepsy).
  * **Short Synacthen Test (SST):** Alternative. Normal peak cortisol >500 nmol/L (note: may be falsely normal in acute secondary adrenal insufficiency as adrenals haven't atrophied yet).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Pituitary Apoplexy (腦下垂體中風)
* **Immediate Resuscitation:** ABCDE approach, IV fluid resuscitation (Normal Saline).
* **Acute Hormonal Rescue:** IV Hydrocortisone 100mg STAT, followed by 50mg Q6H (to cover acute secondary adrenal crisis).
* **Emergent Neurosurgical Consult:** For urgent Transsphenoidal Surgical Decompression.
  * Indications for urgent surgery: Rapidly deteriorating visual acuity, worsening visual field defects, altered consciousness.
* **Monitoring:** Strict fluid balance & electrolytes (monitor for acute central Diabetes Insipidus or SIADH).

### Acute Management of Perioperative Pituitary Stress
* **Pre-operative Stress Dosing:** IV Hydrocortisone 100mg at induction of anesthesia.
* **Post-operative Bridging:** IV/PO Hydrocortisone taper over 48-72 hours, reassess pituitary reserve at 4-6 weeks.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Prolactinoma
* **1st Line (Medical Therapy):** Dopamine Agonists (DA).
  * **Cabergoline:** Drug of choice in HK HA (longer half-life, better tolerated, dosed 1-2x/week).
  * **Bromocriptine:** Alternative (dosed daily), preferred historically in pregnancy (though Cabergoline increasingly accepted).
* **Therapeutic Goals:** Normalize PRL, restore fertility, shrink tumor.
* **2nd Line (Surgery):** Transsphenoidal surgery (TSS) if resistant/intolerant to DA, or rapidly progressing visual loss despite DA.

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Acromegaly
* **1st Line:** Transsphenoidal Surgery (TSS) - curative intent.
* **2nd Line (Medical Therapy - if surgical failure/contraindicated):**
  * **Somatostatin Analogues (SSA):** Octreotide LAR or Lanreotide (inhibits GH release).
  * **Dopamine Agonists:** Cabergoline (effective if co-secreting PRL).
  * **GH Receptor Antagonist:** Pegvisomant (blocks peripheral GH action, reduces IGF-1, does *not* shrink tumor).
* **3rd Line:** Radiotherapy (Stereotactic radiosurgery).

### Chronic Management Algorithm for Cushing's Disease
* **1st Line:** Transsphenoidal Surgery (TSS) (Microadenomectomy).
* **2nd Line (Medical / Bridging Therapy):** Steroidogenesis inhibitors.
  * **Metyrapone:** Blocks 11-beta-hydroxylase.
  * **Ketoconazole:** Blocks multiple CYP450 steroidogenic enzymes (requires strict LFT monitoring).
* **3rd Line:** Bilateral adrenalectomy (requires lifelong steroid replacement) or Radiotherapy.

### Hormone Replacement Algorithm for Hypopituitarism
* **Rule of Order:** ALWAYS replace Glucocorticoids FIRST, then Thyroid hormone. (Thyroxine increases basal metabolic rate and hepatic clearance of cortisol; giving T4 first can precipitate acute Adrenal Crisis).
* **Glucocorticoid:** PO Hydrocortisone (typically 10-15mg morning, 5mg afternoon to mimic diurnal rhythm). Sick day rules apply (double/triple dose during illness).
* **Thyroid:** PO Levothyroxine (monitor fT4 levels, NOT TSH, as TSH is unreliable in secondary hypothyroidism).
* **Sex Steroids:** Testosterone (males), HRT (females) for bone protection.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Knosp Classification (Risk Stratification for Adenomas)
* **Purpose:** Grading cavernous sinus invasion on Coronal MRI.
* **Grade 0-1:** Tumor medial to the inter-carotid line (High chance of complete surgical resection).
* **Grade 2-3:** Tumor extends beyond the inter-carotid line (Subtotal resection likely, higher recurrence risk).
* **Grade 4:** Total encasement of the intracavernous carotid artery (Unresectable surgically, requires adjuvant medical Rx/Radiotherapy).

### Prognostic Indicators in Acromegaly
* **Remission Criteria:** Normal age-matched IGF-1 AND random GH <1.0 mcg/L.
* **Mortality Risk:** Reverts to general population risk ONLY IF biochemical control (IGF-1 normalization) is achieved. Untreated, life expectancy is reduced by ~10 years (predominantly CV death).

### Pituitary Apoplexy Risk Factors
* **Tumor Characteristics:** Large macroadenomas.
* **Precipitating Factors:** Initiation of Bromocriptine/Cabergoline, dynamic endocrine tests (TRH/CRH tests), pregnancy, major surgery, anticoagulation therapy, bleeding disorders.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Untreated Acromegaly
* **Cardiovascular (Leading cause of death):** Biventricular hypertrophy, diastolic dysfunction, hypertension.
* **Metabolic:** Secondary Diabetes Mellitus (GH causes profound insulin resistance).
* **Oncological:** Increased risk of colonic polyps and Colorectal Cancer (requires colonoscopy screening protocol).
* **Musculoskeletal:** Severe osteoarthritis, kyphoscoliosis.

### Complications of Untreated Cushing's Disease
* **Bone:** Severe osteoporosis, avascular necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head, pathological vertebral fractures.
* **Cardiovascular:** Severe hypertension, accelerated atherosclerosis.
* **Immunological:** Severe immunosuppression (opportunistic infections, atypical mycobacteria).

### Adverse Effects of Pituitary Pharmacotherapy
* **Cabergoline (High Dose):** Cardiac valvulopathy (tricuspid regurgitation) requires baseline & annual ECHO. Impulse control disorders (gambling, hypersexuality), psychosis.
* **Somatostatin Analogues (Octreotide):** Gallstones (biliary stasis), steatorrhea, bradycardia.
* **Ketoconazole:** Severe hepatotoxicity.
* **Metyrapone:** Hirsutism/Acne (due to shunting to androgen pathways), hypertension (due to accumulation of mineralocorticoid precursors).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Diagnosing a prolactinoma simply because prolactin is elevated at 1500 mU/L in the presence of a 2cm pituitary mass. Pearl: A 2cm true prolactinoma should have PRL levels >10,000 mU/L. A level of 1500 mU/L with a macroadenoma represents the "Stalk Effect" (a non-functioning adenoma compressing the infundibulum and blocking dopamine). DO NOT give Cabergoline as primary treatment for a large NFPA causing stalk effect; it needs surgery.
* Pitfall: Starting a panhypopituitary patient on Levothyroxine before checking and replacing cortisol. Pearl: Thyroxine ↑ metabolic clearance of cortisol. If you replace T4 before Hydrocortisone, you will precipitate a fatal acute adrenal crisis. Always replace Steroids first!
* Viva Question: How do you adjust the dose of Levothyroxine in a patient with panhypopituitarism? Answer: You must titrate based on free T4 (fT4) levels, aiming for the upper half of the reference range. DO NOT use TSH to titrate, as the pituitary cannot produce TSH in secondary hypothyroidism.
* Trap: Assuming hyperpigmentation is present in secondary adrenal insufficiency (hypopituitarism). Pearl: Hyperpigmentation ONLY occurs in primary adrenal failure (Addison's) due to lack of cortisol feedback causing massive ↑ in POMC/ACTH (which stimulates melanocytes). In hypopituitarism, ACTH is low, so the patient will be characteristically pale (alabaster skin).
* Viva Question: What is the most definitive test to confirm Cushing's disease (pituitary source) when MRI is equivocal? Answer: Bilateral Inferior Petrosal Sinus Sampling (IPSS) with CRH stimulation to demonstrate a central-to-peripheral ACTH gradient.
