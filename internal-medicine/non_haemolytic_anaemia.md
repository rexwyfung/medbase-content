# Non-Haemolytic Anaemia (非溶血性貧血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Non-Haemolytic Anaemia
* **Definition**: ↓ Haemoglobin (Hb) concentration below normal range for age/sex w/o evidence of premature RBC destruction (haemolysis).
* **WHO Criteria**: Hb <13 g/dL in adult males; <12 g/dL in non-pregnant females; <11 g/dL in pregnant females.
* **Core Mechanism**: ↓ RBC production (marrow failure, substrate deficiency) OR acute blood loss.

### Etiological Classification by Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)
* **Microcytic (MCV <80 fL)**: Iron Deficiency Anaemia (IDA) [缺鐵性貧血], Anaemia of Chronic Disease (ACD) [慢性疾病貧血] (late stage), Sideroblastic Anaemia (鐵粒幼細胞貧血).
* **Normocytic (MCV 80-100 fL)**: Acute blood loss, ACD (early stage), Aplastic Anaemia (再生不良性貧血), Marrow infiltration (Leukaemia, Myeloma).
* **Macrocytic (MCV >100 fL)**: 
  * **Megaloblastic**: Vitamin B12 deficiency (維生素B12缺乏), Folate deficiency (葉酸缺乏).
  * **Non-Megaloblastic**: Alcohol excess, Hypothyroidism (甲狀腺功能減退), Liver disease, Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS) [骨髓增生異常綜合症].

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong (HA Context)
* **IDA**: Most common cause of anaemia in HK. High incidence of colorectal carcinoma (大腸癌) makes unexplained IDA in >50 yrs a red flag requiring urgent 2-week wait (2WW) endoscopy.
* **Thalassemia Trait**: Affects ~8% of HK population; crucial common differential for microcytic anaemia.
* **MDS/Aplastic Anaemia**: Increasing incidence with aging HK population; often presents as refractory anaemia.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Iron Deficiency Anaemia (IDA)
* **Iron Depletion Phase**: Depletion of storage iron (macrophages/liver) → ↓ serum Ferritin.
* **Iron Deficient Erythropoiesis**: ↓ Serum Fe, ↑ Transferrin (TIBC) production by liver to maximize transport → ↓ Transferrin saturation.
* **Anaemia Phase**: Exhausted iron supply → ↓ Haem (血紅素) synthesis → Cytoplasmic maturation lags behind nuclear maturation → Microcytic, hypochromic RBCs.

### Pathophysiology of Anaemia of Chronic Disease (ACD)
* **Inflammatory Cytokines**: ↑ IL-6 from chronic inflammation/malignancy/autoimmunity.
* **Hepcidin (肝素) Upregulation**: IL-6 stimulates liver ↑ Hepcidin synthesis.
* **Ferroportin Internalization**: Hepcidin binds/internalizes **Ferroportin** (iron exporter) on enterocytes and macrophages.
* **Iron Sequestration**: Dietary iron not absorbed; recycled iron trapped in RES macrophages → Functional iron deficiency despite normal/↑ total body stores.

### Pathophysiology of Megaloblastic Anaemia
* **DNA Synthesis Failure**: B12 (Cobalamin) and Folate are essential co-factors for Thymidylate synthesis (DNA precursor).
* **Nuclear-Cytoplasmic Asynchrony**: ↓ DNA synthesis delays nuclear division, but RNA/protein synthesis continues → large cells (Macrocytosis) with immature nuclei (Megaloblasts).
* **Neurological Mechanism (B12 ONLY)**: B12 is required to convert Methylmalonyl-CoA to Succinyl-CoA. ↓ B12 → ↑ Methylmalonic acid (MMA) → Abnormal myelin synthesis in dorsal/lateral spinal columns.

### Pathophysiology of Aplastic Anaemia & MDS
* **Aplastic Anaemia**: T-cell mediated autoimmune destruction of haematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) → Empty, fatty bone marrow (Pancytopenia).
* **MDS**: Clonal HSC disorder → Ineffective haematopoiesis → Intramedullary apoptosis (hypercellular marrow but peripheral cytopenia) + risk of AML transformation.

## 3. Clinical Assessment 

### General Anaemia Symptoms and Signs
* **Symptoms**: Exertional dyspnoea (勞動性氣促), fatigue, palpitations, tinnitus, presyncope, angina (if pre-existing CAD).
* **Signs**: Conjunctival pallor (結膜蒼白), palmar crease pallor (Hb typically <8 g/dL), resting tachycardia, flow murmur (ejection systolic), tachypnoea.

### Specific Physical Signs of Iron Deficiency Anaemia
* **Nails**: Koilonychia (匙狀甲) [spoon-shaped nails], brittle nails.
* **Oral**: Angular stomatitis (口角炎), atrophic glossitis (萎縮性舌炎) [smooth, beefy red tongue].
* **GI**: Dysphagia (Plummer-Vinson syndrome: esophageal webs).

### Specific Signs of Vitamin B12/Folate Deficiency
* **Skin/GI**: Mild jaundice (lemon-yellow tint due to ineffective erythropoiesis/mild haemolysis in marrow), glossitis.
* **Neurological Signs (B12 ONLY)**: 
  * **Dorsal Column**: Loss of vibration sense (earliest sign) and proprioception → Sensory ataxia (+ve Romberg's sign).
  * **Corticospinal Tract**: Spastic paraparesis, brisk reflexes, +ve Babinski sign (Subacute Combined Degeneration of Cord).
  * **Neuropsychiatric**: Dementia, irritability, psychosis.

### History of Blood Loss & Red Flags
* **GI Bleeding**: Melena (黑糞), haematemesis, haematochezia, altered bowel habit, weight loss.
* **Gynaecological**: Menorrhagia (經血過多) [clots, flooding, pad usage].
* **Medications**: NSAIDs, antiplatelets, anticoagulants.

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Bedside Investigations
* **ECG**: Check for ischaemic changes (Type II MI due to supply/demand mismatch).
* **Urine Dipstick**: Haematuria (rule out GU bleeding).
* **FOBT (大便隱血)**: Replaced largely by FIT in HK HA, but direct endoscopy is preferred if overt symptoms.

### Blood Investigations & Diagnostic Criteria
* **Complete Blood Profile (CBP)**: 
  * ↓ Hb, MCV, MCH.
  * ↑ RDW (Red Cell Distribution Width) → Suggests IDA (anisocytosis); RDW is normal in Thalassemia trait.
* **Reticulocyte Count**: 
  * ↓ or Inappropriately Normal: Production failure (IDA, B12/Folate, Marrow failure).
  * ↑: Acute bleeding (or haemolysis).
* **Iron Profile (IDA Criteria)**: Ferritin <30 µg/L is absolute diagnostic for IDA (Note: Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant; in chronic inflammation/CKD, threshold for IDA is <100 µg/L or Tsat <20%).
* **B12 / Folate Levels**: Active B12 (Holotranscobalamin) <35 pmol/L. Serum folate <3 µg/L.
* **MDS / Aplastic Anaemia Screen**: Peripheral blood smear shows pancytopenia, tear-drop cells, or dysplastic neutrophils.

### Imaging & Endoscopic Investigations
* **Oesophago-gastro-duodenoscopy (OGD) + Colonoscopy**: Mandatory for all men, and post-menopausal women with IDA to rule out occult GI malignancy.
* **Capsule Endoscopy**: If OGD/Colonoscopy are normal but GI bleeding is highly suspected (small bowel source).
* **Pelvic USG**: For pre-menopausal women with menorrhagia (fibroids, adenomyosis).

### Special / Invasive Diagnostics
* **Bone Marrow Aspiration and Trephine (BMAT)**:
  * **Indication**: Unexplained pancytopenia, macrocytosis (suspected MDS), normal haematinics.
  * **Findings**: 
    * *Aplastic*: Hypocellular/Empty marrow (<25% cellularity), fat spaces.
    * *MDS*: Hypercellular, multilineage dysplasia, ring sideroblasts (Perls' stain).
* **Anti-Intrinsic Factor / Anti-Parietal Cell Abs**: Diagnostic for Pernicious Anaemia (惡性貧血) in B12 deficiency.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Resuscitation for Acute Blood Loss Anaemia
* **ABCDE Approach**: 100% O2, attach continuous monitoring.
* **IV Access**: Insert 2x large-bore IV cannulae (14G or 16G).
* **Fluid Resuscitation**: STAT IV 0.9% NaCl or Hartmann's (Max 1-2L); avoid over-dilution.
* **Haemorrhage Control**: Urgent OGD/Colonoscopy (within 24 hrs for GI bleed), or surgical/IR embolization intervention.
* **Major Haemorrhage Protocol (MHP)**: Trigger if massive bleeding. Transfuse PRBC:FFP:Platelets in 1:1:1 ratio.

### Transfusion Thresholds & Protocols (PRBCs)
* **Haemodynamically Unstable / Active Bleeding**: Transfuse O-negative (females <50) or O-positive (males/females >50) immediately pending crossmatch.
* **Stable with Ischaemic Heart Disease (IHD)**: Transfuse if Hb <8 g/dL (Target 8-9 g/dL).
* **Stable without IHD**: Transfuse if Hb <7 g/dL (Target 7-8 g/dL).
* **Administration**: 1 unit PRBC typically raises Hb by ~1 g/dL. Prescribe with IV Furosemide (Lasix) 20-40mg in elderly/heart failure to prevent TACO.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Pharmacological Management of Iron Deficiency Anaemia
* **1st Line (Oral Iron)**: Tab. Ferrous Sulphate (硫酸亞鐵) 300mg OM/BD or Ferrous Fumarate. 
  * *Instructions*: Take on empty stomach with Vitamin C (orange juice) to ↑ absorption. Avoid taking with tea/coffee/milk (tannins/calcium ↓ absorption).
  * *Side Effects*: Constipation, black stools, nausea.
* **2nd Line (IV Iron)**: IV Iron Polymaltose (CosmoFer) or IV Ferric Derisomaltose (Monofer).
  * *Indications*: Intolerance to PO iron, active IBD (PO iron worsens inflammation), severe malabsorption, or CKD on dialysis.
  * *Monitoring*: Risk of anaphylaxis (anaphylactic precautions required during infusion).
* **Duration**: Continue PO iron for 3-6 months AFTER Hb normalizes to replenish marrow stores.

### Pharmacological Management of Megaloblastic Anaemia
* **B12 Deficiency**: 
  * *Regimen*: IM Hydroxocobalamin 1mg on alternate days for 2 weeks (loading), then 1mg every 3 months for life (if pernicious anaemia).
* **Folate Deficiency**: 
  * *Regimen*: PO Folic Acid 5mg daily for 4 months.
* **Combined Deficiency Rule**: ALWAYS replace B12 BEFORE starting Folic Acid. (Giving Folate first accelerates haematopoiesis, rapidly depleting remaining B12 → precipitating irreversible Subacute Combined Degeneration of Cord).

### Management Algorithms for Bone Marrow Failure
* **Anaemia of Chronic Disease (ACD)**: Treat underlying condition. Avoid iron unless concurrent IDA. Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) may be considered in CKD or cancer.
* **Aplastic Anaemia**:
  * *Young (<50 yrs) & Fit*: Allogeneic Haematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT).
  * *Older (>50 yrs) or Unfit*: Immunosuppressive Therapy (IST) - Anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) + Cyclosporin + Eltrombopag (TPO agonist).
* **MDS**: Supportive (transfusions), ESAs, Hypomethylating agents (Azacitidine), or HSCT for high-risk IPSS-R.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### WHO Severity Grading of Anaemia
| Grade | Hb Threshold (g/dL) - Adult Non-Pregnant | Clinical Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | 11.0 - 11.9 (F) / 11.0 - 12.9 (M) | Usually asymptomatic |
| **Moderate** | 8.0 - 10.9 | Symptomatic on exertion |
| **Severe** | <8.0 | High risk of cardiac decompensation |

### IPSS-R (Revised International Prognostic Scoring System) for MDS
* **Variables Evaluated**:
  * Cytogenetics (Karyotype anomalies).
  * Marrow blast percentage (<5%, 5-10%, >10%).
  * Haemoglobin (<8, 8-10, >10).
  * Platelets (<50, 50-100, >100).
  * Absolute Neutrophil Count (<0.8).
* **Clinical Utility**: Stratifies MDS into Very Low, Low, Intermediate, High, Very High risk. Guides decision between supportive care (low risk) vs Azacitidine/HSCT (high risk).

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### End-Organ Damage from Chronic Severe Anaemia
* **Cardiovascular**: High-output cardiac failure (↑ venous return + ↑ contractility to maintain O2 delivery → ventricular hypertrophy → failure).
* **Neurological**: Subacute Combined Degeneration of Cord (Irreversible if untreated B12 deficiency).
* **Pregnancy**: Pre-term labour, low birth weight, foetal neural tube defects (Folate deficiency).

### Complications of Blood Transfusion (輸血併發症)
* **Acute**: Acute Haemolytic Transfusion Reaction (ABO incompatibility), Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI), Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO) [common in HK elderly], Febrile Non-Haemolytic Reaction.
* **Chronic**: 
  * **Transfusion Hemosiderosis (Iron Overload)**: 1 unit PRBC = 250mg Iron. Body cannot excrete excess. Leads to liver cirrhosis, bronze diabetes, cardiomyopathy. Requires Iron Chelation (e.g., Deferasirox) if Ferritin >1000 µg/L.
  * **Alloimmunization**: Developing antibodies to minor red cell antigens, complicating future crossmatches.
  * **Infection**: HBV, HCV, HIV, Syphilis (Rare in HK due to strict Red Cross screening).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical investigation in a 65-year-old male presenting with asymptomatic Iron Deficiency Anaemia? Answer: Urgent bi-directional endoscopy (OGD and Colonoscopy) to exclude occult gastrointestinal malignancy.
* Trap: Treating macrocytic anaemia with folic acid alone before checking B12 levels. Pearl: Always test both and replace B12 first; unopposed folate replacement in B12 deficiency corrects the anaemia but precipitates irreversible neurological damage (SCDC).
* Pitfall: Reassuring a patient that their Iron Deficiency Anaemia is "cured" once their Haemoglobin normalizes. You must continue oral iron therapy for an additional 3-6 months to replenish bone marrow iron stores (Ferritin), otherwise early relapse is inevitable.
* Viva Question: How does Hepcidin cause Anaemia of Chronic Disease? Answer: Inflammatory IL-6 stimulates liver hepcidin release, which binds and internalizes ferroportin channels on macrophages and enterocytes, trapping iron and preventing its delivery to the marrow.
* Trap: Misdiagnosing Thalassemia Trait as Iron Deficiency Anaemia because both are microcytic. Pearl: Check the Mentzer Index (MCV/RBC count). A ratio <13 suggests Thalassemia (high RBC count despite low MCV), whereas >13 suggests IDA. Confirm with Ferritin and Haemoglobin pattern (Hb electrophoresis).
