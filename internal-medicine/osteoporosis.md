# Osteoporosis (骨質疏鬆症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Osteoporosis
* **Definition:** Systemic skeletal disorder characterized by ↓ bone mineral density (BMD) and microarchitectural deterioration of bone tissue → ↑ bone fragility and ↑ susceptibility to low-trauma fragility fractures.
* **WHO Diagnostic Threshold:** Bone mineral density (BMD) T-score $\le$ -2.5 standard deviations below the young adult mean.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** Highly prevalent in HK's aging population; affects ~30% of postmenopausal women and ~10% of men aged >50.
* **Morbidity:** Hip fractures incidence in HK is among the highest in Asia; associated with 20% 1-year mortality and 50% permanent loss of independence.

### Primary Osteoporosis Classification
* **Type 1 (Postmenopausal):** Estrogen deficiency in women → rapid trabecular bone loss → vertebral and distal radius fractures.
* **Type 2 (Senile):** Aging-related cortical and trabecular bone loss in men and women >70 yrs → hip and pelvic fractures.

### Secondary Causes of Osteoporosis
* **Endocrine:** Hyperthyroidism, Hyperparathyroidism, Cushing's syndrome, Hypogonadism (premature menopause <45 yrs).
* **Gastrointestinal:** Celiac disease, Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD), post-gastrectomy/bariatric surgery, primary biliary cholangitis (PBC).
* **Rheumatological:** Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA), Ankylosing Spondylitis.
* **Medications (CRITICAL):** 
  * Glucocorticoids (GIOP) (e.g., Prednisolone $\ge$5mg/day for >3 months).
  * Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) (long-term use → ↓ Ca absorption).
  * Aromatase Inhibitors & Androgen Deprivation Therapy (ADT).
  * Anticonvulsants (e.g., Phenytoin, Carbamazepine → ↑ Vit D metabolism).
  * Heparin (long-term).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Bone Remodeling Cycle Pathophysiology
* **Normal state:** Coupled balance between **Osteoclasts (破骨細胞)** (bone resorption) and **Osteoblasts (成骨細胞)** (bone formation).
* **Osteoporosis state:** Uncoupling → Resorption rate > Formation rate → net loss of bone mass and thinning of trabeculae.

### RANK/RANKL/OPG Pathway
* **Osteoprotegerin (OPG):** Decoy receptor produced by osteoblasts; binds RANKL to inhibit osteoclastogenesis.
* **RANKL (Receptor Activator of NF-κB Ligand):** Expressed on osteoblasts; binds RANK on osteoclast precursors → activates osteoclasts.
* **Menopausal Mechanism:** Estrogen withdrawal → ↓ OPG production and ↑ RANKL expression → unchecked osteoclast activation and lifespan ↑ → severe trabecular bone loss.

### Glucocorticoid-Induced Osteoporosis (GIOP) Mechanisms
* **Direct effects:** ↑ Osteoblast and osteocyte apoptosis → profound ↓ in bone formation (early and rapid bone loss).
* **Indirect effects:** ↓ Intestinal calcium absorption and ↑ renal calcium excretion → secondary hyperparathyroidism. ↓ Gonadal hormones.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for Osteoporosis Risk
* **Demographics & Repro Hx:** Age, female sex, age of menarche/menopause, history of amenorrhea, nulliparity.
* **Fracture Hx:** Previous fragility fractures (fracture from standing height or less).
* **Lifestyle:** Dietary calcium/dairy intake, sun exposure (Vit D), smoking, heavy EtOH use, physical inactivity/immobility.
* **Medication Hx:** Steroids, PPIs, anti-epileptics.
* **Family Hx:** Parental history of hip fracture (strong independent risk factor).

### Physical Examination Findings
* **General:** Often entirely asymptomatic ("silent disease") until a fracture occurs.
* **Spinal Deformity:** **Kyphosis (駝背)** / Dowager's hump (due to anterior wedge compression fractures of thoracic vertebrae).
* **Height Loss:** Historical height loss >4cm or prospective height loss >2cm.
* **Thoracic-Pelvic Proximity:** Rib-pelvis distance $\le$2 fingerbreadths (indicates lumbar compression fractures).
* **Occiput-Wall Distance:** >0 cm when standing with heels and buttocks against a wall (indicates thoracic kyphosis).
* **Secondary Stigmata:** Cushingoid features, thyrotoxicosis signs, rheumatoid joint deformities.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Features vs Osteomalacia (骨軟化症)
* **Osteoporosis:** Matrix to mineral ratio is NORMAL (just less total bone). Ca, PO4, ALP, PTH are strictly NORMAL.
* **Osteomalacia:** Defective mineralization of osteoid (Vit D deficiency). Labs show ↓ Ca, ↓ PO4, ↑ ALP, ↑ PTH. Patient has bone pain and proximal myopathy.

### Discriminatory Features vs Multiple Myeloma
* **Osteoporosis:** Normal inflammatory markers, normal hemoglobin, normal renal function.
* **Myeloma:** Punched-out lytic lesions (not diffuse osteopenia), anemia, hypercalcemia, renal failure, +ve Bence Jones protein, monoclonal band on SPEP/UPEP.

### Discriminatory Features vs Paget's Disease of Bone
* **Osteoporosis:** Diffuse low bone mass, normal ALP.
* **Paget's:** Focal bone enlargement/deformity (e.g., bowing of tibia, skull enlargement), massively ↑ ALP with normal Ca/PO4, "cotton wool" appearance on X-ray.

### Discriminatory Features vs Renal Osteodystrophy
* **Osteoporosis:** Normal renal function, normal PTH.
* **Renal Osteodystrophy:** Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) stages 4-5, ↑↑ PTH (secondary/tertiary hyperparathyroidism), ↑ PO4, ↓ Ca, rugger-jersey spine on X-ray.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Routine Blood Investigations (Secondary Screen)
* **Bone Profile:** Serum Calcium, Phosphate, Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) (All normal in primary osteoporosis).
* **Endocrine Panel:** 25-OH Vitamin D, Intact PTH, TSH/Free T4, morning Testosterone (in men).
* **Renal/Hepatic:** Cr, Urea, eGFR, LFTs (essential before starting bisphosphonates).
* **Myeloma Screen:** CBC, ESR, SPEP (Serum Protein Electrophoresis) if atypical fractures or severe osteoporosis.

### Imaging: Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry (DEXA) (雙能量X光吸收測量儀)
* **Modality of Choice:** Measures Bone Mineral Density (g/cm²).
* **Target Sites:** Lumbar spine (L1-L4) and Proximal Femur (Total hip and Femoral neck).
* **Distal 1/3 Radius:** Measured if spine/hip cannot be interpreted (e.g., severe osteoarthritis, implants) or in hyperparathyroidism.

### WHO Diagnostic Criteria for Osteoporosis (Based on DEXA)

| Diagnostic Category | T-score criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | $\ge$ -1.0 |
| **Osteopenia (Low Bone Mass)** | Between -1.0 and -2.5 |
| **Osteoporosis** | $\le$ -2.5 |
| **Severe Osteoporosis** | $\le$ -2.5 PLUS $\ge$1 fragility fracture |

### Bone Turnover Markers (BTMs)
* **Resorption Marker:** Serum CTX (C-terminal telopeptide of type 1 collagen).
* **Formation Marker:** Serum P1NP (Procollagen type 1 N-terminal propeptide).
* **Utility:** NOT for diagnosis. Used to monitor early response to therapy (e.g., ↓ CTX at 3 months confirms anti-resorptive compliance/efficacy) or assess risk before drug holidays.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Medical Management of Vertebral Crush Fractures
* **Analgesia:** Scheduled paracetamol + weak opioids (e.g., Tramadol). Avoid NSAIDs if frail/renal impairment.
* **Calcitonin (Miacalcic):** Short-term use (SC/IM) for 2-4 weeks specifically for analgesic effect in acute osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures.
* **Mobilization:** Early mobilization with physiotherapy to prevent further bone loss from bed rest; avoid prolonged immobilization.

### Indications for Acute Interventional Procedures
* **Vertebroplasty / Kyphoplasty:** Injection of PMMA bone cement into the collapsed vertebral body.
* **Indications:** Refractory, severe focal back pain lasting >3-4 weeks despite optimal medical therapy, without neurological compromise.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Lifestyle Modifications & Baseline Supplementation
* **Diet & Exercise:** Weight-bearing impact exercise, smoking cessation, limit alcohol.
* **Calcium Supplementation:** Target total intake (diet + supplements) of **1000-1200 mg/day**. (e.g., Calcium Carbonate takes with meals).
* **Vitamin D Supplementation:** Target **800-1000 IU/day** (Cholecalciferol) to maintain serum 25-OH Vit D >75 nmol/L.

### First-Line Pharmacological Algorithm (Anti-Resorptives)
* **Oral Bisphosphonates (雙磷酸鹽):** First-line for postmenopausal & male osteoporosis.
  * **Alendronate (Fosamax):** 70mg PO once weekly.
  * **Risedronate (Actonel):** 35mg PO once weekly or 150mg once monthly.
  * **Administration Rules:** Take fasting on waking with full glass of plain water. Remain strictly upright for 30-60 mins and wait 30 mins before food/other meds to prevent esophagitis and ensure absorption.
* **IV Bisphosphonates:** For PO intolerance, malabsorption, or cognitive impairment.
  * **Zoledronic Acid (Aclasta):** 5mg IV infusion once YEARLY. Ensure adequate hydration.

### Second-Line & Advanced Targeted Therapies
* **Denosumab (Prolia) (RANKL Inhibitor):**
  * **Dose:** 60mg SC every 6 months.
  * **Indications:** First-line alternative, especially useful in severe osteoporosis or severe CKD (eGFR <30) where bisphosphonates are contraindicated.
* **Teriparatide (Forteo) (Anabolic Agent / Recombinant PTH):**
  * **Mechanism:** Intermittent exogenous PTH uniquely stimulates osteoblasts > osteoclasts.
  * **Dose:** 20mcg SC daily for MAX 24 months.
  * **Indications:** Severe osteoporosis (T-score < -3.0 with multiple fractures) or failed bisphosphonates. Must follow with an anti-resorptive.
* **Romosozumab (Evenity) (Sclerostin Inhibitor):**
  * **Mechanism:** Dual effect (↑ bone formation and ↓ bone resorption). Given SC monthly for 12 months.

### Bisphosphonate Drug Holiday Protocol
* **Rationale:** Mitigate risk of Atypical Femoral Fractures (AFF) and Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ) from over-suppression of bone turnover.
* **Reassessment Timeline:** Assess after 5 years of PO (or 3 years of IV) bisphosphonates.
* **High Risk (Continue Rx):** Previous hip/vertebral fracture, T-score still $\le$ -2.5, or active oral steroids → Continue therapy up to 10 years.
* **Low/Moderate Risk (Drug Holiday):** T-score > -2.5 and no recent fractures → Stop drug for 2-3 years, monitor BMD/BTMs, and restart if density drops or fracture occurs.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### FRAX Score (Fracture Risk Assessment Tool) - Hong Kong Version
* **Function:** Validated algorithm calculating the **10-year probability** of:
  1. Major Osteoporotic Fracture (Hip, spine, forearm, humerus).
  2. Hip Fracture.
* **Inputs:** Age, Sex, Weight, Height, Previous Fracture, Parent Hip Fracture, Current Smoking, Steroids, Rheumatoid Arthritis, Secondary Osteoporosis, Alcohol $\ge$3 units/day, Femoral Neck BMD.
* **Treatment Thresholds (HK Guidelines):** Initiate treatment if 10-year probability of Major Fracture $\ge$20% OR Hip Fracture $\ge$3%.

### OSTA (Osteoporosis Self-Assessment Tool for Asians)
* **Formula:** (Weight in kg - Age in years) $\times$ 0.2.
* **Utility:** Simple screening tool for Asian women.
* **Cut-offs:** High risk (< -4) → Refer for DEXA. Low risk (> -1) → Reassure.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications
* **Morbidity from Fractures:** Hip fractures (DVT/PE risk, pneumonia, decubitus ulcers from immobility), Colles' fractures (loss of independence).
* **Restrictive Lung Disease:** Severe progressive thoracic kyphosis ↓ thoracic volume → restrictive ventilatory defect.
* **Gastrointestinal:** Severe kyphosis reducing abdominal space → early satiety, abdominal pain.

### Adverse Effects of Anti-Resorptive Agents (Bisphosphonates / Denosumab)
* **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Exposed, non-healing necrotic jaw bone. Risk ↑ with IV bisphosphonates, dental extractions, and poor oral hygiene. (Dental clearance required before starting IV Zoledronate/Denosumab).
* **Atypical Femoral Fractures (AFF):** Subtrochanteric or diaphyseal femur fractures with minimal trauma after long-term (>5 yrs) suppression of bone turnover. Prodrome: lateral thigh/groin pain.
* **PO Bisphosphonates Specific:** Erosive esophagitis, esophageal strictures.
* **IV Zoledronic Acid Specific:** Acute-phase reaction (fever, myalgia, flu-like symptoms 24-48 hrs post-infusion; prevent with Paracetamol).
* **Denosumab Specific:** Severe hypocalcemia (especially in CKD/renal failure if Vit D is not repleted prior). Rebound vertebral fractures upon missed doses.

### Adverse Effects of Anabolic Agents
* **Teriparatide:** Hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria. Black box warning for Osteosarcoma (contraindicated in Paget's disease, prior skeletal radiation, or unexplained ↑ ALP).
* **Romosozumab:** ↑ Risk of Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE). Contraindicated in patients with MI or stroke in the preceding 12 months.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the significance of a Z-score versus a T-score on a DEXA scan? Answer: A T-score compares the patient's BMD to a healthy young adult (used for diagnosing osteoporosis in postmenopausal women and men >50). A Z-score compares BMD to age-matched peers; a Z-score $\le$ -2.0 in premenopausal women or younger men prompts rigorous investigation for secondary causes.
* Trap: Assuming normal serum calcium and alkaline phosphatase rules out clinically significant osteoporosis. Pearl: Primary osteoporosis typically has completely normal bone biochemistry (Ca, PO4, ALP, PTH). If calcium or ALP are abnormal, immediately suspect secondary metabolic bone diseases like osteomalacia, hyperparathyroidism, or malignancy.
* Pitfall: Prescribing a "drug holiday" for a patient on Denosumab after 5 years of treatment. Denosumab is not incorporated into the bone matrix like bisphosphonates. Discontinuation leads to a rapid, severe rebound increase in bone resorption, resulting in rapid BMD loss and multiple spontaneous vertebral fractures. If Denosumab must be stopped, immediately transition the patient to a bisphosphonate for at least 1-2 years to lock in the bone density gains.
* Viva Question: Why must you check renal function before prescribing IV Zoledronic acid? Answer: Zoledronic acid is nephrotoxic and strictly contraindicated in patients with an eGFR <35 mL/min. Denosumab should be used instead for severe renal impairment.
* Trap: Reassuring a patient that their lumbar spine T-score of -1.5 (Osteopenia) means they don't need treatment, despite a history of a Colles' fracture from a standing height fall. Pearl: A clinical diagnosis of severe osteoporosis is made in ANY patient who sustains a fragility fracture, regardless of their DEXA T-score. They require pharmacological treatment immediately.
