# Inherited Thrombophilia (遺傳性血栓形成傾向)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Inherited Thrombophilia
* **Inherited Thrombophilia (遺傳性血栓形成傾向):** Genetically determined hypercoagulable state predisposing patients to premature or recurrent Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) (靜脈血栓栓塞).

### Major Etiologies of Inherited Thrombophilia
* **Factor V Leiden (FVL) (第五凝血因子萊頓變異):** Point mutation (G1691A) causing activated Protein C (aPC) resistance.
* **Prothrombin G20210A Mutation (凝血酶原基因突變):** Point mutation causing ↑ circulating prothrombin.
* **Protein C (PC) Deficiency (C蛋白缺乏症):** ↓ quantity or function of natural anticoagulant.
* **Protein S (PS) Deficiency (S蛋白缺乏症):** ↓ free PS (cofactor for PC).
* **Antithrombin (AT) Deficiency (抗凝血酶缺乏症):** ↓ inhibition of thrombin and Factor Xa.

### Epidemiology of Thrombophilia in Hong Kong
* **Caucasian Bias:** FVL and Prothrombin G20210A are the most common inherited causes in Caucasians (approx 5% carrier rate).
* **Hong Kong Chinese Epidemiology:** FVL and Prothrombin mutations are **extremely rare** (<1%) in the local HK/Asian population.
* **Local Predominance:** PC, PS, and AT deficiencies account for the vast majority of inherited thrombophilia cases in Hong Kong Chinese.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Factor V Leiden & Prothrombin Mutation
* **Factor V Leiden:** Normal Factor Va is inactivated by cleavage via aPC. FVL mutation alters the cleavage site → FVL resists degradation → prolonged procoagulant drive (aPC resistance).
* **Prothrombin G20210A:** Mutation in 3' untranslated region of the prothrombin gene → ↑ mRNA stability → ↑ Prothrombin (Factor II) levels by 30% → ↑ thrombin generation.

### Pathophysiology of Protein C, Protein S & Antithrombin Deficiencies
* **Protein C & S:** Thrombin binds thrombomodulin → activates PC. aPC + free PS (cofactor) → inactivates Factors Va and VIIIa. Deficiency → uninhibited Va/VIIIa → unregulated coagulation cascade.
* **Antithrombin:** Natural inhibitor of serine proteases (Factor IIa/Thrombin, Xa, IXa, XIa, XIIa). Heparin normally binds AT to accelerate its inhibitory action 1000-fold. Deficiency → unregulated thrombin/Xa and **heparin resistance**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Clues Indicating Inherited Thrombophilia
* **Age:** Unprovoked VTE at young age (<45-50 years).
* **Family History:** Strong first-degree relative Hx of unprovoked VTE.
* **Recurrence:** Recurrent unprovoked DVT/PE.
* **Atypical Sites:** VTE in unusual vasculature (e.g., cerebral venous sinus, mesenteric, portal, or hepatic veins [Budd-Chiari syndrome]).
* **Obstetric Hx:** Recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL) (習慣性流產), especially 2nd/3rd trimester, severe early-onset pre-eclampsia.
* **Triggers:** VTE provoked by weak/minor risk factors (e.g., short-haul flight, Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill [COCP] use, pregnancy).

### Physical Examination Findings in Inherited Thrombophilia
* **General:** Often entirely normal unless acute clot or complication is present.
* **Vascular Signs:** Unilateral limb swelling, erythema, warmth, pitting edema, distended superficial collaterals (signs of DVT).
* **Dermatological Signs:** 
* **Warfarin-induced skin necrosis (華法林引起的皮膚壞死):** Erythematous/purpuric lesions rapidly progressing to necrotic eschars on breasts, thighs, or buttocks.
* **Purpura fulminans (暴發性紫癜):** Extensive microvascular thrombosis and hemorrhagic skin necrosis (seen in homozygous PC/PS deficiency in neonates).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Hypercoagulable States
* **Acquired Thrombophilia:** Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS) (抗磷脂綜合症).
* **Malignancy:** Trousseau syndrome (migratory thrombophlebitis), solid tumors.
* **Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (MPN):** Polycythemia Vera, Essential Thrombocythemia (JAK2 V617F +ve).
* **Renal:** Nephrotic syndrome (urinary loss of Antithrombin III).
* **Hematological:** Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH) (CD55/CD59 -ve).

### Discriminatory Table: Inherited vs Acquired Hypercoagulable States
| Feature | Inherited Thrombophilia | Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS) | Malignancy-Associated |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Typical Clot Type** | Venous (Arterial is rare) | Venous **AND** Arterial | Venous (often recurrent/migratory) |
| **Family History** | Often +ve | Usually -ve | Usually -ve |
| **Lab Markers** | Genetic mutations, ↓ PC/PS/AT | +ve Lupus Anticoagulant, aCL, anti-β2GPI | ↑ Tumor markers, anemia, abnormal imaging |
| **Systemic Signs** | None (pure clotting disorder) | Livedo reticularis, SLE features | Weight loss, cachexia, night sweats |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Indications for Thrombophilia Testing
* **Targeted Testing ONLY:** Do NOT screen unselected patients with VTE.
* **Test Indications:** Unprovoked VTE age <50, VTE in atypical sites, recurrent unprovoked VTE, VTE + strong FHx, females planning pregnancy with prior VTE or strong FHx.

### Blood Investigations for Inherited Thrombophilia
* **Genetic Assays (PCR):** FVL (G1691A) mutation analysis, Prothrombin (G20210A) mutation analysis. (Unaffected by acute clot or anticoagulants).
* **Functional Phenotypic Assays:**
* **Protein C Activity:** Clotting or chromogenic assay.
* **Protein S:** Free Protein S antigen level AND Protein S activity.
* **Antithrombin Activity:** Heparin cofactor assay.

### Confounding Factors in Thrombophilia Testing (CRITICAL)
| Clinical State/Drug | Effect on Thrombophilia Testing | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Thrombosis** | ↓ PC, ↓ PS, ↓ AT (consumptive) | Delay testing until 2-4 weeks post-acute event |
| **Warfarin (Vitamin K antagonist)** | ↓ PC, ↓ PS (Vitamin K dependent) | Stop Warfarin x 2-4 weeks before testing |
| **Heparin (UFH/LMWH)** | ↓ AT activity | Do not test AT while on Heparin |
| **DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban)** | False +ve/-ve in functional assays | Stop DOAC x 2-4 weeks before testing |
| **Pregnancy / COCP** | ↓ Free PS (physiologic) | Retest PS >6 weeks post-partum/stopping COCP |
| **Liver Disease / DIC** | ↓ PC, ↓ PS, ↓ AT (↓ synthesis / ↑ consumption) | Interpret with extreme caution |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Warfarin-Induced Skin Necrosis
* **Immediate Cessation:** Stop Warfarin immediately.
* **Reversal:** Administer IV Vitamin K to reverse residual Warfarin effect.
* **Alternative Anticoagulation:** Commence IV Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) or therapeutic LMWH.
* **Factor Replacement:** Administer Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) or highly purified Protein C concentrate to rapidly replenish Protein C levels.

### Acute Management of Purpura Fulminans (Neonates)
* **Immediate Replacement:** IV Protein C concentrate or FFP.
* **Anticoagulation:** Systemic anticoagulation.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of Asymptomatic Carriers
* **Routine Prophylaxis:** NO long-term pharmacological anticoagulation indicated.
* **Situational Prophylaxis:** Prescribe LMWH prophylaxis during high-risk transient periods (e.g., long-haul flights >8 hrs, post-operative state, trauma).
* **Lifestyle:** Avoid smoking, maintain optimal BMI, regular mobilization.

### Anticoagulation Algorithm for VTE in Inherited Thrombophilia
* **First Provoked VTE:** Standard treatment (3-6 months DOAC/Warfarin), then stop.
* **First Unprovoked VTE (Low/Mod Risk Defect):** Standard treatment (3-6 months). Consider indefinite therapy based on patient preference and bleeding risk (HAS-BLED).
* **First Unprovoked VTE (High Risk Defect - e.g., AT deficiency):** Strongly consider lifelong/indefinite anticoagulation.
* **Recurrent VTE:** Indefinite/lifelong anticoagulation (DOAC or Warfarin).

### Pregnancy and Estrogen Management in Thrombophilia
* **Contraception / HRT:** Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCP) and systemic estrogen HRT are **ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED**. Recommend Progesterone-only pill (POP), Mirena IUS, or copper IUD.
* **Pregnancy Management (Asymptomatic High-Risk Defect or Prior VTE):**
* **Antepartum:** Prophylactic LMWH (e.g., Enoxaparin) throughout pregnancy.
* **Postpartum:** Continue LMWH for 6 weeks post-partum (highest risk period).
* **Delivery:** Stop LMWH 12-24 hours prior to planned induction/neuraxial anesthesia.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### VTE Risk Stratification by Inherited Defect
| Defect Type | Relative Risk of 1st VTE | Risk Stratification Classification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Heterozygous FVL** | 5-7x | Low / Moderate Risk |
| **Heterozygous Prothrombin** | 3-4x | Low / Moderate Risk |
| **Protein C / S Deficiency** | 10-15x | Moderate / High Risk |
| **Antithrombin Deficiency** | 20-50x | **High Risk** |
| **Homozygous FVL / Prothrombin** | 50-80x | **High Risk** |
| **Compound Heterozygotes** | >20x | **High Risk** |

### Prognosis of Inherited Thrombophilia
* **Morbidity:** High lifetime risk of recurrent VTE, especially in high-risk groups.
* **Mortality:** Lethality usually secondary to massive PE. Homozygous Antithrombin deficiency is typically incompatible with life (fetal demise in utero).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Pathological Complications of Recurrent VTE
* **Post-Thrombotic Syndrome (PTS) (創傷後血栓綜合症):** Chronic venous hypertension → leg swelling, pain, venous claudication, stasis dermatitis, and venous ulceration.
* **Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension (CTEPH) (慢性血栓栓塞性肺動脈高壓):** Fibrotic organized clots in pulmonary arteries → progressive right heart failure (Cor Pulmonale).
* **Obstetric Complications:** Placental thrombosis → intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR), recurrent miscarriage, placental abruption, severe pre-eclampsia.

### Drug Adverse Effects in Thrombophilia Management
* **Warfarin-Induced Skin Necrosis:** PC has a shorter half-life (8 hrs) than procoagulant Factors II, IX, X. Warfarin causes rapid PC depletion → transient severe hypercoagulable state before therapeutic anticoagulation is achieved. High risk in underlying PC deficiency.
* **Heparin Resistance:** Inability to achieve therapeutic aPTT with standard UFH doses. Caused by Antithrombin deficiency (Heparin lacks its necessary cofactor).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: A 25-year-old woman is admitted with acute DVT. The intern sends off a full thrombophilia screen including PC, PS, and AT levels. Why is this inappropriate? Answer: Acute thrombosis consumes PC, PS, and AT, leading to false-positive deficiencies. Heparin therapy also falsely lowers AT levels. Testing must be delayed 2-4 weeks after stopping anticoagulation.
* Trap: Prescribing Warfarin monotherapy on day 1 for a patient with newly diagnosed DVT and known Protein C deficiency. Pearl: Always overlap Warfarin with LMWH/UFH for at least 5 days AND until INR > 2.0 to prevent Warfarin-induced skin necrosis, as Protein C drops rapidly causing a transient paradoxical hypercoagulable state.
* Viva Question: A patient on an intravenous Heparin infusion for massive PE fails to reach therapeutic aPTT despite massive dose escalations. What is the underlying inherited defect? Answer: Antithrombin (AT) deficiency. Heparin works by potentiating AT; without AT, Heparin is ineffective (Heparin resistance).
* Pitfall: Diagnosing Protein S deficiency in a pregnant patient. Pregnancy causes a physiological drop in free Protein S levels, making tests unreliable until at least 6 weeks postpartum.
* Viva Question: Why do we rarely test for Factor V Leiden and Prothrombin G20210A in Hong Kong Chinese patients presenting with unprovoked VTE? Answer: Because these specific genetic mutations are almost exclusively found in Caucasian populations and are exceptionally rare (<1%) in ethnic Chinese.
