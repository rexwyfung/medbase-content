# Atypical & Unculturable Bacteria (非典型及不可培養細菌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Atypical Bacteria
* **Atypical Nature:** Cannot be isolated on standard culture media (e.g., blood/MacConkey agar) AND/OR do not stain with conventional Gram stain.
* **Primary Reasons:**
  * Lack peptidoglycan cell wall (e.g., *Mycoplasma*).
  * Obligate intracellular organisms (require host ATP/machinery; e.g., *Chlamydia*, *Rickettsia*).
  * Too thin/fastidious (e.g., *Treponema*).
* **Clinical Consequence:** Intrinsically resistant to cell-wall inhibitors (β-lactams). Require antibiotics targeting protein synthesis (Ribosome 30S/50S) or DNA replication.

### Major Bacterial Families & Pathogens
* **Cell Wall Deficient:** *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* (黴漿菌), *Ureaplasma*.
* **Obligate Intracellular:** 
  * *Chlamydia spp.* (披衣菌): *C. trachomatis*, *C. pneumoniae*, *C. psittaci*.
  * *Rickettsiaceae*: *Rickettsia rickettsii*, *Orientia tsutsugamushi* (Scrub typhus/叢林斑疹傷寒).
  * *Coxiella burnetii* (Q fever/Q熱).
* **Spirochetes (螺旋體):** *Treponema pallidum* (Syphilis/梅毒), *Leptospira interrogans* (Leptospirosis/鉤端螺旋體病), *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme disease/萊姆病).

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong Context)
* **Scrub Typhus:** Endemic in HK; ↑ incidence in autumn (hikers exposed to chiggers in scrubby vegetation/country parks).
* **Leptospirosis:** Associated with HK water sports, flooding, and rodent contact (wild rats).
* **Mycoplasma pneumoniae:** Epidemic cycles every 3-4 years; peak in school-aged children and young adults; increasing macrolide resistance locally.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Mycoplasma & Chlamydia
* ***Mycoplasma pneumoniae*:** Utilizes **P1 adhesion protein** to bind respiratory epithelium → produces hydrogen peroxide/superoxide → ciliostasis and epithelial necrosis. 
* **Molecular Mimicry:** *Mycoplasma* antigens resemble RBC I-antigen → triggers IgM autoantibodies → Cold Agglutinin Disease (AIHA).
* ***Chlamydia* Biphasic Life Cycle:**
  * **Elementary Body (EB):** Extracellular, enfectious (infectious) form; inert, enters via endocytosis.
  * **Reticulate Body (RB):** Intracellular, replicating form; synthesizes glycogen/proteins → reorganizes back into EBs → host cell lysis.

### Pathophysiology of Obligate Intracellular Zoonoses
* ***Rickettsia* & *Orientia*:** Transmitted via arthropod bite → tropism for vascular endothelial cells → stimulates endocytosis → escapes phagosome to replicate in cytoplasm → endothelial damage → **systemic vasculitis**, microthrombi, and vascular leakage.
* ***Coxiella burnetii*:** Inhaled via aerosolized animal fluids (placenta/feces of sheep/cattle) → phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages → thrives in highly acidic phagolysosomes → granuloma formation (liver/bone marrow). Forms spore-like variants surviving in harsh environments.

### Pathophysiology of Spirochetes
* **Spirochete Motility:** Contain **axial filaments** (endoflagella) located in periplasmic space → allows corkscrew motility through highly viscous mediums (e.g., mucus, connective tissue, CNS).
* ***Treponema pallidum*:** Lacks LPS → evades innate immunity ("stealth pathogen"); induces intense delayed-type hypersensitivity (DTH) response → obliterative endarteritis (syphilitic lesions/gummas).
* ***Leptospira*:** Penetrates abraded skin/mucosa from contaminated water → disseminates hematogenously → immune complex deposition in renal tubules (ATN) and hepatic capillaries.

## 3. Clinical Assessment 

### History Taking for Atypical Infections
* **Exposure Hx:** Hiking in HK country parks (Scrub typhus); water sports/floods/rat exposure (Leptospirosis); parrot/bird handler (*C. psittaci*); sheep/cattle abattoir worker (*Coxiella*).
* **Sexual Hx:** Unprotected intercourse, MSM, multiple partners (*T. pallidum*, *C. trachomatis*).
* **Symptom Onset:** Insidious/gradual onset of non-productive cough, low-grade fever, prominent extrapulmonary symptoms (headache, myalgia, fatigue) → points to atypical pneumonia.

### Physical Examination: Mycoplasma & Chlamydia
* ***Mycoplasma*:** Pharyngeal erythema without exudate, bullous myringitis (tympanic membrane vesicles), widespread coarse crepitations, targetoid rash (Erythema multiforme).
* ***Chlamydia trachomatis* (Reactive Arthritis triad):** Uveitis/conjunctivitis, urethritis/cervicitis, asymmetric oligoarthritis.

### Physical Examination: Rickettsial & Zoonotic
* **Scrub Typhus (*Orientia*):** **Eschar** (cigarette-burn like necrotic crust with erythematous halo; often in groin/axilla), generalized maculopapular rash, tender regional lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly.
* **Leptospirosis:** **Conjunctival suffusion** (redness without purulent exudate), severe calf/lumbar muscle tenderness, icterus (jaundice).

### Physical Examination: Spirochetes
* **Primary Syphilis:** **Chancre** (single, painless, indurated ulcer with clean base), painless rubbery regional lymphadenopathy.
* **Secondary Syphilis:** Copper-colored generalized maculopapular rash (involves palms & soles), **Condylomata lata** (highly infectious gray/white warty lesions in moist areas), patchy "moth-eaten" alopecia.
* **Tertiary Syphilis:** Gummas (destructive granulomatous lesions), Argyll Robertson pupil (accommodates but does not react to light), tabes dorsalis (sensory ataxia, broad-based gait).
* **Lyme Disease (*Borrelia*):** **Erythema migrans** (expanding target/bulls-eye rash), unilateral/bilateral facial nerve palsy.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Typical vs. Atypical Pneumonia
| Feature | Typical (e.g., *Strep. pneumoniae*) | Atypical (e.g., *Mycoplasma*, *Chlamydia*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Sudden, acute | Gradual, insidious |
| **Sputum** | Purulent / Rusty | Scanty / Mucoid / Dry cough |
| **Systemic Features** | Rigors, pleuritic chest pain | Headache, prominent myalgia, GI symptoms |
| **WCC Count** | Neutrophilia (prominent) | Normal or slightly elevated |

### Differential Diagnoses of Maculopapular Rash involving Palms & Soles
* **Secondary Syphilis:** Co-existing generalized lymphadenopathy, preceding painless chancre.
* **Scrub Typhus / RMSF:** History of tick/mite bite, severe systemic toxicity, eschar.
* **Coxsackievirus (Hand, Foot & Mouth):** Vesicular lesions primarily, oral enanthem.
* **Drug Eruption:** Temporal relationship to new medication (e.g., Penicillin/Sulfa).

### Culture-Negative Endocarditis Discriminators
* ***Coxiella burnetii*:** Hx of livestock exposure, isolated hepatomegaly, hypergammaglobulinemia.
* ***Bartonella spp.*:** Cat scratch/flea exposure, chronic alcoholism, homeless status.
* **HACEK group:** Slowly growing Gram-negatives (often requires 2-3 weeks incubation).

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Bedside & Microscopy Tests
* **Dark-field Microscopy:** Direct visualization of corkscrew motility of *T. pallidum* from primary chancre exudate (too thin for Gram stain/light microscopy).
* **Giemsa / Wright Stain:** Can visualize *Borrelia*, *Rickettsia*, and *Chlamydia* inclusion bodies.

### Bloods & Serology (The Mainstay of Atypical Dx)
* **Cold Agglutinins:** Bedside test for *Mycoplasma* (blood sample clumps on ice, reverses at 37°C); confirmed via ↑ Reticulocytes, ↑ LDH, +ve Direct Coombs (IgM).
* ***Mycoplasma* Serology:** Paired sera showing 4-fold ↑ in IgG titer, or single +ve IgM (limited by delay in Ab production).
* **Weil-Felix Test:** Historical cross-reaction of rickettsial antibodies with *Proteus* antigens (poor sensitivity/specificity, largely replaced by IFA).
* **Indirect Immunofluorescence Assay (IFA):** Gold standard for *Orientia*, *Rickettsia*, *Coxiella*, *Leptospira*.
* **NAAT / PCR:** Highly sensitive and specific (e.g., Throat/NPA swab for *M. pneumoniae*/*C. pneumoniae*; Urine/swab for *C. trachomatis*).

### Syphilis Serology Algorithms
| Test Category | Examples | Use Case | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Treponemal** | VDRL, RPR | Screening, monitoring Rx | Detects anti-cardiolipin Abs. False +ves (SLE, pregnancy, leprosy). Titers ↓ with successful Rx. |
| **Treponemal** | TPPA, TPHA, FTA-ABS | Confirmation | Detects specific *T. pallidum* Abs. Remains +ve for life (cannot differentiate active vs past infection). |

### Imaging & Advanced Diagnostics
* **CXR (*Mycoplasma*):** Bilateral, patchy interstitial infiltrates ("worse radiologically than clinically"), small pleural effusions.
* **Echocardiogram:** Transesophageal Echo (TOE) for culture-negative endocarditis (*Coxiella*).
* **Lumbar Puncture:** For neurosyphilis → CSF VDRL +ve, ↑ mononuclear cells, ↑ protein.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Acute Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction
* **Clinical Scenario:** Fever, chills, rigor, hypotension, and exacerbation of rash occurring 2-24 hours after initiation of antibiotics for spirochete infections (Syphilis, Leptospirosis, Lyme).
* **Mechanism:** Massive release of endotoxin-like lipoproteins from dying spirochetes.
* **Acute Rx:** Supportive care (IV fluids), antipyretics (Paracetamol), NSAIDs. **DO NOT** stop antibiotics. Consider short-course corticosteroids prior to Abx ONLY in cardiovascular/neurosyphilis to prevent fatal swelling.

### Severe Leptospirosis (Weil's Disease) Resuscitation
* **Clinical Emergency:** Acute renal failure, massive pulmonary hemorrhage, severe jaundice, DIC.
* **Acute Rx:** 
  * Early intubation with low tidal volume strategy (ARDS protocol) for pulmonary hemorrhage.
  * Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT) for acute oliguric renal failure.
  * IV Penicillin G or IV Ceftriaxone (HA guidelines) initiated urgently.

### Cold Agglutinin Crisis (Mycoplasma)
* **Clinical Emergency:** Severe symptomatic autoimmune hemolytic anemia, acrocyanosis, digital ischemia.
* **Acute Rx:** Keep patient in a warm room; warm all IV fluids and blood products via blood warmer prior to transfusion; systemic corticosteroids for severe hemolysis.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Targeted Pharmacotherapy for Atypical Organisms
* ***Mycoplasma* & *Chlamydia* (Respiratory):** 
  * **1st Line:** Oral Macrolides (Azithromycin or Clarithromycin).
  * **2nd Line / Macrolide-Resistant (common in HK):** Oral Doxycycline or Fluoroquinolones (Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin).
* **Scrub Typhus (*Orientia*):**
  * **1st Line:** Oral Doxycycline (100mg BD for 7-14 days).
  * **Alternative (pregnancy/allergy):** Azithromycin.
* **Leptospirosis (Mild/Moderate):**
  * Oral Doxycycline or Amoxicillin.
* **Q Fever Endocarditis (*Coxiella*):**
  * **Chronic Rx:** Doxycycline + Hydroxychloroquine (alkalinizes phagolysosome, making Doxycycline bactericidal) for minimum 18-24 months.

### Syphilis Treatment Algorithms
* **Early Syphilis (Primary, Secondary, Early Latent <1 year):**
  * IM Benzathine Penicillin G (Single dose, 2.4 million units).
  * Penicillin-allergic: Oral Doxycycline x 14 days (except in pregnancy → must admit for penicillin desensitization).
* **Late Latent (>1 year) or Unknown Duration:**
  * IM Benzathine Penicillin G (Weekly for 3 weeks).
* **Neurosyphilis / Ocular Syphilis:**
  * IV Aqueous Penicillin G (Continuous infusion or 4-hourly for 10-14 days).

### Post-Therapy Monitoring (Syphilis)
* **Chronic Algorithm:** Repeat VDRL/RPR at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months.
* **Successful Rx criteria:** ≥4-fold decline in non-treponemal titer (e.g., 1:32 → 1:8).
* **Treatment failure/Re-infection:** Failure of titer to fall 4-fold, or a 4-fold increase → requires retreatment and CSF examination.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Syphilis Stage Progression & Prognosis
| Stage | Timeline | Infectivity | Prognosis without Rx |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary** | 2-3 weeks post-exposure | High | Spontaneous healing of chancre (3-6 wks), progresses to 2° |
| **Secondary** | 4-10 weeks post-chancre | High | Resolves spontaneously (1-6 mos), enters latency |
| **Latent** | Early (<1 yr); Late (>1 yr) | Early: Low; Late: Non-infectious | 30% progress to Tertiary; 70% remain asymptomatic |
| **Tertiary** | 1-30 years post-exposure | Non-infectious | Irreversible end-organ damage (CV, Neuro) |

### Severity Predictors for Scrub Typhus
* **Poor Prognostic Markers:** Advanced age, absence of eschar (leads to delayed clinical recognition), elevated AST/ALT >3x upper limit, acute kidney injury (creatinine >150 µmol/L), delayed initiation of Doxycycline (>5 days of fever).

### Atypical Pneumonia Severity (SMART-COP)
* Used to predict need for intensive respiratory or vasopressor support (IRVS):
  * **S**ystolic BP <90 mmHg
  * **M**ultilobar involvement
  * **A**lbumin <35 g/L
  * **R**espiratory rate adjusted for age
  * **T**achycardia ≥125 bpm
  * **C**onfusion
  * **O**xygen level (low pO2)
  * **P**H < 7.35

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* Extrapulmonary Complications
* **Neurological:** Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS), aseptic meningitis, transverse myelitis.
* **Hematological:** Cold Agglutinin Disease (IgM AIHA).
* **Dermatological:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Erythema multiforme, toxic epidermal necrolysis.
* **Cardiac:** Myocarditis, pericarditis.

### *Chlamydia trachomatis* Complications
* **Genitourinary:** Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) → tubal scarring → ectopic pregnancy & infertility.
* **Hepatic:** Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome (perihepatitis causing RUQ pain with "violin string" adhesions).
* **Systemic:** Reactive arthritis (formerly Reiter's syndrome) → cross-reactivity with HLA-B27.

### Spirochetal Complications
* **Syphilis (Tertiary):**
  * **Cardiovascular:** Aortitis affecting the vasa vasorum → ascending aortic aneurysm, aortic regurgitation.
  * **Neurological:** Tabes dorsalis (demyelination of posterior columns/roots), general paresis (dementia, personality changes).
* **Lyme Disease (*Borrelia*):** Chronic Lyme arthritis, third-degree AV block.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why are Beta-lactams completely ineffective against *Mycoplasma*? Answer: Beta-lactams inhibit peptidoglycan cross-linking in the cell wall, but *Mycoplasma* inherently lacks a cell wall (its membrane contains sterols instead).
* Viva Question: A hiker presents with fever, a black eschar on the groin, and a generalized rash. What is the diagnosis and the definitive treatment? Answer: Scrub typhus (*Orientia tsutsugamushi*); empirical therapy is Doxycycline.
* Trap: Presuming a positive VDRL is definitive for Syphilis. Pearl: VDRL is a non-treponemal test that detects anti-cardiolipin antibodies. It is highly sensitive but prone to biological false positives (e.g., SLE, Anti-phospholipid syndrome, pregnancy). It MUST be confirmed with a specific Treponemal test (e.g., TPPA).
* Pitfall: Stopping antibiotics in a patient who develops rigors and fever 4 hours after IM Penicillin for Syphilis. This is the Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (toxin release from dying spirochetes), NOT a penicillin allergy. Treat symptomatically with antipyretics and continue the antibiotic course.
* Trap: Misinterpreting an active syphilis infection using a Treponemal test. Pearl: Treponemal tests (TPPA, FTA-ABS) remain positive for life even after successful treatment. You must use the non-treponemal titer (VDRL/RPR) to assess disease activity and treatment response.
* Viva Question: Which atypical bacteria causes Q-fever and is a known etiology for culture-negative endocarditis? Answer: *Coxiella burnetii*.
