# Gastrointestinal Bleeding (消化道出血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Upper vs Lower Gastrointestinal Bleeding
* **Upper GI Bleed (UGIB) (上消化道出血):** Bleeding originating proximal to the Ligament of Treitz (十二指腸懸韌帶) at the duodenojejunal flexure.
* **Lower GI Bleed (LGIB) (下消化道出血):** Bleeding originating distal to the Ligament of Treitz.
* **Obscure GI Bleeding (OGIB) (不明原因消化道出血):** Recurrent or persistent bleeding (overt or occult) with negative initial esophagogastroduodenoscopy (OGD) and colonoscopy. Usually arises from the small bowel.

### Etiologies of Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding (UGIB)
* **Non-Variceal (80%):**
  * Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD) (消化性潰瘍) (most common)
  * Gastroduodenal erosions / Severe gastritis
  * Mallory-Weiss Tear (食道賁門黏膜撕裂症候群)
  * Esophagitis (食道炎)
  * Upper GI Malignancy (e.g., Gastric Carcinoma)
  * Dieulafoy's Lesion (杜氏病)
  * Angiodysplasia (血管發育不良)
* **Variceal (20%):**
  * Esophageal Varices (食道靜脈曲張)
  * Gastric Varices (胃靜脈曲張)

### Etiologies of Lower Gastrointestinal Bleeding (LGIB)
* Diverticular Disease (憩室病) (most common)
* Hemorrhoids (痔瘡) / Anal Fissures
* Ischemic Colitis (缺血性結腸炎)
* Colorectal Carcinoma (大腸癌) & Polyps
* Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)
* Angiodysplasia (more common in right colon)
* Post-polypectomy bleed

### Epidemiology & Risk Factors in Hong Kong
* UGIB incidence: ~100-150 per 100,000 adults annually.
* Major precipitants: Aging population, NSAID/Aspirin use, widespread use of Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs), Endemic Hepatitis B (B型肝炎) causing Cirrhosis (肝硬化).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Portal Hypertension & Variceal Bleeding
* Cirrhosis → ↑ Hepatic vascular resistance + ↑ Splanchnic vasodilation.
* Portal pressure gradient (HVPG) > 10 mmHg → Formation of portosystemic collaterals.
* HVPG > 12 mmHg → High risk of variceal rupture.
* Wall tension > vessel elasticity → Rupture → Massive hemorrhage.

### Pathophysiology of Non-Variceal Bleeding
* **Mucosal Injury:** Imbalance between protective factors (mucus, bicarbonate, prostaglandins) and destructive factors (acid, pepsin, NSAIDs, ischemia).
* **Vascular Anomalies:** Angiodysplasia (submucosal arteriovenous malformations) → fragile, prone to shear stress and rupture.
* **Mallory-Weiss:** Severe retching/vomiting → sudden ↑ intragastric pressure → longitudinal mucosal tear at gastroesophageal junction.

### Hemodynamic Cascade in Severe Hemorrhage
* Blood loss → ↓ Venous return → ↓ Cardiac output → ↓ Tissue perfusion.
* Compensatory mechanisms: ↑ Sympathetic tone (tachycardia, vasoconstriction) + Activation of RAAS (fluid retention).
* Decompensation → Hemorrhagic shock (出血性休克) → Tissue hypoxia, lactic acidosis, multiorgan failure.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Features of Bleeding Presentation
* **Hematemesis (吐血):** Fresh red blood (active upper bleed) vs "Coffee-ground" (altered blood, indicates slower upper bleed).
* **Melena (黑糞):** Tarry, foul-smelling black stool. Typically UGIB (requires ~50-100mL blood and >14h gut transit), but can be right-sided LGIB.
* **Hematochezia (便血):** Fresh blood per rectum. Typically LGIB, but can occur in massive, rapid UGIB (usually accompanied by hemodynamic instability).
* **Associated Symptoms:** Syncope (暈厥), presyncope, diaphoresis, chest pain/angina (ischemia secondary to anemia).

### Historical Risk Factors & Drug Review
* Liver disease history (Hepatitis B/C, alcohol, jaundice).
* Previous GI bleed, known PUD, or prior abdominal surgeries (e.g., AAA repair → Aortoenteric fistula).
* Medications: Aspirin, NSAIDs, Clopidogrel, Warfarin, DOACs, systemic steroids.

### System-Specific Physical Signs
* **Vitals:** Postural/Orthostatic hypotension (>20 mmHg drop in systolic BP or >10 bpm ↑ in HR upon standing), resting tachycardia, delayed capillary refill.
* **Abdominal Exam:** Epigastric tenderness (PUD), hepatosplenomegaly (portal hypertension), ascites (shifting dullness).
* **Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease:** Spider nevi (蜘蛛痣), palmar erythema, jaundice, caput medusae, gynecomastia, asterixis.
* **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE) (直腸指檢):** Mandatory to confirm presence of melena, hematochezia, or palpable rectal mass. Inspect for perianal disease (hemorrhoids).
* **Skin/Mucosa:** Pallor (palmar creases, conjunctivae), mucocutaneous telangiectasias (Osler-Weber-Rendu syndrome), pigmentation (Peutz-Jeghers syndrome).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Upper vs Lower GI Bleed
* **UGIB:** Hematemesis/coffee-ground +ve, Melena typical. Hemodynamic instability more common.
* **LGIB:** Hematochezia typical. Hematemesis absent. Melena rare (unless slow right colon bleed).

### Differentiating True vs Spurious GI Bleeding
* **Iron Supplements:** Stools are dark/black but not tarry or foul-smelling. DRE negative for true melena.
* **Bismuth Subsalicylate:** Causes black stool, benign.
* **Dietary:** Beetroot (red stool/urine), liquorice, blueberries, squid ink.

### Extra-Gastrointestinal Sources of Bleeding
* **Hemoptysis (咳血):** Swallowed blood from respiratory tract presenting as hematemesis/melena. Clues: frothy blood, history of chronic cough/lung disease.
* **Epistaxis (鼻出血):** Swallowed nasopharyngeal blood. Check posterior pharynx.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Rule out secondary myocardial ischemia (Type 2 MI) induced by profound anemia/tachycardia.
* **Nasogastric Aspirate:** Rarely used now, but blood/coffee grounds confirm UGIB; clear bile does not definitively rule out UGIB (duodenal bulb spasm).

### Laboratory Blood Investigations
* **Complete Blood Profile (CBP):** Serial Hb (may initially be normal before hemodilution), Platelets (thrombocytopenia in cirrhosis/hypersplenism).
* **Renal Function Test (RFT):** ↑ Urea:Creatinine ratio (>100:1 when measured in mmol/L suggests UGIB due to breakdown of blood proteins in gut and hypovolemia).
* **Liver Function Test (LFT):** Albumin, Bilirubin, ALT/AST to assess underlying liver disease.
* **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR/APTT):** Baseline for coagulopathy (liver disease or anticoagulants).
* **Type & Screen (T&S) / Crossmatch (GXM):** Crucial for prompt transfusion (prepare 2-4 units depending on severity).

### Endoscopic & Imaging Modalities
* **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (OGD) (胃鏡):** Gold standard for UGIB.
  * *Timing:* <12 hours for suspected variceal/high-risk; <24 hours for stable non-variceal.
* **Colonoscopy (大腸鏡):** First-line for stable LGIB after adequate bowel prep.
* **CT Mesenteric Angiography (CTMA):** Used for massive LGIB or obscure bleed. Requires active bleeding rate >0.5 mL/min.
* **Conventional Angiography:** Diagnostic and therapeutic (embolization). Requires bleeding rate >0.5-1.0 mL/min.
* **Radionuclide Scintigraphy (RBC Scan):** Can detect very slow bleeds (0.1 mL/min) but poor anatomic localization.
* **Capsule Endoscopy (PillCam) (膠囊內視鏡):** Investigation of choice for Obscure GI Bleeding (OGIB) to visualize small bowel.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Resuscitation & Transfusion Thresholds
* **Airway/Breathing:** Intubation (氣管插管) if massive hematemesis with altered mental status (prevent aspiration).
* **Circulation:** 2x large-bore IV access (16G or 18G). Fluid resuscitation with crystalloids (Normal Saline or Plasmalyte).
* **Transfusion Targets (Restrictive Strategy):**
  * Healthy adults: Transfuse if Hb < 7 g/dL (Target 7-9 g/dL).
  * High-risk (IHD, stable CVD): Transfuse if Hb < 8 g/dL (Target 8-10 g/dL).
  * Suspected Variceal: STRICTLY maintain Hb 7-8 g/dL (Over-transfusion ↑ portal pressure → rebleeding).
* **Coagulopathy Correction:** Target Platelets > 50 x 10^9/L. Correct INR < 1.5 if actively bleeding (PCC for Warfarin, Idarucizumab for Dabigatran).

### Acute Variceal Bleeding Protocol
* **Vasoactive Agents:** IV Terlipressin (特利加壓素) 2mg Q4H (or IV Octreotide/Somatostatin). Give immediately upon suspicion, before OGD. Maintain for 3-5 days.
* **Prophylactic Antibiotics:** IV Ceftriaxone (頭孢曲松) 1g daily for max 7 days. Significantly ↓ mortality and rebleeding.
* **Endotherapy:** OGD with Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL) (靜脈曲張結紮術) / Banding. Cyanoacrylate glue injection for gastric varices.
* **Rescue Therapy:** Balloon tamponade (Sengstaken-Blakemore tube) as bridge (max 24h). Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt (TIPS) (經頸靜脈肝內門體靜脈支架分流術).

### Acute Non-Variceal Bleeding Protocol
* **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPI):** IV Pantoprazole or Esomeprazole (80mg bolus → 8mg/h continuous infusion for 72h) if high-risk stigmata found on OGD. High-dose oral PPI (e.g., 40mg BID) is an alternative for stable patients in HK HA.
* **Pre-Endoscopic Prokinetics:** IV Erythromycin 250mg or IV Metoclopramide 10mg 30-120 mins pre-OGD to clear stomach of clots (optional, case-by-case).
* **Endotherapy:** Dual therapy preferred (e.g., Adrenaline injection + thermal coagulation/Hemoclip).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of Portal Hypertension
* **Primary/Secondary Prophylaxis of Varices:**
  * Non-Selective Beta-Blockers (NSBB): Oral Propranolol (普萘洛爾) or Carvedilol (卡維地洛). Titrate to resting HR 55-60 bpm.
  * Repeated EVL sessions every 2-4 weeks until varices are obliterated.
  * Contraindications to NSBB: Refractory ascites (sometimes), severe asthma, severe hypotension.

### Follow-up for Obscure Bleeding & Iron Deficiency
* If OGD and colonoscopy are negative, but occult bleeding persists (e.g., unexplained Iron Deficiency Anemia):
  * **Step 1:** Video Capsule Endoscopy (VCE).
  * **Step 2:** Device-assisted enteroscopy (e.g., Single/Double Balloon Enteroscopy) for therapeutic intervention (argon plasma coagulation of angiodysplasia).
  * **Step 3:** Oral Iron replacement therapy to keep up with occult losses if source cannot be found/treated.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Glasgow-Blatchford Score (GBS)
* **Purpose:** Identifies UGIB patients needing early clinical intervention (OGD, transfusion, surgery). Done *pre-endoscopy*.
* **Score Interpretation:** Score 0-1 = Very low risk (consider outpatient discharge). Score >1 indicates higher risk.

### Rockall Score (Pre- and Post-Endoscopy)
* **Purpose:** Predicts mortality and rebleeding risk. Includes *post-endoscopic* variables.

| Parameter | Score 0 | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Age** | < 60 | 60 - 79 | ≥ 80 | - |
| **Shock (HR/SBP)** | No shock | HR >100, SBP >100 | SBP < 100 | - |
| **Comorbidities** | None major | - | IHD, CCF, Major morbid | Renal/Liver failure, Disseminated Malignancy |
| **Diagnosis (Endoscopic)** | M-W tear, no lesion | All other Dx | GI malignancy | - |
| **Stigmata of Bleeding** | None, dark spot | - | Blood in stomach, visible vessel, clot | - |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Severe Hemorrhage
* **Cardiovascular:** Type 2 Myocardial Infarction (due to supply/demand mismatch).
* **Renal:** Pre-renal Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) (急性腎損傷) and Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) if prolonged shock.
* **Hepatic:** Gastrointestinal bleeding represents a massive protein load (blood in the gut) → broken down by gut bacteria into ammonia → precipitates Hepatic Encephalopathy (肝性腦病) in cirrhotics.
* **Pulmonary:** Aspiration pneumonia (especially during massive hematemesis/OGD).

### Adverse Effects of Pharmacotherapy
* **Terlipressin:** Potent systemic vasoconstrictor. Can cause myocardial ischemia, peripheral ischemia (gangrene of digits/bowel ischemia), hyponatremia. Must monitor ECG and Na+.
* **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation:** Post-banding ulceration (can cause delayed massive bleeding ~7-14 days post-procedure), dysphagia, esophageal stricture.
* **Proton Pump Inhibitors (Long-term):** Clostridioides difficile infection, osteoporotic fractures, hypomagnesemia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why do we use antibiotics like Ceftriaxone in acute variceal bleeding? Answer: Cirrhotic patients with GI bleeds have highly impaired immunity and gut translocation of bacteria. Prophylactic antibiotics significantly reduce early rebleeding rates and mortality.
* Viva Question: What is the target Hb in variceal bleeding and why? Answer: Target Hb is 7-8 g/dL. Over-transfusion expands intravascular volume, increases splanchnic blood flow, raises portal venous pressure, and dislodges clots, leading to catastrophic rebleeding.
* Trap: Giving a normal saline bolus rapidly to a cirrhotic patient with a suspected variceal bleed to normalize blood pressure. Pearl: Permissive hypotension is preferred. Aim for SBP ~90-100 mmHg. Aggressive fluid resuscitation worsens portal hypertension.
* Trap: Assuming a negative nasogastric aspirate (clear fluid) completely rules out an upper GI bleed. Pearl: The bleeding could be from a duodenal ulcer, and duodenal spasm or a competent pyloric sphincter prevents reflux of blood into the stomach.
* Pitfall: Stopping PPI infusion early because no active bleeding was seen on OGD. Remember high-risk stigmata (e.g., visible vessel, adherent clot) require 72 hours of IV/high-dose oral PPI therapy to stabilize the clot by maintaining a gastric pH > 6.
* Pitfall: Delaying Terlipressin until OGD confirms varices. Give vasoactive agents immediately at presentation if variceal bleeding is clinically suspected based on stigmata of chronic liver disease.
