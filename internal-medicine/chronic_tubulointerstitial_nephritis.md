# Chronic Tubulointerstitial Nephritis (慢性腎小管間質性腎炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Chronic Tubulointerstitial Nephritis
* **Chronic Tubulointerstitial Nephritis (CTIN)**: Irreversible, progressive scarring of the renal tubulointerstitial compartment.
* Primary defect involves tubular dysfunction + interstitial fibrosis, while glomeruli + renal vasculature are largely spared until late stages.
* **Result**: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) (慢性腎病) characterized by disproportionately severe tubular dysfunction relative to the degree of Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) decline.

### Major Etiologies of Chronic Tubulointerstitial Nephritis
* **Drugs/Toxins**:
  * **Analgesic Nephropathy (止痛藥腎病)**: Chronic NSAID/paracetamol/aspirin abuse.
  * **Lithium (鋰)**: Chronic bipolar disorder (躁鬱症) treatment.
  * **Heavy Metals**: Lead (鉛) (occupational, moonshine), Cadmium (鎘).
  * Calcineurin Inhibitors (CNIs): Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus.
* **Chinese Herbal Nephropathy (CHN) (中草藥腎病)**: Aristolochic acid (馬兜鈴酸) ingestion.
* **Immunologic/Systemic**: Sjögren’s Syndrome (乾燥綜合症), SLE, Sarcoidosis, IgG4-related disease.
* **Metabolic**: Chronic hyperuricemia, Hypercalcemia, Hypokalemia, Oxalosis.
* **Hematologic**: Multiple Myeloma (Cast nephropathy).
* **Structural**: Reflux nephropathy (Vesicoureteral reflux), Chronic obstructive uropathy (BPH, stones).

### Local Hong Kong Epidemiology of CTIN
* **Aristolochic Acid Nephropathy**: Historically significant in HK due to widespread Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) usage.
* Culprit herbs: *Aristolochia fangchi* (廣防己), *Aristolochia manshuriensis* (關木通) (often prescribed for weight loss or rheumatological complaints).
* Aristolochic acid is universally banned in HK, but cases still present due to delayed progression or overseas TCM sourcing.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Core Pathogenesis of Interstitial Fibrosis
* Chronic insult → Sublethal tubular epithelial cell injury.
* Inflammatory cell infiltration (macrophages, T-cells) into interstitium.
* Secretion of profibrotic cytokines (specifically **TGF-β1**, PDGF).
* Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) + local fibroblast activation.
* Deposition of Extracellular Matrix (ECM) → **Interstitial Fibrosis and Tubular Atrophy (IFTA)**.
* Capillary rarefaction → Medullary ischemia → Secondary focal global glomerulosclerosis (late).

### Molecular Mechanism of Chinese Herbal Nephropathy
* **Aristolochic Acid (AA)** → Metabolized in proximal tubule.
* Forms covalent DNA adducts (AA-DNA adducts) specifically targeting the **TP53 tumor suppressor gene**.
* Results in A→T transversion mutations.
* Dual consequence: Rapid interstitial fibrosis + High malignancy potential in the urothelium.

### Molecular Mechanism of Analgesic Nephropathy
* Chronic cumulative ingestion of combinations (e.g., NSAID + Paracetamol).
* NSAIDs ↓ Prostaglandins → Afferent arteriole vasoconstriction → Medullary ischemia.
* Paracetamol → Depletion of local glutathione → Oxidative stress in the papillae.
* Synergistic toxicity → **Renal Papillary Necrosis (腎乳頭壞死)** → Secondary cortical tubulointerstitial scarring.

### Molecular Mechanism of Lithium Nephrotoxicity
* Lithium enters principal cells of the collecting duct via ENaC channels.
* Inhibits Glycogen Synthase Kinase 3β (GSK3β) + disrupts cAMP signaling.
* → Downregulation of Aquaporin-2 (AQP2) water channels → **Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus (NDI)**.
* Chronic exposure → Microcyst formation in distal tubules + diffuse interstitial fibrosis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Key Historical Clues for CTIN
* **Tubular Symptoms**:
  * Polyuria (多尿), nocturia (夜尿) (loss of concentrating ability).
  * Polydipsia (多飲) (compensatory).
  * Muscle weakness, cramps (electrolyte wasting).
* **Etiological Clues**:
  * Chronic joint/back pain, dysmenorrhea → daily OTC analgesic use.
  * TCM consumption (slimming pills, joint remedies, "detox" teas).
  * Psychiatric history → prolonged Lithium usage.
  * Recurrent childhood UTIs → Reflux nephropathy.
  * Sicca symptoms (dry eyes/mouth) → Sjögren’s Syndrome.

### System-Specific Physical Signs in CTIN
* **Vitals**: Blood pressure is classically NORMAL in early CTIN (unlike glomerulonephritis where HTN is early). Late HTN occurs as GFR falls.
* **General**: Severe pallor (貧血) (often out of proportion to renal failure).
* **Volume Status**: Signs of dehydration/volume depletion (↓ skin turgor, dry mucous membranes) due to salt/water wasting.
* **Rheumatological (if autoimmune)**: Parotid enlargement, xerophthalmia (Sjögren’s); malar rash (SLE); gouty tophi (Urate nephropathy).
* **Neurological/Musculoskeletal**: Proximal muscle weakness (hypokalemia/osteomalacia), bone tenderness (renal osteodystrophy).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### CTIN vs Other Causes of CKD
| Feature | Chronic Tubulointerstitial Nephritis | Chronic Glomerulonephritis | Hypertensive Nephrosclerosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Proteinuria** | Mild, tubular (<1g/day) | Heavy, often nephrotic (>3g/day) | Moderate (<2g/day) |
| **Urine Sediment** | Sterile pyuria, WBC casts, no RBCs | Dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts | Benign, occasional hyaline casts |
| **Hypertension** | Late complication | Early & prominent | Primary driver, very early |
| **Anemia** | Early, severe (↓ EPO) | Correlates strictly with GFR | Correlates strictly with GFR |
| **Ultrasound** | Small, irregular contour, scarred | Small, smooth contour | Small, smooth, symmetric |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Urinalysis in CTIN
* **Dipstick**: +ve Leucocytes (sterile pyuria), Trace/1+ Protein, -ve Blood, +ve Glucose (with normal serum glucose = renal glycosuria).
* **Microscopy**: WBCs, WBC casts, broad waxy casts (in advanced CKD). NO dysmorphic RBCs.

### Blood Biochemistry & Tubular Function Tests
| Defect Location | Specific Laboratory Findings in CTIN |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Proximal Tubule** | Normal AG Metabolic Acidosis (Type 2 RTA), Hypokalemia, Hypophosphatemia, Hypouricemia (Fanconi Syndrome pattern). |
| **Distal Tubule** | Normal AG Metabolic Acidosis (Type 1 RTA), Hyperkalemia (Type 4 RTA, esp. analgesic nephropathy/lead), Hypokalemia (Type 1). |
| **Medulla/Collecting Duct** | ↑ Serum Osmolality, Hypernatremia (if unable to drink to match NDI polyuria). |
| **General CKD** | ↑ Urea, ↑ Creatinine, severe Normocytic Normochromic Anemia (↓ EPO). |

### Renal Imaging Findings in CTIN
* **USG Kidneys/KUB**:
  * Bilaterally small, shrunken kidneys.
  * Increased cortical echogenicity with loss of corticomedullary differentiation.
  * Cortical scarring → irregular, "bumpy" contours.
  * Echolucent cysts (Lithium toxicity).
* **CT Urogram (CTU)**:
  * Papillary calcifications / "ring sign" (Analgesic nephropathy).
  * Blunted calyces overlying cortical scars (Reflux nephropathy).
  * Rule out upper tract transitional cell carcinoma (Aristolochic acid).

### Renal Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria
* **Indication**: Rarely needed if etiology is obvious (e.g., Lithium hx + classic cysts). Done if rapid progression or suspected autoimmune etiology (e.g., IgG4, Sarcoidosis) where immunosuppression is considered.
* **Histology**:
  * Extensive Interstitial Fibrosis and Tubular Atrophy (IFTA).
  * Mononuclear cell infiltration (lymphocytes, plasma cells).
  * Peritubular capillary loss.
  * Glomeruli: Relative preservation initially, periglomerular fibrosis later.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Decompensation Management in CTIN
* **Hypovolemic Shock (from uncontrolled polyuria)**:
  * IV 0.9% NaCl resuscitation. Monitor carefully as kidneys cannot self-regulate sodium.
* **Severe Hyperkalemia (Type 4 RTA decompensation)**:
  * IV Calcium Gluconate 10% 10ml (cardioprotective).
  * IV Actrapid 10 units + 50ml 20% Dextrose.
  * PO Potassium binding resins (e.g., Resonium).
* **Acute Superimposed Papillary Necrosis (Sloughed papilla)**:
  * Presents with acute flank pain, hematuria, acute post-renal obstruction.
  * Resuscitation, broad-spectrum IV Abx (e.g., Cefuroxime/Amoxicillin-Clavulanate) if infected.
  * Urgent urology consultation for stenting/percutaneous nephrostomy (PCN).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Step-Up Chronic Management Algorithm for CTIN
* **Step 1: Removal of Offending Agent (Crucial)**
  * Cease all NSAIDs, TCM, and herbal supplements.
  * If Lithium-induced: Consult psychiatry to switch to Valproate or Lamotrigine. (Note: NDI may be irreversible if advanced).
* **Step 2: Correct Specific Tubular Defects**
  * **Metabolic Acidosis**: PO Sodium Bicarbonate (維持血清 HCO3 > 22 mmol/L to slow CKD progression and prevent bone demineralization).
  * **Renal Salt Wasting**: Liberalize dietary sodium (unlike standard CKD advice), careful PO fluid matching for polyuria.
  * **Lithium-induced NDI**: Paradoxical use of Thiazide diuretics (e.g., Indapamide) + sodium restriction to induce mild volume depletion, enhancing proximal tubule water reabsorption.
* **Step 3: Non-Specific Renoprotection**
  * Target BP < 130/80 mmHg.
  * Use ACEI/ARB (e.g., Ramipril, Losartan) IF significant proteinuria or HTN present. Caution: High risk of hyperkalemia if Type 4 RTA is present.
  * SGLT2 Inhibitors (e.g., Dapagliflozin): Emerging role to slow fibrosis regardless of diabetes status.
* **Step 4: Management of Severe Anemia**
  * Exclude iron deficiency.
  * Subcutaneous Erythropoiesis-Stimulating Agents (ESAs) (e.g., Darbepoetin alfa / Aranesp) instituted much earlier than in typical CKD.

### Malignancy Surveillance Protocol for Aristolochic Acid Nephropathy
* **Mandatory Lifelong Screening**:
  * Due to 40-50% lifetime risk of Urothelial Carcinoma (Upper Tract Urothelial Carcinoma - UTUC & Bladder cancer).
  * Annual Urine Cytology.
  * Regular flexible cystoscopy + CT Urogram / MR Urogram.
  * Prophylactic bilateral nephroureterectomy is often indicated if progressing to End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) prior to transplantation.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### KDIGO CKD Staging for Prognosis
| KDIGO GFR Category | GFR (ml/min/1.73m²) | Clinical Significance in CTIN |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **G1 / G2** | > 60 | Tubular defects present, often clinically silent. Stopping toxin may halt progression. |
| **G3a / G3b** | 30-59 | Irreversible IFTA established. Focus on slowing progression & RTA correction. |
| **G4** | 15-29 | Prepare for Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT). Severe anemia prominent. |
| **G5** | < 15 | ESRD. Dialysis or Transplant required. |

### Prognostic Risk Factors for Rapid Progression
* Continued exposure to nephrotoxin.
* High cumulative dose of Aristolochic acid (↑ risk of rapid ESRD + ↑ cancer risk).
* Heavy baseline interstitial fibrosis on biopsy.
* Uncontrolled severe metabolic acidosis.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Major Renal & Urological Complications
* **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) (末期腎功能衰竭)**: Requiring hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis.
* **Urothelial Malignancies**: Specifically upper tract (renal pelvis, ureters) and bladder transitional cell carcinomas (TCC) in Aristolochic acid and Analgesic nephropathy.
* **Renal Papillary Necrosis (腎乳頭壞死)**: Can slough off and cause acute ureteric obstruction + severe infection.
* **Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus (NDI)**: Leading to dangerous hypernatremia if free water access is restricted.

### Systemic Complications
* **Severe Renal Osteodystrophy**: Acidosis buffering consumes bone carbonate/phosphate, accelerating osteomalacia/osteoporosis out of proportion to GFR.
* **Cardiovascular**: Uremic cardiomyopathy, accelerated atherosclerosis (secondary to CKD).
* **Hematological**: Refractory normocytic anemia requiring high-dose ESA therapy.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic urinalysis finding in Chronic Tubulointerstitial Nephritis compared to Glomerulonephritis? Answer: Mild tubular proteinuria (usually <1g/day) with sterile pyuria, lacking red cell casts or heavy albuminuria.
* Viva Question: How do you treat the Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus caused by chronic Lithium toxicity? Answer: Paradoxically, use a thiazide diuretic combined with dietary sodium restriction to induce mild volume contraction, which enhances proximal tubular reabsorption of sodium and water.
* Trap: Assuming all patients with CKD and anemia have an EPO deficiency perfectly proportionate to their GFR decline. Pearl: CTIN patients develop severe anemia early and out of proportion to their GFR drop because the peritubular interstitial fibroblasts (which are the primary site of EPO production) are destroyed directly by the fibrotic process.
* Trap: Recommending strict salt and water restriction for all CKD patients. Pearl: Patients with CTIN often have obligate salt and water wasting due to tubular destruction; strict restriction can precipitate hypovolemic shock and acute-on-chronic renal failure.
* Pitfall: Stopping at the diagnosis of Chinese Herbal Nephropathy without arranging urological surveillance. Patients with Aristolochic acid exposure require lifelong regular cystoscopy and upper tract imaging due to a massive lifetime risk (up to 50%) of urothelial carcinoma.
