# Nephritic Syndrome (腎炎症綜合症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Nephritic Syndrome
* **Nephritic Syndrome (腎炎症綜合症)**: A clinical syndrome caused by acute glomerular inflammation (glomerulonephritis), characterized by the classic triad of haematuria (血尿), hypertension (高血壓), and renal impairment (AKI/oliguria).

### Etiologies of Nephritic Syndrome
* **Immune-Complex Mediated**: IgA Nephropathy, Post-Streptococcal GN (PSGN), Membranoproliferative GN (MPGN).
* **Pauci-Immune**: ANCA-associated vasculitides (e.g., Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis, Microscopic Polyangiitis).
* **Anti-GBM Disease**: Goodpasture's disease.

### Local Epidemiology of Nephritic Syndrome in Hong Kong
* **IgA Nephropathy (IgA腎病)**: Most common primary glomerulonephritis in Hong Kong and Asia; highest prevalence in Chinese populations.
* **Peak Age**: Usually presents in young adults (20-30s) with male predominance.
* **End-Stage Renal Disease**: IgA nephropathy is a leading cause of primary GN progressing to End-Stage Kidney Disease (ESKD) in HK.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Glomerular Inflammation
* **Immune Deposition**: Deposition of immune complexes (IgA/IgG) or autoantibodies (Anti-GBM) in glomeruli.
* **Complement Activation**: Classical or alternative pathway activation → recruitment of neutrophils and macrophages.
* **Cellular Damage**: Release of lysosomal enzymes and reactive oxygen species → destruction of glomerular capillary walls.
* **Crescent Formation**: Severe injury leads to fibrin leakage into Bowman's space → parietal epithelial cell proliferation → crescentic GN (Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis, RPGN).

### Pathophysiology of Clinical Features in Nephritic Syndrome
* **Haematuria & Casts**: Capillary wall rupture → RBCs forced through fenestrations → mechanical deformation (dysmorphic RBCs) and trapping in Tamm-Horsfall protein (RBC casts).
* **Oliguria & AKI**: Glomerular inflammation → ↓ capillary perfusion & ↓ Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR).
* **Hypertension & Oedema**: ↓ GFR → primary renal sodium and water retention → intravascular volume expansion → ↑ hydrostatic pressure.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### History in Nephritic Syndrome
* **Macroscopic Haematuria (肉眼血尿)**: Cola-coloured or tea-coloured urine.
* **Urine Output**: Oliguria (少尿) or anuria in severe cases.
* **Timing of Antecedent Illness**: 
  * Synpharyngitic (1-2 days post-URTI/GI infection) → favours IgA Nephropathy.
  * Post-infectious (1-3 weeks post-streptococcal pharyngitis/skin infection) → favours PSGN.
* **Systemic Symptoms**: Hemoptysis (Goodpasture's, ANCA), rash/arthralgia (HSP, SLE).

### Physical Examination Findings in Nephritic Syndrome
* **Vitals**: Hypertension (often severe, volume-dependent).
* **Volume Status**: 
  * Periorbital oedema (classic morning presentation).
  * Peripheral pitting oedema (mild compared to nephrotic syndrome).
  * Signs of fluid overload: ↑ JVP, bibasal pulmonary crepitations, S3 gallop.
* **Systemic Signs**: Purpuric rash (Henoch-Schönlein Purpura), skin ulcers, nasal septal perforation (ANCA vasculitis).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Nephritic vs Nephrotic Syndrome
| Feature | Nephritic Syndrome | Nephrotic Syndrome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Mechanism** | Glomerular inflammation & rupture | Podocyte damage & altered permeability |
| **Urine RBCs** | +++ (Dysmorphic, RBC casts) | - or microscopic |
| **Proteinuria** | Mild-moderate (<3.5 g/day) | Massive (≥3.5 g/day) |
| **Serum Albumin** | Normal or mildly ↓ | Markedly ↓ |
| **Oedema** | Mild (intravascular expansion) | Severe (↓ oncotic pressure) |
| **Blood Pressure** | ↑↑↑ (Hypertension common) | Normal (usually) |

### Differentiating Causes of Glomerulonephritis by Complement Levels
| Complement Profile | Associated Glomerulonephritides |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Low Complement (C3/C4)** | Post-infectious GN (PSGN), Cryoglobulinaemia, MPGN, Lupus Nephritis, Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis |
| **Normal Complement** | IgA Nephropathy, Anti-GBM Disease, ANCA-associated Vasculitis |

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Bedside Investigations for Nephritic Syndrome
* **Urine Dipstick**: +ve Blood, +ve Protein (non-nephrotic range).
* **ECG**: Check for tall T-waves (hyperkalaemia from AKI), LVH (chronic hypertension).

### Blood Investigations for Nephritic Syndrome
* **Renal Function**: ↑ Creatinine, ↑ Urea, hyperkalaemia, metabolic acidosis.
* **Immunological Screen**: 
  * C3 / C4 levels.
  * ANA, Anti-dsDNA (rule out Lupus).
  * Anti-GBM antibody.
  * ANCA (p-ANCA/MPO, c-ANCA/PR3).
  * ASOT & Anti-DNAse B (for PSGN).

### Urine Investigations for Nephritic Syndrome
* **Urine Microscopy**: **Dysmorphic RBCs** (畸形紅血球), **RBC casts** (紅血球管型), granular casts.
* **Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (UPCR)**: Quantify proteinuria.

### Renal Biopsy (LM, IF, EM) Findings in Nephritic Syndrome
| Disease | Light Microscopy (LM) | Immunofluorescence (IF) | Electron Microscopy (EM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **IgA Nephropathy** | Mesangial hypercellularity | Granular **IgA** & C3 in mesangium | Mesangial electron-dense deposits |
| **PSGN** | Diffuse proliferative GN (hypercellular) | Granular IgG & C3 ("starry sky") | Subepithelial humps |
| **Anti-GBM** | Crescents in Bowman's space | **Linear** IgG along GBM | No immune deposits |
| **ANCA-associated** | Focal necrosis, crescents | **Pauci-immune** (negative IF) | No immune deposits |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Acute Stabilization of Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN)
* **Volume Overload / Pulmonary Oedema**: 
  * IV Furosemide (呋塞米) high-dose.
  * Fluid & sodium restriction.
* **Hypertensive Emergency**: 
  * IV Labetalol or IV Hydralazine.
* **Emergent Haemodialysis (HD)**: Initiate for refractory hyperkalaemia, severe acidosis, volume overload, or uraemic encephalopathy/pericarditis (AEIOU criteria).

### Acute Immunosuppressive Induction Therapy (Severe GN / RPGN)
* **Pulse Corticosteroids**: IV Methylprednisolone 500mg-1g daily for 3 days.
* **Plasma Exchange (Plasmapheresis)**: Emergent indication for Anti-GBM disease and severe ANCA vasculitis with pulmonary haemorrhage.
* **Cytotoxic Therapy**: IV Cyclophosphamide (環磷酰胺) or IV Rituximab induction.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Chronic Conservative & Anti-Proteinuric Management
* **Blood Pressure Targets**: Strict control <120/80 mmHg (HA guideline preference for proteinuric CKD).
* **Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) Blockade**: 
  * First-line: ACE inhibitors (e.g., Ramipril) or ARBs (e.g., Losartan).
  * Titrate to maximum tolerated dose to ↓ proteinuria and ↓ intraglomerular pressure.
* **SGLT2 Inhibitors**: Dapagliflozin (達格列淨) added if persistent proteinuria/CKD progression despite maximal RAAS blockade.
* **Lifestyle**: Dietary sodium restriction (<2g/day).

### Chronic Immunosuppressive Maintenance Therapy
* **IgA Nephropathy**: Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., oral Prednisolone 0.5-1mg/kg) if proteinuria >1g/day persists despite 3-6 months of optimized supportive care.
* **ANCA / Anti-GBM Maintenance**: Step-down to oral Azathioprine (硫唑嘌呤) or Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF) for 18-24 months to prevent relapse.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Indicators in Nephritic Syndrome
* **Poor Prognostic Markers**: ↑ Serum creatinine at presentation, heavy proteinuria, severe hypertension, extensive crescent formation (>50% glomeruli involved) on biopsy.

### Scoring System: Oxford Classification of IgA Nephropathy (MEST-C Score)
| Variable | Description | Score |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **M**esangial hypercellularity | >50% glomeruli showing hypercellularity | M0 or M1 |
| **E**ndocapillary proliferation | Hypercellularity within capillary lumens | E0 or E1 |
| **S**egmental glomerulosclerosis | Presence of sclerosis/adhesions | S0 or S1 |
| **T**ubular atrophy / fibrosis | Percentage of cortical area involved | T0, T1, T2 |
| **C**rescents | Cellular/fibrocellular crescents present | C0, C1, C2 |

### Prognosis of Specific Glomerulonephritides
* **PSGN**: Excellent in children (>95% complete recovery); adults have higher risk of progressing to CKD/ESKD.
* **IgA Nephropathy**: ~25-30% progress to ESKD over 20 years.
* **Anti-GBM / RPGN**: High risk of permanent renal failure requiring chronic dialysis if induction therapy is delayed.

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### End-Organ Complications of Nephritic Syndrome
* **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), End-Stage Kidney Disease (ESKD) (末期腎病).
* **Cardiovascular**: Volume-overload heart failure, left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), hypertensive encephalopathy.

### Iatrogenic Complications from Immunosuppression
* **Systemic Corticosteroids**: Osteoporosis, iatrogenic diabetes mellitus, avascular necrosis of femoral head, opportunistic infections.
* **Cyclophosphamide**: Haemorrhagic cystitis, myelosuppression, premature ovarian failure/infertility, increased risk of transitional cell carcinoma (bladder) and haematological malignancies.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate the timing of IgA Nephropathy versus Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)? Answer: IgA nephropathy is synpharyngitic (presents 1-2 days concurrent with infection); PSGN is post-infectious (presents 1-3 weeks after pharyngitis or 3-6 weeks after impetigo).
* Trap: Assuming a normal urine dipstick with isolated macroscopic haematuria is nephritic syndrome. Pearl: Glomerular bleeding produces dysmorphic RBCs and RBC casts; isomorphic (normal) RBCs without casts or proteinuria highly suggests lower urinary tract pathology (e.g., malignancy, stones) requiring cystoscopy.
* Pitfall: Waiting for renal biopsy results to initiate treatment in suspected Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN). Start empiric pulse IV Methylprednisolone immediately if clinical suspicion (rapidly rising creatinine, active urinary sediment, haemoptysis) is high, as irreversible nephron loss occurs within days.
