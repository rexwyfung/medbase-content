# Acne Vulgaris (尋常痤瘡)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Acne Vulgaris
* **Acne Vulgaris (尋常痤瘡):** Chronic inflammatory disorder of the **pilosebaceous unit** (hair follicle + sebaceous gland) characterized by comedones, papules, pustules, and nodules.

### Etiologies and Triggers for Acne
* **Genetics:** Strong family Hx ↑ risk and severity.
* **Hormonal:** ↑ Androgen activity (e.g., puberty, PCOS, congenital adrenal hyperplasia).
* **Medications (Drug-induced acne):** Corticosteroids, Lithium, Anticonvulsants, Androgenic steroids.
* **Mechanical:** Friction/occlusion (e.g., helmets, masks - "maskne").
* **Dietary factors:** High glycemic index diets, dairy (controversial, but heavily associated).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* Peak incidence during adolescence (12-24 yrs); affects >80% of teenagers.
* ↑ Prevalence of adult-onset acne in females.
* **Asian skin phenotype (Fitzpatrick III-IV):** Highly prone to **Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)**, making early aggressive Rx crucial in HK.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### The 4 Pillars of Acne Pathophysiology
* **1. Follicular Hyperkeratosis:** Abnormal keratinocyte desquamation → retention hyperkeratosis → blocks follicular ostium → **Microcomedone** formation.
* **2. Excess Sebum Production:** Androgens stimulate sebocytes → sebum hypersecretion.
* **3. Bacterial Proliferation:** *Cutibacterium acnes* (formerly *Propionibacterium acnes*, anaerobic Gram -ve rod) colonizes lipid-rich, anaerobic blocked follicle.
* **4. Inflammation:** *C. acnes* lipases convert triglycerides to free fatty acids → highly pro-inflammatory → recruits neutrophils → follicular wall rupture → dermal inflammation (papules/pustules/nodules).

### Hormonal Influence in Acne Pathophysiology
* Sebaceous glands possess **5-alpha reductase (Type 1)**.
* Converts testosterone → **DHT (Dihydrotestosterone)** → binds androgen receptors → massive ↑ sebum output.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Key History Elements for Acne Assessment
* **Onset & Duration:** Age of onset, relation to menstrual cycle (premenstrual flares).
* **Past Rx:** OTC products, prior topical/oral antibiotics, retinoids (assess compliance & duration).
* **Endocrine Hx (Female):** Oligo/amenorrhea, hirsutism, alopecia, deepened voice, weight gain (suggests **PCOS (多囊卵巢綜合症)**).
* **Medication Hx:** Steroids, lithium, iodides, bromides.
* **Psychosocial Hx:** Impact on mood, self-esteem, screening for depression.

### Dermatological Examination Findings in Acne
* **Distribution:** Face (U-zone/jawline in adults, T-zone in teens), chest, upper back, shoulders.
* **Non-inflammatory Lesions:**
  * **Closed Comedones (Whiteheads):** ~1-2mm pale, slightly elevated papules; no visible ostium.
  * **Open Comedones (Blackheads):** Dilated follicular ostium packed with oxidized keratin/melanin (NOT dirt).
* **Inflammatory Lesions:**
  * **Papules:** <5mm erythematous bumps.
  * **Pustules:** Papules with central purulent core.
  * **Nodules/Cysts:** >5mm deep, tender, fluctuant lesions.
* **Post-inflammatory Changes:**
  * **Erythema (PIE):** Red macules at resolving lesions.
  * **Hyperpigmentation (PIH):** Brown macules (very common in HK patients).
* **Scarring Morphologies:**
  * **Ice-pick:** Deep, narrow (<2mm), sharply demarcated.
  * **Rolling:** Broad (4-5mm), undulating base (loss of subcutaneous tethering).
  * **Boxcar:** Broad, sharply punched-out vertical edges.
  * **Hypertrophic/Keloidal:** Thickened, raised scars (chest/jawline).
* **Systemic Signs of Hyperandrogenism:** Hirsutism (Ferriman-Gallwey score), acanthosis nigricans, androgenetic alopecia.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Acne from Clinical Mimics
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Rosacea (玫瑰痤瘡)** | Older onset (30-50s); **absence of comedones**; prominent erythema, telangiectasia, flushing; worsened by spicy food/alcohol/heat. |
| **Folliculitis (毛囊炎)** | Monomorphic pustules pierced by a hair; +ve bacterial swab (*S. aureus* or Gram -ve); spares the face usually (trunk/limbs). |
| **Perioral Dermatitis** | Monomorphic micropapules/vesicles around mouth/nose; spares vermillion border; often follows topical steroid abuse. |
| **Drug-induced Acne** | Monomorphic papulopustular eruption; sudden onset; temporal relation to offending drug; NO comedones. |
| **Pityrosporum Folliculitis** | Pruritic, monomorphic papules on trunk; caused by *Malassezia*; responds to antifungals. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Diagnostic Approach to Acne
* Clinical diagnosis based on presence of **comedones** (essential for classic acne vulgaris).
* Investigations generally **NOT** required for routine cases.

### Blood Investigations for Hyperandrogenism/PCOS Suspicion
* Indicated ONLY if: Severe recalcitrant acne, sudden onset, signs of virilization, or irregular menses.
* **Serum Free & Total Testosterone**
* **DHEAS (Dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate):** ↑ suggests adrenal source.
* **LH / FSH ratio:** >2:1 suggestive of PCOS.
* **SHBG (Sex Hormone Binding Globulin)**
* **Prolactin & 17-OH Progesterone:** Exclude prolactinoma and Late-Onset Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia.

### Bedside & Imaging Investigations
* **Microbiological Swab:** Target pustules if suspicious of Gram-negative folliculitis (often secondary to long-term oral antibiotics).
* **Pelvic Ultrasound:** To check for polycystic ovaries if PCOS clinically suspected.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Acne Fulminans (暴發性痤瘡)
* **Definition:** Rare dermatological emergency; acute onset severe cystic/ulcerative acne + systemic Sx (fever, arthralgia, leukocytosis, ↑ ESR/CRP).
* **Acute Rx Protocol:**
  * **Oral Corticosteroids:** Prednisolone 0.5-1 mg/kg/day for 2-4 weeks to rapidly suppress inflammation.
  * **Oral Isotretinoin:** Introduced *after* initial steroid suppression (start low dose e.g., 0.1 mg/kg/day) to prevent paradoxical worsening, then gradually up-titrate.
  * Avoid antibiotics.

### Acute Management of Severe Inflammatory Cysts
* **Intralesional Steroid Injection:** Triamcinolone acetonide (2.5-5.0 mg/mL) injected directly into deep nodulocystic lesions.
* **Effect:** Rapid involution within 48-72 hours, ↓ pain, ↓ risk of permanent scarring.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### General Skincare and Lifestyle Modifications
* Wash face BD with gentle, non-comedogenic synthetic detergent (syndet) cleanser.
* Avoid mechanical picking/squeezing (↑ risk of scarring/PIH).
* Use non-comedogenic (oil-free) cosmetics and sunscreens.
* Counsel that improvement takes **6-8 weeks**; adherence is paramount.

### Step-Up Pharmacological Algorithm for Acne
| Severity Stage | First-Line Therapy Options | Alternative / Adjuncts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Comedonal (Mild)** | **Topical Retinoid** (Adapalene or Tretinoin) | Azelaic acid, Salicylic acid. |
| **Mild Papulopustular** | Topical Retinoid + **Topical BPO (Benzoyl Peroxide)** OR Topical Antibiotic (Clindamycin) + BPO | Fixed-dose combinations (e.g., Epiduo = Adapalene + BPO). |
| **Moderate Papulopustular** | **Oral Antibiotic** (Doxycycline/Lymecycline) + Topical Retinoid + BPO | Female patients: **COCP** (Combined Oral Contraceptive) or Spironolactone. |
| **Severe / Nodulocystic** | **Oral Isotretinoin (異維A酸)** monotherapy | Systemic anti-androgens (females). |

### Mechanism and Principles of Key Pharmacotherapies
* **Benzoyl Peroxide (BPO):** Generates free radicals to kill *C. acnes*; **no bacterial resistance**; bleaches hair/clothing.
* **Topical Retinoids (Adapalene, Tretinoin):** Comedolytic & anti-inflammatory; normalizes desquamation; causes local irritation/photosensitivity.
* **Oral Antibiotics:** Anti-inflammatory at sub-antimicrobial doses (Tetracyclines). Limit to max 3-6 months to prevent resistance. MUST use with BPO. Avoid in pregnancy/children <8yrs.

### Oral Isotretinoin (異維A酸) Prescribing & Monitoring Protocol
* **Indications:** Severe nodulocystic acne, scarring acne, failure of >2 courses of oral antibiotics, severe psychological distress.
* **Dosing:** 0.5-1.0 mg/kg/day until cumulative dose of 120-150 mg/kg is reached (reduces relapse rate).
* **Mandatory Pre-Rx & Monitoring (HA Protocol):**
  * **Pregnancy Test:** Absolute contraindication (highly teratogenic). Must use 2 forms of contraception 1 month before, during, and 1 month after Rx.
  * **Fasting Lipids:** Baseline and regular monitoring (causes hypertriglyceridemia).
  * **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** Baseline and regular monitoring (hepatotoxic).
  * **Psychiatric Screening:** Monitor for mood changes/depression (controversial but mandatory counseling point).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Clinical Grading of Acne Severity
| Severity Level | Clinical Features | Treatment Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | Few to several comedones, <10 papules/pustules. No nodules. | Topical Rx only. |
| **Moderate** | Several to many comedones, 10-40 papules/pustules. Mild trunk involvement. | Oral antibiotics ± topical Rx. |
| **Severe** | Numerous comedones/papules/pustules, prominent nodules/cysts. Marked scarring. | Oral Isotretinoin. |

### Prognosis and Relapse Risk
* **Relapse rate post-isotretinoin:** ~20-30%, often requiring a second course, especially in patients with early onset, severe truncal acne, or PCOS.
* Without treatment, nodulocystic acne almost universally progresses to permanent physiological scarring.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Dermatological Complications of Acne
* **Permanent Scarring:** Atrophic (ice-pick, rolling, boxcar) or Hypertrophic/Keloid. Requires physical modalities (laser, microneedling, subcision) for definitive correction.
* **Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH):** Melanin deposition in dermis/epidermis; fades very slowly over months to years.

### Psychosocial Complications
* Severe impact on quality of life.
* Social withdrawal, low self-esteem, anxiety.
* Clinical depression and ↑ risk of suicidal ideation.

### Adverse Effects of Major Acne Medications
* **Oral Isotretinoin:** Teratogenicity (craniofacial/cardiac defects), cheilitis (dry lips - universal, indicates absorption), xerosis (dry skin), epistaxis, myalgia, ↑ transaminases, hypertriglyceridemia, benign intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor cerebri).
* **Tetracyclines (Doxycycline, Minocycline):** Photosensitivity, pill esophagitis, tooth discoloration (if <8 yrs old), vestibular toxicity, lupus-like syndrome (Minocycline).
* **Drug Interaction Warning:** Concurrent use of Tetracyclines + Isotretinoin is strictly contraindicated (massively ↑ risk of benign intracranial hypertension).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the most critical counseling point for a female of childbearing age commencing isotretinoin? Answer: Strict pregnancy prevention (2 reliable forms of contraception) due to severe teratogenicity; required 1 month before, continuously during, and for 1 month after ceasing therapy.
* Viva Question: Why must you never prescribe oral tetracyclines alongside oral isotretinoin? Answer: Both drugs independently increase intracranial pressure; combining them dramatically increases the risk of Benign Intracranial Hypertension (Pseudotumor Cerebri).
* Trap: Prescribing a topical or oral antibiotic as monotherapy for acne. Pearl: Never use antibiotics alone. Always combine with **Benzoyl Peroxide (BPO)** or a topical retinoid to prevent the rapid development of *C. acnes* antibiotic resistance.
* Trap: Diagnosing rosacea as adult-onset acne because both present with facial papules and pustules. Pearl: Look for **comedones**. The presence of comedones confirms acne; their absolute absence, coupled with background erythema/telangiectasia, points to rosacea.
* Pitfall: Patients stopping topical retinoids after 1-2 weeks due to facial redness and peeling. Counsel proactively that "retinoid dermatitis" is expected early on. Advise them to use a "pea-sized" amount for the whole face, apply on dry skin, use on alternate nights initially, and apply non-comedogenic moisturizers to build tolerance.
* Pitfall: Reassuring a patient that acne treatments will work within a week. Remind the patient that the lifecycle of a comedone is long, and all active treatments take a minimum of **6 to 8 weeks** to show significant clinical improvement.
