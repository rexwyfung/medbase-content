# Muscular Dystrophies (肌肉營養不良症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Muscular Dystrophies
* **Muscular Dystrophies (MD)**: A heterogeneous group of inherited, progressive, non-inflammatory myopathies characterized by primary degeneration of skeletal muscle fibers.

### Etiologies and Genetic Inheritance Patterns
* **Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) (杜興氏肌肉營養不良症)**: X-linked recessive (XLR). Out-of-frame mutation in *DMD* gene (Xp21) → complete absence of dystrophin.
* **Becker Muscular Dystrophy (BMD) (貝克氏肌肉營養不良症)**: XLR. In-frame mutation in *DMD* gene → truncated, partially functional dystrophin.
* **Myotonic Dystrophy Type 1 (DM1) (強直性肌肉營養不良症)**: Autosomal dominant (AD). CTG trinucleotide repeat expansion in *DMPK* gene (19q13.3).
* **Facioscapulohumeral Dystrophy (FSHD) (面肩胛肱型肌營養不良症)**: AD. Deletion of D4Z4 repeats on chromosome 4q35 → toxic *DUX4* expression.
* **Limb-Girdle Muscular Dystrophies (LGMD)**: Highly heterogeneous (AD or AR subtypes) affecting various structural muscle proteins (e.g., sarcoglycans, calpain).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* DMD: Most common childhood MD. Incidence ~1:3,500-5,000 live male births. ~1/3 are *de novo* mutations (no family history).
* DM1: Most common adult-onset MD. 
* Local HA focus is on multidisciplinary care; novel gene therapies (e.g., exon skipping) are often non-formulary and require compassionate/self-financed pathways in HK.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Dystrophinopathies (DMD/BMD)
* **Dystrophin Glycoprotein Complex (DGC)** structurally links the intracellular actin cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix (laminin) across the sarcolemma.
* Absent/Defective Dystrophin → Sarcolemma instability during muscle contraction.
* Microtears → Massive intracellular Ca2+ influx → Activation of calpains (proteases) → Muscle fiber necrosis.
* Failed regeneration → Fibrofatty replacement (pseudohypertrophy) and progressive fibrosis.

### Molecular Mechanisms of Myotonic Dystrophy (DM1)
* **RNA Toxicity**: CTG expansion transcribed into mutant mRNA which aggregates in the nucleus.
* Sequestration of RNA-binding proteins (e.g., Muscleblind-like 1, MBNL1) → Widespread aberrant alternative splicing of multiple genes.
* Misfolding of CLC-1 chloride channel → Reduced resting membrane potential stability → **Myotonia**.
* **Anticipation**: CTG repeats expand in successive generations → earlier onset and more severe phenotype (especially with maternal transmission).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History in Dystrophinopathies (DMD/BMD)
* Delayed motor milestones (e.g., walking later than 18 months).
* Frequent falls, difficulty climbing stairs, or keeping up with peers.
* Toe walking (due to Achilles tendon contractures).
* History of unexplained developmental delay or isolated speech delay.
* BMD typically presents later (teens to early adulthood) with milder progressive weakness.

### Physical Examination of Duchenne/Becker MD
* **Gait**: Waddling (Trendelenburg) gait, lumbar lordosis (compensating for gluteal weakness).
* **Gowers' Sign (高爾斯徵)**: Patient uses hands to "climb up" their own legs to stand from the floor (indicates proximal lower limb weakness).
* **Muscle Trophism**: **Calf Pseudohypertrophy (小腿假性肥大)** (firm, enlarged calves due to fat/fibrosis, not true muscle hypertrophy). Wasting of thigh/gluteal muscles.
* **Reflexes**: Depressed or absent deep tendon reflexes (knee jerk lost early; ankle jerk preserved until late).
* **Tone**: Decreased (hypotonia).

### Clinical Features of Myotonic Dystrophy (DM1)
* **Weakness**: Predominantly *distal* (foot drop, weak grip) and facial weakness.
* **Myotonia (肌強直)**: Delayed muscle relaxation after voluntary contraction (e.g., inability to release a handshake) or percussion (e.g., thenar eminence struck with reflex hammer).
* **Facies**: "Hatchet face" (narrow face, wasting of masseter/temporalis), frontal balding, ptosis.

### Facioscapulohumeral Dystrophy (FSHD) Signs
* Asymmetrical weakness of face (inability to whistle, close eyes tightly) and shoulder girdle.
* **Scapular Winging (肩胛骨翼狀突出)**: Prominent when arms are abducted/flexed forward.
* Preserved deltoid bulk with wasted pectoralis and biceps.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Features of Proximal Weakness
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features vs MD |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)** | LMN signs (fasciculations), AR inheritance, sensory sparing, *SMN1* mutation, absent pseudohypertrophy. |
| **Inflammatory Myopathies (Dermatomyositis/Polymyositis)** | Acute/subacute onset, adult/elderly onset, skin rashes (Gottron's, Heliotrope), muscle pain/tenderness, positive autoantibodies (e.g., Anti-Jo-1). |
| **Myasthenia Gravis (MG)** | Fatigability (worsens throughout day), extraocular involvement (diplopia, ptosis), bulbar symptoms, normal reflexes, normal CK. |
| **Congenital Myopathies (e.g., Nemaline)** | Present at birth (floppy infant), static or very slowly progressive, dysmorphic features, specific biopsy findings (rods, central cores). |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **Creatine Kinase (CK)**: 
  * DMD: Massively ↑↑↑ (10x to 100x upper limit of normal; often >10,000 U/L in early stages).
  * BMD/LGMD: Moderately ↑↑.
  * DM1/FSHD: Normal or mildly ↑.
* **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**: AST and ALT are markedly ↑ in DMD (muscle origin, not liver). *Do not mistakenly diagnose hepatitis.*

### Genetic Testing (Gold Standard Diagnostic Modality)
* **Multiplex Ligation-dependent Probe Amplification (MLPA)**: 1st line for DMD/BMD. Detects exonic deletions/duplications (~70% of cases).
* **Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) / Whole Exome Sequencing (WES)**: 2nd line if MLPA is negative to detect point mutations.
* **PCR with Southern Blot**: Standard for DM1 (quantifies CTG repeat length).

### Electromyography (EMG) and Imaging Findings
* **EMG**: Myopathic pattern (short-duration, low-amplitude, polyphasic motor unit action potentials - MUAPs). 
  * *DM1 specific*: "Dive-bomber" sound on audio output due to myotonic discharges.
* **Echocardiography (ECHO)**: Crucial for detecting dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM).
* **ECG**: DMD (Tall R waves in V1, deep Q waves in lateral leads). DM1 (PR prolongation, AV blocks, bundle branch blocks).

### Muscle Biopsy (Historical/Second-line)
* Indicated *only* if genetic testing is inconclusive.
* **H&E Stain**: Variation in fiber size, necrotic/regenerating fibers, internal nuclei, fibrofatty replacement.
* **Immunohistochemistry**: 
  * DMD: Complete absence of dystrophin staining.
  * BMD: Patchy/reduced dystrophin staining.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Respiratory Failure Resuscitation
* Often triggered by chest infections (e.g., pneumonia) + impaired cough mechanics.
* **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP)**: 1st-line for hypercapnic respiratory failure (Type 2). 
* **Mechanical In-Exsufflation (Cough Assist)**: Mobilizes secretions acutely.
* **Avoid pure high-flow O2** without ventilatory support to prevent loss of hypoxic drive and fatal hypercapnia.

### Acute Arrhythmia and Heart Failure Management
* DM1 patients presenting with syncope/palpitations: Immediate ECG monitoring for high-degree AV blocks or VT. May require emergent temporary pacing.
* Acute decompensated heart failure in DMD/BMD: IV Loop Diuretics (Frusemide), vasodilators. Inotropic support (e.g., Dobutamine/Milrinone) if cardiogenic shock ensues.

### Anesthesia and Malignant Hyperthermia Risk
* DMD/BMD patients are at high risk of **Rhabdomyolysis and Hyperkalemic Cardiac Arrest** with depolarizing muscle relaxants (Suxamethonium/Succinylcholine) and inhalational anesthetics.
* Acute Rx of Hyperkalemia: IV Calcium Gluconate (cardioprotection), Insulin + Dextrose, Salbutamol nebulizer.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### DMD Disease-Modifying Pharmacotherapy (Corticosteroids)
* **Goal**: Delays loss of ambulation by ~2-3 years, preserves respiratory function, and reduces scoliosis progression.
* **Initiation**: Generally started at motor plateau (age 4-5 years) before decline begins.
* **First-line Regimens (HA Formulary)**:
  * Prednisolone: 0.75 mg/kg/day.
  * Deflazacort: 0.9 mg/kg/day (preferred due to ↓ weight gain and ↓ behavioral side effects, but ↑ risk of cataracts).
* **Monitoring**: Closely monitor for weight gain, Cushingoid features, delayed puberty, osteoporosis, and behavioral changes.

### Targeted Genetic Therapies (Off-label / Non-formulary context)
* **Exon Skipping (e.g., Eteplirsen for exon 51, Golodirsen for exon 53)**: Induces in-frame shift to convert DMD phenotype to milder BMD phenotype. (Highly expensive, limited HA availability).
* **Nonsense Mutation Read-through (Ataluren)**: For patients with premature stop codons.

### Chronic Multidisciplinary Cardiopulmonary Algorithm
* **Respiratory**:
  * Baseline FVC tracking. 
  * Prophylactic nocturnal NIV initiated when FVC <50% predicted or symptomatic sleep hypoventilation (morning headaches).
  * Pneumococcal & annual Influenza vaccinations.
* **Cardiology**:
  * Prophylactic **ACE Inhibitors (Perindopril/Lisinopril)** or ARBs initiated by age 10 in DMD (before ECHO changes appear) to delay cardiomyopathy remodeling.
  * Beta-blockers (Bisoprolol/Carvedilol) added if LV systolic dysfunction develops.
* **DM1 Specifics**:
  * Annual Holter monitor / ECG to screen for conduction defects → prophylactic Pacemaker/ICD insertion.
  * Myotonia treatment: Mexiletine (Class 1B antiarrhythmic) or Carbamazepine.

### Orthopedic and Rehabilitation Care
* **Physiotherapy**: Daily passive stretching to prevent Achilles and hip flexor contractures. Night splints (Ankle-foot orthoses - AFOs).
* **Scoliosis Rx**: Posterior spinal fusion surgery often required in DMD once non-ambulatory and Cobb angle >20-30 degrees.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Motor Function Scoring Systems
* **North Star Ambulatory Assessment (NSAA)**: 17-item rating scale specifically designed to measure functional motor abilities in ambulant children with DMD.
* **6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT)**: Used to track disease progression and therapeutic efficacy of steroids/gene therapies.

### Disease Trajectory & Prognosis (DMD vs BMD)
| Feature | Duchenne (DMD) | Becker (BMD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Loss of Ambulation** | Typically wheelchair-dependent by age 10-12 | Ambulation preserved > 16 years (often into adulthood) |
| **Cardiac Involvement** | Universal (DCM) by late teens | Variable, but DCM can be severe/disproportionate to skeletal weakness |
| **Life Expectancy** | Late 20s to 30s (Death via respiratory/heart failure) | Near-normal to mid-adulthood |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Systemic Complications of Dystrophinopathies
* **Neurological**: Non-progressive intellectual disability/cognitive impairment (Dystrophin isoforms are expressed in the brain). High rates of Autism/ADHD.
* **Musculoskeletal**: Severe scoliosis, joint contractures, osteoporosis (exacerbated by steroids and immobility) → pathological fractures.
* **Metabolic/Endocrine**: Obesity (steroids + immobility), adrenal suppression (exogenous steroids), delayed puberty.

### Multi-System Complications of Myotonic Dystrophy (DM1)
* **Ophthalmological**: Early-onset iridescent posterior subcapsular cataracts.
* **Endocrine**: Insulin resistance/Diabetes Mellitus, testicular atrophy, frontal baldness.
* **Gastrointestinal**: Pharyngeal weakness (dysphagia/aspiration), delayed gastric emptying, pseudo-obstruction, cholelithiasis.
* **Cardiac**: Progressive conduction system disease (AV blocks, bundle branch blocks), sudden cardiac death.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Assuming a massively elevated AST/ALT in a young boy indicates viral hepatitis or liver failure. Pearl: Isolated high AST/ALT in a male toddler is often muscle origin. Always check a CK level to rule out DMD before invasive liver workups.
* Viva Question: Why do we not give pure high-flow oxygen to a late-stage DMD patient presenting with shortness of breath? Answer: Late-stage DMD patients have chronic Type 2 respiratory failure. They rely on hypoxic drive. Uncontrolled O2 suppresses this drive, leading to lethal hypercapnia and CO2 narcosis. They require NIV (BiPAP).
* Pitfall: Administering Suxamethonium to a patient with unrecognized muscular dystrophy for intubation. This triggers massive potassium release, causing life-threatening hyperkalemia and cardiac arrest.
* Viva Question: What is the primary difference in the genetic mechanism between Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophy? Answer: Both affect the DMD gene, but DMD involves an out-of-frame mutation causing absent dystrophin, whereas BMD involves an in-frame mutation producing a truncated, partially functional dystrophin (the "Reading Frame Rule").
* Trap: Delaying cardiac screening in DMD until the patient develops heart failure symptoms. Pearl: Cardiomyopathy is often asymptomatic early due to the patient's immobility. Prophylactic ACE inhibitors should start by age 10 regardless of symptoms or ECHO findings.
