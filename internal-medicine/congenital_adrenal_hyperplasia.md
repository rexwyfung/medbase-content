# Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (先天性腎上腺增生症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia
* **Definition:** Group of autosomal recessive (體染色體隱性遺傳) disorders characterized by enzymatic defects in adrenal steroidogenesis.
* **Core Result:** ↓ Cortisol (皮質醇) → lack of negative feedback → ↑ ACTH (促腎上腺皮質激素) → adrenal cortical hyperplasia & overproduction of intermediate metabolites.

### Etiologies & Enzyme Deficiencies
* **21-Hydroxylase Deficiency (21-OHD):** >95% of CAH cases. Mutation in CYP21A2 gene.
* **11β-Hydroxylase Deficiency (11β-OHD):** ~5% of cases. Causes ↑ deoxycorticosterone (DOC) → hypertension + hypokalemia.
* **Rare Variants:** 17α-Hydroxylase deficiency, 3β-Hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (3β-HSD) deficiency, StAR protein deficiency (Congenital lipoid adrenal hyperplasia).

### Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Classical CAH Incidence:** ~1 in 28,000 live births in HK (lower than Caucasian populations ~1:15,000).
* **Non-Classical CAH (NC-CAH):** Highly prevalent globally (1:200 to 1:1000). Common unrecognized cause of female hyperandrogenism in adult HK outpatient clinics.
* **Screening:** Universal neonatal screening for 17-OHP is *not* routinely implemented in all HK HA hospitals; largely driven by clinical suspicion & family history.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### 21-Hydroxylase (21-OH) Blockade Consequences
* **Glucocorticoid Pathway:** Blocks 17-OH Progesterone (17-OHP) → 11-deoxycortisol. Result: ↓ Cortisol.
* **Mineralocorticoid Pathway:** Blocks Progesterone → 11-deoxycorticosterone (DOC). Result: ↓ Aldosterone (醛固酮).
* **ACTH Drive:** ↓ Cortisol → ↑ Pituitary ACTH secretion → Adrenal hyperstimulation.

### Androgen Shunting
* **Mechanism:** Accumulated precursors (17-OHP & Progesterone) are diverted into the intact sex steroid pathway.
* **Result:** ↑ Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), ↑ Androstenedione, ↑ Testosterone → prenatal & postnatal virilization.

### Phenotypic Spectrum (21-OHD)
* **Salt-Wasting (SW) Classical:** <1% enzyme activity. Absolute cortisol & aldosterone deficiency.
* **Simple Virilising (SV) Classical:** 1-2% enzyme activity. Sufficient aldosterone to prevent SW crisis, but marked hyperandrogenism.
* **Non-Classical (NC-CAH):** 20-50% enzyme activity. Normal cortisol/aldosterone baseline, asymptomatic at birth, mild-moderate hyperandrogenism in adolescence/adulthood.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Neonatal/Infantile Presentation (Classical CAH)
* **Hx (SW Crisis):** Poor feeding, recurrent vomiting, severe lethargy, failure to thrive, weight loss.
* **Hx (Genital):** Ambiguous genitalia (性別不明) noted at birth in 46,XX females. 46,XY males appear normal at birth.
* **PE (Genitalia):** Clitoromegaly, partial/complete labial fusion, urogenital sinus in females. Hypospadias or cryptorchidism in males (rarely, primarily in 3β-HSD or StAR mutations).
* **PE (General):** Diffuse hyperpigmentation (色素沉著) (creases, areolae, genitalia) due to ↑ ACTH/MSH co-secretion. Profound dehydration & shock signs.

### Childhood/Adolescent Presentation (SV Classical)
* **Hx:** Precocious puberty (早熟), rapid somatic growth in early childhood, early cessation of growth.
* **PE:** Premature pubarche/adrenarche (early pubic/axillary hair, adult body odor). Short adult stature (premature epiphyseal fusion).

### Adult Presentation (Non-Classical CAH)
* **Hx:** Hirsutism (多毛症), treatment-resistant severe cystic acne, oligomenorrhea, primary/secondary amenorrhea, subfertility, recurrent miscarriages.
* **PE:** Ferriman-Gallwey score >8 (hirsutism), androgenic alopecia (frontoparietal thinning), absent hyperpigmentation.
* **PE (Male):** Often asymptomatic. May have palpable firm, bilateral testicular masses (Testicular Adrenal Rest Tumors - TARTs).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Adult Female Hyperandrogenism Differentials
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)** | Normal/mild ↑ 17-OHP. Prominent insulin resistance/acanthosis nigricans. USG shows polycystic ovaries. |
| **NC-CAH** | Markedly ↑ 17-OHP. Family history of CAH. Hyperandrogenism often more severe than PCOS. |
| **Androgen-Secreting Tumour** | Rapid onset virilization (voice deepening, clitoromegaly in adult). Markedly ↑↑ Testosterone (>5 nmol/L) or ↑↑ DHEAS. |
| **Cushing's Syndrome** | Proximal myopathy, striae, bruising, central obesity. Fails dexamethasone suppression. |

### Neonatal Ambiguous Genitalia / Shock Differentials
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **11β-Hydroxylase Deficiency** | Phenotypically similar virilization, but typically presents with **Hypertension** (not shock). |
| **Pyloric Stenosis** | Vomiting infant, but features *hypo*kalemic *alkalosis* (vs hyperkalemic acidosis in CAH). Palpable "olive" mass. |
| **Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome** | 46,XY phenotypic female. Blind vaginal pouch, absent uterus, undescended testes. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **ECG:** Tall peaked T waves, widened QRS, absent P waves, sine wave pattern (secondary to severe hyperkalemia in SW crisis).
* **Capillary Blood Glucose:** Hypoglycemia (due to hypocortisolism).

### Blood Investigations (Endocrine & Biochemical)
* **17-OH Progesterone (17-OHP) (17-羥孕酮):** Primary screening/diagnostic marker. Early morning sample. ↑↑ in classical CAH.
* **Short Synacthen Test (SST):** Gold standard for NC-CAH. Baseline and 60-min post-250mcg ACTH (Cosyntropin) IV. NC-CAH shows exaggerated 17-OHP response (>30 nmol/L post-stimulation).
* **Serum Androgens:** ↑ Androstenedione, ↑ Testosterone. Normal/mildly ↑ DHEAS.
* **Aldosterone & Renin:** ↓ Aldosterone, ↑↑ Plasma Renin Activity (PRA) or Direct Renin (marker of SW).
* **Electrolytes/ABG:** Hyponatremia, Hyperkalemia, Non-anion gap metabolic acidosis (SW crisis).

### Imaging & Special Tests
* **Pelvic USG:** Confirms presence of uterus/ovaries in 46,XX infants with ambiguous genitalia. Rules out ovarian tumors in adults.
* **Scrotal USG (Males):** Detects hypoechoic, bilateral Testicular Adrenal Rest Tumors (TARTs).
* **Adrenal CT/MRI:** Bilateral adrenal hyperplasia (bulky glands). Excludes adrenal adenoma/carcinoma.
* **Karyotype:** 46,XX or 46,XY to confirm chromosomal sex in ambiguous genitalia.

### Diagnostic Criteria (17-OHP levels)
| CAH Phenotype | Basal 17-OHP (Morning) | Stimulated 17-OHP (Post-SST) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classical CAH** | >300 nmol/L (Diagnostic) | Not usually required |
| **Non-Classical (NC-CAH)** | >6 nmol/L (Indicates need for SST) | >30 nmol/L (Diagnostic) |
| **Heterozygote Carrier** | Normal or slightly ↑ | <30 nmol/L |
| **Normal** | <6 nmol/L | <10 nmol/L |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Adrenal Crisis (Salt-Wasting) Resuscitation Protocol
* **Fluid Resuscitation:** Stat IV 0.9% NaCl 20 mL/kg bolus. Use 0.9% NaCl + 5% Dextrose for maintenance to prevent/treat hypoglycemia. Avoid hypotonic fluids.
* **Acute Glucocorticoid Replacement:** Stat IV Hydrocortisone (氫化可的松). 
  * Neonates/Infants: 25 mg IV stat.
  * Children: 50 mg IV stat.
  * Adults: 100 mg IV stat, followed by 200 mg/24h continuous infusion or 50 mg Q6H IV.
* **Hyperkalemia Management (If ECG changes or K+ >6.5):**
  * IV 10% Calcium Gluconate (protect myocardium).
  * IV Insulin-Dextrose infusion.
  * Inhaled Salbutamol.
* **Mineralocorticoid Therapy:** Fludrocortisone is NOT effective acutely. High-dose IV Hydrocortisone provides sufficient cross-reactivity at the mineralocorticoid receptor during resuscitation.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Glucocorticoid (GC) Replacement Therapy
* **Goal:** Replace physiological cortisol + suppress ACTH (to reduce excess androgens).
* **Paediatric Regimen:** Oral Hydrocortisone (10-15 mg/m²/day in 3 divided doses). Preferred to minimize growth suppression.
* **Adult Regimen (Transitioned post-puberty):**
  * Oral Dexamethasone (地塞米松) 0.25-0.75 mg ON. Long-acting, single bedtime dose effectively suppresses the early morning ACTH surge.
  * Alternative: Oral Prednisolone (潑尼松龍) 5-7.5 mg daily (split dose or single bedtime).
  * Alternative: Oral Hydrocortisone 15-25 mg/day (e.g., 10 mg AM, 5 mg afternoon, 5 mg ON) - mimics diurnal rhythm but harder compliance.

### Mineralocorticoid (MC) & Salt Replacement
* **Indication:** Classical SW patients, or SV patients with elevated plasma renin.
* **Pharmacology:** Oral Fludrocortisone (氟氫可的松) 0.05-0.2 mg daily.
* **Salt Supplementation:** Infants/Neonates require 1-2g NaCl supplementation daily (infant kidneys are insensitive to MC). Discontinued when solid foods introduced.

### Non-Classical CAH (NC-CAH) Management
* **Asymptomatic:** No active treatment required.
* **Symptomatic Adult Females (Hirsutism/Oligo-ovulation):** 
  * 1st Line: Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COCP) + Anti-androgens (e.g., Spironolactone 螺內酯 or Cyproterone acetate).
  * 2nd Line (or if fertility desired): Low-dose bedtime GC (e.g., Prednisolone 5mg ON) to suppress adrenal androgens and trigger ovulation.
* **Adult Males:** Treatment rarely needed unless TARTs develop or subfertility occurs.

### Antenatal Management (High-Risk Pregnancies)
* **Goal:** Prevent virilization of affected 46,XX fetus.
* **Protocol:** Maternal administration of oral Dexamethasone (crosses placenta un-metabolized by 11β-HSD2) starting at 6-7 weeks gestation (before external genitalia differentiation).
* **Step-Down:** Discontinue if chorionic villus sampling (CVS) confirms male fetus or unaffected female fetus. Maintain until term if affected female.

### "Sick Day" Rules & Lifestyle
* **Fever/Minor Illness/Minor Surgery:** Double or triple standard oral GC dose. NO change to Fludrocortisone.
* **Vomiting/Diarrhoea/Major Trauma:** Requires IM Hydrocortisone (100mg for adults) or immediate A&E admission for IV therapy. Provide patient with steroid alert card/bracelet.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prader Scale for Genital Virilization (Neonatal)
Used to stage the degree of virilization in 46,XX neonates:
* **Stage 1:** Clitoromegaly only.
* **Stage 2:** Clitoromegaly + partial labial fusion.
* **Stage 3:** Significant fusion, single urogenital opening (funnel shape).
* **Stage 4:** Micro-penis appearance, completely fused scrotum-like labia, but urethral meatus at base.
* **Stage 5:** Complete male appearance (penile urethra) but empty scrotum (cryptorchidism).

### Monitoring Titration & Target Parameters
| Parameter | Target in CAH Management | Indication of Over-treatment | Indication of Under-treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **17-OHP** | Upper limit of normal / mildly elevated (12-30 nmol/L) | Suppressed (<3 nmol/L) | Markedly elevated |
| **Androstenedione** | Age/sex appropriate normal limits | Suppressed | Elevated |
| **Plasma Renin** | Mid-to-upper normal range | Suppressed (↓) | Elevated (↑) |
| **Bone Age (Paeds)** | Matches chronological age | Delayed bone age | Advanced bone age |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications (Under-treatment)
* **Reproductive:** Infertility, secondary amenorrhea, severe hyperandrogenism.
* **Urological/Male:** TARTs expansion → compression of seminiferous tubules → irreversible obstructive azoospermia and gonadal failure.
* **Skeletal:** Premature epiphyseal fusion leading to compromised final adult height.
* **Psychiatric:** Psychosexual difficulties, gender dysphoria (in severely virilized females).

### Iatrogenic Complications (Over-treatment with GC/MC)
* **Metabolic (GC Excess):** Iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome, visceral obesity, insulin resistance, Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.
* **Skeletal (GC Excess):** Osteopenia/Osteoporosis (bone densitometry monitoring required in adult patients), avascular necrosis of femoral head.
* **Cardiovascular (MC Excess):** Refractory hypertension, fluid retention, hypokalemia (due to Fludrocortisone excess).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you biochemically differentiate Non-Classical CAH from PCOS in an adult female? Answer: Measure early morning 17-OHP in the follicular phase. If 17-OHP is >6 nmol/L but <30 nmol/L, perform a Short Synacthen Test. NC-CAH will hyper-respond (>30 nmol/L), PCOS will not.
* Trap: Prescribing Fludrocortisone for a patient with 11β-Hydroxylase deficiency CAH. Pearl: 11β-OHD patients accumulate 11-deoxycorticosterone (DOC), a potent mineralocorticoid, making them hypertensive and hypokalemic. Prescribing Fludrocortisone will precipitate severe hypertensive crisis.
* Pitfall: Attempting to titrate glucocorticoid doses to push 17-OHP down to the "normal" range. Pearl: Complete normalization of 17-OHP usually requires supra-physiological doses of steroids, inevitably causing severe iatrogenic Cushing's and metabolic syndrome. The target 17-OHP is mildly elevated (e.g., 12-30 nmol/L) to strike a balance between suppressing androgens and avoiding steroid toxicity.
* Viva Question: Why do we use Dexamethasone for antenatal therapy but prefer Hydrocortisone for postnatal childhood maintenance? Answer: Dexamethasone bypasses placental 11β-HSD2 to effectively suppress fetal ACTH, but its potent, long-acting nature severely stunts linear bone growth in children. Hydrocortisone is short-acting, minimizing growth suppression during development.
* Trap: Assuming a CAH patient experiencing severe vomiting at home can simply "double" their oral steroid dose per sick day rules. Pearl: Vomiting negates oral absorption. They must be instructed to self-administer IM Hydrocortisone immediately and present to A&E for IV fluids and IV steroids to prevent adrenal crisis.
