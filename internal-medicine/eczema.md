# Eczema (濕疹)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology
### Definition of Eczema (濕疹)
* **Clinical phenotype:** Chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease.
* **Hallmark triad:** Intense pruritus (瘙癢), erythema (紅斑), and scaling/vesiculation.
* **Morphological spectrum:** Acute (vesicles/weeping) → Subacute (scaling/crusting) → Chronic (lichenification/thickening).

### Etiological Subtypes of Eczema
* **Atopic Dermatitis (異位性皮炎):** Genetic + environmental Th2 immune dysregulation.
* **Contact Dermatitis (接觸性皮炎):** Exogenous triggers. 
  * **Irritant:** Direct toxic damage (80%).
  * **Allergic:** Type IV delayed hypersensitivity (20%).
* **Seborrhoeic Dermatitis (脂溢性皮炎):** Immune reaction to *Malassezia* spp. yeast.
* **Discoid/Nummular Eczema (盤狀濕疹):** Idiopathic, dry skin-related, highly pruritic coin-shaped plaques.
* **Venous/Stasis Eczema (靜脈曲張性濕疹):** Secondary to chronic venous insufficiency/hypertension.
* **Dyshidrotic Eczema / Pompholyx (汗皰疹):** Idiopathic vesicular eruption on palms/soles, ↑ with stress/sweating.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** Highly prevalent in HK children (~15-20% affected); ~3-5% of adults.
* **Climate factor:** HK's hot, humid summer ↑ sweating/pruritus (sweat retention); air-conditioning ↑ winter xerosis (乾燥).
* **Atopic March:** Eczema often precedes food allergy → asthma (哮喘) → allergic rhinitis (鼻敏感).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms
### Pathophysiology of Atopic Dermatitis
* **Genetic barrier defect:** *FLG* (Filaggrin) gene loss-of-function mutation → defective epidermal tight junctions.
* **Physical consequence:** ↑ Transepidermal water loss (TEWL) + ↑ allergen/microbe penetration.
* **Immunological skewing:** Epidermal keratinocyte damage releases TSLP/IL-33 → skews naive T-cells to **Th2 phenotype**.
* **Cytokine cascade:** Th2 cells release IL-4, IL-13, IL-31 (the "itch cytokine") → IgE production, eosinophil recruitment, and pruritus.

### Pathophysiology of Contact Dermatitis
* **Irritant Contact Dermatitis (ICD):** Non-immunological. Harsh chemicals (soaps, solvents, alkalis) disrupt stratum corneum lipids → direct keratinocyte apoptosis/necrosis.
* **Allergic Contact Dermatitis (ACD):** Type IV (delayed) hypersensitivity.
  * **Sensitization phase:** Hapten penetrates skin → binds self-protein → taken up by Langerhans cells → migrates to lymph node → activates naive T cells to memory T cells (7-14 days).
  * **Elicitation phase:** Re-exposure → memory T cells release IFN-γ, IL-2 → rapid macrophage/cytotoxic T-cell mediated tissue damage (24-72 hours).

### Pathophysiology of Stasis Eczema
* **Mechanism:** Valvular incompetence → venous hypertension → capillary distension → extravasation of fibrinogen (forms pericapillary barrier restricting O2) and RBCs.
* **Sequelae:** RBC breakdown deposits hemosiderin (brown pigment); tissue hypoxia triggers microvascular inflammation → eczema.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)
### History in Eczema Assessment
* **Core symptom:** Pruritus ("the itch that rashes"). If no itch, question the Dx of AD.
* **Atopic history:** Personal or 1st-degree family history of the atopic triad.
* **Environmental triggers:** Hot water, sweating, wool/synthetic clothing, stress, aeroallergens (dust mites, pet dander).
* **Contact history:** Occupation (hairdressers, cleaners, mechanics), hobbies, new cosmetics/jewelry.

### Age-Specific Distribution of Atopic Dermatitis
* **Infantile (<2 yrs):** Face (cheeks), scalp, extensor surfaces of limbs. *Crucial:* Spares the diaper area (amniotic fluid protection / humidity).
* **Childhood (2-12 yrs):** Flexural creases (antecubital and popliteal fossae), neck, wrists, ankles.
* **Adult (>12 yrs):** Flexures, face (eyelids), hands. Prominent lichenification.

### Physical Signs of Specific Eczema Subtypes
* **Atopic:** Ill-defined erythematous patches, excoriation marks, lichenification (exaggerated skin markings).
* **Discoid (Nummular):** Well-demarcated, coin-shaped eczematous plaques; predominantly on limbs; intensely itchy.
* **Dyshidrotic (Pompholyx):** Deep-seated, "tapioca pudding-like" vesicles on lateral aspects of fingers, palms, and soles.
* **Seborrhoeic:** Ill-defined, greasy yellow scales on erythematous base. Affects sebum-rich areas: scalp (dandruff), nasolabial folds, eyebrows, presternal area.
* **Stasis/Venous:** Lower legs (gaiter region). Erythema, scaling, woody induration (lipodermatosclerosis), brown hemosiderin staining, varicose veins.
* **Contact:** Distribution strictly mirrors the area of exposure (e.g., infraumbilical from belt buckle [Nickel], dorsum of feet from shoe dye/rubber).

### Eponymous Signs & Stigmata of Atopy
* **Dennie-Morgan lines:** Prominent folds/pleats below the inferior eyelids.
* **Hertoghe sign:** Thinning or loss of the lateral third of the eyebrows (due to chronic rubbing).
* **Keratosis pilaris:** Rough, horny follicular papules on extensor upper arms/thighs.
* **Hyperlinear palms:** Exaggerated palmar skin creases.
* **White dermographism:** Blanching (vasoconstriction) upon stroking the skin (instead of normal erythema).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features
### Differentiating Eczema vs Psoriasis (銀屑病)
| Feature | Eczema (AD) | Psoriasis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Borders** | Ill-defined | Well-demarcated |
| **Scale** | Fine, bran-like, excoriated | Thick, silvery-white |
| **Distribution** | Flexural (antecubital, popliteal) | Extensor (knees, elbows, scalp, sacrum) |
| **Pruritus** | Severe, constant | Variable, often mild/absent |
| **Nails** | Nonspecific pitting/ridging | Deep pitting, oil-drop sign, onycholysis |

### Differentiating Eczema Subtypes vs Other Conditions
* **Discoid Eczema vs Tinea Corporis (Body Ringworm):** 
  * **Tinea:** Annular (ring-like) with central clearing and an active, scaly leading edge. Unilateral.
  * **Discoid:** Uniformly scaly/inflamed throughout (no central clearing). Bilateral.
* **Atopic Dermatitis vs Scabies (疥瘡):** 
  * **Scabies:** Pruritus aggressively worse at night, presence of burrows in web spaces/genitals, positive close-contact history.
* **Seborrhoeic Dermatitis vs Rosacea:** 
  * **Rosacea:** Erythema, telangiectasia, papules/pustules on central face; ↑ by hot drinks/spicy food/alcohol. Lacks the greasy scale of seborrhoeic dermatitis.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)
### Bedside & Clinical Investigations
* **Skin Swab:** For bacterial C&S (rule out *S. aureus* superinfection) or Viral PCR (rule out HSV/Eczema Herpeticum).
* **Skin Scraping:** For KOH microscopy and fungal culture to exclude Tinea/Dermatophyte infection.

### Blood Tests for Atopy
* **Serum Total IgE:** Usually markedly ↑ (correlates loosely with disease severity).
* **Eosinophil Count:** ↑ in peripheral blood.
* **Allergen-specific IgE (RAST/ImmunoCAP):** To identify specific food/aeroallergen triggers (reserved for cases unresponsive to therapy or clear clinical suspicion).

### Special Tests: Patch Testing (貼布試驗)
* **Indication:** Gold standard for diagnosing Allergic Contact Dermatitis.
* **Methodology:** Standard series of allergens (e.g., European Baseline Series: Nickel, fragrance mix, rubber accelerators, preservatives) applied to intact skin on the back in shallow chambers.
* **Interpretation:** Left for 48 hours → removed and read → read again at 96 hours. A positive reaction shows localized erythema, induration, or vesiculation (Type IV hypersensitivity).
* **Contraindication:** Do NOT test during a severe generalized flare or if taking oral immunosuppressants (false negatives).

### Diagnostic Criteria for Atopic Dermatitis (UK Working Party Criteria)
* **Mandatory criterion:** An itchy skin condition (or parental report of scratching/rubbing).
* **Plus ≥ 3 of the following minor criteria:**
  * **1.** History of involvement of skin creases (antecubital, popliteal fossae, front of ankles, neck) OR cheeks if <10 years old.
  * **2.** Personal history of asthma or hay fever (or history of atopic disease in a 1st-degree relative if child <4 years old).
  * **3.** History of general dry skin in the past year.
  * **4.** Visible flexural eczema (or eczema involving cheeks/forehead and outer limbs in children <4 years old).
  * **5.** Onset under the age of 2 years (not used if child is <4 years old).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)
### Acute Severe Eczema Flare Management
* **Wet Wrap Therapy ("Soak and Seal"):** Apply topical steroid → cover with wet tubular bandage (Tubifast) → cover with dry bandage. ↑ Stratum corneum hydration + ↑ 10x absorption of steroid. Max 7-14 days.
* **Systemic Corticosteroids:** Oral Prednisolone (30-40mg daily for 5-7 days) reserved ONLY for severe, intractable flares not responding to topicals. Warn patient about rebound flare upon withdrawal.
* **Sedating Antihistamines:** Oral Chlorpheniramine (Piriton) or Hydroxyzine at night strictly to break the "itch-scratch cycle" and aid sleep. (Non-sedating antihistamines like Loratadine have little role in AD pruritus, as it is non-histamine mediated).

### Resuscitation Protocol for Eczema Herpeticum (皰疹性濕疹)
* **Definition:** Medical emergency. Disseminated HSV-1/2 infection superimposed on AD skin barrier defect.
* **Clinical signs:** Monomorphic (all same stage), punched-out, umbilicated vesicles/erosions with hemorrhagic crusts, fever, lethargy, lymphadenopathy.
* **Management:**
  * **Admit:** Immediate hospital admission + Contact Isolation.
  * **Antiviral:** IV Acyclovir (阿昔洛韋) urgently (Do NOT wait for swab PCR results). Switch to oral once new lesions cease.
  * **Hold immunomodulators:** Immediately STOP topical calcineurin inhibitors (TCIs) as they impair local viral defense. (Topical steroids may be continued or cautiously reduced).
  * **Ophthalmology Consult:** Urgent referral if lesions are near the eye (periorbital) to rule out HSV keratitis (risk of blindness).

### Management of Secondary Bacterial Infection
* **Causative agent:** 90% driven by *Staphylococcus aureus* colonization/infection.
* **Signs:** Golden/honey-colored crusting, weeping exudate, pustules, rapidly worsening erythema.
* **Localized infection:** Topical antibiotics (Fusidic acid or Mupirocin) for 7-10 days. Avoid prolonged use to prevent resistance.
* **Widespread/Systemic infection:** Oral systemic antibiotics. 1st line in HK: Cloxacillin or Cephalexin. (Use Macrolides like Erythromycin/Clarithromycin if penicillin-allergic).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)
### Base Therapy: Lifestyle & Emollients (潤膚膏)
* **Avoidance:** Avoid harsh soaps, hot water, biological washing powders, wool/synthetic clothing.
* **Bathing protocol:** Luke-warm water, <10 minutes. Use soap substitutes (e.g., Aqueous cream, Emulsifying ointment, Bath oils).
* **Emollient application ("3-Minute Rule"):** Apply thick emollients liberally and frequently (at least BID), ideally within 3 minutes of exiting the bath to trap moisture.
* **Emollient hierarchy:** Ointments (high lipid, very greasy, highly occlusive, best for extreme xerosis) > Creams (lipid/water emulsion, cosmetically acceptable) > Lotions (high water, evaporative, poor moisturization).

### Topical Corticosteroids (TCS) Potency Ladder (HK HA Formulary)
| Potency | HK HA Examples (Generics) | Indication / Site of Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | Hydrocortisone 1% ointment | Face, eyelids, flexures, genitals, infants |
| **Moderate** | Clobetasone butyrate 0.05% (Eumovate) | Mild trunk/limb disease, step-down maintenance |
| **Potent** | Betamethasone valerate 0.1% (Betnovate), Mometasone furoate 0.1% | Severe flares on trunk, limbs, scalp |
| **Very Potent** | Clobetasol propionate 0.05% (Dermovate) | Palms, soles, severely lichenified plaques (max 2 weeks) |
* **Dosing (Fingertip Unit - FTU):** 1 FTU (line of ointment from adult distal IP joint to fingertip) = ~0.5g = treats an area equal to two flat adult palms.
* **Application technique:** Apply TCS strictly to inflamed areas; apply emollient to the whole body (wait 15-30 mins between the two to prevent diluting the steroid).

### Topical Calcineurin Inhibitors (TCIs)
* **Agents:** Tacrolimus 0.03% or 0.1% ointment (Protopic), Pimecrolimus 1% cream (Elidel).
* **Mechanism:** Inhibit calcineurin → block NFAT dephosphorylation → inhibit T-cell transcription of IL-2.
* **Indication:** 2nd-line for moderate-to-severe AD. **Steroid-sparing:** Ideal for thin-skinned areas (face, eyelids, neck, flexures) where prolonged TCS causes atrophy.
* **Adverse effects:** Localized burning/stinging sensation upon application (counsel patient: usually resolves after 1 week of use).

### Step-Up Algorithm for Severe/Refractory AD
* **Step 1: Phototherapy (光照治療):** Narrowband UVB (311nm). Immunosuppressive in the skin. Given 2-3x/week.
* **Step 2: Oral Immunosuppressants:**
  * Cyclosporine A: Fastest onset (weeks). Risk of nephrotoxicity, hypertension.
  * Methotrexate: Slower onset. Risk of hepatotoxicity, myelosuppression. Requires folic acid.
  * Azathioprine / Mycophenolate Mofetil: Alternatives for long-term maintenance.
* **Step 3: Biologics (生物製劑):** 
  * **Dupilumab (達必妥):** Fully human monoclonal antibody targeting **IL-4Rα** subunit. Dual inhibition of IL-4 and IL-13 signaling. Highly effective, safe profile. AE: Injection site reactions, conjunctivitis.
  * Tralokinumab: Anti-IL-13 monoclonal antibody.
* **Step 4: JAK Inhibitors:**
  * Oral small molecules (Upadacitinib, Baricitinib, Abrocitinib). Block JAK/STAT pathway. Extremely rapid itch relief. Require lab monitoring (CBC, lipids, LFTs).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis
### SCORAD (SCORing Atopic Dermatitis) Index
| Assessment Area | Measurement Criteria | Score Range |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A: Extent** | Rule of Nines calculation of affected body surface area (BSA) | 0 - 100 |
| **B: Intensity** | Evaluate 6 signs: Erythema, edema/papulation, oozing/crusting, excoriation, lichenification, dryness (each graded 0-3) | 0 - 18 |
| **C: Subjective** | Patient-reported Visual Analog Scale (VAS) for Pruritus and Sleep Loss | 0 - 20 |
* **Calculation:** (A/5) + (7B/2) + C
* **Stratification:** Mild (<25), Moderate (25-50), Severe (>50).

### EASI (Eczema Area and Severity Index)
* **Usage:** Preferred scoring system in HA clinics to determine eligibility for biologic therapy funding.
* **Method:** Divides body into 4 regions (Head/Neck, Trunk, Upper Limbs, Lower Limbs). Grades extent (0-6) and 4 severity signs (erythema, induration/papulation, excoriation, lichenification) on a 0-3 scale. Max score: 72.

### Prognosis & Natural History
* **Childhood AD:** ~60-70% of infantile AD undergoes spontaneous remission by adolescence (puberty).
* **Adult-onset AD:** Tends to be more chronic and recalcitrant to basic therapies.
* **Poor prognostic factors:** Early age of onset, severe widespread disease in childhood, concomitant asthma/allergic rhinitis, strong family history, very high IgE levels.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)
### Eczema Disease Complications
* **Infectious:** ↑ Susceptibility to bacterial (*S. aureus* bacteremia/cellulitis), viral (Eczema Herpeticum, Eczema Coxsackium, Molluscum contagiosum), and superficial dermatophyte infections.
* **Ophthalmological (Atopic Keratoconjunctivitis):** Chronic eye rubbing leads to keratoconus (conical cornea) and anterior subcapsular cataracts. High risk of vision impairment.
* **Psychosocial/Psychiatric:** Severe sleep deprivation → poor school/work performance, ADHD-like symptoms in children, high rates of clinical depression and anxiety, social isolation due to skin appearance.

### Iatrogenic Complications (Therapy-Related)
* **Topical Corticosteroid Side Effects:**
  * **Cutaneous:** Skin atrophy (epidermal thinning), striae (stretch marks), telangiectasia (spider veins), purpura, delayed wound healing.
  * **Rebound:** Steroid-induced rosacea, perioral dermatitis, Tachyphylaxis (tolerance).
  * **Systemic (rare, requires massive use):** Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome, growth retardation in children.
* **Calcineurin Inhibitors:** Black box warning (FDA) for theoretical long-term malignancy risk (lymphoma/skin cancer), though extensive epidemiological data has not proven a causal link.
* **Dupilumab:** High rates of paradoxical allergic conjunctivitis and transient eosinophilia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)
* Viva Question: What is the classic dermatological presentation of Eczema Herpeticum? Answer: Monomorphic, punched-out erosions or umbilicated vesicles on an erythematous base with hemorrhagic crusting, accompanied by systemic symptoms like fever and lymphadenopathy.
* Trap: Prescribing potent topical steroids for facial eczema. Pearl: The face has very thin skin and is highly susceptible to steroid-induced irreversible atrophy and telangiectasia. Always use mild steroids (Hydrocortisone 1%) or steroid-sparing agents (Tacrolimus) for facial, eyelid, and flexural involvement.
* Pitfall: Confusing Seborrhoeic Dermatitis with Atopic Dermatitis in infants. Seborrhoeic dermatitis typically affects the nappy area and axillae but is largely NON-itchy. Atopic dermatitis almost always spares the nappy area and is intensely pruritic.
* Viva Question: How does Dupilumab specifically work in the treatment of Atopic Dermatitis? Answer: It is a monoclonal antibody that targets and binds to the IL-4 receptor alpha subunit (IL-4Rα). This blocks both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling cascades, which are the primary Th2 cytokines driving atopic inflammation and pruritus.
* Trap: Recommending aqueous cream as a leave-on emollient. Pearl: Aqueous cream contains sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS), a known skin irritant. It should ONLY be used as a wash-off soap substitute, never as a leave-on moisturizer, as it can exacerbate eczema barrier breakdown.
* Pitfall: Under-dosing topical steroids due to "steroid phobia". Informing patients to apply a "thin smear" leads to subtherapeutic dosing and chronic poorly-controlled disease. Teach the Fingertip Unit (FTU) method to ensure adequate, measurable application.
* Viva Question: A patient with venous eczema presents with a sudden, severe, sharply demarcated erythematous flare extending beyond the usual eczematous area, with fever. What is the diagnosis? Answer: Superimposed cellulitis. Venous eczema compromises the skin barrier, creating a portal of entry for Streptococcus pyogenes or Staphylococcus aureus.
