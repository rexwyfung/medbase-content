# Gram-Negative Cocci (革蘭氏陰性球菌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Microbiological Classification of Gram-Negative Cocci
* **Gram-Negative Diplococci (革蘭氏陰性雙球菌):** Usually appear as pairs of kidney-bean or coffee-bean shaped cells.
* **Key Human Pathogens:**
  * *Neisseria meningitidis* (腦膜炎雙球菌): Encapsulated, normal flora in nasopharynx of 10% adults.
  * *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* (淋球菌): Non-encapsulated, obligate human pathogen (never normal flora).
  * *Moraxella catarrhalis* (卡他莫拉菌): Commensal of upper respiratory tract, opportunistic pathogen.
* **Shared Biochemical Traits:** Oxidase +ve, Catalase +ve.

### Epidemiology of Neisseria meningitidis in Hong Kong
* **Incidence:** Low but highly fatal (case fatality ~10%). Endemic with occasional small clusters.
* **Serogroups:** Defined by polysaccharide capsule.
  * Globally: A, B, C, W, X, Y.
  * HK Epidemiology: Serogroup B most common, followed by Serogroup C and W. 
* **Transmission:** Respiratory droplets/secretions; requires close contact.
* **Peak Age:** Bimodal (infants <1 yr, adolescents/young adults).

### Epidemiology of Neisseria gonorrhoeae in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** 2nd most common bacterial STI globally & locally (after *Chlamydia*).
* **AMR Threat:** Rapidly ↑ antimicrobial resistance (AMR).
  * HK Social Hygiene Clinics report widespread fluoroquinolone & tetracycline resistance.
  * Emerging threat: Decreased susceptibility to 3rd-gen cephalosporins (Ceftriaxone) & Macrolides (Azithromycin).
* **High-Risk Groups:** MSM, commercial sex workers, young adults <25 yrs.

### Epidemiology of Moraxella catarrhalis
* **Prevalence:** 3rd most common cause of acute otitis media (children) & acute bacterial exacerbations of COPD (AECOPD) in adults (after *H. influenzae* & *S. pneumoniae*).
* **AMR Profile:** >95% strains globally and in HK produce β-lactamase (inherently resistant to amoxicillin/ampicillin).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Shared Virulence Factors of Neisseria Species
* **Lipooligosaccharide (LOS) (脂寡糖):** Endotoxin lacking the repeating O-antigen of classical LPS. Causes massive immune activation → TNF-α release → septic shock.
* **IgA1 Protease:** Cleaves secretory IgA → mucosal colonization.
* **Pili (Fimbriae):** Allow attachment to mucosal cells; undergo rapid antigenic variation to evade immune detection.

### Pathogenesis of Neisseria meningitidis
* **Polysaccharide Capsule:** Crucial for survival in bloodstream; prevents phagocytosis & complement-mediated lysis. Determines serogroup.
* **Factor H Binding Protein (fHbp):** Binds host Factor H to surface → inhibits alternative complement pathway.
* **Pathophysiologic Cascade:** Nasopharyngeal colonization → transmigration across epithelium → survival in blood (capsule) → LOS blebbing → massive endothelial injury → Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) & capillary leak.

### Pathogenesis of Neisseria gonorrhoeae
* **Lack of Capsule:** Restricts survival in bloodstream (usually localized mucosal infection), but allows robust intracellular survival.
* **Opa Proteins (Opacity Proteins):** Facilitate tight adherence and internalization into neutrophils and epithelial cells.
* **Intracellular Survival:** Survives and multiplies within polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs) → purulent exudate.

### Pathogenesis of Moraxella catarrhalis
* **Biofilm Formation:** Protects against host immunity and antibiotics → chronic colonization in structural lung disease (COPD, bronchiectasis).
* **BRO β-lactamases:** Plasmids encode BRO-1/BRO-2 enzymes → cleave β-lactam ring of penicillins.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History & Signs of Meningococcemia (腦膜炎雙球菌血症)
* **History:** Sudden onset severe myalgia, fever, cold hands/feet, prior upper respiratory prodrome. Close contact in dormitories/camps.
* **Dermatological Signs:** 
  * Early: Blanching erythematous maculopapular rash.
  * Late (Pathognomonic): Non-blanching petechiae (瘀點) evolving into purpura fulminans (extensive ecchymoses with central necrosis).
* **Cardiovascular Signs:** Profound hypotension, delayed capillary refill, mottled skin (livedo reticularis).

### History & Signs of Disseminated Gonococcal Infection (DGI)
* **History:** Recent unprotected sexual contact. Often asymptomatic mucosal primary infection.
* **Classic Triad:**
  * **Tenosynovitis (腱鞘炎):** Multiple tendons (wrists, fingers, ankles, toes).
  * **Dermatitis:** Painless, non-pruritic pustular or vesiculopustular skin lesions on erythematous base (usually distal extremities).
  * **Polyarthralgia:** Migratory, asymmetric joint pain.
* **Septic Arthritis:** Purulent, monoarticular swelling (often knee or wrist) lacking skin lesions.

### History & Signs of Moraxella catarrhalis Infection
* **History (AECOPD):** Known COPD, ↑ dyspnea, ↑ sputum volume, ↑ sputum purulence.
* **Respiratory Signs:** Wheeze, coarse crepitations, use of accessory muscles, prolonged expiratory phase.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Gram-Negative Cocci & Similar Pathogens
| Feature | *N. meningitidis* | *N. gonorrhoeae* | *M. catarrhalis* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Site** | CNS, Blood | Genitourinary, Joints, Pharynx | Respiratory tract, Middle ear |
| **Morphology** | Kidney-bean diplococci | Kidney-bean diplococci (intracellular in PMNs) | Diplococci |
| **Maltose Fermentation** | +ve | -ve | -ve |
| **Capsule** | Yes | No | No |
| **Typical Host** | Healthy young adults/infants | Sexually active individuals | COPD patients, young children |

### Differentiating Urethral/Cervical Discharge
* ***N. gonorrhoeae*:** Copious, thick, purulent (yellow/green) discharge. Rapid onset (2-7 days post-exposure).
* ***Chlamydia trachomatis*:** Scanty, mucoid or watery discharge. Slower onset (1-3 weeks).

### Differentiating AECOPD Pathogens
* ***M. catarrhalis*:** Indistinguishable clinically, but strictly suspected if patient fails initial amoxicillin monotherapy.
* ***H. influenzae*:** Gram -ve coccobacilli.
* ***S. pneumoniae*:** Gram +ve diplococci.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Gram Stain Findings for Gram-Negative Cocci
* **Gram Stain of CSF/Blood/Skin Aspirate (Meningococcal):** Gram -ve diplococci, often extracellular and intracellular.
* **Gram Stain of Urethral Exudate (Gonococcal):** Gram -ve diplococci strictly *intracellular* within PMNs. Highly sensitive in symptomatic males (>95%); less sensitive in females (require NAAT).
* **Gram Stain of Sputum (*M. catarrhalis*):** Intra/extracellular Gram -ve diplococci amid abundant PMNs.

### Culture Media & Biochemical Diagnostics for Neisseria
* **Thayer-Martin Agar:** Selective chocolate agar with antibiotics (Vancomycin, Colistin, Nystatin, Trimethoprim) to inhibit normal flora (specifically required for *N. gonorrhoeae* from mucosal sites).
* **Standard Chocolate Agar:** Cultivates both *N. meningitidis* & *N. gonorrhoeae* from sterile sites (blood, CSF, joint fluid).
* **Oxidase Test:** Rapidly +ve (turns dark purple).
* **Carbohydrate Utilization:** 
  * *N. gonorrhoeae*: Glucose +ve ONLY.
  * *N. meningitidis*: Glucose +ve, Maltose +ve.

### Molecular Diagnostics (NAAT/PCR)
* **Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT):** Gold standard for *N. gonorrhoeae*. Specimens: First-catch urine (males), vaginal/endocervical swab (females), rectal/pharyngeal swabs.
* **Multiplex PCR:** CSF/Blood PCR for *N. meningitidis* (crucial if antibiotics already given, rendering cultures sterile).

### Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST)
* **E-test/Broth Microdilution:** Essential for *N. gonorrhoeae* to determine Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) for Ceftriaxone & Azithromycin.
* **Nitrocefin Disk Test:** Rapid bedside/lab test for β-lactamase production (*M. catarrhalis* & resistant gonococci).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Targeted Acute Antibiotic Therapy for N. meningitidis
* **Pre-hospital/Primary Care (if suspected & transport delayed):** IM/IV Benzylpenicillin (or Ceftriaxone) stat dose.
* **Hospital Definitive Rx (Blood/CSF directed):**
  * **1st Line:** IV Ceftriaxone 2g Q12H (high dose for CNS penetration).
  * **Alternative (if susceptible & pen-allergic):** IV Chloramphenicol (rarely used, strict indications).
  * *Note: Transition to IV Benzylpenicillin only if isolate proven susceptible (MIC <0.1 mg/L).*
* **Duration:** Typically 7 days.

### Targeted Acute Antibiotic Therapy for N. gonorrhoeae
* **Uncomplicated Urogenital/Anorectal Infection (HK Social Hygiene Guidelines):**
  * **1st Line:** IM Ceftriaxone 1g stat (Single dose) *PLUS* treatment for concurrent Chlamydia if not excluded (PO Doxycycline 100mg BD x 7 days).
  * *Note: Ceftriaxone dose increased from 500mg to 1g due to shifting MIC creep.*
* **Pharyngeal Infection:** IM Ceftriaxone 1g stat. (Pharynx is a reservoir for AMR development).
* **Disseminated Gonococcal Infection (DGI):** IV Ceftriaxone 1g Q24H for 7 days (convert to PO guided by susceptibility once improving).

### Targeted Acute Antibiotic Therapy for M. catarrhalis
* **AECOPD / Otitis Media 1st Line:** PO Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) to overcome inherent β-lactamase.
* **Alternatives (Penicillin Allergic):** PO Macrolides (Clarithromycin, Azithromycin), 2nd/3rd gen Cephalosporins (Cefuroxime), or Respiratory Fluoroquinolones (Levofloxacin).
* **Contraindicated:** Amoxicillin or Ampicillin monotherapy (near 100% resistance).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chemoprophylaxis Protocols for N. meningitidis Contacts
* **Indication:** Close contacts (household members, roommates, kissing contacts, healthcare workers with unprotected direct exposure to respiratory secretions, e.g., during intubation).
* **Timing:** Administer ideally within 24 hours of identifying index case (ineffective if >14 days post-exposure).
* **Regimens (HK Centre for Health Protection Options):**
  1. PO Ciprofloxacin 500mg stat (Single dose - most common for adults).
  2. PO Rifampicin 600mg Q12H for 2 days (Avoid in pregnancy; counseling on red-orange urine/tears and oral contraceptive failure).
  3. IM Ceftriaxone 250mg stat (Drug of choice for pregnancy).
* **Index Case Eradication:** If index case was treated with IV Benzylpenicillin, they *must* receive chemoprophylaxis prior to discharge to eradicate nasopharyngeal carriage (Ceftriaxone effectively treats *and* eradicates carriage).

### Meningococcal Vaccination Schedules in Hong Kong
* **Not in routine HK childhood schedule** but recommended for high-risk individuals (asplenia, complement deficiency, travelers to endemic areas/Hajj, students entering overseas dormitories).
* **Vaccine Types:**
  * **Quadrivalent Conjugate (MenACWY):** Protects against A, C, W, Y. (e.g., Menactra, Nimenrix).
  * **Recombinant Protein (MenB):** Protects against serogroup B (e.g., Bexsero, Trumenba). Crucial as Group B capsule is poorly immunogenic (resembles human neural tissue NCAM).

### Public Health Management for N. gonorrhoeae
* **Contact Tracing:** Mandatory partner notification and empiric treatment of sexual contacts within the past 60 days.
* **Test of Cure (TOC):** Recommended for pharyngeal infections or if alternative/second-line regimens used. Wait ≥2 weeks for culture, ≥3-4 weeks for NAAT to avoid false +ves from dead DNA.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Risk Factors in Meningococcemia
* High mortality associated with:
  * Absence of meningitis (isolated meningococcemia has higher mortality than meningococcal meningitis).
  * Extreme age (<1 year or >60 years).
  * Shock (Systolic BP <90 mmHg) or rapid development of purpura fulminans.
  * Leukopenia or severe thrombocytopenia.
  * Absence of marked CSF pleocytosis (suggests poor immune response).

### High-Risk Host Profiles for Neisseria Infections
* **Terminal Complement Deficiency (C5-C9):** Inability to form Membrane Attack Complex (MAC) → highly susceptible to recurrent systemic *Neisseria* infections.
* **Eculizumab Therapy:** Monoclonal antibody blocking C5 (used for PNH/aHUS) → confers ~1,000-fold ↑ risk of meningococcal disease.
* **Asplenia:** ↑ risk of overwhelming sepsis from encapsulated *N. meningitidis*.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Severe Meningococcal Disease
* **Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome (華特豪斯-弗里德里希森症候群):** Massive bilateral adrenal hemorrhage secondary to DIC → acute adrenal insufficiency → refractory shock.
* **Purpura Fulminans & Ischemia:** Microvascular thrombosis → autoamputation of digits/limbs.
* **Neurological Sequelae:** Sensorineural hearing loss, cognitive impairment, seizures (if meningitis present).

### Complications of Untreated N. gonorrhoeae
* **Females:** Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) → chronic pelvic pain, ectopic pregnancy, tubal factor infertility.
* **Fitz-Hugh-Curtis Syndrome (Perihepatitis):** Direct spread of GC (or Chlamydia) into peritoneal cavity causing inflammation of Glisson's capsule → "violin string" adhesions between liver and abdominal wall.
* **Neonates:** Ophthalmia neonatorum → rapidly progressive purulent conjunctivitis → corneal ulceration and blindness.
* **Males:** Epididymo-orchitis, urethral strictures.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why can't we easily make a vaccine against the polysaccharide capsule of Neisseria meningitidis serogroup B?
  Answer: The Serogroup B capsule contains polysialic acid, which is structurally identical to neural cell adhesion molecules (NCAMs) in human fetal brain tissue. It is poorly immunogenic and risks inducing autoimmunity. MenB vaccines target subcapsular recombinant proteins instead.
* Trap: Prescribing PO Amoxicillin monotherapy for an acute exacerbation of COPD caused by Moraxella catarrhalis.
  Pearl: M. catarrhalis universally (>95%) produces beta-lactamases. Monotherapy will fail; you must prescribe Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) or an alternative non-penicillin class.
* Pitfall: Reassuring a patient treated for Gonorrhoea that they have lifelong immunity.
  Patients can be reinfected immediately. Neisseria gonorrhoeae undergoes continuous rapid antigenic variation of its pili and Opa proteins, completely evading long-term adaptive immune memory.
* Viva Question: What is the classic laboratory culture medium for Neisseria gonorrhoeae and why is it used?
  Answer: Thayer-Martin agar. It is a chocolate agar supplemented with antibiotics (Vancomycin, Colistin, Nystatin, Trimethoprim) specifically designed to suppress normal flora from non-sterile mucosal sites while allowing Neisseria to grow.
* Trap: Discharging a patient recovered from meningococcal sepsis without ensuring index case eradication.
  Pearl: If the patient was treated with IV Benzylpenicillin, it cures the systemic disease but does NOT reliably clear nasopharyngeal carriage. They require a pre-discharge dose of PO Ciprofloxacin or IM Ceftriaxone to prevent transmission to others upon returning home.
