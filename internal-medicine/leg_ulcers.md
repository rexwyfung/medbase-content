# Leg Ulcers (腿部潰瘍)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Leg Ulcers
* **Leg Ulcer (腿部潰瘍)**: Full-thickness loss of epidermis + dermis on lower extremity, failing to heal within 2–6 weeks.

### Etiologies of Leg Ulcers
* **Venous (靜脈性)**: Most common (~70-80%).
* **Arterial (動脈性)**: ~10-15%.
* **Mixed Venous-Arterial**: ~10%.
* **Neuropathic (神經性)**: Commonly secondary to **Diabetes Mellitus (糖尿病)**.
* **Atypical**: 
  * Malignancy: **Marjolin’s Ulcer (馬喬林潰瘍)** (Squamous Cell Carcinoma / SCC).
  * Inflammatory: **Pyoderma Gangrenosum (壞疽性膿皮病)**, Vasculitis (e.g., Rheumatoid arthritis, ANCA).
  * Infective: Syphilis, Tuberculosis, Leprosy.

### Local Epidemiology of Leg Ulcers in Hong Kong
* Highly prevalent in elderly HK population (aging demographic).
* High baseline **Diabetes Mellitus** prevalence in HK → high incidence of mixed (arterial + neuropathic) ulcers.
* Often managed in specialized **Wound Clinics** in HA outpatient settings (e.g., GOPC / Community Nursing Services).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Venous Ulcers
* **Ambulatory Venous Hypertension** → venous valve incompetence (often post-DVT) → calf muscle pump failure.
* Venous pooling → ↑ capillary hydrostatic pressure → macromolecule leakage (fibrinogen).
* **Fibrin Cuff Theory**: Extravasated fibrinogen polymerizes into pericapillary fibrin cuffs → blocks O2/nutrient diffusion → tissue ischemia.
* **White Cell Trapping Theory**: Leukocytes marginate in microcirculation → release proteolytic enzymes & ROS → tissue destruction & ulceration.

### Pathophysiology of Arterial Ulcers
* **Atherosclerosis (動脈粥樣硬化)** → macrovascular / microvascular stenosis.
* ↓ arterial perfusion → tissue hypoxia → cell death.
* Exacerbated by minor trauma (tissue incapable of healing due to lack of O2/nutrients).

### Pathophysiology of Neuropathic Ulcers
* **Sensory Neuropathy**: Loss of protective pain sensation → unrecognized repetitive microtrauma.
* **Motor Neuropathy**: Atrophy of intrinsic foot muscles → arch collapse / structural deformities → abnormal pressure points (e.g., metatarsal heads).
* **Autonomic Neuropathy**: ↓ sweating → dry, fissured skin (entry for bacteria) + arteriovenous shunting (warm foot but poor capillary nutrient exchange).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for Leg Ulcers
* **Pain Characteristics**:
  * **Venous**: Aching, heavy pain; **relieved by elevation**; worse at end of day.
  * **Arterial**: Claudication, severe rest pain; **worse with elevation**, relieved by hanging leg over bed (dependency).
  * **Neuropathic**: Painless (often).
* **Past Medical History**: DVT, varicose veins, DM, ischemic heart disease, smoking, prolonged standing occupation, connective tissue diseases.

### Physical Examination of Venous Ulcers
* **Location**: Gaiter area (lower 1/3 of leg), especially **medial malleolus**.
* **Appearance**: Shallow, irregular borders, sloping edges, highly exudative base (granulation tissue / slough).
* **Surrounding Skin (Stigmata of Venous Disease)**:
  * **Hemosiderin Staining (含鐵血黃素沉積)** (brown pigmentation).
  * **Lipodermatosclerosis (脂膜硬化)** ("inverted champagne bottle" leg).
  * **Atrophie Blanche (白色萎縮)** (white scarring with telangiectasia).
  * **Venous Eczema / Varicose Veins**.
  * Pulses: Intact (may be obscured by edema).

### Physical Examination of Arterial Ulcers
* **Location**: Distal extremities (toes, heel), lateral malleolus, pressure points.
* **Appearance**: Deep, "punched-out", regular margins, necrotic/pale base, minimal exudate.
* **Surrounding Skin**: Pale, hairless, shiny, thin. Cold to touch.
* **Signs**: Absent or weak peripheral pulses, prolonged capillary refill time (>2s), +ve Buerger's test.

### Physical Examination of Neuropathic Ulcers
* **Location**: Plantar aspect of foot, under metatarsal heads, heel (high friction/pressure areas).
* **Appearance**: Deep, "punched-out", surrounded by thick hyperkeratotic **callus**.
* **Surrounding Skin**: Warm, dry, fissured.
* **Signs**: Palpable pulses (unless mixed), ↓ fine touch/pinprick/vibration (stocking distribution).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnosis Table for Leg Ulcers
| Feature | Venous Ulcer | Arterial Ulcer | Neuropathic Ulcer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Location** | Medial malleolus / Gaiter | Toes, lateral malleolus | Plantar, pressure points |
| **Edge** | Sloping, irregular | Punched-out, well-defined | Punched-out + callus |
| **Base** | Pink granulation, exudate | Pale, necrotic, slough | Granulation / exposed bone |
| **Pain** | Mild, ache, ↓ by elevation | Severe, ↓ by dependency | Painless |
| **Skin** | Hemosiderin, lipodermatosclerosis | Shiny, hairless, cold | Dry, fissured, warm |
| **Pulses** | Normal (often palpable) | Absent / ↓ | Normal (bounding if autonomic) |

### Clinical Features of Atypical Ulcers
* **Marjolin’s Ulcer (SCC)**: Non-healing chronic wound, everted/rolled edges, exuberant hypergranulation tissue, bleeding easily.
* **Pyoderma Gangrenosum**: Associated with IBD / RA / Myeloma. Starts as pustule → rapidly expanding painful ulcer with **violaceous, undermined borders**.
* **Vasculitic Ulcer**: Punched-out, deep, very painful, often multiple, palpable purpura surrounding.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Leg Ulcers
* **Ankle-Brachial Pressure Index (ABPI) (踝肱指數)**: **Mandatory** for ALL leg ulcers before initiating compression. 
* **Neurological Testing**: 10g Semmes-Weinstein Monofilament (protective sensation), tuning fork (128Hz).
* **Handheld Doppler**: Assess dorsalis pedis / posterior tibial pulses (biphasic/triphasic vs monophasic).
* **Wound Swab for C/ST**: ONLY if clinical signs of infection (erythema, warmth, purulence, systemic signs). Do NOT swab routinely (colonization is normal).

### Blood Investigations for Leg Ulcers
* **FBP & Inflammatory Markers**: Leukocytosis, ↑ CRP/ESR if infected/vasculitis.
* **Metabolic Screen**: Fasting glucose, HbA1c, Renal/Liver function.
* **Autoimmune Screen**: ANA, ANCA, RF (if atypical/suspected vasculitis).

### Imaging for Leg Ulcers
* **Venous Duplex Ultrasound**: Assess deep/superficial venous reflux, rule out concurrent DVT.
* **Arterial Duplex / CTA / MRA Lower Limbs**: If ABPI <0.8 or arterial etiology suspected.
* **X-Ray of Affected Limb**: Rule out underlying **Osteomyelitis (骨髓炎)** (periosteal reaction, bone destruction) or subcutaneous gas.

### Special Investigations & Biopsy for Leg Ulcers
* **Wedge Biopsy**: Indicate if ulcer is atypical, refractory (>3 months of optimal Rx), or suspicious for SCC (Marjolin's). Sample must include the edge and base.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Infected Leg Ulcers
* **Systemic Antibiotics**: Guided by HA antibiogram. Start empirical oral (e.g., Amoxicillin-Clavulanate) or IV (e.g., Ampicillin/Sulbactam) if cellulitis, systemic sepsis, or deep tissue involvement.
* **Analgesia**: Stepwise approach (paracetamol → weak opioids).
* **Surgical Debridement**: Urgent surgical consult if necrotizing fasciitis, wet gangrene, or deep abscess is suspected.
* **Elevation**: Strict leg elevation for acute venous flares.

### Acute Management of Critical Limb Ischemia
* **Urgent Vascular Referral**: For endovascular angioplasty or surgical bypass.
* **Avoid Compression**: Strictly contraindicated.
* **Analgesia & Positioning**: Hang leg dependent to maximize perfusion; aggressive pain control (often requires opioids).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### General Wound Bed Preparation (TIME Principle)
* **T - Tissue Debridement**: Autolytic (hydrogels), enzymatic, mechanical, or sharp surgical removal of necrotic tissue.
* **I - Infection/Inflammation Control**: Topical antimicrobials (cadexomer iodine, silver dressings) *only if* localized critical colonization.
* **M - Moisture Balance**: Optimize dressing selection based on exudate level.
* **E - Edge Advancement**: Protect peri-wound skin (barrier creams).

### Dressing Selection Algorithm for Leg Ulcers
| Wound Status | Preferred Dressing (HA Formulary Options) | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Exudate** | Alginate (Kaltostat), Hydrofiber (Aquacel), Foam | Absorbs moisture, prevents maceration. |
| **Low Exudate / Dry** | Hydrogel (Intrasite Gel), Hydrocolloid (Duoderm) | Donates moisture, promotes autolytic debridement. |
| **Infected / Malodorous** | Silver-impregnated, Charcoal, Cadexomer Iodine | Reduces bacterial burden. |
| **Sloughy / Necrotic** | Hydrogel | Rehydrates slough for autolysis. |

### Chronic Management of Venous Ulcers
* **Compression Therapy (壓迫治療)**: The cornerstone of treatment.
  * Reverses venous hypertension, ↓ edema.
  * Options: 4-layer bandage (high compression, 40mmHg at ankle), 2-layer short-stretch bandage, or compression stockings (Class II/III for maintenance after healing).
* **Pharmacological**: **Pentoxifylline (己酮可可鹼)** 400mg TDS PO (improves microcirculation, speeds healing with compression).
* **Surgical**: Endovenous ablation (laser/radiofrequency) of superficial venous reflux if conservative Rx fails.
* **Lifestyle**: Leg elevation > heart level (3-4x daily), calf exercises.

### Chronic Management of Arterial Ulcers
* **Revascularization**: PTCA (Percutaneous Transluminal Coronary Angioplasty) / stenting or surgical bypass graft.
* **Risk Modification**: Smoking cessation (critical), Statins, Antiplatelets (Aspirin/Clopidogrel), strict DM/BP control.
* **Contraindication**: NEVER apply multi-layer compression.

### Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (VAC) Indications
* **Indications**: Deep wounds with large defects, promoting granulation before skin grafting.
* **Contraindications**: Untreated osteomyelitis, frank malignancy in wound, exposed vessels/nerves, unexplored fistulas, severe untreated coagulopathy, necrotic tissue with eschar.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Ankle-Brachial Pressure Index (ABPI) Interpretation & Action Table
| ABPI Value | Clinical Interpretation | Compression Therapy Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **> 1.3** | Incompressible / Calcified vessels (DM / CKD) | Invalid result. Need Toe-Brachial Index (TBI). |
| **0.9 – 1.2** | Normal arterial flow | **Safe** for full compression (40mmHg). |
| **0.8 – 0.9** | Mild PAD | Safe for full compression (monitor closely). |
| **0.5 – 0.8** | Moderate PAD (Mixed ulcer) | **Reduced compression** (max ~20mmHg). Refer Vascular. |
| **< 0.5** | Severe PAD / Critical Limb Ischemia | **Absolute Contraindication** to compression. Urgent referral. |

### Referral Thresholds for Leg Ulcers
* **Vascular Surgery**: ABPI <0.8, rest pain, rapid deterioration, unhealed venous ulcer after >2-3 months of optimal compression.
* **Dermatology/Plastic Surgery**: Atypical ulcer (suspected malignancy, pyoderma gangrenosum), non-healing requiring split-thickness skin graft (STSG).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Leg Ulcers
* **Infectious**: Cellulitis, recurrent skin/soft tissue infections, underlying **Osteomyelitis**.
* **Oncological**: Malignant transformation into **Marjolin’s Ulcer** (SCC) due to chronic inflammation.
* **Surgical**: Intractable pain, major lower limb amputation (if untreated arterial or severe neuroischemic).
* **Psychological**: Social isolation, depression secondary to malodorous wounds and chronic pain.
* **Dermatological**: Contact allergic dermatitis to dressings (e.g., lanolin, topical antibiotics, adhesives).

### Adverse Effects of Ulcer Treatments
* **Compression Bandaging**: Pressure necrosis/gangrene (if applied tightly on unrecognized arterial disease), nerve palsy (common peroneal nerve compression at fibular head if applied incorrectly).
* **Topical Iodine/Silver**: Delayed wound healing / toxicity to fibroblasts if used excessively or continuously beyond 14 days without reassessment.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical investigation before applying compression bandaging to a venous ulcer? Answer: ABPI to exclude significant arterial disease (must be >0.8).
* Viva Question: A patient with long-standing RA develops a rapidly enlarging, painful ulcer with purplish overhanging edges. Diagnosis? Answer: Pyoderma gangrenosum.
* Trap: Prescribing routine systemic antibiotics for a heavily colonized, non-healing venous ulcer. Pearl: Antibiotics do not heal ulcers; only prescribe if clinical signs of active infection (cellulitis, sepsis, deep tissue involvement) are present.
* Pitfall: Reassuring a diabetic patient with an ABPI of 1.4 that their arterial supply is normal. The vessels are likely calcified and incompressible (Mönckeberg's arteriosclerosis), rendering the ABPI falsely high; require a Toe-Brachial Index (TBI) or duplex scan.
* Trap: Taking a wound swab from the sloughy center of a chronic ulcer. Pearl: Swabs should be taken from viable tissue after debridement/cleaning; surface swabs only reflect colonization, not the infecting pathogen.
* Pitfall: Using heavy compression on a mixed venous-arterial ulcer with an ABPI of 0.6. This will worsen ischemia; only modified/reduced compression under specialist vascular guidance should be used.
