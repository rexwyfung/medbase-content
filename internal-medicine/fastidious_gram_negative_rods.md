# Fastidious Gram-Negative Rods (營養要求性革蘭氏陰性桿菌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Fastidious Gram-Negative Rods
* **Concept**: Gram-negative bacilli requiring complex/specific nutritional supplements (e.g., hemin, NAD, specific amino acids) or specialized atmospheric conditions (microaerophilic) for in vitro growth.

### Haemophilus influenzae (流感嗜血桿菌)
* **Encapsulated (Type b - Hib)**: Historically caused pediatric meningitis, epiglottitis (會厭炎). Incidence in HK ↓ dramatically post-universal childhood Hib vaccination (1990s).
* **Non-typable (NTHi)**: Unencapsulated. Major cause of acute exacerbations of COPD (AECOPD) and bronchiectasis (支氣管擴張) in HK adults.

### Bordetella pertussis (百日咳桿菌)
* **Disease**: Pertussis / Whooping Cough (百日咳).
* **Epidemiology**: Highly contagious (droplet). Re-emerging globally due to waning vaccine immunity; HK sees periodic sporadic clusters in adults/adolescents, who transmit to unvaccinated infants.

### Legionella pneumophila (嗜肺退伍軍人桿菌)
* **Disease**: Legionnaires' disease (退伍軍人症) & Pontiac fever.
* **Epidemiology**: Aquatic habitats, cooling towers, hot water systems. Peaks during summer/air-conditioning season in HK. High mortality in elderly/immunocompromised.

### Zoonotic Fastidious Rods (Brucella & Francisella)
* **Brucella (布魯氏菌)**: Undulant fever. Acquired via unpasteurized dairy (endemic in Mediterranean/Middle East) or livestock contact. Rare in HK.
* **Francisella tularensis (土拉弗朗西斯菌)**: Tularemia. Tick bites, handling infected rabbits. Extremely rare in HK.

### Enteric Fastidious Rods (Campylobacter & Helicobacter)
* **Campylobacter jejuni (空腸彎曲菌)**: Major cause of bacterial gastroenteritis in HK. Linked to undercooked poultry/cross-contamination.
* **Helicobacter pylori (幽門螺旋桿菌)**: Highly prevalent in HK population (~40-50%). Major driver of peptic ulcer disease and gastric malignancy.

### The HACEK Group
* **Organisms**: *Haemophilus* spp., *Aggregatibacter* spp., *Cardiobacterium hominis*, *Eikenella corrodens*, *Kingella kingae*.
* **Epidemiology**: Normal human oropharyngeal flora. Notable for causing culture-negative infective endocarditis (IE).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Haemophilus influenzae
* **Capsule (Hib)**: Polyribosylribitol phosphate (PRP) capsule → evades phagocytosis → invasive hematogenous spread.
* **IgA1 Protease**: Cleaves mucosal IgA → facilitates respiratory tract colonization.

### Toxin-Mediated Pathogenesis of Bordetella pertussis
* **Pertussis Toxin (PTx)**: AB toxin. ADP-ribosylates Gi protein → disables Gi → ↑ intracellular cAMP → disables chemokine receptors → lymphocyte trapping in blood (dramatic lymphocytosis) & impaired phagocytosis.
* **Tracheal Cytotoxin**: Damages ciliated respiratory epithelium → loss of mucociliary escalator → severe paroxysmal coughing.

### Intracellular Survival of Legionella pneumophila
* **Mechanism**: Inhaled into alveoli → phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages → uses Dot/Icm Type IV secretion system → prevents phagosome-lysosome fusion → replicates intracellularly in specialized vacuoles.

### Immune Evasion of Brucella species
* **Mechanism**: Facultative intracellular pathogen of the reticuloendothelial system (RES). Prevents phagolysosome fusion in macrophages → forms non-caseating granulomas in liver, spleen, bone marrow.

### Gastric Survival Mechanisms of Helicobacter pylori
* **Urease Production**: Converts urea to NH3 + CO2 → neutralizes local gastric acid cloud → allows survival.
* **Toxins**: CagA (cytotoxin-associated gene A, strongly linked to cancer) and VacA (vacuolating cytotoxin) → disrupt epithelial junctions & induce apoptosis.

### Autoimmune Molecular Mimicry of Campylobacter jejuni
* **Mechanism**: Lipo-oligosaccharide (LOS) on bacterial cell wall cross-reacts with human peripheral nerve gangliosides (GM1, GD1a) → autoimmune destruction of myelin (Guillain-Barré syndrome).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Clinical Signs of Haemophilus influenzae Epiglottitis
* **Presentation**: Rapid onset of high fever, severe dysphagia, muffled "hot potato" voice.
* **Posture**: Sniffing dog posture (tripod position) to maximize airway caliber.
* **Signs**: Drooling (inability to swallow saliva), inspiratory stridor, intercostal retractions.

### Triphasic Presentation of Bordetella pertussis
* **Catarrhal Stage (1-2 weeks)**: Coryza, mild fever. (Most infectious).
* **Paroxysmal Stage (2-8 weeks)**: Bursts of rapid coughs ending in a high-pitched inspiratory "whoop". Post-tussive emesis common.
* **Physical Signs**: Subconjunctival hemorrhages (from ↑ intrathoracic pressure), exhaustion.

### Systemic Signs of Legionnaires' Disease
* **Vitals**: High fever (>40°C), relative bradycardia (Faget sign - temp ↑ but HR does not ↑ proportionally).
* **GI/Neuro**: Watery diarrhea, altered mental status/confusion (disproportionate to respiratory distress).
* **Chest**: Bilateral basal crepitations.

### Clinical Assessment of Brucellosis
* **History**: Occupational exposure (abattoir workers, veterinarians), unpasteurized cheese consumption.
* **Vitals**: Undulant fever (rises by day, falls by night), profuse malodorous night sweats.
* **Abdomen**: Hepatosplenomegaly.
* **MSK**: Sacroiliitis, spondylitis (vertebral tenderness).

### Signs of HACEK Infective Endocarditis
* **Cardiac**: New or changing heart murmurs.
* **Peripheral Stigmata**: Osler nodes (tender, pads of digits), Janeway lesions (painless, palms/soles), splinter hemorrhages, Roth spots.
* **Note**: Indolent/subacute course compared to *S. aureus*.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Culture-Negative Endocarditis Differentials
* **HACEK Group**: Subacute course, large friable vegetations, prior dental procedures.
* **Coxiella burnetii (Q fever)**: Animal exposure (sheep/goats), extreme hepatosplenomegaly.
* **Bartonella spp.**: Cat scratch history (*B. henselae*), body lice/homelessness (*B. quintana*).
* **Prior Antibiotics**: Most common cause of culture-negative IE in HK.

### Atypical Pneumonia Pathogen Differentiation
* **Legionella**: + Diarrhea, + Confusion, + Hyponatremia.
* **Mycoplasma pneumoniae**: + Cold agglutinins (hemolytic anemia), + Erythema multiforme, younger patients (dorms/camps).
* **Chlamydophila psittaci**: + Bird exposure (parrots/pigeons).

### Acute Stridor Differentiation in Adults/Adolescents
* **Epiglottitis (Hib)**: Rapid onset, drooling, severe dysphagia, no cough.
* **Croup (Parainfluenza)**: Gradual onset, barking "seal-like" cough, mostly infants.
* **Anaphylaxis**: + Urticaria, + Wheeze, + Known allergen exposure.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Specialized Growth Media for Fastidious Organisms
| Pathogen | Required Growth Medium | Key Growth Factors/Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **H. influenzae** | Chocolate Agar | Factor V (NAD) and Factor X (Hematin) |
| **B. pertussis** | Bordet-Gengou / Regan-Lowe | Charcoal, blood, antibiotics to inhibit flora |
| **L. pneumophila** | BCYE (Buffered Charcoal Yeast Extract) | Iron and Cysteine |
| **C. jejuni** | Skirrow Agar (Campy agar) | 42°C, Microaerophilic (5% O2) |
| **Brucella** | Castaneda biphasic media | Prolonged incubation (up to 4-6 weeks) |

### Bedside & Point-of-Care Tests
* **Legionella Urinary Antigen Test (UAT)**: Rapid enzyme immunoassay. *Caveat*: Only detects *L. pneumophila* Serogroup 1 (causes ~80% of cases; misses other serogroups).
* **Campylobacter**: Fecal direct smear (Gram stain) showing "seagull-wing" shaped Gram-negative curved rods.

### Blood Tests for Fastidious Gram-Negative Rods
* **B. pertussis**: Marked leukocytosis with absolute lymphocytosis (unusual for bacteria).
* **L. pneumophila**: Severe hyponatremia (Na <130 mmol/L, often SIADH-mediated), ↑ transaminases (ALT/AST), ↑ CK (rhabdomyolysis), hypophosphatemia.
* **HACEK IE**: Prolonged blood cultures (inform lab to hold for up to 14-21 days, though modern automated systems often detect them within 5 days).

### Imaging & Endoscopy Modalities
* **Epiglottitis**: Lateral neck soft tissue X-ray → classic **Thumbprint sign** (swollen epiglottis).
* **Legionella**: CXR showing patchy, unilobar infiltrates rapidly progressing to multilobar consolidation without cavitation.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation for Epiglottitis (Airway Emergency)
* **Airway Control**: Keep patient calm. Do NOT attempt to visualize throat in ward (risks complete spasm/obstruction). Transfer directly to Operating Theatre (OT) with ENT/Anesthetist for endotracheal intubation or tracheostomy.
* **Empiric IV Therapy**: IV Ceftriaxone (coverage for Hib) + IV Dexamethasone (to ↓ airway edema).

### Acute Management of Legionnaires' Disease
* **Immediate Pharmacotherapy**: Intracellular penetration required.
* **First-line**: IV Levofloxacin OR IV Azithromycin.
* **Duration**: 7-10 days for uncomplicated cases, 14-21 days if immunocompromised.

### Infection Control for Pertussis
* **Isolation**: Strict droplet precautions.
* **Acute Pharmacotherapy**: Oral Macrolides (Azithromycin or Clarithromycin).
* **Rationale**: Eradicates organism from nasopharynx to stop transmission. (Note: Does *not* alter the clinical course of coughing if given in the paroxysmal stage).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Eradication Therapy for Brucellosis
* **Regimen**: Prolonged intracellular therapy to prevent relapse.
* **Standard Adult Rx**: Oral Doxycycline (6 weeks) + Oral Rifampicin (6 weeks).
* **Alternative**: Oral Doxycycline (6 weeks) + IM Streptomycin/Gentamicin (first 2-3 weeks).

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (Public Health)
* **Hib Prophylaxis**: Oral Rifampicin for 4 days for all unimmunized household contacts <4 years old.
* **Pertussis Prophylaxis**: Macrolides (Azithromycin) for all close household contacts, regardless of immunization status.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Relapse Risk Stratification in Brucellosis
* **High Risk for Relapse**: Treatment duration <6 weeks, localized focal disease (e.g., spondylitis, endocarditis), monotherapy (strictly contraindicated).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological & MSK Complications of Campylobacter jejuni
* **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)**: Acute ascending flaccid paralysis & areflexia (1-2 weeks post-diarrhea) due to molecular mimicry.
* **Reactive Arthritis**: HLA-B27 associated. Asymmetric oligoarthritis (Reiter's syndrome phenotype: arthritis, urethritis, conjunctivitis).

### Complications of Legionella pneumophila
* **Renal**: Tubulointerstitial nephritis, ATN (from rhabdomyolysis).
* **Electrolyte**: Refractory hyponatremia leading to seizures.

### Complications of HACEK Endocarditis
* **Vascular**: High risk of major arterial embolization (cerebral, splenic, renal infarcts) due to large, friable vegetations.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What specialized culture medium is required to isolate Haemophilus influenzae and why? Answer: Chocolate agar. It contains lysed red blood cells which release Factor X (hematin) and Factor V (NAD) essential for its growth.
* Viva Question: An elderly patient with pneumonia has a serum sodium of 122 mmol/L, confusion, and diarrhea. What is the diagnosis and empirical treatment? Answer: Legionnaires' disease. Treat with an intracellular antibiotic like Levofloxacin or Azithromycin (beta-lactams are ineffective).
* Trap: Suspecting epiglottitis in a distressed, drooling adult and using a tongue depressor to confirm the diagnosis. Pearl: Never instrument the airway in suspected epiglottitis outside of the OT; it can provoke fatal laryngospasm and complete airway occlusion. Secure the airway in a controlled setting first.
* Trap: A negative Legionella urinary antigen test rules out Legionnaires' disease. Pearl: The urinary antigen test only detects *Legionella pneumophila* Serogroup 1. It misses other serogroups and non-pneumophila species; always order lower respiratory tract culture on BCYE agar if suspicion remains high.
* Pitfall: Treating Bordetella pertussis with azithromycin expecting it to cure the paroxysmal cough. Explanation: The cough is mediated by toxins already bound to the ciliated epithelium. Antibiotics given in the paroxysmal stage do not change the clinical course but are crucial to eradicate nasopharyngeal carriage and stop transmission.
