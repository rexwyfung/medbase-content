# Demyelinating Diseases (脫髓鞘疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Demyelinating Diseases
* **Definition:** Acquired immune-mediated disorders characterized by focal destruction of central nervous system (CNS) myelin, relative preservation of axons, and neuroglial scarring (gliosis).
* **Core Syndromes:**
  * **Multiple Sclerosis (多發性硬化症, MS):** Disseminated CNS demyelination in time and space.
  * **Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (視神經脊髓炎光譜疾病, NMOSD):** Autoantibody-mediated astrocytopathy affecting optic nerves and spinal cord.
  * **Myelin Oligodendrocyte Glycoprotein Antibody-Associated Disease (MOGAD):** Inflammatory demyelination targeting MOG.
  * **Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis (急性播散性腦脊髓炎, ADEM):** Monophasic post-infectious demyelination.

### Subtypes of Multiple Sclerosis
* **Relapsing-Remitting MS (RRMS):** 
  * Accounts for ~85% of onset.
  * Discrete attacks with full/partial recovery.
  * No disease progression between relapses.
* **Secondary Progressive MS (SPMS):** 
  * Initial RRMS course → gradual neurologic deterioration.
  * Occurs in ~65% of RRMS if untreated (after 15-20 years).
* **Primary Progressive MS (PPMS):** 
  * ~15% of cases. 
  * Steady progression from onset without distinct relapses.
* **Progressive-Relapsing MS (PRMS):** 
  * Progressive from onset + acute relapses.

### Local Epidemiology of MS and NMOSD in Hong Kong
* **MS Incidence/Prevalence:** 
  * Lower in HK compared to Caucasians.
  * Prevalence: ~7-9 per 100,000.
  * Demographics: Female > Male (3:1), peak onset 20-40 years.
* **NMOSD Incidence/Prevalence:**
  * Proportionally higher in Asian populations.
  * Historically misdiagnosed as "Opticospinal MS".
  * Extremely female-predominant (up to 9:1).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Multiple Sclerosis
* **Immune Dysregulation:** Autoreactive T-cells (Th1/Th17) cross Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB).
* **Target:** Myelin basic protein & structural components of CNS myelin sheaths.
* **Cellular Response:** 
  * T-cells recruit B-cells, macrophages, and microglia.
  * → Secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IFN-γ, TNF-α).
  * → Oligodendrocyte apoptosis and focal myelin destruction.
* **Chronicity:** Early disease = inflammation (relapses); Late disease = axonal degeneration/atrophy (progression).

### Pathophysiology of NMOSD (視神經脊髓炎光譜疾病)
* **Antibody Target:** **Aquaporin-4 (AQP4) water channels**.
* **Location:** Concentrated on astrocyte foot processes at BBB and ependymal surfaces.
* **Mechanism:** 
  * AQP4-IgG binds target → Complement cascade activation.
  * → Massive eosinophil/neutrophil infiltration.
  * → Primary astrocyte necrosis (astrocytopathy) → Secondary demyelination/axonal loss.
* **Severity:** Tissue destruction is highly necrotic, forming cystic cavities in spinal cord.

### Pathophysiology of MOGAD
* **Antibody Target:** Myelin Oligodendrocyte Glycoprotein (MOG).
* **Location:** Outer surface of myelin sheaths and oligodendrocyte membranes.
* **Mechanism:** True primary demyelination (oligodendropathic) without major astrocyte destruction.
* **Reversibility:** Often highly responsive to steroids; myelin can regenerate.

### Pathophysiology of ADEM
* **Trigger:** Preceding viral infection (e.g., Measles, VZV) or vaccination (rare).
* **Mechanism:** **Molecular mimicry**.
* **Process:** Cross-reactive T-cells target endogenous myelin → Widespread perivenous demyelination throughout brain/spine.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### General History in Suspected Demyelination
* **Temporal Profile:** Acute/subacute onset (hours to days), plateau (weeks), partial/full recovery (months).
* **Relapse Definitions:** 
  * New neurological symptom lasting >24 hours.
  * Absence of fever/infection (exclude pseudoexacerbation).
* **Triggers:**
  * History of recent infections.
  * Post-partum state (↑ MS relapse risk).

### Optic Neuritis (視神經炎) Clinical Features
* **Visual Symptoms:** Subacute unilateral vision loss.
* **Pain:** Retro-orbital pain, worsened by eye movement (highly characteristic).
* **Color Vision:** Dyschromatopsia (red desaturation).
* **Physical Signs:**
  * **Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD / Marcus Gunn pupil):** +ve on swinging flashlight test.
  * **Fundoscopy:** Papillitis (swollen disc in 1/3) or retrobulbar neuritis (normal disc in 2/3).
  * *(Note: Exclude ophthalmological causes → cross-reference Acute Visual Loss)*

### Transverse Myelitis (橫貫性脊髓炎) Clinical Features
* **Motor:** Paraparesis or quadriparesis (depending on lesion level).
* **Sensory:** Clear dermatomal sensory level.
* **Autonomic:** Bowel/bladder dysfunction (urinary retention, constipation).
* **Spinal Shock (Acute phase):** Flaccid tone, areflexia.
* **Chronic phase:** Spasticity, hyperreflexia, +ve Babinski sign, clonus.

### Brainstem & Cerebellar Syndromes in MS
* **Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO):** 
  * Lesion in **Medial Longitudinal Fasciculus (MLF)**.
  * Sign: Failure of ipsilateral adduction + Contralateral abducting nystagmus.
  * Bilateral INO is highly pathognomonic for MS.
* **Cranial Neuropathies:**
  * **Trigeminal Neuralgia:** Especially if bilateral or in young adults.
  * Facial nerve palsy.
* **Cerebellar Signs (Charcot's Neurologic Triad):**
  * Nystagmus.
  * Intention tremor.
  * Scanning speech (dysarthria).

### Eponymous Signs in Multiple Sclerosis
* **Lhermitte's Sign:** 
  * Electric shock-like sensation radiating down spine/limbs upon neck flexion.
  * Indicates cervical cord demyelination.
* **Uhthoff's Phenomenon:** 
  * Transient worsening of neurological symptoms with ↑ core body temperature (e.g., hot bath, exercise, fever).
  * Caused by temperature-dependent conduction block in demyelinated axons.
* **Pulfrich Effect:** Altered perception of motion trajectory due to optic nerve conduction delays.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating MS vs NMOSD vs MOGAD

| Feature | MS | NMOSD | MOGAD |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demographics** | F>M (3:1), Caucasians > Asians | F>>M (9:1), Asians/Africans | M=F, Children > Adults |
| **Optic Neuritis** | Unilateral, mild/moderate | Bilateral, severe, chiasm involvement | Bilateral, severe disc swelling |
| **Transverse Myelitis** | Short segment (<3 vertebral bodies) | **Longitudinally Extensive (LETM) (≥3 segments)** | LETM, affects conus medullaris |
| **Brain Lesions** | Periventricular, Dawson's fingers | Area postrema (intractable vomiting) | Fluffy, large ADEM-like lesions |

### Infectious Differential Diagnoses
* **Neurosyphilis:** +ve VDRL/RPR.
* **Lyme Disease:** Borrelia burgdorferi serology, typical rash.
* **HIV/PML:** Opportunistic JC virus demyelination.

### Autoimmune & Inflammatory Mimics
* **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** +ve ANA, anti-dsDNA, arthritis, rash.
* **Sjögren's Syndrome:** +ve Anti-Ro/La, dry eyes/mouth.
* **Neurosarcoidosis:** Basilar meningitis, cranial neuropathies, hilar lymphadenopathy.
* **Behçet's Disease:** Oral/genital ulcers, uveitis.

### Metabolic & Genetic Mimics
* **Vitamin B12 Deficiency:** Subacute combined degeneration (dorsal columns + lateral corticospinal tracts).
* **Leukodystrophies:** E.g., Adrenoleukodystrophy (X-linked, adrenal insufficiency).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Clinical Tests for Demyelination
* **Visual Evoked Potentials (VEP):** Prolonged P100 latency (indicates delayed conduction in optic nerve).
* **Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT):** Shows retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thinning post-optic neuritis.
* **Post-Void Residual (PVR):** Bedside bladder ultrasound to assess neurogenic bladder.

### Blood Investigations for Demyelinating Diseases
* **Autoantibodies:**
  * **AQP4-IgG (NMO-IgG):** Highly specific for NMOSD.
  * **MOG-IgG:** Specific for MOGAD.
* **Exclude Mimics:**
  * ANA, Anti-dsDNA, ENA (Anti-Ro/La) → SLE, Sjögren's.
  * Vitamin B12 level.
  * Syphilis serology (VDRL/RPR).
  * HIV serology.
  * Thyroid function tests (TFT).

### Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Analysis
* **Oligoclonal Bands (OCBs):** 
  * ≥2 unique IgG bands in CSF not present in serum.
  * Present in >90% of MS patients (Supports Dissemination in Time).
  * Often negative or transient in NMOSD/MOGAD.
* **IgG Index:** Elevated (reflects intrathecal IgG synthesis).
* **Basic Cell Count:** 
  * MS: Mild lymphocytic pleocytosis (<50 cells/µL). Normal protein.
  * NMOSD: Marked pleocytosis (>50 cells/µL), may have neutrophils/eosinophils.

### MRI Brain and Spine Findings in MS
* **Sequence:** T2/FLAIR (hyperintense lesions), T1 pre/post-Gadolinium (active inflammation shows enhancement).
* **Characteristic MS Lesions:**
  * **Dawson's Fingers:** Ovoid perivenular demyelinating plaques perpendicular to ventricles.
  * **Juxtacortical:** Involving U-fibers.
  * **Infratentorial:** Brainstem/cerebellum.
  * **Spinal Cord:** Short lesions (<3 segments), peripherally located.
* **"Black Holes":** Hypointense lesions on T1 representing chronic severe axonal loss.

### McDonald Criteria 2017 for Multiple Sclerosis Diagnosis
* **Core Principle:** Prove Dissemination in Space (DIS) and Dissemination in Time (DIT).
* **Clinical Presentation & Required Additional Criteria:**

| Clinical Presentation | Additional Data Needed for MS Diagnosis |
| :--- | :--- |
| ≥2 clinical attacks AND ≥2 lesions | None (clinical diagnosis confirmed) |
| ≥2 clinical attacks AND 1 lesion | **DIS:** MRI evidence of DIS |
| 1 clinical attack AND ≥2 lesions | **DIT:** MRI evidence of DIT OR +ve CSF OCBs |
| 1 clinical attack AND 1 lesion (CIS) | **DIS + DIT:** MRI DIS + (MRI DIT OR +ve CSF OCBs) |
| Insidious neurological progression | 1 year progression + 2 of: (1) MRI brain DIS, (2) MRI cord DIS, (3) +ve CSF OCBs |

* **Definitions of DIS/DIT:**
  * **DIS:** ≥1 T2 lesion in ≥2 of 4 areas (Periventricular, Cortical/Juxtacortical, Infratentorial, Spinal Cord).
  * **DIT:** Simultaneous presence of Gadolinium-enhancing and non-enhancing lesions at any time OR a new T2/enhancing lesion on follow-up MRI.

### Diagnostic Criteria for NMOSD
* **With AQP4-IgG +ve:** ≥1 core clinical characteristic (Optic neuritis, Acute myelitis, Area postrema syndrome [unexplained hiccups/vomiting]) + positive AQP4 test.
* **With AQP4-IgG -ve/Unknown:** Requires stricter criteria (≥2 core clinical characteristics, at least one being ON/LETM/Area postrema) + strict MRI requirements.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of MS Relapse
* **Indication:** Functionally disabling symptoms (e.g., visual loss, motor weakness). Mild sensory symptoms usually observed.
* **First-line Pharmacotherapy:**
  * **IV Methylprednisolone:** 1g daily for 3-5 days.
  * Alternate HA outpatient option: High-dose oral Methylprednisolone (500mg - 1g daily) for 3-5 days.
  * *Note: Does NOT alter long-term disease progression, only accelerates recovery.*
* **Second-line (Steroid-refractory severe relapse):**
  * **Plasmapheresis (Plasma Exchange, PLEX):** 5-7 exchanges over 10-14 days.

### Acute Management of Severe NMOSD / Refractory Relapses
* **Aggressive early treatment required** (NMOSD relapses cause permanent severe damage).
* **First-line:** IV Methylprednisolone 1g daily x5 days.
* **Second-line/Early Escalation:** PLEX is strongly favored early if poor steroid response or severe motor/visual deficit.
* **Third-line:** IV Immunoglobulin (IVIG) 2g/kg over 5 days.

### Acute Management of ADEM
* **First-line:** IV Methylprednisolone 1g daily for 3-5 days → prolonged oral prednisolone taper (4-6 weeks) to prevent recurrence.
* **Refractory cases:** PLEX or IVIG.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### General Approach to Disease-Modifying Therapies (DMTs) in MS
* **Goal:** Reduce relapse rate, reduce MRI lesion burden, delay disability progression (EDSS).
* **HA Prescribing Reality:** Tier 1 drugs are standard formulary. Tier 2 requires special application (Samaritan Fund/Community Care Fund) after failing Tier 1 or presenting with highly active disease.
* **Pre-Treatment Screening:** LFTs, FBC, VZV serology, HBV/HCV screening, TB screening, baseline MRI.

### Tier 1 Disease-Modifying Therapies (Moderate Efficacy)
* **Interferon beta-1a / beta-1b (S/C or I/M):**
  * MoA: Immunomodulatory cytokines.
  * Side effects: Flu-like symptoms, injection site reactions, transaminitis, depression.
* **Glatiramer Acetate (S/C):**
  * MoA: Myelin basic protein decoy, shifts T-cell phenotype (Th1 → Th2).
  * Side effects: Post-injection systemic reaction (flushing, chest tightness, palpitations - benign).
* **Teriflunomide (Oral):**
  * MoA: Pyrimidine synthesis inhibitor (targets rapidly dividing T/B cells).
  * Side effects: Hepatotoxicity, teratogenic (strict contraception needed).

### Tier 2 Disease-Modifying Therapies (High Efficacy)
* **Natalizumab (IV):**
  * MoA: Anti-α4 integrin monoclonal antibody (blocks lymphocyte entry into BBB).
  * Major Risk: Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) via JC virus reactivation.
* **Ocrelizumab (IV) / Ofatumumab (S/C):**
  * MoA: Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (depletes B-cells).
  * Indication: Approved for RRMS and highly active PPMS.
  * Side effects: Infusion reactions, ↑ risk of infections (HBV reactivation).
* **Fingolimod (Oral):**
  * MoA: S1P receptor modulator (traps lymphocytes in lymph nodes).
  * Major Risk: First-dose bradycardia/AV block (requires 6-hour ECG monitoring during initiation), macular edema.

### Chronic Management of NMOSD
* **Rule of Thumb:** DO NOT use MS DMTs (Interferons, Natalizumab, Fingolimod) for NMOSD → Can cause catastrophic exacerbation.
* **Maintenance Immunosuppression (Standard HA):**
  * **Oral Prednisolone** combined with **Azathioprine** or **Mycophenolate Mofetil**.
* **Biologics (Targeted Therapy):**
  * **Rituximab (Anti-CD20):** Very effective for relapse prevention.
  * Eculizumab (Anti-C5 complement), Inebilizumab (Anti-CD19), Satralizumab (Anti-IL6) – newer agents, heavily restricted funding.

### Symptomatic Management in Demyelinating Diseases
* **Spasticity:** Baclofen (GABA-B agonist), Tizanidine, Dantrolene, Botox injections.
* **Fatigue:** Amantadine, Modafinil, aggressive cooling strategies.
* **Neuropathic Pain:** Pregabalin, Gabapentin, Amitriptyline, Duloxetine.
* **Neurogenic Bladder:** 
  * Hyperreflexic (Urge): Oxybutynin, Tolterodine (Anticholinergics).
  * Flaccid (Retention): Intermittent self-catheterization (ISC).
* **Mobility:** Physiotherapy, occupational therapy.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) in MS
* **Purpose:** Quantifies MS disability, determines DMT eligibility and progression.
* **Score Range:** 0 (Normal) to 10 (Death).
* **Key Landmarks:**
  * **EDSS 0:** Normal neurologic exam.
  * **EDSS 4.0:** Significant disability but fully ambulatory without aid for ≥500m.
  * **EDSS 6.0:** Requires unilateral walking aid (cane/crutch) to walk 100m.
  * **EDSS 6.5:** Requires bilateral walking aid (walker).
  * **EDSS 7.0:** Wheelchair bound.

### Prognostic Factors in Multiple Sclerosis

| Favorable Prognosis | Poor Prognosis |
| :--- | :--- |
| Female gender | Male gender |
| Young age at onset (<40) | Older age at onset (>40) |
| Relapsing-remitting initial course | Primary progressive course |
| Sensory or visual onset symptoms | Motor, cerebellar, or sphincter onset |
| Complete recovery from first attack | Incomplete recovery from first attack |
| Low early lesion burden on MRI | High lesion burden / early brain atrophy |

### Prognosis of NMOSD and MOGAD
* **NMOSD:** Poor without treatment. Relapses are severe and cumulative. High risk of blindness and wheelchair dependency within 5 years if untreated. Mortality associated with cervical cord lesions causing respiratory failure.
* **MOGAD:** Generally better prognosis. Often highly steroid-responsive. Less residual disability compared to NMOSD, though relapses can occur.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological and Systemic Complications of MS
* **Neuro-cognitive:** Early dementia, memory impairment, severe clinical depression (↑ suicide risk).
* **Urological:** Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections (UTI), pyelonephritis, renal failure (due to chronic retention and reflux).
* **Respiratory:** Aspiration pneumonia (due to bulbar palsy/dysphagia), hypoventilation in advanced disease.
* **Musculoskeletal:** Severe flexion contractures, osteoporosis (from immobility and recurrent steroid use).
* **Skin:** Decubitus ulcers (pressure sores) in bed-bound state (EDSS >8.0).

### Complications of NMOSD
* **Respiratory Failure:** Due to ascending cervical myelitis extending to the brainstem (medullary respiratory centers).
* **Intractable Vomiting/Dehydration:** Due to persistent Area Postrema syndrome.
* **Bilateral Blindness:** Permanent optic nerve atrophy from severe bilateral optic neuritis.

### Adverse Effects of High-Efficacy DMTs
* **PML (Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy):** Risk heavily tied to Natalizumab use + +ve JCV serology + prior immunosuppression.
* **Thyroid Autoimmunity:** Strongly associated with Alemtuzumab (anti-CD52).
* **Lymphopenia & Opportunistic Infections:** Reactivation of Tuberculosis (TB), Hepatitis B, Herpes Zoster.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What are the typical CSF findings in Multiple Sclerosis? Answer: Normal opening pressure, normal protein/glucose, mild lymphocytic pleocytosis (<50 cells/µL), positive Oligoclonal Bands (OCBs), and elevated IgG index.
* Trap: Prescribing Interferon or Fingolimod for a patient diagnosed with NMOSD. Pearl: MS-specific disease-modifying therapies can severely exacerbate NMOSD. Always check AQP4-IgG before initiating DMTs in atypical or severe demyelinating cases.
* Pitfall: Assuming bilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO) in an older adult is automatically MS. Pearl: While highly suggestive of MS in young adults, INO in the elderly is more commonly caused by an ischemic brainstem stroke.
* Viva Question: What does the Lhermitte's sign indicate anatomically? Answer: Demyelinating plaques or mechanical compression in the cervical spinal cord (specifically the posterior columns).
* Trap: Confusing an MS relapse with a pseudoexacerbation. Pearl: Always check for underlying systemic infections (e.g., UTI, pneumonia) or fever (Uhthoff's phenomenon) before prescribing high-dose IV steroids, as these triggers transiently worsen old neurological deficits without forming new lesions.
* Viva Question: What is the cardinal MRI feature differentiating transverse myelitis in MS versus NMOSD? Answer: MS typical has short-segment cord lesions (<3 vertebral bodies), whereas NMOSD classically presents with Longitudinally Extensive Transverse Myelitis (LETM), spanning ≥3 contiguous vertebral bodies.
