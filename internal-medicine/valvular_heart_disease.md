# Valvular Heart Disease (瓣膜性心臟病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Valvular Heart Disease
* **Valvular Stenosis (狹窄):** Failure of a valve to open completely → impedes forward flow → pressure overload.
* **Valvular Regurgitation (倒流):** Failure of a valve to close completely → allows retrograde flow → volume overload.
* **Mixed Disease:** Coexistence of stenosis and regurgitation in the same valve or multiple valves.

### Etiologies of Left-Sided Valve Disease
* **Aortic Stenosis (AS):** Degenerative calcification (most common in HK aging pop), bicuspid aortic valve (congenital), rheumatic heart disease (RHD).
* **Aortic Regurgitation (AR):** Valvular (bicuspid valve, infective endocarditis, RHD) vs. Aortic root dilation (Marfan syndrome, aortic dissection, syphilis, ankylosing spondylitis).
* **Mitral Stenosis (MS):** RHD (>99% of cases, often immigrants or older HK population), severe MAC (Mitral Annular Calcification).
* **Mitral Regurgitation (MR):** 
  * **Primary (Organic):** Mitral valve prolapse (MVP), RHD, infective endocarditis, connective tissue diseases.
  * **Secondary (Functional):** Ischemic cardiomyopathy (papillary muscle dysfunction/rupture), dilated cardiomyopathy (annular dilation).

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong Context)
* **Degenerative Calcific AS:** Leading cause of adult valvular surgery in HA hospitals due to aging demographics.
* **Rheumatic Heart Disease (風濕性心臟病):** Incidence ↓ dramatically post-antibiotic era, but remains highly relevant in older adults (diagnosed decades ago) and regional migrant populations.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Aortic Stenosis (AS) Pathophysiology
* LV outflow obstruction → ↑ LV systolic pressure (Pressure Overload) → Compensatory Concentric LV Hypertrophy (LVH).
* Thickened myocardium → ↓ LV compliance (diastolic dysfunction) & ↑ myocardial O2 demand.
* Capillary density mismatch + high wall stress → subendocardial ischemia (angina despite normal coronaries).
* Eventual exhaustion of compensatory mechanisms → LV dilation → Systolic Heart Failure.

### Aortic Regurgitation (AR) Pathophysiology
* Diastolic reflux of blood from Aorta to LV → ↑ LV End-Diastolic Volume (Volume Overload).
* Compensatory Eccentric LV Hypertrophy and LV dilation → ↑ stroke volume (SV) to maintain forward cardiac output.
* High SV rapidly empties into aorta → ↑ systolic BP; rapid diastolic drop-off → ↓ diastolic BP (wide pulse pressure).
* Prolonged volume overload → progressive LV wall stress → interstitial fibrosis → irreversible LV dysfunction.

### Mitral Stenosis (MS) Pathophysiology
* Restricted LV inflow → ↑ Left Atrial (LA) pressure → LA dilation & remodeling → ↑ risk of Atrial Fibrillation (AFib) & mural thrombus.
* Backwards transmission of pressure → ↑ pulmonary venous pressure → pulmonary congestion (dyspnea).
* Chronic ↑ pulmonary pressure → Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH) → Right Ventricular (RV) hypertrophy & failure (Cor Pulmonale).
* LV remains protected (underfilled, normal size/function).

### Mitral Regurgitation (MR) Pathophysiology
* **Acute MR:** Sudden volume overload on unyielding, non-compliant LA → sudden ↑ LA pressure → Flash Pulmonary Edema.
* **Chronic MR:** Progressive regurgitation into LA during systole → eccentric hypertrophy & dilation of BOTH LA and LV (Volume Overload).
* Compensatory LV enlargement maintains forward SV initially.
* Eventually, LV contractility declines → progressive Heart Failure.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Clinical History for Aortic Valve Disease
* **AS Symptoms:** Classical triad of Angina, Syncope (exertional due to fixed cardiac output), Heart Failure (dyspnea, orthopnea, PND).
* **AR Symptoms:** Palpitations (forceful contractions), dyspnea on exertion, angina, head pounding.

### Clinical History for Mitral Valve Disease
* **MS Symptoms:** Exertional dyspnea, hemoptysis (rupture of bronchial veins), palpitations (AFib), fatigue, systemic thromboembolism (stroke).
* **MR Symptoms:** Fatigue, lethargy, exertional dyspnea, right-sided heart failure symptoms late in disease (ankle edema, ascites).

### Physical Examination: Aortic Stenosis (AS) Signs
* **Pulse:** Slow-rising (pulsus parvus et tardus), narrow pulse pressure.
* **Palpation:** Heaving (sustained) apex beat, palpable systolic thrill in aortic area/neck.
* **Auscultation:** Ejection Systolic Murmur (ESM) loudest at aortic area, radiating to carotids. Soft/absent A2. S4 gallop (LVH). Paradoxical splitting of S2 in severe cases.

### Physical Examination: Aortic Regurgitation (AR) Signs
* **Pulse:** Collapsing (water-hammer) pulse, bounding, wide pulse pressure.
* **Palpation:** Displaced, thrusting apex beat.
* **Auscultation:** Early Diastolic Murmur (EDM) at lower left sternal edge (patient leaning forward, end-expiration). Ejection systolic flow murmur. Austin Flint murmur (mid-diastolic rumble due to AR jet hitting anterior mitral leaflet).
* **Eponymous Signs:** Corrigan's (visible carotid pulsation), Quincke's (capillary pulsation in nail beds), De Musset's (head bobbing), Duroziez's (femoral diastolic murmur), Traube's (pistol-shot femorals).

### Physical Examination: Mitral Stenosis (MS) Signs
* **General:** Malar flush (plum-red cheeks due to CO2 retention/low output).
* **Pulse:** Irregularly irregular if AFib is present.
* **Palpation:** Tapping apex beat (palpable loud S1), RV heave (if pulmonary HTN).
* **Auscultation:** Loud S1, Opening Snap (OS) after S2, low-pitched Mid-Diastolic Murmur (MDM) with presystolic accentuation (if in sinus rhythm) at the apex (patient in left lateral position, bell of stethoscope).

### Physical Examination: Mitral Regurgitation (MR) Signs
* **Pulse:** May be brisk or irregular (AFib).
* **Palpation:** Displaced, hyperdynamic apex beat. Systolic thrill at apex.
* **Auscultation:** Pan-Systolic Murmur (PSM) loudest at apex, radiating to axilla. Soft S1. S3 gallop (rapid ventricular filling due to large volume from LA).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentials for Ejection Systolic Murmur
* **Aortic Sclerosis:** Murmur does not radiate to carotids; normal pulse volume/character; A2 is preserved.
* **Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM):** Murmur ↑ with Valsalva/standing (↓ preload), ↓ with squatting (↑ preload). Jerky pulse.
* **Pulmonary Stenosis:** Best heard at pulmonary area; does not radiate to carotids; prominent *a* wave in JVP.
* **Innocent Flow Murmur:** Soft, short, asymptomatic (common in pregnancy, anemia, thyrotoxicosis).

### Differentials for Pan-Systolic Murmur
* **Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD):** Loudest at lower left sternal edge; frequent parasternal thrill; does not usually radiate to axilla.
* **Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR):** Prominent *v* waves in JVP; pulsatile liver; murmur ↑ on inspiration (Carvallo's sign).

### Differentials for Diastolic Murmur
* **Pulmonary Regurgitation (Graham Steell murmur):** Associated with severe pulmonary HTN; lacks peripheral AR signs (e.g., collapsing pulse).
* **Atrial Myxoma:** Can mimic MS with a "tumor plop" (mimicking opening snap) and a diastolic rumble; systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, ↑ ESR).

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **ECG:** 
  * **AS/AR:** LVH (S in V1 + R in V5/V6 > 35mm), ST-T wave strain patterns, left axis deviation, LBBB.
  * **MS:** P-mitrale (bifid P waves in II) or AFib, RVH, Right Axis Deviation.
* **Urinalysis:** Microscopic hematuria if infective endocarditis is suspected.

### Blood Tests
* **Biomarkers:** NT-proBNP (assesses HF severity and timing of intervention).
* **IE Workup:** Blood cultures (3 sets from different sites) BEFORE antibiotics, CRP, ESR, CBC.
* **Pre-op Workup:** U&E, LFT, Coagulation screen (INR/aPTT), Group & Screen.

### Imaging (Non-Invasive)
* **CXR:** 
  * **AS:** Calcified aortic valve, post-stenotic aortic dilation. Boot-shaped heart (LVH).
  * **MR/AR:** Cardiomegaly (LV/LA dilation), signs of pulmonary edema.
  * **MS:** Splaying of carina (angle >90°) by enlarged LA, double right heart border, prominent pulmonary arteries (Kerley B lines).
* **Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE):** Gold standard for diagnosis, severity grading, and LV function.
* **Transesophageal Echocardiogram (TOE):** Required for assessing endocarditis (vegetations), precise mitral valve morphology for repair (e.g., TEER), and excluding LA appendage thrombus.

### Diagnostic Criteria for Severe Aortic & Mitral Disease (Echo)

| Parameter | Severe AS | Severe AR | Severe MS | Severe MR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Valve Area** | < 1.0 cm² | - | < 1.5 cm² | - |
| **Mean Gradient** | ≥ 40 mmHg | - | > 10 mmHg | - |
| **Peak Velocity** | ≥ 4.0 m/s | - | - | - |
| **Regurgitant Fraction** | - | ≥ 50% | - | ≥ 50% |
| **Vena Contracta** | - | > 0.6 cm | - | ≥ 0.7 cm |

### Invasive Investigations
* **Coronary Angiography (Cardiac Catheterization):** Mandatory before valvular surgery in patients >40 years or those with CAD risk factors to rule out coexisting ischemic heart disease (guides concurrent CABG).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Pulmonary Edema in Valvular Disease
* **Acute Severe MR/AR (e.g., Papillary rupture, IE):**
  * **Oxygenation:** High-flow O2, CPAP/BiPAP.
  * **Pharmacotherapy:** IV Diuretics (Furosemide). IV Vasodilators (Nitrates, Sodium Nitroprusside) to ↓ afterload and improve forward flow.
  * **Mechanical Support:** Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP) for MR with cardiogenic shock. **CONTRAINDICATED in AR** (inflates in diastole → worsens regurgitation).
  * **Definitive Rx:** Urgent emergency surgical valve replacement/repair.

### Cardiogenic Shock in Critical Aortic Stenosis
* **Resuscitation:** Cautious volume optimization (LV needs preload to overcome obstruction). Avoid aggressive diuresis or vasodilators (can precipitate fatal hypotension).
* **Inotropes:** Low-dose Dobutamine if hypotensive.
* **Bridge to Surgery:** Balloon aortic valvuloplasty (BAV) as an emergency bridge in hemodynamically unstable patients awaiting definitive SAVR/TAVI.

### Rapid Atrial Fibrillation in Mitral Stenosis
* **Hemodynamically Unstable:** Urgent synchronized DC Cardioversion.
* **Hemodynamically Stable:** Rate control strictly required (IV Beta-blockers or Amiodarone) to ↑ diastolic filling time. Digoxin used if concurrent systolic HF.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Medical Management (Conservative)
* **Heart Failure Rx:** ACEi/ARBs, Beta-blockers, MRA (Spironolactone), SGLT2i for symptomatic LV dysfunction (Note: Vasodilators MUST be used with extreme caution in severe AS/MS).
* **Anticoagulation:** 
  * MS with AFib: **Warfarin** ONLY (DOACs are contraindicated in moderate/severe MS and mechanical valves). Target INR 2.0-3.0 (or 2.5-3.5 for metallic mitral valves).
  * MR/AR/AS with AFib: DOACs (e.g., Apixaban, Rivaroxaban) are standard in HK HA guidelines.
* **IE Prophylaxis:** Routine antibiotics before dental procedures ONLY for high-risk groups (prosthetic valves, previous IE, specific congenital heart diseases).

### Intervention Algorithm: Aortic Stenosis
* **Indication for intervention:** Symptomatic severe AS, asymptomatic severe AS with LVEF <50%, or undergoing other cardiac surgery.
* **Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement (SAVR):** Standard for low surgical risk, younger patients (<65-70 years), or bicuspid valves. Mechanical (requires lifelong warfarin) vs. Bioprosthetic (degenerates in 10-15 yrs, no warfarin).
* **Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation (TAVI):** Standard of care for high/prohibitive surgical risk, and increasingly the choice for intermediate-risk elderly patients (≥75 years) in HA network.

### Intervention Algorithm: Mitral Regurgitation
* **Indication for intervention:** Symptomatic acute severe MR, symptomatic chronic severe MR (LVEF >30%), or asymptomatic severe MR with LV dysfunction (LVEF ≤60% or LVESD ≥40mm).
* **Surgery:** Mitral Valve Repair (preferred over replacement to preserve subvalvular apparatus and LV function). Mitral Valve Replacement (MVR) if repair unfeasible.
* **Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair (TEER / MitraClip):** For highly symptomatic patients with prohibitive surgical risk (Heart Team consensus).

### Intervention Algorithm: Mitral Stenosis
* **Indication for intervention:** Symptomatic moderate/severe MS (MVA ≤1.5 cm²).
* **Percutaneous Transvenous Mitral Commissurotomy (PTMC / Balloon Valvuloplasty):** First-line if valve anatomy is favorable (pliable, non-calcified) and NO LA thrombus or significant MR.
* **Surgical Valve Replacement:** If PTMC contraindicated (calcified valves, significant subvalvular fusion, LA appendage clot).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Surgical Risk Scoring Systems
* **EuroSCORE II:** Predicts operative mortality after cardiac surgery based on patient, cardiac, and operative factors. Guides Heart Team decision between SAVR vs TAVI.
* **STS Score (Society of Thoracic Surgeons):** Validated score for estimating risk of mortality and morbidities in valvular surgeries. 
  * Low Risk: <4%
  * Intermediate Risk: 4-8%
  * High Risk: >8%

### Prognosis of Aortic Stenosis (Rule of 5-3-2)
* Average survival after onset of classical symptoms in severe AS without surgery:
  * **Angina:** 5 years.
  * **Syncope:** 3 years.
  * **Heart Failure:** 2 years.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Valvular & Cardiac Complications
* **Heart Failure:** Irreversible LV decompensation (burn-out) if timing of surgery is missed.
* **Arrhythmias:** 
  * Atrial fibrillation (high risk of LA thrombus/Stroke).
  * Heart block (especially in AS/Infective Endocarditis if aortic root abscess extends into the interventricular septum/bundle of His).
* **Infective Endocarditis (IE):** Damaged endothelium provides a nidus for bacterial vegetation.

### Systemic Complications
* **Thromboembolism:** Ischemic stroke, mesenteric ischemia, acute limb ischemia (highest risk in MS with AFib).
* **Pulmonary Hypertension (肺動脈高壓):** Right heart failure, congestive hepatomegaly, ascites.
* **Heyde's Syndrome:** Association of severe AS with gastrointestinal bleeding from angiodysplasia (AS causes acquired Von Willebrand disease type 2A due to shear stress destroying large vWF multimers).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are the echocardiographic criteria for severe aortic stenosis? Answer: Valve area < 1.0 cm², mean pressure gradient ≥ 40 mmHg, peak aortic jet velocity ≥ 4.0 m/s.
* Viva Question: How do you choose between a mechanical and a bioprosthetic valve? Answer: Mechanical for younger patients (<60) who can tolerate lifelong Warfarin. Bioprosthetic for older patients (>65-70) or those with contraindications to anticoagulation, accepting the risk of structural valve deterioration over 10-15 years.
* Trap: Prescribing aggressive nitrates or ACE inhibitors to a patient with heart failure symptoms who has critical aortic stenosis. Pearl: Vasodilators cause a precipitous drop in afterload. Because the AS patient has a fixed cardiac output, they cannot compensate, leading to profound, irreversible hypotension and circulatory collapse.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with severe acute MR (e.g., ruptured papillary muscle) will have a loud holosystolic murmur and a massively dilated LA. Pearl: In acute MR, the LA has not had time to dilate. Pressures equalize rapidly between the LV and LA, meaning the murmur may be remarkably short, soft, or even absent despite florid pulmonary edema.
* Pitfall: Using DOACs for a patient with mechanical heart valves or moderate-to-severe rheumatic mitral stenosis. Pearl: DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban) are strictly contraindicated. You MUST prescribe Warfarin for these patients to prevent catastrophic valve thrombosis and stroke.
