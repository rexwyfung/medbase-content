# Pressure Injuries (壓瘡)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition
* **Pressure Injury (壓瘡)**: Localized damage to the skin and/or underlying tissue, usually over a bony prominence, resulting from sustained pressure (including pressure associated with shear).
* Previously termed pressure ulcers, decubitus ulcers, or bedsores.

### Etiologies & Key Forces
* **Pressure (壓力)**: Sustained vertical force compressing tissue between bone and external surface → capillary occlusion → ischemia.
* **Shear (剪切力)**: Parallel sliding of tissue planes (e.g., sliding down in bed) → stretching, kinking, or tearing of subcutaneous blood vessels.
* **Friction (摩擦力)**: Epidermal stripping against support surfaces (e.g., dragging during transfers).
* **Microclimate**: Moisture [incontinence (大小便失禁), perspiration] → tissue maceration (浸漬) → ↑ susceptibility to friction/shear.

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong Context)
* High point prevalence in Hospital Authority (HA) acute medical wards, geriatrics (老人科), and ICU.
* **High-Risk Populations**: Advanced stroke (中風), spinal cord injury (脊髓損傷), advanced dementia (腦退化症), hip fractures.
* **Common Sites**: Sacrum/coccyx (骶骨/尾骨), heels (足跟), ischial tuberosities (坐骨結節), greater trochanters (大轉子), occiput (枕骨).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Ischemic Cascade
* Capillary closing pressure is normally ~32 mmHg.
* External pressure > capillary pressure → microvascular occlusion → tissue hypoxia → anaerobic metabolism → accumulation of toxic metabolites → cellular necrosis.
* **Time-Pressure Curve**: High pressure over short duration OR low pressure over long duration → irreversible tissue damage.

### Reperfusion Injury
* Release of pressure → sudden reoxygenation of ischemic tissue.
* Generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) + inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) → endothelial damage, thrombosis, and exacerbated local necrosis.

### "Tip of the Iceberg" Phenomenon
* Muscle tissue is highly metabolically active and more susceptible to ischemia than the epidermis/dermis.
* Necrosis typically begins deeply at the bone-muscle interface and spreads outward.
* Extensive deep tissue destruction often exists beneath relatively intact-appearing skin (Deep Tissue Pressure Injury).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Pertinent Patient History
* **Immobility**: Bedbound/chairbound status, contractures, transfer ability.
* **Sensory Deficits**: Neuropathy (DM), spinal cord injury, cerebrovascular accident.
* **Moisture Status**: Urinary/fecal incontinence, diaphoresis.
* **Nutritional Status**: Recent weight loss, dysphagia, NG tube/PEG feeding.
* **Pain**: Neuropathic or nociceptive pain at pressure sites (if patient has intact sensation).

### Physical Examination Findings
* **Skin Integrity & Coloration**:
  * Non-blanching erythema (early sign) → press finger on red area; if it remains red, microcirculation is damaged.
  * Purple/maroon localized areas of intact skin (deep tissue injury).
* **Wound Bed Characteristics**:
  * **Granulation Tissue (肉芽組織)**: Red, beefy, moist (healing).
  * **Slough (腐肉)**: Yellow, green, grey, non-viable, stringy/mucinous tissue.
  * **Eschar (焦痂)**: Black, brown, leathery, dry necrotic tissue.
* **Wound Geometry**:
  * Measurement: Length × Width × Depth (cm).
  * **Undermining**: Tissue destruction extending under intact wound edges.
  * **Tunneling/Sinus Tracts**: Narrow channels extending into surrounding tissue/fascia.
* **Signs of Local Infection**: Periwound erythema, induration, warmth, purulent exudate, malodour, friable granulation tissue, new/increasing pain.
* **Bone/Tendon Exposure**: Direct visualization or "probe-to-bone" +ve.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Moisture/Incontinence Related
* **Incontinence-Associated Dermatitis (IAD)**: Located in skin folds, perineum, inner thighs. Superficial (epidermal loss), irregular/diffuse edges. "Kissing" lesions. *Distinguishing feature: Not strictly over bony prominences; primarily moisture-driven.*

### Vascular Ulcers
* **Diabetic Foot Ulcer (糖尿病足部潰瘍)**: Located on plantar surfaces, metatarsal heads, tips of toes. Surrounded by thick callus. Associated with peripheral neuropathy.
* **Arterial Ulcer**: Located on toes, lateral malleolus. "Punched out" appearance, pale wound bed. Associated with weak pulses, intermittent claudication.
* **Venous Ulcer**: Located in gaiter area (medial malleolus). Shallow, highly exudative, irregular margins. Associated with hemosiderin staining, lipodermatosclerosis.

### Others
* **Skin Tears**: Traumatic separation of epidermis from dermis. Flap-like, occurring on extremities (arms/hands).
* **Malignancy (Marjolin's Ulcer)**: Squamous cell carcinoma developing in a chronic, non-healing wound. Rolled/everted edges.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Tests
* **Probe-to-Bone Test**: Sterile blunt probe mapping wound depth; feeling hard, gritty bone confirms high likelihood of underlying osteomyelitis.
* **Wound Swab for C&S**: *Do not swab routinely.* Indicated ONLY if clinical signs of infection (cellulitis, purulence). Must swab deep, viable tissue (Levine technique) after cleaning, targeting local HA pathogens (MRSA, VRE, Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter).

### Blood Investigations
* **Infectious Markers**: CBC (WBC ↑), CRP, ESR (persistently ↑ > 70 mm/hr suggests osteomyelitis).
* **Nutritional/Metabolic Profile**: HbA1c (glycemic control), Renal/Liver function. 
* **Note on Albumin/Prealbumin**: Reflects systemic inflammatory state rather than pure nutritional status, but used locally as prognostic baseline.

### Imaging (To R/O Osteomyelitis)
* **Plain Radiographs (X-ray)**: Bedside detection of underlying bone destruction, periosteal reaction, or soft tissue gas. (Low sensitivity in early stages <2 weeks).
* **MRI with Contrast**: Gold standard for detecting osteomyelitis (骨髓炎) or deep abscesses.

### Biopsy & Invasive
* **Bone Biopsy**: Definitive diagnostic standard for osteomyelitis to guide targeted long-term antibiotics (often done by orthopaedics).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Sepsis Secondary to Infected Pressure Injury
* **Resuscitation**: ABCDE approach. IV fluid boluses (crystalloids) if hypotensive.
* **Microbiology**: Blood cultures × 2 sets before Abx. Wound swab (deep tissue) or aspirate from deep abscess.
* **Empirical IV Antibiotics**: Broad-spectrum covering MRSA, anaerobes, and Gram-negatives (e.g., Piperacillin-Tazobactam + Vancomycin, adjusted per HA antibiogram).
* **Source Control**: Urgent surgical consultation for bedside incision & drainage (I&D) if fluctuant abscess, or formal debridement if spreading necrotizing fasciitis (壞死性筋膜炎).

### Acute Hemorrhage
* **Management**: Apply direct local pressure (pressure dressing/alginate). Topical adrenaline or tranexamic acid soaks if diffuse capillary oozing. Consult surgery if arterial bleeding from eroded vessel.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Step 1: Pressure Offloading & Prevention (Cornerstone)
* **Repositioning**: Turn patient every 2-4 hours (翻身). Use 30-degree lateral inclined position.
* **Support Surfaces**:
  * *High-Risk*: High-specification foam mattresses.
  * *Existing PI (Stage III/IV)*: Alternating air pressure mattresses (氣墊床).
* **Heel Protection**: Heel offloading boots/pillows (ensure heels "float").
* **Microclimate Control**: Prompt changing of soiled diapers, use of barrier creams (e.g., Cavilon, Zinc oxide) for IAD prevention.

### Step 2: Local Wound Care (TIME Framework)
* **T (Tissue Management/Debridement)**:
  * *Autolytic*: Hydrocolloid/hydrogel dressings (promotes natural enzyme action).
  * *Enzymatic*: E.g., Collagenase (rare in HA).
  * *Conservative Sharp*: Scalpel/forceps bedside debridement of slough.
  * *Biological*: Maggot therapy (rarely used locally).
  * *Exception*: Stable, hard, dry, non-infected eschar on heels MUST NOT be debrided.
* **I (Infection/Inflammation)**:
  * Avoid systemic Abx for mere colonization.
  * *Topical Antimicrobials*: Silver-impregnated dressings (Aquacel Ag), Cadexomer iodine (Iodosorb) for heavily colonized/malodorous wounds (max 2 weeks).
* **M (Moisture Balance)**:
  * *Dry Wounds*: Hydrogel to donate moisture.
  * *Mild Exudate*: Hydrocolloid or semi-permeable film.
  * *Heavy Exudate*: Alginate or Polyurethane Foam dressings to absorb.
* **E (Edge & Periwound)**:
  * Protect periwound skin with barrier films.

### Step 3: Nutritional Support
* **Target Calories**: 30-35 kcal/kg/day.
* **Target Protein**: 1.25-1.5 g/kg/day (crucial for collagen synthesis).
* **Supplementation**: Oral nutritional supplements (e.g., Resource Arginaid, Cubitan) rich in Arginine, Vitamin C, and Zinc (local HA dietitian protocol).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### NPUAP / NPIAP Pressure Injury Staging System
| Stage | Clinical Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1** | Non-blanchable erythema of intact skin. |
| **Stage 2** | Partial-thickness skin loss with exposed dermis. Blistering (intact or ruptured). No slough/eschar. |
| **Stage 3** | Full-thickness skin loss. Subcutaneous fat visible. Slough/eschar may be present. Undermining/tunneling may occur. Fascia/muscle/bone NOT exposed. |
| **Stage 4** | Full-thickness skin and tissue loss. Exposed fascia, muscle, tendon, ligament, cartilage, or bone. |
| **Unstageable** | Full-thickness skin/tissue loss where base is completely obscured by slough/eschar. True depth cannot be determined (usually Stage 3 or 4). |
| **Deep Tissue Injury (DTI)** | Persistent non-blanchable deep red, maroon, or purple discoloration. Intact or non-intact skin. Epidermal separation revealing dark wound bed. |

### Risk Assessment Tools (Conducted on Admission)
| Tool | Components Assessed | Scoring Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Braden Scale** (Widely used in HA) | Sensory perception, Moisture, Activity, Mobility, Nutrition, Friction/Shear. | **Lower score = Higher risk**.<br>≤9: Severe risk.<br>10-12: High risk.<br>13-14: Moderate risk. |
| **Waterlow Score** | BMI, Continence, Skin type, Mobility, Sex/Age, Appetite, Special risks (Neuro deficit, Surgery). | **Higher score = Higher risk**.<br>≥10: At risk.<br>≥15: High risk.<br>≥20: Very high risk. |

### Prognosis
* Stage 1 & 2: Highly reversible with excellent prognosis upon pressure relief.
* Stage 3 & 4: Require prolonged healing (months). High risk of recurrence (scar tissue is only 80% tensile strength of original skin).
* Presence of PI in elderly hospitalized patients independently correlates with ↑ 1-year mortality and prolonged length of stay.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Local Complications
* **Osteomyelitis (骨髓炎)**: Extension of infection into underlying bone.
* **Septic Arthritis**: Joint space involvement (e.g., hip joint from trochanteric PI).
* **Sinus Tracts / Fistulas**: E.g., communication with bowel or bladder in severe sacral/pelvic PI.
* **Marjolin's Ulcer**: Squamous cell carcinoma arising in chronic unhealed PI (rare).

### Systemic Complications
* **Sepsis & Bacteremia (敗血症/菌血症)**: Anaerobic/polymicrobial spread.
* **Infective Endocarditis**: Secondary to continuous low-grade bacteremia.
* **Protein-Calorie Malnutrition**: Massive protein loss through highly exudative Stage 4 wounds (exudate is protein-rich).
* **Amyloidosis**: Secondary (AA) amyloidosis due to chronic systemic inflammation (rare but recognized in decades-long spinal cord injury patients).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate a Stage 2 pressure injury from Incontinence-Associated Dermatitis (IAD)? Answer: Stage 2 PI is strictly localized over a bony prominence due to pressure/shear, whereas IAD is moisture-driven and typically occurs in skin folds, buttocks, or perineum with diffuse, irregular edges ("kissing lesions").
* Trap: Swabbing a clinically uninfected pressure injury to "see what's growing". Pearl: All chronic wounds are colonized by bacteria. Only perform a swab if there are clinical signs of infection (cellulitis, increasing pain, purulent discharge, malodour) or delayed healing to avoid inappropriate antibiotic prescribing.
* Pitfall: Aggressively debriding a dry, stable, intact eschar on the heel. The heel lacks rich collateral subcutaneous circulation; an intact eschar acts as a natural biological cover. Debridement without confirming adequate vascular perfusion can expose underlying structures to limb-threatening infection.
* Viva Question: What is the "probe-to-bone" test and what is its clinical utility? Answer: It involves using a sterile, blunt instrument to probe the depths of a chronic wound. Palpating a hard, gritty surface (bone) has a high positive predictive value for underlying osteomyelitis.
* Trap: Believing that a superficial skin breakdown implies superficial injury. Pearl: Remember the "tip of the iceberg" phenomenon. Muscle tissue is more susceptible to ischemic necrosis than skin; extensive deep tissue destruction (Deep Tissue Pressure Injury) often exists beneath a small or intact skin surface.
