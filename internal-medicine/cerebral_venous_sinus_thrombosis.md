# Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (腦靜脈竇血栓形成)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of CVST
* **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (腦靜脈竇血栓形成)**: Thrombus formation within the dural venous sinuses or cerebral veins → obstruction of venous drainage and CSF absorption.

### Etiologies and Risk Factors for CVST
* **Hypercoagulable States**:
  * Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COCP)
  * Pregnancy and Puerperium (產褥期) (High risk up to 6 weeks postpartum)
  * Malignancy
  * Severe Dehydration (especially in extremes of age)
  * Nephrotic Syndrome (loss of Antithrombin III)
* **Local Infections**:
  * Otitis Media / Mastoiditis (→ Transverse/Sigmoid sinus thrombosis)
  * Sinusitis / Facial infections (→ Cavernous sinus thrombosis)
* **Mechanical / Trauma**: Head injury, neurosurgery, jugular vein catheterization.

### Epidemiology of CVST in Hong Kong
* **Incidence**: Rare, ~1% of all strokes. 
* **Demographics**: Strong female predominance (F:M = 3:1) due to COCP and pregnancy. 
* **Peak Age**: Young adults (20-35 years).
* **Local HK Context**: Baseline VTE rates are lower in Southern Chinese vs Caucasians, but incidence is rising locally due to ↑ COCP usage and postpartum prothrombotic state.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Venous Infarction in CVST
* **Venous Occlusion**: Thrombus blocks local cerebral veins.
* **Hemodynamic Consequence**: ↑ Venous pressure → ↑ Capillary hydrostatic pressure.
* **Tissue Injury**: BBB breakdown → Vasogenic edema → Rupture of venules → **Hemorrhagic Venous Infarction (出血性靜脈梗塞)**.

### Pathophysiology of Intracranial Hypertension in CVST
* **CSF Blockade**: Thrombus in major sinuses (e.g., Superior Sagittal Sinus) blocks arachnoid granulations.
* **Hemodynamic Consequence**: Failure of CSF absorption into the systemic venous system.
* **Tissue Injury**: Diffuse ↑ Intracranial Pressure (ICP) (顱內高壓) without ventriculomegaly → Papilloedema (視乳頭水腫) and global headaches.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Historical Features of CVST
* **Headache**: Most common symptom (90%); diffuse, progressive, often worsens with Valsalva maneuver or lying flat.
* **Seizures (癲癇)**: Occurs in ~40% (focal or generalized); highly suggestive if present in setting of new headache.
* **Visual Symptoms**: Transient visual obscurations, diplopia.
* **Cognitive/Motor**: Unilateral or bilateral limb weakness, altered mental status, aphasia.

### Physical Examination Signs in CVST
* **Fundoscopy**: Bilateral Papilloedema (視乳頭水腫) (loss of venous pulsations, optic disc swelling, flame hemorrhages).
* **Cranial Nerves**: 
  * CN VI Palsy (Abducens): False localizing sign secondary to ↑ ICP.
  * Cavernous Sinus Syndrome (if localized here): Proptosis, chemosis, periorbital edema, CN III/IV/V1/V2/VI palsies.
* **Neurology**: Focal upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (hyperreflexia, +ve Babinski, hemiparesis).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for CVST Presentation

| Condition | Discriminatory Clinical & Diagnostic Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CVST** | Headache + Seizures + Focal deficits. Venous infarcts (often bilateral) on neuroimaging. |
| **Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH)** | Obese young female. Headache + Papilloedema. **NO** focal deficits. Normal MRV (no thrombus). |
| **Meningitis / Encephalitis** | Fever, meningism (nuchal rigidity), altered sensorium. CSF shows pleocytosis. |
| **Brain Abscess** | Focal mass effect. Ring-enhancing lesion on MRI. Associated with fever/endocarditis. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside and Blood Investigations for CVST
* **D-Dimer**: 
  * Normal level does **NOT** exclude CVST (especially in subacute/chronic presentations).
  * High negative predictive value only in acute (<1 week) cases.
* **Thrombophilia Screen**:
  * Protein C/S, Antithrombin III, Lupus Anticoagulant, Anti-Cardiolipin, Anti-beta-2-glycoprotein 1, Factor V Leiden, Prothrombin G20210A.
  * *Note*: Do NOT test during acute thrombosis or while on acute anticoagulants (causes false +ve/-ve).

### Neuroimaging Findings in CVST

| Modality | Key Diagnostic Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Contrast CT Brain** | **Dense Delta Sign**: Hyperdense thrombus in posterior sagittal sinus.<br>**Cord Sign**: Hyperdense cortical vein.<br>Venous hemorrhage (parasagittal, bilateral). |
| **CT Venogram (CTV)** | **Empty Delta Sign**: Contrast outlines the triangular sinus wall, leaving a central dark filling defect (the thrombus). |
| **MRI Brain + MR Venogram (MRV)** | **Gold Standard**. Absence of normal flow void in dural sinuses. Differentiates vasogenic vs cytotoxic edema. |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Anticoagulation Protocol for CVST
* **Primary Therapy (抗凝治療)**: Initiate parenteral anticoagulation **IMMEDIATELY**.
* **Drug Choice**: Subcutaneous LMWH (e.g., Enoxaparin weight-based) or IV Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) infusion.
* **Critical Rule**: Anticoagulation is indicated **EVEN IF** hemorrhagic venous infarction is present on initial CT/MRI (benefits of recanalization far outweigh bleeding expansion risk).

### Management of Raised ICP and Seizures in CVST
* **ICP Control**: Head elevation to 30°, IV Mannitol or Hypertonic Saline for impending herniation.
* **Neurosurgery**: Decompressive hemicraniectomy indicated for malignant edema/midline shift causing brainstem compression.
* **Anti-Epileptic Drugs (AEDs)**: IV Levetiracetam or IV Phenytoin ONLY if clinical seizures occur (prophylactic AEDs not routinely recommended).

### Endovascular Therapy for Refractory CVST
* **Indications**: Clinical deterioration (deepening coma, expanding infarcts) despite therapeutic heparinization.
* **Procedure**: Endovascular mechanical thrombectomy +/- intrasinus thrombolysis.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Anticoagulation Algorithm for CVST
* **Transition**: Switch from LMWH/UFH to oral anticoagulation once clinically stabilized.
* **First-line Agent**: Oral Warfarin (Target INR 2.0 - 3.0).
* **Alternative**: Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) (e.g., Dabigatran, Apixaban) are increasingly used off-label in HA if no severe thrombophilia (e.g., anti-phospholipid syndrome).

### Duration of Chronic Anticoagulation in CVST

| Clinical Scenario | Recommended Duration of Anticoagulation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Provoked** (e.g., COCP, pregnancy, infection) | 3 to 6 months |
| **Unprovoked** (Idiopathic) | 6 to 12 months |
| **Recurrent CVST or Severe Thrombophilia** | Lifelong |

### Lifestyle and Counseling for CVST Survivors
* **Contraception**: Absolute contraindication to estrogen-containing COCPs. Transition to progesterone-only pill (POP) or intrauterine device (IUD).
* **Pregnancy Planning**: High risk of recurrence postpartum. Require prophylactic LMWH during future pregnancies and 6 weeks puerperium.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Indicators in CVST
* **ISCVT Poor Prognosis Predictors**:
  * Coma at presentation
  * Deep venous system involvement (Internal cerebral veins, Vein of Galen, Straight sinus)
  * Right hemisphere hemorrhage
  * Malignancy
  * Age > 37 years

### Long-Term Prognosis and Recurrence Rates in CVST
* **Overall Survival**: Mortality < 10% (mostly due to acute herniation).
* **Functional Recovery**: >80% achieve complete or near-complete independent recovery (modified Rankin Scale 0-1).
* **Recurrence**: Low (~2-4%), but VTE (e.g., DVT/PE) occurs in ~5% of survivors.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological Complications of CVST
* **Visual Loss**: Permanent optic nerve atrophy due to prolonged, untreated ↑ ICP.
* **Chronic Epilepsy**: Secondary to cortical hemorrhagic scarring (requires long-term AEDs).
* **Vascular**: Development of dural arteriovenous fistulas (dAVF) due to abnormal angiogenesis during sinus recanalization.

### Pharmacological Adverse Effects in CVST Treatment
* **Heparin**: Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT) (Monitor platelets on days 4-14).
* **Warfarin**: Intracranial or major GI hemorrhage (Reversal with IV Prothrombin Complex Concentrate + IV Vitamin K).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why do we still give therapeutic anticoagulation to a CVST patient who already has a hemorrhagic venous infarction on their CT scan? Answer: The hemorrhage is a direct result of extreme venous hypertension and capillary rupture. Anticoagulation arrests thrombus propagation, allowing endogenous fibrinolysis to restore venous drainage, which paradoxically stops the bleeding.
* Trap: Believing a normal D-dimer safely rules out CVST in a patient with a persistent headache. Pearl: D-dimer sensitivity drops significantly after the first week of symptom onset. If clinical suspicion is high (e.g., young woman on COCP with new severe headache), you MUST proceed directly to MRV or CTV regardless of the D-dimer level.
* Pitfall: Withholding anticoagulation because a patient presents with an isolated seizure and small parasagittal hemorrhage without checking venous imaging. Always check CTV/MRV in young patients with atypical, bilateral, or parasagittal hemorrhages to rule out CVST before assuming it is a primary intracerebral hemorrhage.
