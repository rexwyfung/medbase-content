# Tinea & Fungal Skin Infections (癬及皮膚真菌感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Superficial Fungal Infections
* **Dermatophytosis (Tinea)**: Superficial infection of keratinized tissue (skin, hair, nails) by dermatophyte fungi.
* **Cutaneous Candidiasis**: Opportunistic infection of intertriginous skin/mucosa by *Candida* species.
* **Pityriasis Versicolor (汗斑)**: Mild, chronic infection of stratum corneum by *Malassezia* yeasts.

### Etiology of Dermatophytes
* **Trichophyton**: Infects skin, hair, nails (e.g., *T. rubrum* → most common cause of Tinea Pedis/Cruris/Corporis).
* **Microsporum**: Infects skin, hair (e.g., *M. canis* from cats/dogs).
* **Epidermophyton**: Infects skin, nails (e.g., *E. floccosum*).

### Etiology of Opportunistic Yeasts
* **Candida**: *C. albicans* (most common), *C. glabrata*.
* **Malassezia**: *M. furfur*, *M. globosa* (normal skin flora).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Climate Impact**: HK's hot, humid summer heavily ↑ incidence of Tinea Cruris, Pedis, and Pityriasis Versicolor.
* **Demographics**: Tinea Capitis primarily in children; Tinea Pedis/Cruris/Unguium predominantly in adult males. 
* **Zoonotic Transmission**: *M. canis* highly prevalent in HK pet owners (especially stray cats).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Dermatophytes
* **Keratinophilic Nature**: Fungi secrete **keratinases** → degrade keratin for nutrients.
* **Tissue Restriction**: Infection limited to dead, keratinized layer (stratum corneum); does NOT penetrate viable tissue due to host immune response (transferrin binds required iron, macrophages clear deep invasion).
* **Host Response**: Fungal antigens (mannans) trigger delayed-type hypersensitivity (Type IV) → inflammation, erythema, pruritus, scale.

### Pathophysiology of Malassezia Furfur
* **Lipophilic Yeast**: Thrives in sebum-rich areas (chest, back).
* **Dimorphic Shift**: Converts from yeast (saprophytic) to mycelial form (pathogenic) under hot/humid conditions.
* **Depigmentation Mechanism**: Produces **azelaic acid** → inhibits **tyrosinase** → ↓ melanin production → hypopigmented macules.

### Pathophysiology of Candida Albicans
* **Microenvironmental Triggers**: Moisture, friction, warmth (intertriginous zones) compromise stratum corneum barrier.
* **Immune Evasion**: Phenotypic switching from yeast to pseudohyphae facilitates tissue invasion.
* **Predisposing States**: ↑ glucose in sweat/secretions (DM), ↓ commensal bacteria (broad-spectrum Abx), localized immunosuppression (topical steroids).

## 3. Clinical Assessment 

### History for Superficial Fungal Infections
* **Primary Symptom**: Pruritus (often intense in pedis/cruris; variable in corporis).
* **Aggravating Factors**: Sweating, tight clothing, immunosuppression, poor hygiene.
* **Epidemiological Clues**: Contact with animals (cats/dogs), sharing towels/combs, barefoot walking in public pools.
* **Systemic Symptoms**: Generally -ve (absence differentiates from systemic mycoses).

### Physical Signs of Tinea Corporis (體癬) & Tinea Cruris (股癬)
* **Tinea Corporis (Ringworm)**: Annular (ring-shaped) erythematous plaques, central clearing, active advancing scaly border. 
* **Tinea Cruris (Jock Itch)**: Erythematous, well-demarcated plaques in inguinal folds; bilateral but asymmetric.
* **Key Discriminator in Cruris**: Strictly **SPARES the scrotum/penis** (unlike candidiasis).

### Physical Signs of Tinea Pedis (足癬)
* **Interdigital Type**: Maceration, fissuring, erythema between toes (most common in 4th/5th web spaces).
* **Moccasin Type**: Diffuse hyperkeratosis, erythema, fine scaling on soles and lateral borders.
* **Vesiculobullous Type**: Vesicles/bullae on medial instep.

### Physical Signs of Tinea Capitis (頭癬)
* **Non-inflammatory (Grey patch)**: Scaly patches of alopecia, hairs broken above scalp.
* **Black Dot**: Hairs broken flush with scalp surface, mimicking black dots.
* **Kerion (膿癬)**: Boggy, indurated, tender, erythematous plaque studded with pustules (severe hypersensitivity reaction).
* **Favus**: Yellow, cup-shaped crusts (scutula) around hair follicles → scarring alopecia.

### Physical Signs of Tinea Unguium / Onychomycosis (甲癬)
* **Distal Subungual**: Subungual hyperkeratosis, yellow-brown discoloration, onycholysis (nail plate separation).
* **Proximal White Subungual**: White patches near lunula (highly associated with HIV/immunosuppression).

### Physical Signs of Cutaneous Candidiasis
* **Intertrigo (念珠菌對擦疹)**: "Beefy-red" macerated plaques in skin folds (axillae, submammary, groin).
* **Key Feature**: **Satellite papules and pustules** beyond the main border.
* **Balanitis (龜頭炎)**: Erythema, papules, superficial erosions, white curd-like exudate on glans penis/prepuce; **INVOLVES the scrotum**.

### Physical Signs of Pityriasis Versicolor (汗斑)
* **Morphology**: Multiple, well-demarcated macules/patches (hypopigmented, hyperpigmented, or erythematous) on upper trunk/shoulders.
* **Besnier's Sign**: Subtle scale becomes visible upon gently scratching the lesion.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for Groin Eruptions
| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tinea Cruris** | Active scaly border, central clearing, **spares scrotum**. |
| **Candidal Intertrigo** | Beefy red, **satellite lesions**, **involves scrotum**. |
| **Erythrasma** | Uniformly brown/red, finely scaly, **coral-red fluorescence** on Wood's lamp. |
| **Inverse Psoriasis** | Deep red, smooth (non-scaly), fissured, +ve signs elsewhere (gluteal cleft, nails). |

### Differential Diagnoses for Annular (Ring) Lesions
| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tinea Corporis** | Active scaly border, +ve fungal scrapings. |
| **Nummular Eczema** | Coin-shaped, uniformly scaly/crusted, intense pruritus, no central clearing. |
| **Granuloma Annulare** | Smooth, firm, dermal papules forming rings; **no scale**. |
| **Pityriasis Rosea** | Herald patch → "Christmas tree" distribution of smaller scaly plaques (collarette of scale). |

### Differential Diagnoses for Hypopigmentation
| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pityriasis Versicolor** | Upper trunk, fine scale (Besnier's sign), +ve Wood's lamp (yellow/green). |
| **Vitiligo** | Complete depigmentation (chalk-white), completely smooth (no scale), acral/periorificial. |
| **Pityriasis Alba** | Ill-defined, mildly scaly hypopigmented patches on face/cheeks of atopic children. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations 

### Bedside Investigations
* **Wood's Lamp (Black light at 365 nm)**:
  * **Microsporum spp.**: Bright blue-green fluorescence (hair shafts in Tinea Capitis).
  * **Trichophyton spp.**: No fluorescence (usually).
  * **Malassezia furfur**: Yellow-green or coppery-orange fluorescence.
  * **Corynebacterium minutissimum (Erythrasma)**: Coral-red fluorescence.

### Laboratory Investigations
* **Skin / Nail Scrapings**: Scrape from the **active advancing margin** using a blunt scalpel. For nails, collect subungual debris.
* **Hair Plucking**: Pluck affected hairs (with roots) for Tinea Capitis.
* **KOH (Potassium Hydroxide) Mount**: 10-20% KOH dissolves keratin, allowing visualization of fungal elements.
* **Fungal Culture**: Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA) containing cycloheximide and chloramphenicol. Takes 2-6 weeks to grow.

### Diagnostic Criteria & Microscopic Findings
| Condition | Characteristic KOH Microscopy Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dermatophytosis (Tinea)** | Septate, branching hyphae ("train tracks"). |
| **Candidiasis** | Budding yeasts and pseudohyphae. |
| **Pityriasis Versicolor** | Short, stubby hyphae + clusters of spores (**"Spaghetti and Meatballs"** appearance). |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Acute Management of Kerion (膿癬) & Severe Inflammatory Tinea
* **Rationale**: Severe immune response can lead to permanent scarring alopecia.
* **Systemic Steroids**: Oral Prednisolone (0.5-1 mg/kg/day) for 1-2 weeks concurrently with systemic antifungals to rapidly ↓ inflammation.
* **Antibiotics**: Only if secondary bacterial infection is confirmed (do not empirically treat "pus" in kerion with Abx, as it is sterile allergic pus).
* **Analgesia**: Oral NSAIDs/Paracetamol for pain.

### Acute Management of Id Reaction (Dermatophytid)
* **Autoeczematization**: Sterile vesicular eruption (often on hands) secondary to severe primary fungal infection (often Tinea Pedis).
* **Treatment**: Treat the primary site. Apply potent topical corticosteroids to the Id reaction site.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Treatment Algorithm for Tinea Corporis / Cruris / Pedis
* **First-line (Mild/Localized)**: Topical Antifungals applied 1-2 cm beyond the lesion margin, continued 1-2 weeks *after* clinical clearing.
  * **Terbinafine 1% cream** (HA formulary) OR **Clotrimazole / Miconazole cream**.
* **Second-line (Extensive / Refractory / T. Rubrum syndrome)**: Oral Antifungals.
  * **Oral Terbinafine**: 250 mg daily for 2-4 weeks.
  * **Oral Itraconazole**: 100 mg BD for 1-2 weeks.

### Treatment Algorithm for Tinea Capitis (MUST be Systemic)
* **First-line for Microsporum**: **Oral Griseofulvin** (requires 6-12 weeks; take with fatty meal to ↑ absorption).
* **First-line for Trichophyton**: **Oral Terbinafine** (2-4 weeks).
* **Adjunct**: Antifungal shampoo (Ketoconazole 2% or Selenium Sulphide) twice weekly to ↓ spore shedding.

### Treatment Algorithm for Tinea Unguium (Onychomycosis)
* **Topical**: **Amorolfine 5% nail lacquer** 1-2x/week for 6-12 months (only if <50% distal nail involved, no matrix involvement).
* **Systemic (First-line)**: **Oral Terbinafine** 250mg daily (6 weeks for fingernails; 12 weeks for toenails).
* **Systemic (Alternative)**: **Oral Itraconazole** pulsed therapy (200mg BD for 1 week/month; 2 pulses for fingernails, 3 for toenails).

### Treatment Algorithm for Pityriasis Versicolor
* **First-line**: Topical shampoos used as body wash (leave on 10 mins before rinsing).
  * **Ketoconazole 2% shampoo** or **Selenium Sulphide 2.5% suspension**.
  * Use daily for 1-2 weeks.
* **Second-line (Extensive/Refractory)**: **Oral Itraconazole** 200mg daily for 7 days (requires sweating via exercise to deliver drug to stratum corneum). *Note: Oral terbinafine is INEFFECTIVE for Malassezia.*

### Treatment Algorithm for Cutaneous Candidiasis
* **First-line**: Topical azoles (**Clotrimazole, Miconazole**) or Polyenes (**Nystatin cream/ointment**).
* **Adjunct**: Weak topical steroid (e.g., Hydrocortisone 1%) for a few days to quickly ↓ severe inflammation. Keep intertriginous areas dry.
* **Refractory/Immunocompromised**: **Oral Fluconazole** 50-100mg daily for 1-2 weeks.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Onychomycosis Severity Index (OSI)
| Score Parameter | Criteria | Point Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Area of Involvement** | 1-10% to >25% | 1-3 |
| **Proximity to Matrix** | Distance from proximal fold | 1-5 |
| **Presence of Dermatophytoma/Spike** | Clinical presence | +10 |
* **Interpretation**: Mild (1-5), Moderate (6-15), Severe (16-35). Directs decision between topical vs. systemic therapy.

### Host Risk Stratification for Refractory Fungal Infections
* **High Risk for Failure/Recurrence**: 
  * Uncontrolled DM (↑ glucose promotes Candida/dermatophyte growth).
  * Immunocompromised (HIV, systemic corticosteroids, chemotherapy).
  * Peripheral Arterial Disease (↓ drug delivery to toenails).
  * Chronic venous insufficiency (venous eczema barrier disruption).

## 9. Multi-System Complications 

### Local & Systemic Complications
* **Secondary Bacterial Cellulitis**: Fissuring in Tinea Pedis provides a portal of entry for *Staphylococcus aureus* or *Streptococcus pyogenes* → lower limb cellulitis (Requires urgent IV Abx, e.g., Cloxacillin/Cefazolin in HA).
* **Cicatricial (Scarring) Alopecia**: Permanent hair loss secondary to Kerion or Favus (follicular destruction).
* **Majocchi's Granuloma**: Deep fungal folliculitis caused by treating misdiagnosed Tinea Corporis with high-potency topical steroids. Requires systemic antifungals.
* **Persistent Hypopigmentation**: In Pityriasis Versicolor, dyspigmentation can persist for months *after* the fungus is eradicated (reassure patient).

### Antifungal Drug Adverse Effects
* **Oral Terbinafine**: 
  * **Hepatotoxicity**: Must check LFTs at baseline and monitor. 
  * **Dysgeusia (Taste loss)**: Can persist for weeks after stopping.
* **Oral Griseofulvin**: 
  * **CYP450 Inducer**: ↓ efficacy of OCPs, Warfarin.
  * Teratogenic (Avoid pregnancy for 1 month post-treatment in women, 6 months in men).
* **Oral Azoles (Itraconazole/Fluconazole)**: 
  * **CYP450 Inhibitors**: ↑ levels of statins (risk of rhabdomyolysis), digoxin, warfarin.
  * **Heart Failure**: Itraconazole has negative inotropic effects (Contraindicated in HFrEF).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate candidal intertrigo from tinea cruris? Answer: Candidal intertrigo typically exhibits satellite pustules beyond the main border and commonly involves the scrotum; Tinea cruris strictly spares the scrotum.
* Trap: Treating a scaly, annular rash with empirical topical steroid/antifungal combination creams (e.g., "four-in-one" creams). Pearl: Potent steroids can alter the clinical appearance (Tinea Incognito), suppress local immunity causing deep invasion (Majocchi's granuloma), and delay proper diagnosis. Always use pure topical antifungals.
* Pitfall: Prescribing oral terbinafine for Pityriasis Versicolor. *Malassezia* species lack the squalene epoxidase enzyme targeted efficiently by terbinafine in systemic concentrations. Use topical ketoconazole or oral itraconazole instead.
* Trap: Re-treating Pityriasis Versicolor because the hypopigmented macules haven't disappeared after 2 weeks of antifungal shampoo. Pearl: The fungus is dead, but it takes months for melanin to repopulate the epidermis. Base cure on the absence of scale (negative Besnier's sign), not repigmentation.
* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of recurrent lower limb cellulitis in older patients? Answer: Untreated interdigital Tinea Pedis acting as a recurrent portal of entry for streptococci.
* Pitfall: Discharging a patient with a confirmed Kerion with only topical antifungals. Topical treatments do not penetrate the hair follicle deeply enough. Systemic antifungals (and often brief systemic steroids) are mandatory to prevent permanent scarring alopecia.
