# Coronary Artery Disease (冠狀動脈疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Coronary Artery Disease
* **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)**: Narrowing/blockage of coronary arteries (usually via **Atherosclerosis (動脈粥樣硬化)**) → Imbalance between myocardial O2 supply & demand → Ischemia.
* **Spectrum**: Ranges from **Chronic Coronary Syndrome (CCS)** (Stable Angina) to **Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)** (Unstable Angina, NSTEMI, STEMI).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence/Mortality**: 3rd leading cause of death in HK (after Cancers & Pneumonia).
* **Demographics**: ↑ incidence in South Asian populations in HK; males > females (pre-menopause), equalizes post-menopause.
* **Burden**: High admission rates to HA hospitals for **Myocardial Infarction (心肌梗塞)**.

### Modifiable Risk Factors for CAD
* **Hypertension (高血壓)**: Causes endothelial shear stress.
* **Diabetes Mellitus (糖尿病)**: ↑ advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) → severe, diffuse, multi-vessel disease.
* **Dyslipidemia (血脂異常)**: ↑ LDL-C, ↓ HDL-C.
* **Smoking (吸煙)**: ↑ oxidative stress, ↑ thrombogenicity.
* **Obesity / Sedentary Lifestyle**: Central adiposity worsens insulin resistance.

### Non-Modifiable Risk Factors for CAD
* **Age**: Men ≥ 45, Women ≥ 55.
* **Gender**: Male risk > Female (until menopause).
* **Family History**: Premature CAD in 1st-degree relative (Male < 55 yrs, Female < 65 yrs).
* **Genetics**: e.g., Familial Hypercholesterolemia.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Atherosclerosis
* **Endothelial Injury**: Toxins (smoking), HTN, or ↑ lipids disrupt endothelium → ↑ permeability.
* **Lipid Infiltration**: LDL enters intima → oxidized by macrophages.
* **Foam Cell Formation**: Macrophages ingest ox-LDL via scavenger receptors → become foam cells → form **Fatty Streak**.
* **Fibrous Cap Formation**: Smooth muscle cells (SMCs) migrate from media to intima → deposit extracellular matrix (collagen) → form fibroatheroma.

### Plaque Rupture & Thrombosis (ACS Mechanism)
* **Vulnerable Plaque**: Thin fibrous cap, large lipid core, ↑ inflammatory cells (macrophages secrete metalloproteinases degrading collagen).
* **Rupture Event**: Cap breaks → exposes highly thrombogenic tissue factor (TF) & lipid core to blood.
* **Thrombosis**: Platelet adhesion/aggregation + coagulation cascade activation → partial (UA/NSTEMI) or complete (STEMI) vessel occlusion.

### The Ischemic Cascade
* **Sequence of events during ischemia**:
  1. Biochemical alterations (↓ ATP, ↑ Lactate).
  2. Diastolic dysfunction (impaired relaxation, ↑ LVEDP).
  3. Systolic dysfunction (wall motion abnormalities).
  4. ECG changes (ST-T wave abnormalities).
  5. Clinical symptom: **Angina (心絞痛)**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History: Typical vs Atypical Angina
* **Typical Angina** requires all 3:
  1. Substernal chest discomfort (crushing, squeezing, heavy).
  2. Provoked by exertion or emotional stress.
  3. Relieved by rest or **Nitroglycerin (硝酸甘油)** < 5 mins.
* **Atypical Angina**: Meets 2 of 3 criteria.
* **Non-Anginal Pain**: Meets 1 or 0 criteria.

### History: Associated Symptoms & Angina Equivalents
* **Autonomic Symptoms**: Diaphoresis, nausea, vomiting, pallor.
* **Angina Equivalents** (common in elderly, diabetics, females): Dyspnea, extreme fatigue, presyncope, epigastric discomfort.
* **Radiation**: Left arm (ulnar aspect), neck, jaw, interscapular region.

### Physical Examination: Signs of Dyslipidemia
* **Corneal Arcus**: White/grey ring around iris (significant if < 50 yrs).
* **Xanthelasma**: Yellowish cholesterol deposits on eyelids.
* **Tendon Xanthomas**: Nodules on Achilles tendon or extensor tendons of hands (classic for Familial Hypercholesterolemia).

### Physical Examination: Cardiac & Vascular Signs
* **Pulse**: Tachycardia (sympathetic drive) or bradycardia (inferior MI affecting AV node).
* **Blood Pressure**: HTN or hypotension (cardiogenic shock).
* **Palpation**: Dyskinetic apical impulse (if prior anterior MI).
* **Auscultation**:
  * **S4 Gallop**: Stiff, ischemic LV (decreased compliance).
  * **S3 Gallop**: Ventricular failure/fluid overload.
  * **New Systolic Murmur**: Ischemic mitral regurgitation (papillary muscle dysfunction) or Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD).
* **Peripheral**: Carotid/femoral bruits (generalized atherosclerosis), absent pedal pulses.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Cardiac & Vascular Differentials for Chest Pain
| Differential Diagnosis | Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Aortic Dissection** | Tearing/ripping pain radiating to back, unequal BP in arms, widened mediastinum. |
| **Pericarditis** | Sharp, pleuritic pain, relieved by leaning forward, diffuse ST elevation + PR depression. |
| **Myocarditis** | Viral prodrome, global ECG changes, ↑ Trop without coronary stenosis. |

### Pulmonary Differentials for Chest Pain
| Differential Diagnosis | Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | Sudden dyspnea, pleuritic pain, hemoptysis, calf swelling/DVT, S1Q3T3 on ECG (rare but classic). |
| **Pneumothorax** | Sudden unilateral pleuritic pain, ↓ breath sounds, hyperresonant percussion. |

### Gastrointestinal & MSK Differentials for Chest Pain
| Differential Diagnosis | Discriminatory Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **GERD / Esophageal Spasm** | Burning pain, worse lying down, related to meals, acidic taste. |
| **Costochondritis** | Highly localized, reproducible by chest wall palpation. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for CAD
* **12-Lead ECG**: Essential within 10 mins of ED arrival for suspected ACS.
  * **STEMI**: ST elevation (≥1mm in ≥2 contiguous leads; for V2-V3: ≥2.5mm in men <40, ≥2mm in men ≥40, ≥1.5mm in women), new LBBB.
  * **NSTEMI/UA**: ST depression, T-wave inversion, or normal.
  * **Prior MI**: Pathological Q waves.

### Blood Tests for CAD
* **High-Sensitivity Troponin (hs-cTnI/T)**: Rises in 2-4 hrs, peaks at 24-48 hrs. Serial testing (0h/1h or 0h/2h algorithm) to detect Δ in NSTEMI. Normal in UA.
* **Lipid Profile (Fasting)**: Target driven by risk level. Total Chol, LDL-C, HDL-C, Triglycerides.
* **HbA1c & Fasting Glucose**: Screen for concomitant DM.
* **Renal Function (Urea/Cr)**: Pre-angiogram check (risk of contrast-induced nephropathy).
* **CBC**: Exclude severe anemia causing Type 2 MI (demand ischemia).

### Non-Invasive Functional Imaging (Stress Tests)
* **Treadmill Exercise Tolerance Test (ETT)**: Look for horizontal/downsloping ST depression ≥1mm. Low cost, but lower sensitivity/specificity.
* **Stress Echocardiography**: Dobutamine or exercise. Detects stress-induced regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA).
* **Myocardial Perfusion Scan (MPS)**: Thallium/Sestamibi with dipyridamole/adenosine. Detects reversible (ischemia) vs fixed (infarct) perfusion defects.

### Non-Invasive & Invasive Anatomical Imaging
* **CT Coronary Angiography (CTCA)**: High negative predictive value. Excellent for ruling out CAD in low-intermediate risk patients.
* **Coronary Angiography (Cardiac Catheterization)**: **Gold Standard**. Invasive. Defines exact anatomy, severity of stenosis, allows concurrent Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI).

### Diagnostic Criteria for Myocardial Infarction (4th Universal Definition)
* Acute myocardial injury (↑ Troponin with ≥1 value > 99th percentile URL) + ≥1 of the following:
  1. Symptoms of myocardial ischemia.
  2. New ischemic ECG changes.
  3. Development of pathological Q waves.
  4. Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new RWMA.
  5. Identification of coronary thrombus by angiography.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of STEMI (HA Protocol)
* **Immediate Resuscitation (MONA-B)**:
  * **Oxygen**: ONLY if SpO2 < 90%.
  * **Nitrates**: GTN sublingual (Avoid if inferior MI + RV involvement or recent PDE5 inhibitor use).
  * **Aspirin (阿士匹靈)**: 300mg PO stat.
  * **P2Y12 Inhibitor**: Ticagrelor 180mg OR Prasugrel 60mg OR Clopidogrel 600mg stat.
* **Revascularization Strategy**:
  * **Primary PCI (PPCI)**: Gold standard. Target Door-to-Balloon time < 90 mins.
  * **Thrombolysis**: Tenecteplase/Alteplase. ONLY if PPCI cannot be done within 120 mins. (Rarely needed in HK due to dense hospital network).
* **Anticoagulation**: Heparin (UFH or LMWH) peri-procedure.

### Acute Management of NSTEMI / Unstable Angina
* **Initial Rx**: Same MONA-B as STEMI.
* **Risk Stratification**: Use GRACE score.
* **Invasive Strategy (Angiography ± PCI)**:
  * **Immediate (<2h)**: Hemodynamic instability, refractory pain, life-threatening arrhythmias, mechanical complications.
  * **Early (<24h)**: High risk (GRACE > 140, dynamic ST changes, ↑ Troponin).
* **Conservative Strategy**: Low risk (GRACE < 109, normal ECG/Trop). Proceed to non-invasive stress testing.
* **Anticoagulation**: Fondaparinux (preferred in HA for non-invasive) or Enoxaparin.

### Acute Pulmonary Edema / Cardiogenic Shock Complicating ACS
* **Pulmonary Edema**: Sit up, High-flow O2, IV Furosemide, IV Nitrates (if BP permits), CPAP/BiPAP.
* **Cardiogenic Shock**: Vasopressors (Norepinephrine), Inotropes (Dobutamine). Urgent Echocardiography to rule out mechanical complications. Intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) or ECMO consideration.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Coronary Syndrome: Anti-Anginal Symptom Control
* **First-Line**:
  * **Beta-Blockers (BB)**: Bisoprolol, Metoprolol. ↓ HR, ↓ contractility → ↓ O2 demand. (Target HR 55-60 bpm).
  * **Calcium Channel Blockers (CCB)**: Diltiazem/Verapamil (if BB contraindicated) OR Amlodipine (add-on to BB). Promotes vasodilation.
* **Second-Line (Add-on)**:
  * **Long-acting Nitrates**: Isosorbide Mononitrate (ISMN). Must have 10-14 hr nitrate-free interval to prevent tolerance.
  * **Ivabradine**: If HR > 70 bpm in sinus rhythm despite max BB.
  * **Trimetazidine**: Metabolic modulator (improves myocardial glucose utilization).

### Secondary Prevention Pharmacology (The "A-B-A-S" Protocol)
* **A - Antiplatelets**: Aspirin 80mg daily indefinitely. (Dual Antiplatelet Therapy [DAPT] for 6-12 months post-ACS or post-stent).
* **B - Beta-Blockers**: Proven mortality benefit post-MI, especially if reduced LVEF.
* **A - ACE Inhibitors / ARBs**: Ramipril/Losartan. Mandatory if anterior MI, LVEF ≤ 40%, HTN, or DM. Prevents adverse cardiac remodeling.
* **S - Statins**: High-intensity (Atorvastatin 40-80mg or Rosuvastatin 20-40mg). Target LDL-C < 1.4 mmol/L and ≥50% reduction from baseline.

### Revascularization: CABG vs PCI Indications
| Clinical Scenario | Revascularization Choice |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1- or 2-vessel disease (non-proximal LAD)** | **PCI** (Percutaneous Coronary Intervention) |
| **Left Main Stem disease (>50%)** | **CABG** (Coronary Artery Bypass Graft) |
| **3-vessel disease + Diabetes Mellitus** | **CABG** (Survival benefit over PCI) |
| **High SYNTAX Score (>22)** | **CABG** |

### Lifestyle Modifications & Cardiac Rehabilitation
* **Smoking Cessation**: Absolute necessity.
* **Diet**: Mediterranean diet, ↓ saturated fats, ↓ sodium.
* **Exercise**: 150 mins/week moderate aerobic exercise. Enrol in HA Cardiac Rehabilitation Program (Phases I-IV).
* **Vaccination**: Annual influenza and pneumococcal vaccines.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### TIMI Score for NSTEMI/UA
* **Purpose**: Estimates 14-day risk of mortality, new/recurrent MI, or severe recurrent ischemia.
* **AMERICA (1 point each)**:
  * **A**ge ≥ 65
  * **M**arkers (↑ Cardiac enzymes)
  * **E**CG (ST deviation ≥0.5mm)
  * **R**isk factors (≥3 CAD risk factors)
  * **I**schemia (≥2 angina events in 24 hrs)
  * **C**AD (Known stenosis ≥50%)
  * **A**spirin use in past 7 days
* **Interpretation**: 0-2 (Low risk), 3-4 (Intermediate), 5-7 (High risk).

### GRACE Risk Score
* **Purpose**: More accurate than TIMI. Predicts 6-month mortality post-ACS.
* **Variables**: Age, HR, Systolic BP, Initial Creatinine, Killip Class at presentation, Cardiac arrest at admission, ST-segment deviation, ↑ Cardiac markers.
* **Interpretation**: >140 indicates high risk → mandates early invasive strategy (<24 hours).

### SYNTAX Score
* **Purpose**: Angiographic grading tool to determine complexity of coronary anatomy.
* **Application**: Decides between PCI vs CABG in Left Main or 3-vessel disease.
* **Thresholds**: ≤22 (PCI or CABG equivalent), ≥23 (CABG preferred).

### Killip Classification
* **Purpose**: Clinical assessment of heart failure in acute MI context; predicts 30-day mortality.
* **Class I**: No signs of HF (Mortality ~6%).
* **Class II**: S3 gallop, crackles in lower half of lungs, elevated JVP (Mortality ~17%).
* **Class III**: Acute pulmonary edema (Mortality ~38%).
* **Class IV**: Cardiogenic shock (Mortality ~81%).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Arrhythmic Complications of Post-MI
* **Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) / VT**: Major cause of pre-hospital death.
* **Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動)**: Often due to atrial ischemia or HF.
* **Bradyarrhythmias/Heart Blocks**: Common in Inferior MI (RCA supplies AV node in 90% of population).

### Mechanical Complications (Days 3-14 Post-MI)
* **Papillary Muscle Rupture**: Causes acute severe Mitral Regurgitation (new holosystolic murmur at apex). Common in inferior MI (posteromedial papillary muscle has single blood supply).
* **Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)**: Rupture of septum. Pansystolic murmur at left lower sternal border + harsh thrill.
* **Ventricular Free Wall Rupture**: Leads to sudden cardiac tamponade and Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA) arrest.

### Inflammatory Complications
* **Post-MI Pericarditis**: Occurs within days 1-3. Fibrinous pericarditis over necrotic myocardium.
* **Dressler Syndrome**: Autoimmune pericarditis occurring 2-8 weeks post-MI. Rx: High-dose Aspirin + Colchicine (Avoid NSAIDs/Steroids as they impair scar healing).

### Complications of CAD Medications
* **Statins**: Myopathy, myalgia, ↑ ALT/AST (drug-induced liver injury).
* **ACE Inhibitors**: Dry cough (bradykinin accumulation), hyperkalemia, angioedema.
* **Ticagrelor**: Dyspnea (adenosine accumulation), bleeding.
* **Aspirin**: Peptic ulcer disease, GI bleeding.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common artery occluded in an acute myocardial infarction? Answer: The Left Anterior Descending (LAD) artery, causing an anterior STEMI.
* Trap: Giving sublingual GTN for chest pain without checking BP or ECG. Pearl: Never give Nitrates in an Inferior MI with Right Ventricular involvement (V4R ST elevation), as these patients are highly preload-dependent and it will cause profound hypotension.
* Pitfall: Stopping Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) prematurely for elective non-cardiac surgery. Elective surgeries must be delayed (usually at least 6 months post-DES) to prevent catastrophic in-stent thrombosis.
* Viva Question: Why do we prefer Ticagrelor or Prasugrel over Clopidogrel in acute STEMI? Answer: They have a more rapid onset of action, are more potent, and have more predictable inhibition of the P2Y12 receptor compared to Clopidogrel (which is a prodrug requiring hepatic activation).
* Trap: Assuming a normal initial troponin rules out an acute coronary syndrome. Pearl: High-sensitivity Troponin takes 2-4 hours to rise above the 99th percentile URL; you must repeat it at 1 or 2 hours according to the local HA algorithm.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing a new STEMI in a patient with an existing Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB) using standard criteria. You must apply the Sgarbossa Criteria (or modified Smith-Sgarbossa Criteria) to identify concordant ST elevation/depression.
