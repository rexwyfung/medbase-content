# Myasthenia Gravis (重症肌無力)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Myasthenia Gravis
* **Autoimmune Neuromuscular Junction (NMJ) Disorder**: Autoantibody-mediated disruption of neuromuscular transmission.
* **Hallmark**: Fluctuating, fatigable skeletal muscle weakness that worsens with exertion and improves with rest.

### Etiologies & Autoantibody Profiles
* **Anti-AChR (Acetylcholine Receptor) Antibodies**: ~85% of generalized MG (全身性重症肌無力); ~50% of ocular MG.
* **Anti-MuSK (Muscle-Specific Kinase) Antibodies**: ~5-10% of generalized MG; often bulbar/respiratory predominant; females > males.
* **Anti-LRP4 Antibodies**: ~1-3% of MG cases.
* **Seronegative MG**: ~10% of cases (undetectable AChR/MuSK/LRP4), though likely unrecognized Abs.
* **Thymic Pathology**: ~70% have **Thymic Hyperplasia** (胸腺增生); 10-15% have **Thymoma** (胸腺瘤).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Bimodal Age Distribution**: 
  * Early-onset: 20-30s (Females > Males).
  * Late-onset: >60s (Males > Females).
* **Associations**: Strong link with other autoimmune diseases (e.g., Autoimmune Thyroid Disease, SLE, Rheumatoid Arthritis).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Mechanism of Anti-AChR Mediated Myasthenia
* **IgG1/IgG3 Abs** bind postsynaptic **Nicotinic AChRs** at NMJ.
* **Complement Activation**: Membrane attack complex (MAC) destroys postsynaptic membrane architecture → flattened folds.
* **Accelerated Internalization**: Cross-linking of AChRs → rapid endocytosis & degradation.
* **Functional Blockade**: Direct competitive antagonism at ACh-binding sites.
* **End Result**: ↓ Available AChRs → decreased amplitude of **End-Plate Potentials (EPPs)** → failure to reach threshold for muscle action potential → fatigable weakness.

### Mechanism of Anti-MuSK Mediated Myasthenia
* **IgG4 Abs** (does NOT activate complement).
* Blocks **LRP4-MuSK interaction** → prevents agrin-induced clustering of AChRs on postsynaptic membrane.
* Leads to progressive dispersion of AChRs away from the synapse.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Presenting Illness
* **Fatigability**: Weakness worsens towards evening or after repetitive use.
* **Ocular Symptoms** (typically initial presentation): **Diplopia** (復視), **Ptosis** (眼瞼下垂).
* **Bulbar Symptoms**: **Dysphagia** (吞嚥困難), **Dysarthria** (構音障礙) (nasal speech), difficulty chewing (worsens during a meal).
* **Proximal Limb Weakness**: Difficulty climbing stairs, raising arms (brushing hair).
* **Respiratory Symptoms**: Exertional dyspnea, orthopnea (diaphragmatic weakness).
* **Spared Modalities**: NO sensory loss, NO bowel/bladder dysfunction, NO cognitive impairment.

### Ocular & Facial Physical Examination
* **Asymmetrical Ptosis**: Fluctuating; worsens with prolonged upward gaze (sustained for 1-2 mins).
* **Cogan's Lid Twitch**: Rapid overshoot of upper eyelid when eyes move from downgaze to primary position.
* **Peek Sign**: Orbicularis oculi weakness → sclera becomes visible after prolonged forceful eye closure.
* **Complex Ophthalmoplegia**: Asymmetric extraocular muscle weakness that does not conform to single cranial nerve (CN III, IV, VI) lesions.
* **Myasthenic Snarl**: Weak smile due to orbicularis oris weakness (looks like a snarl).

### Bulbar, Limb & Neurological Physical Examination
* **Bulbar Sign**: Palatal weakness causing nasal regurgitation or nasal twang.
* **Limb Strength**: Proximal > distal weakness; fatigability demonstrated by repetitive deep knee bends or arm abduction (e.g., 30+ reps).
* **Neck Weakness**: Flexors usually weaker than extensors ("dropped head" sign in severe cases).
* **Reflexes & Sensation**: Deep tendon reflexes (DTRs) are PRESERVED; sensory exam is completely NORMAL.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating NMJ and Neuromuscular Disorders
| Condition | Key Discriminatory Clinical & Pathophysiological Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Myasthenia Gravis (MG)** | Worsens with exertion. Proximal weakness. Ocular/bulbar onset. **Normal DTRs**. AChR/MuSK +ve. |
| **Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS)** | **Improves** with exertion. Proximal weakness. Autonomic symptoms (dry mouth, impotence). **Absent/↓ DTRs**. Presynaptic VGCC Abs +ve. Associated with SCLC. |
| **Botulism (肉毒桿菌中毒)** | **Descending** flaccid paralysis. Fixed dilated pupils. Bulbar palsies. Autonomic symptoms. Toxin inhibits presynaptic ACh release. |
| **ALS (肌萎縮性脊髓側索硬化症)** | Upper AND Lower Motor Neuron signs (hyperreflexia + fasciculations). No sensory loss. Progressive, NOT fluctuating. |
| **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)** | **Ascending** flaccid paralysis. Areflexia. Sensory symptoms (distal paresthesia). CSF albuminocytologic dissociation. |
| **Oculopharyngeal Muscular Dystrophy** | Slowly progressive (not rapidly fluctuating). Autosomal dominant. Older age onset. Ptosis and dysphagia, but no response to cholinesterase inhibitors. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Provocative Tests
* **Ice Pack Test**: Apply ice pack to ptotic eye for 2 mins → cooling inhibits acetylcholinesterase → resolution of ptosis (>2 mm improvement is +ve). Highly sensitive for ocular MG.
* **Edrophonium (Tensilon) Test**: IV short-acting AChE inhibitor. Rarely used locally now due to risk of bradycardia/cardiac arrest (requires bedside atropine & ECG monitoring).

### Serological Investigations (Autoantibodies)
* **Anti-AChR Antibody**: 1st-line test. Highly specific.
* **Anti-MuSK Antibody**: Check if AChR is -ve.
* **Anti-LRP4 Antibody**: Check if AChR and MuSK are both -ve (Double seronegative).
* **Concurrent Autoimmune Screen**: TFTs (for Grave's disease), ANA, ENA, Rheumatoid Factor, Anti-dsDNA (due to autoimmune overlap).

### Neurophysiological Studies (EMG)
* **Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS)**: Supramaximal stimulation at 2-3 Hz. 
  * **+ve finding**: >10% **decremental response** in CMAP (Compound Muscle Action Potential) amplitude between 1st and 4th/5th stimuli.
* **Single-Fiber EMG (SFEMG)**: Most sensitive test (~95%).
  * **+ve finding**: Increased **"jitter"** (variability in time interval between action potentials of adjacent muscle fibers in the same motor unit) or **"blocking"**.

### Radiological Imaging
* **CT/MRI Thorax with Contrast**: MANDATORY for all newly diagnosed MG patients to screen for **Thymoma** or Thymic Hyperplasia.
* **MRI Brain**: If purely ocular presentation to rule out structural brainstem/cranial nerve pathology.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Myasthenic Crisis (重症肌無力危機) Definitions & Triggers
* **Definition**: Life-threatening exacerbation requiring mechanical ventilation (respiratory muscle or severe bulbar weakness causing airway compromise).
* **Triggers**: Infections (most common), surgery, tapering immunosuppression, pregnancy, or interacting drugs (e.g., aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, macrolides, beta-blockers, magnesium).

### Acute Stabilization & Resuscitation Protocol
* **ICU Admission**: Mandatory for close respiratory monitoring.
* **Airway/Breathing**: 
  * Monitor **FVC** (Forced Vital Capacity) and **NIF** (Negative Inspiratory Force) Q4H.
  * **Intubation Criteria**: FVC < 15-20 mL/kg, NIF < -20 cmH2O, hypercapnia (late sign), or severe aspiration risk.
* **Temporarily HOLD Pyridostigmine**: Reduces airway secretions and avoids confusing myasthenic crisis with cholinergic crisis.

### Rapid-Acting Immunomodulation
* **Plasma Exchange (PLEX/Plasmapheresis)**:
  * Mechanism: Directly removes pathogenic autoantibodies.
  * Regimen: 5 exchanges over 7-14 days. Often preferred for impending crisis as onset is slightly faster than IVIG.
* **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**:
  * Regimen: 2g/kg divided over 2-5 days.
  * Effective, easier to administer locally (HA hospitals) if central line/PLEX facilities are unavailable.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Step 1: Symptomatic Therapy
* **Pyridostigmine (Mestinon)**: 
  * Mechanism: Reversible Acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitor; ↑ ACh in NMJ.
  * Dosing: Typically 30-60mg PO Q4-6H. Peak effect in 2 hrs.
  * Used as monotherapy ONLY in mild/purely ocular MG. Does not alter disease progression.
  * Add **Hyoscine** or **Propantheline** if severe muscarinic side effects (diarrhea, cramps, hypersalivation).

### Step 2: First-Line Immunosuppression (Disease-Modifying)
* **Corticosteroids (Prednisolone)**: 
  * Started low, titrated up (e.g., 20mg/day ↑ by 5mg every 3-5 days to 1mg/kg/day).
  * Rapid high-dose initiation can trigger paradoxical transient worsening (requires hospital monitoring).
* **Steroid-Sparing Agents**: Started concurrently due to long latency (months) of steroid effect.
  * **Azathioprine**: 1st-line local HA choice. Check TPMT levels before starting. Takes 6-12 months for full effect.
  * **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)**: 2nd-line local choice. Faster onset than Azathioprine (2-3 months).

### Step 3: Surgical Intervention (Thymectomy)
* **Indications**: 
  * ALL patients with **Thymoma** (regardless of Ab status or severity).
  * Non-thymomatous generalized MG with **Anti-AChR +ve** (especially age 18-50) to improve probability of remission and reduce steroid requirements.
* **Contraindications**: Pure ocular MG, Anti-MuSK +ve MG (thymectomy shows no benefit).

### Step 4: Refractory & Targeted Biologics
| Drug Class | Agent | Indication & Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **B-Cell Depletion** | **Rituximab** | Anti-CD20. Highly effective for **Anti-MuSK +ve** MG. |
| **Complement Inhibitor** | **Eculizumab** | Anti-C5 monoclonal Ab. For severe, refractory AChR +ve generalized MG. |
| **FcRn Inhibitor** | **Efgartigimod** | Blocks neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) → accelerates IgG breakdown. Fast acting. |

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America (MGFA) Clinical Classification
| Class | Clinical Description of Severity |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Class I** | Any ocular muscle weakness; strictly NO other muscle weakness. |
| **Class II** | Mild weakness affecting muscles other than ocular. (IIa = limb/axial; IIb = bulbar/respiratory). |
| **Class III** | Moderate weakness affecting muscles other than ocular. (IIIa = limb/axial; IIIb = bulbar/respiratory). |
| **Class IV** | Severe weakness affecting muscles other than ocular. (IVa = limb/axial; IVb = bulbar/respiratory). |
| **Class V** | Intubation with or without mechanical ventilation (Myasthenic Crisis), except during routine postoperative care. |

### Prognosis
* Spontaneous remission is rare.
* Treated correctly, life expectancy is normal. 
* Morbidity mainly driven by prolonged immunosuppression and steroid side effects.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Specific Complications
* **Respiratory Failure**: Hypoventilation, hypercapnia.
* **Aspiration Pneumonia**: Due to severe bulbar palsy.

### Pharmacological Adverse Effects
* **Cholinergic Crisis (SLUDGE syndrome)**: Due to Pyridostigmine overdose.
  * **S**alivation, **L**acrimation, **U**rination, **D**iaphoresis/Diarrhea, **G**I cramping, **E**mesis.
  * Distinguish from Myasthenic crisis via history, pupil size (miosis in cholinergic), and muscle fasciculations.
* **Long-term Corticosteroid Effects**: Osteoporosis, iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, opportunistic infections, cataracts.
* **Azathioprine Toxicity**: Bone marrow suppression (leukopenia), hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a myasthenic crisis from a cholinergic crisis clinically? Answer: Both present with severe weakness/respiratory failure. Cholinergic crisis presents with prominent muscarinic signs (miosis, hypersalivation, diarrhea, bradycardia) and muscle fasciculations, whereas myasthenic crisis lacks these and has normal/dilated pupils.
* Trap: Assuming normal reflexes rule out neuromuscular junction disorders. Pearl: DTRs are completely preserved in Myasthenia Gravis, which helps differentiate it from Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (absent/reduced reflexes).
* Pitfall: Starting high-dose prednisolone rapidly in a newly diagnosed MG patient without admission. This can cause a transient, severe exacerbation of weakness precipitating respiratory failure within the first 1-2 weeks.
* Viva Question: What is the most sensitive diagnostic test for Myasthenia Gravis? Answer: Single-fiber EMG (SFEMG) demonstrating increased jitter or blocking, though it is less specific than AChR antibody testing.
* Pitfall: Performing a thymectomy for a patient with Anti-MuSK positive Myasthenia Gravis. The thymus is typically radiologically and histologically normal in MuSK+ disease, and surgery offers no clinical benefit.
* Trap: Using aminoglycoside antibiotics for pneumonia in an MG patient. Pearl: Aminoglycosides (gentamicin), macrolides, and fluoroquinolones impair neuromuscular transmission and can trigger a life-threatening myasthenic crisis.
