# Oesophageal Disorders (食道疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition and Epidemiology of GERD (胃食道反流病)
* **Definition**: Troublesome symptoms or mucosal damage resulting from retrograde flow of gastric contents into the esophagus
* **Epidemiology**: ↑ prevalence in Hong Kong (approx. 12% in local population), linked to ↑ obesity and Westernized diet
* **Phenotypes**: Erosive Esophagitis (EE) vs Non-Erosive Reflux Disease (NERD) vs Barrett's Oesophagus

### Definition and Etiology of Achalasia (賁門失弛緩症)
* **Definition**: Primary esophageal motility disorder characterized by failure of lower esophageal sphincter (LES) to relax and absent esophageal peristalsis
* **Etiology**: Idiopathic (most common), Chagas disease (Trypanosoma cruzi - rare in HK), Pseudoachalasia (malignancy at gastroesophageal junction)

### Definition of Eosinophilic Oesophagitis (EoE) (嗜酸性食道炎)
* **Definition**: Chronic, immune-mediated, antigen-driven esophageal disease characterized clinically by esophageal dysfunction and histologically by eosinophil-predominant inflammation

### Definition of Diffuse Oesophageal Spasm (DES) (瀰漫性食道痙攣)
* **Definition**: Motility disorder with uncoordinated, premature contractions of the esophagus with preserved LES relaxation

### Definition of Oesophageal Diverticula (食道憩室)
* **Definition**: Outpouchings of the esophageal wall
* **Zenker's Diverticulum**: Pulsion diverticulum located above the upper esophageal sphincter (Killian's triangle)
* **Traction Diverticulum**: Mid-esophageal, due to external mediastinal inflammation (e.g., Tuberculosis in HK)

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of GERD
* **Transient LES Relaxations (TLESRs)**: Vago-vagal reflex without swallowing, main mechanism for NERD
* **Mechanical Incompetence**: Hypotensive LES (<10 mmHg), exacerbated by Hiatal Hernia (sliding type disrupts crural diaphragm pinch)
* **Impaired Clearance**: ↓ saliva production, impaired peristalsis → prolonged acid/bile mucosal contact
* **Gastric Factors**: Delayed gastric emptying (gastroparesis), ↑ intragastric pressure (obesity, pregnancy)

### Molecular Mechanisms of Barrett's Oesophagus (巴雷特食道)
* **Metaplasia**: Chronic acid + bile salt exposure → reprogramming of stem cells
* **Histological Shift**: Normal stratified squamous epithelium → specialized intestinal columnar epithelium (with goblet cells)
* **Oncogenesis**: Metaplasia → Low-Grade Dysplasia (LGD) → High-Grade Dysplasia (HGD) → Adenocarcinoma (driven by p53 and p16 mutations)

### Neuromuscular Pathophysiology of Achalasia
* **Neuronal Degeneration**: Selective loss of postganglionic inhibitory neurons (nitric oxide and VIP) in the myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus
* **Imbalance**: Unopposed excitatory (cholinergic) stimulation → rigid, hypertonic LES + aperistalsis

### Pathophysiology of Eosinophilic Oesophagitis (EoE)
* **Allergic Cascade**: Food/aero-allergens trigger Th2 response → IL-5, IL-13 release → massive eosinophil recruitment via Eotaxin-3
* **Remodeling**: Eosinophil degranulation (Major Basic Protein) → subepithelial fibrosis, stricture formation, mucosal rings

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Typical and Atypical History in GERD
* **Typical Symptoms**: Heartburn (pyrosis) retrosternally radiating upwards, acid regurgitation, water brash (hypersalivation)
* **Atypical/Extra-esophageal (Laryngopharyngeal Reflux)**: Chronic cough (esp. nocturnal), asthma exacerbations, hoarseness, globus sensation, dental erosions
* **Postural Exacerbation**: Worse lying flat (decubitus), bending over, post-prandial (large, fatty meals)

### Alarm Features (Red Flags) for Upper GI Malignancy
* **Symptoms**: Progressive dysphagia, odynophagia (pain on swallowing), unintentional weight loss, recurrent vomiting, palpable epigastric mass
* **Anemia Signs**: Fatigue, melena, hematemesis

### Discriminatory History in Dysphagia (吞嚥困難)
* **Oropharyngeal (Transfer) Dysphagia**: Coughing/choking upon swallowing, nasal regurgitation, drooling
* **Esophageal Structural (Mechanical)**: Progressive dysphagia (solids → liquids over time)
* **Esophageal Motility**: Paradoxical or intermittent dysphagia (solids AND liquids simultaneously from onset)

### History in Specific Motility and Structural Disorders
* **Achalasia**: Long-standing progressive dysphagia (solids + liquids), bland regurgitation of *undigested* food (no acid taste), nocturnal cough/aspiration
* **DES/Nutcracker Esophagus**: Intermittent dysphagia + severe retrosternal chest pain (mimics angina, often relieved by GTN)
* **Zenker's Diverticulum**: Halitosis (foul breath), regurgitation of days-old food, gurgling in neck, dysphagia
* **EoE**: Solid food dysphagia, acute food impaction episodes, strong personal/family Hx of atopy (asthma, eczema, rhinitis)

### Physical Examination Findings
* **General**: Usually unremarkable in early disease. Look for cachexia/wasting (malignancy, severe stricture).
* **Skin/Hands**: Scleroderma stigmata (CREST syndrome) → secondary motility disorder (absent peristalsis + hypotensive LES).
* **Neck**: Palpable, fluctuant mass in left neck (Zenker's diverticulum). Cervical lymphadenopathy (Virchow's node - malignancy).
* **Respiratory**: Wheezing (atopy/EoE, reflux-induced asthma), basal crepitations (aspiration pneumonia).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnosis of Esophageal Symptoms
| Diagnosis Category | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **GERD** | Heartburn, acid taste, ↑ lying down, normal motility on manometry |
| **Achalasia** | Solids + liquids dysphagia, regurgitation of undigested food, weight loss |
| **Eosinophilic Esophagitis** | Atopy history, food impaction, young male predominance |
| **Esophageal Spasm (DES)** | Angina-like chest pain, intermittent symptoms, triggered by hot/cold liquids |
| **Scleroderma Esophagus** | CREST stigmata, severe severe GERD (hypotensive LES), smooth muscle atrophy |
| **Esophageal Carcinoma** | Older age, rapid weight loss, solids → liquids dysphagia, anemia |
| **Cardiac (ACS)** | Exertional chest pain, radiation to jaw/arm, ECG changes (MUST EXCLUDE) |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Indications for Upper Endoscopy (OGD / EGD)
* **Immediate**: Alarm symptoms present
* **Screening**: Men >50 with >5 years chronic GERD symptoms + central obesity (evaluate for Barrett's)
* **Refractory**: Failure to respond to 4-8 weeks of standard twice-daily PPI therapy

### Structural Imaging (Barium Swallow / Fluoroscopy)
* **Achalasia**: Dilated esophagus, absent peristalsis, smooth tapering at LES ("Bird's beak" appearance)
* **Diffuse Esophageal Spasm**: Simultaneous, multi-peaked contractions ("Corkscrew esophagus" or "Rosary bead" appearance)
* **Zenker's Diverticulum**: Barium collection in posterior midline pouch above cricopharyngeus

### Functional Testing (Manometry & pH Monitoring)
* **High-Resolution Manometry (HRM)**: Gold standard for motility.
  * **Achalasia**: Incomplete LES relaxation (Integrated Relaxation Pressure [IRP] >15 mmHg) + 100% failed peristalsis (aperistalsis).
  * **DES**: Premature contractions (Distal Latency <4.5s) in >20% swallows.
* **24-hour Ambulatory pH-Impedance Monitoring**:
  * **Indication**: Confirm GERD diagnosis if OGD is normal (NERD) AND patient is considered for anti-reflux surgery.
  * **Method**: Done OFF PPI therapy. Positive if DeMeester score >14.7 or acid exposure time (AET) >6%.

### Endoscopic and Biopsy Diagnostic Criteria
* **Eosinophilic Oesophagitis**: OGD shows trachealization (feline rings), linear furrows, white exudates. **Biopsy:** ≥15 eosinophils per High Power Field (HPF) in mid/proximal and distal esophagus.
* **Barrett's Oesophagus**: OGD shows salmon-pink mucosa extending >1cm above GE junction. **Biopsy:** Intestinal metaplasia (goblet cells on Alcian blue/PAS stain).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Esophageal Food Impaction Management
* **Pharmacological**: Glucagon 1mg IV (relaxes smooth muscle, limited efficacy but trialed while awaiting OGD). Avoid effervescent agents/meat tenderizers (risk of perforation).
* **Endoscopic**: Emergent therapeutic OGD using push technique (gently advancing bolus into stomach) or pull technique (retrieval net/snare).

### Acute Exacerbation of GERD/Esophagitis
* **Initial Stabilization**: Immediate dose escalation to high-dose IV or PO PPI (e.g., Pantoprazole 40mg IV).
* **Analgesia**: Mucosal protectants (Alginates/Gaviscon liquid PO).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### GERD Step-Up Management Algorithm
| Step | Intervention | Target Patient Population |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Lifestyle** | Weight loss (highest yield), head of bed elevation, stop smoking/EtOH, avoid late meals (within 3h of bed) | All GERD patients |
| **2. Empirical Tx** | Standard dose PPI daily for 8 weeks (e.g., Omeprazole 20mg/Pantoprazole 40mg before breakfast) | Typical GERD without alarm features |
| **3. Refractory Tx** | Optimize compliance → Twice daily PPI → Add H2RA at night (if nocturnal acid breakthrough) | Partial responders to Step 2 |
| **4. Neuromodulation** | TCAs (Amitriptyline) or SSRIs | Functional heartburn (normal acid exposure) |
| **5. Surgical Tx** | Laparoscopic Nissen Fundoplication or Magnetic Sphincter Augmentation (LINX) | Medically refractory, severe regurgitation, patient refuses long-term PPI |

### Achalasia Treatment Algorithm (Surgical & Endoscopic)
* **Goal**: Relieve LES mechanical obstruction (no treatment restores peristalsis).
* **High Surgical Risk Patients**:
  * **Botulinum Toxin Injection**: Endoscopic injection into LES. Efficacy wanes after 6-12 months, requires repeats. Fibrosis makes future surgery difficult.
  * **Medical Tx**: Calcium Channel Blockers (Nifedipine) or Long-acting Nitrates PO/SL before meals (poor efficacy, rarely used).
* **Low/Standard Surgical Risk Patients**:
  * **Pneumatic Dilatation (PD)**: Forceful balloon dilation to tear LES muscle fibers. ~3% perforation risk.
  * **Peroral Endoscopic Myotomy (POEM)**: Submucosal tunneling and division of circular muscle fibers. Excellent efficacy, but high post-op GERD rates.
  * **Laparoscopic Heller Myotomy (LHM)**: Surgical division of LES + partial fundoplication (Dor/Toupet) to prevent iatrogenic GERD.

### Eosinophilic Oesophagitis Step-Up Therapy
* **First-line Pharmacology**: High-dose PPI therapy (twice daily) - ~50% respond (PPI-responsive esophageal eosinophilia).
* **Topical Steroids**: Swallowed (not inhaled) Fluticasone propionate (MDI) or viscous Budesonide slurry. Ensure patient rinses mouth to prevent oral thrush.
* **Dietary Therapy**: Six-Food Elimination Diet (SFED: milk, wheat, egg, soy, nuts, seafood) → gradual reintroduction to identify trigger.
* **Endoscopic**: Serial dilatations for established fibrotic strictures (high risk of mucosal tearing).

### Barrett's Oesophagus Chronic Management Algorithm
* **Base Therapy**: Lifelong high-dose PPI therapy (suppresses acid, reduces progression risk).
* **Dysplasia Management**:
  * **No Dysplasia**: Endoscopic surveillance.
  * **Low-Grade Dysplasia (LGD)**: Endoscopic Eradication Therapy (EET) via Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA) OR intensive surveillance every 6 months.
  * **High-Grade Dysplasia (HGD)**: Mandatory EET (Endoscopic Mucosal Resection [EMR] for visible nodules + RFA for flat segments).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Los Angeles (LA) Classification for Erosive Esophagitis
* **Grade A**: ≥1 mucosal breaks ≤5 mm, not bridging the tops of mucosal folds.
* **Grade B**: ≥1 mucosal breaks >5 mm, not bridging the tops of mucosal folds.
* **Grade C**: Mucosal breaks bridging ≥2 mucosal folds, involving <75% of esophageal circumference.
* **Grade D**: Mucosal breaks involving ≥75% of esophageal circumference.

### Chicago Classification v4.0 for Achalasia Subtypes (Prognostic)
* **Type I (Classic)**: 100% absent peristalsis + no esophageal pressurization. (Good response to Heller Myotomy).
* **Type II (With Compression)**: Absent peristalsis + panesophageal pressurization in ≥20% swallows. (Best prognosis, responds well to all treatments).
* **Type III (Spastic)**: Premature/spastic contractions in ≥20% swallows. (Worst prognosis, best treated with POEM due to ability to perform longer proximal myotomy).

### Prague C & M Criteria for Barrett's Oesophagus
* **Purpose**: Standardized grading of Barrett's extent during OGD.
* **C**: Circumferential extent (in cm from GE junction).
* **M**: Maximum extent (longest tongue of Barrett's segment in cm).

### Barrett's Endoscopic Surveillance Intervals (HK / BSG Guidelines)
| Histology | Surveillance Interval |
| :--- | :--- |
| **No Dysplasia (short segment <3cm)** | Every 3-5 years |
| **No Dysplasia (long segment ≥3cm)** | Every 2-3 years |
| **Indefinite for Dysplasia** | Optimize PPI for 3-6 months, then repeat OGD |
| **Low-Grade Dysplasia** | Every 6 months (if not ablated) |

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Chronic GERD
* **Peptic Stricture**: Fibrosis leading to benign esophageal narrowing → mechanical dysphagia.
* **Barrett's Oesophagus & Adenocarcinoma**: Affects lower 1/3 of esophagus.

### Complications of Achalasia
* **Pulmonary**: Aspiration pneumonia, lung abscess, bronchiectasis (due to nocturnal regurgitation).
* **Malignancy**: Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC). 10-50x ↑ risk due to chronic stasis/inflammation. Affects mid-upper esophagus.
* **Nutritional**: Severe malnutrition, cachexia.

### Adverse Effects of Chronic PPI Therapy
* **GI Flora Alteration**: ↑ risk of *Clostridioides difficile* infection, enteric infections (Salmonella/Campylobacter), Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO).
* **Metabolic/Nutritional**: Hypomagnesemia (class effect, requires monitoring), Vitamin B12 deficiency (reduced gastric acid impairs cleavage from dietary protein), Iron malabsorption.
* **Bone Health**: ↑ risk of osteoporotic hip fractures (impaired calcium absorption).
* **Renal**: Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN), possible link to CKD progression.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important first step in investigating a 60-year-old presenting with new-onset heartburn and weight loss? Answer: Urgent upper endoscopy (OGD) to rule out esophageal malignancy, as weight loss is an alarm symptom.
* Viva Question: A patient with known asthma presents with recurrent exacerbations despite maximal inhaler therapy. What esophageal condition must you consider and how do you treat it? Answer: Reflux-induced asthma (Atypical GERD). Treat with an empirical trial of high-dose daily PPI for 8-12 weeks.
* Trap: Assuming standard PPI therapy works for Eosinophilic Oesophagitis because the patient reported symptom improvement. Pearl: ~50% of EoE patients have PPI-responsive esophageal eosinophilia. PPI is a first-line treatment for EoE, not just GERD. You must re-biopsy after PPI trial to prove histological resolution.
* Pitfall: Ordering an upper endoscopy to establish the primary diagnosis of achalasia. Pearl: OGD cannot definitively diagnose motility disorders; it is performed to exclude pseudoachalasia (malignancy at the GE junction). High-Resolution Manometry is the gold standard for diagnosis.
* Trap: A young man presents with recurrent food impaction. OGD shows concentric rings ("trachealization"). You dilate the esophagus aggressively to fix the stricture. Pearl: Aggressive dilation in EoE carries a very high risk of deep mucosal tears and esophageal perforation. Dilate cautiously only after attempting topical steroids or diet modification.
* Pitfall: Performing an immediate diagnostic endoscopy in a patient suspected of having a Zenker's diverticulum. Pearl: Blind intubation of the esophagus carries a massive risk of perforating the diverticulum. A Barium Swallow must be performed first to confirm the anatomy.
