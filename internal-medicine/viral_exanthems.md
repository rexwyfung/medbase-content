# Viral Exanthems (病毒性出疹性疾病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Exanthems
* **Exanthem (皮疹):** Widespread rash typically occurring in children/young adults, usually accompanied by systemic symptoms (e.g., fever, malaise, headache).
* **Enanthem (黏膜疹):** Eruptions on mucous membranes (e.g., Koplik spots, oral ulcers) occurring concomitantly with exanthems.
* **Classic Pediatric Numbering (Historical):** 1st = Measles, 2nd = Scarlet Fever, 3rd = Rubella, 5th = Erythema Infectiosum, 6th = Roseola Infantum.

### Etiology and Pathogens of Major Exanthems
* **Measles (麻疹):** Paramyxovirus (RNA).
* **Rubella (德國麻疹):** Togavirus (RNA).
* **Erythema Infectiosum / Fifth Disease (傳染性紅斑):** Parvovirus B19 (DNA).
* **Roseola Infantum / Exanthem Subitum (玫瑰疹):** Human Herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6) & HHV-7 (DNA).
* **Hand-Foot-Mouth Disease / HFMD (手足口病):** Coxsackievirus A16, Enterovirus 71 (EV71) (RNA).
* **Scarlet Fever (猩紅熱):** Streptococcus pyogenes (Group A Beta-Hemolytic Strep / GAS) (Bacteria, included as classic differential).
* **Chickenpox (水痘):** Varicella-Zoster Virus / VZV (DNA).

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of Exanthems
* **Statutory Notifiable Diseases (法定呈報傳染病) to CHP:** Measles, Rubella, Chickenpox, Scarlet Fever, HFMD (only EV71 or severe complications).
* **HFMD / EV71:** Bimodal peak in HK (May–July & Oct–Dec); institutional outbreaks (kindergartens/schools) common.
* **Scarlet Fever:** ↑ incidence in HK post-2011 (emergence of macrolide-resistant emm12 GAS clones).
* **Measles:** Endemic transmission eliminated in HK; cases mostly imported or localized outbreaks (e.g., HKIA airport staff outbreak in 2019).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Maculopapular Rashes
* **Direct Viral Cytopathology:** Viral replication in epidermal cells → local necrosis & cellular swelling.
* **Immune-Mediated Endothelial Damage:** Cytotoxic T-cell response against viral antigens in dermal microvasculature → local inflammation & vasodilation (e.g., Measles).
* **Immune Complex Deposition:** Type III hypersensitivity rxn in dermal capillaries (e.g., Rubella, Parvovirus B19 polyarthropathy).

### Pathogenesis of Vesicular Rashes
* **Intracellular Replication:** Virus infects keratinocytes (e.g., VZV, Coxsackie) → ballooning degeneration of cells → intraepidermal fluid accumulation (vesicles).
* **Viral Dissemination:** Primary viremia (lymphatics) → RES replication → Secondary viremia (seeding to epidermis).

### Toxin-Mediated Pathogenesis (Scarlet Fever)
* **Streptococcal Pyrogenic Exotoxins (SPEs):** Superantigens secreted by GAS → massive T-cell activation & cytokine release (TNF-α, IL-1) → widespread capillary dilation (exanthem) & systemic toxicity.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History Taking for Exanthems
* **Onset & Evolution:** Cephalocaudal spread? Centrifugal vs centripetal?
* **Prodrome:** Fever, coryza, sore throat, GI symptoms preceding rash?
* **Exposure Hx:** Contact with sick children, kindergarten outbreaks, vaccination status (MMR, Varicella), travel Hx.
* **Maternal Hx:** Pregnancy status (CRITICAL for Rubella, Parvovirus B19).

### Physical Examination of Maculopapular Exanthems
* **Measles (麻疹):** 
  * **Prodrome:** The "3 Cs" (Cough, Coryza, Conjunctivitis) + High Fever.
  * **Enanthem:** Koplik spots (柯氏斑點) (1-3mm bluish-white papules on buccal mucosa opposite 2nd molars; pathognomonic).
  * **Exanthem:** Erythematous, confluent maculopapular rash; begins at hairline/behind ears → spreads downwards (cephalocaudal); fades with desquamation.
* **Rubella (德國麻疹):**
  * **Px:** Prominent tender suboccipital & postauricular lymphadenopathy (淋巴結腫大).
  * **Enanthem:** Forchheimer spots (petechiae on soft palate).
  * **Exanthem:** Pink, discrete macules; rapid cephalocaudal spread (clears in 3 days).
* **Parvovirus B19 (Fifth Disease):**
  * **Px:** "Slapped cheek" appearance (confluent erythema on cheeks, sparing perioral region) → later lacy, reticular rash on trunk/limbs. Exacerbated by sunlight/heat.
* **Roseola Infantum (HHV-6):**
  * **Px:** High fever (often >39.5°C) for 3-5 days → Fever abruptly drops → blanching macular rash appears starting on trunk/neck → spreads to face/limbs.

### Physical Examination of Vesicular & Toxin-Mediated Exanthems
* **HFMD:**
  * **Enanthem:** Painful aphthous-like ulcers on tongue, buccal mucosa, soft palate.
  * **Exanthem:** Greyish-white oval vesicles on erythematous base across palms, soles, buttocks.
* **Scarlet Fever (猩紅熱):**
  * **Oral:** Exudative pharyngitis, Strawberry tongue (草莓舌) (white coating initially → red, hypertrophied papillae).
  * **Exanthem:** Diffuse, blanching erythema with "sandpaper" texture (starts on neck/groin → trunk); Circumoral pallor.
  * **Pastia's lines:** Accentuated linear petechiae in skin folds (antecubital, axillary).
* **Chickenpox (水痘):**
  * **Px:** Pruritic vesicles on erythematous base ("dewdrop on a rose petal"); multiple stages present simultaneously (macules → papules → vesicles → crusts).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Exanthem Onset and Progression Comparison

| Condition | Pathogen | Prodrome | Rash Characteristics | Rash Spread / Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Measles** | Paramyxovirus | Cough, Coryza, Conjunctivitis | Confluent maculopapular; Koplik spots | Cephalocaudal (hairline → down); lasts 6-7 days |
| **Rubella** | Togavirus | Mild fever, postauricular LAD | Discrete maculopapular; Forchheimer spots | Cephalocaudal; rapid clearance (3 days) |
| **Fifth Disease** | Parvovirus B19 | Mild fever/URTI | Slapped cheeks → reticular/lacy rash | Face → Trunk/Extremities |
| **Roseola** | HHV-6 | High fever (3-5 days) | Blanching maculopapular | **Trunk → Face/Extremities** (appears AFTER fever breaks) |
| **HFMD** | Coxsackie/EV71 | Fever, anorexia, sore throat | Vesicular/papular | Oral cavity → Palms, Soles, Buttocks |
| **Scarlet Fever**| S. pyogenes (GAS)| High fever, severe sore throat | Sandpaper rash, Pastia's lines, Strawberry tongue| Trunk → Extremities; spares perioral (circumoral pallor) |
| **Chickenpox** | VZV | Fever, malaise | Vesicular (multiple stages simultaneously) | Centrifugal (Trunk/Face → Extremities) |

### Non-Infectious Differentials for Exanthems
* **Drug Eruptions:** Morbilliform drug rash, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) (Hx of new meds e.g., Penicillins, Anticonvulsants, Allopurinol).
* **Kawasaki Disease (川崎病):** Fever >5 days, polymorphous rash, bilateral non-purulent conjunctivitis, cervical LAD, mucosal changes (strawberry tongue, cracked lips), extremity changes (erythema/edema).
* **EBV (Infectious Mononucleosis):** Aminopenicillin-induced generalized maculopapular rash.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Exanthems
* **Throat Swab / Rapid Antigen Detection Test (RADT):** For Group A Strep (Scarlet fever).
* **ECG:** Exclude myocardial involvement (myocarditis in EV71).

### Blood Tests for Exanthems
* **CBP & Diff:** 
  * Measles: Leukopenia, severe lymphopenia.
  * Parvovirus B19: Reticulocytopenia, isolated ↓ Hb or pancytopenia (aplastic crisis).
  * Scarlet Fever: Neutrophilic leukocytosis, eosinophilia.
* **Serology (IgM / IgG):**
  * Parvovirus B19 IgM (+ve = acute infection) / IgG (immunity).
  * Measles/Rubella IgM (+ve indicates acute infection, but cross-reactivity exists).
  * ASOT / Anti-DNase B: Retrospective Dx of GAS infection.

### Microbiological and Molecular Diagnostics
* **Nasopharyngeal Aspirate (NPA) / Swab Multiplex PCR:** Detects Enterovirus, Parainfluenza, Adenovirus, Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV).
* **Measles / Rubella PCR:** Throat swab, NPA, or Urine (gold standard for CHP statutory notification; highest sensitivity).
* **Parvovirus B19 PCR:** Blood (crucial in immunocompromised/aplastic crisis where IgM may be -ve).
* **Vesicle Fluid PCR / Viral Culture:** Coxsackie A16/EV71 (HFMD) or VZV.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Isolation Protocols in Hong Kong
* **Airborne & Contact Isolation:** Measles, Chickenpox (VZV), Disseminated Zoster. (Negative pressure room required in HA hospitals).
* **Droplet Precautions:** Rubella, Parvovirus B19, Scarlet Fever (until 24h of Abx completed).
* **Contact Precautions:** HFMD (Enteroviruses).

### Emergency Management of Exanthem Complications
* **Severe Measles Protocol:**
  * **Vitamin A (維生素A):** Administer immediately to all children with severe measles (↓ morbidity/mortality, prevents blindness/xerophthalmia). Dose varies by age (e.g., 200,000 IU for >1 yr).
  * **Respiratory Support:** High-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) or intubation for giant cell pneumonia.
* **Toxic Shock Syndrome (GAS / Scarlet Fever):**
  * Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation.
  * IV Clindamycin (suppresses toxin production) + IV Penicillin G / Ceftriaxone.
  * IVIG (Intravenous Immunoglobulin) for refractory shock.
* **Aplastic Crisis (Parvovirus B19):** Urgent packed red blood cell (PRBC) transfusion; oxygen therapy.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Supportive Management Algorithm for Exanthems
* **Antipyretics:** PO Paracetamol / Ibuprofen. (STRICT AVOIDANCE of Aspirin in children → risk of Reye's Syndrome).
* **Hydration:** Generous oral fluids. IV fluids if PO intake poor (especially in HFMD due to oral ulcers).
* **Pruritus Control:** Topical Calamine lotion, PO Antihistamines (e.g., Chlorpheniramine).

### Targeted Pharmacotherapy for Exanthems
* **Scarlet Fever:**
  * **1st Line:** PO Penicillin V x 10 days (prevents Rheumatic Fever).
  * **Alternatives (if Penicillin allergic):** PO Erythromycin / Azithromycin (Note: ↑ macrolide resistance in HK, monitor closely) or 1st-Gen Cephalosporin (if non-anaphylactic).
* **Parvovirus B19:**
  * Chronic pure red cell aplasia in immunocompromised: IVIG.
* **Chickenpox / VZV (Exclude deep detail as per prompt, but for systemic tx):**
  * PO Acyclovir (if presented <24h of rash, >12 yrs old, or chronic cutaneous/pulmonary conditions).

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis and Lifestyle Modifications
* **Statutory Notification:** Notify Centre for Health Protection (CHP) for Measles, Rubella, Scarlet fever.
* **School Exclusion Policies (HK CHP Guidelines):**
  * **Measles:** Exclude until 4 days after appearance of rash.
  * **Rubella:** Exclude until 7 days after appearance of rash.
  * **HFMD:** Exclude until all vesicles dried and fever subsided (or 14 days after onset for EV71).
  * **Scarlet Fever:** Exclude until 24 hrs after starting appropriate Abx.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for Severe HFMD (EV71)
* **High-Risk Features for CNS/Cardiopulmonary Involvement:**
  * Sustained high fever (>39°C) > 3 days.
  * Neurological signs: Myoclonus (jerks), lethargy, irritability, ataxia, tremor.
  * Autonomic instability: Tachycardia out of proportion to fever, tachypnea, mottled skin.
* **Prognosis:** EV71 carries a significantly higher risk of fatal brainstem encephalitis and neurogenic pulmonary edema compared to Coxsackie A16.

### Prognostic Indicators in Exanthems
* **Parvovirus B19 in Pregnancy:** Infection in 1st/2nd trimester carries ↑ risk of fetal loss (hydrops fetalis).
* **Rubella in Pregnancy:** Infection in 1st trimester → >90% risk of Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS).
* **Measles:** Highest mortality in infants < 1 year, malnourished children (Vit A deficiency), and immunocompromised.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological Complications of Exanthems
* **Subacute Sclerosing Panencephalitis (SSPE):** Fatal, progressive degenerative CNS disease occurring 7-10 years post-Measles infection (due to persistent mutated virus).
* **Encephalitis / Meningoencephalitis:** Can complicate Measles (1/1000), EV71 (Brainstem encephalitis), and VZV.
* **Febrile Seizures:** Extremely common complication of Roseola (HHV-6) due to rapid fever spike.

### Cardiopulmonary Complications of Exanthems
* **Respiratory:** Giant cell pneumonia (Measles), Secondary bacterial pneumonia (S. aureus/S. pneumoniae).
* **Cardiac:** Myocarditis (EV71, Parvovirus B19). Neurogenic pulmonary edema (EV71).

### Hematological and Obstetric Complications
* **Hematological:** Transient aplastic crisis (Parvovirus B19) in patients with high RBC turnover (Sickle cell, Spherocytosis, Thalassemia). ITP (Idiopathic Thrombocytopenic Purpura) post-Rubella or Measles.
* **Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS):** Classic triad: Sensorineural hearing loss, Ocular anomalies (Cataracts), Congenital heart disease (PDA).
* **Hydrops Fetalis:** Parvovirus B19 targets fetal erythroid progenitors → severe fetal anemia → high-output heart failure → severe ascites/edema (Hydrops).

### Post-Infectious Immune Complications (Scarlet Fever)
* **Acute Rheumatic Fever (ARF):** Affects joints, heart, brain, skin. (Preventable with 10-day Penicillin course).
* **Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN):** Hematuria, edema, hypertension. (NOT strictly preventable by early Abx).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic prodrome of Measles? Answer: The "3 Cs": Cough, Coryza, Conjunctivitis, accompanied by high fever and pathognomonic Koplik spots on the buccal mucosa.
* Trap: Prescribing Aminopenicillins (Amoxicillin/Ampicillin) for a misdiagnosed viral sore throat. Pearl: If the patient actually has EBV (Infectious Mononucleosis), Aminopenicillins induce a classic, generalized, pruritic maculopapular rash (an immune reaction, not a true IgE allergy). Differentiate this from primary viral exanthems.
* Pitfall: Reassuring a pregnant woman exposed to Parvovirus B19 (Fifth Disease) without serological testing. Always check maternal IgG/IgM and closely monitor the fetus with serial ultrasound (MCA doppler) for signs of severe fetal anemia and hydrops fetalis if the mother is acutely infected.
* Viva Question: Which childhood exanthem characteristically causes a high fever for days, followed by a rash that appears only AFTER the fever abruptly drops? Answer: Roseola Infantum (Exanthem Subitum), caused by Human Herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6).
* Trap: Thinking a child with a sandpaper rash and strawberry tongue has Kawasaki Disease. Pearl: Always swab for Group A Strep (Scarlet Fever). Scarlet fever is toxin-mediated and responds rapidly to Penicillin, whereas Kawasaki requires urgent IVIG and Aspirin to prevent coronary artery aneurysms.
* Pitfall: Giving Aspirin to a child with a viral exanthem for fever control. Strictly avoid Aspirin in children with viral illnesses (especially Varicella and Influenza) due to the severe risk of Reye's Syndrome (fulminant hepatic failure and toxic encephalopathy). Use Paracetamol instead.
