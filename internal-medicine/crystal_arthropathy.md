# Crystal Arthropathy (Gout & Pseudogout) (晶體性關節病（痛風與假性痛風）)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Gout and Pseudogout
* **Gout (痛風)**: Inflammatory arthritis caused by tissue deposition of monosodium urate (MSU) crystals secondary to hyperuricaemia.
* **Pseudogout (假性痛風)**: Acute inflammatory arthritis caused by calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate (CPPD) crystal deposition, classically in larger joints.

### Etiologies of Hyperuricaemia in Gout
* **Underexcreters (90%)**:
  * **Renal**: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) (慢性腎病).
  * **Drugs**: Thiazides, loop diuretics, low-dose aspirin, cyclosporine, pyrazinamide.
  * **Metabolic**: Lactic acidosis, ketoacidosis.
* **Overproducers (10%)**:
  * **Dietary**: ↑ Purine intake (seafood, red meat, alcohol/beer, fructose).
  * **Cell Turnover**: Myeloproliferative/lymphoproliferative disorders, severe psoriasis, tumour lysis syndrome.
  * **Enzyme Defects**: Hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT) deficiency (Lesch-Nyhan syndrome), PRPP synthetase overactivity.

### Etiologies of Calcium Pyrophosphate Deposition (CPPD)
* **Idiopathic**: Ageing (most common), primary osteoarthritis.
* **Metabolic/Endocrine (The 4 H's)**:
  * **H**yperparathyroidism (甲狀旁腺功能亢進).
  * **H**aemochromatosis (血色病).
  * **H**ypothyroidism (甲狀腺功能減退).
  * **H**ypomagnesaemia (低鎂血症) / Hypophosphatasia.
* **Trauma**: Prior joint injury or surgery (e.g., meniscectomy).

### Epidemiology of Crystal Arthropathy in Hong Kong
* **Gout**: Most common inflammatory arthritis in Hong Kong men.
* **Sex**: Male predominance (4:1) for gout; CPPD has equal M:F ratio.
* **Age**: Gout typically 40-60 yrs (males) or post-menopausal (females); CPPD primarily >60 yrs.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Gout (痛風)
* **Purine Metabolism**: Purines → Hypoxanthine → Xanthine → Uric Acid (catalyzed by Xanthine Oxidase).
* **Solubility Limit**: Serum urate > 360 µmol/L (6.0 mg/dL) exceeds physiological solubility limit.
* **Crystallization**: MSU precipitates in cooler peripheral joints (e.g., 1st MTPJ) and avascular tissues (cartilage).
* **Inflammatory Cascade**:
  * **Phagocytosis**: Macrophages engulf MSU crystals.
  * **NLRP3 Inflammasome**: Intracellular crystals activate NLRP3 inflammasome.
  * **Cytokine Release**: Massive release of Interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and TNF-α.
  * **Neutrophil Influx**: Chemotaxis of neutrophils → acute joint inflammation and erythema.

### Pathophysiology of Pseudogout (假性痛風)
* **Cartilage Matrix Alteration**: ↑ Extracellular inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi) production in abnormal articular cartilage.
* **Crystallization**: PPi combines with calcium → CPPD crystals form within pericellular matrix of chondrocytes.
* **Shedding**: Crystals shed into synovial space → phagocytosed by neutrophils → acute inflammatory response similar to gout.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Gout & Pseudogout Presentations
* **Onset**: Hyper-acute onset (peaks within 12-24 hours).
* **Timing**: Frequently awakens patient from sleep at night/early morning.
* **Severity**: Exquisite pain (unable to tolerate bedsheet resting on joint).
* **Triggers (Gout)**: Heavy alcohol/meat consumption, dehydration, starvation, acute systemic illness, post-operative state, trauma.
* **Joint Pattern**:
  * **Gout**: Monoarticular (early) → Oligo/polyarticular (late).
  * **Pseudogout**: Typically monoarticular, occasionally polyarticular resembling RA.

### Physical Signs of Acute Crystal Arthropathy
* **Inspection**: Erythema, profound swelling, shiny stretched skin over joint, desquamation as attack resolves.
* **Palpation**: Extreme tenderness, calor (warmth), palpable effusion.
* **Movement**: Severely restricted active and passive range of motion.
* **Typical Sites**:
  * **Podagra (足痛風)**: 1st Metatarsophalangeal Joint (MTPJ) - most classic for gout.
  * **Other Gout Sites**: Midfoot, ankle, knee.
  * **Pseudogout Sites**: Knee (>50%), wrist, pubic symphysis.

### Physical Signs of Chronic Tophaceous Gout
* **Tophi (痛風石)**: Firm, irregular, chalky subcutaneous nodules.
* **Tophus Locations**: Helix of pinna, olecranon bursa, Achilles tendon, extensor surfaces of fingers/toes.
* **Skin Changes**: Overlying skin may be thin, ulcerated, extruding white/yellow chalky material.
* **Joint Deformity**: Asymmetrical nodular swellings causing mechanical restriction.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Crystal Arthropathies vs Septic Arthritis vs Rheumatoid Arthritis
| Feature | Gout / Pseudogout | Septic Arthritis (化膿性關節炎) | Rheumatoid Arthritis (類風濕性關節炎) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Hyper-acute (< 24h) | Acute to Subacute (Days) | Insidious (Weeks to Months) |
| **Systemic Features** | Low-grade fever possible | High fever, rigors, systemic toxicity | Fatigue, weight loss, low-grade fever |
| **Joint Distribution** | 1st MTPJ (Gout), Knee (CPPD), Asymmetrical | Monoarticular (Knee/Hip most common) | Symmetrical, polyarticular (MCPJ, PIPJ, wrists) |
| **Skin/Extra-articular** | Tophi, overlying erythema | Puncture wound, overlying cellulitis | Rheumatoid nodules, sparing of DIPJ |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Synovial Fluid Analysis for Crystal Arthropathies
* **Arthrocentesis (Joint Aspiration)**: Gold standard diagnostic test. Must perform to exclude septic arthritis.
* **Gross Appearance**: Cloudy, yellow/milky, reduced viscosity.
* **Synovial Fluid Microscopy**:
  * **Gout (MSU)**: Needle-shaped crystals, strongly negatively birefringent (Yellow when parallel to compensator axis, Blue when perpendicular).
  * **Pseudogout (CPPD)**: Rhomboid/brick-shaped crystals, weakly positively birefringent (Blue when parallel, Yellow when perpendicular).
* **Cell Count**: Inflammatory (WBC 2,000 - 50,000/µL, >50% neutrophils).
* **Gram Stain & Culture**: -ve (Must order to exclude concurrent infection).

### Blood Investigations for Crystal Arthropathy
* **Serum Uric Acid**: May be NORMAL or LOW during an acute gout flare (due to uricosuric effect of inflammatory cytokines). Re-check 2-4 weeks post-flare.
* **Renal Function Tests (RFT)**: ↑ Creatinine (guides NSAID/Colchicine safety and Allopurinol dosing).
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ↑ CRP, ↑ ESR, ↑ WBC during acute flare.
* **Pseudogout Workup (Patients <50 yrs or polyarticular)**: Ca, PO4, Mg, ALP, Iron profile (exclude secondary causes).
* **HLA-B*5801 Allele Testing**: MANDATORY in Hong Kong (Han Chinese) before initiating Allopurinol to prevent severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SJS/TEN).

### Imaging Modalities for Gout and Pseudogout
* **X-ray Findings**:
  * **Acute Gout**: Often normal, soft tissue swelling only.
  * **Chronic Gout**: "Punched-out" periarticular erosions with sclerotic margins ("overhanging edges" or "rat-bite" lesions), preservation of joint space until late disease, no periarticular osteopenia.
  * **Pseudogout**: Chondrocalcinosis (軟骨鈣化) - linear calcification parallel to articular surface (classic in knee meniscus, wrist triangular fibrocartilage complex).
* **Ultrasound**:
  * **Gout**: "Double-contour sign" (hyperechoic band of MSU crystals on surface of hyaline cartilage), tophi appear as hyperechoic heterogeneous masses.
* **Dual-Energy CT (DECT)**: Color-coded detection of urate deposition (high sensitivity/specificity).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Flare Management for Gout
* **Principles**: Initiate therapy ASAP (within 24 hours of onset). Do NOT alter current Urate-Lowering Therapy (ULT) dose during an acute flare.
* **First-Line Pharmacotherapy (Choose ONE based on comorbidities)**:
  * **NSAIDs**: Naproxen, Indomethacin, or Coxibs (e.g., Etoricoxib). Maximum dose acutely (e.g., Naproxen 500mg BD). Avoid in CKD, peptic ulcer disease, heart failure.
  * **Colchicine (秋水仙鹼)**: Low-dose regimen preferred in HA. Typically 0.5mg TDS until flare resolves or GI toxicity occurs. Require dose reduction in CKD. Avoid concurrent use with strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors (e.g., Clarithromycin).
* **Second-Line / Refractory / Contraindications to NSAID/Colchicine**:
  * **Systemic Corticosteroids**: Oral Prednisolone (30-35mg/day for 3-5 days, then taper). Use if NSAIDs/Colchicine contraindicated (e.g., severe CKD).
  * **Intra-articular Corticosteroids**: Methylprednisolone injection (ideal for monoarthritis affecting a large joint, strictly AFTER excluding septic arthritis).

### Acute Flare Management for Pseudogout
* **First-Line**: Intra-articular corticosteroid injection (highly effective for single large joint like the knee).
* **Systemic**: NSAIDs or Colchicine (similar dosing and precautions as gout).
* **Refractory**: Oral corticosteroids.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Indications for Urate-Lowering Therapy (ULT) in Gout
* **Clinical Tophi**: Present on clinical exam or imaging.
* **Frequent Flares**: ≥ 2 acute gout attacks per year.
* **Radiographic Damage**: Evidence of gouty arthropathy on X-ray.
* **Comorbidities (Consider initiating after 1st flare if)**: CKD stage ≥ 2, urolithiasis, or serum urate > 540 µmol/L.

### Urate-Lowering Therapy (ULT) Pharmacological Algorithm
* **Timing**: Initiate 2-4 weeks AFTER acute flare has completely resolved.
* **Treat-to-Target Strategy**: Titrate ULT dose every 2-4 weeks to achieve Serum Urate < 360 µmol/L (or < 300 µmol/L in tophaceous gout).
* **Step 1: First-Line Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitor (XOI)**:
  * **Allopurinol (別嘌醇)**: Check HLA-B*5801 first. Start 100mg daily (50mg in CKD), titrate up to max 800mg/day.
* **Step 2: Second-Line XOI (If Allopurinol contraindicated, intolerance, or CKD)**:
  * **Febuxostat (非布司他)**: Start 40mg daily, titrate to 80mg. Note: Caution in patients with high cardiovascular risk.
* **Step 3: Uricosurics (Add-on or Alternative, rarely first-line in HA)**:
  * **Probenecid / Benzbromarone**: Promotes renal urate excretion. Contraindicated in urolithiasis or CrCl < 50 mL/min. Requires abundant hydration.

### Chronic Flare Prophylaxis during ULT Initiation
* **Pathophysiology**: Starting ULT mobilizes tissue urate stores → paradoxically triggers acute flares.
* **Prophylactic Regimen**: Must co-prescribe prophylaxis when initiating ULT.
* **Drug**: Colchicine 0.5mg daily or BD (first-line) OR low-dose NSAID.
* **Duration**: Continue for 3 to 6 months.

### Chronic Management & Lifestyle Modifications
* **Dietary Restriction**: ↓ Purine-rich foods (red meats, organ meats, shellfish, sardines).
* **Beverage Restriction**: Avoid alcohol (especially beer/spirits) and high-fructose corn syrup beverages.
* **Dietary Promotion**: Low-fat dairy products, adequate hydration (>2L water/day), coffee, Vitamin C.
* **Weight Management**: Gradual weight loss in obesity (avoid crash diets/starvation which trigger gout).
* **Medication Review**: Discontinue offending drugs if possible (e.g., switch thiazide to Losartan, which has mild uricosuric properties).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognostic Goals and Monitoring Parameters
* **Target Serum Urate**: Monitor every 2-4 weeks until target reached, then every 6 months.
* **Long-term Efficacy**: Maintaining urate < 360 µmol/L leads to crystal dissolution, cessation of flares, and shrinkage of tophi.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Chronic Gout
* **Musculoskeletal**: Chronic urate arthropathy, secondary osteoarthritis, joint destruction, tendon rupture.
* **Renal**: Uric acid nephrolithiasis (kidney stones), Chronic urate nephropathy (interstitial urate deposition leading to CKD).
* **Cardiovascular**: Independent risk factor for metabolic syndrome, hypertension, and ischaemic heart disease.

### Adverse Effects of Urate-Lowering and Anti-inflammatory Therapies
* **Allopurinol Hypersensitivity Syndrome (AHS)**: Fever, rash, eosinophilia, hepatitis, renal failure.
* **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome / Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (SJS/TEN)**: Severe cutaneous reaction, highly associated with HLA-B*5801 in Han Chinese.
* **Colchicine Toxicity**: Dose-dependent gastrointestinal toxicity (severe diarrhoea), neuromyopathy, bone marrow suppression (especially if co-administered with CYP3A4 inhibitors like macrolides in renal impairment).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)
* Viva Question: What is the most important investigation for a newly swollen, red, hot knee? Answer: Diagnostic arthrocentesis with synovial fluid analysis and culture to rule out septic arthritis.
* Viva Question: Why can the serum urate be normal during an acute gout flare? Answer: Acute phase reactants and cytokines (like IL-6) increase renal excretion of uric acid temporarily.
* Trap: Stopping or reducing Allopurinol during an acute gout flare. Pearl: Never stop or adjust ULT during an acute flare, as fluctuating urate levels will prolong the attack.
* Trap: Starting Allopurinol during an acute flare. Pearl: Wait 2-4 weeks after the flare completely resolves before initiating ULT to prevent mobilizing crystals and worsening the flare.
* Pitfall: Prescribing Allopurinol to a Hong Kong patient without checking genetics. In Hong Kong, you MUST check HLA-B*5801 before starting Allopurinol due to the high risk of fatal SJS/TEN in Han Chinese populations.
* Pitfall: Prescribing Colchicine and Clarithromycin together in a CKD patient. Fatal colchicine toxicity (bone marrow suppression/myopathy) can occur because Clarithromycin is a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate MSU and CPPD crystals on polarized light microscopy? Answer: MSU crystals are needle-shaped and strongly negatively birefringent, while CPPD crystals are rhomboid-shaped and weakly positively birefringent.
