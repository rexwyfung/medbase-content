# Renal Cell Carcinoma (腎細胞癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Renal Cell Carcinoma
* **Renal Cell Carcinoma (腎細胞癌) (RCC)**: Primary adenocarcinoma originating from renal tubular epithelium (proximal tubule or collecting duct).
* **Incidentaloma (意外發現瘤)**: >60% of cases are currently detected incidentally during abdominal imaging for unrelated reasons.

### Local Epidemiology of RCC in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: Top 10 most common cancer in Hong Kong.
* **Gender**: Male predominance (M:F ≈ 2:1).
* **Age**: Peak incidence between 60-70 years.
* **Trend**: Incidence steadily ↑ due to wider use of USG/CT imaging and rising obesity rates.

### Etiological Risk Factors for Sporadic RCC
* **Lifestyle**: Smoking (↑ risk 2x, dose-dependent), Obesity (↑ estrogen/IGF-1), Hypertension (高血壓).
* **Medical Conditions**: Acquired cystic kidney disease (ESRD patients on long-term dialysis → ↑ risk by 30x).
* **Occupational Exposures**: Asbestos, cadmium, petroleum by-products, trichloroethylene.
* **Medications**: Unopposed estrogen, chronic heavy NSAID/paracetamol abuse (phenacetin historically).

### Hereditary Syndromes Associated with RCC
* **Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) Syndrome (希佩爾-林道綜合症)**: Autosomal dominant (AD), chromosome 3p mutation. Predisposes to early-onset, bilateral, multifocal Clear Cell RCC.
* **Hereditary Papillary Renal Carcinoma**: AD, *MET* proto-oncogene mutation → Type 1 Papillary RCC.
* **Birt-Hogg-Dubé Syndrome**: AD, *FLCN* gene mutation → Chromophobe RCC.
* **Tuberous Sclerosis Complex (結節性硬化症)**: AD, *TSC1/TSC2* mutations → Angiomyolipomas (benign) and ↑ risk of RCC.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Clear Cell RCC (透明細胞癌)
* **Prevalence**: 70-80% of all RCCs.
* **Origin**: Proximal convoluted tubule.
* **Histology**: Cells rich in glycogen and lipids (dissolve during processing → "clear" cytoplasm).
* **Molecular Mechanism**: 
  * Loss of function of VHL tumor suppressor gene (Chromosome 3p deletion/mutation).
  * Normally, VHL degrades Hypoxia-Inducible Factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α).
  * VHL loss → HIF-1α accumulation → ↑ transcription of angiogenic factors (VEGF, PDGF, TGF-α).
  * **Result**: Highly vascular, hypervascular tumor dependent on angiogenesis.

### Pathophysiology of Non-Clear Cell RCC
* **Papillary RCC (乳頭狀細胞癌) (10-15%)**: 
  * **Origin**: Proximal tubule.
  * **Pathology**: Frond-like papillae. Type 1 (*MET* driven, better prognosis), Type 2 (worse prognosis).
* **Chromophobe RCC (嫌色細胞癌) (5%)**:
  * **Origin**: Intercalated cells of collecting duct.
  * **Pathology**: Pale, reticular cytoplasm with perinuclear halos ("plant-like"). Best prognosis among major subtypes.
* **Collecting Duct Carcinoma (<1%)**: Medullary location, highly aggressive, dismal prognosis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Classical Symptomatic Presentation of RCC
* **Asymptomatic**: Most common presentation today.
* **Classic Triad (經典三聯症)** (Present in <10%, indicates advanced disease):
  * **Hematuria (血尿)**: Painless, macroscopic or microscopic.
  * **Flank Pain (腰痛)**: Dull, continuous ache.
  * **Palpable Mass (腹部腫塊)**: Felt in the flank/abdomen.
* **Systemic Symptoms**: Weight loss, fever of unknown origin (FUO), night sweats, cachexia, generalized fatigue.
* **Metastatic Symptoms**: Bone pain, pathological fractures, focal neurological deficits (brain mets), hemoptysis/dyspnea (lung mets).

### Paraneoplastic Syndromes (副腫瘤綜合症) in RCC
* **Polycythemia (紅血球增多症)**: Ectopic Erythropoietin (EPO) production.
* **Hypercalcemia (高血鈣症)**: Ectopic Parathyroid Hormone-related Protein (PTHrP) production.
* **Hypertension (高血壓)**: Ectopic Renin secretion.
* **Cushing's Syndrome (庫興氏症候群)**: Ectopic ACTH production.
* **Stauffer Syndrome**: Non-metastatic hepatic dysfunction (reversible hepatomegaly and cholestasis driven by IL-6).

### Physical Examination Findings in RCC
* **Abdominal Exam**: Ballotable, firm, non-tender renal mass.
* **Vascular Exam**: 
  * **Left Varicocele (左側精索靜脈曲張)**: Sudden onset, does NOT empty on recumbency (highly suspicious for left renal vein/IVC tumor thrombus).
  * **Lower Limb Edema**: Bilateral, suggests IVC compression/thrombosis.
* **General Exam**: Pallor (anemia) or plethoric facies (polycythemia), cervical/supraclavicular lymphadenopathy (metastasis).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses for a Solid Renal Mass
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **RCC (Clear Cell/Papillary)** | Hypervascular, heterogeneous enhancement on CT, necrotic center. |
| **Renal Angiomyolipoma (AML)** | Benign hamartoma. Contains macroscopic fat (low Hounsfield units on CT). Associated with Tuberous Sclerosis. |
| **Renal Oncocytoma** | Benign. Characteristic "central stellate scar" on imaging/pathology. Spoke-wheel angiographic pattern. |
| **Urothelial Carcinoma (Renal Pelvis)** | Centrally located in collecting system, irregular filling defect on urography. Strong association with smoking/dyes. |
| **Renal Cyst (Bosniak I/II)** | Thin-walled, water density, no septations, no contrast enhancement. |
| **Renal Abscess** | Fever, severe flank pain, ↑ WBC. Thick-walled cystic lesion with peripheral enhancement. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for RCC Suspicions
* **Urinalysis (尿液分析)**: +ve for RBCs (microscopic hematuria). No casts or dysmorphic RBCs (differentiates from glomerulonephritis).
* **ECG**: To assess for QT prolongation (if hypercalcemia present) or baseline prior to TKI therapy.

### Blood Investigations for RCC Assessment
* **CBC**: Normochromic normocytic anemia (chronic disease/hematuria) OR Polycythemia (↑ Hb/Hct due to ectopic EPO). ↑ Platelets.
* **RFT**: Baseline creatinine and eGFR (crucial for staging and pre-nephrectomy planning).
* **LFT**: ↑ Alkaline Phosphatase (bone mets or Stauffer syndrome), ↓ Albumin.
* **Bone Profile**: ↑ Calcium (PTHrP effect or osteolytic bone mets), ↓ Phosphate.
* **Coagulation Profile**: PT/APTT/INR (baseline prior to surgery).

### Radiological Imaging for RCC Diagnosis & Staging
* **Triphasic CT Abdomen & Pelvis (with contrast)**: Gold standard Dx. Shows enhancement in corticomedullary phase, washout in excretory phase. Assesses local extension (perinephric fat, adrenal) and venous thrombus extent.
* **CT Thorax**: Standard for staging to rule out pulmonary metastases (most common site).
* **USG KUB**: First-line for investigating hematuria; differentiates solid mass from simple cyst.
* **MRI Abdomen/Venography**: Superior to CT for delineating the exact superior extent of IVC tumor thrombus.
* **Bone Scan / Brain MRI**: Only indicated if symptomatic (bone pain, neuro signs) or ↑ Alk Phos.

### Role of Renal Biopsy in RCC
* **Standard Practice**: NOT routinely performed for radiologically classic operable RCCs (due to theoretical risk of needle-track seeding and high CT diagnostic accuracy).
* **Indications for Biopsy**:
  1. Small renal masses (<4cm) where active surveillance or focal ablation is planned.
  2. Suspected lymphoma, metastasis from another primary, or infective abscess.
  3. Metastatic RCC prior to initiating systemic targeted/immunotherapy.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Management of Acute Severe Hematuria in RCC
* **Resuscitation**: ABC approach, large-bore IV access, group & crossmatch.
* **Urological**: Continuous bladder washout (CBWI) with 3-way Foley catheter to prevent clot retention.
* **Medical**: IV Tranexamic acid (unless contraindicated by active VTE/tumor thrombus).
* **Intervention**: Urgent angio-embolization by Interventional Radiology (IR) if refractory.

### Management of Acute Hypercalcemia of Malignancy (高血鈣症)
* **Fluid Resuscitation**: Aggressive IV Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) up to 3-4L/day (forces renal calcium excretion).
* **Pharmacotherapy**: IV Bisphosphonates (e.g., Zoledronic acid 4mg or Pamidronate) → takes 48-72 hrs to peak.
* **Adjuncts**: SC Calcitonin (rapid onset, tachyphylaxis occurs within 48h).

### Management of Acute Cord Compression in Metastatic RCC
* **Medical**: Immediate IV Dexamethasone (16mg daily in divided doses) to ↓ peritumoral edema.
* **Imaging**: Urgent whole spine MRI.
* **Definitive**: Urgent neurosurgical decompression or urgent palliative radiotherapy (RCC is notoriously radioresistant, surgery often preferred if isolated met).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Localized RCC Management Algorithm (Stage I-III)
* **Standard of Care**: Surgical resection (Primary curative intent).
  * **Partial Nephrectomy (Nephron-Sparing Surgery)**: Preferred for T1a (<4cm) and T1b (4-7cm) tumors to preserve renal function and ↓ risk of CKD.
  * **Radical Nephrectomy**: Indicated for larger tumors (T2-T4) or non-amenable central locations.
* **Active Surveillance**: Recommended for elderly/frail patients with significant comorbidities and small renal masses (<3-4cm). Follow-up with serial imaging.
* **Focal Ablation**: Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) or Cryotherapy for patients unfit for surgery with tumors <3cm.

### Advanced/Metastatic RCC Pharmacological Algorithm (First-Line)
* **Treatment Paradigm**: RCC is highly resistant to traditional cytotoxic chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Relies on Immunotherapy (ICI) and Targeted Therapy (TKI).
* **Algorithm (Based on IMDC Risk Score)**:
  * **Favorable Risk**:
    * Dual combination: TKI + ICI (e.g., Pembrolizumab + Axitinib OR Pembrolizumab + Lenvatinib).
    * Single agent: TKI monotherapy (e.g., Sunitinib or Pazopanib) - *Often used in HK HA settings due to drug formulary funding constraints.*
  * **Intermediate / Poor Risk**:
    * Dual ICI: Nivolumab (anti-PD-1) + Ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4).
    * TKI + ICI combinations as above.

### Advanced/Metastatic RCC Pharmacological Algorithm (Subsequent Lines)
* **Second-line / Refractory**:
  * Cabozantinib (multi-kinase inhibitor targeting VEGFR, MET, AXL).
  * Everolimus (mTOR inhibitor).
* **Bone-Targeted Therapy**: Regular IV Zoledronic acid or SC Denosumab to prevent skeletal-related events in bone metastases.

### Follow-up & Surveillance Protocol for Post-Resection RCC
* **Frequency**: Baseline CT at 3-6 months post-op, then regular USG/CT Abdomen and CXR/CT Thorax every 6-12 months depending on stage.
* **Duration**: Minimum 5 years (late recurrences >5 years are well documented in RCC).
* **Renal Protection**: BP control (target <130/80), avoid nephrotoxins (NSAIDs), smoking cessation.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Fuhrman Nuclear Grading System for RCC
* **Use**: Histological grading system based on nuclear size, shape, and nucleolar prominence (Graded 1 to 4).
* **Grade 1**: Small, round nuclei, absent/inconspicuous nucleoli (Good prognosis).
* **Grade 4**: Large, pleomorphic nuclei, prominent nucleoli, highly malignant (Poor prognosis).

### TNM Staging System for RCC
| Stage | Tumor Characteristics (T) | Nodal / Metastasis (N/M) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage I** | T1: Tumor ≤7cm, confined to kidney. (T1a ≤4cm, T1b 4-7cm) | N0, M0 |
| **Stage II** | T2: Tumor >7cm, confined to kidney. | N0, M0 |
| **Stage III** | T3: Tumor extends into major veins (renal vein/IVC) or perinephric fat, but strictly within Gerota's fascia. | N0/N1, M0 |
| **Stage IV** | T4: Tumor extends beyond Gerota's fascia (including ipsilateral adrenal). | Any N, M1 (Distant Mets) |

### IMDC (Heng) Risk Model for Metastatic RCC
* **Use**: Stratifies survival and guides systemic therapy choices in mRCC.
* **6 Adverse Factors**:
  1. Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) < 80%.
  2. Time from diagnosis to systemic treatment < 1 year.
  3. Hemoglobin < lower limit of normal.
  4. Calcium > upper limit of normal.
  5. Neutrophils > upper limit of normal.
  6. Platelets > upper limit of normal.
* **Scoring**:
  * **0 factors**: Favorable risk (Median OS ~43 months).
  * **1-2 factors**: Intermediate risk (Median OS ~22 months).
  * **3-6 factors**: Poor risk (Median OS ~8 months).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Disease-Related Complications of RCC
* **Metastatic Spread**: Lung (cannonball lesions, 50-60%) > Bone (osteolytic) > Liver > Brain.
* **Vascular Complications**: Renal vein / IVC thrombosis → Budd-Chiari syndrome (hepatic vein occlusion), massive Pulmonary Embolism (PE), or lower limb venous stasis.
* **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)**: Progressive loss of functional nephrons or post-nephrectomy hyperfiltration injury.

### Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor (TKI) Adverse Effects (Sunitinib/Pazopanib/Axitinib)
* **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (highly common, due to VEGF inhibition), QT prolongation, Left Ventricular dysfunction.
* **Dermatological**: Hand-foot syndrome (palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia), hair depigmentation.
* **Gastrointestinal**: Diarrhea, stomatitis, hepatotoxicity.
* **Endocrine**: Hypothyroidism (requires regular TFT monitoring).

### Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor (ICI) Adverse Effects (Nivolumab/Pembrolizumab)
* **Mechanism**: Unrestricted T-cell activation leading to autoimmune "itis".
* **Specifics**: Pneumonitis (cough/dyspnea), Colitis (severe diarrhea/perforation), Hepatitis (↑ LFTs), Endocrinopathies (thyroiditis, hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency).
* **Management**: Prompt cessation of ICI and high-dose systemic corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone 1-2mg/kg).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why does a left-sided varicocele strongly indicate advanced RCC compared to a right-sided one?
* Answer: The left testicular vein drains at a 90-degree angle directly into the left renal vein. An RCC tumor thrombus traversing the left renal vein will block this drainage, causing retrograde dilation. The right testicular vein drains directly into the IVC, bypassing the right renal vein.
* Trap: Assuming every renal mass needs a biopsy prior to surgical resection.
* Pearl: Radiologically classic RCC (hypervascular, contrast-enhancing mass on CT) does NOT routinely require biopsy prior to nephrectomy due to the high diagnostic accuracy of CT and the theoretical (though small) risk of tumor needle-track seeding.
* Pitfall: Ignoring new-onset hypertension in an RCC patient.
* Pearl: Always investigate new-onset HTN in RCC; it could be a primary paraneoplastic manifestation (renin-secreting) OR an adverse effect of TKI therapy (VEGF inhibitors destroy endothelial nitric oxide signaling, causing vasoconstriction).
* Viva Question: How do RCC bone metastases differ radiologically from prostate cancer bone metastases?
* Answer: RCC bone metastases are typically osteolytic (destructive, radiolucent), whereas prostate cancer bone metastases are characteristically osteosclerotic/osteoblastic (bone-forming, radiodense).
