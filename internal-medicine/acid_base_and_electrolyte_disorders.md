# Acid-Base & Electrolyte Disorders (酸鹼平衡及電解質紊亂)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Major Acid-Base and Electrolyte Disorders
* **Acid-Base Disorders:** Disturbances in physiological pH homeostasis (Normal: 7.35–7.45) via respiratory (CO2) or metabolic (HCO3) derangements.
* **Electrolyte Imbalance (電解質失衡):** Deviation of key serum ions (Na+, K+, Ca2+, Mg2+, PO4 3-) outside tightly regulated physiological ranges, causing multi-system electrical and osmotic dysfunction.

### Etiologies of High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (高陰離子隙代謝性酸中毒)
* **Definition:** AG > 12 mEq/L (due to unmeasured anions).
* **MUDPILES Mnemonic:**
	* **M:** Methanol (甲醇)
	* **U:** Uraemia (尿毒症)
	* **D:** Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) / Starvation Ketoacidosis
	* **P:** Paracetamol / Propylene glycol
	* **I:** Infection / Iron / Isoniazid
	* **L:** Lactic acidosis (乳酸性酸中毒) (Type A: ischemia; Type B: Metformin/liver failure)
	* **E:** Ethylene glycol
	* **S:** Salicylates (Aspirin)

### Etiologies of Normal Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (正常陰離子隙代謝性酸中毒)
* **Definition:** AG 8–12 mEq/L (hyperchloraemic acidosis due to HCO3- loss matched by Cl- gain).
* **HARDUP Mnemonic:**
	* **H:** Hyperalimentation
	* **A:** Acetazolamide
	* **R:** Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA) (腎小管酸中毒)
	* **D:** Diarrhoea (腹瀉) (most common GI HCO3- loss)
	* **U:** Uretero-pelvic shunt
	* **P:** Pancreatic fistula / Spironolactone

### Etiologies of Metabolic Alkalosis (代謝性鹼中毒)
* **Definition:** Primary ↑ HCO3- → ↑ pH.
* **Volume Depleted (Chloride-Responsive):** Vomiting/NG drainage (H+ loss), Loop/Thiazide diuretics.
* **Volume Expanded (Chloride-Resistant):** Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's), Cushing's syndrome, severe profound Hypokalaemia.

### Etiologies of Dysnatraemia (Sodium Disorders)
* **Hyponatraemia (低鈉血症) (<135 mmol/L):**
* **Hypovolaemic:** Diuretics, vomiting, diarrhoea, burns.
* **Euvolaemic:** Hypothyroidism, Glucocorticoid deficiency.
* **Hypervolaemic:** Heart failure (心臟衰竭), Cirrhosis (肝硬化), Nephrotic syndrome.
* **Hypernatraemia (高鈉血症) (>145 mmol/L):** Always a net water deficit (insensible losses, osmotic diuresis) + inability to access free water.

### Etiologies of Dyskalaemia (Potassium Disorders)
* **Hypokalaemia (低鉀血症) (<3.5 mmol/L):** GI losses (diarrhoea), Renal losses (diuretics, hyperaldosteronism), intracellular shifts (insulin, alkalosis, β2-agonists).
* **Hyperkalaemia (高鉀血症) (>5.1 mmol/L):** Renal failure, ACEi/ARB, Spironolactone, NSAIDs, cellular release (rhabdomyolysis, tumor lysis, haemolysis), acidosis.


## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Acid-Base Buffering & Compensation
* **Primary Buffers:** Extracellular HCO3- and intracellular proteins/phosphates bind free H+ immediately.
* **Respiratory Compensation:** Chemoreceptors sense pH changes → rapid alteration of minute ventilation to adjust PaCO2 (minutes to hours).
* **Renal Compensation:** Proximal tubular HCO3- reabsorption and distal tubular H+ excretion as titratable acids/NH4+ (takes 3-5 days).

### Molecular Mechanisms of Dysnatraemia & Osmoregulation
* **Normal Osmoregulation:** ADH (Vasopressin) release from posterior pituitary → inserts Aquaporin-2 channels in collecting duct → free water reabsorption.
* **Hyponatraemic Cellular Adaptation:** Rapid ↓ extracellular osmolality → water shifts into neurons (cerebral edema). Within 48h, brain cells expel osmolytes (myo-inositol, glutamate) to normalize cell volume.
* **Hypernatraemic Cellular Adaptation:** Neurons accumulate "idiogenic osmoles" to pull water back in and prevent brain shrinkage.

### Cellular Shifts in Potassium Homeostasis
* **Na+/K+-ATPase Pump:** Drives K+ intracellularly. Stimulated by Insulin and β2-adrenergic agonists.
* **pH Dependency:** Acidosis drives H+ into cells in exchange for K+ shifting out → hyperkalaemia. Alkalosis drives K+ into cells → hypokalaemia.

### Pathophysiology of Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA)
* **Type 1 (Distal):** Failure of α-intercalated cells in distal tubule to secrete H+. Leads to severe acidosis and calcium phosphate stones.
* **Type 2 (Proximal):** Defect in proximal tubule HCO3- reabsorption. Self-limiting as distal tubule eventually reabsorbs remaining HCO3-.
* **Type 4 (Hyperkalaemic):** Aldosterone deficiency/resistance downregulates ENaC and ROMK channels → impaired Na+ reabsorption and impaired K+/H+ excretion.


## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History in Acid-Base and Electrolyte Disorders
* **Fluid Status Inquiries:** Exact frequency of vomiting/diarrhea, stoma output volumes, thirst, polyuria, recent weight changes.
* **Dietary & Drug History:** Strictly review drug charts (diuretics, ACEi/ARBs, NSAIDs, over-the-counter herbal meds containing licorice/steroids), dietary sodium/potassium intake.
* **Neurological Symptoms:** Progressive lethargy, confusion, gait instability, muscle cramps, peri-oral numbness.

### Neurological and Neuromuscular Signs of Electrolyte Imbalance
* **Altered Mental Status:** Confusion, agitation, stupor, coma (indicates severe dysnatraemia or hypercalcaemia).
* **Hypocalcaemia / Hypomagnesaemia Signs:**
	* **Chvostek’s Sign:** Facial muscle twitching upon tapping the facial nerve anterior to the ear.
	* **Trousseau’s Sign:** Carpal spasm induced by inflating a BP cuff above systolic pressure for 3 minutes.
	* **Hyperreflexia:** Brisk deep tendon reflexes, clonus.
	* **Hypokalaemia Signs:** Flaccid paralysis, ↓ reflexes (hyporeflexia), fasciculations, muscle tenderness (rhabdomyolysis).

### Cardiovascular and Respiratory Signs of Electrolyte/Acid-Base Imbalance
* **Acidosis Signs:**
	* **Kussmaul Breathing:** Deep, rapid, sighing respirations (respiratory compensation for profound metabolic acidosis).
	* **Arrhythmia Signs:** Irregular pulse (AF, ectopics, ventricular escape rhythms) marking severe K+ or Mg2+ derangements.

### Hydration Status and Volume Assessment
* **Hypovolaemia (Dehydration):**
	* **Skin/Mucosa:** Decreased skin turgor, dry mucous membranes, sunken eyes.
	* **Haemodynamics:** Postural hypotension (systolic ↓ >20 mmHg or diastolic ↓ >10 mmHg on standing), tachycardia, delayed capillary refill (>2 secs).
* **Hypervolaemia (Fluid Overload):**
	* **Signs:** Elevated JVP, bibasal pulmonary crackles, peripheral pitting edema, ascites.


## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Distinguishing High vs Normal Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis
| Feature | High AG Acidosis | Normal AG Acidosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anion Gap (AG)** | > 12 mEq/L | 8 – 12 mEq/L |
| **Chloride (Cl-)** | Normal | Elevated (Hyperchloraemic) |
| **Primary Cause** | Addition of unmeasured acids | Loss of HCO3- matched by Cl- retention |
| **Classic Examples** | Lactic acidosis, Toxins, Renal failure | Diarrhoea, RTA, Fistulas |

### Differentiating Types of Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA)
| Feature | Type 1 (Distal) | Type 2 (Proximal) | Type 4 (Hyperkalaemic) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Defect** | ↓ H+ secretion | ↓ HCO3- reabsorption | ↓ Aldosterone effect |
| **Serum Potassium** | Low (Hypokalaemia) | Low (Hypokalaemia) | High (Hyperkalaemia) |
| **Urine pH** | > 5.5 (Cannot acidify) | < 5.5 (Distal tubule works) | < 5.5 |
| **Nephrolithiasis** | Present (CaPO4 stones) | Absent | Absent |

### Distinguishing True vs Pseudo-hyponatraemia
* **True Hyponatraemia:** Serum osmolality is low (< 275 mOsm/kg). Clinically significant.
* **Pseudo-hyponatraemia:** Serum osmolality is normal (275-295) or high (> 295).
* **Isotonic Pseudo:** Hyperlipidaemia (marked hypertriglyceridaemia), severe Hyperproteinaemia (Multiple Myeloma). Lab artifact due to displacement of plasma water.
* **Hypertonic Pseudo:** Hyperglycaemia, Mannitol infusion. High osmoles drag water into extracellular space, diluting Na+. (Corrected Na+ = measured Na+ + 1.6 for every 5.5 mmol/L increase in glucose above normal).

### Differentiating Chloride-Responsive vs Resistant Metabolic Alkalosis
* **Chloride-Responsive:** Urine Cl- < 20 mmol/L. Due to volume contraction (vomiting, prior diuretic use). Corrects with IV normal saline.
* **Chloride-Resistant:** Urine Cl- > 20 mmol/L. Due to severe K+ depletion, primary hyperaldosteronism, or active diuretic use. Does not correct with IV saline alone.


## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Stepwise Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) Interpretation Protocol
1. **Check pH:** Acidosis (< 7.35) or Alkalosis (> 7.45).
2. **Check Primary Derangement:** Is PaCO2 (respiratory) or HCO3- (metabolic) shifting in the same direction as pH?
3. **Assess Compensation:** Apply specific formulas (see below) to see if the secondary variable has adjusted appropriately.
4. **Calculate Anion Gap (if Metabolic Acidosis):** $AG = Na^+ - (Cl^- + HCO_3^-)$. (Normal: 8-12).
5. **Calculate Delta Ratio (if High AG):** See Section 8.

### Formulas for Acid-Base Compensation
* **Winter's Formula (Metabolic Acidosis):**
	* Expected PaCO2 = $(1.5 \times HCO_3^-) + 8 \pm 2$.
	* If measured PaCO2 > expected → concurrent Respiratory Acidosis.
	* If measured PaCO2 < expected → concurrent Respiratory Alkalosis.
* **Metabolic Alkalosis Compensation:** Expected PaCO2 = $(0.7 \times HCO_3^-) + 21 \pm 1.5$.
* **Respiratory Acidosis Compensation (Acute):** HCO3- ↑ by 1 for every 10 mmHg ↑ in PaCO2.
* **Respiratory Acidosis Compensation (Chronic):** HCO3- ↑ by 4 for every 10 mmHg ↑ in PaCO2.
* **Respiratory Alkalosis Compensation (Acute):** HCO3- ↓ by 2 for every 10 mmHg ↓ in PaCO2.
* **Respiratory Alkalosis Compensation (Chronic):** HCO3- ↓ by 5 for every 10 mmHg ↓ in PaCO2.

### Bloods and Bedside Investigations for Electrolyte Disorders
* **Core Bloods:** U&E (renal function, Na, K), Cl, Ca, Mg, PO4.
* **Serum Osmolality:** Mandatory for all hyponatraemia to exclude pseudo-hyponatraemia.
* **Thyroid & Cortisol:** TFTs, Short Synacthen Test (SST) to exclude secondary causes of euvolaemic hyponatraemia.

### Diagnostic Workup Algorithm for True Hypo-osmolar Hyponatraemia
1. **Confirm True Hypo-osmolality:** Serum Osmolality < 275 mOsm/kg.
2. **Check Urine Osmolality:**
	* < 100 mOsm/kg: Primary polydipsia, beer potomania.
	* > 100 mOsm/kg: Kidneys are concentrating urine (ADH is active). Proceed to Step 3.
3. **Check Urine Sodium (Spot UNa):**
	* **UNa < 30 mmol/L (Effective Arterial Volume Depletion):**
	  * Hypovolaemic (GI loss, skin loss).
	  * Hypervolaemic (Heart failure, Cirrhosis).
	* **UNa > 30 mmol/L:**
	  * Hypovolaemic (Renal loss: diuretics, salt-wasting nephropathy).
	  * Euvolaemic (Endocrine, SIADH, drugs).

### ECG Findings in Potassium and Calcium Disorders
* **Hypokalaemia:** Prominent U waves, ST-segment depression, flattened/inverted T waves, prolonged PR interval.
* **Hyperkalaemia:** Tall peaked/tented T waves, prolonged PR, flattened/absent P waves, widened QRS complex, sinusoidal wave pattern → VF/Asystole.
* **Hypocalcaemia:** Prolonged QT interval (predisposes to Torsades de Pointes).
* **Hypercalcaemia:** Shortened QT interval, Osborn (J) waves (rare).


## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Severe Symptomatic Hyponatraemia
* **Indication:** Seizures, coma, severe confusion, cardio-pulmonary distress.
* **Protocol (HA Guidelines):**
	* IV 3% Hypertonic Saline (NaCl).
	* Dose: 100-150 ml IV bolus over 20 minutes.
	* Repeat x 1-2 times if severe symptoms persist.
* **Goal:** Rapidly raise serum Na+ by 4-6 mmol/L to halt brain herniation/seizures. DO NOT exceed this acutely. Stop 3% once symptoms resolve.

### Acute Management of Severe Hyperkalaemia (K+ > 6.5 or ECG Changes)
* **1. Stabilize Myocardium (First Line):**
	* IV 10% Calcium Gluconate (葡萄糖酸鈣) 10 ml over 5-10 mins (Cardioprotective; onset 1-3 mins). Repeat if ECG changes persist.
* **2. Intracellular Shift:**
	* Insulin-Dextrose: 10 Units short-acting insulin (Actrapid) + 50 ml of 50% Dextrose (D50W) IV over 15-30 mins.
	* Nebulized Salbutamol: 10-20 mg (β2-agonist drives K+ into cells).
	* IV Sodium Bicarbonate: Only if concurrent severe metabolic acidosis.
* **3. Total Body Elimination:**
	* GI Binding: Resonium A (Polystyrene sulfonate) 15-30g PO/PR.

### Acute Resuscitation for Severe Hypokalaemia
* **Peripheral Line Limitation:** Max IV KCl infusion rate is 10 mmol/hr (to avoid phlebitis and arrhythmias).
* **Central Line Setting:** Can infuse up to 20 mmol/hr under continuous ECG monitoring.
* **Co-factor Replacement:** Always concurrently replace IV Magnesium; K+ will not correct if Mg2+ remains low.

### Acute Management of Hypercalcaemia Crisis
* **Resuscitation:** Aggressive IV fluid rehydration with 0.9% Normal Saline (3-4 L/day) to restore GFR and promote calciuresis.
* **Pharmacology:** IV Bisphosphonates (e.g., Pamidronate 30-90mg or Zoledronic acid 4mg) - takes 48-72h for maximal effect.
* IV Calcitonin (subcutaneous/IM) for rapid but transient reduction (tachyphylaxis occurs >48h).

### Acute Management of Severe Hypocalcaemia
* **Symptomatic (Tetany/Seizures):** IV 10% Calcium Gluconate 10-20 ml over 10-20 minutes, followed by a continuous infusion.


## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management of Hyponatraemia and Hypernatraemia
* **Hypovolaemic Hyponatraemia:** Gradual volume expansion with IV 0.9% NaCl. Stop offending diuretics.
* **Hypervolaemic Hyponatraemia:** Fluid restriction (< 1-1.5 L/day), loop diuretics.
* **Hypernatraemia Treatment Algorithm:**
	* Calculate Free Water Deficit (FWD).
	* Route: Oral or NG tap water is preferred.
	* IV Route: If nil by mouth, use 5% Dextrose (D5W) or 0.45% NaCl.
	* Rate: Correct slowly over 48-72 hours to prevent cerebral edema.

### Chronic Potassium Supplementation Strategies
* **Mild/Moderate Hypokalaemia (K+ 3.0-3.4):** PO Slow-K (Potassium Chloride 600mg tabs) 2 tabs TDS.
* **Dietary Advice:** High K+ foods (bananas, tomatoes, oranges).
* **Spironolactone/Eplerenone:** Potassium-sparing diuretics for chronic hypokalaemia secondary to hyperaldosteronism or heart failure.

### Long-Term Management of Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA)
* **Type 1 & 2 RTA:** Oral alkali therapy (Sodium Bicarbonate tabs or Shohl's solution/potassium citrate). Type 2 requires massive doses.
* **Type 4 RTA:** Low potassium diet, loop/thiazide diuretics to excrete K+, Fludrocortisone (mineralocorticoid replacement) if frank adrenal insufficiency exists.


## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS) Risk Stratification
* **High Risk Factors for ODS:** Chronic hyponatraemia (>48h), malnutrition, alcoholism, advanced liver disease, starting Na+ < 105 mmol/L.
* **Strict Correction Limits:**
	* Standard risk: Max rise of **8-10 mmol/L per 24 hours**, and max 18 mmol/L per 48 hours.
	* High risk for ODS: Max rise of **6-8 mmol/L per 24 hours**.

### Delta Ratio for Mixed Acid-Base Disorders
* **Formula:** $\Delta Ratio = \frac{Measured AG - Normal AG (12)}{Normal HCO_3^- (24) - Measured HCO_3^-}$
* **Interpretation Table:**

| Delta Ratio | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **< 0.4** | Pure Normal AG Metabolic Acidosis |
| **0.4 - 0.8** | Mixed High AG + Normal AG Metabolic Acidosis |
| **1.0 - 2.0** | Pure High AG Metabolic Acidosis (uncomplicated) |
| **> 2.0** | Mixed High AG Acidosis + Metabolic Alkalosis (or compensatory respiratory acidosis) |


## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Complications of Electrolyte Over-Correction
* **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS) / Central Pontine Myelinolysis (CPM):**
	* Pathogenesis: Over-rapid correction of chronic hyponatraemia → water flows out of brain cells faster than idiogenic osmoles can clear → neuronal dehydration and myelin destruction in the pons.
	* Presentation: 2-6 days post-correction with spastic quadriparesis, pseudobulbar palsy, "locked-in" syndrome. Irreversible.
* **Cerebral Edema:** Over-rapid correction of hypernatraemia → water rushes rapidly into osmolyte-loaded brain cells → swelling, herniation, death.

### Neurological Complications of Chronic Electrolyte Imbalance
* **Chronic Severe Hyponatraemia:** Irreversible cognitive impairment, high risk of pathological falls and fractures in the elderly.
* **Chronic Hypercalcaemia:** "Bones, stones, abdominal groans, and psychiatric overtones" → nephrocalcinosis, recurrent renal colic, confusion, depression.

### Cardiovascular Complications of Potassium and Calcium Disorders
* **Arrhythmogenic Death:** Unrecognized severe hyperkalaemia progresses rapidly from peaked T waves to sinusoidal waveforms to fatal ventricular fibrillation.
* **Digoxin Toxicity Modulation:** Hypokalaemia profoundly exacerbates Digoxin toxicity, lowering the threshold for dangerous arrhythmias.


## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you correct serum sodium for hyperglycaemia? Answer: Add 1.6 mmol/L to the measured sodium for every 5.5 mmol/L increase in glucose above the normal baseline (5.5 mmol/L).
* Viva Question: What is the most immediate life-saving drug in severe hyperkalaemia with ECG changes? Answer: IV 10% Calcium Gluconate. It stabilizes the cardiac membrane without lowering the serum potassium level.
* Trap: Administering IV Sodium Bicarbonate early in lactic acidosis. Pearl: Do not give bicarbonate routinely for lactic acidosis as it shifts the oxygen dissociation curve to the left (decreasing tissue O2 delivery) and worsens intracellular acidosis; treat the underlying cause of perfusion failure.
* Trap: Trying to rapidly normalize potassium in a patient with concurrent hypomagnesaemia. Pearl: Potassium cannot enter cells or be retained by the kidneys if magnesium is depleted. Always check and replace IV Magnesium simultaneously.
* Pitfall: Treating euvolaemic hyponatraemia with normal saline. Normal saline (0.9% NaCl) in SIADH can actually worsen hyponatraemia because the solute is excreted but the free water is retained by the kidneys.
* Pitfall: Waiting for the Resonium A (polystyrene sulfonate) to lower potassium in an emergency. It takes hours to work in the gut. You must use Actrapid/Dextrose and Calcium Gluconate for acute cardioprotection and potassium shifting.
* Trap: Assuming a normal anion gap rules out a serious metabolic condition. Pearl: Normal AG metabolic acidosis (hyperchloraemic acidosis) can indicate severe GI losses or underlying Renal Tubular Acidosis requiring targeted replacement.
