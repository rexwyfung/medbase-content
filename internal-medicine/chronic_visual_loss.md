# Chronic Visual Loss (慢性視力喪失)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Chronic Visual Loss
* **Definition**: Gradual, painless deterioration of visual acuity (視力) or visual field (視野) over months to years.

### Common Etiologies of Chronic Visual Loss
* **Lens**: Cataract (白內障)
* **Optic Nerve**: Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma [POAG] (原發性開角型青光眼), Optic Atrophy (視神經萎縮)
* **Macula**: Age-Related Macular Degeneration [ARMD] (老年黃斑退化)
* **Retina**: Diabetic Retinopathy [DR] (糖尿病視網膜病變), Hypertensive Retinopathy (高血壓視網膜病變)

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of Chronic Visual Loss
* **Leading Causes of Blindness in HK**: Cataract (most common, highly treatable), POAG (leading cause of irreversible blindness), ARMD, DR.
* **Prevalence**: Glaucoma affects ~3-4% of HK population >40 yrs. High myopia prevalence in HK ↑ risk of POAG, cataract, and myopic macular degeneration.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiology of Cataract (白內障)
* **Mechanism**: Oxidative stress, UV damage, or metabolic alterations (e.g., ↑ sorbitol in DM) → crystallin protein denaturation & aggregation in lens → opacification.
* **Morphological Types**:
  * **Nuclear Sclerotic**: Central aging, induces myopic shift (second sight).
  * **Cortical**: Spoke-like opacities, affects peripheral vision first, glare.
  * **Posterior Subcapsular**: Plaque-like opacity anterior to posterior capsule; highly symptomatic in bright light; strongly associated with topical/systemic corticosteroids & DM.

### Pathophysiology of Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma (原發性開角型青光眼)
* **Mechanism**: ↑ Resistance to aqueous humor outflow through trabecular meshwork & Schlemm's canal → Intraocular Pressure (IOP) ↑.
* **Cellular Level**: Mechanical stress + ischemia → retinal ganglion cell (RGC) apoptosis → progressive loss of optic nerve fibers (cupping).

### Pathophysiology of Age-Related Macular Degeneration (老年黃斑退化)
* **Dry ARMD**: RPE (retinal pigment epithelium) senescence & metabolic dysfunction → failure to clear photoreceptor debris → **Drusen** (lipofuscin deposits) accumulation in Bruch's membrane → focal RPE atrophy.
* **Wet ARMD**: Progressive hypoxia/inflammation → ↑ VEGF expression → Choroidal Neovascularization (CNV) breaking through Bruch's membrane → leakage of fluid/lipids/blood → rapid photoreceptor destruction & disciform scar.

### Pathophysiology of Diabetic Retinopathy (糖尿病視網膜病變)
* **Microangiopathy**: Chronic hyperglycemia → non-enzymatic glycosylation → pericyte loss & basement membrane thickening → capillary non-perfusion & ischemia.
* **Proliferative Phase**: Retinal ischemia triggers VEGF release → retinal neovascularization (fragile, prone to bleeding/traction).
* **Maculopathy**: Disrupted blood-retinal barrier → fluid & lipoprotein leakage → macular edema (leading cause of visual loss in DM).

### Pathophysiology of Hypertensive Retinopathy (高血壓視網膜病變)
* **Vascular Changes**: Chronic systemic hypertension → arteriosclerosis, intimal thickening, arteriole narrowing.
* **Severe/Malignant Phase**: Fibrinoid necrosis of vessels → focal ischemia (cotton wool spots) & disruption of blood-retinal barrier (hemorrhages, exudates).

### Pathophysiology of Optic Atrophy (視神經萎縮)
* **Mechanism**: End-stage result of any optic nerve insult (ischemia, compression, inflammation, toxins) → loss of axons and myelin → glial tissue proliferation.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### History of Presenting Complaint in Chronic Visual Loss
* **Temporal Profile**: Gradual onset (months/years).
* **Nature of Vision Loss**: 
  * **Central**: Reading difficulty, face recognition difficulty (ARMD).
  * **Peripheral**: Bumping into objects, tunnel vision (POAG).
  * **General Blurring/Glare**: Halos around streetlights, poor night vision (Cataract).
* **Associated Symptoms**: Metamorphopsia (distorted straight lines → Wet ARMD).
* **Risk Factors**: DM, HTN, smoking, family Hx, long-term steroid use.

### Physical Examination of Cataract (白內障)
* **External/Red Reflex**: Leukocoria (white pupil in dense cataract), diminished or absent red reflex.
* **Fundoscopy**: Obscured view of retina (details blurred proportional to density).

### Physical Examination of Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma (青光眼)
* **Visual Fields**: Nasal step, arcuate scotoma, paracentral scotoma, eventual tunnel vision.
* **Fundoscopy (Optic Disc Signs)**: ↑ Cup-to-disc ratio (CDR > 0.5), cup asymmetry > 0.2 between eyes, vertical elongation of cup, focal thinning/notching of neuroretinal rim, splinter (Drance) hemorrhages at disc margin.

### Physical Examination of Age-Related Macular Degeneration (黃斑退化)
* **Visual Fields**: Central scotoma.
* **Fundoscopy**:
  * **Dry ARMD**: Hard (small, distinct) or soft (large, confluent) yellow Drusen at macula, RPE hyper/hypopigmentation, geographic atrophy.
  * **Wet ARMD**: Subretinal fluid, macular hemorrhage, gray-green subretinal membrane, exudates, fibroglial disciform scarring (late).

### Physical Examination of Diabetic & Hypertensive Retinopathy
* **Diabetic Retinopathy (DR)**: Microaneurysms, dot-and-blot hemorrhages, hard exudates (lipid rings), cotton wool spots (CWS), neovascularization at disc (NVD) or elsewhere (NVE), preretinal/vitreous hemorrhage, tractional retinal detachment.
* **Hypertensive Retinopathy**: Arteriolar narrowing (silver/copper wiring), arteriovenous (AV) nipping, flame-shaped hemorrhages, CWS, hard exudates (macular star).

### Physical Examination of Optic Atrophy (視神經萎縮)
* **Pupils**: +ve Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD) if asymmetric.
* **Fundoscopy**: Pale, white, flat optic disc with distinct margins, ↓ number of small vessels crossing the disc edge.
* **Color Vision**: Decreased (dyschromatopsia).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Topographical Patterns of Visual Loss
| Pattern of Loss | Primary Suspects | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Central Vision Loss** | ARMD, Diabetic Maculopathy | Macular signs (+ve Drusen/Exudates); Peripheral vision preserved. |
| **Peripheral Vision Loss** | POAG, Retinitis Pigmentosa | Glaucoma = cupping; RP = bone-spicule pigmentation & night blindness. |
| **Overall Blurring** | Cataract, Refractive Error | Cataract = absent red reflex; Refractive = improves with pinhole. |

### Differentiating Dry vs Wet ARMD
| Feature | Dry ARMD (90%) | Wet ARMD (10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Progression** | Slow (years) | Rapid (days/weeks) |
| **Metamorphopsia** | Absent or mild | Severe (Amsler grid heavily distorted) |
| **Fundus Hallmark** | Drusen, RPE atrophy | Subretinal hemorrhage/fluid |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Ophthalmic Investigations
* **Visual Acuity**: Snellen chart, Pinhole test (corrects refractive errors).
* **Macular Function**: Amsler grid (tests central 10°, detects metamorphopsia).
* **Slit-Lamp Examination (Biomicroscopy)**: Evaluates anterior segment and lens opacities.
* **Applanation Tonometry (Goldmann)**: Gold standard for measuring IOP.
* **Gonioscopy**: Measures iridocorneal angle (differentiates Open vs Closed angle).
* **Pachymetry**: Measures central corneal thickness (CCT) (Thin CCT causes falsely low IOP readings & is an independent risk factor for POAG progression).

### Diagnostic Imaging for Chronic Visual Loss
* **Fundus Photography**: Serial monitoring for DR screening and glaucoma CDR progression.
* **Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)**: 
  * **Macula**: High-res cross-section of retina; detects subretinal fluid in wet ARMD & macular edema in DR.
  * **Optic Nerve**: Measures Retinal Nerve Fiber Layer (RNFL) thickness (objective early detection of POAG).
* **Automated Perimetry (Humphrey Visual Field)**: Maps visual field defects in POAG.
* **Fundus Fluorescein Angiography (FFA)**: Evaluates retinal circulation; localizes CNV leakage in wet ARMD & capillary non-perfusion in DR.

### Diagnostic Criteria for Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma
* **Requires ≥2 of the following**:
  * Intraocular Pressure > 21 mmHg (Note: Normal-tension glaucoma has IOP ≤21).
  * Glaucomatous optic nerve damage (cupping, focal notching, RNFL loss).
  * Typical glaucomatous visual field defects.
* **Crucial Negative Finding**: Open iridocorneal angle on gonioscopy (Grade III/IV).

### Diagnostic Grading Criteria for Diabetic Retinopathy (DR)
| Stage | Diagnostic Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mild NPDR** | Microaneurysms only. |
| **Moderate NPDR** | Microaneurysms + dot/blot hemorrhages/hard exudates/CWS. |
| **Severe NPDR (4-2-1 Rule)** | Requires ≥1: Diffuse hemorrhages in 4 quadrants, Venous beading in ≥2 quadrants, IRMA in ≥1 quadrant. |
| **Proliferative DR (PDR)** | Neovascularization (NVD or NVE), vitreous hemorrhage, tractional RD. |
| **Maculopathy** | Hard exudates/edema within 1 disc diameter of fovea. |

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Wet ARMD Hemorrhage
* **Indication**: Sudden profound visual loss due to macular hemorrhage or fluid accumulation.
* **Resuscitation/Acute Rx**: Urgent referral to ophthalmology → Intravitreal Anti-VEGF injection within days to arrest bleeding/leakage.

### Acute Management of Proliferative DR Complications
* **Vitreous Hemorrhage**: Bed rest with head elevation (settles blood inferiorly) → Urgent pars plana vitrectomy + Endolaser Panretinal Photocoagulation (PRP) if blood does not clear.
* **Tractional Retinal Detachment**: Urgent vitrectomy to relieve traction bands.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Management & Surgical Indications for Cataract
* **Conservative**: Refractive correction (glasses), UV protection.
* **Surgical Indication**: When vision impairment interferes with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs).
* **Definitive Treatment**: Phacoemulsification (ultrasound fragmentation of lens) + Intraocular Lens (IOL) implantation under local anesthesia.

### Stepwise Pharmacological Algorithm for POAG
* **Goal**: Reduce IOP by 20-30% from baseline to halt disease progression.
* **Step 1: Topical Prostaglandin Analogs (First-line)**
  * E.g., Latanoprost, Bimatoprost. (Mechanism: ↑ uveoscleral outflow).
* **Step 2: Topical Beta-Blockers**
  * E.g., Timolol. (Mechanism: ↓ aqueous production).
* **Step 3: Topical Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (CAIs) or Alpha-2 Agonists**
  * CAIs e.g., Dorzolamide (↓ aqueous production).
  * Alpha-2 Agonists e.g., Brimonidine (↓ aqueous production & ↑ uveoscleral outflow).
* **Step 4: Surgical Interventions (if refractory)**
  * Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty (SLT).
  * Trabeculectomy (creates fistula between anterior chamber & sub-Tenon space).

### Chronic Management of Age-Related Macular Degeneration (ARMD)
* **Dry ARMD**:
  * Lifestyle: Smoking cessation (most critical modifiable risk factor), UV protection.
  * Supplementation: AREDS2 formula (Vitamin C, E, Lutein, Zeaxanthin, Zinc, Copper) ↓ risk of progression to wet ARMD by ~25%.
* **Wet ARMD**:
  * **First-line Rx**: Serial Intravitreal Anti-VEGF injections (e.g., Ranibizumab, Aflibercept, Bevacizumab [off-label via HA]).
  * Regimen: Usually given monthly until dry on OCT, then tailored (Treat-and-Extend).

### Chronic Management of Diabetic Retinopathy
* **Systemic Risk Factor Control**: Strict glycemic control (HbA1c <7%), BP control (<130/80), lipid optimization.
* **HK HA Screening Protocol**: RAMP-DM program (annual digital fundus photography for all HA DM patients).
* **Targeted Retinal Therapy**:
  * **Maculopathy (CSME)**: Intravitreal Anti-VEGF or focal/grid laser photocoagulation.
  * **Severe NPDR / Proliferative DR**: Panretinal Photocoagulation (PRP) (sacrifices peripheral retina to ↓ oxygen demand & VEGF drive, saving central vision).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Keith-Wagener-Barker Classification for Hypertensive Retinopathy
| Grade | Clinical Findings | Prognostic Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grade 1** | Mild generalized arteriolar narrowing (silver wiring). | Early, benign hypertension. |
| **Grade 2** | Grade 1 + Focal narrowing and AV nipping (crossing changes). | Chronic sustained hypertension. |
| **Grade 3** | Grade 2 + Retinal hemorrhages, exudates, Cotton Wool Spots. | High risk of end-organ damage (Target organ). |
| **Grade 4** | Grade 3 + Papilledema (disc swelling) +/- Macular star. | Malignant hypertension (Medical Emergency). |

### Risk Stratification for POAG Progression
* **High-Risk Factors for Progression**: Advanced age, high baseline IOP, thin central corneal thickness (CCT <555 μm), large baseline CDR, family history, Black/Hispanic/Asian ethnicity, non-compliance with drops.
* **Target IOP Setting**: Advanced baseline damage requires lower Target IOP (often <15 mmHg) compared to early disease (often <18-21 mmHg).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Systemic Complications of Untreated Chronic Ocular Diseases
* **Functional Decline**: Irreversible blindness, loss of driving privileges, severely ↑ risk of falls and hip fractures in elderly, social isolation, clinical depression.

### Adverse Effects of Glaucoma Medications (Critical for IM)
* **Prostaglandin Analogs (Latanoprost)**: ↑ iris pigmentation (heterochromia), eyelash growth (trichomegaly), periorbital fat atrophy, mild conjunctival hyperemia.
* **Topical Beta-Blockers (Timolol)**: Systemic absorption via nasolacrimal duct → bronchospasm (contraindicated in Asthma/COPD), bradycardia, heart block, masking of hypoglycemia in DM.
* **Topical CAIs (Dorzolamide)**: Bitter taste, allergic contact dermatitis (sulfa allergy cross-reactivity).
* **Alpha-2 Agonists (Brimonidine)**: Allergic conjunctivitis, systemic hypotension, fatigue.

### Complications of Ophthalmic Interventions
* **Intravitreal Injections (Anti-VEGF)**: Endophthalmitis (devastating intraocular infection), retinal detachment, transient IOP spike.
* **Cataract Surgery (Phacoemulsification)**: Posterior capsular opacification (most common, Rx with YAG laser capsulotomy), endophthalmitis, cystoid macular edema.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of irreversible blindness in Hong Kong? Answer: Glaucoma (POAG).
* Viva Question: How do you prevent systemic absorption and side effects of topical Timolol? Answer: Perform punctal occlusion (pressing on the medial canthus) for 2-3 minutes immediately after drop instillation.
* Viva Question: A patient with long-term steroid therapy for SLE presents with gradual blurring of vision and glare. What is the most likely diagnosis? Answer: Posterior subcapsular cataract.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with an IOP of 18 mmHg cannot have glaucoma. Pearl: Up to 30% of glaucoma patients have Normal-Tension Glaucoma (IOP ≤ 21 mmHg but progressive optic neuropathy); look at the disc and visual fields, not just the pressure.
* Trap: Confusing the visual field defects of ARMD and POAG. Pearl: ARMD causes central scotoma (macular damage), whereas POAG classically causes peripheral/arcuate visual field defects (sparing central vision until late stages).
* Pitfall: Prescribing topical non-selective beta-blockers (Timolol) for glaucoma in a patient with undiagnosed COPD. Can precipitate severe bronchospasm and fatal respiratory compromise; always take a thorough respiratory history before prescribing.
* Pitfall: Missing the "4-2-1" rule in diabetic retinopathy grading. Failing to recognize Severe NPDR delays necessary prophylactic Panretinal Photocoagulation (PRP) before dangerous proliferative changes occur.
