# Haemorrhagic Stroke (出血性中風)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Haemorrhagic Stroke (出血性中風):** Acute neurological deficit secondary to spontaneous bleeding into brain parenchyma [**Intracerebral Haemorrhage / ICH** (腦出血)] or ventricular system/subarachnoid space [**Subarachnoid Haemorrhage / SAH** (蛛網膜下腔出血)].

### Etiologies of Intracerebral Haemorrhage
* **Hypertensive Vasculopathy (高血壓性血管病變):** Most common (deep structures: putamen, thalamus, pons, cerebellum). Microaneurysms (**Charcot-Bouchard**).
* **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy / CAA (澱粉樣血管病):** Elderly, lobar (cortical/subcortical) bleeds, recurrent.
* **Vascular Malformations:** Arteriovenous malformation (AVM), cavernous angioma, dural AV fistula.
* **Coagulopathy:** Iatrogenic (warfarin, DOACs, thrombolysis), liver cirrhosis, thrombocytopenia.
* **Others:** Ruptured saccular aneurysm, hemorrhagic transformation of ischemic stroke, cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVST), illicit drugs (cocaine/amphetamines → intense vasospasm & severe HTN).

### Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Incidence:** Accounts for ~25-30% of all strokes in Chinese/Asian populations (significantly higher than ~15% in Western populations).
* **Demographics:** HA data shows ↑ incidence of ICH in young males due to undiagnosed/poorly controlled essential HTN.
* **Mortality/Morbidity:** 30-day mortality approaches 40-50%; majority of survivors left with severe disability.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Primary Injury Mechanics in ICH
* **Vessel Rupture:** Chronic HTN → lipohyalinosis & fibrinoid necrosis of small penetrating arteries (lenticulostriate) → rupture.
* **Haematoma Expansion:** Rapid bleeding creates physical mass effect → direct structural destruction of local neurons/glia.
* **Mass Effect & ↑ICP:** Expanding volume in rigid cranium → ↑ Intracranial Pressure (ICP) → midline shift → brainstem compression/herniation syndromes.

### Secondary Injury Pathways in ICH
* **Thrombin & Iron Toxicity:** Haematoma breakdown releases thrombin and red blood cell lysis products (iron/haem) → intense local neurotoxicity.
* **Peri-haematomal Edema:** Free radicals, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1) → blood-brain barrier (BBB) breakdown → vasogenic edema (peaks at 3-5 days).
* **Ischemic Penumbra (Controversial):** Microvascular compression by hematoma/edema reduces local cerebral blood flow (CBF) → secondary hypoxic-ischemic injury.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Characteristic History of Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Onset:** Hyperacute, sudden onset of focal neurological deficits (minutes to hours).
* **Progression:** Smoothly progressive or stepwise deterioration (distinguishes from sudden max deficit in ischemic stroke).
* **Accompanying Symptoms:** Severe headache, vomiting, early depression of consciousness/GCS.
* **Seizures:** More common in lobar (cortical) ICH compared to deep ICH or ischemic stroke.

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Vitals:** **Cushing Reflex** (bradycardia, irregular respirations, severe systolic HTN) indicating imminent brainstem herniation.
* **Putaminal Haemorrhage Signs:** Contralateral hemiparesis/hemisensory loss, homonymous hemianopia, aphasia (dominant) or neglect (nondominant).
* **Thalamic Haemorrhage Signs:** Contralateral severe sensory loss > motor loss, upward gaze palsy, non-reactive miotic pupils, aphasia.
* **Pontine Haemorrhage Signs:** Coma, quadriplegia, decerebrate posturing, **pinpoint reactive pupils**, absent horizontal eye movements.
* **Cerebellar Haemorrhage Signs:** Occipital headache, severe ataxia, vertigo, nystagmus, ipsilateral cranial nerve palsies. *Motor weakness often ABSENT.*

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table for Acute Neurological Deficits
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ischemic Stroke (缺血性中風)** | Sudden maximal deficit at onset. Headache/vomiting less common early on. Cortical signs (e.g., aphasia) without early coma. |
| **Subarachnoid Haemorrhage** | "Thunderclap" headache, meningism (neck stiffness, photophobia), focal deficits may be absent unless accompanied by vasospasm or intracerebral extension. |
| **Brain Tumour (腦腫瘤) / Space-Occupying Lesion** | Insidious progressive onset over weeks/months. Papilloedema, morning headache. Can present acutely if tumour bleeds. |
| **Todd's Paresis** | Transient focal weakness following a focal or generalized seizure. Resolves typically within 24-48 hours. |
| **Hypoglycemia (低血糖)** | Sweating, tremor, palpitations. Can mimic exact stroke focal deficits (hemiparesis). Reverses completely with IV glucose. |
| **Encephalitis (腦炎)** | Fever, confusion, psychiatric symptoms, prodromal viral illness, new-onset focal seizures. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Suspected Stroke
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG):** Rule out hypoglycemia.
* **ECG:** Check for AF (concurrent embolic risk), arrhythmias, or prominent T-wave inversion (neurogenic T-waves).

### Blood Investigations for Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Platelet count (rule out thrombocytopenia).
* **Coagulation Profile:** PT/INR, APTT (rule out coagulopathy, guide reversal).
* **Renal & Liver Function Tests (LRFT):** Uraemia causes platelet dysfunction; liver failure causes coagulopathy. Check baseline before contrast.
* **Group & Screen / Cross-match:** Prepare for potential surgical intervention.
* **Toxicology Screen:** If illicit drug use (cocaine/amphetamine) suspected in young patients.

### Neuroimaging for Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Non-Contrast CT Brain (NCCT):** Gold standard initially. Shows acute blood as hyperdense (white) lesions. Assesses volume, intraventricular extension, hydrocephalus, and midline shift.
* **CT Angiogram (CTA) Brain/Neck:** Detects spot sign (contrast extravasation indicating active bleeding/high risk of expansion), AVMs, aneurysms, or Moyamoya disease.
* **MRI Brain:** T2* gradient-echo (GRE) or Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI) exquisitely sensitive for chronic microbleeds (indicative of CAA or hypertensive vasculopathy).
* **Catheter Cerebral Angiography (DSA):** Gold standard for vascular malformations. Considered in young normotensive patients, subarachnoid hemorrhage, or unusual ICH locations.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Initial Resuscitation & Blood Pressure Control
* **Airway & Breathing:** Intubate if GCS ≤8, impending herniation, or poor airway protection. Target normoxia/normocarbia (PaCO2 35-40 mmHg).
* **Acute BP Lowering (HA Protocol):** If SBP 150-220 mmHg, acute lowering to **SBP 140 mmHg** is safe and decreases hematoma expansion.
* **First-line Anti-hypertensives:** IV Labetalol (bolus/infusion) or IV Nicardipine/Esmolol infusion. *Avoid* nitrates (e.g., GTN) as they cause cerebral vasodilation and ↑ICP.

### Reversal of Coagulopathy Algorithms
| Anticoagulant | Targeted Reversal Agent & Protocol |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Warfarin (華法林)** | **Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC)** (e.g., Beriplex) + IV Vitamin K (5-10mg). Avoid FFP if PCC available (large volume, slower). |
| **Dabigatran** | **Idarucizumab** (Praxbind) 5g IV. If unavailable: aPCC or PCC. |
| **Anti-Xa (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban)**| **Andexanet alfa** (if available) OR 4-factor PCC (50 units/kg). |
| **Heparin (UFH/LMWH)** | IV **Protamine Sulfate**. |
| **Antiplatelets (Aspirin/Clopidogrel)** | Platelet transfusion (Routine use NOT recommended for spontaneous ICH per PATCH trial, but considered if emergency craniotomy planned). |

### Management of Raised Intracranial Pressure (ICP)
* **Positioning:** Elevate head of bed (HOB) to 30 degrees to optimize venous drainage. Analgesia/sedation to prevent agitation/coughing.
* **Medical Osmotherapy:** IV Mannitol 20% or Hypertonic Saline (e.g., 3%).
* **Hyperventilation:** Temporary bridging measure to operating theatre (Target PaCO2 30-35 mmHg → causes cerebral vasoconstriction).

### Neurosurgical Interventions
* **External Ventricular Drain (EVD):** Inserted for acute obstructive hydrocephalus (e.g., thalamic/cerebellar bleed pressing on 3rd/4th ventricle or intraventricular hemorrhage).
* **Cerebellar Decompression:** Urgent suboccipital craniectomy for cerebellar ICH >3cm or deteriorating neurological status (lifesaving, prevents brainstem compression).
* **Supratentorial Craniotomy:** Considered for lobar clots >30mL within 1cm of surface in deteriorating patients (STICH II criteria).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Pharmacological Management
* **Strict Blood Pressure Control:** Cornerstone of secondary prevention. Target long-term BP <130/80 mmHg. Employ ACEi/ARBs, CCBs, or thiazide diuretics.
* **Antithrombotic Resumption:** Highly individualized. Generally avoid restarting aspirin/anticoagulation in lobar ICH (high recurrence from CAA). Deep ICH (hypertensive) patients with compelling indications (e.g., mechanical heart valve, high CHA2DS2-VASc AF) may restart anticoagulation after 4-8 weeks, often preferring DOACs over warfarin.
* **Statin Therapy:** Cautious approach. High-dose statins (SPARCL trial) showed slightly ↑ risk of recurrent ICH. Use standard targets if clear atherosclerotic indications exist, but avoid aggressive maximization if solely prior ICH.

### Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation & Lifestyle Modifiers
* **Neuro-rehabilitation:** Early mobilization, Physiotherapy (PT) for motor recovery, Occupational Therapy (OT) for ADLs, Speech Therapy (ST) for dysphagia screening/aphasia.
* **Lifestyle Modification:** Absolute smoking cessation, moderate alcohol intake restriction, weight reduction, OSA screening.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### The ICH Score (Mortality Prognostication)
Predicts 30-day mortality. 1 point for each unless otherwise specified. Max 6 points.
| Parameter | Value (Points) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)** | 3-4 (2 pts), 5-12 (1 pt), 13-15 (0 pt) |
| **ICH Volume** | ≥ 30 cm³ (1 pt), < 30 cm³ (0 pt) |
| **Intraventricular Haemorrhage** | Present (1 pt), Absent (0 pt) |
| **Infratentorial Origin** | Yes (1 pt), No (0 pt) |
| **Age** | ≥ 80 years (1 pt), < 80 years (0 pt) |

* **Prognosis Correlation:** Score 0 = 0% mortality; Score 3 = ~72% mortality; Score 5-6 = ~100% mortality.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological Complications
* **Hydrocephalus:** Blood blocking arachnoid granulations or obstructing foramina/aqueduct (requiring EVD/VP Shunt).
* **Seizures/Epilepsy:** Especially in lobar ICH with cortical involvement. (Requires anticonvulsants e.g., Levetiracetam; prophylactic use is NOT recommended).
* **Vasospasm & Delayed Cerebral Ischemia:** Primarily in SAH (days 4-14). Requires Nimodipine.

### Systemic Complications
* **Venous Thromboembolism (DVT/PE):** Due to hemiplegia and immobility. High risk. IPC (Intermittent pneumatic compression) boots acutely; prophylactic LMWH considered after 48h if repeat CT shows stable hematoma.
* **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Due to bulbar palsy, depressed consciousness, and dysphagia.
* **Stress Ulcers & GI Bleed:** Cushing's ulcers due to ↑ vagal tone from ↑ ICP.
* **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Neurogenic stunned myocardium and catecholamine surge.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Trap: Administering antiplatelet therapy for acute focal neurological deficit before neuroimaging. Pearl: Always wait for a non-contrast CT Brain to exclude haemorrhage before administering Aspirin or Thrombolytics. Ischemic and Haemorrhagic strokes cannot be reliably distinguished clinically.
* Pitfall: Assuming pinpoint pupils always indicate opiate overdose in a comatose patient. Pontine haemorrhage causes coma and pinpoint reactive pupils due to disruption of descending sympathetic tracts.
* Viva Question: What is the acute blood pressure target in spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage? Answer: Guidelines recommend acutely lowering systolic blood pressure to 140 mmHg within the first hour for patients presenting with SBP between 150-220 mmHg, to prevent haematoma expansion.
* Pitfall: Giving intravenous GTN or nitroprusside for blood pressure control in acute stroke. These agents cause profound cerebral vasodilation, acutely raising intracranial pressure and worsening mass effect.
* Trap: Transfusing platelets routinely to a patient with an ICH who was taking aspirin. Pearl: The PATCH trial demonstrated that routine platelet transfusion in patients on antiplatelet therapy with spontaneous ICH is associated with higher mortality and worse functional outcomes.
