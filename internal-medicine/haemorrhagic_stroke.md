# Haemorrhagic Stroke (出血性中風)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definitions of Haemorrhagic Stroke (出血性中風)
* **Haemorrhagic Stroke (出血性中風)**: Acute neurological deficit >24h (or leading to death) secondary to non-traumatic intracranial vascular rupture.
* **Intracerebral Haemorrhage (ICH / 腦內出血)**: Extravasation of blood directly into brain parenchyma; may extend into ventricles (**Intraventricular Haemorrhage [IVH / 腦室出血]**).
* **Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH / 蛛網膜下腔出血)**: Extravasation of blood into the subarachnoid space between pia and arachnoid membranes.

### Hong Kong Local Epidemiology of Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Incidence Proportion**: Accounts for **~20–30%** of all acute strokes in Hong Kong (significantly higher than the 10–15% seen in Western populations).
* **Primary ICH Driver**: Undiagnosed or poorly controlled **Essential Hypertension (原發性高血壓)** remains the single greatest local risk factor.
* **Mortality Burden**: Cerebrovascular disease is the 4th leading cause of death in HK; haemorrhagic subtypes carry a 30-day mortality of **30–50%**.

### Etiological Classification of Intracerebral Haemorrhage (ICH)
* **Hypertensive Microangiopathy (高血壓性微血管病變)**: Rupture of deep perforating arteries (lenticulostriate, thalamoperforating, paramedian pontine).
* **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA / 腦澱粉樣血管病變)**: $\beta$-amyloid peptide deposition in small-to-medium cortical/leptomeningeal vessels → **lobar haemorrhage (腦葉出血)** in elderly (>60y).
* **Antithrombotic-Associated ICH**: Iatrogenic coagulopathy via **Warfarin (華法林)**, **DOACs (直接口服抗凝血藥)**, or dual antiplatelet therapy.
* **Structural Vascular Lesions**: **Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM / 動靜脈畸形)** (most common cause in young/normotensive patients), **Cavernous Malformation (海綿狀血管瘤)**, dural arteriovenous fistula.
* **Systemic & Secondary Causes**: Haemorrhagic transformation of ischaemic infarct, **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (腦靜脈竇血栓)**, primary/metastatic brain tumours (melanoma, renal cell, choriocarcinoma, thyroid, lung), sympathomimetic abuse (cocaine, methamphetamine), coagulopathy (thrombocytopenia, haemophilia, liver cirrhosis).

### Etiological Classification of Non-Traumatic Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH)
* **Saccular Berry Aneurysm Rupture (囊狀動脈瘤破裂)**: Accounts for **85%** of spontaneous SAH.
  * **Anterior Communicating Artery (ACom)**: **~30–35%**
  * **Posterior Communicating Artery (PCom) / Internal Carotid Artery (ICA)**: **~25–30%**
  * **Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) Bifurcation**: **~20%**
  * **Posterior Circulation (Basilar tip / PICA)**: **~10–15%**
* **Non-Aneurysmal Perimesencephalic SAH**: **10%**; venous/capillary leak anterior to midbrain; benign course.
* **Rare Causes (5%)**: Mycotic aneurysms (infective endocarditis), AVM rupture, CNS vasculitis, Reversible Cerebral Vasoconstriction Syndrome (RCVS), pituitary apoplexy.
* **Genetic Syndromes Associated with Berry Aneurysms**: **Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD / 多囊腎)**, **Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome Type IV (埃勒斯-當洛斯症候群)**, **Marfan Syndrome (馬凡氏症候群)**, **Coarctation of the Aorta (主動脈縮窄)**, **Fibromuscular Dysplasia (纖維肌性發育不良)**.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Hypertensive Deep Perforator Rupture
* **Lipohyalinosis (脂質透明變性)**: Chronic sheer stress from hypertension → endothelial injury → subintimal fibrinoid necrosis and lipid-laden macrophage infiltration in small perforators (100–400 $\mu$m).
* **Charcot-Bouchard Microaneurysms (夏科-布샤微動脈瘤)**: Focal smooth muscle degeneration → outpouchings at bifurcations of lenticulostriate, thalamogeniculate, and pontine penetrators → acute rupture during blood pressure surges.

### Pathogenesis of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA)
* **$\beta$-Amyloid (A$\beta$40) Deposition**: Acellular eosinophilic amyloid replaces smooth muscle cells and elastic lamina in the tunica media and adventitia of cortical and leptomeningeal arterioles.
* **Vascular Fragility**: Loss of vasoactive compliance + microaneurysmal dilatation + fibrinoid necrosis → spontaneous, recurrent **lobar ICH** and **cortical superficial siderosis (皮質表面鐵質沉著症)** (sparing deep basal ganglia/brainstem).

### Primary and Secondary Brain Injury Cascade in ICH
* **Primary Injury (0–6 hours)**:
  * **Mass Effect**: Direct mechanical disruption of neural tracts by expanding haematoma.
  * **Haematoma Expansion**: Occurs in **~30%** within first 3–6h due to continuous bleeding from primary vessel and mechanical shearing of surrounding secondary arterioles ("avalanche model").
* **Secondary Injury (Hours to Days)**:
  * **Thrombin Cascade**: Coagulation cascade activation → thrombin cleaves fibrinogen + directly activates protease-activated receptors (PAR-1) on microglia → blood-brain barrier (BBB) disruption → **vasogenic peri-haematomal oedema (血管源性血腫周圍水腫)** (peaks at day 3–5).
  * **Erythrocyte Lysis & Iron Toxicity**: RBC lysis releases haemoglobin → degraded by **Haeme Oxygenase-1 (HO-1)** into free $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ → drives **Fenton Reaction** → Hydroxyl free radicals ($\cdot\text{OH}$) → lipid peroxidation and neuronal ferroptosis.
  * **Neuroinflammation**: Damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) activate **Toll-like Receptor 4 (TLR4)** on microglia → release of **TNF-$\alpha$**, **IL-1$\beta$**, and **Matrix Metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9)** → basal lamina degradation.
* **Monro-Kellie Doctrine & Herniation**: Fixed cranial vault volume ($V_{\text{brain}} + V_{\text{CSF}} + V_{\text{blood}} = \text{Constant}$) → uncompensated haematoma + oedema → ↑ **Intracranial Pressure (ICP / 顱內壓)** → ↓ **Cerebral Perfusion Pressure ($\text{CPP} = \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}$)** → cerebral herniation (subfalcine, uncal, tonsillar).

### Pathophysiology of Aneurysmal SAH & Delayed Cerebral Ischaemia (DCI)
* **Aneurysm Formation & Rupture**: Haemodynamic turbulence at arterial bifurcations → internal elastic lamina disruption + tunica media degeneration → Laplace's Law ($T = P \times r$) drives expansion until wall tension exceeds tensile strength.
* **Early Brain Injury (0–72h)**: Sudden arterial jet into subarachnoid space → ICP spikes to approaches Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) → transient global cerebral circulatory arrest (causes initial syncope).
* **Cerebral Vasospasm & Delayed Cerebral Ischaemia (DCI / 遲發性腦缺血) (Days 3–14, peaks Day 7)**:
  * **Oxyhaemoglobin Toxicity**: Lysis of subarachnoid blood releases **oxyhaemoglobin** → scavenges endothelial **Nitric Oxide (NO)** (potent vasodilator) + stimulates **Endothelin-1** (potent vasoconstrictor) production.
  * **Calcium Influx**: Activation of voltage-gated **L-type calcium channels** on vascular smooth muscle → sustained arterial spasm + microvascular thrombosis + cortical spreading depolarisations → delayed infarction.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Clinical History in Suspected Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Symptom Onset & Trajectory**:
  * **ICH**: Sudden onset focal deficit occurring during exertion or emotional stress; characteristially shows **smooth, gradual progression over minutes to hours** (unlike ischaemic stroke which is maximal at onset).
  * **SAH**: **Thunderclap Headache (雷擊性頭痛)** — sudden, excruciating headache reaching maximal intensity (10/10) within seconds to 1 minute ("worst headache of life" / "kicked in the back of the head").
* **Sentinel Headache**: History of a milder, sudden headache 2–8 weeks prior (present in **~20%** of SAH; represents minor warning leak/sentinel bleed).
* **Symptoms of Raised ICP**: Projectile vomiting, progressive drowsiness, photophobia, diplopia.
* **Medication & Social History**: Anticoagulants (Warfarin, Apixaban, Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban, Edoxaban), antiplatelets, NSAIDs, illicit sympathomimetics, binge alcohol consumption, smoking.
* **Past Medical & Family History**: Hypertension, dementia (suggests CAA), liver disease, bleeding diathesis, family history of polycystic kidney disease or intracranial aneurysms (>2 first-degree relatives).

### General & Meningeal Physical Signs
* **Cushing's Triad (庫欣氏三徵)** (late sign of impending coning):
  * **Systemic Hypertension** (widened pulse pressure)
  * **Bradycardia**
  * **Irregular Respiration** (Cheyne-Stokes or ataxic breathing)
* **Meningism (腦膜刺激徵)** (develops 3–12h post-SAH):
  * **Nuchal Rigidity (頸部僵硬)** on passive flexion.
  * **Kernig's Sign (克尼格氏徵)**: Pain/hamstring spasm upon knee extension with hip flexed at 90 degrees.
  * **Brudzinski's Sign (布魯津斯基氏徵)**: Involuntary hip and knee flexion upon passive neck flexion.

### Neuro-Ophthalmic & Fundoscopic Signs
* **Terson Syndrome (特森氏症候群)**: Vitreous, subhyaloid, or retinal haemorrhage visible on fundoscopy in acute SAH (triggered by abrupt ICP surge transmitted down optic nerve sheath; indicates poor prognosis).
* **Papilloedema (視乳頭水腫)**: Blurred optic disc margins, loss of venous pulsations (takes hours to days to develop).
* **Isolated Third Nerve Palsy (動眼神經麻痺)**: Ptosis, "down and out" eye deviation, and **mydriasis (fixed dilated pupil / 瞳孔放大)** due to external compression of dorsal pupillomotor fibres by an expanding **Posterior Communicating Artery (PCom) aneurysm** or **uncal herniation**.
* **False Localising Abducens (CN VI) Palsy**: Failure of lateral gaze due to stretching of CN VI over the petrous temporal ridge from generalized ↑ ICP.

### Anatomical Localization of Intracerebral Haemorrhage by Physical Signs

| Anatomical Site | % of ICH | Classic Motor/Sensory Signs | Classic Neuro-Ophthalmic & Cortical Signs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Putamen / Internal Capsule (殼核/內囊)** | 35–50% | Contralateral dense hemiplegia, contralateral hemisensory loss | **Conjugate gaze deviation TOWARDS the lesion** (away from hemiparesis); aphasia (dominant) or neglect (non-dominant) |
| **Thalamus (丘腦)** | 10–15% | Contralateral hemisensory loss > motor weakness | **Forced downward and inward gaze ("peering at the tip of the nose")**, small sluggishly reactive pupils, convergence paralysis |
| **Pons (橋腦)** | 5–10% | **Quadriplegia**, decerebrate posturing, coma, hyperthermia | **Pinpoint reactive pupils (針尖樣瞳孔)**, loss of horizontal doll's eyes (oculocephalic) reflex, ocular bobbing |
| **Cerebellum (小腦)** | 5–10% | **Ipsilateral limb ataxia**, dysmetria, inability to stand/walk (**truncal ataxia**), vertigo, vomiting (NO hemiparesis initially) | Ipsilateral gaze palsy, skew deviation, nystagmus, ipsilateral sixth nerve palsy or facial weakness |
| **Lobar (Cortex) (腦葉)** | 20–25% | Focal sensorimotor deficit depending on lobe (Frontal: contralateral leg/arm; Parietal: hemisensory) | **Focal seizures** (more common than in deep ICH); Frontal: abulia; Occipital: **contralateral homonymous hemianopia** |
| **Caudate Nucleus (尾狀核)** | <5% | Mild transient contralateral hemiparesis | Contralateral conjugate gaze palsy, prominent confusion, restlessness, memory disorientation (often ruptures into ventricle) |

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnosis of Acute Intracerebral Haemorrhage vs Ischaemic Stroke vs Mimics

| Condition | Discriminating Clinical Features | Discriminating Investigation Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intracerebral Haemorrhage (腦內出血)** | Smooth progression of focal deficits over mins–hrs; prominent early headache, vomiting, ↓ consciousness; severe hypertension | Non-contrast CT (NCCT) brain: Acute **hyperdense** parenchymal lesion (60–80 Hounsfield Units) |
| **Acute Ischaemic Stroke (急性缺血性中風)** | Sudden maximal deficit at onset; step-wise or fluctuating course; history of AF/carotid stenosis; headache/vomiting less frequent | NCCT brain: Early normal or **hypodense** territory, loss of grey-white differentiation, hyperdense MCA sign |
| **Haemorrhagic Transformation of Infarct** | Initial ischaemic syndrome followed by acute clinical deterioration (especially post-tPA or thrombectomy) | Petechial or confluent hyperdensity strictly confined within a pre-existing vascular arterial infarct territory |
| **Haemorrhagic Brain Tumour** | Subacute weeks-long history of headaches, personality change, or focal seizures prior to acute deterioration | Disproportionate **vasogenic oedema (finger-like projections)**, heterogeneous density, nodular enhancement on contrast CT/MRI |
| **Todd's Paralysis / Post-Ictal State** | Witnessed convulsive activity, tongue biting, urinary incontinence; spontaneous complete resolution within 24–48h | Normal structural neuroimaging; EEG shows post-ictal slowing or epileptiform discharges |

### Differential Diagnosis of Thunderclap Headache (Suspected SAH)

| Condition | Discriminating Clinical Features | Discriminating Investigation Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (蛛網膜下腔出血)** | Thunderclap headache, meningism, syncope at onset, Terson syndrome, CN III palsy | NCCT: Hyperdense blood in basal cisterns/Sylvian fissures; LP: Uniformly blood-stained CSF + **Xanthochromia** |
| **Reversible Cerebral Vasoconstriction Syndrome (RCVS / 可逆性腦血管收縮症候群)** | **Recurrent** thunderclap headaches over days–weeks; triggered by postpartum state, vasoactive drugs (SSRIs, cannabis) | CTA/MRA: Diffuse multifocal **"string-of-beads"** segmental vasoconstriction resolving completely by 12 weeks |
| **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST / 腦靜脈竇血栓)** | Subacute or thunderclap headache, papilloedema, seizures; prothrombotic state (OCP, pregnancy, malignancy) | CT Venogram: **Empty delta sign**; NCCT: Dense triangle/cord sign, parasagittal venous haemorrhagic infarcts |
| **Cervical Artery Dissection (頸動脈或椎動脈剝離)** | Thunderclap headache or neck pain post-neck trauma/manipulation; ipsilateral **Horner's syndrome** (carotid) or **Wallenberg syndrome** (vertebral) | CTA/MRI Neck (T1 fat-suppressed): **Crescent sign** (intramural haematoma), intimal flap, flame-shaped tapering |
| **Pituitary Apoplexy (腦下垂體中風)** | Thunderclap retro-orbital headache, **bitemporal hemianopia**, ophthalmoplegia (CN III, IV, VI), acute hypotension (adrenal crisis) | CT/MRI Pituitary: Haemorrhagic/necrotic intrasellar mass compressing optic chiasm |
| **Acute Bacterial Meningitis (急性細菌性腦膜炎)** | High fever, rigors, photophobia, meningism, petechial rash; headache builds over hours rather than seconds | LP: Turbid CSF, ↑↑ polymorphs, ↓↓ glucose (<40% serum), ↑↑ protein, +ve Gram stain/PCR |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (篤手指血糖)**: Mandatory first bedside test to exclude hypoglycaemia (stroke mimic).
* **12-Lead ECG**:
  * Screen for **Atrial Fibrillation (心房顫動)** (explains anticoagulant usage).
  * **Neurogenic ECG Changes** (especially in SAH): Deep symmetric **"cerebral" T-wave inversions**, QT prolongation, ST-segment depression/elevation, U waves (catecholamine surge-mediated myocardial stunning).

### Laboratory Investigations (Bloods)
* **Complete Blood Picture (CBP)**: Check platelet count (exclude severe thrombocytopenia <50 $\times 10^9$/L).
* **Coagulation Profile**: **PT/INR** (crucial for Warfarin), **aPTT** (crucial for Dabigatran/heparin), fibrinogen; **Thrombin Time (TT)** or specific anti-FXa levels if available.
* **Renal & Liver Function Tests (RFT/LFT)**: Assess uraemic platelet dysfunction, creatinine clearance (CrCl) for contrast safety/DOAC clearance, and hepatic synthetic dysfunction (cirrhotic coagulopathy).
* **Serum Electrolytes & Osmolality**: Screen for hyponatraemia in SAH (**SIADH** vs **Cerebral Salt Wasting [CSW / 腦耗鹽症候群]**).
* **Cardiac Troponin**: Often elevated in acute SAH due to neurogenic stunned myocardium.
* **Type & Screen / Crossmatch**: Prepare for potential neurosurgical evacuation or platelet/FFP transfusion.
* **Urine Toxicology Screen**: Exclude cocaine or amphetamine-induced haemorrhage in young patients (<50y).

### First-Line Neuroimaging: Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Brain
* **Diagnostic Gold Standard for Acute Haemorrhage**: Acute clot appears **hyperdense** (white) due to high globin protein content.
* **ICH Volume Estimation — $\text{ABC}/2$ Formula**:
  $$\text{Volume (mL)} = \frac{A \times B \times C}{2}$$
  * **$A$**: Largest diameter of haematoma on the CT slice with maximum area (cm).
  * **$B$**: Largest diameter perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to $A$ on the same slice (cm).
  * **$C$**: Number of CT slices with visible haemorrhage $\times$ slice thickness (cm).
  * *Clinical Cut-off*: Volume **>30 mL** (supratentorial) or diameter **>3 cm** (cerebellar) correlates strongly with herniation and high mortality.
* **NCCT Sensitivity in SAH**:
  * **Within 6 hours of headache onset**: **~98–100%** sensitive (if performed on modern 3rd-generation multidetector CT and interpreted by an experienced neuroradiologist).
  * **At 24 hours**: Drops to **~93%**.
  * **At 5–7 days**: Drops to **~50–85%** as blood is diluted and washed out by CSF turnover.
* **Characteristic SAH Blood Distribution on NCCT**:
  * **ACom Aneurysm**: Anterior interhemispheric fissure and septum pellucidum.
  * **MCA Aneurysm**: Ipsilateral Sylvian fissure (often with temporal lobe parenchymal extension).
  * **PCom / Basilar Aneurysm**: Basal suprasellar cisterns and interpeduncular cistern.

### Second-Line & Vascular Neuroimaging
* **CT Angiography (CTA) of Brain & Neck**:
  * Indicated immediately in all spontaneous SAH (identifies berry aneurysms $\ge 2\text{ mm}$ with **>95%** sensitivity).
  * Indicated in ICH if patient is young (<65y), normotensive, has **lobar haemorrhage**, isolated IVH, or suspected underlying vascular malformation.
  * **CTA "Spot Sign" (斑點徵)**: Punctate focus of contrast extravasation within an acute parenchymal haematoma → independent predictor of ongoing **haematoma expansion** and poor outcome.
* **MRI Brain with Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI) / Gradient Echo (T2\* GRE)**:
  * Performed subacutely (or acutely if stable) to detect **cerebral microbleeds (腦微出血)** (haemosiderin deposits appear as "blooming" black hypointense dots).
  * **Deep Microbleeds** (basal ganglia, thalamus, brainstem) → diagnostic of **Hypertensive Microangiopathy**.
  * **Strictly Lobar Microbleeds** + **Cortical Superficial Siderosis** → diagnostic of **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA)** (per **Boston Criteria v2.0**).
* **Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA / 數碼減影血管造影)**:
  * **Invasive Gold Standard** for identifying occult aneurysms, small AVMs, dural AV fistulae, and characterizing aneurysm neck-to-dome ratio prior to endovascular coiling.
  * If initial CTA and DSA are negative in non-perimesencephalic SAH, **repeat DSA at 1–2 weeks** (initial spasm or thrombosis may mask the aneurysm in 10–15% of cases).
* **Transcranial Doppler (TCD) Ultrasound**:
  * Non-invasive bedside monitoring for **cerebral vasospasm** post-SAH (performed daily from Day 3 to Day 14).
  * **Diagnostic Threshold**: Mean MCA flow velocity **>120 cm/s** (or >200 cm/s for severe spasm) or **Lindegaard Ratio** ($\text{MCA velocity} / \text{Extracranial ICA velocity}$) **>3**.

### Lumbar Puncture (LP / 腰椎穿刺) Protocol in Suspected SAH
* **Strict Indication**: Suspect SAH with a **negative NCCT brain** (especially if presenting >6 hours after headache onset).
* **Timing Rule**: Must delay LP until **at least 12 hours after headache onset** to allow sufficient time for lysed RBC haemoglobin to degrade into bilirubin in vivo (distinguishes true SAH from a **traumatic tap**).
* **Interpretation of CSF in SAH**:
  * **Opening Pressure**: Elevated (>20 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$).
  * **Four-Tube Method**: Unchanged RBC count from Tube 1 to Tube 4 (whereas a traumatic tap demonstrates progressive clearing of RBCs across sequential tubes, though this is not 100% reliable alone).
  * **Xanthochromia (腦脊液黃變)**: Yellowish discoloration of centrifuged CSF supernatant caused by **bilirubin**; gold standard confirmation is **CSF Spectrophotometry** showing an oxyhaemoglobin and **bilirubin absorption peak at 450–460 nm** (sensitivity >98% between 12h and 2 weeks).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Immediate ABCDE Stabilization & Neuro-Protection
* **Airway & Breathing**: Endotracheal intubation with rapid sequence induction (avoiding surges in ICP/BP using fentanyl/propofol) if **GCS $\le 8$**, bulbar dysfunction, loss of protective airway reflexes, or neurogenic pulmonary oedema. Target $\text{SpO}_2 \ge 94\%$ and normocapnia ($\text{PaCO}_2\text{ 35–40 mmHg}$).
* **General Neuro-Protective Nursing Care**:
  * Nurse head-of-bed elevated at **$30^\circ$** with neck in neutral midline alignment (promotes jugular venous drainage to ↓ ICP).
  * Maintain strict **normoglycaemia** (blood glucose 6–10 mmol/L) and **normothermia** (treat fever $>37.5^\circ\text{C}$ aggressively with IV paracetamol/cooling blankets).
  * Strict bed rest in a quiet, dimly lit room; prescribe stool softeners (**Lactulose** or **Senna**) and antiemetics (**Ondansetron**) to prevent Valsalva-induced ICP spikes/rebleeding.
  * **Seizure Management**: Treat clinical or electrographic seizures immediately with IV **Lorazepam** followed by IV **Levetiracetam (左乙拉西坦)** loading; routine prophylactic anticonvulsants are **not recommended** in ICH without clinical seizures.

### Acute Blood Pressure Control in Intracerebral Haemorrhage (ICH)
* **Pathophysiological Rationale**: Rapid BP reduction prevents **haematoma expansion** without inducing peri-haematomal ischaemia (INTERACT2 and ATACH-2 trials).
* **Target Blood Pressure Protocol (HA / AHA Guidelines)**:
  * **Presenting SBP 150–220 mmHg**: Smoothly lower SBP to a target of **140 mmHg** (acceptable range **130–150 mmHg**) within **1 hour** of presentation.
  * **Presenting SBP >220 mmHg**: Aggressive continuous IV infusion reduction coupled with arterial line monitoring; avoid dropping SBP by **>60 mmHg within 1 hour** or dropping below **<130 mmHg** (associated with acute kidney injury and neurological worsening in ATACH-2).
* **First-Line IV Antihypertensive Agents in HK Hospital Authority**:
  * **IV Labetalol (拉貝洛爾)**: Combined $\alpha_1$ and $\beta$-blocker; **10–20 mg IV bolus over 2 mins** (may repeat every 10–15 mins) or continuous IV infusion **2–8 mg/min** (Contraindicated in asthma, severe bradycardia, heart block, decompensated heart failure).
  * **IV Nicardipine (尼卡地平)**: Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker; continuous IV infusion starting at **5 mg/h**, titrating by 2.5 mg/h every 5–15 mins up to maximum **15 mg/h** (preferred in asthmatics or bradycardic patients).
  * **IV Hydralazine**: 5–10 mg IV bolus every 20 mins (second-line).
  * *Contraindicated*: Avoid **IV Sodium Nitroprusside** or **GTN** when possible as cerebral venodilation directly increases cerebral blood volume and ↑ ICP.

### Emergency Reversal of Antithrombotic-Associated ICH

| Antithrombotic Agent | Specific Reversal Agent & Protocol (HA Formulary) | Mechanism & Key Clinical Pearls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Warfarin (華法林)** | 1. **4-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (4F-PCC / 凝血酶原複合濃縮物)** **25–50 IU/kg IV** immediately<br>2. **Vitamin K1 (Phytomenadione)** **10 mg slow IV** over 10–20 mins | PCC replenishes Factors II, VII, IX, X within **15 mins** (Target INR **<1.3**); Vitamin K1 takes 6–24h for hepatic synthesis but sustains reversal. Give **FFP (15–20 mL/kg)** ONLY if PCC is unavailable. |
| **Dabigatran (達比加群)** (Direct Thrombin Inhibitor) | **Idarucizumab (依達賽珠單抗 / Praxbind)** **5 g IV** (given as two consecutive 2.5 g boluses over 5–10 mins) | Humanized monoclonal antibody fragment binding Dabigatran with 350$\times$ higher affinity than thrombin. | **Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Edoxaban** (Factor Xa Inhibitors) | 1. **4-Factor PCC 50 IU/kg IV** (Standard practice in HK HA)<br>2. **Andexanet alfa** (if specifically available) |Andexanet alfa is a recombinant modified decoy FXa protein; 4F-PCC is universally accessible in HK acute hospitals for rapid haemostasis. | **Unfractionated Heparin (UFH)** | **Protamine Sulfate (魚精蛋白)** **1 mg IV per 100 units of UFH** given in the preceding 2–3 hours (Max dose 50 mg over 10 mins) | Positively charged peptide binds negatively charged heparin to form inactive salt. Monitor for anaphylaxis and hypotension. |

| **Antiplatelets (Aspirin, Clopidogrel, Ticagrelor)** | **Stop drug immediately**. **Do NOT transfuse platelets routinely** unless patient is scheduled for emergency neurosurgery or has severe thrombocytopenia. | **PATCH Trial**: Routine platelet transfusion in spontaneous ICH on antiplatelets without surgery **increases mortality and dependence**. Consider **DDAVP (Desmopressin) 0.4 mcg/kg IV** $\times$ 1 dose to release endothelial vWF. |

### Medical Management of Acutely Raised Intracranial Pressure (ICP)
* **Osmotherapy (Transient Temporizing Bridge to Surgery)**:
  * **IV Mannitol (甘露醇) 20%**: **0.5–1.0 g/kg IV bolus** over 15–20 mins, followed by 0.25–0.5 g/kg q6h PRN. Monitor serum osmolality (withhold if osmolality **>320 mOsm/kg** or osmolal gap >10 to prevent acute tubular necrosis).
  * **Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl)**: Bolus 150–250 mL over 20 mins (preferred over Mannitol if patient is hypotensive or hypovolaemic; monitor serum $\text{Na}^+$ to keep <155–160 mmol/L).
* **Controlled Hyperventilation**:
  * Mechanical hyperventilation targeting $\text{PaCO}_2\text{ 30–35 mmHg}$ induces rapid cerebral vasoconstriction → ↓ cerebral blood volume → ↓ ICP within minutes.
  * Used **strictly for <2–4 hours** during acute herniation while wheeling patient to the operating theatre (prolonged hyperventilation causes rebound cerebral ischaemia).
* **Corticosteroids**: **Strictly Contraindicated** in both ICH and SAH (provides no benefit for cytotoxic/clot oedema and increases infection/hyperglycaemia mortality).

### Neurosurgical Indications in Intracerebral Haemorrhage (ICH)
* **Cerebellar Haemorrhage (High Yield Emergency Indication)**:
  * **Mandatory Urgent Posterior Fossa Decompression / Suboccipital Craniectomy** if haematoma diameter is **>3 cm**, OR if there is **brainstem compression**, OR **obstructive hydrocephalus** due to 4th ventricular effacement (medical management alone carries near-100% mortality).
  * *Crucial Rule*: **Never insert an External Ventricular Drain (EVD) alone** for cerebellar ICH without posterior fossa decompression → risks **upward transtentorial herniation** of the cerebellum through the tentorial notch.
* **Intraventricular Haemorrhage (IVH) with Acute Hydrocephalus**:
  * Insert **External Ventricular Drain (EVD / 腦室外引流)** in patients with radiographic hydrocephalus and decreased consciousness (GCS $\le 8$).
* **Supratentorial Haemorrhage**:
  * Routine early craniotomy for deep basal ganglia/thalamic ICH does not improve outcomes (**STICH I** and **STICH II** trials).
  * **Rescue Decompressive Hemicraniectomy** (with or without clot evacuation) or **Minimally Invasive Surgery** is indicated for:
    * **Superficial Lobar ICH** within **1 cm** of the cortical surface (volume 30–100 mL) showing progressive neurological deterioration.
    * Impending uncal/transtentorial herniation or refractory ICP despite maximal medical therapy in salvageable patients (typically GCS 6–12).

### Acute Specific Management of Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH)
* **Pre-Securement Blood Pressure Control**:
  * Maintain **SBP <160 mmHg** (and MAP <110 mmHg) using IV **Labetalol** or **Nicardipine** to minimize the transmural pressure gradient across the fragile aneurysm dome and prevent catastrophic **rebleeding** prior to coiling/clipping.
* **Prevention of Delayed Cerebral Ischaemia (DCI) — Nimodipine Protocol**:
  * **Nimodipine (尼莫地平)**: **60 mg PO or via NGT every 4 hours for 21 days** (must initiate within 48–96 hours of onset).
  * *Mechanism*: Lipophilic dihydropyridine L-type calcium channel blocker that crosses the BBB; reduces poor neurological outcomes and cerebral infarction by **~34%** via neuroprotection and microcirculatory dilation (even without visibly reversing angiographic large-vessel spasm).
  * *Dose Adjustment*: If oral Nimodipine causes systemic hypotension, change dosing to **30 mg PO every 2 hours**.
* **Definitive Aneurysm Securement (Within 24–72 Hours)**:
  * **Endovascular Coiling (血管內彈簧圈栓塞術)** vs **Microsurgical Clipping (開顱動脈瘤夾閉術)**:

| Feature | Endovascular Coiling (Preferred per ISAT Trial) | Microsurgical Clipping |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Indications** | **Posterior circulation aneurysms** (basilar tip, vertebrobasilar), elderly patients (>70y), high-grade poor-condition SAH (WFNS IV–V), narrow aneurysm neck (**dome-to-neck ratio >2**) | **Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) aneurysms**, wide-necked aneurysms (**neck >4 mm**), giant aneurysms (>25 mm), young patients (<40y), **concomitant large intraparenchymal haematoma** requiring evacuation |
| **Pros & Cons** | **7% absolute risk reduction** in death/dependency at 1 year (ISAT); minimally invasive; slightly higher long-term recanalization/retreatment rate | Superior permanent obliteration durability; allows simultaneous clot removal; higher initial procedural morbidity and post-op seizure risk |

* **Management of Symptomatic Cerebral Vasospasm / DCI**:
  * **Euvolaemia & Induced Hypertension**: Maintain strict **euvolaemia** with isotonic **0.9% Normal Saline** and induce controlled hypertension (using IV **Noradrenaline** or **Phenylephrine** to raise SBP to 160–200 mmHg, **ONLY AFTER the aneurysm has been secured**).
  * *Outdated Practice*: Historical "Triple-H Therapy" (Hypervolaemia, Haemodilution, Hypertension) is **abandoned** due to pulmonary oedema and myocardial ischaemia risks.
  * **Endovascular Rescue**: Intra-arterial vasodilator infusion (**Verapamil**, **Nimodipine**, or **Milrinone**) or **Percutaneous Transluminal Balloon Angioplasty** for refractory focal vasospasm.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Long-Term Secondary Stroke Prevention & Blood Pressure Targets
* **Chronic Blood Pressure Target**: Strictly maintain clinic BP **<130/80 mmHg** long-term (reduces recurrent ICH by **~50%**, especially crucial in CAA).
* **Step-Up Antihypertensive Pharmacotherapy**:
  * **First-Line**: **ACE Inhibitor (Perindopril / Lisinopril)** or **ARB (Losartan / Valsartan)** + **Calcium Channel Blocker (Amlodipine)** or **Thiazide-like Diuretic (Indapamide)**.
* **Statin Therapy Controversies in ICH**:
  * High-dose statins (e.g., Atorvastatin 80 mg in SPARCL trial) showed a slight increase in recurrent haemorrhagic stroke, particularly in **Lobar ICH / CAA**.
  * *Clinical Rule*: Avoid initiating de novo high-intensity statins in isolated **CAA/lobar ICH** unless there is a compelling atherosclerotic indication (e.g., prior MI, coronary stent). Continue statins in deep hypertensive ICH if high cardiovascular risk exists.

### Resumption of Antithrombotic Therapy After Intracerebral Haemorrhage
* **Antiplatelet Resumption**:
  * Based on the **RESTART Trial**, resuming single antiplatelet therapy (e.g., **Aspirin** or **Clopidogrel**) in patients with strong occlusive vascular disease indications (coronary stent, prior ischaemic stroke) does **not** significantly increase recurrent ICH and may reduce major vascular events; typically resumed **2–4 weeks** post-ICH after confirming stability.
* **Oral Anticoagulation Resumption (e.g., for Atrial Fibrillation)**:
  * **High Recurrence Risk (Avoid Anticoagulation)**: **Lobar ICH**, diagnosed **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy**, multiple cortical microbleeds on MRI SWI, or cortical superficial siderosis → refer for **Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion (LAAO / 左心耳封堵術)** (e.g., Watchman device).
  * **Acceptable Recurrence Risk (Consider Resumption)**: Deep hypertensive ICH (basal ganglia/thalamus) with well-controlled BP and high $\text{CHA}_2\text{DS}_2\text{-VASc}$ score ($\ge 4$) or mechanical prosthetic heart valve.
  * **Choice & Timing**: Prefer **DOACs** (especially **Apixaban** or **Dabigatran**, which carry ~50% lower intracranial bleeding risk than Warfarin); typically resume **4–8 weeks** post-ICH (earlier at 7–14 days only for mechanical mitral valves with high thrombosis risk).

### Neuro-Rehabilitation & Lifestyle Modification
* **Multidisciplinary Stroke Unit Rehabilitation**:
  * **Physiotherapy (PT)**: Early mobilization (once haemodynamically and neurologically stable after 24–48h; avoid ultra-early intensive mobilization <24h per **AVERT Trial** as it worsens outcomes in ICH), spasticity management (intramuscular **Botulinum Toxin A** or oral **Baclofen**).
  * **Speech Therapy (ST)**: Bedside swallowing screening prior to any oral intake to prevent **aspiration pneumonia**; nasogastric tube feeding if dysphagic.
  * **Occupational Therapy (OT)**: Cognitive assessment, home modification, activities of daily living (ADL) retraining.
* **DVT Prophylaxis**: Apply **Intermittent Pneumatic Compression (IPC) stockings** immediately upon admission; initiate subcutaneous **Low Molecular Weight Heparin (Enoxaparin)** prophylaxis **48–72 hours** after onset ONLY after repeat CT confirms cessation of haematoma expansion.
* **Lifestyle & Screening**:
  * Mandatory **smoking cessation** and **alcohol moderation** (critical to prevent de novo aneurysm formation/rupture).
  * **Familial Screening with MRA/CTA**: Indicated in patients with **$\ge 2$ first-degree relatives** with aneurysmal SAH or patients with **ADPKD**.

### Overall Prognosis of Haemorrhagic Stroke
* **Intracerebral Haemorrhage**: 30-day mortality is **~40%** (half of all deaths occur within the first 48 hours); only **20%** of survivors regain functional independence at 6 months.
* **Subarachnoid Haemorrhage ("Rule of Thirds")**:
  * **~1/3 die** (10–15% die pre-hospital before medical attention).
  * **~1/3 survive with permanent neurological or cognitive disability**.
  * **~1/3 recover to functional independence** (though neurocognitive fatigue and mood disorders are common).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Neurological Complications of SAH & ICH (The "3 R's" of SAH)
* **Rebleeding (再出血)**:
  * Highest risk within the **first 24 hours** (especially first 6 hours: ~4–15% risk); carries a **>70% mortality rate**.
  * Prevented by acute BP control (SBP <160 mmHg) and early coiling/clipping (<24–72h).
* **Delayed Cerebral Ischaemia (DCI) / Cerebral Vasospasm (腦血管痙攣)**:
  * Occurs between **Days 3 and 14** (peaks at **Day 7**); manifests as new focal neurological deficits or an unexplained $\ge 2$-point drop in GCS after excluding hydrocephalus, rebleeding, and hyponatraemia.
* **Hydrocephalus (腦積水)**:
  * **Acute Obstructive (Non-Communicating) Hydrocephalus** (0–3 days): Blood clot mechanically blocks the **Aqueduct of Sylvius** or fourth ventricular outlets → treated with emergency **EVD**.
  * **Chronic Communicating Hydrocephalus** (Weeks to months): Fibrosis and scarring of **arachnoid granulations** impair CSF reabsorption; presents with **Hakim's Triad** (gait apraxia/magnetic gait, urinary incontinence, progressive cognitive decline) → treated with permanent **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt (腦室腹腔分流術)**.
* **Post-Stroke Epilepsy**: Higher incidence in **lobar ICH** and post-surgical clipping of MCA aneurysms.

### Systemic & End-Organ Complications (Cardiopulmonary & Renal/Electrolyte)
* **Hyponatraemia in SAH — Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW) vs SIADH**:

| Parameter | Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW / 腦耗鹽症候群) | SIADH (抗利尿激素分泌不當症候群) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathophysiology** | Brain injury releases **Brain Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)** → renal tubular sodium & water loss | Hypothalamic irritation → autonomous excessive **ADH (Vasopressin)** release → pure water retention |
| **Intravascular Volume Status** | **Hypovolaemic** (orthostatic hypotension, tachycardia, dry mucous membranes, ↓ CVP, ↑ haematocrit/urea) | **Euvolaemic** (normal BP, normal skin turgor, low-normal serum urea and uric acid) |
| **Serum & Urine Biochemistry** | Serum $\text{Na}^+ <135\text{ mmol/L}$, Urine Osmolality $>100\text{ mOsm/kg}$, Urine $\text{Na}^+ \gg 40\text{ mmol/L}$ | Serum $\text{Na}^+ <135\text{ mmol/L}$, Urine Osmolality $>100\text{ mOsm/kg}$, Urine $\text{Na}^+ >20\text{–}40\text{ mmol/L}$ |
| **Management Protocol** | **Aggressive Volume & Sodium Replacement** (0.9% Normal Saline or 3% Hypertonic Saline) + **Fludrocortisone** (0.1–0.2 mg PO BD) | **Fluid Restriction IS CONTRAINDICATED in acute SAH** (precipitates vasospasm/DCI); treat with hypertonic saline or fludrocortisone |

* **Neurogenic Stunned Myocardium / Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy (章魚壺心肌病)**:
  * Massive hypothalamic sympathetic catecholamine storm → apical ballooning of left ventricle, transient LV systolic dysfunction, pulmonary oedema, troponin elevation, and ventricular arrhythmias.
* **Neurogenic Pulmonary Oedema**: Sympathetic surge increases pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure and endothelial permeability.
* **Immobilization Complications**: Aspiration pneumonia, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism, pressure ulcers, contractures.

### Adverse Effects of Key Pharmacotherapies
* **4-Factor PCC**: Arterial and venous thromboembolism (stroke, MI, PE, DIC).
* **Mannitol**: Rebound intracranial hypertension (if BBB is disrupted and mannitol leaks into brain parenchyma), fluid overload followed by hypovolaemia, hypernatraemia, **osmotic nephrosis / acute kidney injury**.
* **Nimodipine**: Systemic hypotension (which can paradoxically drop CPP), flushing, peripheral oedema, reflex tachycardia.
* **Labetalol**: Bronchospasm, severe bradycardia, atrioventricular block, masking of hypoglycaemic tachycardia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why is a cerebellar haemorrhage of 3 cm considered an absolute neurosurgical emergency even if the patient is currently GCS 15? Answer: The posterior fossa is a tight, non-compliant compartment housing the brainstem and fourth ventricle; rapid peri-haematomal oedema can cause sudden fourth ventricular obstruction (acute hydrocephalus) and direct brainstem compression, progressing from alertness to fatal tonsillar herniation and respiratory arrest within hours.
* Trap: Treating hyponatraemia in a patient Day 6 post-aneurysmal SAH with strict fluid restriction because you suspect SIADH. Pearl: Never fluid-restrict a patient in the acute phase of SAH regardless of whether the cause is SIADH or Cerebral Salt Wasting, as intravascular volume depletion markedly accelerates cerebral vasospasm and Delayed Cerebral Ischaemia (DCI); always maintain euvolaemia with isotonic or hypertonic saline and add fludrocortisone if needed.
* Pitfall: Performing an emergency lumbar puncture 3 hours after the onset of a thunderclap headache when the non-contrast CT brain is normal. If performed before 12 hours, red blood cells have had insufficient time to lyse and convert into bilirubin in vivo, rendering CSF xanthochromia spectrophotometry falsely negative and making it impossible to reliably distinguish true SAH from a traumatic tap.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically distinguish a diabetic third nerve palsy from a Posterior Communicating Artery (PCom) aneurysm third nerve palsy? Answer: The parasympathetic pupillomotor fibres travel superficially in the dorsomedial aspect of CN III and receive pial blood supply; therefore, extrinsic mechanical compression by an expanding PCom aneurysm causes a "surgical" CN III palsy with a fixed dilated pupil (mydriasis), whereas microvascular ischaemia from diabetes affects the deep vasa nervorum, causing a "medical" pupil-sparing CN III palsy.
* Trap: Ordering routine platelet transfusions for a patient with a spontaneous basal ganglia haemorrhage who takes daily aspirin or clopidogrel and is being managed conservatively. Pearl: The PATCH trial demonstrated that platelet transfusion in non-surgical antiplatelet-associated ICH significantly increases the risk of death and severe dependence; platelets should only be transfused if the patient is undergoing an emergency neurosurgical procedure.
* Pitfall: Placing an External Ventricular Drain (EVD) in isolation for a large cerebellar haemorrhage with obstructive hydrocephalus without performing a suboccipital decompressive craniectomy. Draining supratentorial CSF relieves pressure ONLY above the tentorium, creating a lethal pressure gradient that sucks the swollen cerebellum upward through the tentorial incisura (upward transtentorial herniation), crushing the midbrain.
* Viva Question: What is the underlying pathology of recurrent lobar intracerebral haemorrhages in an normotensive 78-year-old patient, and what MRI sequence confirms the diagnosis in vivo? Answer: The diagnosis is Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA) caused by beta-amyloid deposition in the media and adventitia of cortical and leptomeningeal arterioles; Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI) or T2*-weighted Gradient Echo (GRE) MRI confirms probable CAA by showing multiple strictly lobar cerebral microbleeds and cortical superficial siderosis while sparing the basal ganglia and pons.
* Trap: Administering Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) as the primary reversal agent for life-threatening Warfarin-associated ICH. Pearl: 4-factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (4F-PCC) combined with IV Vitamin K1 is the mandatory gold standard because PCC normalizes the INR within 15 minutes without requiring ABO crossmatching or large fluid volumes, whereas FFP takes hours to thaw and infuse and frequently causes transfusion-associated circulatory overload (TACO).
