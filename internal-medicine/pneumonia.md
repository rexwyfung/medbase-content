# Pneumonia & Lung Abscess (肺炎及肺膿腫)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Pneumonia & Lung Abscess
* **Pneumonia (肺炎):** Acute inflammation of the lung parenchyma, typically infectious, leading to alveolar consolidation.
* **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):** Acquired outside hospital or within <48 hours of admission.
* **Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP):** Occurring >48 hours after hospital admission.
* **Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP):** Occurring >48 hours after endotracheal intubation.
* **Lung Abscess (肺膿腫):** Localized collection of pus/necrotic tissue within the lung parenchyma, forming a thick-walled cavity with an air-fluid level, usually >2cm.

### Common Bacterial Etiologies in Hong Kong
* **Typical CAP:** *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (most common), *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*.
* **Atypical CAP:** *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* (young adults), *Chlamydophila pneumoniae*, *Legionella pneumophila* (AC cooling towers).
* **HAP/VAP:** *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, Methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA), *Acinetobacter baumannii*.
* **Klebsiella pneumoniae (克雷伯氏肺炎桿菌):** Highly prevalent in HK among diabetics and chronic alcoholics; classically causes upper lobe cavitation and "red currant jelly" sputum.

### Etiologies of Lung Abscess & Aspiration
* **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Polymicrobial, dominated by oral anaerobes (*Peptostreptococcus*, *Fusobacterium*, *Bacteroides*) + aerobes.
* **Post-infectious Necrotizing:** *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas*.
* **Hong Kong Specifics:** *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (結核分枝桿菌) must ALWAYS be considered for any cavitary lung lesion in HK.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathogenesis of Lobar Pneumonia
* **Congestion (Days 1-2):** Vasodilation, alveolar exudate containing protein-rich fluid and bacteria.
* **Red Hepatization (Days 3-4):** Massive exudation of RBCs, neutrophils, and fibrin into alveoli; lung resembles liver tissue.
* **Grey Hepatization (Days 5-7):** Disintegration of RBCs; persistent fibrinosuppurative exudate (macrophages and neutrophils).
* **Resolution (Day 8+):** Macrophages clear debris; exudate digested by enzymes, restoring alveolar architecture.

### Pathophysiology of Lung Abscess
* **Primary Abscess (Aspiration):** Inhalation of oropharyngeal secretions (altered sensorium, bulbar palsy) → bacterial colonization in dependent segments (Right lower lobe superior segment > Right upper lobe posterior segment) → intense suppurative inflammation → liquefactive necrosis → rupture into bronchus → formation of air-fluid level.
* **Secondary Abscess:** Bronchial obstruction (e.g., bronchogenic carcinoma, foreign body) → post-obstructive stasis → secondary infection and necrosis.
* **Hematogenous Spread:** Septic emboli (e.g., Right-sided Infective Endocarditis affecting tricuspid valve) → multiple bilateral lung abscesses.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Patient History & Symptomatology
* **Classic CAP Symptoms:** Acute onset fever, rigors, productive cough, purulent/rust-colored sputum, dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain.
* **Atypical Pneumonia Features:** Gradual onset, dry/hacking cough, extra-pulmonary symptoms (headache, myalgia, diarrhea).
* **Lung Abscess Features:** Insidious onset, swinging fevers, night sweats, weight loss, large volume foul-smelling (putrid) sputum, hemoptysis.
* **Aspiration Risk Factors:** Stroke (CVA), Parkinson's disease, alcoholism, seizures, recent general anesthesia, poor dentition.

### General Physical Examination Findings
* **Vitals:** Tachypnea (RR >20), tachycardia (HR >100), pyrexia (fever), hypotension (if septic).
* **Inspection:** Central cyanosis (hypoxia), altered mental status (especially elderly).
* **Hands:** Finger clubbing (杵狀指) (ONLY in chronic lung abscess or bronchiectasis, absent in acute pneumonia).
* **Facies:** Herpes labialis (cold sores) classically associated with severe *Streptococcal* pneumonia.

### Respiratory System Findings (Signs of Consolidation)
* **Inspection:** ↓ unilateral chest expansion.
* **Palpation:** ↑ tactile vocal fremitus over consolidated lobe.
* **Percussion:** Stony dull (if parapneumonic effusion) or dull percussion note (consolidation).
* **Auscultation:** Bronchial breath sounds, coarse late-inspiratory crepitations (crackles), ↑ vocal resonance, whispering pectoriloquy.
* **Lung Abscess Specifics:** Cavernous/amphoric breathing over large superficial cavities, succussion splash (rarely elicited).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Discriminatory Table for Acute Respiratory Presentations
| Condition | Differentiating Clinical & Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pneumonia (肺炎)** | Fever, purulent sputum, localized consolidative signs, focal crackles. |
| **Acute Bronchitis** | Usually viral, low-grade fever, wheeze, clear/mucosal sputum, diffuse rhonchi, NORMAL chest expansion/percussion. |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | Sudden pleuritic pain, hemoptysis, calf swelling (DVT), out of proportion tachycardia/hypoxia, normal breath sounds. |
| **Acute Heart Failure** | Orthopnea, PND, bilateral basal fine crackles, raised JVP, S3 gallop, bilateral ankle edema. |
| **Pulmonary TB** | Chronic cough >3 weeks, weight loss, night sweats, apical signs, apical cavitation. |
| **Bronchogenic Carcinoma**| Smoker, progressive weight loss, hemoptysis, painless supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, digital clubbing. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Microbiological Tests
* **Sputum Analysis:** Gram stain and culture (needs adequate sample: <10 squamous epithelial cells, >25 PMNs per LPF).
* **Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB) Smear & Culture:** 3 consecutive morning sputa (mandatory in HK to rule out TB for cavitary lesions).
* **Urine Antigens:** *Legionella pneumophila* serogroup 1 Ag, *Streptococcus pneumoniae* Ag.
* **Nasopharyngeal Swab:** Multiplex PCR for Influenza A/B, COVID-19, RSV, Mycoplasma.

### Blood Tests & Biomarkers
* **CBC:** Leukocytosis (Neutrophilia in bacterial, Lymphopenia in viral/atypical).
* **Inflammatory Markers:** ↑ CRP, ↑ Procalcitonin (PCT) (higher specificity for bacterial infection).
* **Renal & Liver Function:** U&E (hyponatremia in *Legionella*), LFT (deranged in atypical pneumonias).
* **Blood Cultures:** 2 sets taken prior to antibiotic initiation.
* **ABG:** Type 1 respiratory failure (↓ PaO2, normal/↓ PaCO2) or Type 2 in COPD exhaust.

### Radiological Imaging
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR) (胸部X光):** 
  * **CAP:** Lobar or patchy consolidation, air bronchograms, silhouette sign (e.g., loss of right heart border = Right Middle Lobe consolidation).
  * **Abscess:** Thick-walled spherical radiolucency with air-fluid level.
* **CT Thorax (Plain/Contrast):** Indicated for non-resolving pneumonia, defining complex abscess architecture, detecting underlying malignancy (post-obstructive), or loculated empyema.

### Special / Invasive Diagnostics
* **Pleural Fluid Aspiration:** For suspected parapneumonic effusion (send for pH, protein, LDH, glucose, Gram stain, culture, AFB, cytology).
* **Flexible Bronchoscopy (支氣管鏡):** Indicated if suspecting foreign body, endobronchial tumor, or to obtain Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL) for culture in non-responding/immunocompromised patients.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Resuscitation & Oxygenation Protocol
* **Airway/Breathing:** Titrate O2 to target SpO2 94-98% (or 88-92% in patients with COPD/Type 2 RF risk). Use Venturi masks or High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC).
* **Circulation:** IV fluid resuscitation (crystalloids 30mL/kg) for sepsis/hypotension. Vasopressors (Norepinephrine) if fluid non-responsive.

### Empiric Antibiotic Therapy Algorithms (HK HA Guidelines)
* **Mild/Moderate CAP (Ward):**
  * PO/IV Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) ± PO Macrolide (Clarithromycin) for atypical coverage.
  * *Penicillin Allergic:* PO/IV Levofloxacin (monotherapy) or IV Ceftriaxone + Macrolide (if non-anaphylactic pen-allergy).
* **Severe CAP (ICU/High Dependency):**
  * IV Ceftriaxone/Cefotaxime + IV Macrolide (Clarithromycin/Azithromycin) OR IV Levofloxacin.
* **Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP/VAP):**
  * IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin) OR IV Cefepime/Ceftazidime (covers *Pseudomonas*).
  * Add IV Vancomycin or Linezolid if high MRSA risk.
* **Lung Abscess / Aspiration Pneumonia:**
  * IV Amoxicillin-Clavulanate OR IV Ceftriaxone + IV Metronidazole (essential for anaerobic coverage).

### Acute Management of Parapneumonic Effusion / Empyema
* **Thoracentesis/Chest Tube:** Insert intercostal drain if fluid pH < 7.2, frank pus, or positive Gram stain (indicates complicated effusion/empyema requiring drainage).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Antibiotic Step-Down & Duration Algorithm
* **Step-Down Criteria:** Switch IV to PO when hemodynamically stable, improving clinically, afebrile for >24 hours, and able to tolerate oral intake.
* **Duration for Uncomplicated CAP:** 5-7 days.
* **Duration for Lung Abscess:** Prolonged course; 4-6 weeks total (IV until clinically improved, then PO Augmentin or Clindamycin) until radiographic resolution or small stable scar on CXR.

### Postural Drainage & Physiotherapy
* **Chest Physiotherapy:** Crucial for lung abscess and bronchiectasis to facilitate sputum clearance (percussion, vibration, dependent positioning).

### Secondary Prevention & Outpatient Follow-up
* **Pneumococcal Vaccination:** 
  * PCV15/20 or sequential PCV13 followed by PPSV23 for adults ≥65 years or high-risk patients (DM, asplenia, chronic lung/heart/renal/liver disease).
* **Influenza Vaccination:** Annual seasonal quadrivalent vaccine.
* **Smoking Cessation:** Nicotine replacement therapy, varenicline, counseling.
* **CXR Follow-up:** Mandatory repeat CXR at 6-8 weeks post-discharge to confirm resolution and rule out underlying bronchial carcinoma (especially in smokers >40 years old).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### CURB-65 Score for CAP Mortality Risk
| Criterion | Definition | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **C**onfusion | AMTS <= 8 or new disorientation | 1 |
| **U**rea | Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) > 7 mmol/L | 1 |
| **R**espiratory Rate| RR >= 30 breaths/min | 1 |
| **B**lood Pressure | SBP < 90 mmHg or DBP <= 60 mmHg | 1 |
| **65** | Age >= 65 years | 1 |

### Interpretation of CURB-65
| Total Score | Mortality Risk | Management Setting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0 - 1** | Low (1.5%) | Outpatient management (PO antibiotics). |
| **2** | Moderate (9.2%) | Inpatient admission (General Ward). |
| **3 - 5** | High (22-57%) | Severe CAP; Consider ICU/HDU admission. |

### CRB-65 Score
* **Utility:** Used in primary care/clinics where blood tests (Urea) are immediately unavailable. Score of >=1 warrants hospital assessment.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Pulmonary & Pleural Complications
* **Parapneumonic Effusion:** Simple (sterile sympathetic fluid) → Complicated (infected, low pH/glucose) → Empyema (frank pus in pleural space).
* **Lung Cavitation & Abscess:** Tissue necrosis (common with *Staph aureus*, *Klebsiella*, anaerobes).
* **ARDS (Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome):** Diffuse alveolar damage, bilateral non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
* **Pneumothorax:** Rupture of cavitary lesion or pneumatocele.

### Systemic Complications
* **Sepsis & Septic Shock:** Hypotension requiring vasopressors, lactate > 2 mmol/L.
* **Bacteremic Seeding:** Infective endocarditis, meningitis, septic arthritis, brain abscess.
* **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Pre-renal (dehydration/shock) or nephrotoxic drugs.

### Antibiotic Adverse Effects
* **Macrolides (Clarithromycin):** QT prolongation, GI upset, P450 enzyme inhibition.
* **Fluoroquinolones (Levofloxacin):** Tendon rupture (Achilles), QT prolongation, dysglycemia, caution in epilepsy.
* **Beta-Lactams:** Type I hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis), Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN).
* **Co-Amoxiclav (Augmentin):** Cholestatic jaundice (drug-induced liver injury).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most important parameter to check in pleural fluid to diagnose a complicated parapneumonic effusion requiring drainage? Answer: Pleural fluid pH. A pH < 7.2 indicates complicated effusion or empyema requiring a chest tube.
* Viva Question: Why do we always order a repeat CXR 6 weeks after a patient recovers from CAP? Answer: To ensure radiological resolution. Persistent consolidation may mask an underlying bronchogenic carcinoma, particularly in older smokers.
* Trap: Prescribing Metronidazole monotherapy for aspiration pneumonia. Pearl: Metronidazole covers anaerobes excellently but has poor activity against microaerophilic *Streptococci* and aerobes involved in aspiration. Always pair it with Ceftriaxone or use Augmentin alone.
* Trap: Assuming a cavitary lung lesion with an air-fluid level is exclusively a bacterial lung abscess. Pearl: In Hong Kong, Tuberculosis and Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the lung can also present with cavitating lesions. Always send 3x sputa for AFB and refer for bronchoscopy if non-resolving.
* Pitfall: Giving Levofloxacin as first-line empiric PO antibiotic for young adults with mild CAP. Avoid indiscriminate fluoroquinolone use due to rising resistance and side effects (tendonitis, C. diff risk). Reserve for true Penicillin allergy or specific pathogens.
