# Irritable Bowel Syndrome (腸易激綜合症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Irritable Bowel Syndrome
* **Functional GI disorder (功能性腸胃病)** characterized by chronic abdominal pain altered by bowel habits (diarrhea, constipation, or both)
* **Diagnosis of exclusion**: Requires absence of structural, inflammatory, or biochemical abnormalities explaining symptoms
* Significant overlap with **Functional Dyspepsia (功能性消化不良)** and **Fibromyalgia (纖維肌痛)**

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: ~5-7% of HK adult population (slightly lower than Western rates)
* **Demographics**: Female > Male (2:1); typical onset age 20-40 years
* **Healthcare burden**: Major cause of absenteeism; accounts for up to 50% of GI outpatient referrals in Hospital Authority (HA) clinics

### Etiological Triggers for IBS
* **Post-infectious (感染後 IBS)**: Follows severe acute gastroenteritis (e.g., *Campylobacter*, *Salmonella*, Norovirus); ↑ risk if associated with severe psychological stress during infection
* **Psychosocial factors**: Anxiety, depression, childhood trauma strongly linked to onset and exacerbations
* **Dietary factors**: Food intolerances, FODMAPs (Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, and Polyols) causing osmotic load and gas production

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Brain-Gut Axis Dysfunction (腦腸軸功能障礙)
* Altered bidirectional neural signaling between CNS and enteric nervous system (ENS)
* Exaggerated motor and sensory responses to normal physiologic stimuli (e.g., food, distension, stress)
* Central amplification of peripheral nociceptive input (impaired descending inhibitory pathways)

### Visceral Hypersensitivity (內臟高敏感)
* Lowered threshold for distension-induced pain in the colon and rectum
* Sensitization of afferent pathways mediated by ↑ local inflammatory mediators (mast cells, lymphocytes, cytokines) in mucosa
* ↑ Serotonin (5-HT) signaling: Altered SERT (serotonin transporter) availability → abnormal gut motility and sensation

### Gut Microbiome Dysbiosis (腸道菌群失調)
* ↓ Microbial diversity; ↑ Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio
* Altered bacterial fermentation → ↑ gas production (hydrogen, methane) → luminal distension
* Bile acid malabsorption (BAM): Accounts for ~30% of IBS-D cases; ↑ colonic bile acids → mucosal irritation & ↑ secretion

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Key History Findings in IBS
* **Pain character**: Crampy, lower abdomen (often LIF), intermittent
* **Pain relationship to defecation**: Exacerbated or relieved by defecation
* **Bowel habit changes**: Alternating diarrhea/constipation, sense of incomplete evacuation, mucus per rectum
* **Bloating**: Progressive worsening throughout the day
* **Aggravating factors**: Stress, meals, menses

### Alarm Features (Red Flags - 紅旗徵兆)
* Age of onset > 50 years (new symptoms)
* Unintentional weight loss
* Nocturnal symptoms (pain or diarrhea awakening patient)
* Family history of Colorectal Cancer (大腸癌), IBD, or Celiac disease
* Rectal bleeding / Melena (黑糞症)
* Progressive/unremitting symptom trajectory

### Physical Examination Findings
* **General**: Usually well-looking; rule out cachexia, pallor
* **Abdominal exam**: Often unremarkable or mild diffuse tenderness (especially left iliac fossa over descending colon)
* **Palpable masses**: Absent (presence mandates workup for malignancy)
* **DRE (Digital Rectal Examination)**: Normal sphincter tone; check for masses, blood, fissures (rule out structural mimics)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Gastrointestinal Differentials
* **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)**: Crohn's/UC; distinguished by bloody diarrhea, weight loss, ↑ inflammatory markers, abnormal endoscopy
* **Microscopic Colitis**: Chronic watery diarrhea, normal macroscopic colonoscopy, requires random biopsies
* **Celiac Disease (乳糜瀉)**: Malabsorption, anemia, dermatitis herpetiformis; distinguished by +ve anti-tTG IgA
* **Colorectal Malignancy**: Progressive symptoms, bleeding, older age; demands colonoscopy
* **Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO)**: Prominent bloating, positive breath test, responds to Rifaximin

### Non-Gastrointestinal Differentials
* **Gynecological**: Endometriosis (cyclical pain), Ovarian Cancer (pelvic mass, ascites, bloating)
* **Endocrine**: Hyperthyroidism (mimics IBS-D), Hypothyroidism (mimics IBS-C), Diabetes (autonomic neuropathy)

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Rome IV Diagnostic Criteria for IBS (Rome IV 診斷標準)
| Criteria Category | Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Core Symptom** | Recurrent abdominal pain, on average, ≥1 day/week in the last 3 months |
| **Associated Features** | Associated with ≥2 of the following: |
| | 1. Related to defecation |
| | 2. Associated with a change in frequency of stool |
| | 3. Associated with a change in form (appearance) of stool |
| **Timeframe** | Criteria fulfilled for the last 3 months with symptom onset ≥6 months prior to diagnosis |

### IBS Subtype Classification (Bristol Stool Form Scale)
| Subtype | Definition (Percentage of abnormal bowel movements) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **IBS-C (Constipation)** | >25% hard/lumpy stools (Bristol 1-2) AND <25% loose/watery stools |
| **IBS-D (Diarrhea)** | >25% loose/watery stools (Bristol 6-7) AND <25% hard/lumpy stools |
| **IBS-M (Mixed)** | >25% hard/lumpy stools AND >25% loose/watery stools |
| **IBS-U (Unclassified)** | Symptomatic but does not meet criteria for C, D, or M |

### Bedside & Blood Investigations
* **Rule out algorithm (positive diagnosis in absence of alarm features)**
* **CBC**: Check for anemia (rule out bleeding/malabsorption)
* **CRP / ESR**: Must be normal (↑ suggests IBD or infection)
* **Thyroid Function (TFT)**: Exclude thyrotoxicosis/hypothyroidism
* **Celiac Serology**: Anti-tTG IgA + total IgA (routinely tested in IBS-D/M, though rare in HK Chinese, consider in Caucasians)
* **Stool Calprotectin (糞便鈣衛蛋白)**: High negative predictive value (<50 µg/g) for IBD; minimizes unnecessary endoscopies
* **Stool routine / O&P**: Only if travel history or exposure risks

### Imaging & Endoscopy Investigations
* **Colonoscopy**: NOT routine for typical IBS < 50yo. Indicated ONLY if:
  * Alarm features present
  * Age > 50 (Colorectal cancer screening)
  * Elevated fecal calprotectin
  * Severe unrelenting IBS-D (to rule out Microscopic Colitis via biopsies)
* **SeHCAT scan**: Consider for suspected Bile Acid Malabsorption (rarely available in HA, empirical trial of cholestyramine preferred)

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Symptom Flare Management
* **Acute Pain/Spasm**: PRN Antispasmodics (e.g., Hyoscine butylbromide 10-20mg QID / Mebeverine 135mg TDS)
* **Acute Severe Diarrhea**: Loperamide 2-4mg PRN (titrate to effect, max 16mg/day)
* **Acute Constipation**: Glycerin suppository or PRN Macrogol (Polyethylene glycol); avoid lactulose (worsens bloating)
* **Reassurance**: Emphasize benign nature; avoid unnecessary hospital admissions or repeated imaging during flares

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Lifestyle & Dietary Modifications
* **First-line (All subtypes)**: Regular meals, adequate hydration, exercise, ↓ caffeine/alcohol
* **Low FODMAP Diet (低FODMAP飲食)**:
  * Elimination phase (2-6 weeks) → Reintroduction → Personalization
  * ↓ Fermentable carbs (e.g., apples, pears, onions, garlic, wheat, milk)
  * Requires Dietitian input; highly effective for bloating and pain
* **Fiber Manipulation**:
  * Soluble fiber (Ispaghula/Psyllium) recommended for IBS-C
  * Insoluble fiber (Bran) actively discouraged (exacerbates bloating/pain)
* **Psychological Therapies**: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Gut-directed Hypnotherapy (refractory cases)

### Step-up Pharmacological Algorithm for IBS-C (便秘型)
| Step | Intervention | Rationale in HK Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | Soluble Fiber (Psyllium) | Improves stool consistency without gas |
| **Step 2** | Osmotic Laxatives (Macrogol) | Safe long-term; avoid Lactulose (gas producer) |
| **Step 3** | Secretagogues (Linaclotide) | GC-C agonist; ↑ fluid secretion & ↓ visceral pain (Specialist Rx) |
| **Step 4** | Prokinetics (Prucalopride) | 5-HT4 agonist; for severe refractory constipation |

### Step-up Pharmacological Algorithm for IBS-D (腹瀉型)
| Step | Intervention | Rationale in HK Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | Loperamide (Imodium) | Mu-opioid agonist; ↓ motility, ↑ tone. Use PRN. |
| **Step 2** | Antispasmodics (Mebeverine, Peppermint Oil) | Smooth muscle relaxants for post-prandial colic |
| **Step 3** | Bile Acid Sequestrants (Cholestyramine) | Empirical trial for presumed BAM |
| **Step 4** | 5-HT3 Antagonists (Ondansetron/Alosetron) | Specialist Rx; slows transit (Alosetron carries ischemic colitis risk) |
| **Step 5** | Non-absorbable Antibiotic (Rifaximin) | 2-week course for refractory bloating/diarrhea without SIBO |

### Neuromodulators for Global Symptoms/Pain
* **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)**: Amitriptyline 10-25mg ON (Titrate slowly)
  * *Mechanism*: Neuromodulation, ↓ visceral hypersensitivity, anticholinergic effects (ideal for IBS-D with pain)
* **SSRIs**: Sertraline, Citalopram
  * *Mechanism*: Prokinetic side effects (ideal for IBS-C with comorbid anxiety/depression)

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Prognosis and Natural History of IBS
* **Chronicity**: Relapsing-remitting course; symptoms persist in >50% of patients long-term
* **Mortality**: No increased risk of mortality, colorectal cancer, or IBD compared to general population
* **Morbidity**: High healthcare utilization, severe impact on QoL

### IBS Severity Scoring System (IBS-SSS)
* Validated questionnaire assessing 5 domains over 10 days:
  * 1. Severity of abdominal pain
  * 2. Frequency of abdominal pain
  * 3. Severity of abdominal distension/bloating
  * 4. Dissatisfaction with bowel habits
  * 5. Interference with life
* **Scoring (Max 500)**:
  * <175: Mild
  * 175-300: Moderate
  * >300: Severe

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Psychosocial & Quality of Life Complications
* Severe anxiety, depression, and somatic symptom disorder
* Social isolation (fear of fecal incontinence or sudden flares)
* Nutritional deficiencies (if excessively strict on Low FODMAP without dietitian guidance)

### Adverse Effects of IBS Pharmacotherapy
* **TCAs (Amitriptyline)**: Dry mouth, urinary retention, sedation, arrhythmias (QT prolongation)
* **Loperamide**: Rebound constipation, toxic megacolon (rare, if underlying IBD missed)
* **Linaclotide**: Severe diarrhea leading to dehydration/electrolyte imbalance
* **Peppermint Oil**: Heartburn, worsening of GERD (relaxes lower esophageal sphincter)

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the single best initial non-invasive test to distinguish IBS-D from IBD?
  Answer: Fecal calprotectin; a normal level strongly rules out IBD.
* Viva Question: Why do we avoid prescribing Lactulose for IBS-C patients?
  Answer: Lactulose is heavily fermented by colonic bacteria, which exacerbates bloating and flatulence.
* Trap: Recommending a high-fiber diet generically to all IBS patients.
  Pearl: Distinguish fiber types. Prescribe soluble fiber (psyllium) for IBS-C, but strictly avoid insoluble fiber (bran), which worsens IBS symptoms.
* Trap: Sending a 55-year-old with new-onset "IBS-C" home with only Macrogol and a Low FODMAP diet.
  Pearl: New onset symptoms > 50 years is a major alarm feature. A colonoscopy is mandatory to rule out colorectal malignancy before diagnosing IBS.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing IBS merely based on abdominal pain and diarrhea. You must ensure the pain is related to defecation or changes in stool frequency/form, fulfilling Rome IV criteria.
* Pitfall: Overlooking Bile Acid Malabsorption (BAM). Up to 1/3 of refractory "IBS-D" is actually BAM; consider a trial of Cholestyramine if standard therapy fails.
