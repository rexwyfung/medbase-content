# Arrhythmias (心律不正)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Arrhythmias
* **Arrhythmia (心律不正):** Abnormal cardiac rhythm originating outside the physiological sinus node automaticity, or an abnormality in impulse conduction.
* Classified anatomically: **Supraventricular** vs **Ventricular**.
* Classified by rate: **Tachyarrhythmias** (>100 bpm) vs **Bradyarrhythmias** (<60 bpm).

### Major Etiologies of Arrhythmias
* **Ischemic Heart Disease (冠心病):** Acute MI (commonest cause of VT/VF), chronic scar.
* **Structural Heart Disease:** Valvular disease (mitral stenosis → AF), cardiomyopathies (HCM, DCM).
* **Metabolic/Electrolyte Disturbances:** ↑/↓ K+, ↓ Mg2+, ↓ Ca2+, hypoxia, acidosis.
* **Endocrine:** Thyrotoxicosis (甲狀腺機能亢進) → AF, SVT.
* **Toxins/Drugs:** Alcohol (Holiday Heart Syndrome), amphetamines, antiarrhythmic pro-arrhythmia (e.g., QT prolongation).
* **Infective/Inflammatory:** Myocarditis, endocarditis (root abscess → heart block).
* **Congenital/Genetic:** WPW syndrome, Brugada syndrome, Long QT syndrome.

### Local Epidemiology of Arrhythmias in Hong Kong
* **Atrial Fibrillation (AF) (心房顫動):** Most common sustained arrhythmia. Prevalence in HK is ~1% in general population, rising to >10% in those >80 years old. Major cause of ischemic stroke in HA hospitals.
* **Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD):** Estimated ~5000 cases/year in HK; >80% due to VF/VT secondary to ischemic heart disease.
* **Brugada Syndrome:** Higher prevalence in Asian populations, significant cause of SCD in young males in HK.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Pathophysiological Mechanisms of Arrhythmogenesis
* **Altered Automaticity:** Cells outside the SA node acquire spontaneous depolarization (e.g., ischemic Purkinje fibers).
* **Triggered Activity:**
  * **Early Afterdepolarizations (EADs):** Occur during Phase 2/3 (prolonged repolarization). Mechanism of **Torsades de Pointes (TdP)** in Long QT.
  * **Delayed Afterdepolarizations (DADs):** Occur in Phase 4 (intracellular Ca2+ overload). Mechanism in digoxin toxicity.
* **Re-entry (迴圈):** Requires 3 conditions:
  * Two distinct conduction pathways forming a circuit.
  * Unidirectional block in one pathway.
  * Slower conduction in the unblocked pathway allowing the blocked pathway to recover.
  * Examples: AVNRT, AVRT (WPW), Atrial Flutter.

### Molecular Pathophysiology of Specific Arrhythmias
* **AF (心房顫動):** Electrical remodeling (shortened refractory period, down-regulation of L-type Ca2+ channels) + Structural remodeling (atrial fibrosis) → multiple micro-reentry wavelets.
* **Long QT Syndrome (LQTS):** Mutations in K+ efflux channels (loss of function) or Na+/Ca2+ influx channels (gain of function) → prolonged AP duration → ↑ risk of EADs.
* **Heart Block (房室傳導阻滯):** Fibrosis (Lenègre-Lev disease), ischemia (RCA infarct affecting AV node), or vagal hypertonia → failure of impulse transmission through AV node/His-Purkinje system.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Detailed History Taking for Arrhythmias
* **Palpitations:** Tap out the rhythm (fast/slow, regular/irregular). Sudden vs gradual onset/offset.
* **Associated Symptoms:** Syncope/presyncope (red flag), chest pain, dyspnea, polyuria (post-SVT ANP release).
* **Precipitating Factors:** Caffeine, alcohol, stress, exercise (catecholaminergic polymorphic VT), sleep (vagal tone).
* **Past Medical History:** Structural heart disease, thyroid disease, previous MI.
* **Drug History:** QT-prolonging drugs (macrolides, psychotropics), diuretics (electrolyte imbalance).
* **Family History:** Sudden unexplained death <40 years old, inherited channelopathies.

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Bradyarrhythmias
* **Pulse:** Slow, regular (Complete Heart Block) or irregular (Wenckebach).
* **JVP:** **Cannon 'a' waves** in Complete Heart Block (AV dissociation → RA contracting against closed tricuspid valve).
* **Auscultation:** Variable intensity of S1 in Complete Heart Block.

### System-Specific Physical Signs of Tachyarrhythmias
* **Pulse:** Irregularly irregular pulse + pulse deficit (AF). Regular bounding pulse (SVT).
* **Blood Pressure:** Hypotension/shock (indicates hemodynamic instability).
* **Thyroid:** Goiter, exophthalmos, tremor, lid lag (thyrotoxicosis stigmata).
* **Heart Failure Stigmata:** Basal crepitations, elevated JVP, pedal edema (tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy).
* **Auscultation:** Absent 'a' wave in JVP, variable S1 (AF).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differential Diagnoses of Narrow Complex Tachycardia (NCT)
| Arrhythmia | Rhythm | Distinguishing ECG/Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sinus Tachycardia** | Regular | Normal P wave axis, gradual onset/offset. |
| **Atrial Fibrillation (AF)** | Irregularly Irregular | No P waves, fibrillatory 'f' waves. |
| **Atrial Flutter (AFL)** | Usually Regular | Sawtooth 'F' waves (II, III, aVF), commonly 2:1 block (ventricular rate ~150 bpm). |
| **AVNRT** | Regular | No visible P waves (buried in QRS) or retrograde P waves immediately after QRS (pseudo-R' in V1). |
| **AVRT (WPW)** | Regular | Delta wave present on baseline sinus ECG. |
| **Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT)** | Irregularly Irregular | ≥3 distinct P wave morphologies, associated with COPD. |

### Differential Diagnoses of Broad Complex Tachycardia (BCT)
| Arrhythmia | Distinguishing Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)** | Extreme axis deviation, AV dissociation (capture/fusion beats), positive/negative concordance across chest leads. **Assume VT until proven otherwise.** |
| **SVT with Aberrancy** | Typical Bundle Branch Block (BBB) morphology, lack of AV dissociation, previous ECG shows same BBB. |
| **Pre-excited AF (AF + WPW)** | Irregularly irregular, extremely fast rate (>200 bpm), variable QRS width. |
| **Torsades de Pointes (TdP)** | Polymorphic VT with twisting morphology around isoelectric line. Preceded by prolonged QT. |

### Differential Diagnoses of Bradycardia
* **Sinus Bradycardia:** Athlete, physiological sleep, hypothermia, hypothyroidism.
* **Sick Sinus Syndrome (SSS):** Tachy-brady syndrome, sinus pauses/arrest.
* **Atrioventricular (AV) Blocks:**
  * **1st Degree:** PR >0.20s, 1:1 conduction.
  * **2nd Degree Mobitz I (Wenckebach):** Progressive PR prolongation until dropped beat.
  * **2nd Degree Mobitz II:** Fixed PR interval with randomly dropped beats.
  * **3rd Degree (Complete):** AV dissociation, independent P and QRS rates.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations for Arrhythmias
* **12-Lead ECG:** Gold standard for Dx. Evaluate rate, rhythm, axis, PR interval, QRS duration, QT interval, and ST/T changes.
* **Rhythm Strip (Lead II):** Continuous tracing to detect paroxysmal blocks/pauses.
* **Vagal Maneuvers (Diagnostic):** Carotid sinus massage (CSM) or Valsalva to unmask flutter waves or terminate AVNRT.

### Blood Investigations for Arrhythmias
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** ↓ Hb (anemia precipitating ischemia/tachycardia), ↑ WBC (infection).
* **Renal/Liver Function (RFT/LFT):** Baseline before starting antiarrhythmics/DOACs.
* **Electrolytes:** K+, Mg2+, Ca2+ (critical for repolarization abnormalities).
* **Thyroid Function Test (TFT):** TSH and fT4 (mandatory for new-onset AF/SVT).
* **Cardiac Biomarkers:** High-sensitivity Troponin I/T (rule out acute MI).
* **Coagulation Profile:** PT/INR, APTT (baseline for anticoagulation).

### Imaging and Special Investigations for Arrhythmias
* **Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE):** Assess LV ejection fraction (LVEF), LA size, valvular structure, regional wall motion abnormalities.
* **Holter Monitor (24-48 hours) / Patch Monitor (up to 14 days):** For paroxysmal palpitations or unexplained syncope.
* **Implantable Loop Recorder (ILR):** For infrequent (e.g., once every few months) unexplained syncope suspected to be arrhythmic.
* **Electrophysiology (EP) Study:** Invasive mapping of electrical pathways to locate re-entry circuits/ectopic foci for ablation.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Resuscitation Protocol for Tachyarrhythmias (ALS)
* **Assess Stability:** Adverse features = Shock, Syncope, Myocardial Ischemia, Heart Failure.
* **If UNSTABLE (Any Rhythm):**
  * Synchronized DC Cardioversion (up to 3 attempts).
  * IV Amiodarone 300mg over 10-20 mins if DC shock fails, then repeat shock.
* **If STABLE Narrow Complex (SVT):**
  * Vagal maneuvers (Valsalva, Carotid Sinus Massage).
  * IV Adenosine (6mg → 12mg → 12mg) via large proximal vein with flush. (Warn patient of "impending doom" feeling).
  * Alternatives if Asthma/COPD: IV Verapamil.
* **If STABLE Broad Complex (VT):**
  * IV Amiodarone 300mg over 20-60 mins, followed by 900mg infusion over 24 hours.
* **If Pre-excited AF (WPW + AF):**
  * IV Ibutilide or Flecainide. **Strictly avoid AV nodal blockers** (Adenosine, Beta-blockers, CCBs, Digoxin) as they force conduction down the accessory pathway → VF.

### Acute Resuscitation Protocol for Bradyarrhythmias (ALS)
* **Assess Stability:** Adverse features present?
* **If STABLE:** Observe and find underlying cause.
* **If UNSTABLE / Risk of Asystole (Mobitz II, Complete Heart Block, Ventricular Pause >3s):**
  * **1st Line:** IV Atropine 500mcg (repeat up to max 3mg).
  * **2nd Line:** Isoprenaline/Adrenaline infusion OR Transcutaneous Pacing (TCP).
  * **Definitive Acute:** Transvenous Pacing (TVP) wire insertion.

### Acute Medical Management of AF with Rapid Ventricular Response (RVR)
* **Rate Control (Default Strategy in ED):**
  * IV/PO Beta-blocker (e.g., Metoprolol).
  * IV/PO Non-dihydropyridine CCB (e.g., Diltiazem/Verapamil). (Avoid if HFrEF).
  * IV Digoxin (if coexisting Heart Failure).
* **Acute Rhythm Control (Consider if onset < 48 hours):**
  * Pharmacological cardioversion: IV Flecainide (if structurally normal heart) or IV Amiodarone.
  * Electrical DC cardioversion (requires fasting, sedation).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Chronic Pharmacological Management of Arrhythmias (Vaughan Williams)
| Class | Mechanism | Drugs (HA Formulary) | Indication & Chronic Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I (Na+ block)** | ↓ Phase 0 upslope | Flecainide (Ic), Propafenone (Ic) | Pill-in-the-pocket for paroxysmal AF. Avoid in IHD/structural disease. |
| **II (Beta block)** | ↓ SA/AV automaticity | Metoprolol, Bisoprolol, Atenolol | First-line rate control in AF, SVT prevention, VT prevention post-MI. |
| **III (K+ block)** | Prolongs AP / repolarization | Amiodarone, Sotalol | Rhythm control in AF, prevention of VT/VF. |
| **IV (Ca2+ block)**| ↓ AV conduction | Verapamil, Diltiazem | Rate control in AF, SVT prevention. |
| **Others** | Na/K ATPase block | Digoxin | Rate control in AF with concomitant HF. |

### Chronic Device and Ablation Therapy
* **Permanent Pacemaker (PPM):**
  * Indications: Symptomatic Sick Sinus Syndrome, symptomatic bradycardia, Mobitz II block, 3rd Degree Heart Block.
* **Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD):**
  * Secondary Prevention: Survivor of VF/VT cardiac arrest.
  * Primary Prevention: LVEF <35% post-MI despite optimal medical therapy.
* **Catheter Ablation (射頻消融術):**
  * First-line for symptomatic WPW, AVNRT, Atrial Flutter (typical circuit).
  * Highly effective for paroxysmal AF (pulmonary vein isolation - PVI).

### Chronic Anticoagulation Algorithm in AF
* **Assess Stroke Risk:** Calculate CHA2DS2-VASc score.
* **Assess Bleeding Risk:** Calculate HAS-BLED score to optimize modifiable risk factors (BP control, alcohol, NSAIDs).
* **Decision:**
  * Score 0 (Men) / 1 (Women): No anticoagulation.
  * Score ≥1 (Men) / ≥2 (Women): Offer anticoagulation.
  * Score ≥2 (Men) / ≥3 (Women): Recommend anticoagulation.
* **Choice of Agent:**
  * **Non-valvular AF:** Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) preferred (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran, Edoxaban).
  * **Valvular AF (Moderate/Severe Mitral Stenosis or Mechanical Valve):** Warfarin (DOACs contraindicated).

| **H** | Hypertension | 1 |

| **D** | Diabetes Mellitus | 1 |
| **S2**| Stroke / TIA / Thromboembolism history | 2 |
| **V** | Vascular disease (prior MI, PAD, aortic plaque) | 1 |
| **A** | Age 65 - 74 years | 1 |
| **Sc**| Sex category (Female) | 1 |

### HAS-BLED Bleeding Risk Stratification
| Letter | Risk Factor | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **H** | Hypertension (Systolic >160 mmHg) | 1 |
| **A** | Abnormal Renal OR Liver Function (1 point each) | 1 or 2 |
| **S** | Stroke history | 1 |
| **B** | Bleeding history / predisposition | 1 |
| **L** | Labile INRs (if on Warfarin, TTR < 60%) | 1 |
| **E** | Elderly (Age > 65) | 1 |
| **D** | Drugs (NSAIDs/Antiplatelets) OR Alcohol excess (1 pt each)| 1 or 2 |
* **Clinical use:** Score ≥3 indicates high bleeding risk; mandates closer follow-up and correction of modifiable factors, but does *not* absolutely contraindicate anticoagulation if stroke risk is high.

### Prognostic Indicators in Ventricular Arrhythmias
* **Poor Prognostic Factors:** Depressed LVEF (<35%), prior history of MI with extensive scar burden, failure to tolerate evidence-based beta-blockade, QT prolongation >500ms.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Arrhythmias
* **Neurological:** Ischemic stroke (AF → LA appendage thrombus), hypoxic brain injury (prolonged VF/VT arrest).
* **Cardiac:** Tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy (prolonged HR >100 bpm → ventricular dilatation and failure).
* **Renal:** Acute kidney injury (AKI) secondary to cardiogenic shock/hypoperfusion in unstable arrhythmias.
* **Gastrointestinal:** Ischemic bowel (mesenteric ischemia from embolic shower in AF or low flow state).

### Adverse Effects of Antiarrhythmic Drugs
* **Amiodarone (The "Everything" Toxin):**
  * Pulmonary: Pulmonary fibrosis (irreversible, monitor CXR/PFT).
  * Thyroid: Hypothyroidism or Hyperthyroidism (contains iodine).
  * Hepatic: Transaminitis / Cirrhosis.
  * Ocular: Corneal microdeposits (halo vision), optic neuritis.
  * Skin: Photosensitivity, slate-grey discoloration.
* **Flecainide:** Pro-arrhythmic in ischemic heart disease (CAST trial - increases mortality post-MI).
* **Adenosine:** Bronchospasm (contraindicated in severe asthma).
* **Digoxin:** Toxicity (nausea, vomiting, xanthopsia/yellow vision, triggered arrhythmias). Toxicity exacerbated by hypokalemia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic ECG finding for Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) syndrome? Answer: Short PR interval (<0.12s), Delta wave (slurred QRS upstroke), and broad QRS complex.
* Viva Question: A patient with suspected SVT receives Adenosine, and the rhythm strip temporarily shows flutter waves before returning to tachycardia. What is the diagnosis? Answer: Atrial Flutter. Adenosine unmasked the underlying flutter waves by temporarily blocking AV conduction.
* Trap: Assuming a broad complex tachycardia in a young person is definitely SVT with aberrancy just because they are young. Pearl: Always treat broad complex tachycardia as Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) until proven otherwise, as giving AV nodal blockers (like Verapamil) to VT can precipitate fatal cardiovascular collapse.
* Trap: Prescribing a DOAC for stroke prevention in a patient with Atrial Fibrillation and a mechanical heart valve. Pearl: DOACs are strictly contraindicated in mechanical heart valves and moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis. Warfarin is mandatory.
* Pitfall: Giving Adenosine to a patient with Atrial Fibrillation and a known WPW accessory pathway. By blocking the AV node completely, you force all atrial impulses down the accessory pathway, driving a rapid 1:1 ventricular response which quickly degenerates into Ventricular Fibrillation.
* Viva Question: What is the target INR for a patient on Warfarin for non-valvular Atrial Fibrillation? Answer: INR 2.0 - 3.0.
* Trap: Stopping Amiodarone and immediately assuming its side effects will resolve. Pearl: Amiodarone is highly lipophilic and has an extremely long half-life (up to 100 days); toxicity can persist or present even after drug cessation.
