# Fungal Infections (真菌感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Invasive Fungal Infections
* **Opportunistic Fungal Infections (機會性真菌感染):** Severe, life-threatening infections occurring predominantly in immunocompromised hosts (e.g., HIV/AIDS, haematological malignancies, post-transplant, prolonged ICU stay).
* **Endemic Fungal Infections (地方性真菌感染):** Infections caused by geographically restricted dimorphic fungi affecting both healthy and immunocompromised individuals.

### Pathogen Classification by Morphology
* **Yeasts (酵母菌):** Unicellular. Reproduce by budding.
* **Moulds (霉菌):** Multicellular. Form branching hyphae (菌絲).
* **Dimorphic Fungi (雙相型真菌):** Exist as mould at room temp (25°C) & yeast at body temp (37°C). "Mould in the Cold, Yeast in the Beast".

### Key Pathogens & Hong Kong Epidemiology
* **Candida spp. (念珠菌):** *C. albicans* (most common); *C. glabrata* & *C. krusei* (fluconazole-resistant); *C. auris* (emerging multidrug-resistant HA superbug causing major nosocomial outbreaks in HK).
* **Aspergillus spp. (曲霉菌):** *A. fumigatus* (most common), *A. flavus*, *A. niger*. Ubiquitous in environment.
* **Cryptococcus spp. (隱球菌):** *C. neoformans* (pigeon guano, HIV-associated); *C. gattii* (eucalyptus trees, immunocompetent).
* **Mucorales (毛黴菌):** *Rhizopus*, *Mucor*. Rare, devastating necrotizing infections.
* **Pneumocystis jirovecii (肺囊蟲):** Atypical fungus (lacks ergosterol). Leading opportunistic pneumonia in late HIV (CD4 <200).
* **Talaromyces marneffei (馬爾尼菲籃狀菌):** Endemic in Southeast Asia, Southern China & HK. Bamboo rat exposure. Highly prevalent in local late HIV cohort.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Candida Pathogenesis
* **Commensal to Pathogen:** Resides in GI/GU tracts. 
* **Mechanism:** Disruption of mucosal barriers (chemo/surgery) or microbiome (Abx) → phenotype switch (yeast to hyphal form) → tissue invasion → haematogenous dissemination.
* **Evasion:** Biofilm (生物薄膜) formation on intravascular catheters and prosthetic valves.

### Aspergillus Pathogenesis
* **Inhalation:** Spores (conidia) inhaled into alveoli.
* **Host Defense Failure:** Normally cleared by macrophages/neutrophils. In profound neutropenia → conidia germinate into septate hyphae.
* **Tissue Destruction:** Highly angioinvasive → invades pulmonary vasculature → thrombosis → local tissue infarction, cavitation & haematogenous spread (CNS/liver).
* **Allergic Mechanism (ABPA):** Hypersensitivity (Type I & III) to *Aspergillus* antigens in airways (asthma/CF patients) → eosinophilic inflammation.

### Cryptococcus Pathogenesis
* **Inhalation:** Spores inhaled into lungs.
* **Virulence Factor:** Thick polysaccharide capsule (莢膜) (glucuronoxylomannan) → prevents phagocytosis by macrophages.
* **Tropism:** Highly neurotropic. Transcytosis across blood-brain barrier → meningoencephalitis.

### Mucormycosis Pathogenesis
* **Inhalation/Inoculation:** Spores enter via respiratory tract or skin trauma.
* **Host Milieu:** Thrives in high iron, hyperglycaemic & acidotic environments (e.g., DKA). Deferoxamine acts as a siderophore for the fungus, supplying it with iron.
* **Tissue Destruction:** Extremely angioinvasive (non-septate broad hyphae) → rapid vascular thrombosis → tissue necrosis (black eschar).

### Pneumocystis jirovecii (PJP) Pathogenesis
* **Attachment:** Trophozoites bind strongly to Type I pneumocytes via major surface glycoprotein (MSG).
* **Mechanism:** Provokes intense alveolar inflammatory response → foamy eosinophilic exudate in alveoli → blocked gas exchange → severe V/Q mismatch & hypoxaemia.

### Talaromyces marneffei Pathogenesis
* **Infection:** Inhalation of conidia from the environment.
* **Intracellular Replication:** Phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages → converts to fission yeast → multiplies intracellularly → reticuloendothelial system dissemination (liver, spleen, bone marrow, skin).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Risk Factor Assessment for Fungal Infections
* **Candidaemia Risks:** Central venous catheters (CVC), total parenteral nutrition (TPN), broad-spectrum Abx, GI surgery, neutropenia, haemodialysis.
* **Invasive Aspergillosis (IA) Risks:** Prolonged/profound neutropenia (AML/ALL), allogeneic HSCT, high-dose corticosteroids, biologic agents.
* **Cryptococcus/PJP Risks:** Undiagnosed/untreated HIV, CD4 <200 (PJP) or <100 (Cryptococcus).

### Assessment of Candidiasis (念珠菌病)
* **Mucosal:** White, cottage-cheese-like plaques on oral mucosa/tongue (can be scraped off leaving raw base); dysphagia/odynophagia (oesophageal thrush); thick white vaginal discharge + pruritus.
* **Disseminated:** Unexplained fever non-responsive to Abx; new-onset murmurs (endocarditis); visual floaters/pain (endophthalmitis).

### Assessment of Aspergillosis (曲霉病)
* **Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis (ABPA):** Exacerbation of asthma, productive cough with brownish bronchial casts.
* **Aspergilloma (Fungal Ball):** Often asymptomatic; massive haemoptysis if erosion into adjacent vessels.
* **Invasive Aspergillosis:** Pleuritic chest pain, haemoptysis, fever in a neutropenic patient.

### Assessment of Cryptococcosis (隱球菌病)
* **CNS:** Indolent, subacute headache (weeks), lethargy, personality changes, fever. Meningism (neck stiffness) often absent/subtle in severe immunosuppression. Signs of ↑ ICP (papilloedema, cranial nerve VI palsy).
* **Skin:** Umbilicated papules (mimics molluscum contagiosum).

### Assessment of Mucormycosis (毛黴菌病)
* **Rhino-orbital-cerebral:** Facial/periorbital swelling, severe facial pain, proptosis, ophthalmoplegia, necrotic black eschar (黑色焦痂) on hard palate or nasal turbinates, altered sensorium.

### Assessment of Pneumocystis Pneumonia (PJP/PCP)
* **Symptoms:** Subacute onset (weeks) of progressive exertional dyspnoea, dry non-productive cough, low-grade fever.
* **Signs:** Tachypnoea, severe exercise-induced desaturation. Chest auscultation often deceptively clear (mismatch with severe hypoxia).

### Assessment of Talaromycosis (馬爾尼菲籃狀菌病)
* **Systemic:** Prolonged fever, weight loss, generalized lymphadenopathy, marked hepatosplenomegaly.
* **Cutaneous:** Characteristic generalized papular rash with central necrosis/umbilication (mostly face, neck, upper trunk).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Differentiating Pulmonary Fungal Infections in Immunocompromised

| Feature | Pneumocystis (PJP) | Invasive Aspergillosis | Aspergilloma | TB (Mycobacterium) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Typical Host** | HIV (CD4 <200) | Prolonged Neutropenic | Pre-existing lung cavity | Endemic/Immunocompromised |
| **Onset** | Subacute (weeks) | Acute to subacute | Chronic | Chronic |
| **Symptom Profile** | Dry cough, profound hypoxia | Pleuritic pain, haemoptysis | Massive haemoptysis | Night sweats, productive cough |
| **Key Exam Finding**| Clear chest, exertional ↓O2| Focal crackles/rub | Cachexia, clubbing | Apical crackles |

### Differentiating Cutaneous Umbilicated Lesions in HIV
* **Talaromyces marneffei:** Accompanied by massive hepatosplenomegaly, prominent fever, anaemia.
* **Cryptococcus neoformans:** Usually accompanied by CNS signs (headache, confusion).
* **Molluscum Contagiosum:** Purely cutaneous, viral, no systemic toxicity or fever.

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside & Microbiological Investigations
* **Sputum/BAL Fluid:** Gram stain (Candida), Calcofluor white stain, Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA) culture.
* **Blood Cultures:** Positive in Candidaemia (yield ~50%), *Talaromyces*, *Cryptococcus*. Always negative for *Aspergillus*.
* **Ophthalmology Consult:** Dilated fundoscopy to rule out Candida endophthalmitis (mandatory in ALL candidaemia patients).

### Serological & Biomarker Blood Tests
* **1,3-Beta-D-Glucan (BDG):** Pan-fungal marker (cell wall component). +ve in *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *PJP*. -ve in *Cryptococcus*, *Mucorales*.
* **Galactomannan (GM) Antigen:** Specific for *Aspergillus*. Tested in serum and Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL) fluid.
* **Cryptococcal Antigen (CrAg):** Serum and CSF. Highly sensitive and specific.
* **HIV Testing:** Mandatory for PJP, Cryptococcus, Talaromyces.

### Radiological Imaging
* **PJP (CXR/HRCT):** Bilateral symmetrical perihilar ground-glass opacities (GGOs) ("bat-wing" appearance). Pneumatoceles/pneumothorax.
* **Invasive Aspergillosis (HRCT Thorax):** Early: Nodules with surrounding GGO (Halo sign - 暈輪徵, reflects haemorrhage). Late (recovery phase): Air-crescent sign (空氣新月徵, reflects cavitation).
* **Aspergilloma (CXR/HRCT):** Solid round mass within a pre-existing cavity (often upper lobe). Mobile with patient positioning (Monod sign).
* **Cryptococcus (CT/MRI Brain):** Exclude mass lesion before LP. May show cryptococcomas or dilated Virchow-Robin perivascular spaces (soap bubble appearance).
* **Mucormycosis (MRI Face/Brain):** Sinus opacification, bone destruction, cavernous sinus thrombosis, absence of enhancement in necrotic tissue.

### Invasive Sampling & Histopathology
* **Lumbar Puncture (for Cryptococcus):**
  * Opening pressure markedly elevated (>25 cmH2O).
  * CSF profile: Mononuclear pleocytosis, ↑ Protein, ↓ Glucose.
  * India Ink Prep: Encapsulated yeasts (low sensitivity).
* **Bronchoscopy (BAL):** Crucial for PJP (Silver stain/Immunofluorescence) and IA (BAL Galactomannan).
* **Tissue Biopsy Stains:**
  * **Grocott's Methenamine Silver (GMS):** Stains fungi black.
  * **Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS):** Stains fungi magenta.
  * *Aspergillus:* Narrow (<45°), septate branching hyphae.
  * *Mucorales:* Broad, non-septate, right-angle branching hyphae.

### EORTC/MSGERC Diagnostic Criteria (for Invasive Fungal Diseases)
* **Proven:** Histopathologic evidence of tissue invasion OR positive culture from sterile site.
* **Probable:** Host factor (e.g., neutropenia) + Clinical features (e.g., Halo sign) + Mycological evidence (e.g., +ve Galactomannan).
* **Possible:** Host factor + Clinical features (no mycological evidence).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Acute Management of Candidaemia (念珠菌血症)
* **Source Control:** IMMEDIATE removal of CVCs and central lines.
* **First-line Empirical:** IV Echinocandin (e.g., Caspofungin or Anidulafungin). Fluconazole no longer empirical 1st line in HA ICU due to resistance risks, unless patient is non-critically ill & azole-naive.

### Acute Management of Invasive Aspergillosis
* **First-line Therapy:** IV Voriconazole.
* **Alternative:** IV Liposomal Amphotericin B or Isavuconazole.
* **Adjunct:** Reduce immunosuppression (taper steroids, G-CSF for neutropenia recovery).

### Acute Management of Cryptococcal Meningitis
* **Induction Therapy (2 weeks):** IV Liposomal Amphotericin B + PO Flucytosine.
* **ICP Management (CRITICAL):** Therapeutic repeat LPs (daily if needed) to drain CSF and maintain opening pressure <20 cmH2O. DO NOT use acetazolamide or mannitol.
* **ART Timing:** Delay antiretroviral therapy (ART) in HIV patients for 4-6 weeks to prevent fatal Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS).

### Acute Management of Mucormycosis
* **Multidisciplinary Emergency:**
  * Urgent aggressive surgical debridement of all necrotic tissue.
  * High-dose IV Liposomal Amphotericin B.
  * Rapid correction of underlying metabolic derangement (DKA reversal).

### Acute Management of Severe PJP (PaO2 < 70 mmHg on Room Air)
* **First-line Therapy:** High-dose IV Co-trimoxazole (Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole) for 21 days.
* **Adjunctive Corticosteroids:** PO Prednisolone 40mg BD (or IV Hydrocortisone) commenced WITHIN 72 HOURS to prevent fatal inflammatory respiratory failure upon treatment initiation.

### Acute Management of Talaromycosis
* **First-line Therapy:** IV Liposomal Amphotericin B for 2 weeks.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Antifungal Pharmacology Classes
| Class | Mechanism of Action | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Azoles** | Inhibit ergosterol synthesis (via 14-α-demethylase) | Fluconazole, Voriconazole, Posaconazole |
| **Polyenes** | Bind to ergosterol, form pores in cell membrane → cell death | Amphotericin B, Nystatin (topical) |
| **Echinocandins** | Inhibit 1,3-beta-D-glucan synthase (disrupt cell wall) | Caspofungin, Anidulafungin, Micafungin |
| **Antimetabolites**| Inhibit fungal DNA/RNA synthesis | Flucytosine |

### Chronic Management of Candidaemia
* **Step-Down Therapy:** Transition from IV Echinocandin to PO Fluconazole (if susceptible) once patient is clinically stable, afebrile, and blood cultures are negative.
* **Duration:** 14 days AFTER the first negative blood culture.

### Chronic Management of Aspergillosis
* **Aspergilloma:** Surgical resection if severe haemoptysis. Bronchial artery embolization as temporizing measure. Antifungals ineffective (poor cavity penetration).
* **ABPA:** PO Itraconazole (reduces fungal burden) + PO Corticosteroids (reduces allergic response).

### Consolidation & Maintenance for Cryptococcus / Talaromyces
* **Consolidation:** PO Fluconazole (high dose) for 8 weeks.
* **Maintenance (Secondary Prophylaxis):** PO Fluconazole (lower dose) indefinitely until CD4 >100-200 on ART for >6 months.

### HIV Primary Antifungal Prophylaxis Indications
* **CD4 < 200:** PO Co-trimoxazole (Prophylaxis against PJP).
* **CD4 < 100 & CrAg positive:** PO Fluconazole (Pre-emptive therapy for Cryptococcus).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Candida Score (Predicts invasive candidiasis in ICU)
* **Variables (1 point each, except severe sepsis):** TPN (+1), Surgery (+1), Multifocal colonization (+1), Severe Sepsis (+2).
* **Interpretation:** Score ≥3 identifies patients who benefit from empirical echinocandin therapy.

### Prognostic Indicators in Cryptococcal Meningitis
* **Poor Prognostic Factors:** High opening pressure (>25 cmH2O), lack of CSF pleocytosis (reflects profound immunosuppression), abnormal mental status at presentation, disseminated disease.

### Mortality Rates
* **Invasive Aspergillosis:** 30-50% (higher in bone marrow transplant).
* **Mucormycosis:** >50% (near 100% if disseminated/CNS involvement).
* **Candidaemia:** ~30-40% attributable mortality.

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### End-Organ Complications of Invasive Fungi
* **Candida:** Endophthalmitis (blindness), infective endocarditis (requires valve replacement), hepatosplenic candidiasis.
* **Aspergillus:** Massive fatal haemoptysis, CNS abscesses, tracheobronchitis.
* **Cryptococcus:** Permanent visual loss (optic nerve compression from high ICP), deafness, cognitive impairment.
* **Mucor:** Palatal/facial destruction, cavernous sinus thrombosis, internal carotid artery occlusion.

### Antifungal Adverse Effects & Toxicity
* **Amphotericin B (Amphoterrible):**
  * Acute infusion reactions: Fever, severe rigors ("shake and bake" - Rx with hydrocortisone/pethidine).
  * Nephrotoxicity: Severe vasoconstriction → AKI.
  * Electrolytes: Wasting of Potassium (severe ↓K) and Magnesium (severe ↓Mg).
* **Azoles (General):** Hepatotoxicity (↑ LFTs), QTc prolongation.
* **Voriconazole (Specific):** Transient visual disturbances (flashes/photophobia), photosensitive rash, squamous cell carcinoma of skin (long-term use), alopecia, hallucinations.
* **Echinocandins:** Generally well-tolerated; mild infusion reactions (histamine release), mild hepatic derangement.
* **Flucytosine:** Profound bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, thrombocytopenia) - requires therapeutic drug monitoring.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why do we always request a formal ophthalmology review for a patient with positive blood cultures for Candida? Answer: To rule out Candida endophthalmitis, which occurs in up to 10% of candidaemia cases. It is often asymptomatic initially but can lead to irreversible blindness and requires prolonged therapy + possible vitrectomy.
* Trap: Treating a positive urine culture for Candida albicans in an asymptomatic catheterized patient. Pearl: Candiduria is almost always benign colonization. Do not treat unless the patient is symptomatic, neutropenic, or undergoing urological instrumentation. Just remove the catheter.
* Pitfall: Forgetting drug-drug interactions with Azoles. Azoles are potent CYP450 3A4 inhibitors. Co-administering with statins, calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus/cyclosporine), or DOACs can lead to fatal toxicities.
* Viva Question: A patient with PJP pneumonia is started on Co-trimoxazole. On day 3, his PaO2 drops from 75 mmHg to 60 mmHg. What did you forget to prescribe? Answer: Adjunctive systemic corticosteroids. Fungal death causes an intense inflammatory burst in the alveoli, worsening hypoxia unless blunted by early steroids.
* Trap: Performing a lumbar puncture in suspected Cryptococcal meningitis without prior neuroimaging. Pearl: Cryptococcomas can cause mass effect. LP without prior CT/MRI brain risks fatal uncal herniation.
* Viva Question: Why do we delay starting anti-HIV drugs (ART) in patients newly diagnosed with Cryptococcal meningitis? Answer: To prevent Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS). Rapid recovery of the immune system while fungal burden is high leads to a massive, potentially fatal inflammatory response in the closed space of the skull, severely spiking ICP.
* Pitfall: Trying to lower intracranial pressure in Cryptococcal meningitis using Mannitol or Dexamethasone. These are ineffective. The only effective treatment for ↑ ICP here is mechanical drainage via repeated therapeutic lumbar punctures or a lumbar drain.
