# Tuberculosis & Mycobacterial Infections (結核病及分枝桿菌感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Tuberculosis and NTM
* **Tuberculosis (結核病)**: Chronic, necrotizing granulomatous communicable disease primarily caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (MTB).
* **Non-Tuberculous Mycobacteria (NTM) (非結核分枝桿菌)**: Opportunistic environmental mycobacteria (e.g., *M. avium* complex [MAC], *M. kansasii*, *M. abscessus*) causing chronic pulmonary or disseminated disease, strictly in structurally damaged lungs (e.g., Bronchiectasis) or immunocompromised hosts.

### Etiological Pathogens
* **M. tuberculosis complex (MTBC)**: Includes *M. tuberculosis*, *M. bovis*, *M. africanum*. 
* **Obligate aerobe**: Non-motile, non-spore-forming bacillus.
* **Acid-Fast Bacillus (AFB) (抗酸桿菌)**: Lipid-rich cell wall (mycolic acid) retains carbolfuchsin dye despite acid-alcohol washing.

### Epidemiological Profile in Hong Kong
* **Endemicity**: Intermediate-to-high burden region. 
* **Notification**: Statutory notifiable disease to the Department of Health (DH) Tuberculosis & Chest Service.
* **Demographics**: Bimodal peak (young immigrants/foreign domestic workers & elderly >65 yrs with reactivation).
* **Vaccination**: Universal neonatal BCG (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin) vaccination program since 1952 (prevents severe childhood disseminated TB/meningitis, but poor efficacy against adult pulmonary TB).
* **Occupational Risk**: Silicotuberculosis (矽肺結核) prevalent among local construction/tunneling workers.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Transmission and Primary Infection
* **Transmission**: Airborne droplet nuclei (<5 µm) inhalation from +ve sputum index case.
* **Alveolar Phagocytosis**: MTB enters alveoli → ingested by alveolar macrophages.
* **Intracellular Survival**: MTB cord factor (Trehalose dimycolate) inhibits phagosome-lysosome fusion → unopposed intracellular bacillary replication.
* **Ghon Focus**: Localized granulomatous inflammation in mid/lower lung zones.
* **Ghon Complex**: Ghon focus + ipsilateral hilar lymphadenopathy.

### Host Immune Response and Granuloma Formation
* **Type IV Hypersensitivity (Type 4 超敏反應)**: Develops 2-4 wks post-infection.
* **Th1 Pathway**: CD4+ T-cells release IFN-γ → activates macrophages.
* **Epithelioid Macrophages**: Activated macrophages transform into epithelioid cells & fuse into Langhans giant cells.
* **Caseous Necrosis (乾酪性壞死)**: Central acellular lipid-rich necrotic core restricts mycobacterial growth (hypoxic/acidic environment). 
* **Latent TB Infection (LTBI) (潛伏性結核感染)**: Fibrotic encapsulation of caseating granulomas with viable dormant bacilli.

### Reactivation Tuberculosis (Post-Primary TB)
* **Trigger**: Waning cellular immunity (HIV, DM, Anti-TNF-α biologics, senescence, malnutrition).
* **Localization**: Apical and posterior segments of upper lobes (highest V/Q ratio → highest pO2 favors aerobic MTB growth).
* **Cavitation (空洞形成)**: Liquefaction of caseous center → ruptures into bronchi → massive airborne dissemination & highly infectious sputum.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Detailed History & System-Specific Physical Signs ONLY)

### Pulmonary TB History (肺結核病史)
* **Chronic Cough (慢性咳嗽)**: Insidious onset, >3 weeks duration.
* **Sputum**: Initially dry → progresses to mucopurulent.
* **Hemoptysis (咯血)**: Blood-streaked sputum to massive volume.
* **B-Symptoms**:
  * **Night sweats (盜汗)** (drenching, requiring change of clothes).
  * **Fever** (low-grade, classically afternoon/evening spikes).
  * **Weight loss (體重下降)** (unintentional).
* **Pleuritic Chest Pain**: If pleural extension occurs.

### Extrapulmonary TB History
* **TB Lymphadenitis (Scrofula) (瘰癧)**: Painless, slowly enlarging cervical neck mass, may develop discharging sinus.
* **Genitourinary TB**: Sterile pyuria, hematuria, frequency, pelvic pain.
* **TB Meningitis**: Subacute insidious headache, photophobia, confusion, cranial nerve palsies (CN VI).
* **Skeletal TB (Pott's Disease) (波特氏病)**: Chronic back pain, lower limb weakness/paresthesia (spinal cord compression).

### General Physical Examination
* **Cachexia (惡病質)**: Muscle wasting, hollow cheeks.
* **Clubbing (杵狀指)**: Indicates advanced lung destruction/bronchiectasis.
* **Erythema Nodosum (結節性紅斑)**: Tender red nodules on anterior shins (immunologic reaction).

### Respiratory System Examination
* **Inspection**: Asymmetrical chest expansion, apical indrawing.
* **Palpation**: Tracheal deviation (towards fibrotic side).
* **Percussion**: 
  * **Stony dullness**: Concurrent TB pleural effusion.
  * **Dullness**: Apical consolidation/fibrosis.
* **Auscultation**: 
  * **Post-tussive apical crepitations/crackles (肺尖爆裂音)** (classic sign).
  * **Amphoric/Bronchial breathing**: Over large apical cavities.
  * **↓ Breath sounds**: Over effusions.

### Extrapulmonary Signs
* **Lymph Nodes**: Matted, firm, non-tender cervical/supraclavicular lymphadenopathy (colloquially 'cold abscess' if fluctuant).
* **Spine**: Gibbus deformity (acute kyphosis) localized to thoracolumbar junction.
* **Ocular**: Choroidal tubercles on fundoscopy (pathognomonic for miliary TB).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features

### Pulmonary Diagnostic Differentials
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Discriminatory Features vs Pulmonary TB |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lung Carcinoma (肺癌)** | Heavy smoking Hx, older age, hard/fixed supraclavicular node, clubbing more prominent, persistent localized wheeze, hemoptysis often precedes cough. |
| **Non-Tuberculous Mycobacteria (NTM)** | Indistinguishable clinically. Tends to affect elderly females with chronic cough (Lady Windermere syndrome). Requires specific culture +ve for diagnosis. |
| **Bronchiectasis (支氣管擴張)** | Copious purulent sputum daily (cupfuls), coarse pan-inspiratory crackles, often basal involvement (unless post-TB). |
| **Bacterial Pneumonia (細菌性肺炎)** | Acute onset (<1 wk), high-grade fever, neutrophil leukocytosis, rapid clinical deterioration. |
| **Sarcoidosis (結節病)** | Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy on CXR, non-caseating granulomas on biopsy, hypercalcemia, uveitis. |
| **Pulmonary Lymphoma (淋巴瘤)** | Massive generalized lymphadenopathy, prominent hepatosplenomegaly, rapid progression of B-symptoms. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations (Bedside, Bloods, Imaging, Biopsy & Diagnostic Criteria)

### Bedside Investigations
* **SpO2 & Vital Signs**: Assess respiratory failure, pyrexia.
* **Tuberculin Skin Test (TST) / Mantoux Test (結核菌素皮試)**: 
  * Intradermal injection of PPD (Purified Protein Derivative).
  * Reads induration (not erythema) at 48-72 hrs.
  * +ve result implies delayed hypersensitivity (does not distinguish LTBI vs Active TB vs prior BCG).

### Blood Investigations
* **Interferon-Gamma Release Assay (IGRA) / QuantiFERON-TB Gold**: 
  * Measures IFN-γ released by sensitized T-cells exposed to specific MTB antigens (ESAT-6, CFP-10).
  * Unaffected by prior BCG vaccination (highly specific for MTB infection).
* **HIV Serology (CRITICAL)**: Mandatory for ALL suspected/confirmed TB cases in HK.
* **Fasting Glucose/HbA1c**: Screen for undiagnosed DM (major risk factor).
* **Baseline LFT / RFT**: Mandatory before initiating hepatotoxic anti-TB drugs.

### Microbiological & Molecular Diagnostics (Sputum)
* **Induction**: Early morning deep cough sputum x3 consecutive days. 
* **Smear Microscopy**: Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain or Auramine-Rhodamine fluorescence for Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB). Rapid but low sensitivity; does not differentiate MTB from NTM.
* **Xpert MTB/RIF (GeneXpert) (快速核酸擴增試驗)**: 
  * Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT).
  * Rapidly confirms MTB presence AND detects *rpoB* gene mutation (Rifampicin resistance) within 2 hrs.
* **Mycobacterial Culture**: 
  * **Solid**: Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) medium (takes 4-8 weeks).
  * **Liquid**: MGIT (Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube) (takes 1-3 weeks).
  * **Gold standard** for definitive diagnosis and phenotypic drug susceptibility testing (DST).

### Radiological Imaging
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR) (胸部X光)**:
  * **Primary TB**: Hilar adenopathy, Ghon focus, lower/mid-zone infiltrates.
  * **Reactivation TB**: Upper lobe fibrocavitary changes, apical volume loss.
  * **Miliary TB (粟粒性結核)**: Diffuse 1-2mm uniform nodular shadowing (millet seeds) throughout both lung fields.
* **High-Resolution CT (HRCT) Thorax**: 'Tree-in-bud' nodules (endobronchial spread), highly sensitive for occult cavities or mediastinal lymphadenopathy.

### Invasive Procedures & Biopsy
* **Bronchoscopy (支氣管鏡檢查)**: Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) if patient cannot produce sputum or smear-negative but high clinical suspicion.
* **Pleural/Lymph Node Biopsy**: 
  * Histology: Caseating granulomas, Langhans giant cells, epithelioid histiocytes.
  * Pleural fluid analysis: Exudate, lymphocyte-predominant, very high Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) (>40 U/L).

### Diagnostic Criteria for Active Pulmonary TB
* **Microbiological (+ve TB)**: Culture positive for MTBC OR Smear +ve with +ve MTB NAAT.
* **Clinical/Radiological (-ve TB)**: Culture -ve but high clinical/radiological suspicion + decision by physician to treat with full anti-TB course.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation (Flares/Emergencies)

### Massive Hemoptysis (>600 mL/24h) Management (大咯血處理)
* **Airway/Breathing**: Endotracheal intubation with large-bore tube if airway compromised. Double-lumen tube to isolate bleeding lung if necessary.
* **Positioning**: Place patient in lateral decubitus position with bleeding side DOWN (prevents blood spillage into healthy lung).
* **Hemodynamic Resuscitation**: Large bore IV access, cross-match blood, massive transfusion protocol.
* **Bronchoscopy**: Rigid bronchoscopy for cold saline lavage, topical adrenaline, or balloon tamponade.
* **Definitive Rx**: Urgent Bronchial Artery Embolization (BAE) via Interventional Radiology. Surgical lobectomy as last resort.

### Spontaneous Pneumothorax (Ruptured Cavity)
* **Decompression**: Urgent insertion of wide-bore intercostal chest drain (underwater seal). 
* **Note**: High risk of broncho-pleural fistula and empyema requiring cardiothoracic surgical intervention (VATS/Decortication).

### Acute Corticosteroid Indications in TB
* **TB Meningitis**: IV Dexamethasone (↓ mortality and neurological deficits from basal exudate).
* **TB Pericarditis**: Prevents progression to constrictive pericarditis.
* **Miliary TB with ARDS**: Prevents fatal respiratory failure.
* **Adrenal TB**: Hydrocortisone replacement for Addisonian crisis.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies (Pharmacology & Lifestyle)

### Core Principles of Anti-TB Pharmacotherapy
* **DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course)**: Mandatory supervised pill swallowing at DH chest clinics to ensure compliance and prevent MDR-TB.
* **First-Line Regimen (Standard 6-Month Course)**:
  * **Intensive Phase (Initial 2 months)**: HRZE (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol).
  * **Continuation Phase (Subsequent 4 months)**: HR (Isoniazid, Rifampicin).
* **Extended Duration**: Prolonged to 9-12 months for TB Meningitis, CNS TB, Bone/Joint TB.

### First-Line Anti-TB Pharmacology & Monitoring
| Drug | Mechanism of Action | Key Co-prescription / Dosage Rules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Isoniazid (H)** (異煙肼) | Inhibits mycolic acid synthesis (Bactericidal) | MUST co-prescribe **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) 10mg/day** to prevent peripheral neuropathy. |
| **Rifampicin (R)** (利福平) | Inhibits RNA polymerase (Bactericidal) | Potent CYP450 inducer (requires dose adjustment of warfarin, OCPs, antiretrovirals). Take on empty stomach. |
| **Pyrazinamide (Z)** (吡嗪酰胺) | Intracellular bactericidal inside acidic macrophages | Weight-based dosing. High hepatotoxicity risk. |
| **Ethambutol (E)** (乙胺丁醇) | Inhibits arabinosyl transferase (Bacteriostatic) | Renally cleared (require dose adjustment in CKD). Avoid in young children (cannot report visual changes). |

### Management of Latent TB Infection (LTBI)
* **Indications in HK**: Close contacts (<35 yrs), HIV+ve, Pre-Biologics/Anti-TNF therapy, Pre-transplant, Silicosis.
* **Regimen Options**:
  * **3HP**: Isoniazid (H) + Rifapentine (P) weekly for 12 weeks (3 months). Directly observed.
  * **6H**: Isoniazid daily for 6 months.

### Management of MDR-TB and NTM
* **MDR-TB (Multi-Drug Resistant TB)**: Resistant to at least Isoniazid and Rifampicin.
  * **Algorithm**: Stop HR. Consult microbiologist/TB specialist. Use Bedaquiline, Linezolid, Fluoroquinolones (Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin), Clofazimine, Cycloserine. Requires minimum 9-18 months therapy.
* **MAC (Mycobacterium avium complex) Lung Disease**:
  * **Algorithm**: Macrolide (Clarithromycin/Azithromycin) + Rifampicin/Rifabutin + Ethambutol. Treat until culture negative for 12 months (often ~18 months total).

### Infection Control & Lifestyle
* **Isolation**: Airborne infection isolation room (negative pressure) upon admission. N95 respirators for all staff.
* **De-isolation criteria**: Minimum 2 weeks of compliant appropriate therapy AND 3 consecutive negative morning AFB smears.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Risk Stratification for Reactivation TB
* **Extreme Risk (RR >20)**: HIV/AIDS (CD4 <200), Organ transplant on immunosuppression.
* **High Risk (RR 10-20)**: Silicosis, Chronic Renal Failure (on dialysis), Anti-TNF-α biologics.
* **Moderate Risk (RR 2-10)**: Diabetes Mellitus (poorly controlled), Prolonged systemic corticosteroids, Malnutrition/Underweight, Heavy smoking.

### Prognostic Indicators (Poor Outcomes)
* Delayed diagnosis > 4 weeks.
* Extensive cavitation or miliary dissemination.
* Multi-drug resistant (MDR-TB) or Extensively drug-resistant (XDR-TB) strains.
* Co-infection with HIV (high risk of paradoxical IRIS - Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome upon starting ARVs).
* Baseline elevated transaminases (limits drug tolerability).

## 9. Multi-System Complications (End-Organ Damage & Drug Adverse Effects)

### Structural & Pulmonary Complications
* **Post-TB Bronchiectasis**: Chronic suppuration and recurrent hemoptysis in fibrotic destroyed lobes.
* **Aspergilloma (Fungus Ball)**: *Aspergillus* colonizes healed TB cavities. Presents as severe intermittent hemoptysis. CXR shows 'Monod sign' (air crescent).
* **Cor Pulmonale**: Right heart failure secondary to extensive fibrotic lung destruction causing pulmonary hypertension.
* **Empyema Thoracis**: Purulent pleural effusion requiring prolonged drainage.

### Systemic Complications
* **Secondary Amyloidosis (AA type)**: Chronic inflammation → Serum Amyloid A deposition in kidneys (nephrotic syndrome).
* **Pott's Paraplegia**: Spinal cord compression from collapsed vertebrae.

### Anti-TB Drug Adverse Effects (Toxicity Profile)
* **Hepatotoxicity (Drug-Induced Liver Injury - DILI)**: 
  * Triggered by Z > H > R. 
  * **Action**: Stop all HRZ if ALT >3x upper limit of normal (with symptoms) OR >5x ULN (without symptoms). Reintroduce sequentially once normalized.
* **Isoniazid (H) Specific**: Peripheral sensory neuropathy (glove & stocking), Lupus-like syndrome (Anti-histone Ab +ve), psychosis.
* **Rifampicin (R) Specific**: Orange/red discoloration of urine/tears (benign but frightens patient), Flu-like syndrome, Thrombocytopenia.
* **Pyrazinamide (Z) Specific**: Hyperuricemia (precipitates acute gout flares), severe polyarthralgia.
* **Ethambutol (E) Specific**: Retrobulbar optic neuritis (↓ visual acuity, red-green color blindness, central scotoma). Baseline Snellen/Ishihara testing mandatory.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a post-BCG TST reaction from true latent TB infection? Answer: Perform an IGRA (QuantiFERON gold blood test), as it is highly specific to MTB antigens (ESAT-6, CFP-10) and is entirely unaffected by prior BCG vaccination.
* Viva Question: What is the significance of sterile pyuria in a young patient? Answer: Highly suspicious for Genitourinary Tuberculosis; requires early morning urine sent for AFB smear and prolonged mycobacterial culture.
* Viva Question: Why do we observe patients swallowing their TB medications in Hong Kong? Answer: DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment) ensures 100% compliance, which is the most effective public health strategy to prevent the emergence of Multi-Drug Resistant TB (MDR-TB).
* Trap: Assuming a patient with a negative AFB sputum smear does not have active pulmonary TB. Pearl: AFB smear has poor sensitivity (~50-60%). A negative smear never rules out TB. Always await the Xpert MTB/RIF and the 8-week mycobacterial culture.
* Trap: Starting TB medications and forgetting to adjust the patient's other routine prescriptions. Pearl: Rifampicin is a potent CYP450 inducer. You must aggressively monitor and increase the doses of concurrent Warfarin, oral contraceptives, and steroids, or they will fail.
* Trap: Ignoring mild numbness in the fingers of a patient on anti-TB therapy. Pearl: This is Isoniazid-induced peripheral neuropathy. Always preemptively co-prescribe Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) 10mg daily to every patient starting DOTS.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing 'pneumonia' in an upper lobe consolidation and discharging them on Levofloxacin. Fluoroquinolones have anti-mycobacterial activity; this will partially treat underlying TB, mask the symptoms, and dangerously induce fluoroquinolone-resistant TB.
* Pitfall: Failing to check visual acuity before starting first-line therapy. Ethambutol causes irreversible optic neuritis and red-green color blindness. Always perform baseline Snellen and Ishihara chart tests before prescribing.
