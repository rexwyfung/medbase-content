# Parkinson's Disease (柏金遜症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology
### Clinical Definition
* **Progressive Neurodegenerative Movement Disorder:** Characterized by dopaminergic neuron loss in the basal ganglia.
* **Core Motor Triad:** Bradykinesia (動作遲緩), Resting Tremor (靜止性震顫), Rigidity (肌肉僵硬).

### Etiologies
* **Idiopathic:** Most common (~90%).
* **Genetic (Early-Onset):** *SNCA*, *LRRK2* (autosomal dominant), *PARKIN*, *PINK1* (autosomal recessive).
* **Environmental/Toxins:** MPTP exposure, heavy metals (manganese), agricultural pesticides (rotenone).

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong Context)
* **Prevalence:** ↑ with age; ~1% in individuals >60 years old.
* **Burden:** Significant cause of disability in HK's ageing population; highly managed in HA Neurology & Geriatric outpatient clinics.

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms
### Molecular Hallmarks
* **Neuronal Loss:** Degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the **substantia nigra pars compacta (黑質)**.
* **Lewy Bodies (路易氏體):** Intracellular eosinophilic inclusions containing aggregated **α-synuclein**.

### Basal Ganglia Circuitry Imbalance
* **Loss of Striatal Dopamine:** ↓ dopamine release to the putamen and caudate.
* **Direct Pathway (Excitatory) Underactivity:** D1 receptors under-stimulated → ↓ facilitation of movement.
* **Indirect Pathway (Inhibitory) Overactivity:** D2 receptors under-stimulated → ↑ inhibition of movement.
* **Net Effect:** Cortical motor suppression → Hypokinesia/Bradykinesia.

### Braak Staging (Pathological Spread)
* **Stages 1-2 (Pre-motor):** Olfactory bulb & lower brainstem (medulla) → Anosmia, REM sleep behaviour disorder, autonomic dysfunction.
* **Stages 3-4 (Motor):** Midbrain (substantia nigra) → Classic motor symptoms.
* **Stages 5-6 (Cognitive):** Neocortical involvement → Dementia, psychosis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment
### Detailed History (Motor & Non-Motor Prodrome)
* **Motor Hx:** Insidious onset, highly **asymmetrical/unilateral** initially. Clumsiness, recurrent falls, micrographia (small handwriting).
* **Non-Motor Prodrome:** Years before motor onset; includes anosmia (loss of smell), constipation, REM sleep behaviour disorder (acting out dreams), depression.
* **Medication Hx:** Explicitly rule out neuroleptics (antipsychotics) and antiemetics (e.g., metoclopramide) to exclude drug-induced parkinsonism.

### System-Specific Physical Signs
* **General/Face:** Hypomimia (mask-like facies), ↓ blink rate, drooling (sialorrhea), soft/monotone voice (hypophonia).
* **Tremor (4-6 Hz):** Asymmetrical resting "pill-rolling" tremor; diminishes with voluntary action, worsens with distraction/stress.
* **Tone:** **Lead-pipe rigidity** (constant resistance) OR **Cogwheel rigidity** (rigidity + tremor felt during passive movement).
* **Movement (Bradykinesia):** Fatiguing/decrement in amplitude and speed during repetitive tasks (e.g., finger tapping, hand opening/closing).
* **Gait & Posture:** Stooped posture, short shuffling steps (festination), ↓ arm swing on affected side, freezing of gait (especially turning or initiating).
* **Balance:** Postural instability (+ve Pull Test: patient takes >2 steps backward or falls when pulled from behind).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses & Discriminatory Features
### Atypical Parkinsonism (Parkinson-Plus Syndromes) vs. Idiopathic PD
| Condition | Discriminatory Features (Red Flags) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Idiopathic PD** | Unilateral onset, robust response to Levodopa, prominent resting tremor. |
| **Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP)** | Early postural instability (falls backward), **vertical supranuclear gaze palsy** (cannot look down), frontotemporal dementia. |
| **Multiple System Atrophy (MSA)** | Early/severe **autonomic failure** (postural hypotension, incontinence), prominent cerebellar signs, poor Levodopa response. |
| **Corticobasal Degeneration (CBD)** | Markedly asymmetric, **alien limb phenomenon**, cortical sensory loss, severe apraxia, myoclonus. |
| **Lewy Body Dementia (LBD)** | Early/fluctuating dementia, **visual hallucinations**, Parkinsonism starting <1 year from cognitive decline. |

### Secondary Parkinsonism & Essential Tremor
| Condition | Discriminatory Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Drug-Induced Parkinsonism** | Symmetrical onset, absence of rest tremor, history of D2 blockers (haloperidol, metoclopramide). |
| **Vascular Parkinsonism** | "Lower-half" Parkinsonism, broad-based gait, prominent pyramidal signs, stepwise deterioration. |
| **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)** | Triad: Gait apraxia ("magnetic gait"), urinary incontinence, dementia. |
| **Essential Tremor** | **Postural/Action tremor**, bilateral, +ve FHx, involves head/voice, improves with alcohol, NO bradykinesia. |

## 5. Tiered Investigations
### Bedside Tests
* **Lying & Standing BP:** Exclude prominent orthostatic hypotension (points towards MSA).
* **Cognitive Assessment (MoCA / MMSE):** Baseline cognitive screening (differentiate PD vs LBD).

### Blood Tests
* **Targeted Screens:** Routine bloods mostly normal. Check TFTs (exclude hypo/hyperthyroidism).
* **Ceruloplasmin & Serum Copper:** Mandatory in young-onset (<40 yrs) to exclude Wilson's disease.

### Neuroimaging (Anatomy & Function)
* **MRI Brain:** Primarily to exclude structural lesions (tumors), vascular disease, NPH, and Atypical Parkinsonism (e.g., "Hot cross bun" sign in MSA, "Hummingbird" sign in PSP).
* **DaTscan (I-123 Ioflupane SPECT):** Shows ↓ dopamine transporter uptake in striatum (asymmetric comma-to-dot sign). Used only if diagnosis is uncertain (differentiates PD/Atypical PD from Essential Tremor or Drug-induced parkinsonism).

### Diagnostic Criteria (MDS Clinical Criteria)
* **Prerequisite:** **Bradykinesia** PLUS at least one of: **Resting Tremor** OR **Rigidity**.
* **Supportive Criteria:** Clear/dramatic beneficial response to dopaminergic therapy, presence of levodopa-induced dyskinesia, olfactory loss.
* **Absolute Exclusion Criteria (Red Flags):** Cerebellar abnormalities, downward vertical gaze palsy, lack of response to high-dose Levodopa.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation
### Parkinsonism-Hyperpyrexia Syndrome (PHS)
* **Pathophysiology:** Rare, life-threatening emergency resembling Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome. Triggered by abrupt withdrawal or poor absorption of dopaminergic medications.
* **Clinical Presentation:** Altered mental status, severe "lead-pipe" rigidity, hyperthermia, autonomic instability, markedly ↑ CK (rhabdomyolysis).
* **Resuscitation & Management:**
  * **ABCDEs & Supportive:** Aggressive IV hydration, active cooling.
  * **Pharmacology:** Immediately **restart Levodopa/dopaminergics** (use NG tube/IV if NBM).
  * **Refractory Cases:** Dantrolene (direct muscle relaxant) or Bromocriptine (dopamine agonist).

### Acute Psychosis & Severe Agitation
* **Trigger:** Often iatrogenic (dose ↑ of dopamine agonists or anticholinergics), infection (UTI/pneumonia), or metabolic derangement.
* **Management:**
  * Treat underlying precipitant (e.g., sepsis).
  * Step-wise ↓ of PD medications (stop anticholinergics/MAO-B inhibitors first, then dopamine agonists, Levodopa last).
  * If antipsychotic needed: Use **Quetiapine** or **Clozapine** (least likely to worsen motor symptoms). Strictly AVOID typical antipsychotics (e.g., Haloperidol).

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies
### General Treatment Philosophy (HA Context)
* **No cure/disease-modifying Rx exists.** Treatment is purely symptomatic.
* **Young (<70 yrs) with mild symptoms:** Goal is to delay Levodopa usage to prevent motor complications (dyskinesias).
* **Elderly (>70 yrs) or significant motor impairment:** Levodopa is first-line for maximum efficacy and QoL.

### Step-Up Pharmacological Algorithm
| Disease Stage | First-Line Therapy | Mechanism / Clinical Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild / Early (Younger)** | **Dopamine Agonists:** Pramipexole, Ropinirole, Rotigotine (patch). OR **MAO-B Inhibitors:** Rasagiline, Selegiline. | Directly stimulate DA receptors or prevent DA breakdown. Delays Levodopa start. Beware of Impulse Control Disorders (gambling). |
| **Moderate / Elderly / Prominent Motor Sx** | **Levodopa + DDC Inhibitor:** Co-careldopa (Sinemet) or Co-beneldopa (Madopar). | Dopamine precursor. DDC inhibitor prevents peripheral breakdown (↓ nausea). Most effective for bradykinesia/rigidity. |
| **Prominent Tremor (Younger)** | **Anticholinergics:** Benzhexol (Trihexyphenidyl). | Restores Dopamine/Ach balance. Avoid in elderly (causes confusion, urinary retention, glaucoma). |
| **Motor Fluctuations ("Wearing-Off")** | Fractionate Levodopa doses. Add **COMT Inhibitor:** Entacapone. | Prevents peripheral Levodopa degradation → extends half-life and "ON" time. |
| **Levodopa-Induced Dyskinesia** | Add **Amantadine**. | NMDA receptor antagonist. Relieves peak-dose dyskinesias. |
| **Severe "OFF" Freezing Episodes** | **Apomorphine** (SC Injection/Pump). | Potent, rapid-acting dopamine agonist rescue therapy. Requires Domperidone pre-treatment for severe nausea. |

### Non-Motor Symptom Pharmacology
* **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Fludrocortisone, Midodrine.
* **Depression:** SSRIs (Sertraline, Citalopram) or SNRIs.
* **REM Sleep Behaviour Disorder:** Clonazepam or Melatonin.
* **Dementia:** Cholinesterase inhibitors (Rivastigmine, Donepezil).

### Device-Assisted & Surgical Therapies
* **Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS):** Target: Subthalamic Nucleus (STN) or Globus Pallidus internus (GPi).
  * **Indications:** Advanced PD with severe motor fluctuations or dyskinesias refractory to medical therapy, but still responsive to Levodopa.
  * **Contraindications:** Severe dementia, atypical parkinsonism, prominent psychiatric disease.
* **Duodopa Pump (Levodopa-Carbidopa Intestinal Gel):** Continuous intra-jejunal infusion for smoothing out severe motor fluctuations.

### Multidisciplinary & Lifestyle Modification
* **Physiotherapy (PT):** Gait training, balance exercises, cueing strategies (visual lines on floor, auditory metronome) for freezing.
* **Speech Therapy (ST):** Swallow assessment (prevent aspiration), Lee Silverman Voice Treatment (LSVT) for hypophonia.
* **Dietetics:** Protein redistribution (avoid high-protein meals with Levodopa doses as amino acids compete for GI absorption).

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis
### Hoehn and Yahr Scale (Disease Staging)
| Stage | Clinical Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1** | Unilateral involvement only. |
| **Stage 2** | Bilateral or midline involvement, WITHOUT impairment of balance. |
| **Stage 3** | Mild to moderate bilateral disease; some postural instability, but physically independent. |
| **Stage 4** | Severe disability; still able to walk or stand unassisted (but markedly impaired). |
| **Stage 5** | Wheelchair-bound or bedridden unless aided. |

### Prognosis & Disease Trajectory
* **Progression:** Highly variable, but inevitably progressive.
* **Mortality:** Life expectancy is near normal, but death is usually due to complications of immobility (e.g., aspiration pneumonia from dysphagia, sepsis from pressure sores, complications from falls).

## 9. Multi-System Complications
### Neurological & Psychiatric
* **Motor Fluctuations:** "Wearing-off" (symptoms return before next dose) and sudden "ON-OFF" phenomenon (unpredictable shifts between mobility and immobility).
* **Levodopa-Induced Dyskinesias:** Choreiform, writhing involuntary movements occurring at peak Levodopa plasma concentrations.
* **Impulse Control Disorders:** Pathological gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating (Classically caused by Dopamine Agonists).
* **Dementia & Psychosis:** Late-stage cognitive decline and visual hallucinations.

### Musculoskeletal & Gastrointestinal
* **MSK:** High fall risk → neck of femur (NOF) fractures. Camptocormia (severe forward flexion of the thoracolumbar spine), Pisa syndrome (lateral trunk flexion).
* **GI/Swallowing:** Severe dysphagia leading to silent aspiration (leading cause of mortality). Severe refractory constipation.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the mechanism of Carbidopa in Sinemet? Answer: It is a peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitor. It cannot cross the blood-brain barrier. It prevents peripheral conversion of Levodopa to Dopamine, which minimizes peripheral side effects (like nausea/arrhythmias) and maximizes Levodopa availability to the CNS.
* Trap: Assuming a resting tremor is required to diagnose Parkinson's disease. Pearl: Around 20-30% of PD patients do not exhibit a tremor. Diagnosis requires Bradykinesia + (Tremor OR Rigidity).
* Pitfall: Prescribing Haloperidol or Metoclopramide for delirium or nausea in a PD patient. These are central D2 receptor antagonists and will precipitate a catastrophic worsening of motor symptoms. Use Quetiapine (for psychosis) or Domperidone (for nausea, as it does not cross the BBB).
* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate Essential Tremor from Parkinsonian Tremor? Answer: Essential Tremor is an action/postural tremor that is often bilateral, frequently involves the head/voice, and improves with alcohol. PD tremor is a resting "pill-rolling" tremor that is asymmetric, initially spares the head, and dampens with voluntary movement.
* Trap: Assuming an elderly patient with new-onset Parkinsonism and urinary incontinence has Idiopathic PD. Pearl: Always consider Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH) or Multiple System Atrophy (MSA). Incontinence occurs very late in idiopathic PD.
* Pitfall: Withholding Levodopa doses when a PD patient is 'Nil By Mouth' (NBM) for surgery. Abrupt cessation can precipitate life-threatening Parkinsonism-Hyperpyrexia Syndrome. Always convert to a rotigotine patch or insert an NG tube for administration.
