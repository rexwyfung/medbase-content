# Osteomalacia & Paget's Disease (骨軟化症及佩吉特氏骨病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Osteomalacia
* **Osteomalacia (骨軟化症):** Defective mineralization of newly formed osteoid matrix in mature trabecular and cortical bone.
* **Rickets (佝僂病):** Defective mineralization of cartilage at the epiphyseal growth plates (occurs in children before growth plate fusion).

### Etiologies of Osteomalacia
* **Vitamin D Deficiency:** Most common cause globally. In Hong Kong, prevalent among institutionalized elderly due to poor sunlight exposure and inadequate dietary intake.
* **Malabsorption:** Celiac disease, short bowel syndrome, post-gastrectomy/bariatric surgery, chronic pancreatitis.
* **Renal Pathology:** Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) → ↓ 1-alpha-hydroxylase activity (inability to activate Vit D). Renal tubular acidosis (Type 2).
* **Hepatic Pathology:** Chronic liver disease (Cirrhosis) → failure to 25-hydroxylate Vitamin D.
* **Hypophosphatemia:** Tumour-induced osteomalacia (FGF23-secreting mesenchymal tumours), X-linked hypophosphatemia.
* **Drug-Induced:** Anticonvulsants (Phenytoin, Carbamazepine → induce hepatic CYP450, accelerating Vit D breakdown).

### Clinical Definition & Epidemiology of Paget's Disease
* **Paget's Disease of Bone (佩吉特氏骨病 / PDB):** Localized, focal disorder of bone remodeling characterized by excessive, disorganized osteoclastic bone resorption followed by chaotic osteoblastic bone formation, resulting in mechanically weak, highly vascular woven bone.
* **Epidemiology:** Rare in Asian populations (including Hong Kong); much more prevalent in Caucasians (UK, Australia). Usually presents in patients >55 years old.
* **Etiology:** Likely combination of genetic susceptibility (**SQSTM1** gene mutation) and slow virus infection (Paramyxovirus/measles RNA found in osteoclasts).

## 2. Systemic Pathophysiology & Molecular Mechanisms

### Vitamin D Metabolism & Osteomalacia Pathophysiology
* **Skin synthesis:** 7-Dehydrocholesterol + UV-B → Cholecalciferol (Vit D3).
* **Hepatic hydroxylation:** Vit D3 → 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] via 25-hydroxylase.
* **Renal hydroxylation:** 25(OH)D → 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D [1,25(OH)2D] (Active Form / Calcitriol) via 1-alpha-hydroxylase (enzyme stimulated by PTH).
* **Bone Mineralization Failure:** ↓ Active Vit D → ↓ Intestinal Ca & PO4 absorption → Hypocalcemia → Secondary hyperparathyroidism (↑ PTH). ↑ PTH → renal Ca reabsorption & massive renal PO4 wasting (phosphaturia). Resultant ↓ systemic Ca and ↓ PO4 prevents physiological precipitation of calcium-hydroxyapatite onto osteoid.

### Paget's Disease Pathophysiology & Remodeling Phases
* **1. Osteolytic Phase:** Intense, focal osteoclastic resorption. Osteoclasts are massive, multi-nucleated (up to 100 nuclei), and hyper-responsive to RANKL.
* **2. Mixed Phase:** Compensatory massive osteoblastic activity forming new bone rapidly but chaotically.
* **3. Osteosclerotic Phase:** Osteoblastic activity dominates. Resorption declines, leaving dense, sclerotic, hypervascular "woven bone" (編織骨) that is structurally unsound and prone to bowing/fracture.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Osteomalacia History & Physical Examination
* **Bone Pain:** Diffuse, dull, aching bone pain, worse on weight-bearing. Classical locations: lower back, pelvis, hips, ribs.
* **Muscle Weakness:** **Proximal myopathy (近端肌肉病變)** is a hallmark. Difficulty rising from a chair or climbing stairs.
* **Gait:** Waddling gait (搖擺步態) due to proximal pelvic girdle weakness.
* **Palpation:** Exquisite bone tenderness (e.g., over tibia, ribs) upon firm manual pressure.
* **Neurological Signs:** Rarely, Chvostek's sign or Trousseau's sign (+ve) if severe concomitant hypocalcemia.

### Paget's Disease History & Physical Examination
* **Presentation:** ~70% asymptomatic (incidental biochemical/radiological finding).
* **Pain:** Deep, boring pain at rest, worse at night (unlike osteoarthritis which is worse with movement). Can also have secondary osteoarthritis pain if peri-articular involvement.
* **Skull Deformity:** Frontal bossing, increasing hat size (**Skull enlargement - 頭骨增大**).
* **Long Bone Deformity:** Anterior bowing of the tibia ("sabre tibia"), bowing of the femur.
* **Neurological Signs:** **Sensorineural deafness (感音神經性耳聾)** due to CN VIII compression at the narrowed auditory foramen. Cranial nerve palsies.
* **Vascular Signs:** Affected bones may be warm to touch (hypervascularity).

## 5. Tiered Investigations

### Blood Investigations for Bone Metabolism
* **Serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D]:** Primary diagnostic marker for osteomalacia. <50 nmol/L = deficiency; <25 nmol/L = severe deficiency.
* **Bone Profile:** Calcium (adjusted for albumin), Phosphate, ALP (specifically bone-specific ALP isoenzyme).
* **Parathyroid Hormone (PTH):** Elevated (secondary hyperparathyroidism) in osteomalacia. Normal in uncomplicated Paget's.
* **Urine:** 24-hour urinary calcium (usually low in osteomalacia).
* **Bone Turnover Markers (Paget's):** Procollagen type 1 N-terminal propeptide (P1NP) to assess osteoblast activity and monitor treatment response.

### Radiological Imaging for Osteomalacia
* **Plain Radiographs (X-rays):** Diagnostic hallmark is **Looser's zones (假性骨折/Pseudofractures)**. Broad, transverse, radiolucent bands perpendicular to the bone cortex, classically found at the medial femoral neck, pubic rami, and lateral scapula.
* **Bone Mineral Density (DEXA):** Shows profoundly low bone density (often mimics osteoporosis T-scores), but is not diagnostic of osteomalacia itself.
* **Bone Biopsy:** Gold standard but rarely needed. Demonstrates widened, unmineralized osteoid seams on tetracycline labeling.

### Radiological Imaging for Paget's Disease
* **Radionuclide Bone Scan (Tc-99m):** Most sensitive tool to map the full extent of skeletal involvement. Shows intense, focal radiotracer uptake ("hot spots") at all active pagetic sites.
* **Plain Radiographs (X-rays) - specific signs:**
* **Skull:** "Osteoporosis circumscripta" (early lytic), followed by **"Cotton wool" appearance (棉花狀外觀)** (late sclerotic).
* **Long Bones:** "Blade of grass" or "V-shaped" advancing osteolytic edge.
* **Spine:** "Picture frame" vertebral body (thickened cortices with central lucency) or "Ivory vertebra" (dense sclerosis).

## 6. Acute Exacerbation Management & Resuscitation

### Acute Symptomatic Hypocalcemia in Osteomalacia
* **Indication:** Tetany, seizures, or severe QT prolongation due to profound secondary hypocalcemia.
* **Pharmacotherapy:** 10 mL of 10% IV Calcium Gluconate over 10-15 minutes with continuous ECG monitoring.
* **Transition:** Follow with slow IV Calcium infusion, then oral Calcium + rapid Vit D replacement.

### Acute Neurological or Orthopaedic Emergencies in Paget's Disease
* **Spinal Cord Compression:** Severe pagetic vertebral enlargement causing acute myelopathy.
* **Medical Resuscitation:** High-dose IV Dexamethasone.
* **Surgical Resuscitation:** Urgent decompressive laminectomy (Neuro/Ortho surgery consult).
* **Acute Fractures:** Complete transverse fractures of pagetic long bones require internal fixation. High risk of excessive intra-operative bleeding due to pagetic hypervascularity.

## 7. Chronic Management Algorithm & Disease-Modifying Therapies

### Pharmacological Management Algorithm for Osteomalacia
* **Tier 1: Normal Renal Function (Deficiency)**
* **Loading Dose:** High-dose Cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3) e.g., 50,000 IU weekly for 8 weeks to replenish fat stores.
* **Maintenance:** Cholecalciferol 800-2,000 IU daily.
* **Adjunct:** Oral Calcium supplements (e.g., Calcium Carbonate) if dietary intake is poor.
* **Tier 2: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD stage 4/5)**
* **Activated Vitamin D:** **Alfacalcidol (1-alpha-hydroxycholecalciferol)** or **Calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol)**. The kidney cannot perform the final 1-alpha hydroxylation, so bypassing this step is mandatory.

### Pharmacological Management Algorithm for Paget's Disease
* **Indications for Treatment:** Bone pain, nerve compression, preventing deformity in weight-bearing bones, preparation for orthopaedic surgery (to ↓ bleeding), asymptomatic but ↑↑ ALP (controversial, but often treated).
* **First-line Agents: Bisphosphonates (雙磷酸鹽)** (Inhibit osteoclast action)
* **Gold Standard:** IV Zoledronic acid (Zoledronate) - single 5mg infusion provides remission for years. First-dose flu-like illness is common.
* **Alternative:** PO Alendronate or PO Risedronate (daily for 2-6 months). Must be taken upright, fasting, with water to avoid severe esophagitis.
* **Adjunct:** Ensure adequate Calcium and Vitamin D status *prior* to Bisphosphonate infusion to prevent severe acute hypocalcemia.
* **Symptomatic Relief:** NSAIDs or Paracetamol for secondary osteoarthritis.

## 8. Risk Stratification, Clinical Scoring Systems & Prognosis

### Monitoring Disease Activity in Paget's Disease
* **Biochemical Target:** Normalization of serum total ALP (or bone-specific ALP). Check every 3-6 months post-treatment, then annually.
* **Relapse Indicator:** Progressive rise in ALP >25% above the nadir post-treatment strongly suggests disease relapse requiring re-dosing of IV Zoledronate.

### Prognostic Factors in Osteomalacia
* **Reversibility:** Bone pain and proximal myopathy completely resolve within weeks/months of aggressive Vitamin D and Calcium replacement.
* **Radiological Healing:** Looser's zones typically fully ossify and heal within 6 months of therapy.

## 9. Multi-System Complications

### End-Organ Complications of Osteomalacia
* **Musculoskeletal:** Pseudofractures progressing to true pathological fractures, permanent bone deformities if pediatric (Rickets - e.g., genu varum/bow legs, rachitic rosary).
* **Neuromuscular:** Prolonged severe proximal myopathy leading to immobility and secondary deconditioning.

### End-Organ Complications of Paget's Disease
* **Oncological:** **Osteosarcoma (骨肉瘤)** (<1% of patients, but suspect if new sudden severe localized pain or rapidly swelling mass in a pagetic bone). Lethal and aggressive.
* **Cardiovascular:** High-output cardiac failure (due to massive arteriovenous shunting in highly vascular pagetic bone).
* **Neurological:** Cranial nerve palsies (CN II, V, VII, VIII) from skull foramina narrowing. Spinal canal stenosis. Hydrocephalus (due to basilar invagination blocking CSF flow).
* **Rheumatological:** Secondary Osteoarthritis (due to altered biomechanics from bowed long bones).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Prescribing standard Cholecalciferol (Vit D3) to a patient with End Stage Renal Failure (ESRF). Pearl: ESRF patients lack 1-alpha-hydroxylase; they MUST receive activated Vitamin D (Alfacalcidol or Calcitriol), otherwise osteomalacia will not improve.
* Viva Question: An elderly patient presents with an isolated grossly elevated ALP (e.g., >800 U/L), normal Bilirubin, normal ALT, and normal Calcium. What is the most likely diagnosis? Answer: Paget's Disease of Bone.
* Pitfall: Giving IV Zoledronate for Paget's disease without checking Vitamin D and Calcium levels first. This can trigger profound, life-threatening acute hypocalcemia as the bisphosphonate abruptly halts all bone resorption.
* Trap: Confusing Looser's zones (pseudofractures) with stress fractures on X-ray. Pearl: Looser's zones in osteomalacia often occur at non-weight-bearing sites (e.g., lateral scapula, ribs) and are typically bilateral and symmetrical, whereas stress fractures are unilateral at weight-bearing sites.
* Viva Question: What is the classic neurological complication of Paget's disease involving the skull? Answer: Sensorineural deafness due to bony overgrowth compressing the vestibulocochlear nerve (CN VIII) at the internal acoustic meatus.
