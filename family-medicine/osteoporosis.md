# Osteoporosis (骨質疏鬆症)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A systemic skeletal disease characterized by low bone mass and microarchitectural deterioration of bone tissue, leading to enhanced bone fragility and a consequent increase in fracture risk.
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common, especially in postmenopausal women and older men. It is a "silent disease" until a fracture occurs. Major cause of morbidity and mortality in the elderly (especially hip fractures).

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- Bone is a dynamic tissue constantly undergoing remodeling (resorption by osteoclasts and formation by osteoblasts).
- **Peak Bone Mass**: Achieved in the late 20s. Determined by genetics, nutrition (calcium/vitamin D), and weight-bearing exercise during youth.
- **Postmenopausal Osteoporosis**: The sharp decline in estrogen after menopause leads to increased osteoclast activity and rapid bone loss.
- **Age-Related Osteoporosis**: Gradual decline in osteoblast function and decreased calcium absorption with advancing age.

## 3. Risk Factors (危險因素)
- **Non-modifiable**: Advanced age, female gender, Caucasian or Asian ethnicity, family history of osteoporosis/fragility fractures, small body frame.
- **Modifiable**:
  - Low body weight (BMI < 19).
  - Sedentary lifestyle (lack of weight-bearing exercise).
  - Smoking.
  - Excessive alcohol intake.
  - Dietary deficiencies (Calcium, Vitamin D).
- **Secondary Causes (Medical conditions & Medications)**:
  - **Glucocorticoids (Steroids)**: The most common drug-induced cause (e.g., Prednisolone > 5mg/day for > 3 months).
  - Endocrine: Hyperthyroidism, Hyperparathyroidism, Cushing's syndrome, Hypogonadism.
  - Gastrointestinal: Malabsorption syndromes (Celiac disease), Inflammatory Bowel Disease.
  - Rheumatological: Rheumatoid Arthritis.
  - Medications: Aromatase inhibitors (breast cancer), Androgen deprivation therapy (prostate cancer), PPIs (long-term), Antiepileptics.

## 4. Clinical Features & Complications (臨床特徵與併發症)
- Osteoporosis itself is **asymptomatic**.
- The clinical consequence is a **Fragility Fracture** (a fracture resulting from low-energy trauma, such as a fall from standing height or less).
- **Common Fracture Sites**:
  - **Vertebral Compression Fractures**: Most common. Often asymptomatic, but can cause acute back pain, progressive loss of height, and kyphosis (Dowager's hump).
  - **Hip Fractures (Neck of femur)**: Most devastating. High 1-year mortality rate (~20-30%) and significant loss of independence.
  - **Distal Radius Fractures (Colles' fracture)**: Often the first sign of osteoporosis, typically occurring when breaking a fall with an outstretched hand.

## 5. Diagnosis & Screening (診斷與篩查)
- **Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry (DEXA / DXA Scan - 雙能量X光骨質密度檢查)**: The gold standard for measuring Bone Mineral Density (BMD). Usually measured at the lumbar spine and total hip.
- **T-score**: Compares the patient's BMD to the mean BMD of a healthy, young adult of the same sex.
  - **Normal**: T-score ≥ -1.0
  - **Osteopenia (Low bone mass)**: T-score between -1.0 and -2.5
  - **Osteoporosis**: **T-score ≤ -2.5**
  - **Severe Osteoporosis**: T-score ≤ -2.5 PLUS a fragility fracture.
- **Clinical Diagnosis**: Osteoporosis can be diagnosed clinically, regardless of the T-score, if a patient has sustained a fragility fracture (especially of the hip or spine).
- **FRAX Score**: A fracture risk assessment tool that calculates the 10-year probability of a major osteoporotic fracture and a hip fracture, based on clinical risk factors and (optionally) femoral neck BMD. Used to guide treatment decisions, especially in patients with osteopenia.

## 6. Management (治療與管理)
### 1. Lifestyle & Nutritional Interventions (For all patients)
- **Calcium**: Adequate intake (1000-1200 mg/day), preferably from diet (dairy, leafy greens). Supplement if dietary intake is insufficient.
- **Vitamin D**: Essential for calcium absorption. Target serum 25(OH)D > 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L). Supplementation (e.g., 800-1000 IU/day) is often required.
- **Exercise**: Regular weight-bearing exercise (walking, jogging, dancing) and muscle-strengthening/balance exercises (Tai Chi) to prevent falls.
- **Fall Prevention**: Crucial in the elderly. Review medications (sedatives, antihypertensives), check vision, remove home hazards (loose rugs, poor lighting).
- **Smoking cessation and moderation of alcohol**.

### 2. Pharmacological Therapy
- *Indications*: T-score ≤ -2.5, history of fragility fracture, or osteopenia with a high FRAX score.
- **Antiresorptive Agents (Inhibit osteoclasts)**:
  - **Bisphosphonates (雙磷酸鹽)**: First-line therapy.
    - *Examples*: Alendronate (oral weekly), Risedronate (oral weekly/monthly), Zoledronic acid (IV yearly).
    - *Instructions for oral*: Must be taken on an empty stomach, with a full glass of water, and the patient must remain upright for 30-60 minutes to prevent erosive esophagitis.
    - *Rare but serious side effects*: Osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ), Atypical femoral fractures (AFF) - risk increases with long-term use (>5 years). Consider a "drug holiday" after 3-5 years of stability.
  - **Denosumab (Prolia - 狄諾塞麥)**:
    - *Mechanism*: Monoclonal antibody against RANKL, potently inhibiting osteoclast formation.
    - *Administration*: Subcutaneous injection every 6 months.
    - *Note*: Cannot be stopped abruptly due to a rapid rebound increase in bone turnover and risk of multiple vertebral fractures. Must transition to a bisphosphonate if stopped.
  - **Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulators (SERMs)**: Raloxifene. Prevents vertebral fractures, also reduces breast cancer risk. Increases VTE risk.
  - **Menopausal Hormone Therapy (MHT)**: Effective for prevention, but usually reserved for women who also have severe menopausal symptoms.
- **Anabolic Agents (Stimulate osteoblasts - build new bone)**:
  - **Teriparatide (Forteo)**: Recombinant PTH. Daily subcutaneous injection. Used for severe osteoporosis (T-score < -3.0 or multiple fractures). Limited to 2 years of use.
  - **Romosozumab (Evenity)**: Monoclonal antibody against sclerostin. Dual effect (anabolic and antiresorptive).
