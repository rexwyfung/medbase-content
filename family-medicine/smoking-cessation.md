# Smoking Cessation (戒煙)

## 1. Epidemiology & Health Impact (流行病學與健康影響)
- **Epidemiology**: Smoking is the leading preventable cause of death worldwide. In Hong Kong, the smoking prevalence has been steadily declining but remains a significant public health issue.
- **Health Impact**:
  - **Cardiovascular**: Major risk factor for IHD, MI, stroke, peripheral arterial disease (PAD), and abdominal aortic aneurysm.
  - **Respiratory**: Primary cause of COPD, exacerbates asthma, increases risk of pneumonia and TB.
  - **Oncology**: Causes cancers of the lung, oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, esophagus, pancreas, bladder, kidney, and cervix.
  - **Reproductive**: Infertility, low birth weight, premature birth, SIDS.
  - **Others**: Peptic ulcer disease, osteoporosis, premature skin aging, impaired wound healing.

## 2. Nicotine Addiction & Withdrawal (尼古丁成癮與退癮症狀)
- **Mechanism**: Nicotine binds to nicotinic acetylcholine receptors in the brain, triggering the release of dopamine (the reward pathway), leading to addiction.
- **Withdrawal Symptoms**: Peak within the first few days of quitting and can last for weeks.
  - Craving for cigarettes (the most prominent symptom).
  - Irritability, frustration, or anger.
  - Anxiety and restlessness.
  - Difficulty concentrating.
  - Depressed mood.
  - Increased appetite and weight gain.
  - Insomnia.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (臨床評估)
Every patient should be asked about their smoking status at every visit.
- **The 5 A's Approach**:
  1. **Ask**: Identify and document tobacco use status for every patient at every visit.
  2. **Advise**: In a clear, strong, and personalized manner, urge every tobacco user to quit. Link quitting to their current health condition.
  3. **Assess**: Determine willingness to make a quit attempt at this time (Stages of Change model: Pre-contemplation, Contemplation, Preparation, Action, Maintenance).
  4. **Assist**: For the patient willing to make a quit attempt, use counseling and pharmacotherapy to help them. Set a quit date (ideally within 2 weeks).
  5. **Arrange**: Schedule follow-up contact, in person or by telephone, preferably within the first week after the quit date.
- **Fagerström Test for Nicotine Dependence (FTND)**: Assesses the level of physical dependence. Key questions:
  - How soon after you wake up do you smoke your first cigarette? (≤ 5 mins indicates high dependence).
  - How many cigarettes do you smoke per day? (≥ 21 indicates high dependence).

## 4. Management: Behavioral Interventions (行為干預)
- **Motivational Interviewing**: Especially useful for patients not yet ready to quit (Pre-contemplation/Contemplation). Use the **5 R's**:
  - **Relevance**: Tailor advice to the patient's specific health/family situation.
  - **Risks**: Ask the patient to identify potential negative consequences of smoking.
  - **Rewards**: Ask the patient to identify potential benefits of quitting (health, financial, protecting family from secondhand smoke).
  - **Roadblocks**: Identify barriers to quitting (e.g., stress, weight gain, withdrawal) and discuss coping strategies.
  - **Repetition**: Repeat the motivational intervention every time an unmotivated patient visits.
- **Cognitive Behavioral Strategies**:
  - Identify triggers (e.g., drinking alcohol, after meals, stress) and develop avoidance or coping strategies.
  - Delaying tactics when cravings hit (the "4 D's": Delay, Deep breathing, Drink water, Do something else).

## 5. Management: Pharmacotherapy (藥物治療)
Pharmacotherapy significantly increases the success rate of quitting. It should be offered to all dependent smokers trying to quit, unless contraindicated.

### Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT - 尼古丁補充療法)
- **Mechanism**: Provides a clean, controlled dose of nicotine to relieve withdrawal symptoms and cravings without the toxic chemicals in tobacco smoke.
- **Forms**:
  - *Long-acting*: Nicotine Patch (provides a steady baseline level of nicotine).
  - *Short-acting*: Nicotine Gum, Lozenge, Inhaler, Nasal Spray (used for breakthrough cravings).
- **Strategy**: Combination therapy (Patch + a short-acting form) is more effective than monotherapy and is highly recommended for highly dependent smokers.
- **Side effects**: Local irritation (skin rash with patch, mouth/throat irritation with gum/lozenge), vivid dreams/insomnia (with 24-hour patch - can remove at night).

### Varenicline (伐尼克蘭 / Champix)
- **Mechanism**: Partial agonist at the α4β2 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor. It relieves withdrawal symptoms (agonist effect) and blocks the rewarding effects of smoking if the patient relapses (antagonist effect).
- **Efficacy**: The most effective monotherapy for smoking cessation.
- **Dosing**: Start 1-2 weeks *before* the target quit date. Titrate dose up over the first week. Standard course is 12 weeks.
- **Side effects**: Nausea (most common - take with food and water), vivid/abnormal dreams, insomnia.
- **Precautions**: Previously had a "black box" warning for neuropsychiatric events (depression, suicidal ideation), but recent large studies (EAGLES trial) showed no significant increase in risk compared to placebo. Still, monitor for mood changes.

### Bupropion (安非他酮 / Zyban)
- **Mechanism**: Atypical antidepressant. Inhibits reuptake of dopamine and norepinephrine. Reduces cravings and withdrawal symptoms.
- **Dosing**: Start 1-2 weeks *before* the target quit date.
- **Side effects**: Insomnia, dry mouth.
- **Contraindications**: History of seizures (lowers seizure threshold), eating disorders (anorexia/bulimia), concurrent use of MAOIs.

## 6. Relapse Prevention & Follow-up (預防復發與跟進)
- Relapse is common, often occurring within the first few weeks.
- Frame relapse as a learning opportunity, not a failure. Analyze the trigger that led to the relapse and plan a new strategy.
- Emphasize the "Not a Single Puff" rule.
- Monitor weight gain and provide dietary advice.
