# Smoking Cessation

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Brief advice (5As): Ask, Advise, Assess willingness, Assist, Arrange follow-up — even 3 min of advice increases quit rates
**Answer**: 簡短建議（5As）：詢問、建議、評估意願、協助、安排隨訪——即使3分鐘的建議也能提高戒煙率

**Examiner's Pearl**: Motivational interviewing (PACE): Partnership, Acceptance, Compassion, Evocation — use OARS: Open questions, Affirmations, Reflective listening, Summaries
**Answer**: 動機式訪談（PACE）：夥伴關係、接納、同情、喚起——使用OARS：開放式問題、肯定、反映式聆聽、總結

**Examiner's Pearl**: First-line pharmacotherapy: Varenicline (Champix) > NRT combination > Bupropion. Combination NRT (patch + fast-acting) > single NRT
**Answer**: 一線藥物治療：伐尼克蘭（畅沛）>聯合NRT>安非他酮。聯合NRT（貼片+速效）>單一NRT

**Examiner's Pearl**: Varenicline: 0.5mg OD ×3 days → 0.5mg BD ×4 days → 1mg BD ×12 weeks. Set quit date (usually 1-2 weeks after starting). Monitor mood (rare: depression, suicidal ideation)
**Answer**: 伐尼克蘭：0.5mg每日一次×3天→0.5mg每日兩次×4天→1mg每日兩次×12週。設定戒煙日期（通常開始後1-2週）。監測情緒（罕見：抑鬱、自殺意念）

**Examiner's Pearl**: HK-specific: COSH (Chest of Smoking and Health) 1833-1833 (24h quitline); HA Smoking Cessation Clinics; SOCO hotline; Brief HI (5-10 min) intervention reimbursed in GOPC
**Answer**: 香港特定：吸煙與健康委員會1833-1833（24小時戒煙熱線）；醫管局戒煙診所；簡短健康輔導（5-10分鐘）在普通科門診可獲補貼

**Examiner's Pearl**: Withdrawal symptoms: irritability, anxiety, difficulty concentrating, increased appetite, restlessness — peak 2-3 days, resolve by 2-4 weeks. Cravings last 3-5 minutes each
**Answer**: 戒斷症狀：易怒、焦慮、難以集中注意力、食慾增加、坐立不安——2-3天達到峰值，2-4週消退。每次渴望持續3-5分鐘

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Cheung, 55M, chronic smoker (1 pack/day for 30 years). Presents for a routine check-up. He has a chronic morning cough but no red flags. He has tried quitting cold turkey twice but failed due to severe cravings and irritability. His wife recently had a heart attack.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: 30-year smoking habit, high nicotine dependence.
- Precipitating: Wife's recent heart attack (a strong "teachable moment" and motivator).
- Perpetuating: Nicotine withdrawal symptoms (cravings, irritability) causing previous relapses.
- Protective: Patient is attending the clinic, has a strong external motivator (wife's health).

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "I see you have been smoking for 30 years and have tried to quit before. How has your wife's recent heart attack affected your thoughts on smoking?"
- Affect: "How do you feel when you try to quit and the cravings hit?"
- Trouble: "What is the absolute hardest part about quitting for you? Is it the stress, the habit, or the physical cravings?"
- Handling: "How did you try to cope with the irritability the last time you tried to quit?"
- Empathy: "It is incredibly difficult to break a 30-year habit. Nicotine is highly addictive, so it is completely understandable that quitting cold turkey was so hard."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "Why do you think the previous attempts to quit didn't work out?"
- Concerns: "Are you worried about your own heart health now, given what happened to your wife?"
- Expectations: "What kind of support were you hoping we could offer you today to make this attempt successful?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Severe nicotine dependence.
- Smoker's cough (need to rule out COPD or lung cancer, though no red flags currently).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Fear of failure due to previous unsuccessful attempts.
- Anxiety regarding wife's health.

SOCIAL:
- Smoking may be deeply ingrained in his daily routine or social interactions.

BARRIERS:
- Misconception that he has to rely solely on willpower ("cold turkey").
- Lack of knowledge about Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT) or medications.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"You are making a great decision for your health and your wife's health. You do not have to do this alone using just willpower. We have medical tools that make a huge difference."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS - THE 5 As):
- Ask/Assess: "You smoke 1 pack a day. You are highly dependent, which explains the severe withdrawals."
- Advise: "As your doctor, the single best thing you can do for your heart and lungs is to stop smoking completely."
- Assist: "When you quit cold turkey, your brain screams for nicotine, causing the irritability. We can use Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT), like patches and gum. The patch gives you a steady baseline of nicotine to stop the background cravings, and you chew the gum when you get a sudden urge."
- "This satisfies the physical addiction safely, without the tar and cancer-causing chemicals of a cigarette, allowing you to focus on breaking the physical habit of holding a cigarette."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe Nicotine Patches (21mg for a heavy smoker) to be applied daily, and Nicotine Gum (2mg) to use when you have a breakthrough craving."
- (Alternative: Varenicline/Champix if available and appropriate, explaining it blocks nicotine receptors).

REFER:
- Refer to a Smoking Cessation Clinic or counselor for behavioral support, as combining medication with counseling doubles the success rate.

INVESTIGATE:
- Arrange a chest X-ray to investigate the chronic cough, given his 30-pack-year history.
- Arrange fasting lipids and HbA1c to check his cardiovascular risk profile.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor withdrawal symptoms and NRT side effects (e.g., vivid dreams with patches).

PLAN:
- "Let's set a Quit Date for next Monday. I want to see you back here in 2 weeks to see how you are coping with the patches and gum."

SAFETY NET:
"If you cough up blood, experience unexplained weight loss, or get chest pain, come back immediately."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain how the patch and gum work together, then ask, "Does it make sense how using both the patch and the gum will help stop those severe cravings you had last time?"

ICE:
Directly address his fear of failing again by validating that cold turkey is the hardest way to quit, and reframing this attempt as a medically supported one.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"Are you ready to set a Quit Date today, or do you need some time to think about the options we discussed?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about why quitting was so hard last time, and then we will discuss the medications that can make it much easier this time."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: Patient is a chronic smoker who wants to quit but has failed multiple times due to severe cravings. He is asking for help.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned you want to quit smoking but have found it very difficult in the past. Can you tell me more about your previous attempts?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Allow the patient to describe their struggles with quitting without interrupting.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
ASSESS SMOKING HISTORY (PACK-YEARS):
- "How many cigarettes do you smoke a day?"
- "How many years have you been smoking?"

ASSESS NICOTINE DEPENDENCE (FAGERSTROM TEST):
- "How soon after you wake up do you smoke your first cigarette?" (Within 5 mins = high dependence).
- "Do you wake up in the middle of the night to smoke?"

ASSESS PREVIOUS ATTEMPTS:
- "What was the longest time you managed to stop?"
- "What made you start smoking again? Was it stress, cravings, or social situations?"

SCREEN FOR COMPLICATIONS:
- "Do you have a chronic cough, shortness of breath, or chest pain?"
- "Have you ever coughed up blood?"

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"Why is it important for you to quit right now?"

CONCERNS:
"What are you most worried about when you think about quitting again?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What kind of help were you hoping to get from me today?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"You have a high level of nicotine dependence. This is a physical addiction, not just a bad habit."

EXPLANATION:
"When you try to stop suddenly, your brain goes into withdrawal because it is missing the nicotine it is used to. This causes the severe cravings, irritability, and anxiety that made you start smoking again in the past."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does that explain why it was so hard to quit using just willpower?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"Because this is a medical addiction, we have medical treatments that can make it much easier. You don't have to suffer through those severe withdrawals."

INVESTIGATE:
"I would like to arrange a chest X-ray just to make sure your lungs are okay, given how long you have been smoking."

PLAN:
"I recommend using Nicotine Replacement Therapy. We can use a patch to give you a steady dose of clean nicotine to stop the background cravings, and gum to chew when you get a sudden urge."

ADVICE:
"Let's set a Quit Date. Throw away all your ashtrays and lighters the night before. Tell your family you are quitting so they can support you."

SAFETY NET:
"If you experience severe chest pain, shortness of breath, or cough up blood, please go to the A&E immediately."
