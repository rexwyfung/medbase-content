# Otitis Media

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Acute Otitis Media (AOM) is common in children; often follows URTI.
**Answer**: 急性中耳炎（AOM）在兒童中很常見；通常繼發於上呼吸道感染。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Otoscopy shows bulging, erythematous tympanic membrane with loss of light reflex.
**Answer**: 耳鏡檢查顯示鼓膜膨出、紅腫，光反射消失。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Most cases resolve spontaneously; antibiotics (Amoxicillin) if severe, bilateral, or <2 years old.
**Answer**: 大多數病例會自行痊癒；如果病情嚴重、雙側或小於2歲，則使用抗生素（阿莫西林）。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Complications: Mastoiditis, hearing loss, tympanic membrane perforation.
**Answer**: 併發症：乳突炎、聽力受損、鼓膜穿孔。

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: A 4-year-old boy presents with ear pain, fever, and irritability for 2 days following a cold. Mother is concerned about hearing loss and demands antibiotics.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
PHYSICAL (4P):
- Presenting complaint: Ear pain, fever, irritability.
- Precipitating factors: Recent URTI/cold.
- Pattern: Constant pain, worse at night.
- Palliation/Provocation: Paracetamol helps slightly.

BATHE:
- Background: "How has he been over the past few days?"
- Affect: "How is this affecting his sleep and your stress levels?"
- Trouble: "What is the most difficult part of this for you?"
- Handling: "How have you been managing his fever and pain?"
- Empathy: "It must be exhausting to care for a crying child all night."

ICE:
- Ideas: "What do you think is causing his ear pain?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned hearing loss. Are you worried about permanent damage?"
- Expectations: "You asked for antibiotics. What is your understanding of when they are needed?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Acute Otitis Media (AOM), likely viral or self-limiting bacterial.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Maternal anxiety regarding hearing loss and complications.

SOCIAL:
- Sleep deprivation for both child and parents.

BARRIERS:
- Misconception that all ear infections require immediate antibiotics.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"It is very common for children to get ear infections after a cold. The fluid behind the ear can muffle hearing temporarily, but it rarely causes permanent damage."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Most ear infections clear up on their own within 3 days without antibiotics. Antibiotics can cause side effects like diarrhea and rash."
- "We use a 'watchful waiting' approach. I will give you a prescription for antibiotics, but please wait 48 hours. Only use it if he doesn't improve or gets worse."

PRESCRIBE:
- Adequate analgesia (Paracetamol and Ibuprofen regularly, not just for fever but for pain).
- Delayed antibiotic prescription (e.g., Amoxicillin) with clear instructions on when to fill it.

REFER:
- Not needed currently.

INVESTIGATE:
- None required.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor temperature and oral intake.

PLAN:
- Review in 48-72 hours if no improvement, or sooner if red flags develop.

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the "watchful waiting" approach, then ask: "Just to make sure I explained it clearly, when will you start the antibiotics?"

ICE:
Directly address the mother's fear of hearing loss by explaining the difference between temporary fluid and permanent nerve damage.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know it's hard to see him in pain. Let's focus on keeping him comfortable with painkillers first."

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about how to manage his pain tonight, and then we will discuss the plan for antibiotics."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: A mother brings her 4-year-old son who has been pulling at his right ear and crying, especially at night, after a runny nose for 3 days.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned he's been pulling at his ear. Can you tell me more about how he's been acting?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Listen for signs of pain, fever, and preceding upper respiratory symptoms.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
ASSESS SYMPTOMS:
- "Does he have a fever? How high?"
- "Is there any discharge or fluid coming out of the ear?"
- "Is he eating and drinking normally?"

RED FLAGS:
- "Has he been unusually drowsy or difficult to wake up?"
- "Is there any swelling or redness behind the ear?" (Mastoiditis)
- "Has he been vomiting or complaining of a stiff neck?"

PHYSICAL EXAM:
- Otoscopy: Check for bulging, erythematous tympanic membrane with loss of light reflex.
- Check mastoid process for tenderness.

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"Why do you think his ear is hurting after the cold?"

CONCERNS:
"What worries you the most about his ear pain?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What were you hoping we could do for him today?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"Based on his symptoms and looking inside his ear, he has Acute Otitis Media, which is a middle ear infection."

EXPLANATION:
"The tube connecting the back of the nose to the ear gets blocked during a cold. Fluid builds up behind the eardrum, causing pressure and pain. It's like a blocked pipe."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does that explanation make sense?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"This is very common and usually gets better on its own within a few days."

PLAN:
"We will use regular painkillers like Paracetamol and Ibuprofen to keep him comfortable. I will also give you a 'delayed prescription' for antibiotics to use ONLY if he doesn't improve in 48 hours."

ADVICE:
"Keep him hydrated. Prop his head up slightly when sleeping to reduce ear pressure."

SAFETY NET:
"If he becomes very lethargic, stops drinking, or if you see swelling behind the ear, bring him back immediately."
