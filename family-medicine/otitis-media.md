# Acute Otitis Media (AOM) (急性中耳炎)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: Acute inflammation and infection of the middle ear space, characterized by the presence of middle ear effusion (MEE) and rapid onset of signs and symptoms of middle ear inflammation.
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common in childhood, peaking between 6 and 24 months of age. By age 3, over 80% of children will have had at least one episode. Less common in adults.

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- **Eustachian Tube Dysfunction**: The primary underlying mechanism. The Eustachian tube connects the middle ear to the nasopharynx. In children, it is shorter, more horizontal, and flaccid compared to adults.
- **Mechanism**: An antecedent upper respiratory tract infection (URTI), usually viral, causes mucosal inflammation and edema in the nasopharynx and Eustachian tube. This leads to Eustachian tube obstruction.
- **Negative Pressure & Effusion**: Obstruction creates negative pressure in the middle ear, drawing in fluid (effusion).
- **Secondary Bacterial Infection**: Nasopharyngeal bacteria ascend into the middle ear fluid, proliferate, and cause acute suppuration.

## 3. Etiology (病因)
- **Bacterial (Most common causes of AOM)**:
  1. *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (肺炎鏈球菌) - historically the most common and causes the most severe disease.
  2. *Haemophilus influenzae* (non-typeable) (流感嗜血桿菌) - increasingly common, often associated with concurrent conjunctivitis (otitis-conjunctivitis syndrome).
  3. *Moraxella catarrhalis* (卡他莫拉菌).
- **Viral**: RSV, Rhinovirus, Influenza, Adenovirus. Viruses often pave the way for bacterial infection or can cause AOM alone.

## 4. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Symptoms**:
  - **Otalgia (Ear pain - 耳痛)**: The hallmark symptom. In infants, this may present as ear tugging/rubbing, excessive crying, irritability, or poor feeding.
  - **Fever (發燒)**: Present in ~50% of cases.
  - **Otorrhea (Ear discharge - 耳漏)**: If the tympanic membrane (TM) ruptures, purulent discharge will be seen in the ear canal, often accompanied by a sudden *relief* of pain.
  - Hearing loss (conductive, usually transient).
  - Preceding or concurrent URTI symptoms (cough, rhinorrhea).
- **Otoscopic Examination (Key to Diagnosis)**:
  - **Bulging Tympanic Membrane (鼓膜膨出)**: The most specific sign of AOM.
  - Erythema (Redness): Less specific, as a red TM can be caused by crying or fever alone.
  - Loss of normal landmarks (handle of malleus, light reflex).
  - Opacity or cloudy/yellow fluid behind the TM.
  - Decreased mobility on pneumatic otoscopy.

## 5. Diagnosis & Differential Diagnosis (診斷與鑑別診斷)
- **Diagnosis**: Requires (1) acute onset of symptoms, (2) presence of middle ear effusion (bulging TM), and (3) signs of middle ear inflammation (marked erythema or otalgia).
- **Otitis Media with Effusion (OME - 滲出性中耳炎)**: Fluid in the middle ear *without* signs of acute inflammation (no pain, no fever, TM is retracted or neutral, not bulging). Often follows AOM or occurs with allergies.
- **Otitis Externa (OE - 外耳炎 / "Swimmer's ear")**: Infection of the ear canal. Pain is elicited by pulling the pinna or pressing the tragus. The canal is swollen and erythematous, but the TM (if visible) is usually normal.

## 6. Management (治療與管理)
### 1. Analgesia (Pain Control)
- Crucial first step. Use Paracetamol or Ibuprofen. Topical analgesic drops (e.g., local anesthetics) can be used if the TM is intact.

### 2. Observation Option ("Watchful Waiting")
- Many cases of AOM resolve spontaneously without antibiotics.
- **Criteria for Observation**: Can be offered for 48-72 hours in children **≥ 6 months** with **mild symptoms** (mild pain, temp < 39°C) and **unilateral** AOM (or bilateral if ≥ 2 years old), provided follow-up is assured.
- Provide a "safety-net" prescription to be filled if symptoms worsen or do not improve in 48-72 hours.

### 3. Antibiotic Therapy
- **Indications for immediate antibiotics**:
  - Age < 6 months.
  - Severe symptoms (moderate/severe pain, temp ≥ 39°C).
  - Bilateral AOM in children < 2 years.
  - Otorrhea (TM perforation).
  - Immunocompromised or craniofacial abnormalities (e.g., cleft palate).
- **First-line**: **Amoxicillin (阿莫西林)** (High dose: 80-90 mg/kg/day). High dose is needed to overcome resistant *S. pneumoniae*.
- **Second-line** (if failure after 48-72 hours, or if concurrent purulent conjunctivitis indicating *H. influenzae*): **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin)**.
- **Penicillin Allergy**:
  - Non-severe (e.g., mild rash): Cefdinir, Cefuroxime.
  - Severe (anaphylaxis): Macrolides (Azithromycin, Clarithromycin) - note high resistance rates in *S. pneumoniae*.

## 7. Complications (併發症)
- **Tympanic Membrane Perforation**: Usually heals spontaneously.
- **Hearing Loss**: Conductive hearing loss due to persistent effusion. Can affect speech and language development if prolonged.
- **Acute Mastoiditis (急性乳突炎)**: Infection spreads to the mastoid air cells. Presents with post-auricular swelling, erythema, tenderness, and outward/downward displacement of the pinna. Requires IV antibiotics and possible surgery.
- **Intracranial Complications (Rare but severe)**: Meningitis, brain abscess, epidural abscess, lateral sinus thrombosis.
