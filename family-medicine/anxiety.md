# Anxiety / Panic Disorder

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: GAD-7 ≥10 = moderate anxiety; PHQ-9 always screen concurrent depression (high comorbidity)
**Answer**: GAD-7 ≥10 = 中度焦慮；PHQ-9始終篩查同時存在的抑鬱症（高共病率）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Panic attack: acute paroxysmal episode of intense fear + physical symptoms (palpitations, chest pain, dyspnoea, paraesthesia, dizziness, sweating, derealization); usually peaks in 10 min; resolves in 20-30 min
**Answer**: 驚恐發作：急性陣發性強烈恐懼+身體症狀（心悸、胸痛、呼吸困難、感覺異常、頭暈、出汗、現實解體）；通常在10分鐘內達到峰值；20-30分鐘內消退

**Examiner's Pearl**: Panic disorder: recurrent unexpected panic attacks + ≥1 month of concern about future attacks or maladaptive behavioural change (avoidance)
**Answer**: 驚恐障礙：反覆意外驚恐發作+≥1個月擔心未來發作或適應不良行為改變（迴避）

**Examiner's Pearl**: 1st line: CBT (gold standard for both GAD and panic disorder); SSRI second line or combined; benzodiazepines ONLY for short-term acute rescue (addiction risk)
**Answer**: 一線：認知行為治療（廣泛性焦慮和驚恐障礙的金標準）；二線或聯合使用SSRI；苯二氮䓬類僅用於短期急性救援（上癮風險）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Exclude medical causes: hyperthyroidism (TFT), cardiac arrhythmia (ECG/Holter), hypoglycaemia, phaeochromocytoma, drug effects (caffeine, sympathomimetics)
**Answer**: 排除醫學原因：甲狀腺功能亢進（甲狀腺功能）、心律不整（心電圖/動態心電圖）、低血糖、嗜鉻細胞瘤、藥物影響（咖啡因、擬交感神經藥）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Avoid: Caffeine, alcohol, illicit drugs; sleep hygiene; regular exercise; diaphragmatic breathing; graded exposure (for panic + agoraphobia)
**Answer**: 避免：咖啡因、酒精、非法藥物；睡眠衛生；定期運動；膈肌呼吸；分級暴露（驚恐障礙+廣場恐懼症）

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Ho, 30M, IT professional. Presents with 6 months of constant worrying, muscle tension, and poor sleep. He frequently feels his heart racing and fears he is having a heart attack. Multiple previous ECGs were normal.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Perfectionist personality, high-stress IT job.
- Precipitating: Recent project deadline or promotion.
- Perpetuating: Catastrophizing physical symptoms (heart racing = heart attack), caffeine intake, lack of exercise.
- Protective: Insight that his worry might be excessive, seeking medical help.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You mentioned you have been constantly worried and feeling your heart racing for 6 months. How is this affecting your work and personal life?"
- Affect: "How do you feel emotionally when you get these episodes where you think you are having a heart attack?"
- Trouble: "Out of the poor sleep, the muscle tension, and the heart racing, what is the most difficult part for you to handle?"
- Handling: "How have you been trying to cope with this constant anxiety?"
- Empathy: "It must be absolutely terrifying to feel like your heart is failing, even when the tests say otherwise. I can see how exhausting this is for you."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "Since the ECGs were normal, what do you think is causing your heart to race and your muscles to tense up?"
- Concerns: "Are you still worried that we have missed a hidden heart condition?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to help you manage these feelings?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Somatic symptoms of anxiety (palpitations, muscle tension, insomnia).
- Need to rule out hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis) or arrhythmias, though less likely given normal ECGs.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) with potential panic attacks.
- Health anxiety (hypochondriasis).

SOCIAL:
- Occupational stress exacerbating symptoms.
- Potential reliance on caffeine to combat poor sleep, creating a vicious cycle.

BARRIERS:
- Difficulty accepting a psychological diagnosis for physical symptoms.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I have reviewed your ECGs and examined you. Your heart is structurally very healthy. The racing heart you feel is a real physical sensation, but it is caused by your body's 'fight or flight' adrenaline response, not a failing heart."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Anxiety is like a car alarm that is too sensitive. It goes off and floods your body with adrenaline even when there is no real danger, causing your heart to race and muscles to tense."
- "Cut down on coffee and energy drinks, as caffeine mimics adrenaline and triggers this alarm."
- "When you feel your heart racing, try 'box breathing': breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4. This physically forces your heart rate to slow down."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I recommend starting an SSRI medication (like Escitalopram). It helps calm down that oversensitive alarm system in your brain."
- "It takes 2 to 4 weeks to work. In the meantime, I can give you a very short course of a mild relaxant (e.g., Propranolol to block the adrenaline on your heart) to use only when you have a severe attack."

REFER:
- Clinical Psychologist for Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) to learn how to challenge catastrophic thoughts.

INVESTIGATE:
- Thyroid Function Test (TFT) to completely rule out an overactive thyroid.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor anxiety levels using the GAD-7 questionnaire.

PLAN:
- Follow up in 2 weeks to check medication tolerance and review blood tests.

SAFETY NET:
"If you ever feel completely overwhelmed and have thoughts of harming yourself, go to the Accident and Emergency department immediately."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the car alarm analogy, then ask, "Does it make sense how your brain can trick your heart into racing even when your heart is healthy?"

ICE:
Directly address his fear of a heart attack by validating his physical symptoms but reframing the cause.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"Are you open to trying the breathing exercises and cutting down caffeine first, or would you prefer to start the medication today?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about why your heart is racing, and then we will discuss how we can turn down that internal alarm."
