# Dyslipidaemia

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: QRISK3 (or Framingham for HK): calculate 10-year CVD risk — treat if ≥10% with lifestyle + statin. High-risk groups (DM, CKD, CVD, FH) = treat regardless of score
**Answer**: QRISK3（香港用Framingham）：計算10年心血管病風險——若≥10%則用生活方式+他汀類治療。高風險群體（糖尿病、慢性腎病、心血管病、家族性高膽固醇血症）=無論評分如何均治療

**Examiner's Pearl**: LDL targets: Primary prevention <3.0 mmol/L; high CVD risk <1.8 mmol/L (ESC); very high risk <1.4 mmol/L
**Answer**: LDL目標：初級預防<3.0 mmol/L；高心血管病風險<1.8 mmol/L（ESC）；極高風險<1.4 mmol/L

**Examiner's Pearl**: First-line statin: Atorvastatin 20-40mg (high-intensity: 40-80mg); Rosuvastatin 10-20mg. Take at bedtime (cholesterol synthesis peaks at night). No grapefruit juice
**Answer**: 一線他汀類：阿托伐他汀20-40mg（高強度：40-80mg）；瑞舒伐他汀10-20mg。睡前服用（膽固醇合成在夜間達到峰值）。不要飲葡萄柚汁

**Examiner's Pearl**: Statin side effects: Myalgia most common (check CK if severe); hepatotoxicity (LFT monitoring); statin-induced diabetes (small risk); drug interactions (simvastatin + clarithromycin = rhabdomyolysis)
**Answer**: 他汀類副作用：肌痛最常見（若嚴重則查CK）；肝毒性（LFT監測）；他汀類誘導的糖尿病（小風險）；藥物相互作用（辛伐他汀+克拉黴素=橫紋肌溶解症）

**Examiner's Pearl**: FH (Familial Hypercholesterolaemia): LDL >4.9 mmol/L + family history + tendon xanthomata/corneal arcus → refer lipid clinic. Simon Broome criteria for diagnosis
**Answer**: 家族性高膽固醇血症：LDL >4.9 mmol/L+家族史+肌腱黃色瘤/角膜弓→轉介血脂診所。Simon Broome標準用於診斷

**Examiner's Pearl**: Non-statin options: Ezetimibe 10mg OD (reduces LDL 15-20%); PCSK9 inhibitors (evolocumab/alirocumab) for very high risk or statin intolerance; fibrates for hypertriglyceridaemia
**Answer**: 非他汀類選擇：依折麥布10mg每日一次（降低LDL 15-20%）；PCSK9抑製劑（依洛尤單抗/阿利庫單抗）用於極高風險或他汀類不耐受；貝特類用於高甘油三酯血症

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Wong, 50M, asymptomatic. Routine health check showed high LDL cholesterol (4.5 mmol/L). He is overweight (BMI 28), has a sedentary office job, and eats fast food frequently. He does not want to start medication and wants to try diet first.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Age 50, male, overweight (BMI 28).
- Precipitating: Sedentary lifestyle, high saturated fat diet (fast food).
- Perpetuating: Busy work schedule preventing exercise and healthy cooking.
- Protective: Asymptomatic, motivated to try lifestyle changes to avoid medication.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "Your recent blood test showed high cholesterol. How does your typical daily routine, including meals and activity, look right now?"
- Affect: "How do you feel about getting this abnormal blood test result?"
- Trouble: "What do you think will be the hardest part about changing your diet and exercise habits?"
- Handling: "Have you ever tried to lose weight or change your diet in the past?"
- Empathy: "It can be quite a shock to feel perfectly healthy but be told your blood tests are abnormal. I understand why you want to avoid medication if possible."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you think is the main reason your cholesterol is high?"
- Concerns: "Are you worried about the side effects of cholesterol medications, or just prefer a natural approach?"
- Expectations: "You mentioned wanting to try diet first. How much time were you hoping to give yourself to see if lifestyle changes work?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Primary Dyslipidaemia (High LDL).
- Overweight (BMI 28).
- Increased cardiovascular risk (need to calculate QRISK or ASCVD score).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Reluctance to start long-term medication (statin hesitancy).

SOCIAL:
- Occupational barriers to healthy living (sedentary job, reliance on fast food).

BARRIERS:
- Misconception that high cholesterol causes symptoms (e.g., dizziness).
- Difficulty sustaining strict dietary changes in a busy lifestyle.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"You are completely asymptomatic right now, which is good. Finding this early gives us a great opportunity to prevent future heart problems before they happen."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "High cholesterol is like grease building up inside your water pipes. You can't feel it happening, but over years, it can block the pipes and cause a heart attack or stroke."
- "Since you want to try lifestyle changes first, we can do a 3-month trial. You need to drastically cut down on saturated fats (animal fat, skin, fast food, fried food) and eat more soluble fiber (oats, beans, vegetables)."
- "Aim for 150 minutes of moderate exercise a week, like brisk walking, to help raise your 'good' cholesterol and lower your weight."

PRESCRIBE:
- "We will hold off on medications (Statins) for now, as per your preference, to see if diet and exercise can bring the LDL down."

REFER:
- Dietitian for a structured, personalized meal plan to replace fast food.

INVESTIGATE:
- Check fasting glucose/HbA1c, blood pressure, and smoking status to complete cardiovascular risk assessment.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor weight and BMI.

PLAN:
- "We will repeat the fasting lipid profile in 3 months. If the LDL is still high, we will need to have a serious discussion about starting a Statin to protect your heart."

SAFETY NET:
"Even though high cholesterol doesn't cause symptoms, if you ever experience chest pain, shortness of breath, or weakness on one side of your body, go to the A&E immediately."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the "grease in the pipes" analogy, then ask, "Does it make sense why high cholesterol is dangerous even if you feel perfectly fine right now?"

ICE:
Directly address his reluctance for medication by agreeing to a 3-month lifestyle trial, creating a collaborative partnership.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I support your decision to try diet first. Are you willing to see a dietitian to get some practical tips on avoiding fast food during your busy workdays?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about what the blood test means for your heart, and then we will discuss the 3-month diet and exercise plan."
