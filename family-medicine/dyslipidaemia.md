# Dyslipidemia (血脂異常)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: Abnormal levels of lipids in the blood. This typically involves elevated Total Cholesterol (TC), elevated Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C), elevated Triglycerides (TG), and/or low High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-C).
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common globally and in Hong Kong. A major, modifiable risk factor for Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD).

## 2. Pathophysiology & Lipid Fractions (病理生理學與血脂成分)
- **LDL-C (Low-Density Lipoprotein - "Bad" Cholesterol)**: The primary atherogenic lipoprotein. It penetrates the arterial endothelium, becomes oxidized, and is engulfed by macrophages to form foam cells, initiating the atherosclerotic plaque. Lowering LDL-C is the primary target of therapy.
- **HDL-C (High-Density Lipoprotein - "Good" Cholesterol)**: Involved in reverse cholesterol transport (bringing cholesterol from peripheral tissues back to the liver for excretion). Low HDL-C is an independent risk factor for ASCVD, but pharmacologically raising it has not consistently shown cardiovascular benefit.
- **Triglycerides (TG)**: Energy storage molecules. Very high levels (> 10 mmol/L or 880 mg/dL) significantly increase the risk of **Acute Pancreatitis**. Moderately elevated TG is often associated with metabolic syndrome and increased ASCVD risk.

## 3. Etiology (病因)
- **Primary (Genetic/Familial)**:
  - **Familial Hypercholesterolemia (FH)**: Autosomal dominant mutation (most commonly in the LDL receptor gene). Characterized by severely elevated LDL-C from birth and premature ASCVD. Suspect if LDL-C > 4.9 mmol/L in adults or > 4.0 mmol/L in children, or strong family history of premature CAD. Physical signs: tendon xanthomas (Achilles, extensor tendons of hands), xanthelasma, corneal arcus in young patients.
- **Secondary (Acquired)**:
  - **Diet**: High intake of saturated fats, trans fats, and excess calories.
  - **Lifestyle**: Sedentary behavior, obesity, excessive alcohol intake (significantly raises TG).
  - **Endocrine/Metabolic**: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (typically causes high TG, low HDL, and small dense LDL particles), Hypothyroidism (decreases LDL receptor expression, causing high LDL), Cushing's syndrome.
  - **Renal**: Nephrotic syndrome (causes severe hypercholesterolemia), Chronic Kidney Disease.
  - **Hepatic**: Obstructive liver disease (e.g., Primary Biliary Cholangitis).
  - **Medications**: Thiazide diuretics, Beta-blockers, Corticosteroids, Atypical antipsychotics, Oral contraceptives, Retinoids.

## 4. Clinical Evaluation & Screening (臨床評估與篩查)
- Dyslipidemia is typically asymptomatic until ASCVD complications occur.
- **Screening**: Recommended for all adults > 40 years, or earlier if risk factors are present (e.g., family history of premature CVD, diabetes, hypertension, smoking, obesity).
- **Lipid Profile**: Measures TC, TG, HDL-C. LDL-C is usually calculated using the Friedewald equation (LDL = TC - HDL - TG/2.2 in mmol/L), provided TG is < 4.5 mmol/L. Direct LDL-C measurement is needed if TG is very high.
- **Cardiovascular Risk Assessment**: Dyslipidemia management is NOT just about treating a number; it's about treating the patient's overall cardiovascular risk. Use risk calculators (e.g., Framingham, SCORE, ACC/AHA ASCVD Risk Estimator) to determine the 10-year risk of a major cardiovascular event.

## 5. Management: Treatment Targets (治療目標)
Targets are based on the patient's overall ASCVD risk category (European Society of Cardiology - ESC guidelines approach):
- **Very High Risk** (Established ASCVD, DM with target organ damage, severe CKD, calculated 10-year risk ≥ 10%): **LDL-C target < 1.4 mmol/L** AND ≥ 50% reduction from baseline.
- **High Risk** (Markedly elevated single risk factor like FH, DM without target organ damage > 10 years, moderate CKD, calculated risk 5 to <10%): **LDL-C target < 1.8 mmol/L** AND ≥ 50% reduction from baseline.
- **Moderate Risk** (Young patients with DM < 10 years, calculated risk 1 to <5%): **LDL-C target < 2.6 mmol/L**.
- **Low Risk** (Calculated risk < 1%): **LDL-C target < 3.0 mmol/L**.

## 6. Management: Interventions (治療與管理)
### Lifestyle Modifications (Foundation for all)
- **Diet**: Reduce saturated fat (< 7% of total calories) and eliminate trans fats. Increase dietary fiber (oats, legumes, fruits). Mediterranean diet pattern.
- **Weight Management**: Achieve and maintain a healthy BMI.
- **Physical Activity**: ≥ 150 mins/week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise.
- **Smoking Cessation**: Crucial for overall ASCVD risk reduction; also improves HDL-C.
- **Alcohol**: Reduce intake, especially if triglycerides are elevated.

### Pharmacological Therapy
- **Statins (HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors - 他汀類藥物)**: First-line therapy for LDL-C reduction and ASCVD prevention.
  - *Mechanism*: Inhibit the rate-limiting enzyme in hepatic cholesterol synthesis, leading to upregulation of LDL receptors and increased clearance of LDL-C from the blood. Pleiotropic effects: stabilize atherosclerotic plaques, reduce inflammation.
  - *Examples*: High-intensity (Atorvastatin 40-80mg, Rosuvastatin 20-40mg), Moderate-intensity (Simvastatin 20-40mg, Atorvastatin 10-20mg).
  - *Side effects*: Myalgia (muscle aches - common), Myopathy/Rhabdomyolysis (rare but serious - check CK if severe muscle pain/weakness occurs), transient elevation of liver enzymes (ALT/AST), slightly increased risk of incident diabetes.
  - *Contraindications*: Active liver disease, pregnancy/breastfeeding.
- **Ezetimibe (依澤替米貝)**: Second-line.
  - *Mechanism*: Inhibits intestinal absorption of dietary and biliary cholesterol (NPC1L1 inhibitor).
  - *Use*: Added to a statin if LDL-C target is not met, or used as monotherapy if statins are not tolerated.
- **PCSK9 Inhibitors** (e.g., Alirocumab, Evolocumab):
  - *Mechanism*: Monoclonal antibodies that prevent the degradation of LDL receptors, leading to massive clearance of LDL-C.
  - *Use*: Subcutaneous injections. Used for very high-risk patients not reaching targets on maximum tolerated statin + ezetimibe, or for Familial Hypercholesterolemia. Very potent but expensive.
- **Fibrates** (e.g., Fenofibrate, Gemfibrozil):
  - *Mechanism*: PPAR-alpha agonists. Primarily lower Triglycerides and raise HDL-C.
  - *Use*: First-line for severe hypertriglyceridemia (TG > 10 mmol/L) to prevent acute pancreatitis. Can be added to statins for mixed dyslipidemia, but increases the risk of myopathy (especially Gemfibrozil + Statin).
- **Omega-3 Fatty Acids (Fish Oils)**: High doses (2-4g/day) can lower triglycerides. Icosapent ethyl has shown cardiovascular benefit in specific high-risk populations with elevated TG.
