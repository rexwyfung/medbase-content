# Menopause & Menstrual Irregularity (更年期與月經失調)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Menopause (更年期)**: The permanent cessation of menstruation due to the loss of ovarian follicular activity. Clinically defined retrospectively after **12 consecutive months of amenorrhea** (absence of periods) in a woman of appropriate age, with no other pathological cause.
- **Perimenopause (Menopausal Transition)**: The period leading up to menopause, characterized by fluctuating hormone levels, irregular menstrual cycles, and the onset of menopausal symptoms. Can last for several years.
- **Epidemiology**: The average age of menopause is ~51 years. Premature menopause is defined as occurring before age 40.

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- Ovarian aging leads to a depletion of viable follicles.
- As follicles deplete, production of **Estrogen (Estradiol) and Progesterone declines**.
- The loss of negative feedback from estrogen and inhibin causes the pituitary gland to secrete high levels of **Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH)** and Luteinizing Hormone (LH).
- The fluctuating and eventually low levels of estrogen are responsible for the clinical symptoms and long-term health consequences of menopause.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
Symptoms vary widely in severity and duration among women.
- **Vasomotor Symptoms (VMS - 血管舒縮症狀)**: The most common and hallmark symptoms.
  - **Hot flushes (潮熱)**: Sudden feelings of heat, often accompanied by flushing of the face/chest and sweating.
  - **Night sweats (盜汗)**: Severe hot flushes occurring during sleep, leading to sleep disruption and fatigue.
- **Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause (GSM / Vaginal Atrophy)**:
  - Vaginal dryness, burning, and itching.
  - Dyspareunia (painful intercourse).
  - Urinary symptoms: Frequency, urgency, recurrent UTIs.
- **Psychological/Cognitive Symptoms**:
  - Mood swings, irritability, anxiety, depression.
  - "Brain fog," difficulty concentrating, memory issues.
- **Physical Changes**:
  - Joint and muscle aches.
  - Weight gain, changes in fat distribution (increased central adiposity).
  - Thinning of hair, dry skin.

## 4. Long-Term Health Consequences (長期健康影響)
The loss of estrogen's protective effects leads to increased risks of:
- **Osteoporosis (骨質疏鬆症)**: Rapid bone loss occurs in the first few years after menopause, significantly increasing the risk of fragility fractures.
- **Cardiovascular Disease**: The risk of IHD and stroke increases and catches up to that of men post-menopause, due to adverse changes in lipid profiles and vascular function.

## 5. Diagnosis & Assessment (診斷與評估)
- **Diagnosis is Clinical**: In women > 45 years with typical symptoms and irregular periods (or 12 months of amenorrhea), no laboratory testing is needed.
- **Hormone Testing (FSH)**:
  - *Not recommended* for women > 45, as levels fluctuate wildly during perimenopause and do not change management.
  - *Indicated* for women < 40 with suspected Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI). Elevated FSH (> 30 IU/L) on two occasions 4-6 weeks apart confirms POI.
- **Red Flags (Require Investigation)**:
  - **Postmenopausal Bleeding (PMB)**: ANY bleeding after 12 months of amenorrhea is abnormal and must be investigated urgently (Transvaginal Ultrasound and Endometrial Biopsy) to rule out **Endometrial Cancer**.

## 6. Management (治療與管理)
Management should be individualized based on symptom severity, personal risk factors, and patient preference.

### 1. Lifestyle Modifications
- For hot flushes: Dress in layers, keep environments cool, avoid triggers (spicy food, caffeine, alcohol, stress).
- For bone/heart health: Weight-bearing exercise, adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, smoking cessation, healthy diet.

### 2. Menopausal Hormone Therapy (MHT / HRT - 荷爾蒙補充療法)
- **Indications**: The most effective treatment for moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms and GSM. Also prevents bone loss.
- **Principles of Prescribing**:
  - **Women WITH a uterus**: MUST receive **Estrogen + Progestogen** (Combined MHT). Unopposed estrogen causes endometrial hyperplasia and increases the risk of endometrial cancer.
  - **Women WITHOUT a uterus (post-hysterectomy)**: Receive **Estrogen-only** MHT.
- **Routes of Administration**:
  - *Oral*: Convenient, but undergoes first-pass liver metabolism (increases clotting factors).
  - *Transdermal* (Patches, gels, sprays): Bypasses the liver. **Preferred** for women with a higher risk of VTE (Venous Thromboembolism), obesity, or liver disease.
- **Risks of MHT**:
  - *Breast Cancer*: Small increased risk with combined MHT (Estrogen + Progestogen) after > 5 years of use. Risk drops after stopping. Estrogen-only MHT has little to no increased risk.
  - *VTE and Stroke*: Small increased risk with oral estrogen. Transdermal estrogen does not appear to increase VTE risk.
- **Contraindications to MHT**: History of breast cancer, estrogen-dependent cancers, undiagnosed vaginal bleeding, history of VTE, active liver disease, untreated hypertension, recent MI/stroke.
- **Timing**: The "Window of Opportunity" hypothesis suggests MHT is safest and most beneficial if started within 10 years of menopause or before age 60.

### 3. Non-Hormonal Therapies
- **For Vasomotor Symptoms**:
  - SSRIs/SNRIs (e.g., Venlafaxine, Paroxetine, Escitalopram).
  - Gabapentin or Pregabalin.
  - Clonidine.
- **For Genitourinary Symptoms (GSM)**:
  - **Vaginal Estrogen** (creams, tablets, rings): Highly effective, minimal systemic absorption, safe for most women (even those who cannot take systemic MHT). Does not require co-prescription of progestogen.
  - Vaginal moisturizers (used regularly) and lubricants (used during intercourse).
