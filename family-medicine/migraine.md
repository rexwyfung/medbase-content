# Migraine (偏頭痛)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A primary headache disorder characterized by recurrent attacks of moderate to severe, typically unilateral, pulsating headache, often associated with nausea, vomiting, photophobia, and phonophobia.
- **Epidemiology**: Very common, affecting ~15% of the general population. Three times more common in women than men. Peak incidence is in the 30s and 40s. Significant cause of disability and lost productivity.

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- Previously thought to be purely vascular (vasodilation causing pain).
- Now understood as a **neurovascular disorder**.
- **Cortical Spreading Depression (CSD)**: A wave of neuronal and glial depolarization spreading across the cerebral cortex. This is believed to be the physiological basis of the migraine aura.
- **Trigeminovascular System Activation**: CSD and other triggers activate the trigeminal nerve afferents that innervate the meninges and cranial blood vessels.
- **Neuropeptide Release**: Activation leads to the release of vasoactive neuropeptides, most notably **Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP)**, Substance P, and Neurokinin A. This causes neurogenic inflammation, vasodilation, and sensitization of pain pathways (peripheral and central sensitization), leading to the throbbing pain and allodynia.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
A typical migraine attack has four phases (though not all patients experience all phases):
1. **Prodrome (Premonitory phase)**: Hours to days before the headache. Symptoms: fatigue, mood changes (irritability, depression), food cravings, neck stiffness, yawning.
2. **Aura (先兆 - occurs in ~25% of patients)**: Fully reversible neurological symptoms developing gradually over 5-20 minutes and lasting < 60 minutes. Usually precedes the headache.
   - *Visual (most common)*: Fortification spectra (zigzag lines), scintillating scotoma (blind spots with flickering edges), flashes of light.
   - *Sensory*: Numbness/tingling spreading from hand to face.
   - *Speech/Language*: Dysphasia.
3. **Headache Phase**:
   - Unilateral (but can be bilateral).
   - Pulsating / throbbing quality.
   - Moderate to severe intensity (aggravated by routine physical activity).
   - Duration: 4 to 72 hours (if untreated).
   - Associated symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, photophobia (sensitivity to light), phonophobia (sensitivity to sound), osmophobia (sensitivity to smells).
4. **Postdrome**: "Migraine hangover." Fatigue, cognitive impairment, depressed mood, neck pain lasting for 24-48 hours after the headache resolves.

## 4. Diagnosis & Red Flags (診斷與紅旗症狀)
Diagnosis is clinical, based on history.
- **Diagnostic Criteria (without aura)**: ≥ 5 attacks lasting 4-72 hours, with ≥ 2 characteristics (unilateral, pulsating, moderate/severe, aggravated by activity) AND ≥ 1 associated symptom (nausea/vomiting, photophobia/phonophobia).
- **Red Flags (SNOOP10 - indicating secondary headache requiring neuroimaging/investigation)**:
  - **S**ystemic symptoms (fever, weight loss) or Secondary risk factors (HIV, cancer).
  - **N**eurological symptoms or abnormal signs (confusion, impaired alertness, focal signs, papilledema).
  - **O**nset: Sudden, abrupt, or split-second ("Thunderclap headache" - suspect Subarachnoid Hemorrhage).
  - **O**lder: New onset and progressive headache, especially > 50 years (suspect Temporal Arteritis or mass lesion).
  - **P**attern change: First headache, or different from previous headaches.
  - **P**ositional: Worse when upright (CSF leak) or worse when lying down (raised ICP).
  - **P**recipitated by sneezing, coughing, or exercise (raised ICP).
  - **P**apilledema.
  - **P**regnancy or puerperium.
  - **P**ainful eye with autonomic features.

## 5. Management: Acute Treatment (急性發作治療)
Aim to treat early in the attack while pain is mild.
- **First-line (Mild to Moderate)**:
  - Simple analgesics: NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac) or Paracetamol.
  - Often combined with an antiemetic (e.g., Metoclopramide, Domperidone) to treat nausea and improve gastric emptying (enhancing analgesic absorption).
- **Second-line / Moderate to Severe (Triptans - 曲普坦類藥物)**:
  - *Mechanism*: 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonists. Cause cranial vasoconstriction and inhibit neuropeptide (CGRP) release.
  - *Examples*: Sumatriptan (oral, nasal, subcutaneous), Zolmitriptan, Rizatriptan.
  - *Timing*: Take at the onset of the *headache* phase (not during the aura, as they are less effective then).
  - *Contraindications*: Ischemic heart disease, previous stroke/TIA, uncontrolled hypertension, peripheral arterial disease, pregnancy. (Due to vasoconstrictive properties).
- **Newer Acute Therapies**: Gepants (CGRP receptor antagonists, e.g., Ubrogepant) and Ditans (5-HT1F agonists, e.g., Lasmiditan) - useful for patients with cardiovascular contraindications to triptans.

## 6. Management: Preventive Treatment (預防性治療)
- **Indications**: Frequent attacks (≥ 4 headache days/month), severe disability despite acute treatment, acute medication overuse, or contraindications to acute therapies.
- **Lifestyle & Trigger Avoidance**: Maintain regular sleep, meals, and hydration. Manage stress. Avoid known dietary triggers (e.g., alcohol, caffeine withdrawal, aged cheeses, MSG - though triggers are highly individual).
- **Pharmacological Prophylaxis** (Requires 2-3 months to assess efficacy; aim for 50% reduction in frequency):
  - **Beta-blockers**: Propranolol, Metoprolol. (Avoid in asthma).
  - **Antidepressants**: Amitriptyline (TCA - good if co-existing tension headache or insomnia), Venlafaxine (SNRI).
  - **Anticonvulsants**: Topiramate (side effects: weight loss, cognitive dulling, paresthesia, teratogenic), Sodium Valproate (highly teratogenic - avoid in women of childbearing age).
  - **Calcium Channel Blockers**: Flunarizine.
  - **CGRP Monoclonal Antibodies**: Erenumab, Fremanezumab, Galcanezumab. Highly effective, specific for migraine, administered via monthly subcutaneous injection. Used for refractory cases.
  - **Botulinum Toxin A**: Injections for Chronic Migraine (≥ 15 headache days/month).

## 7. Complications (併發症)
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH)**: A chronic daily headache caused by the frequent use of acute abortive medications (e.g., taking triptans, NSAIDs, or paracetamol > 10-15 days per month). Treatment requires withdrawal of the overused medication.
- **Status Migrainosus**: A debilitating migraine attack lasting > 72 hours.
