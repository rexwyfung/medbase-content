# Falls in the Elderly (長者跌倒)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: An event which results in a person coming to rest inadvertently on the ground or floor or other lower level.
- **Epidemiology**: A major geriatric syndrome. Approximately 30% of community-dwelling adults over 65 fall each year, and the rate increases with age. Falls are the leading cause of injury-related morbidity and mortality in older adults.

## 2. Etiology & Risk Factors (病因與危險因素)
Falls are rarely due to a single cause; they usually result from a complex interaction of intrinsic (patient-related) and extrinsic (environmental) factors.

### Intrinsic Factors (Host)
- **Age-related physiological changes**: Decreased visual acuity, impaired proprioception, slower reaction times, decreased muscle mass (sarcopenia).
- **Medical Conditions**:
  - *Neurological*: Stroke, Parkinson's disease, dementia, peripheral neuropathy.
  - *Cardiovascular*: Postural (orthostatic) hypotension, arrhythmias, structural heart disease (aortic stenosis).
  - *Musculoskeletal*: Osteoarthritis (especially knees/hips), foot deformities.
  - *Sensory*: Cataracts, macular degeneration, vestibular dysfunction.
- **Medications (Crucial factor)**:
  - **Polypharmacy** (taking ≥ 4-5 medications).
  - **Psychotropics**: Benzodiazepines, antidepressants, antipsychotics (cause sedation, confusion, impaired balance).
  - **Cardiovascular drugs**: Antihypertensives, diuretics (cause postural hypotension).
- **History of previous falls**: The strongest predictor of future falls.
- **Fear of falling**: Leads to activity restriction, physical deconditioning, and paradoxically, an increased risk of falling.

### Extrinsic Factors (Environment)
- **Home hazards**: Clutter, loose rugs, trailing wires, poor lighting, lack of grab bars in the bathroom, slippery floors, inappropriate footwear.
- **Outdoor hazards**: Uneven pavements, poor street lighting.

## 3. Clinical Evaluation (臨床評估)
The goal is to identify modifiable risk factors and determine the cause of the fall (was it a mechanical trip, or a syncopal event?).
- **History (SPLATT)**:
  - **S**ymptoms before the fall (dizziness, palpitations, chest pain suggest a medical cause/syncope; tripping suggests mechanical/environmental).
  - **P**revious falls.
  - **L**ocation of the fall.
  - **A**ctivity at the time of the fall.
  - **T**ime of day.
  - **T**rauma (injuries sustained).
- **Medication Review**: Meticulous review of all prescribed, OTC, and herbal medications.
- **Physical Examination**:
  - *Cardiovascular*: Check lying and standing blood pressure (to detect postural hypotension: a drop of >20 mmHg systolic or >10 mmHg diastolic after 3 mins of standing), auscultate for murmurs.
  - *Neurological*: Assess cognitive function (MMSE/MoCA), cerebellar function, proprioception, peripheral sensation.
  - *Musculoskeletal*: Assess joint ROM, muscle strength, examine the feet and footwear.
  - *Vision*: Check visual acuity.
- **Functional Assessment**:
  - **Get Up and Go Test (or Timed Up and Go - TUG)**: Observe the patient standing up from a chair, walking 3 meters, turning, walking back, and sitting down. Assesses gait, balance, and mobility. Taking > 12-15 seconds indicates high fall risk.

## 4. Investigations (檢查)
Tailored to the history and examination findings.
- **Basic Bloods**: CBC (anemia), U&Es (electrolyte imbalance, dehydration), Glucose (hypo/hyperglycemia), Vitamin D level.
- **ECG**: To check for arrhythmias or heart block (especially if syncope or palpitations are suspected).
- **Holter Monitor / Echocardiogram**: If cardiac syncope is suspected.
- **CT Head**: If there is a history of head trauma, focal neurological signs, or use of anticoagulants (to rule out subdural hematoma).
- **DEXA Scan**: To assess bone mineral density (osteoporosis) and fracture risk.

## 5. Management & Prevention (治療與預防)
A multifactorial, multidisciplinary approach is the most effective strategy.

### 1. Medical Interventions
- **Medication Optimization**: Deprescribe unnecessary medications. Taper and stop psychotropics (especially benzodiazepines) if possible. Adjust antihypertensives if postural hypotension is present.
- **Manage Underlying Conditions**: Treat arrhythmias, optimize Parkinson's treatment, provide cataract surgery.
- **Postural Hypotension Management**: Advise slow position changes, adequate hydration, compression stockings.
- **Bone Health**: Prescribe Calcium and Vitamin D supplementation. Initiate osteoporosis treatment (e.g., Bisphosphonates) if indicated to prevent fractures if a fall occurs.

### 2. Physical Interventions
- **Physical Therapy**: The cornerstone of prevention. Prescribe tailored exercise programs focusing on **strength and balance training** (e.g., Tai Chi, Otago Exercise Programme).
- **Mobility Aids**: Provide and train in the correct use of walking sticks or frames.
- **Podiatry**: Treat foot problems, advise on safe, well-fitting, low-heeled footwear with non-slip soles.

### 3. Environmental Interventions
- **Home Safety Assessment**: Often conducted by an Occupational Therapist.
- **Modifications**: Remove loose rugs/clutter, improve lighting (especially nightlights), install grab bars in the bathroom and next to the toilet, use non-slip mats in the shower, ensure stair handrails are secure.

## 6. Complications of Falls (跌倒的併發症)
- **Fractures**: Hip fractures (most severe, high mortality), Colles' fractures (wrist), vertebral compression fractures.
- **Head Injuries**: Subdural hematoma (especially in patients on anticoagulants; can present insidiously weeks after a minor bump).
- **Soft Tissue Injuries**: Lacerations, severe bruising.
- **"Long Lie"**: Inability to get up after a fall, remaining on the floor for > 1 hour. Can lead to hypothermia, dehydration, pressure sores, rhabdomyolysis, and pneumonia.
- **Psychological**: Fear of falling, leading to loss of confidence, restricted activity, and social isolation.
