# Falls

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: An 80-year-old woman fell in her bathroom 2 days ago. She did not hit her head or break any bones, but she is now very afraid of falling again and refuses to leave her bed.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
PHYSICAL (4P):
- Presenting complaint: Recent fall, fear of falling.
- Precipitating factors: Slippery bathroom floor, possible intrinsic factors (e.g., poor vision, medications).
- Pattern: Single recent episode, but significant psychological impact.
- Palliation/Provocation: Staying in bed reduces fear but worsens deconditioning.

BATHE:
- Background: "You had a fall in the bathroom a couple of days ago. How are you feeling physically now?"
- Affect: "How has this fall made you feel emotionally?"
- Trouble: "What is the most frightening part about getting out of bed now?"
- Handling: "How are you managing to go to the toilet or get food if you are staying in bed?"
- Empathy: "It is completely understandable that you feel scared. Falls can be very shaking experiences."

ICE:
- Ideas: "Why do you think you fell in the bathroom?"
- Concerns: "Are you worried that if you get up, you will fall and break a bone?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to help you feel safe enough to move around again?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Recent fall. Need to assess for underlying causes (e.g., postural hypotension, arrhythmias, visual impairment, polypharmacy). Risk of deconditioning and pressure sores from bed rest.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Severe fear of falling (FOF), leading to self-imposed mobility restriction.

SOCIAL:
- Loss of independence, increased reliance on caregivers.

BARRIERS:
- Overcoming the psychological barrier to mobilize.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I know the fall was scary, but staying in bed actually makes your muscles weaker, which increases your risk of falling again. We are going to work together to make your home safer and get your strength back."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "We need to get you moving again slowly. A physiotherapist can teach you exercises to improve your balance and strength."
- "Let's arrange for an occupational therapist to visit your home. They can suggest adding grab rails in the bathroom and removing any loose rugs."
- "When you stand up from sitting or lying down, do it very slowly to prevent dizziness."

PRESCRIBE:
- Review and stop any medications that increase fall risk (e.g., sedatives, certain blood pressure pills).
- Prescribe Vitamin D and Calcium if indicated for bone health.

REFER:
- Refer to Physiotherapy (PT) for balance and gait training.
- Refer to Occupational Therapy (OT) for home safety assessment and modifications.

INVESTIGATE:
- Check lying and standing blood pressure.
- Review vision and hearing.
- ECG to rule out arrhythmias if syncopal.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor mobility, confidence levels, and any further falls.

PLAN:
- Schedule a follow-up in 2 weeks to review progress with PT and OT.

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the referrals to PT and OT, then ask: "Just to make sure I explained it clearly, how will the physiotherapist and occupational therapist help you?"

ICE:
Directly address her fear by validating it, but explaining the counter-intuitive fact that bed rest worsens the risk.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know you are scared to walk. Would you be willing to just try sitting in a chair for your meals today, as a first small step?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's review the medications you are taking to see if any of them might have made you dizzy, and then we will talk about how to make your home safer."
