# Chronic Hepatitis B (CHB) (慢性乙型肝炎)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: Persistence of Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) in the blood for **more than 6 months**.
- **Epidemiology**: Highly endemic in Asia (including Hong Kong) and Sub-Saharan Africa. In Hong Kong, the prevalence is ~7-8%. It is the leading cause of liver cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in the region.
- **Transmission**:
  - **Vertical (Perinatal)**: Mother-to-child transmission during birth. This is the most common route in endemic areas and carries a >90% risk of developing *chronic* infection if the infant is not given immunoprophylaxis.
  - **Horizontal**: Sexual contact, exposure to infected blood (sharing needles, occupational exposure). Infection in adulthood carries a <5% risk of becoming chronic.

## 2. Serology & Virology (血清學與病毒學)
Understanding the markers is crucial for diagnosis and staging.
- **HBsAg (Surface Antigen - 表面抗原)**: The hallmark of infection. If positive > 6 months = Chronic Hepatitis B.
- **Anti-HBs (Surface Antibody - 表面抗體)**: Indicates immunity (either from vaccination or resolved past infection).
- **Anti-HBc (Core Antibody - 核心抗體)**:
  - *IgM anti-HBc*: Indicates acute infection (or severe acute exacerbation of chronic).
  - *Total anti-HBc (IgG)*: Indicates past or current infection. (Vaccination does *not* produce anti-HBc).
- **HBeAg (E Antigen - e抗原)**: A marker of active viral replication and high infectivity.
- **Anti-HBe (E Antibody - e抗體)**: Indicates lower viral replication and lower infectivity (usually).
- **HBV DNA**: Measures the actual viral load in the blood.

## 3. Natural History & Phases of Chronic Infection (自然史與疾病階段)
The relationship between the virus and the host immune system is dynamic.
1. **Immune Tolerant Phase (HBeAg positive chronic infection)**:
   - High HBV DNA, HBeAg positive.
   - Normal ALT (no liver inflammation).
   - The immune system ignores the virus. Common in young patients infected at birth. Treatment is usually not indicated.
2. **Immune Clearance Phase (HBeAg positive chronic hepatitis)**:
   - The immune system starts attacking infected hepatocytes.
   - Fluctuating/high ALT, High HBV DNA, HBeAg positive.
   - Risk of liver damage. Treatment often indicated.
3. **Inactive Carrier Phase (HBeAg negative chronic infection)**:
   - Successful immune control (Seroconversion: loss of HBeAg, development of anti-HBe).
   - Normal ALT, low or undetectable HBV DNA (< 2000 IU/mL).
   - Good prognosis, but requires monitoring.
4. **Reactivation Phase (HBeAg negative chronic hepatitis)**:
   - The virus mutates (pre-core mutant) to stop producing HBeAg, but replication resumes.
   - Elevated ALT, moderate/high HBV DNA, HBeAg negative, Anti-HBe positive.
   - Progressive liver damage. Treatment indicated.

## 4. Clinical Evaluation & Monitoring (臨床評估與監測)
- Most patients are asymptomatic until advanced cirrhosis or HCC develops.
- **Initial Assessment**:
  - History: Family history of HBV/HCC, alcohol use, other risk factors.
  - Physical Exam: Signs of chronic liver disease (spider nevi, palmar erythema, splenomegaly, ascites, jaundice).
  - Bloods: Full HBV serology panel, HBV DNA level, LFTs (ALT/AST, Bilirubin, Albumin), CBC (platelets drop in cirrhosis), PT/INR.
  - Screen for co-infections: HCV, HIV.
- **Assessment of Liver Fibrosis**:
  - Non-invasive: Transient Elastography (FibroScan) to measure liver stiffness.
  - Liver biopsy (rarely needed now).
- **HCC Surveillance (Crucial)**:
  - Ultrasound of the liver +/- Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) every 6 months.
  - Indicated for high-risk groups: Asian men >40, Asian women >50, anyone with cirrhosis, or family history of HCC.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
### Goals of Therapy
- Long-term suppression of HBV DNA.
- Normalization of ALT.
- Prevention of disease progression to cirrhosis, hepatic decompensation, and HCC.
- *Note*: Complete cure (loss of HBsAg) is rare with current oral therapies.

### Indications for Treatment
Generally, treat if there is evidence of active liver damage (elevated ALT) AND active viral replication (elevated HBV DNA), OR if cirrhosis is present regardless of ALT/DNA levels.
- HBeAg positive or negative chronic hepatitis with HBV DNA > 2000 IU/mL and elevated ALT.
- Cirrhosis with detectable HBV DNA.

### Pharmacological Therapy
- **Nucleos(t)ide Analogues (NAs - 核苷/核苷酸類似物)**: First-line therapy.
  - *Mechanism*: Inhibit HBV reverse transcriptase, halting viral replication.
  - *First-line agents*: **Entecavir (恩替卡韋)** or **Tenofovir (替諾福韋 - TDF or TAF)**.
  - *Advantages*: Highly potent, very low risk of viral resistance, well-tolerated oral daily pill.
  - *Disadvantages*: Usually requires lifelong therapy (stopping can cause severe flares).
  - *Monitoring*: Monitor renal function and bone density with TDF; TAF has a better renal/bone safety profile.
- **Pegylated Interferon-alpha (PEG-IFN)**:
  - *Mechanism*: Immune modulator and antiviral.
  - *Advantages*: Finite duration of treatment (usually 48 weeks), higher chance of HBeAg and HBsAg loss compared to NAs.
  - *Disadvantages*: Subcutaneous injection, numerous side effects (flu-like symptoms, depression, cytopenias), contraindicated in decompensated cirrhosis.

## 6. Prevention (預防)
- **Universal Vaccination**: 3-dose series (0, 1, 6 months). Highly effective.
- **Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission (PMTCT)**:
  - All pregnant women must be screened for HBsAg.
  - Infants born to HBsAg+ mothers must receive Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG) and the first dose of the HepB vaccine within 12 hours of birth.
  - Mothers with high viral load (HBV DNA > 200,000 IU/mL) should receive antiviral therapy (e.g., Tenofovir) during the 3rd trimester to further reduce transmission risk.
