# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) (慢性阻塞性肺病)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A common, preventable, and treatable disease characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation that is due to airway and/or alveolar abnormalities, usually caused by significant exposure to noxious particles or gases.
- **Epidemiology**: A leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Highly prevalent in older adults with a history of smoking.

## 2. Pathophysiology & Etiology (病理生理學與病因)
- **Etiology**:
  - **Tobacco Smoking**: The primary cause (>80% of cases).
  - **Biomass Fuel Exposure**: Important cause in developing countries (indoor cooking/heating).
  - **Occupational Dusts and Chemicals**.
  - **Genetic**: Alpha-1 Antitrypsin (AAT) Deficiency (suspect in young patients <45 years with emphysema, especially lower lobe, and minimal smoking history; also associated with liver cirrhosis).
- **Pathophysiology**: Chronic inflammation leads to two main structural changes (which often coexist):
  1. **Chronic Bronchitis**: Inflammation and narrowing of the small airways (bronchiolitis), with mucous gland hypertrophy and hypersecretion. Clinically defined as a chronic productive cough for at least 3 months in 2 consecutive years.
  2. **Emphysema**: Destruction of the alveolar walls, leading to permanent enlargement of the airspaces distal to the terminal bronchioles. This causes loss of elastic recoil, leading to airway collapse during expiration and gas trapping (hyperinflation).

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Symptoms**:
  - Chronic, progressive dyspnea (shortness of breath), initially on exertion, later at rest.
  - Chronic cough (often the first symptom).
  - Chronic sputum production.
  - Wheezing and chest tightness.
- **Physical Examination**:
  - *Early*: May be normal, or prolonged expiratory phase, mild wheeze.
  - *Advanced*: Hyperinflated "barrel" chest, use of accessory muscles, pursed-lip breathing, decreased breath sounds, hyperresonance on percussion, cyanosis, signs of right heart failure (cor pulmonale - elevated JVP, peripheral edema, right ventricular heave).
- **Phenotypes (Historical, but clinically useful)**:
  - *Pink Puffer (Emphysema predominant)*: Thin, severe dyspnea, hyperinflated, relatively normal blood gases until late.
  - *Blue Bloater (Chronic Bronchitis predominant)*: Overweight, cyanotic, chronic productive cough, prone to hypoxia, hypercapnia, and cor pulmonale.

## 4. Diagnosis & Assessment (診斷與評估)
- **Spirometry (肺功能測試)**: Required to establish the diagnosis.
  - **Diagnostic Criterion**: Post-bronchodilator **FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70** confirms the presence of persistent airflow limitation.
  - *Severity of airflow limitation (GOLD Grades 1-4)* is based on the post-bronchodilator FEV1 % predicted (≥80%, 50-79%, 30-49%, <30%).
- **Assessment of Symptoms and Exacerbation Risk (GOLD ABE Assessment)**:
  - *Symptoms*: Assessed using the mMRC dyspnea scale or CAT (COPD Assessment Test) score.
  - *Exacerbation History*: Number of exacerbations in the past year (0-1 not leading to hospital admission vs. ≥2 or ≥1 leading to hospital admission).
  - This combined assessment (Groups A, B, E) guides initial pharmacological therapy.
- **Other Investigations**:
  - Chest X-ray: To rule out other conditions (e.g., lung cancer, heart failure). May show hyperinflation, flattened diaphragm, bullae.
  - Alpha-1 Antitrypsin level: Check once in all patients with COPD.
  - Arterial Blood Gas (ABG): In severe COPD or acute exacerbations to check for type II respiratory failure (hypoxia + hypercapnia).

## 5. Management: Stable COPD (穩定期的治療與管理)
### Non-Pharmacological (Crucial for altering disease course)
- **Smoking Cessation**: The ONLY intervention proven to slow the accelerated decline in lung function.
- **Pulmonary Rehabilitation**: Highly effective for improving exercise capacity, reducing symptoms, and improving quality of life.
- **Vaccinations**: Annual Influenza, Pneumococcal, COVID-19, RSV (for older adults).
- **Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT)**: Proven to improve survival in patients with severe chronic resting hypoxemia (PaO2 ≤ 55 mmHg or SaO2 ≤ 88%, OR PaO2 56-59 mmHg with evidence of cor pulmonale/polycythemia). Must be used for >15 hours/day.

### Pharmacological Therapy (Symptom relief and exacerbation reduction)
Unlike asthma, bronchodilators are the mainstay of treatment, and ICS is used only in specific situations.
- **Bronchodilators**:
  - **SABA / SAMA**: As needed for symptom relief in all patients.
  - **LABA** (Long-Acting Beta2-Agonist, e.g., Salmeterol, Formoterol) or **LAMA** (Long-Acting Muscarinic Antagonist, e.g., Tiotropium).
  - *Initial Therapy*: Start with a single long-acting bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA). LAMA is slightly better at preventing exacerbations.
  - *Step-up*: If symptoms persist or exacerbations occur, combine **LABA + LAMA** (more effective than monotherapy).
- **Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)**:
  - **NEVER use as monotherapy in COPD** (increases risk of pneumonia without improving survival).
  - *Indication*: Add to LABA/LAMA (Triple Therapy: LABA + LAMA + ICS) ONLY for patients with frequent exacerbations despite dual bronchodilators, OR patients with a history of asthma, OR patients with elevated blood eosinophils (≥ 300 cells/µL).
- **Other Agents**: Azithromycin (macrolide antibiotic with anti-inflammatory properties) or Roflumilast (PDE4 inhibitor) for severe, frequently exacerbating patients.

## 6. Acute Exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD) (慢性阻塞性肺病急性發作)
- **Definition**: An acute worsening of respiratory symptoms (increased dyspnea, increased sputum volume, increased sputum purulence) that results in additional therapy.
- **Triggers**: Viral infections (most common), bacterial infections, environmental pollutants.
- **Management**:
  - **Oxygen**: Controlled oxygen therapy. Target SpO2 88-92% to avoid worsening hypercapnia (due to Haldane effect and V/Q mismatch changes). Use Venturi masks. Check ABG.
  - **Bronchodilators**: Nebulized SABA (Salbutamol) and SAMA (Ipratropium).
  - **Systemic Corticosteroids**: Oral Prednisolone (e.g., 40mg daily for 5 days). Improves lung function, reduces recovery time and risk of early relapse.
  - **Antibiotics**: Indicated if all 3 cardinal symptoms are present (increased dyspnea, sputum volume, sputum purulence), OR if 2 are present and one is increased purulence, OR if requiring mechanical ventilation. (e.g., Amoxicillin-Clavulanate, Macrolide, or Doxycycline for 5-7 days).
  - **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP)**: Indicated for acute respiratory acidosis (pH ≤ 7.35 and PaCO2 > 45 mmHg) despite optimal medical therapy. Reduces mortality and need for intubation.
