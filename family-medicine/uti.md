# Urinary Tract Infection (UTI) (尿道炎)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: Infection of any part of the urinary system.
  - *Lower UTI (Cystitis)*: Infection of the bladder.
  - *Upper UTI (Pyelonephritis)*: Infection of the kidneys.
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common, especially in women (due to shorter urethra and proximity to the anus). 50% of women will have at least one UTI in their lifetime. Less common in men (if present, implies complicated UTI or anatomical abnormality).

## 2. Classification & Etiology (分類與病因)
- **Uncomplicated UTI**: Infection in a healthy, non-pregnant, premenopausal female with a normal urinary tract.
- **Complicated UTI**: Infection associated with factors that increase the risk of treatment failure or serious outcomes. Includes: males, pregnant women, children, elderly, catheterized patients, anatomical/functional abnormalities (e.g., BPH, stones, strictures), immunosuppression (e.g., diabetes), and healthcare-associated infections.
- **Pathogens**:
  - ***Escherichia coli* (E. coli)**: Causes 75-90% of uncomplicated UTIs.
  - *Staphylococcus saprophyticus*: Common in young, sexually active females.
  - *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Proteus mirabilis* (associated with struvite stones), *Enterococcus* species.
  - *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*: Common in complicated or catheter-associated UTIs.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
### Lower UTI (Cystitis - 膀胱炎)
- Dysuria (排尿灼痛 - burning sensation on urination).
- Frequency (尿頻 - needing to pass urine often).
- Urgency (尿急 - sudden, compelling urge to urinate).
- Suprapubic pain or heaviness.
- Hematuria (血尿 - visible or microscopic).
- Cloudy, foul-smelling urine.
- *Note*: Systemic symptoms (high fever, rigors, significant nausea) are usually ABSENT in simple cystitis.

### Upper UTI (Acute Pyelonephritis - 急性腎盂腎炎)
- Symptoms of cystitis may or may not be present.
- **Flank pain / Loin pain** (unilateral or bilateral).
- **Costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness** on percussion.
- **Systemic symptoms**: High fever (>38°C), rigors/chills, nausea, vomiting, malaise.

### UTIs in the Elderly
- Classic symptoms may be absent.
- May present atypically with **acute confusion (delirium)**, generalized weakness, falls, or new-onset urinary incontinence.

## 4. Investigations (檢查)
- **Urine Dipstick (尿液試紙)**: First-line point-of-care test.
  - **Nitrites**: Highly specific for Enterobacteriaceae (like *E. coli*), which convert nitrates to nitrites.
  - **Leukocyte Esterase (LE)**: Indicates pyuria (white blood cells in urine). Highly sensitive but less specific.
  - *Interpretation*: If both are positive in a woman with typical symptoms, diagnosis is highly likely.
- **Mid-stream Urine (MSU) for Microscopy, Culture, and Sensitivity (MC&S)**:
  - *Indications*: Complicated UTIs, men, pregnant women, children, suspected pyelonephritis, recurrent UTIs, failed empirical treatment, or atypical symptoms.
  - *Not routinely required* for simple, uncomplicated cystitis in healthy women.
- **Blood Tests**: CBC, CRP, U&Es, Blood cultures (indicated for suspected pyelonephritis or sepsis).
- **Imaging (Ultrasound KUB or CT Urogram)**: Indicated for recurrent UTIs, suspected stones, obstruction, pyelonephritis not responding to treatment within 48-72 hours, or men with UTIs.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
### Uncomplicated Lower UTI (Cystitis in Women)
- **Empirical Antibiotics** (Local resistance patterns must be considered; *E. coli* resistance to Ampicillin and Cotrimoxazole is high in many areas):
  - **Nitrofurantoin (呋喃妥因)**: 100mg BD for 5 days. (Avoid if eGFR < 45 or near term pregnancy).
  - **Fosfomycin (磷黴素)**: 3g single dose sachet.
  - **Trimethoprim**: 200mg BD for 3 days (only if local resistance is low).
  - *Alternative*: Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) or Cefuroxime for 3-5 days.
- **Symptomatic Relief**: Increased fluid intake. Urinary analgesics (e.g., Phenazopyridine - warn patient it turns urine orange) for severe dysuria (max 2 days).

### Complicated UTI / Acute Pyelonephritis
- Requires longer courses of antibiotics (7-14 days) and often broader coverage.
- **Mild/Moderate (Outpatient)**: Oral Ciprofloxacin or Levofloxacin (if fluoroquinolone resistance is low), or Amoxicillin-Clavulanate.
- **Severe (Inpatient admission required)**: Inability to tolerate oral fluids, signs of sepsis, pregnant, or immunocompromised.
  - IV antibiotics: Ceftriaxone, Piperacillin-Tazobactam, or Aminoglycosides (e.g., Gentamicin). Step down to oral based on culture results.

### UTIs in Pregnancy
- **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria MUST be treated** in pregnancy to prevent progression to pyelonephritis and risk of preterm birth/low birth weight.
- Safe antibiotics: Nitrofurantoin (avoid in 3rd trimester), Amoxicillin-Clavulanate, Cefalexin.
- *Avoid*: Trimethoprim (teratogenic in 1st trimester), Fluoroquinolones (cartilage damage).

## 6. Prevention of Recurrent UTIs (預防復發)
Defined as ≥ 2 infections in 6 months or ≥ 3 in 1 year.
- **Lifestyle**: High fluid intake, voiding after sexual intercourse, wiping front to back, avoiding spermicides/diaphragms.
- **Postmenopausal women**: Topical vaginal estrogen therapy (reverses vaginal atrophy and restores normal flora).
- **Prophylaxis**: If lifestyle measures fail, consider post-coital antibiotic prophylaxis (single dose) or continuous low-dose prophylaxis (e.g., Nitrofurantoin 50mg daily) for 6 months.
