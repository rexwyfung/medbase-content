# Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Uncomplicated cystitis (young, non-pregnant, no structural abnormality): symptoms (dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain) + positive dipstick/MSU — treat empirically
**Answer**: 單純性膀胱炎（年輕、非妊娠、無結構異常）：症狀（排尿困難、尿頻、尿急、恥骨上疼痛）+尿液試紙/中段尿培養陽性——經驗性治療

**Examiner's Pearl**: 1st line uncomplicated cystitis (HK/NICE): Nitrofurantoin 100mg MR twice daily × 5 days (if eGFR ≥30); trimethoprim 200mg BD × 7 days; or cefalexin 500mg QDS × 7 days
**Answer**: 單純性膀胱炎一線（香港/NICE）：呋喃妥因100mg緩釋每日兩次×5天（若eGFR≥30）；甲氧苄啶200mg每日兩次×7天；或頭孢氨苄500mg每日四次×7天

**Examiner's Pearl**: Pyelonephritis features: fever, rigors, loin pain, vomiting, systemically unwell — requires IV antibiotics (if severe/pregnant) or oral quinolone (if sensitive); hospitalisation criteria if septic
**Answer**: 腎盂腎炎特徵：發燒、寒戰、腰痛、嘔吐、全身不適——需要靜脈抗生素（若嚴重/妊娠）或口服喹諾酮類（若敏感）；若敗血症則入院

**Examiner's Pearl**: CAUTI (catheter-associated): do NOT treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in catheterised patients; treat only if symptomatic
**Answer**: 導管相關尿路感染：不要治療導尿管患者的無症狀細菌尿；僅在有症狀時治療

**Examiner's Pearl**: Recurrent UTI (≥2 in 6 months / ≥3 in 12 months): investigate (renal USS, MSU, urine cytology), consider prophylaxis (cranberry, D-mannose, topical vaginal oestrogen in post-menopausal, low-dose antibiotic prophylaxis)
**Answer**: 復發性尿路感染（≥6個月2次/≥12個月3次）：調查（腎臟超聲、中段尿、尿液細胞學），考慮預防（蔓越莓、D-甘露糖、絕經後局部陰道雌激素、低劑量抗生素預防）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Nitrofurantoin AVOID in: eGFR <30 (ineffective + nephrotoxic), G6PD deficiency, term pregnancy (risk of neonatal haemolysis)
**Answer**: 呋喃妥因禁忌症：eGFR <30（無效+腎毒性）、G6PD缺乏症、足月妊娠（新生兒溶血風險）

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Ms Wong, 28F, sexually active. Presents with 2 days of dysuria, frequency, and lower abdominal pain. Urine dipstick is positive for nitrites and leukocytes. She is worried about a sexually transmitted infection (STI).

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Female anatomy (short urethra), sexually active.
- Precipitating: Recent sexual intercourse, poor hydration, holding urine.
- Perpetuating: Anxiety about STIs, wiping back-to-front.
- Protective: No red flags (no fever, no flank pain, no vomiting), seeking early treatment.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You mentioned it burns when you pee and you have to go very often. How has this been affecting your day?"
- Affect: "How do you feel about having these symptoms?"
- Trouble: "What is the most uncomfortable or worrying part about this?"
- Handling: "How have you been managing the pain so far? Have you been drinking more water?"
- Empathy: "It is incredibly uncomfortable and disruptive to have this burning sensation. I understand why you are worried."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you think is causing this burning sensation?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned being worried about a sexually transmitted infection. Can you tell me more about why you are concerned about that?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to clear this up and ease your mind?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Uncomplicated lower Urinary Tract Infection (Cystitis).
- Must rule out Pyelonephritis (upper UTI) by checking for fever and loin pain.
- Must rule out pregnancy.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- High anxiety regarding potential STIs.

SOCIAL:
- Impact on sexual relationship due to anxiety and discomfort.

BARRIERS:
- Embarrassment discussing sexual history and hygiene practices.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"Your urine test shows signs of a simple bacterial infection in your bladder. This is extremely common in women and is NOT necessarily a sexually transmitted infection."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Because women have a short urethra, bacteria from the outside can easily be pushed inside during sex. This causes a simple bladder infection, often called 'honeymoon cystitis'."
- "To prevent this in the future, always try to pee immediately after having sex to flush out any bacteria."
- "Always wipe from front to back after using the toilet. Drink plenty of water every day, and do not hold your urine when you need to go."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe a 3-day course of antibiotics (e.g., Nitrofurantoin or Trimethoprim). It is very effective and you should feel better within 24 hours."
- "I will also give you some medicine to make your urine less acidic, which will stop the burning sensation quickly."

REFER:
- Not required for a simple UTI. (Offer STI screening if she is still anxious or has new partners).

INVESTIGATE:
- Urine culture is usually not needed for a first-time simple UTI, but can be sent if symptoms are atypical or recurrent.
- Offer STI screening (Chlamydia/Gonorrhea swab) for her peace of mind.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor for symptom resolution.

PLAN:
- No follow-up needed if symptoms resolve.

SAFETY NET:
"If you develop a high fever, start vomiting, or get severe pain in your back or sides, go to the A&E immediately, as this means the infection has spread to your kidneys."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the hygiene and post-coital voiding advice, then ask, "Does it make sense why peeing after sex helps prevent this?"

ICE:
Directly address her fear of STIs by explaining the anatomical reasons for UTIs in women, normalizing the condition.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I can offer you an STI check today just to give you complete peace of mind. Is that something you would like to do?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about what the urine test showed, and then we will discuss how to treat it and prevent it from happening again."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: Patient presents with burning pain when passing urine and needing to go to the toilet frequently for the past 2 days.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned it burns when you pee and you are going frequently. Can you tell me more about what you are experiencing?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Allow the patient to describe the dysuria and frequency without interrupting.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
ASSESS UTI SYMPTOMS:
- "When did the burning start?"
- "Are you passing small amounts of urine very often?"
- "Is there any blood in your urine, or does it look cloudy or smell bad?"
- "Do you have any pain in your lower tummy?"

SCREEN FOR RED FLAGS (PYELONEPHRITIS - MUST ASK):
- "Have you had a fever, chills, or felt shivery?"
- "Do you have any pain in your back or your sides (flank pain)?"
- "Have you been feeling nauseous or vomiting?"

SCREEN FOR STIs / VAGINITIS:
- "Do you have any unusual vaginal discharge or itching?"
- "Is there any chance you could be pregnant?"

PHYSICAL EXAM:
- Palpate lower abdomen for suprapubic tenderness.
- Percuss the back (costovertebral angle) to check for kidney tenderness.

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"What do you think is causing this burning sensation?"

CONCERNS:
"Are you worried about a kidney infection or anything else?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What were you hoping we could do to help you today?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"Based on your symptoms and the urine test, you have a simple Urinary Tract Infection, or a bladder infection."

EXPLANATION:
"This happens when normal bacteria from the outside get pushed up into the short tube that leads to your bladder. The bacteria irritate the lining of the bladder, which causes the burning pain and the feeling that you constantly need to pee."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does that explanation make sense to you?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"This is very common in women and is easily treated with a short course of antibiotics."

INVESTIGATE:
"We have done the urine dipstick today which confirms the infection. We do not usually need to send it to the lab for a simple first-time infection."

PLAN:
"I will prescribe a 3-day course of antibiotics. You must finish all 3 days. I will also give you a powder to mix with water that makes your urine less acidic and stops the burning."

ADVICE:
"Drink plenty of water to flush the bacteria out. Always wipe from front to back, and try to pee immediately after sex."

SAFETY NET:
"If you develop a high fever, back pain, or start vomiting, go to the A&E immediately as it means the infection has reached your kidneys."

## Station 4 — Medical Record + Prescription

### Subjective (S)
Miss Chan Wai Ling (F/26), office worker, presents with 2-day history of dysuria, urinary frequency (every 30-60 min), suprapubic discomfort, and urgency. No fever, no rigors, no loin pain, no vomiting. No macroscopic haematuria. No vaginal discharge. Sexually active (stable partner ×6 months). LMP 2 weeks ago (regular). No previous UTIs. PMH: Nil. NKDA. Not pregnant (urine hCG negative today). G6PD status: not known. Social: Non-smoker. Occasional alcohol. Drinks 1-1.5L fluid/day (increasing today).

### Objective (O)
Vitals: Temp 36.8°C (afebrile), BP 114/72 mmHg, HR 78 bpm.
Abdomen: Mild suprapubic tenderness. NO loin/renal angle tenderness bilaterally. No organomegaly.
Urinalysis (clinic dipstick):
  Nitrites: +++ (gram-negative bacteria strongly suggested)
  Leucocytes: +++ (pyuria)
  Blood: + (microscopic haematuria — common in cystitis)
  Glucose: Negative
  Protein: Negative
  pH: 6.0
Pregnancy test (urine hCG): NEGATIVE

### Assessment (A)
PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS: Uncomplicated Acute Cystitis (bacterial UTI — most likely E. coli)
Evidence: Classic triad (dysuria + frequency + suprapubic pain); dipstick positive (nitrites+++ + leucocytes+++); afebrile (no systemic features); non-pregnant; no structural abnormality; no upper tract signs.

NOT: Pyelonephritis (no fever, no loin pain, afebrile); STI (no discharge, stable partner, no urethritis pattern); interstitial cystitis (chronic pattern — first episode).

### Plan (P)
1. ANTIBIOTIC: Nitrofurantoin 100mg modified release (MR) twice daily × 5 days.
   Take with food. Urine may turn dark brown — normal and harmless. Complete full course.
   ⚠️ Check G6PD status at next visit (nitrofurantoin causes haemolytic anaemia in G6PD deficiency).

2. MSU: Midstream urine sent for MC&S (microscopy, culture, sensitivity). Will receive result in 48-72h. If organisms resistant to nitrofurantoin → will contact patient and change antibiotic.

3. SYMPTOMATIC RELIEF: Paracetamol 500mg-1g PRN for suprapubic discomfort. Phenazopyridine not routinely used in HK (colours urine orange — patient education if prescribed elsewhere).

4. HYDRATION: Increase fluid intake to 2-3L water/day — flushes bladder. Avoid caffeine and alcohol (irritants).

5. HYGIENE ADVICE: Wipe front to back. Void after sexual intercourse. Avoid bubble baths/perfumed products near urethra.

6. SAFETY NET: Return/A&E if: fever develops, rigors, loin/back pain, vomiting (pyelonephritis — needs escalation). If no improvement after 48h of antibiotics.

7. FOLLOW-UP: MSU result in 48-72h (phone review). Return if recurrence (≥2 in 6 months → recurrent UTI workup including renal USS, STI screen, cystoscopy referral).

### Prescription
- **Drug**: Nitrofurantoin
- **Preparation**: Modified-release capsules (macrocrystalline)
- **Dose**: 100mg
- **Route**: Oral
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (with food)
- **Duration**: 5 days
- **Quantity**: 10 capsules

**Notes**: Take with food to reduce nausea. Urine will turn dark yellow/brown — this is harmless. Complete full course even if symptoms resolve. Do not use if eGFR <30 mL/min or G6PD deficiency.

**Tips**:
- "Nitrofurantoin" (generic) NOT "Macrobid" alone
- Specify "modified release" (MR) — different from standard release (different dosing)
- "100mg" NOT "100 mg"
- "Twice daily" NOT "BD"
- "Oral" route must be stated
- "With food" — must be specified
- "10 capsules" (2/day × 5 days)
- CONTRAINDICATIONS to check: eGFR <30 (renal failure — check U&E), G6PD deficiency, term pregnancy, history of nitrofurantoin lung toxicity
- In pregnancy: safe in 1st/2nd trimester; AVOID at term (≥36 weeks) — risk of neonatal haemolysis
