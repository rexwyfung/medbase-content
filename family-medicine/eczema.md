# Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema) (異位性皮膚炎 / 濕疹)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A chronic, highly pruritic, inflammatory skin disease characterized by a defective skin barrier and immune dysregulation.
- **Epidemiology**: Very common, affecting 15-20% of children and 1-3% of adults. Often begins in infancy.
- **Atopic March**: The typical progression of allergic diseases: Eczema (infancy) → Food Allergy → Allergic Rhinitis (childhood) → Asthma.

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- **Skin Barrier Dysfunction**: Mutations in the **Filaggrin (FLG)** gene lead to a defective epidermal barrier. This causes increased transepidermal water loss (dry skin) and allows penetration of allergens and microbes.
- **Immune Dysregulation**: Th2-skewed immune response. Overproduction of cytokines (IL-4, IL-13, IL-31) leading to inflammation, IgE production, and severe pruritus (itch).
- **Microbiome Alteration**: Decreased microbial diversity with heavy colonization by *Staphylococcus aureus*, which exacerbates inflammation and causes secondary infections.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Pruritus (Itch - 痕癢)**: The hallmark symptom. "The itch that rashes." Scratching leads to excoriation and lichenification (thickening of skin), creating a vicious itch-scratch cycle.
- **Morphology & Distribution (Age-dependent)**:
  - **Infants (< 2 years)**: Acute, weeping, erythematous papules and plaques. Primarily affects the **face (cheeks), scalp, and extensor surfaces** of limbs. Diaper area is usually spared.
  - **Children (2-12 years)**: Less exudative, more lichenified plaques. Primarily affects **flexural areas** (antecubital and popliteal fossae), neck, wrists, and ankles.
  - **Adults**: Chronic, lichenified, scaly plaques. Flexural involvement, but also prominent on hands, face (eyelids), and neck.
- **Associated Features**: Xerosis (generalized dry skin), Dennie-Morgan lines (folds below the lower eyelid), keratosis pilaris, hyperlinear palms.

## 4. Diagnosis & Assessment (診斷與評估)
- Diagnosis is clinical, based on history and physical examination. No specific laboratory test is diagnostic.
- **Diagnostic Criteria (UK Working Party)**:
  - Must have an itchy skin condition (or parental report of scratching) PLUS ≥ 3 of the following:
    1. History of involvement in skin creases (flexures).
    2. Personal history of asthma or allergic rhinitis (or history of atopic disease in a first-degree relative if child < 4 years).
    3. History of generally dry skin in the past year.
    4. Visible flexural eczema (or eczema involving cheeks/forehead and outer limbs in children < 4 years).
    5. Onset under the age of 2 years.
- **Assessment of Severity**: SCORAD (Scoring Atopic Dermatitis) or EASI (Eczema Area and Severity Index) tools evaluate erythema, edema/papulation, excoriation, lichenification, and surface area involved.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
Management requires a stepwise approach focusing on barrier repair, inflammation control, and trigger avoidance.

### 1. Basic Skin Care (The Foundation)
- **Emollients (潤膚霜)**: The cornerstone of therapy. Must be applied liberally and frequently (at least twice daily, ideally immediately after bathing to lock in moisture). Ointments are better than creams/lotions for very dry skin.
- **Bathing**: Short, lukewarm baths/showers (5-10 mins). Use soap-free, fragrance-free cleansers (soap substitutes). Avoid bubble baths.

### 2. Topical Anti-inflammatory Therapy
- **Topical Corticosteroids (TCS - 外用類固醇)**: First-line for acute flares.
  - *Principle*: Use the lowest potency needed to control the flare, then step down or stop.
  - *Mild* (e.g., Hydrocortisone 1%): For face, neck, groin, axillae, and infants.
  - *Moderate* (e.g., Clobetasone butyrate 0.05% - Eumovate): For body and limbs.
  - *Potent* (e.g., Betamethasone valerate 0.1% - Betnovate): For severe flares on thick skin (palms, soles) or lichenified plaques.
  - *Side effects of overuse*: Skin atrophy (thinning), striae, telangiectasia, systemic absorption (rare).
- **Topical Calcineurin Inhibitors (TCI)**:
  - *Examples*: Tacrolimus ointment (Protopic), Pimecrolimus cream (Elidel).
  - *Use*: Second-line, steroid-sparing agents. Excellent for sensitive areas (face, eyelids, neck) where prolonged steroid use is risky. Does not cause skin atrophy.
  - *Side effect*: Transient burning/stinging upon initial application.

### 3. Management of Pruritus and Infection
- **Antihistamines**: Oral sedating antihistamines (e.g., Chlorpheniramine, Hydroxyzine) at night to help with sleep disrupted by itch. Non-sedating antihistamines are generally ineffective for eczema itch (which is not primarily histamine-driven).
- **Treating Secondary Infection**: Suspect if crusting (golden/honey-colored), weeping, or pustules appear.
  - *Pathogen*: Usually *Staphylococcus aureus*.
  - *Treatment*: Topical antibiotics (e.g., Mupirocin, Fusidic acid) for localized areas. Oral antibiotics (e.g., Flucloxacillin, Cephalexin) for widespread infection.

### 4. Systemic Therapy (For Severe/Refractory Cases)
- Requires dermatology referral.
- **Phototherapy**: Narrowband UVB.
- **Systemic Immunosuppressants**: Oral corticosteroids (short courses only for severe acute flares, avoid long-term), Cyclosporine, Methotrexate, Azathioprine.
- **Biologics**: Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα, blocks IL-4 and IL-13 signaling). Highly effective for moderate-to-severe eczema.
- **JAK Inhibitors**: Oral (e.g., Upadacitinib, Abrocitinib) or topical (Ruxolitinib).

## 6. Complications (併發症)
- **Eczema Herpeticum**: Disseminated Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) infection on eczematous skin. Presents with punched-out erosions, vesicles, and fever. **Medical emergency** requiring systemic Acyclovir.
- **Bacterial Superinfection**: Cellulitis, impetigo.
- **Psychosocial Impact**: Sleep deprivation, poor school/work performance, depression, anxiety, social stigma.
