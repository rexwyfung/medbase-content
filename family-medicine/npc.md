# Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: High incidence in Southern China (including HK). Associated with EBV infection.
**Answer**: 在華南地區（包括香港）發病率高。與EB病毒感染有關。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Presents with neck mass (lymphadenopathy), epistaxis, nasal obstruction, unilateral hearing loss/tinnitus.
**Answer**: 表現為頸部腫塊（淋巴結腫大）、流鼻血、鼻塞、單側聽力下降/耳鳴。

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: A 45-year-old man presents with a painless lump on the right side of his neck for 1 month. He also reports occasional blood-stained sputum and a blocked feeling in his right ear. He is a smoker.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
PHYSICAL (4P):
- Presenting complaint: Painless neck mass, epistaxis/blood-stained sputum, unilateral hearing loss/tinnitus.
- Precipitating factors: Smoking, possible EBV exposure, family history.
- Pattern: Progressive over 1 month.
- Palliation/Provocation: None.

BATHE:
- Background: "You've had this lump on your neck for a month, along with some ear blockage and blood in your spit. How has this been affecting you?"
- Affect: "How are you feeling about these symptoms?"
- Trouble: "What is the most worrying part of this for you?"
- Handling: "How have you been coping with the worry?"
- Empathy: "It is completely understandable to be anxious when you find a new lump."

ICE:
- Ideas: "What do you think might be causing the lump and the ear blockage?"
- Concerns: "Are you worried that this might be cancer?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we would do today to investigate this?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- High suspicion of Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC) given the classic triad: neck mass (cervical lymphadenopathy), unilateral ear symptoms (Eustachian tube dysfunction), and blood-stained sputum.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- High anxiety regarding a potential cancer diagnosis.

SOCIAL:
- Potential impact on work and family life if a cancer diagnosis is confirmed and treatment is required.

BARRIERS:
- Fear of the diagnosis may lead to denial or delay in seeking specialist care.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I know it is very scary to hear that this could be serious. We are going to act quickly to get you to the right specialists so we can find out exactly what is going on and start treatment if needed."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Because of the combination of the lump, the ear blockage, and the blood in your spit, we need to urgently rule out a tumor at the back of your nose, called Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma."
- "You must stop smoking immediately, as smoking can worsen these conditions and affect your recovery."
- "Please make sure you attend the urgent appointment with the Ear, Nose, and Throat specialist."

PRESCRIBE:
- No specific medications indicated at this stage. Analgesia if the mass becomes painful.

REFER:
- URGENT referral to Ear, Nose, and Throat (ENT) specialist for nasoendoscopy and biopsy.

INVESTIGATE:
- EBV serology (EBV DNA or VCA-IgA) can be checked, but should not delay the ENT referral.
- Imaging (MRI/CT head and neck) will be arranged by ENT.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor for any new symptoms like severe bleeding, difficulty swallowing, or changes in vision.

PLAN:
- Review in 1-2 weeks to ensure the ENT appointment has been scheduled and attended.

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the suspicion of NPC and the urgent referral, then ask: "I have given you a lot of serious information just now. Can you tell me what the next step is and who you will be seeing?"

ICE:
Directly address his fear of cancer. "You mentioned you were worried about cancer. I cannot rule that out today, which is why we are taking this very seriously and referring you urgently."

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"Is there anyone at home you can talk to about this? Would you like me to explain the situation to your wife or a family member?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, I am going to explain what I think might be causing these symptoms, and then we will talk about the urgent tests we need to arrange."
