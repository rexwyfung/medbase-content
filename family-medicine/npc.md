# Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC) (鼻咽癌)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A squamous cell carcinoma arising from the epithelial lining of the nasopharynx (the upper part of the throat behind the nose), most commonly originating in the Fossa of Rosenmüller.
- **Epidemiology**:
  - Highly distinct geographic and ethnic distribution.
  - **Endemic in Southern China (including Hong Kong and Guangdong province)**, Southeast Asia, and among the Inuit population.
  - In Hong Kong, it is one of the most common cancers in men, particularly affecting those in their 40s to 60s.
  - Male to female ratio is approximately 3:1.

## 2. Etiology & Risk Factors (病因與危險因素)
The pathogenesis of NPC is multifactorial, involving a complex interplay of viral, genetic, and environmental factors.
- **Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) Infection**: The most critical factor. Nearly all undifferentiated NPCs (the endemic type) are strongly associated with EBV. The viral genome is found within the tumor cells.
- **Genetic Susceptibility**: Strong familial aggregation. Certain HLA class I genotypes (e.g., HLA-A*02:07) are associated with an increased risk in Southern Chinese populations.
- **Environmental Factors**:
  - **Diet**: High consumption of salt-cured fish and preserved meats (which contain volatile nitrosamines) from an early age is a well-established risk factor.
  - **Smoking**: Increases the risk, particularly for differentiated squamous cell types.
  - Occupational exposure to wood dust or formaldehyde.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
Because the nasopharynx is a hidden anatomical space, NPC often grows silently and presents late. Symptoms depend on the direction of tumor spread.
- **Neck Mass (Cervical Lymphadenopathy)**:
  - **The most common presenting symptom (70-80%)**.
  - Often the *only* symptom.
  - Typically presents as a painless, firm, enlarging lump in the upper neck (Level II, deep to the sternocleidomastoid muscle) or posterior triangle. It is often bilateral.
- **Nasal Symptoms (Tumor growing anteriorly)**:
  - Epistaxis (nosebleeds) or blood-tinged sputum/saliva (especially when "hawking" or spitting in the morning).
  - Unilateral nasal obstruction or discharge.
- **Otologic (Ear) Symptoms (Tumor obstructing the Eustachian tube laterally)**:
  - Unilateral hearing loss (conductive).
  - Tinnitus (ringing in the ear).
  - Aural fullness or a sensation of a blocked ear.
  - **Unilateral Otitis Media with Effusion (OME) in an adult is a major red flag for NPC until proven otherwise.**
- **Neurological Symptoms (Tumor invading the skull base superiorly)**:
  - Cranial nerve palsies.
  - Most commonly affects CN V (Trigeminal - facial numbness/pain), CN VI (Abducens - diplopia/double vision due to lateral rectus palsy), and CN III, IV.
  - Headache.

## 4. Assessment & Diagnosis (評估與診斷)
- **History**: Ask specifically about the triad of symptoms: neck mass, ear symptoms (unilateral blockage/hearing loss), and nasal symptoms (blood-stained sputum).
- **Physical Examination**:
  - Thorough palpation of the neck for lymphadenopathy.
  - Cranial nerve examination.
  - Otoscopy (look for unilateral middle ear effusion).
- **Referral**: Any patient with suspicious symptoms (especially a painless neck mass or unilateral OME) must be referred urgently to an ENT (Ear, Nose, and Throat) specialist.
- **Definitive Diagnosis**:
  - **Nasoendoscopy**: Direct visualization of the nasopharynx.
  - **Endoscopic Biopsy**: The gold standard for definitive tissue diagnosis.
- **EBV Serology & DNA**:
  - Blood tests for EBV IgA antibodies (VCA-IgA, EA-IgA) are used for screening and diagnosis.
  - **Plasma EBV DNA**: A highly sensitive and specific biomarker. Used for screening, diagnosis, monitoring response to treatment, and detecting early recurrence.

## 5. Staging & Investigations (分期與檢查)
Once diagnosed, staging is crucial to determine the extent of the disease and guide treatment.
- **MRI of the Head and Neck**: The imaging modality of choice to assess the primary tumor extent, skull base invasion, and cervical lymph node involvement.
- **PET-CT Scan**: Used to detect distant metastases (most commonly to bone, lung, and liver).

## 6. Management (治療與管理)
Unlike many other head and neck cancers, surgery is *not* the primary treatment for NPC because the nasopharynx is difficult to access surgically and the tumor is highly radiosensitive.
- **Radiotherapy (RT)**:
  - The cornerstone of treatment for all stages.
  - Intensity-Modulated Radiotherapy (IMRT) is the standard technique, allowing high doses to the tumor while sparing adjacent critical structures (salivary glands, brainstem, optic nerves).
- **Chemotherapy**:
  - **Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy (CCRT)**: The standard of care for Stage II to IV disease (locally advanced). Cisplatin is the most commonly used agent.
  - Induction or Adjuvant chemotherapy may also be used in advanced cases.
- **Surgery**:
  - Reserved for salvage treatment in cases of local recurrence or persistent neck nodes after radiotherapy.
- **Immunotherapy**:
  - PD-1 inhibitors (e.g., Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab) are increasingly used for recurrent or metastatic NPC.

## 7. Complications of Treatment (治療併發症)
Radiotherapy to the head and neck causes significant side effects:
- **Acute**: Severe mucositis (mouth ulcers, pain), dysphagia, loss of taste, radiation dermatitis.
- **Late/Chronic**: Xerostomia (severe dry mouth due to salivary gland damage, leading to rampant dental caries), trismus (difficulty opening the mouth due to fibrosis of masticatory muscles), sensorineural hearing loss, hypothyroidism, radiation-induced necrosis of the temporal bone or brain.
