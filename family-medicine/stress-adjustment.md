# Stress and Adjustment Disorders (壓力與適應障礙)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: An Adjustment Disorder is an abnormal and excessive emotional or behavioral reaction to an identifiable psychosocial stressor (e.g., job loss, divorce, illness, financial problems). The symptoms must develop within 3 months of the onset of the stressor.
- **Epidemiology**: Very common in primary care and general hospital settings. Can affect individuals of any age.

## 2. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Symptoms**: The presentation is heterogeneous and can include:
  - Depressed mood, tearfulness, feelings of hopelessness.
  - Anxiety, worry, nervousness, feeling overwhelmed.
  - Behavioral changes (e.g., social withdrawal, poor performance at work/school, reckless behavior).
  - Somatic symptoms (e.g., insomnia, fatigue, headaches, GI distress).
- **Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5)**:
  1. Emotional or behavioral symptoms develop within **3 months** of the stressor.
  2. The distress is **out of proportion** to the severity of the stressor (taking cultural context into account), OR causes **significant impairment** in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning.
  3. The stress-related disturbance does not meet the criteria for another mental disorder (e.g., Major Depressive Disorder, GAD) and is not merely an exacerbation of a preexisting disorder.
  4. The symptoms do not represent normal bereavement.
  5. Once the stressor (or its consequences) has terminated, the symptoms do not persist for more than an additional **6 months**.

## 3. Differential Diagnosis (鑑別診斷)
Adjustment disorder is often considered a "sub-threshold" diagnosis. It is crucial to rule out more severe psychiatric conditions:
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)**: If the patient meets the full criteria for MDD (e.g., ≥5 symptoms for 2 weeks, including pervasive anhedonia or depressed mood), the diagnosis is MDD, even if triggered by a stressor.
- **Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)**: Requires symptoms lasting ≥ 6 months and worry about multiple domains, not just a specific stressor.
- **Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)**: The stressor in PTSD must be extreme (exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence), whereas the stressor in adjustment disorder can be any severity (e.g., a breakup).
- **Normal Stress Reaction**: Normal reactions do not cause significant functional impairment and are proportional to the event.

## 4. Assessment & Red Flags (評估與紅旗症狀)
- **History**: Identify the stressor, assess the timeline, and evaluate the impact on daily functioning.
- **Screening**: Use tools like PHQ-9 (for depression) and GAD-7 (for anxiety) to gauge severity and rule out major disorders.
- **Red Flags (Require urgent psychiatric referral)**:
  - **Suicidal Ideation**: Patients with adjustment disorders are at increased risk for suicide, especially adolescents and those with impulsive traits. Always assess for suicidal thoughts, plans, and intent.
  - Psychotic symptoms (hallucinations, delusions).
  - Severe inability to care for oneself (e.g., not eating/drinking).

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
The primary goal is to help the patient cope with the stressor, build resilience, and return to their baseline level of functioning.

### 1. Psychological Therapy (First-line)
- **Supportive Counseling**: Active listening, validation of feelings, empathy, and providing a safe space to discuss the stressor.
- **Problem-Solving Therapy**: Helping the patient identify actionable steps to address the stressor or its consequences.
- **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)**: Identifying and challenging maladaptive thoughts related to the stressor, and developing healthy coping mechanisms.
- **Stress Management Techniques**: Relaxation training, mindfulness, deep breathing exercises.

### 2. Social Interventions
- Encourage utilizing social support networks (family, friends, support groups).
- Address practical issues if possible (e.g., referral to a Medical Social Worker for financial or housing assistance).
- Consider a short period of sick leave if occupational stress is the primary trigger and functioning is severely impaired, but encourage a graded return to work to prevent avoidance behaviors.

### 3. Pharmacological Therapy
- **Generally NOT recommended as first-line treatment** for adjustment disorders.
- Medications do not fix the underlying psychosocial problem.
- **Short-term symptomatic relief**:
  - If insomnia is severe, a short course (few days) of a hypnotic (e.g., Zopiclone) or a sedating antihistamine may be considered.
  - If anxiety is paralyzing, a very short course of a benzodiazepine might be used cautiously, but the risk of dependence must be weighed.
- **Antidepressants (SSRIs)**: Usually reserved for cases that progress to Major Depressive Disorder or Generalized Anxiety Disorder.

## 6. Prognosis (預後)
- Generally excellent. Most patients recover fully within 6 months of the stressor resolving or adapting to the new situation.
- A minority may progress to develop a major psychiatric disorder (MDD, GAD).
