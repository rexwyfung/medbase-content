# Screening for Diabetes and Hypertension (糖尿病與高血壓篩查)

## 1. Principles of Screening (篩查原則)
- **Definition**: The systematic application of a test to an asymptomatic population to identify individuals with an unrecognized disease or precursor state.
- **Wilson and Jungner Criteria for Screening**:
  1. The condition should be an important health problem (High prevalence, significant morbidity/mortality).
  2. There should be a recognizable latent or early symptomatic stage.
  3. The natural history of the condition should be adequately understood.
  4. There should be a suitable, acceptable, and accurate screening test.
  5. There should be an accepted and effective treatment for patients with recognized disease.
  6. Facilities for diagnosis and treatment should be available.
  7. Early treatment should yield better outcomes than late treatment.
  8. The cost of screening should be economically balanced in relation to possible expenditure on medical care as a whole.
- **Why screen for DM and HTN?**: Both perfectly fulfill these criteria. They are highly prevalent "silent killers" (asymptomatic in early stages) that cause severe microvascular and macrovascular complications. Early detection and treatment significantly reduce the risk of stroke, myocardial infarction, renal failure, and blindness.

## 2. Hypertension Screening (高血壓篩查)
### Rationale
Hypertension is the leading global risk factor for cardiovascular disease and premature death. It is easily detectable and highly treatable.

### Screening Guidelines (Hong Kong Reference Framework / International)
- **Who to screen**: **ALL adults aged 18 years and older.**
- **Frequency**:
  - **Annually**: Adults aged ≥ 40 years, or those at increased risk (e.g., high-normal BP 130-139/85-89 mmHg, overweight/obese, family history of HTN, diabetes, CKD).
  - **Every 3-5 years**: Adults aged 18-39 years with normal blood pressure (< 130/85 mmHg) and no other risk factors.
- **Method**:
  - Office Blood Pressure Measurement (OBPM).
  - Must be measured correctly: Patient seated quietly for 5 mins, arm supported at heart level, appropriate cuff size, no smoking/caffeine 30 mins prior.
- **Follow-up of Positive Screen**:
  - A single high reading does NOT diagnose hypertension (unless severely elevated, e.g., > 180/110 mmHg).
  - Diagnosis requires elevated readings on at least two separate occasions.
  - **Ambulatory BP Monitoring (ABPM)** or **Home BP Monitoring (HBPM)** is strongly recommended to confirm the diagnosis and rule out "white-coat hypertension" (elevated in clinic, normal at home) or identify "masked hypertension" (normal in clinic, elevated at home).

## 3. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Screening (二型糖尿病篩查)
### Rationale
Type 2 DM has a long pre-clinical phase (prediabetes). Microvascular complications (retinopathy, nephropathy) may already be present at the time of clinical diagnosis if screening is not performed.

### Screening Guidelines (Hong Kong Reference Framework / ADA)
- **Who to screen**:
  1. **Universal Screening**: All adults starting at age **45 years** (ADA recently lowered this to 35 years for the US population, but 45 is still widely used as a baseline in many regions; HK guidelines emphasize risk-based screening but generally recommend starting at 45 for average risk).
  2. **Risk-Based Screening (Screen at ANY age if overweight/obese BMI ≥ 23 in Asians PLUS one or more risk factors)**:
     - First-degree relative with diabetes.
     - History of cardiovascular disease.
     - Hypertension (≥ 140/90 mmHg or on therapy).
     - Dyslipidemia (HDL < 0.9 mmol/L or TG > 2.8 mmol/L).
     - Women with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS).
     - Women with a history of Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM).
     - Physical inactivity.
     - Clinical conditions associated with insulin resistance (e.g., severe obesity, acanthosis nigricans).
- **Frequency**:
  - If results are normal, repeat testing at least every **3 years**.
  - If results show Prediabetes, test **annually**.
- **Screening Tests (Any of the following are acceptable)**:
  1. **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG)**: Fasting for ≥ 8 hours. (Normal < 5.6 mmol/L; Prediabetes 5.6-6.9; Diabetes ≥ 7.0).
  2. **HbA1c**: Does not require fasting. Reflects average glucose over 2-3 months. (Normal < 5.7%; Prediabetes 5.7-6.4%; Diabetes ≥ 6.5%). *Note: Inaccurate in conditions affecting red blood cell turnover (e.g., anemia, hemoglobinopathies like Thalassemia, which is common in HK).*
  3. **75g Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)**: 2-hour plasma glucose. The most sensitive test but less convenient.
- **Follow-up of Positive Screen**:
  - Unless there is unequivocal hyperglycemia with classic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss), a positive screening test requires **confirmation with a second test** (either repeating the same test or using a different one) on a separate day to establish the diagnosis.

## 4. Pre-test and Post-test Counseling (篩查前後諮詢)
- **Pre-test**: Explain the purpose of the test, the implications of a positive or negative result, and the potential need for confirmatory testing.
- **Post-test (Negative)**: Reassure, but emphasize the need for continued healthy lifestyle choices and regular re-screening according to guidelines.
- **Post-test (Prediabetes)**: Crucial intervention point. Emphasize that progression to diabetes is not inevitable. Prescribe intensive lifestyle modifications (diet, 5-10% weight loss, exercise). Consider Metformin for high-risk individuals.
- **Post-test (Positive for Disease)**: Deliver the diagnosis empathetically. Initiate comprehensive management (lifestyle, pharmacotherapy, screening for complications).
