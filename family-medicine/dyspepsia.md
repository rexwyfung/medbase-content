# Dyspepsia / PUD (H. pylori)

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: H. pylori prevalence in HK: ~50%; test-and-treat if <55 and no alarm features
**Answer**: 香港幽門螺旋桿菌患病率：約50%；若<55歲且無警示症狀則採用「測試並治療」策略

**Examiner's Pearl**: Alarm features (refer/scope urgently): dysphagia, progressive unintentional weight loss, persistent vomiting, haematemesis/melaena, epigastric mass, iron deficiency anaemia
**Answer**: 警示症狀（緊急轉介/胃鏡）：吞嚥困難、進行性非意願體重減輕、持續嘔吐、嘔血/黑便、上腹部腫塊、缺鐵性貧血

**Examiner's Pearl**: H. pylori testing: urea breath test (UBT) — most accurate non-invasive (stop PPI 2 weeks before); stool antigen; serology (less accurate)
**Answer**: 幽門螺旋桿菌檢測：尿素呼氣試驗（UBT）——最準確非侵入性（2週前停用PPI）；糞便抗原；血清學（準確性較差）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Eradication: Triple therapy — PPI + amoxicillin 1g + clarithromycin 500mg, all BD ×7-14 days; confirm eradication with UBT 4 weeks after completing antibiotics
**Answer**: 根除治療：三聯療法——PPI +阿莫西林1g+克拉黴素500mg，均每日兩次×7-14天；抗生素完成4週後用UBT確認根除

**Examiner's Pearl**: NSAIDs are the other major cause of PUD — always check NSAID use; use lowest dose, with food, co-prescribe PPI
**Answer**: 非甾體抗炎藥是消化性潰瘍的另一主要原因——始終檢查非甾體抗炎藥使用；使用最低劑量，隨餐服用，同時處方PPI

**Examiner's Pearl**: PPI choice: Omeprazole 20mg OD or 40mg OD (active ulcer); take 30 mins before meal for maximum effect
**Answer**: PPI選擇：奧美拉唑20mg每日一次或40mg每日一次（活動性潰瘍）；飯前30分鐘服用效果最佳

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Chan, 45M, office worker. Complains of upper abdominal pain and bloating for 3 months. Positive Urea Breath Test for H. pylori. He is anxious about stomach cancer because his uncle died of it.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Male, age 45, family history of gastric cancer (uncle).
- Precipitating: H. pylori infection, severe stress at work, irregular meal times.
- Perpetuating: Extreme anxiety about cancer, potential NSAID use for headaches, smoking or alcohol use.
- Protective: No red flags (no weight loss, no bleeding), seeking medical help early.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You have had stomach pain for 3 months and the test showed a stomach bacteria. How has this been affecting your daily life?"
- Affect: "How do you feel emotionally when you get these stomach pains?"
- Trouble: "What is worrying you the absolute most about this stomach issue?"
- Handling: "How have you been coping with the pain and the constant worry?"
- Empathy: "It is completely understandable to be highly anxious, especially given what happened to your uncle."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you know about this H. pylori bacteria we found?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned your uncle. Are you worried that this pain means you already have stomach cancer?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to sort this out and ease your mind?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- H. pylori positive dyspepsia. 
- Must ensure absolutely no red flags (ALARM symptoms: Anemia, Loss of weight, Anorexia, Recent onset, Melena/Hematemesis, Swallowing difficulty).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Severe health anxiety and cancer phobia driven by family history.

SOCIAL:
- Work stress contributing to irregular meals and exacerbating acid production.

BARRIERS:
- Fear of the diagnosis might make him demand an immediate, potentially unnecessary endoscopy.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I completely understand your worry about cancer. However, you do not have any alarm signs like weight loss or bleeding. The pain is very likely caused by this bacteria, which we can cure completely."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "H. pylori is a very common stomach bacteria that causes inflammation and ulcers. It is not cancer."
- "Avoid spicy foods, caffeine, and alcohol while your stomach heals."
- "Try to eat regular meals and manage your work stress, as stress directly increases stomach acid."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe a 'Triple Therapy' for 14 days. This includes a strong stomach protector (PPI) and two antibiotics (e.g., Amoxicillin and Clarithromycin)."
- "It is CRUCIAL that you finish the entire 14-day course, even if you feel better after a few days, to completely kill the bacteria."

REFER:
- Routine OGD (endoscopy) referral if age >50. Since he is 45, it is not strictly necessary unless red flags appear, but you can refer routinely for reassurance given his strong family history.

INVESTIGATE:
- Urea Breath Test to confirm eradication. This MUST be done at least 4 weeks AFTER finishing the antibiotics and stopping the PPI.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor for antibiotic side effects (diarrhea, metallic taste in mouth).

PLAN:
- Follow up in 6 to 8 weeks to arrange the repeat breath test.

SAFETY NET:
"If you vomit blood, pass black tarry stools, or have sudden severe abdominal pain, go to the Accident and Emergency department immediately."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the 14-day antibiotic course, then ask, "Can you tell me how long you need to take the antibiotics for, and why it is important not to stop early?"

ICE:
Directly address his fear of stomach cancer and explain clearly why it is unlikely right now based on his symptoms.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"Are you okay with taking multiple pills a day for the next two weeks?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about what this bacteria is, and then we will discuss the specific treatment to get rid of it."
