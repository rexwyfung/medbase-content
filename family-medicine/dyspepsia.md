# Dyspepsia (消化不良)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A symptom complex originating from the gastroduodenal region, characterized by epigastric pain, epigastric burning, postprandial fullness, or early satiation.
- **Epidemiology**: Very common, affecting up to 20-30% of the population. Significantly impacts quality of life and healthcare utilization.

## 2. Etiology & Classification (病因與分類)
Dyspepsia is a symptom, not a diagnosis. It is broadly divided into:
- **Uninvestigated Dyspepsia**: Patients presenting with new dyspeptic symptoms who have not yet had an endoscopy.
- **Investigated Dyspepsia**:
  - **Organic Dyspepsia (~25%)**: A clear structural or metabolic cause is found.
    - Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD - Gastric or Duodenal ulcer), often related to *H. pylori* or NSAIDs.
    - Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD) - often overlaps with dyspepsia.
    - Gastric or Esophageal Malignancy (Stomach cancer).
    - Biliary tract disease (gallstones), Pancreatitis.
    - Medication-induced (NSAIDs, iron supplements, bisphosphonates, calcium channel blockers).
  - **Functional Dyspepsia (FD) (~75%)**: No structural abnormality found on endoscopy to explain symptoms. Diagnosed by Rome IV criteria.
    - *Postprandial Distress Syndrome (PDS)*: Meal-induced fullness and early satiation.
    - *Epigastric Pain Syndrome (EPS)*: Epigastric pain or burning not exclusively related to meals.

## 3. Clinical Evaluation & Red Flags (臨床評估與紅旗症狀)
The primary goal of initial assessment is to identify patients who need urgent endoscopy to rule out malignancy.
- **History**: Characterize the pain (relationship to meals, antacids), associated symptoms (heartburn, regurgitation, nausea, bloating), medication history (NSAIDs, aspirin), smoking, and alcohol use.
- **Red Flags (ALARM symptoms - 必須轉介內窺鏡檢查)**:
  - **A**nemia (iron deficiency) or GI bleeding (hematemesis, melena).
  - **L**oss of weight (unintentional and significant).
  - **A**norexia (loss of appetite).
  - **R**ecent onset of progressive symptoms.
  - **M**elena / Hematemesis.
  - **S**wallowing difficulty (Dysphagia - difficulty swallowing, Odynophagia - painful swallowing).
  - **Age > 55-60 years** with new-onset dyspepsia (threshold varies by guideline, but older age increases malignancy risk).
  - Family history of upper GI cancer.
  - Palpable abdominal mass or lymphadenopathy (Virchow's node).

## 4. Investigations (檢查)
- **Upper GI Endoscopy (OGD - 胃鏡)**: The gold standard. Indicated for patients with ALARM symptoms or older age (>55) with new-onset symptoms.
- **Non-invasive *H. pylori* Testing**:
  - Urea Breath Test (UBT - 碳13/14尿素呼氣測試): Highly sensitive and specific. Must stop PPIs for 2 weeks and antibiotics for 4 weeks before the test to avoid false negatives.
  - Stool Antigen Test: Also highly accurate; same medication restrictions apply.
  - Serology (IgG): Indicates past or present infection; cannot distinguish active from eradicated infection. Not recommended in areas with high prevalence.
- **Blood tests**: CBC (to check for anemia), LFTs, Amylase (if biliary/pancreatic cause suspected).

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
### Uninvestigated Dyspepsia (No Red Flags, Age < 55)
Two main strategies are commonly employed:
1. **"Test and Treat" for *H. pylori***: Perform non-invasive *H. pylori* test. If positive, give eradication therapy. If negative, give a trial of PPI. This is highly recommended in populations with high *H. pylori* prevalence (like Hong Kong).
2. **Empirical Acid Suppression**: Trial of Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) for 4-8 weeks. If symptoms resolve, stop PPI. If symptoms relapse, test for *H. pylori* or consider endoscopy.

### *Helicobacter pylori* Eradication (幽門螺旋菌根除治療)
- **Standard Triple Therapy (14 days)**:
  - PPI (e.g., Omeprazole 20mg BD or Pantoprazole 40mg BD) +
  - Clarithromycin (克拉黴素) 500mg BD +
  - Amoxicillin (阿莫西林) 1g BD (or Metronidazole 400mg BD if penicillin allergic).
- **Bismuth Quadruple Therapy (14 days)** (Often used as first-line in areas with high clarithromycin resistance or as second-line rescue therapy):
  - PPI BD + Bismuth subcitrate QID + Metronidazole QID + Tetracycline QID.
- **Confirmation of Eradication**: Always re-test (using UBT or stool antigen) at least 4 weeks after completing antibiotics and 2 weeks after stopping PPI to confirm eradication.

### Functional Dyspepsia Management
- **Reassurance and Education**: Explain the benign nature of the condition.
- **Lifestyle/Diet**: Eat smaller, more frequent meals. Avoid trigger foods (fatty, spicy, caffeine, alcohol). Stop smoking.
- **Pharmacotherapy**:
  - PPIs or H2-receptor antagonists (H2RAs) (more effective for EPS).
  - Prokinetics (e.g., Domperidone, Metoclopramide, Itopride) (more effective for PDS).
  - Neuromodulators: Low-dose Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs, e.g., Amitriptyline) or SSRIs/SNRIs for refractory symptoms, targeting visceral hypersensitivity.

## 6. Complications of Peptic Ulcer Disease (消化性潰瘍併發症)
If dyspepsia is due to PUD, complications can include:
- **Bleeding**: Most common complication. Presents with hematemesis or melena.
- **Perforation**: Presents with sudden, severe, generalized abdominal pain, peritonitis (rigid abdomen), and pneumoperitoneum on erect CXR. Requires emergency surgery.
- **Gastric Outlet Obstruction**: Due to scarring/edema at the pylorus. Presents with early satiety, bloating, and profuse vomiting of undigested food.
