# Constipation (便秘)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A symptom, not a disease. Generally defined as infrequent bowel movements (typically < 3 per week), passage of hard or lumpy stools, straining during defecation, or a sensation of incomplete evacuation.
- **Epidemiology**: Very common, affecting up to 20% of the population. More prevalent in women, the elderly, and institutionalized individuals.

## 2. Etiology & Classification (病因與分類)
Constipation can be primary (functional) or secondary to an underlying condition.

### Primary (Functional) Constipation
No structural or systemic cause identified. Subdivided into:
- **Normal Transit Constipation**: Most common. Stool moves through the colon at a normal rate, but patients feel constipated (often associated with IBS-C).
- **Slow Transit Constipation**: Decreased colonic motility, leading to infrequent bowel movements and hard stools (due to prolonged water absorption).
- **Defecatory Disorders (Pelvic Floor Dyssynergia)**: Inability to coordinate the pelvic floor muscles and anal sphincter during defecation (paradoxical contraction instead of relaxation).

### Secondary Constipation
- **Dietary/Lifestyle**: Inadequate fiber intake, inadequate fluid intake, physical inactivity.
- **Medications (Very common cause)**:
  - Opioids (e.g., Codeine, Tramadol, Morphine).
  - Anticholinergics (e.g., TCAs, antihistamines, antispasmodics).
  - Iron supplements.
  - Calcium channel blockers (e.g., Verapamil).
  - Antacids containing calcium or aluminum.
- **Endocrine/Metabolic**: Hypothyroidism, Hypercalcemia, Hypokalemia, Diabetes Mellitus (autonomic neuropathy), Pregnancy.
- **Neurological**: Parkinson's disease, Multiple Sclerosis, Spinal cord injury, Stroke.
- **Structural/Colorectal**: Colorectal cancer (causing obstruction), strictures, anal fissures (pain causes withholding), hemorrhoids.

## 3. Clinical Evaluation & Red Flags (臨床評估與紅旗症狀)
- **History**: Define what the patient means by "constipation" (frequency, consistency using Bristol Stool Scale, straining). Ask about diet, fluid intake, physical activity, and perform a thorough medication review.
- **Red Flags (ALARM symptoms - require urgent investigation, e.g., colonoscopy)**:
  - New onset in older adults (> 50 years).
  - Rectal bleeding or melena.
  - Unintentional weight loss.
  - Family history of colorectal cancer or IBD.
  - Severe, persistent abdominal pain.
  - Iron deficiency anemia.
  - Palpable abdominal or rectal mass.
- **Physical Examination**:
  - Abdominal exam: Palpable fecal loading.
  - **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)**: Essential. Assess for masses, strictures, anal fissures, hemorrhoids, resting sphincter tone, and the ability to bear down (assessing for pelvic floor dyssynergia).

## 4. Investigations (檢查)
- **Usually not required** for young patients with mild symptoms and no red flags.
- **Blood tests**: TFTs (TSH), Calcium, U&Es, CBC (if red flags or suspected secondary cause).
- **Colonoscopy**: Indicated if red flags are present or if age > 50 to rule out colorectal cancer.
- **Specialized testing (for refractory cases)**: Anorectal manometry (to diagnose defecatory disorders), Colonic transit study (using radiopaque markers).

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
A stepwise approach is recommended.

### Step 1: Lifestyle and Dietary Modifications
- **Increase Dietary Fiber**: Aim for 25-30g per day. Increase gradually to avoid bloating. Sources: whole grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes.
- **Adequate Hydration**: Ensure sufficient fluid intake (1.5 - 2 liters/day).
- **Physical Activity**: Regular exercise stimulates bowel motility.
- **Bowel Habit Training**: Encourage attempting defecation at the same time daily, usually 15-30 minutes after a meal (taking advantage of the gastrocolic reflex). Use proper posture (feet elevated on a small stool to straighten the anorectal angle).

### Step 2: Bulk-Forming Laxatives (膨脹性瀉藥)
- *Mechanism*: Absorb water to increase stool bulk and soften consistency, stimulating peristalsis.
- *Examples*: Psyllium (Ispaghula husk / Fybogel), Methylcellulose.
- *Note*: Must be taken with plenty of water. May cause bloating initially. Good for long-term use.

### Step 3: Osmotic Laxatives (滲透性瀉藥)
- *Mechanism*: Draw water into the colonic lumen by osmosis, softening stool and increasing volume.
- *Examples*:
  - **Polyethylene Glycol (PEG / Macrogol)**: Highly effective, well-tolerated, good for chronic use.
  - **Lactulose**: A synthetic disaccharide. Bacteria ferment it, producing gas (can cause significant bloating and flatulence).
  - **Magnesium salts** (e.g., Milk of Magnesia): Avoid in renal failure.

### Step 4: Stimulant Laxatives (刺激性瀉藥)
- *Mechanism*: Directly stimulate the enteric nervous system to increase colonic motility and secretion.
- *Examples*: Senna (番瀉葉), Bisacodyl (Dulcolax).
- *Note*: Often used as rescue therapy or for opioid-induced constipation. Long-term daily use is generally discouraged due to potential for dependence or cramping, but may be necessary in some cases (e.g., chronic opioid use).

### Step 5: Other Agents & Interventions
- **Stool Softeners**: Docusate sodium. Low efficacy, generally not recommended as monotherapy.
- **Suppositories/Enemas**: Glycerin suppositories, Phosphate enemas. Used for rapid relief of severe constipation or fecal impaction.
- **Prokinetics/Secretagogues**: Prucalopride (5-HT4 agonist), Linaclotide (for refractory cases).
- **Biofeedback Therapy**: The treatment of choice for pelvic floor dyssynergia (defecatory disorders).

## 6. Complications (併發症)
- **Fecal Impaction**: Severe accumulation of hard stool in the rectum. Can present with paradoxical "overflow diarrhea" (liquid stool leaking around the impaction). Requires manual disimpaction or enemas.
- Hemorrhoids and Anal Fissures (due to straining and hard stools).
- Rectal Prolapse.
