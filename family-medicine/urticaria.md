# Urticaria (Hives) (蕁麻疹 / 風癩)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A vascular reaction of the skin characterized by the sudden appearance of intensely pruritic (itchy), erythematous (red), raised lesions called **wheals** (hives), often accompanied by **angioedema** (swelling of deeper dermal/subcutaneous tissues).
- **Epidemiology**: Very common. Up to 20% of the population will experience acute urticaria at some point in their lives. Chronic urticaria is less common but significantly impacts quality of life.

## 2. Classification (分類)
Based on duration:
- **Acute Urticaria**: Lasts **< 6 weeks**. Often a self-limiting response to an identifiable trigger.
- **Chronic Urticaria**: Lasts **≥ 6 weeks** (wheals occurring daily or almost daily). Further divided into:
  - *Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria (CSU)*: No specific external trigger identified (often autoimmune).
  - *Inducible Urticaria*: Triggered by specific physical stimuli (e.g., cold, heat, pressure, sunlight, vibration, cholinergic/sweating).

## 3. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- The central mechanism is the **degranulation of mast cells** in the dermis.
- Mast cells release preformed mediators, primarily **Histamine**, along with leukotrienes, prostaglandins, and cytokines.
- Histamine binds to H1 receptors on endothelial cells, causing vasodilation (erythema) and increased vascular permeability (fluid leaks out, causing the raised wheal and edema). It also binds to sensory nerve endings, causing intense pruritus.
- **Triggers for Mast Cell Degranulation**:
  - *IgE-mediated (Type I Hypersensitivity)*: Classic allergy (foods, drugs, insect stings).
  - *Non-IgE-mediated (Direct mast cell degranulation)*: Opiates, radiocontrast media, certain foods (strawberries, tomatoes).
  - *Autoimmune*: In many cases of CSU, patients have IgG autoantibodies against the IgE receptor (FcεRI) or IgE itself, causing chronic mast cell activation.

## 4. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Wheals (風團)**:
  - Raised, erythematous plaques with a central pallor.
  - Highly variable in size and shape (can coalesce into giant lesions).
  - **Intensely itchy**.
  - **Evanescent**: A key diagnostic feature. Individual wheals typically appear rapidly, change shape, and **resolve completely within 24 hours** without leaving any marks, bruising, or scarring, only for new ones to appear elsewhere.
- **Angioedema (血管性水腫)**:
  - Occurs in ~40% of patients with urticaria.
  - Swelling of the deeper dermis and subcutaneous/submucosal tissues.
  - Often affects the face (lips, eyelids), tongue, extremities, or genitals.
  - Usually described as painful or burning rather than itchy.
  - Resolves slower than wheals (up to 72 hours).
  - *Danger*: Laryngeal angioedema can cause airway obstruction (a medical emergency).

## 5. Etiology & Triggers (病因與誘發因素)
- **Acute Urticaria**:
  - **Infections**: The most common cause in children (viral URTI, GI infections).
  - **Medications**: Antibiotics (Penicillins, Cephalosporins), NSAIDs (Aspirin, Ibuprofen - can trigger or exacerbate), ACE inhibitors (cause angioedema without urticaria).
  - **Foods**: Peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, eggs, milk (usually IgE-mediated, rapid onset).
  - **Insect stings/bites**.
- **Chronic Urticaria**:
  - Often idiopathic (no cause found).
  - Autoimmune (associated with autoimmune thyroid disease).
  - Physical triggers (Dermographism - stroking the skin produces a wheal; Cold urticaria).

## 6. Assessment & Red Flags (評估與紅旗症狀)
- **History**: Detailed timeline (duration of individual lesions is crucial), potential triggers (new foods, drugs, infections, physical stimuli), associated symptoms.
- **Red Flags (Anaphylaxis - 過敏性休克)**: Urticaria can be the cutaneous manifestation of a systemic, life-threatening anaphylactic reaction. Assess for:
  - Respiratory compromise (stridor, wheezing, dyspnea, lip/tongue swelling).
  - Cardiovascular compromise (hypotension, tachycardia, syncope, dizziness).
  - Severe GI symptoms (vomiting, severe cramps).
  - *Action*: Requires immediate IM Epinephrine (Adrenaline).
- **Red Flags (Urticarial Vasculitis)**:
  - Individual lesions last **> 24 hours**.
  - Lesions are painful/burning rather than itchy.
  - Lesions leave residual bruising or hyperpigmentation upon resolution.
  - Associated with systemic symptoms (fever, arthralgia). Requires skin biopsy.

## 7. Management (治療與管理)
### 1. Avoidance of Triggers
- Identify and eliminate any known triggers (foods, drugs).
- Avoid exacerbating factors: NSAIDs, alcohol, tight clothing, extreme heat/stress.

### 2. Pharmacological Therapy (Stepwise Approach)
- **First-line: Second-Generation H1-Antihistamines (非鎮靜抗組織胺)**
  - *Examples*: Cetirizine (Zyrtec), Loratadine (Clarityne), Fexofenadine (Telfast), Bilastine.
  - *Efficacy*: Highly effective for reducing itch and wheals. Preferred over first-generation due to lack of sedation and anticholinergic side effects.
  - *Dosing*: If standard dose is ineffective, guidelines recommend increasing the dose up to **4 times the standard dose** (e.g., Cetirizine 40mg/day) before moving to second-line therapies.
- **Second-line (Add-on therapies)**:
  - *First-generation H1-antihistamines* (e.g., Chlorpheniramine, Hydroxyzine): Can be added at night to help with sleep disrupted by itch (sedating effect).
  - *H2-Antihistamines* (e.g., Famotidine): Sometimes added to H1-antihistamines for synergistic effect, though evidence is weak.
  - *Leukotriene Receptor Antagonists* (e.g., Montelukast): May be useful, especially in NSAID-exacerbated urticaria.
- **Third-line (For refractory Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria)**:
  - **Omalizumab (Xolair)**: Anti-IgE monoclonal antibody. Highly effective for CSU unresponsive to high-dose antihistamines. Administered via subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks.
  - **Cyclosporine**: Systemic immunosuppressant. Used for severe, refractory cases. Requires close monitoring of blood pressure and renal function.
- **Systemic Corticosteroids (e.g., Oral Prednisolone)**:
  - *Role*: Should ONLY be used for short courses (e.g., 3-7 days) to gain control of severe acute exacerbations.
  - **Avoid long-term use** in chronic urticaria due to severe side effects.
