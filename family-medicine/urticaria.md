# Urticaria (Hives)

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Intensely itchy, erythematous wheals that typically resolve within 24 hours without scarring.
**Answer**: 極度痕癢的紅斑性風團，通常在24小時內消退且不留疤痕。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Acute (<6 weeks): Often viral, food allergy, drug allergy. Chronic (>6 weeks): Often idiopathic or autoimmune.
**Answer**: 急性（<6週）：通常是病毒、食物過敏、藥物過敏。慢性（>6週）：通常是特發性或自體免疫性。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Red flag: Angioedema (swelling of lips/tongue) and Anaphylaxis (respiratory distress, hypotension).
**Answer**: 危險信號：血管性水腫（嘴唇/舌頭腫脹）和過敏性休克（呼吸困難、低血壓）。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Treatment: Non-sedating H1 antihistamines (Loratadine, Cetirizine). Up to 4x standard dose in chronic cases.
**Answer**: 治療：非鎮靜H1抗組織胺藥（氯雷他定、西替利嗪）。在慢性病例中可高達標準劑量的4倍。

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: A 30-year-old woman presents with intensely itchy, raised red welts all over her body that appeared suddenly this morning. She ate some prawns last night.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
PHYSICAL (4P):
- Presenting complaint: Sudden onset of itchy, raised red welts (hives).
- Precipitating factors: Ate prawns last night (possible food allergy).
- Pattern: Acute onset.
- Palliation/Provocation: Scratching makes it worse.

BATHE:
- Background: "You woke up with this itchy rash today. How are you feeling right now?"
- Affect: "How is this sudden rash making you feel?"
- Trouble: "What is the most uncomfortable part? The itching or the appearance?"
- Handling: "Have you taken any allergy medicine or applied any creams?"
- Empathy: "It must be very alarming to wake up covered in an itchy rash like this."

ICE:
- Ideas: "Do you think the prawns caused this, or could it be something else?"
- Concerns: "Are you worried about your breathing or swelling in your face?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to stop the itching?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Acute Urticaria (hives), likely secondary to food allergy (prawns). Need to rule out anaphylaxis (airway compromise).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Anxiety and distress due to sudden severe symptoms.

SOCIAL:
- Temporary disruption of daily activities due to severe pruritus.

BARRIERS:
- Identifying the exact trigger can sometimes be difficult if multiple new exposures occurred.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"This is an allergic reaction called hives. Your breathing is fine, which is the most important thing. The rash looks severe, but it usually responds very well to medication."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Avoid eating prawns or any other shellfish until we can confirm if that was the cause."
- "Try not to scratch the rash, as this releases more histamine and makes the itching worse."
- "Take cool showers and wear loose, comfortable clothing to help soothe the skin."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe a non-drowsy antihistamine (e.g., Cetirizine or Loratadine) to take daily. This will block the allergic reaction and stop the itching."
- "I will also give you a soothing cream (e.g., Calamine lotion) to apply to the itchy areas."
- If severe, consider a short course of oral corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone).

REFER:
- Refer to an allergist/immunologist for formal allergy testing if the trigger is unclear or reactions are severe/recurrent.

INVESTIGATE:
- No immediate investigations needed for acute uncomplicated urticaria.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor for signs of anaphylaxis (lip/tongue swelling, wheezing, dizziness).

PLAN:
- Review in a few days if symptoms do not improve with antihistamines.

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the role of antihistamines, then ask: "Just to make sure I explained it clearly, how will this medication help your rash?"

ICE:
Directly address her concern about breathing by explicitly stating that her airway is clear and explaining the warning signs to watch for.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know the itching is driving you crazy. We will get you started on the medication right away to give you some relief."

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about the medication to stop the itching, and then we will discuss what you need to avoid eating for now."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: A 30-year-old woman presents with intensely itchy, raised red welts all over her body that appeared suddenly this morning. She ate some prawns last night.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned waking up with this itchy rash. Can you tell me exactly when it started and how it has changed?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Listen for the rapid onset, the nature of the rash (migratory, raised), and potential triggers.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
ASSESS SYMPTOMS:
- "Are the spots moving around, disappearing in one place and appearing in another?"
- "Did you eat anything unusual or take any new medications recently?"
- "Have you been stung by an insect or used any new soaps or lotions?"

RED FLAGS (Anaphylaxis):
- "Do you feel any swelling in your lips, tongue, or throat?"
- "Are you having any difficulty breathing or swallowing?"
- "Do you feel dizzy, lightheaded, or like you might faint?"
- "Do you have any abdominal pain, nausea, or vomiting?"

PHYSICAL EXAM:
- Assess Airway, Breathing, Circulation (ABC) immediately.
- Inspect the rash: Look for erythematous, edematous plaques (wheals) that blanch with pressure.
- Check lips, tongue, and oropharynx for angioedema.
- Auscultate lungs for wheezing.

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"Do you think the prawns caused this, or could it be something else?"

CONCERNS:
"Are you worried about your breathing or swelling in your face?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What were you hoping we could do today to stop the itching?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"You have acute urticaria, commonly known as hives. It is very likely an allergic reaction to the prawns you ate last night."

EXPLANATION:
"Your immune system mistakenly identified the prawns as harmful and released a chemical called histamine into your skin. This histamine causes the blood vessels to leak fluid, creating the itchy, red swellings."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does this make sense to you?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"Your breathing is normal, which is excellent. The hives are uncomfortable but they respond very quickly to antihistamine medication."

PLAN:
"I will prescribe a strong antihistamine for you to take. This will block the histamine and stop the itching and swelling."

ADVICE:
"Avoid eating prawns or any shellfish in the future. Try not to scratch the rash, and take cool showers to soothe the skin."

SAFETY NET:
"This is crucial: If you suddenly feel your lips or throat swelling, have difficulty breathing, or feel faint, you must call an ambulance or go to the A&E immediately, as this could be a severe allergic reaction."

## Station 4 — Medical Record + Prescription Writing

### Subjective (S)
Ms Lee Sze Man (F/30), presents with a 2-hour history of a generalized, intensely itchy rash. It started 30 minutes after eating a seafood buffet (shrimp, crab). The rash is moving around her body. No shortness of breath, no wheezing. No lip, tongue, or throat swelling. No dizziness or syncope. No previous similar episodes. PMH: Nil. NKDA.

### Objective (O)
Vitals: BP 110/70 mmHg, HR 85 bpm, RR 16/min, SpO2 99% on room air.
Skin: Generalized erythematous, raised, edematous plaques (wheals) of varying sizes on the trunk and limbs. Blanchable. No target lesions.
HEENT: No facial, lip, or tongue angioedema. Uvula midline, airway patent.
Chest: Clear to auscultation bilaterally, no wheeze.

### Assessment (A)
PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS: Acute Urticaria (likely IgE-mediated food allergy to seafood).
Evidence: Acute onset of wheals shortly after eating seafood, intense pruritus.

NOT: Anaphylaxis (no respiratory compromise, no hypotension, no mucosal involvement).

### Plan (P)
1. MEDICATION: Prescribed Cetirizine 10mg daily for 1 week to control symptoms. May add a sedating antihistamine (e.g., Chlorpheniramine) at night if sleep is disturbed by itch.
2. ALLERGEN AVOIDANCE: Advised strict avoidance of all shellfish and seafood until allergy testing can be done.
3. SAFETY NET (CRITICAL): Educated on the signs of anaphylaxis (difficulty breathing, swelling of face/throat, dizziness). Advised to call 999 or go to the nearest A&E immediately if these occur.
4. FOLLOW-UP: Review in 1 week. Consider referral to Immunology/Allergy clinic for formal testing if needed.

### Prescription
- **Drug**: Cetirizine
- **Preparation**: Tablet
- **Dose**: 10 mg
- **Route**: Oral
- **Frequency**: Once daily (OM)
- **Duration**: 7 days
- **Quantity**: 7 tablets

**Tips**:
- Prescribe a non-sedating H1 antihistamine
- Specify once daily dosing
