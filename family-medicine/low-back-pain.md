# Low Back Pain

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: 90% of LBP is non-specific (mechanical); only 1% is serious pathology (cancer, infection, fracture, cauda equina)
**Answer**: 90%下背痛為非特異性（機械性）；只有1%為嚴重病理（癌症、感染、骨折、馬尾綜合征）

**Examiner's Pearl**: RED FLAGS (TUTOR): Trauma, Under 20 or Over 55 at onset, Thoracic pain, cancer Oncology/Osteoporosis/steroids history, Rest pain (night pain), Rigidity (AS), Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss), Cauda equina symptoms (saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel dysfunction)
**Answer**: 紅旗症狀（TUTOR）：創傷、20歲以下或55歲以上發病、胸部疼痛、腫瘤/骨質疏鬆/類固醇史、靜息痛（夜間痛）、僵硬（強直性脊柱炎）、全身症狀（發燒、體重減輕）、馬尾症狀（鞍區麻痺、膀胱/腸道功能障礙）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Cauda equina syndrome is a surgical emergency: saddle anaesthesia + bilateral leg weakness + urinary retention/incontinence → immediate MRI + surgical referral
**Answer**: 馬尾綜合征是外科急症：鞍區麻痺+雙側腿無力+尿瀦留/失禁→立即MRI+外科轉介

**Examiner's Pearl**: Management: Stay active (bed rest worsens outcomes); NSAIDs ± weak opioid for acute; physiotherapy; CBT for chronic; antidepressants (amitriptyline/duloxetine) for neuropathic/chronic
**Answer**: 管理：保持活躍（臥床休息使結果更差）；急性期NSAIDs±弱阿片類；物理治療；慢性期認知行為治療；神經病理性/慢性疼痛用抗抑鬱藥（阿米替林/度洛西汀）

**Examiner's Pearl**: X-ray rarely helpful in acute non-specific LBP; consider if red flags or >6 weeks no improvement; MRI for neurological features
**Answer**: X光對急性非特異性下背痛幾乎無幫助；若有紅旗症狀或6週以上無改善考慮；神經系統症狀需MRI

**Examiner's Pearl**: SLR test: positive if pain radiates below knee at <60° hip flexion (L4/L5/S1 nerve root involvement)
**Answer**: 直腿抬高試驗：若髖部屈曲<60°時疼痛放射到膝以下則陽性（L4/L5/S1神經根受累）

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Lee, 40M, warehouse worker. Complains of lower back pain for 2 weeks after lifting a heavy box. No red flags. He is demanding an MRI and 2 weeks of sick leave.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Occupation involving heavy lifting (warehouse worker), age 40.
- Precipitating: Acute lifting injury 2 weeks ago.
- Perpetuating: Fear of serious injury (demanding MRI), desire for prolonged rest (sick leave), physical demands of job.
- Protective: No red flags (no bowel/bladder dysfunction, no saddle anesthesia, no progressive neurological deficit).

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You mentioned your back has been hurting for two weeks since lifting that box. How is this affecting your daily life?"
- Affect: "How do you feel about this pain not going away yet?"
- Trouble: "What is the most frustrating part about dealing with this back pain?"
- Handling: "How have you been managing the pain at home or at work?"
- Empathy: "It is incredibly frustrating to be in pain, especially when your job requires physical labor."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you think is actually causing the pain in your back?"
- Concerns: "You asked for an MRI. Are you worried that you have a slipped disc or something more serious?"
- Expectations: "You mentioned wanting two weeks of sick leave. What were you hoping that prolonged rest would achieve?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Mechanical lower back pain (likely muscle strain or ligament sprain).
- Must explicitly rule out Cauda Equina Syndrome and fracture.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- High anxiety regarding the severity of the injury.
- Fear-avoidance behavior (believing that movement will cause further damage).

SOCIAL:
- Occupational impact: Inability to perform heavy lifting duties.
- Potential financial stress if sick leave is unpaid or prolonged.

BARRIERS:
- Patient's strong belief that an MRI is necessary for diagnosis.
- Patient's misconception that prolonged bed rest is the best treatment.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I have examined you thoroughly. Your nerves and reflexes are perfectly normal. There are no danger signs. This is a mechanical muscle strain, which is very common and heals well."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Regarding the MRI: Guidelines show that an MRI is not helpful for this type of muscle pain. It often shows normal age-related changes that can cause unnecessary worry. We only do an MRI if there are danger signs, which you do not have."
- "Regarding sick leave: Prolonged bed rest actually makes back pain worse because your muscles become stiff and weak. I will give you 3 days of sick leave to rest the acute pain, but then you need to start moving."
- "Try to stay active with gentle walking. Avoid heavy lifting, but do not stay in bed all day."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe a regular painkiller (Paracetamol) and a stronger anti-inflammatory (NSAID like Naproxen) to take with food. This will reduce the inflammation so you can move comfortably."

REFER:
- Physiotherapy for core strengthening and manual therapy.
- Occupational Therapy for ergonomic advice on safe lifting techniques at work.

INVESTIGATE:
- None required at this stage (no X-ray or MRI needed for simple mechanical back pain without red flags).

OBSERVE:
- Monitor pain levels and return to work status.

PLAN:
- Follow up in 2 weeks if the pain has not improved.

SAFETY NET:
"If you develop numbness around your private parts, lose control of your bowel or bladder, or feel weakness shooting down both legs, go to the Accident and Emergency department immediately."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain why an MRI is not needed, then ask, "Does it make sense why we are holding off on the scan for now?"

ICE:
Directly address his demand for 2 weeks of sick leave by explaining the medical evidence against prolonged bed rest.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know you wanted two weeks off, but how about we start with 3 days, add some strong painkillers, and see how you feel? We can always review it if you are still struggling."

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's discuss what is causing the pain, and then we will talk about the MRI and the sick leave you asked for."
