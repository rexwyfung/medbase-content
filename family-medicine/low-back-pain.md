# Low Back Pain (下背痛)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: Pain, muscle tension, or stiffness localized below the costal margin and above the inferior gluteal folds, with or without leg pain (sciatica).
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common; lifetime prevalence is up to 80%. A leading cause of disability and work absenteeism globally. Most cases are acute and self-limiting, but recurrence is common.

## 2. Classification & Etiology (分類與病因)
Low back pain is classified into three main categories based on the underlying cause:
1. **Non-specific Low Back Pain (85-90%)**: No specific underlying pathology (e.g., infection, tumor, fracture, structural deformity) can be identified. Often termed "mechanical" back pain (muscle strain, ligament sprain).
2. **Radicular Syndrome (Sciatica) (5-10%)**: Pain radiating down the leg, often accompanied by numbness, tingling, or weakness, typically due to nerve root compression (e.g., herniated disc, spinal stenosis).
3. **Specific Spinal Pathology (<1-2%)**: Serious underlying conditions requiring urgent intervention (Red Flags).

## 3. Clinical Evaluation & Red Flags (臨床評估與紅旗症狀)
The primary goal of the initial assessment is a "triage" to rule out specific, serious spinal pathologies.
- **History**: Onset, duration, character, radiation, exacerbating/relieving factors (e.g., worse with flexion suggests disc; worse with extension/walking suggests stenosis).
- **Red Flags (TUNA FISH - 必須轉介或進一步檢查)**:
  - **T**rauma (major trauma, or minor trauma in elderly/osteoporotic).
  - **U**nexplained weight loss.
  - **N**eurological symptoms (Cauda Equina Syndrome - see below).
  - **A**ge > 50 or < 20 (new onset).
  - **F**ever.
  - **I**V drug use.
  - **S**teroid use (chronic, leading to osteoporosis/fracture).
  - **H**istory of cancer (especially prostate, breast, kidney, thyroid, lung - PB KTL).
- **Cauda Equina Syndrome (馬尾神經綜合症) - SURGICAL EMERGENCY**:
  - Saddle anesthesia (numbness in the perineal/buttock region).
  - Recent onset of bladder dysfunction (urinary retention or incontinence).
  - Bowel incontinence.
  - Severe or progressive bilateral neurological deficit in the legs.
- **Physical Examination**:
  - Inspection (posture, deformity like scoliosis/kyphosis).
  - Palpation (spinous process tenderness suggests fracture/infection; paraspinal muscle spasm).
  - Range of motion (flexion, extension, lateral flexion).
  - Neurological exam of lower limbs: Tone, power (L2-S1 myotomes), reflexes (knee - L3/L4, ankle - S1/S2), sensation (dermatomes).
  - Straight Leg Raise (SLR) test: Positive if pain radiates below the knee between 30-70 degrees (suggests L4-S1 nerve root irritation).

## 4. Investigations (檢查)
- **Imaging (X-ray, MRI, CT)**: **NOT routinely indicated** for acute, non-specific low back pain. Imaging correlates poorly with symptoms (many asymptomatic people have disc bulges).
- **Indications for Imaging**:
  - Presence of Red Flags.
  - Severe or progressive neurological deficits.
  - Suspected Cauda Equina Syndrome (Urgent MRI).
  - Symptoms persisting > 4-6 weeks despite conservative management (to rule out specific pathology or plan surgery/injections).
- **Blood tests**: CBC, ESR/CRP if infection (discitis, osteomyelitis) or inflammatory arthritis (ankylosing spondylitis) is suspected.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
### Acute Non-specific Low Back Pain (< 6 weeks)
- **Reassurance**: Emphasize that it is common, usually self-limiting, and not a sign of serious disease.
- **Activity**: **Avoid bed rest**. Encourage staying active and continuing normal daily activities as tolerated. Bed rest delays recovery.
- **Pharmacotherapy**:
  - First-line: NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen) at the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible time. Add a PPI if GI risk is high.
  - Second-line: Paracetamol (alone is no longer considered effective for acute LBP, but can be used if NSAIDs are contraindicated).
  - Muscle relaxants (e.g., Diazepam, Baclofen) for short-term use (few days) if severe muscle spasm is present.
  - *Note*: Opioids are generally discouraged due to addiction risk and lack of long-term efficacy.
- **Physical Therapy**: Heat therapy may provide short-term relief.

### Chronic Non-specific Low Back Pain (> 12 weeks)
- **Exercise Therapy**: Structured exercise programs (aerobic, strengthening, stretching, yoga, Pilates) are highly effective.
- **Psychological Therapy**: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) to address fear-avoidance beliefs and improve coping strategies.
- **Pharmacotherapy**: NSAIDs. Consider Duloxetine (SNRI) for chronic pain.
- **Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation**: Combining physical and psychological interventions.

### Radicular Pain (Sciatica)
- Most cases resolve spontaneously within weeks to months.
- Initial management is similar to non-specific LBP (stay active, NSAIDs).
- Neuropathic pain agents (e.g., Pregabalin, Gabapentin, Amitriptyline) may be considered, though evidence is mixed.
- Epidural steroid injections for short-term relief of severe radicular pain.
- Surgical referral (microdiscectomy) if pain is severe, unremitting, or accompanied by progressive neurological deficit.
