# Diabetes Mellitus Type 2

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Diagnosis: HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (≥6.5%), OR fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, OR 2h OGTT glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L (on 2 occasions if asymptomatic)
**Answer**: 診斷：HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol（≥6.5%），或空腹血糖≥7.0 mmol/L，或OGTT 2小時血糖≥11.1 mmol/L（無症狀者需兩次確認）

**Examiner's Pearl**: HbA1c target: 48-53 mmol/mol (6.5-7.0%) for most; 58-64 mmol/mol (7.5-8.0%) for elderly/at-risk of hypoglycaemia
**Answer**: HbA1c目標：大多數48-53 mmol/mol（6.5-7.0%）；老年人/低血糖風險者58-64 mmol/mol（7.5-8.0%）

**Examiner's Pearl**: 1st line: Metformin 500mg BD (titrate up); SGLT2i if CVD/CKD/HF; GLP-1 RA if obese
**Answer**: 一線用藥：二甲雙胍500mg每日兩次（逐步增量）；若有心血管病/慢性腎病/心衰則用SGLT2i；若肥胖則用GLP-1受體激動劑

**Examiner's Pearl**: ANNUAL review: HbA1c, BP, cholesterol, eGFR, urine ACR, feet exam, fundoscopy, weight
**Answer**: 年度複查：HbA1c、血壓、膽固醇、eGFR、尿ACR、足部檢查、眼底鏡、體重

**Examiner's Pearl**: Complications: Nephropathy (ACR ≥3 mg/mmol), Retinopathy, Neuropathy, Macrovascular (CVD, PAD)
**Answer**: 並發症：腎病（ACR ≥3 mg/mmol）、視網膜病變、神經病變、大血管病（心血管病、周圍動脈病）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Sick day rules: NEVER stop metformin/SGLT2i during acute illness without medical advice (SGLT2i → DKA risk)
**Answer**: 患病日規則：急性病期間切勿自行停用二甲雙胍/SGLT2i（SGLT2i→酮症酸中毒風險）

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Wong, 60M, newly diagnosed Type 2 Diabetes (HbA1c 8.5%). He loves eating rice and dim sum. He is afraid of starting insulin and thinks diabetes means he can never eat sweets again.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Age 60, Asian ethnicity, strong family history of type 2 diabetes.
- Precipitating: High carbohydrate diet (rice, dim sum), sedentary lifestyle, recent weight gain.
- Perpetuating: Misconceptions about diet ("never eat sweets again"), fear of insulin injections, lack of nutritional knowledge.
- Protective: Attends clinic, willing to discuss, has family support.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "Your recent blood test showed your HbA1c is 8.5%, which means your blood sugar is high. How have you been feeling lately?"
- Affect: "How do you feel about this new diagnosis of diabetes?"
- Trouble: "What is the most difficult part of hearing this news for you?"
- Handling: "How are you coping with the idea of having to change your diet?"
- Empathy: "It is completely understandable to feel overwhelmed. Diabetes is a big word, but we will manage it together step by step."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you understand about what diabetes actually is?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned being afraid of insulin. Are you worried you have to start injections today?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could focus on today regarding your treatment?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Poorly controlled Type 2 Diabetes (HbA1c 8.5%).
- Need to screen for microvascular (retinopathy, neuropathy, nephropathy) and macrovascular (IHD, stroke, PAD) complications.
- Potential obesity or metabolic syndrome.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Fear of needles and insulin therapy.
- Anxiety about extreme dietary restrictions and loss of quality of life.

SOCIAL:
- Dietary habits deeply ingrained in culture (dim sum, white rice).
- Potential lack of time for exercise.

BARRIERS:
- Misinformation about diabetes management (all-or-nothing thinking regarding sugar).

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"You do not need insulin today. Most people start with tablets. And you can still enjoy your food, just in moderation and with smarter choices."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Diabetes means your body cannot process sugar well. It is like a car engine getting clogged. The sugar stays in your blood instead of giving you energy."
- "You do not have to stop eating rice completely. Try replacing white rice with brown or red rice. Eat a large portion of vegetables first before eating your rice and meat."
- "For dim sum, avoid the fatty meats and sweet buns. Choose steamed items like Siu Mai or vegetables, and avoid drinking the sauce."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I suggest starting a medication called Metformin 500mg daily. It helps your body use its own insulin better."
- "It might cause some mild stomach upset or diarrhea initially, which is why you MUST take it with or immediately after meals."

REFER:
- RAMC (Risk Assessment and Management Programme) for comprehensive complication screening (eye photo, foot check).
- Dietitian for personalized meal planning.

INVESTIGATE:
- Check renal function, lipid profile, and urine ACR (for early kidney damage).

OBSERVE:
- Monitor fasting glucose at home if he is willing to buy a glucometer.

PLAN:
- Follow up in 4 weeks to check tolerance to Metformin and review blood tests.

SAFETY NET:
"If you feel very dizzy, sweating, shaking, and hungry, these are signs of low blood sugar. Drink half a cup of juice or eat 3 sweets immediately, and tell your family."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the diet changes, then ask, "Just to make sure I was clear, what is one change you can try at your next dim sum visit?"

ICE:
Directly address his fear of insulin and reassure him it is not the first step, but explain that diabetes is progressive.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"What is one small change in your diet you feel ready to make this week?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"Let's talk about your diet first, and then we will discuss the medication I want to prescribe."
