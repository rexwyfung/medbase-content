# Osteoarthritis — Knee

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Clinical diagnosis: age >45, activity-related pain, brief (<30 min) morning stiffness, no systemic features — X-ray not needed routinely
**Answer**: 臨床診斷：年齡>45歲，活動相關疼痛，晨僵時間短（<30分鐘），無全身性症狀——常規不需要X光

**Examiner's Pearl**: NICE core treatments (ALL patients): exercise (aerobic + strengthening), weight loss if overweight, topical NSAIDs
**Answer**: NICE核心治療（所有患者）：運動（有氧+肌肉強化）、超重者減重、局部NSAIDs

**Examiner's Pearl**: Paracetamol: no longer first-line for OA (NICE 2022); limited efficacy proven in large RCTs
**Answer**: 撲熱息痛：不再是OA一線治療（NICE 2022）；大型RCT中療效有限

**Examiner's Pearl**: Intra-articular corticosteroid: for moderate-severe acute flare; short-term benefit 4–8 weeks; no more than 3–4 per joint per year
**Answer**: 關節內皮質類固醇注射：用於中重度急性發作；短期效益4–8週；每個關節每年不超過3–4次

**Examiner's Pearl**: Total knee replacement (TKR): when adequate trial of conservative + pharmacological fails, severe impact on QoL; refer orthopaedics
**Answer**: 全膝關節置換術（TKR）：保守和藥物治療充分嘗試後無效、嚴重影響生活質素時；轉介骨科

**Examiner's Pearl**: Beware NSAIDs in elderly: GI bleed (add PPI), renal impairment, cardiovascular risk, hypertension worsening
**Answer**: 老年患者NSAIDs注意事項：胃腸道出血（加PPI）、腎功能損害、心血管風險、高血壓加重

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Madam Wong, 65F, retired restaurant worker. Presents with bilateral knee pain for 3 years, worse on the right. Pain is worse after walking or climbing stairs, and relieved by rest. She has morning stiffness lasting 15 minutes. BMI is 32. She is asking for knee replacement surgery because her friend had it.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Age 65, female, obesity (BMI 32), previous occupational wear and tear (restaurant worker).
- Precipitating: Gradual cartilage degeneration over years.
- Perpetuating: Excess body weight placing mechanical stress on knees, lack of quadriceps strengthening.
- Protective: Pain relieved by rest, short morning stiffness (<30 mins rules out Rheumatoid Arthritis).

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You mentioned your knees have been hurting for 3 years, especially when walking or doing stairs. How is this affecting your daily life and mobility?"
- Affect: "How do you feel when the pain stops you from doing things you want to do?"
- Trouble: "What is the most difficult part of dealing with this knee pain every day?"
- Handling: "How have you been managing the pain so far? Have you tried any painkillers or walking aids?"
- Empathy: "It is incredibly frustrating to have your mobility limited by pain. I can understand why you are looking for a permanent solution like surgery."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you think has caused the cartilage in your knees to wear down?"
- Concerns: "Are you worried that you will eventually end up in a wheelchair if we don't do surgery?"
- Expectations: "You mentioned wanting a knee replacement like your friend. What were you hoping the surgery would achieve for you right now?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Osteoarthritis (OA) of the knees.
- Obesity (BMI 32) significantly exacerbating the mechanical load.
- Must rule out inflammatory arthritis (e.g., Rheumatoid Arthritis - morning stiffness is short, so unlikely).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Frustration with loss of mobility and independence.

SOCIAL:
- Impact on daily activities (grocery shopping, climbing stairs).

BARRIERS:
- Misconception that surgery is the first or only option.
- Difficulty losing weight due to pain limiting exercise.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I have examined your knees. You have Osteoarthritis, which is 'wear and tear' of the cartilage. It is very common. The good news is there is no severe inflammation like Rheumatoid Arthritis, and there are many things we can do before considering surgery."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Regarding surgery: A knee replacement is a major operation with risks. Orthopedic surgeons usually require patients to try weight loss, physiotherapy, and medications first. Surgery is the last resort when nothing else works."
- "Weight loss is the most powerful treatment. Every 1 pound of weight you lose takes 4 pounds of pressure off your knees with every step. Even a small amount of weight loss will significantly reduce your pain."
- "You need to strengthen the thigh muscles (quadriceps) above the knee. Strong muscles act like shock absorbers, taking the pressure off the worn-out cartilage."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe a regular painkiller (Paracetamol) to take every day, and an anti-inflammatory gel (NSAID gel) to rub directly on the knee when it is very painful. This avoids the stomach side effects of oral pills."

REFER:
- Physiotherapy for quadriceps strengthening exercises.
- Dietitian for a structured weight loss plan.

INVESTIGATE:
- X-ray of the knees (weight-bearing) to assess the degree of joint space narrowing and confirm the baseline severity.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor pain levels, mobility, and weight.

PLAN:
- Follow up in 3 months to review weight loss progress and physiotherapy results. If pain is still unmanageable, we can discuss a referral to Orthopedics.

SAFETY NET:
"If your knee suddenly becomes red, hot, swollen, and extremely painful, or if you get a fever, go to the A&E immediately as it could be an infection in the joint."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the "1 pound lost = 4 pounds less pressure" concept, then ask, "Does it make sense why losing even a little bit of weight is actually more effective than surgery right now?"

ICE:
Directly address her request for surgery by validating it as a future option, but clearly explaining the medical protocol of trying conservative management first.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know it is hard to exercise when you are in pain. Are you willing to see the physiotherapist to learn exercises you can do while sitting down to protect your knees?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about why surgery isn't the first step, and then we will discuss the three things we can do today to reduce your pain."
