# Osteoarthritis of the Knee (膝關節炎 / 退化性關節炎)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A chronic, degenerative joint disease characterized by the progressive loss of articular cartilage, subchondral bone thickening (sclerosis), formation of osteophytes (bone spurs), and low-grade synovial inflammation. It represents a failure of joint repair.
- **Epidemiology**: The most common form of arthritis worldwide. Prevalence increases strongly with age. A leading cause of chronic pain and mobility disability in the elderly. More common in women after age 50.

## 2. Risk Factors & Pathophysiology (危險因素與病理生理學)
- **Risk Factors**:
  - **Age**: The strongest predictor.
  - **Obesity**: Increases mechanical stress on weight-bearing joints (knees, hips) and systemic low-grade inflammation (adipokines).
  - **Joint Trauma/Injury**: Previous meniscal tears, ligament ruptures (e.g., ACL tear), or intra-articular fractures significantly increase the risk of secondary OA later in life.
  - **Occupational/Repetitive Stress**: Jobs requiring frequent kneeling, squatting, or heavy lifting.
  - **Genetics**: Family history plays a role.
  - **Female Gender**.
- **Pathophysiology**: Not merely "wear and tear." It is an active process involving an imbalance between cartilage degradation (mediated by matrix metalloproteinases - MMPs) and synthesis. Chondrocytes undergo apoptosis. The subchondral bone becomes stiff and sclerotic. Osteophytes form at the joint margins in an attempt to stabilize the joint.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Pain**: The hallmark symptom.
  - Typically **worse with weight-bearing and activity** (walking, climbing stairs, standing from a seated position).
  - **Relieved by rest**.
  - In advanced stages, pain may occur at rest or at night, disrupting sleep.
- **Stiffness**:
  - **Morning stiffness**: Typically brief, lasting **< 30 minutes** (unlike Rheumatoid Arthritis, which lasts > 1 hour).
  - "Gelling" phenomenon: Stiffness after periods of inactivity (e.g., sitting for a movie), which improves after a few minutes of movement.
- **Physical Examination Signs**:
  - **Crepitus**: A palpable or audible grating/crunching sensation during joint movement.
  - **Reduced Range of Motion (ROM)**: Loss of full extension or flexion.
  - **Bony Enlargement**: Due to osteophyte formation (feels hard, unlike the soft swelling of an effusion).
  - **Joint Line Tenderness**.
  - **Deformity**: Varus (bow-legged - most common in knees due to medial compartment OA) or Valgus (knock-kneed) deformity in advanced disease.
  - **Mild Effusion**: Cool, non-inflammatory fluid (unlike the hot, red, swollen joint of gout or septic arthritis).

## 4. Diagnosis & Investigations (診斷與檢查)
Diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on typical history and examination findings in an older adult.
- **X-ray (Weight-bearing AP, Lateral, and Skyline views)**: The primary imaging modality.
  - **Radiographic Hallmarks of OA (LOSS)**:
    1. **L**oss of joint space (typically asymmetric, e.g., medial compartment narrowing in the knee).
    2. **O**steophytes (bone spurs at joint margins).
    3. **S**ubchondral sclerosis (increased bone density below the cartilage).
    4. **S**ubchondral cysts (geodes).
  - *Note*: Radiographic severity often correlates poorly with clinical symptoms. Treat the patient, not the X-ray.
- **Blood Tests**: Usually normal (ESR, CRP, Rheumatoid Factor are normal). Done only to rule out inflammatory arthritis if suspected.
- **Joint Aspiration (Arthrocentesis)**: Indicated if there is a large, acute effusion to rule out crystal arthropathy (gout/pseudogout) or septic arthritis. OA fluid is clear, viscous, with low WBC count (< 2000 cells/µL).

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
Management requires a multimodal approach, starting with conservative measures.

### 1. Core Non-Pharmacological Treatments (First-line for ALL patients)
- **Patient Education**: Explain the condition, manage expectations (it is chronic, but symptoms can be managed).
- **Weight Loss**: Crucial for overweight/obese patients. Even a 5-10% weight reduction significantly reduces pain and slows progression.
- **Exercise Therapy**:
  - **Strengthening**: Particularly the quadriceps and hamstrings, to improve joint stability and absorb shock.
  - **Low-impact Aerobic Exercise**: Swimming, cycling, walking. Avoid high-impact activities (running, jumping).
- **Physical Therapy**: Gait training, use of assistive devices (cane held in the *contralateral* hand to reduce joint loading).
- **Bracing/Taping**: Unloader braces for unicompartmental knee OA.

### 2. Pharmacological Therapy (Symptom Relief)
- **Topical NSAIDs** (e.g., Diclofenac gel): Recommended as first-line pharmacological therapy for knee OA. Effective with minimal systemic side effects.
- **Oral NSAIDs** (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Celecoxib): More effective than paracetamol. Use at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.
  - *Caution*: High risk of GI bleeding (co-prescribe PPI), cardiovascular events, and renal impairment, especially in the elderly. Consider COX-2 inhibitors (Celecoxib) if high GI risk but low CV risk.
- **Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)**: Historically first-line, but recent evidence shows minimal efficacy for OA pain. May be used for mild pain or if NSAIDs are contraindicated.
- **Intra-articular Injections**:
  - **Corticosteroids**: Provide significant short-term relief (1-4 weeks) for acute flares. Do not alter disease progression. Limit to 3-4 times per year per joint.
  - **Hyaluronic Acid (Viscosupplementation)**: Controversial efficacy; may provide moderate relief for some patients over several months.
- **Opioids**: Generally discouraged due to adverse effects and addiction risk. Weak opioids (e.g., Tramadol) may be considered as a last resort for severe pain if surgery is not an option.

### 3. Surgical Treatment
- **Total Joint Replacement (Arthroplasty - 全膝關節置換術)**: Highly effective for relieving pain and restoring function in patients with severe, end-stage OA who have failed conservative management and whose quality of life is significantly impaired.
- *Note*: Arthroscopic lavage and debridement are NO LONGER recommended for knee OA, as they do not provide benefit over placebo.

## 6. Complications (併發症)
- Chronic, debilitating pain leading to depression and sleep disturbance.
- Severe mobility limitation, increasing the risk of falls, weight gain, and cardiovascular disease due to inactivity.
