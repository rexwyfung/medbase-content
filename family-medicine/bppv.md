# Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) (良性陣發性位置性眩暈 / 耳石症)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A biomechanical disorder of the inner ear characterized by brief episodes of mild to intense vertigo triggered by specific changes in the position of the head.
- **Epidemiology**: The most common cause of peripheral vertigo. Lifetime prevalence is ~2.4%. More common in women and older adults (peak onset between 50-70 years).

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- **Mechanism**: Otoconia (calcium carbonate crystals, "耳石") detach from the utricle (where they normally reside to sense linear acceleration) and migrate into one of the semicircular canals (which sense angular acceleration).
- **Canalithiasis (Most common)**: Free-floating otoconia in the canal fluid (endolymph). When the head moves, gravity causes the otoconia to move, creating abnormal fluid flow that deflects the cupula, sending false signals of rotation to the brain.
- **Cupulolithiasis (Less common)**: Otoconia adhere directly to the cupula, making it sensitive to gravity.
- **Affected Canals**:
  - **Posterior Canal (85-95%)**: Most gravity-dependent when lying down.
  - Horizontal Canal (5-15%).
  - Anterior Canal (rare, <1%).

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Vertigo (眩暈)**: A spinning sensation (the room is spinning or the patient feels they are spinning).
- **Triggers**: Provoked by specific head movements:
  - Rolling over in bed.
  - Lying down or sitting up from bed.
  - Looking up (e.g., reaching for a high shelf).
  - Bending forward (e.g., tying shoes).
- **Duration**: Brief. Episodes typically last **less than 1 minute** (usually 10-30 seconds).
- **Associated Symptoms**: Nausea, vomiting (if severe), imbalance/unsteadiness (which can persist for hours/days after the vertigo stops).
- **Absence of other neurological or otological symptoms**: NO hearing loss, NO tinnitus, NO aural fullness (these suggest Meniere's disease). NO focal neurological signs (e.g., diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, weakness - these suggest central causes like stroke).

## 4. Diagnosis & Assessment (診斷與評估)
Diagnosis is confirmed by observing characteristic nystagmus during provocative positioning maneuvers.

### Dix-Hallpike Maneuver (For Posterior Canal BPPV)
- **Procedure**: Patient sits upright. Turn head 45° to one side. Rapidly lay the patient back with the head hanging 20° over the edge of the bed. Observe the eyes for at least 30 seconds. Repeat on the other side.
- **Positive Test (Classic features of Posterior Canal BPPV nystagmus)**:
  1. **Latency**: A delay of 2-20 seconds before nystagmus starts.
  2. **Direction**: Upbeating and torsional (rotary) nystagmus, beating toward the downward (affected) ear.
  3. **Duration**: Lasts < 1 minute (usually 10-30s).
  4. **Fatigability**: The nystagmus weakens with repeated testing.
  5. **Reversal**: When the patient sits back up, the nystagmus may briefly reverse direction.

### Supine Roll Test (For Horizontal Canal BPPV)
- Performed if Dix-Hallpike is negative but history is highly suggestive of BPPV. Patient lies supine, head flexed 30°, then head is turned quickly 90° to one side, then the other. Produces purely horizontal nystagmus.

### Red Flags (Central Vertigo - requires urgent neuroimaging/referral)
- Nystagmus that is purely vertical (downbeating or upbeating without torsion) or purely torsional.
- Nystagmus that does not fatigue or lacks latency.
- Presence of focal neurological signs (the "5 Ds": Dizziness + Diplopia, Dysarthria, Dysphagia, Dysmetria, Drop attacks).
- Severe headache or neck pain.
- Inability to stand or walk even with eyes open.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
BPPV is treated mechanically, not pharmacologically.

### Canalith Repositioning Maneuvers (First-line)
These maneuvers use gravity to guide the otoconia out of the semicircular canal back into the utricle.
- **Epley Maneuver (耳石復位法)**: The standard treatment for Posterior Canal BPPV. Highly effective (~80% success rate with a single maneuver).
  1. Start in the positive Dix-Hallpike position (head turned 45° to affected side, hanging back). Hold until nystagmus stops + 30 seconds.
  2. Turn head 90° to the opposite side (still extended). Hold for 30 seconds.
  3. Patient rolls onto their shoulder (in the direction the head is facing), turning head another 90° so they are looking at the floor. Hold for 30 seconds.
  4. Patient sits up sideways.
- **Semont Maneuver**: An alternative to Epley. Involves rapid movement from lying on one side to lying on the other.
- **Lempert (BBQ Roll) Maneuver**: Used for Horizontal Canal BPPV.

### Pharmacological Therapy
- **Vestibular Suppressants** (e.g., Prochlorperazine, Promethazine, Cinnarizine, Betahistine, Diazepam):
  - **NOT recommended for routine treatment of BPPV**. They do not cure the condition and can delay central vestibular compensation.
  - May be used briefly (1-2 days) ONLY if nausea/vomiting is severe, or given 30 minutes *before* performing a repositioning maneuver in highly anxious or highly symptomatic patients.

### Patient Education & Follow-up
- Reassure the patient that the condition is benign and highly treatable.
- Post-maneuver restrictions (e.g., sleeping upright for 48 hours) are traditionally advised but recent evidence suggests they may not be strictly necessary.
- Warn about recurrence (occurs in ~15-30% of patients within a year). Teach them to return if symptoms recur, or teach them self-Epley maneuvers.
- Assess fall risk, especially in the elderly.
