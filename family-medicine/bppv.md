# Dizziness / BPPV

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: BPPV: most common cause of vertigo; brief episodes (<1 min) triggered by head position change; posterior canal (90%)
**Answer**: BPPV：最常見的眩暈原因；頭部位置改變觸發短暫發作（<1分鐘）；後半規管（90%）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Dix-Hallpike test: diagnostic for posterior canal BPPV — rotatory nystagmus toward affected ear, latency, fatigable
**Answer**: Dix-Hallpike試驗：診斷後半規管BPPV——旋轉性眼球震顫朝向患側耳，有潛伏期，可疲勞

**Examiner's Pearl**: Epley manoeuvre: single session cures >80% of posterior canal BPPV; can teach self-Semont or modified Epley
**Answer**: Epley復位術：單次治療>80%後半規管BPPV；可教授自我Semont或改良Epley

**Examiner's Pearl**: Central causes (require urgent referral): sudden onset, no latency, non-fatigable, direction-changing nystagmus, other neuro signs
**Answer**: 中樞性原因（需緊急轉介）：突然發作，無潛伏期，不可疲勞，方向改變性眼球震顫，其他神經系統體征

**Examiner's Pearl**: HINTS exam (Head Impulse, Nystagmus, Test of Skew): to distinguish central vs peripheral vertigo in acute setting
**Answer**: HINTS檢查（甩頭試驗、眼球震顫、眼偏斜試驗）：在急性情況下區分中樞性與周圍性眩暈

**Examiner's Pearl**: Vestibular neuritis: continuous vertigo days-weeks after viral illness; no hearing loss; managed with short-term vestibular sedatives + vestibular rehab
**Answer**: 前庭神經炎：病毒性疾病後持續眩暈數天至數週；無聽力損失；短期前庭鎮靜劑+前庭康復治療

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Madam Lee, 60F. Presents with sudden, severe spinning dizziness (vertigo) lasting for 30 seconds every time she rolls over in bed or looks up. Accompanied by nausea but no vomiting. No hearing loss or tinnitus. She is terrified she is having a stroke.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Age 60, female (BPPV is more common in older adults).
- Precipitating: Specific head movements (rolling over in bed, looking up).
- Perpetuating: Fear of moving the head, leading to stiff neck and anxiety.
- Protective: Short duration of attacks (<1 min), no neurological red flags (no weakness, no speech difficulty), no hearing loss.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You mentioned getting this severe spinning feeling when you roll over in bed. How is this affecting your sleep and your ability to get around during the day?"
- Affect: "How do you feel when the room suddenly starts spinning like that?"
- Trouble: "What is the most frightening part of these dizzy spells?"
- Handling: "How have you been trying to cope with this? Have you been avoiding moving your head?"
- Empathy: "Vertigo is an absolutely terrifying sensation. It makes you feel completely out of control, and it is very understandable that you are worried about a stroke."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you think is causing the room to spin when you move your head?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned being terrified of a stroke. Are you worried that this dizziness is a sign of brain damage?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to stop the spinning and give you peace of mind?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV).
- Must explicitly rule out Stroke/TIA (no focal neurological deficits) and Meniere's disease (no hearing loss/tinnitus).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Severe anxiety and fear of stroke.

SOCIAL:
- Fall risk, especially in an older adult.
- Inability to perform daily activities safely (e.g., cooking, crossing the road).

BARRIERS:
- Reluctance to perform the Dix-Hallpike or Epley maneuver due to fear of triggering the severe dizziness.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I have examined your nervous system and it is completely normal. You are NOT having a stroke. Your condition is called BPPV, which is a problem in your inner ear, not your brain. It is benign, meaning it is not dangerous."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Inside your inner ear, there are tiny crystals that help you balance. In BPPV, some of these crystals have broken loose and floated into the wrong canal. When you move your head, the crystals roll around, sending confusing signals to your brain, which makes you feel like the room is spinning."
- "The spinning only lasts for a few seconds until the crystals settle down again."
- "To fix this, I can perform a series of head movements called the Epley Maneuver. It is like a puzzle game to roll the crystals out of the canal and back to where they belong."

PRESCRIBE:
- "Medications like anti-dizziness pills (e.g., Betahistine or Prochlorperazine) don't actually fix the loose crystals, but I can prescribe a short course to help with the nausea while we wait for the crystals to settle."

REFER:
- Physiotherapy or ENT for vestibular rehabilitation if the Epley maneuver in the clinic is unsuccessful or if symptoms persist.

INVESTIGATE:
- Perform the Dix-Hallpike maneuver in the clinic to confirm the diagnosis (look for nystagmus). No brain scan is needed.

OBSERVE:
- Observe for nystagmus (eye flicking) during the Dix-Hallpike test.

PLAN:
- Perform the Epley maneuver today. Follow up in 1 week to see if the dizziness has resolved.

SAFETY NET:
"If you develop a severe headache, double vision, difficulty speaking, or weakness in your arms or legs, go to the A&E immediately, as those are actual signs of a stroke."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the "loose crystals in the ear" analogy, then ask, "Does it make sense why moving your head in certain ways triggers the spinning?"

ICE:
Directly address her fear of a stroke by explicitly explaining the difference between an inner ear mechanical problem and a brain blood flow problem.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"The Epley maneuver will make you feel dizzy for a few seconds while we do it. Are you comfortable with me guiding your head through these movements today to try and fix the problem?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about why we know this isn't a stroke, and then we will discuss the physical maneuver we can do right now to put the crystals back in place."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: Patient presents with sudden, severe spinning dizziness that lasts for a few seconds every time she rolls over in bed or looks up. She is terrified of having a stroke.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned having severe spinning dizziness. Can you tell me exactly what happens when you get an attack?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Allow the patient to describe the vertigo episodes without interrupting.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
ASSESS VERTIGO SYMPTOMS:
- "Does the room feel like it is spinning around you?"
- "How long does the spinning last? Is it seconds, minutes, or hours?" (BPPV is usually <1 minute).
- "Does it only happen when you move your head in a certain way, like rolling over in bed?"
- "Do you feel nauseous or vomit during the spinning?"

SCREEN FOR RED FLAGS (STROKE / TIA - MUST ASK):
- "Have you had any weakness or numbness in your face, arms, or legs?"
- "Have you had any difficulty speaking or swallowing?"
- "Have you had any double vision or difficulty walking?"

SCREEN FOR OTHER EAR PATHOLOGY (MENIERE'S / INFECTION):
- "Have you noticed any hearing loss or ringing in your ears (tinnitus)?"
- "Have you had a recent cold or ear infection?"

PHYSICAL EXAM:
- Perform a basic neurological exam (cranial nerves, cerebellar tests like finger-to-nose, gait).
- Examine the ears with an otoscope.
- Perform the Dix-Hallpike maneuver to confirm BPPV (warn patient it will make them dizzy).

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"What do you think is causing this spinning sensation?"

CONCERNS:
"You mentioned being terrified of a stroke. Are there any other serious conditions you are worried about?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What were you hoping we could do to stop the dizziness today?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"Based on your symptoms and the normal neurological exam, you have a condition called BPPV. It is a problem in your inner ear, not your brain."

EXPLANATION:
"Inside your ear, there are tiny balance crystals. Some have broken loose and floated into the wrong tube. When you move your head, they roll around and send false signals to your brain, making you feel like you are spinning."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does that explanation make sense to you?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"You are definitely not having a stroke. This is a benign, mechanical problem in the ear, and it is very treatable."

INVESTIGATE:
"We do not need to do a brain scan today because your symptoms are classic for BPPV and your nerve exam is normal."

PLAN:
"I can perform a series of head movements right now called the Epley Maneuver. It rolls the crystals back to where they belong. I will also prescribe some anti-sickness tablets."

ADVICE:
"After the maneuver, try to sleep with your head propped up on two pillows for a few nights, and avoid sudden head movements."

SAFETY NET:
"If you develop a severe headache, weakness on one side, or difficulty speaking, go to the A&E immediately as those are signs of a stroke."
