# Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH) (良性前列腺增生)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A non-malignant enlargement of the prostate gland due to cellular hyperplasia of both glandular and stromal elements in the transition zone (the area surrounding the urethra). This enlargement can cause Bladder Outlet Obstruction (BOO) and Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms (LUTS).
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common in aging men. Prevalence increases significantly after age 50. By age 80, up to 80-90% of men have histological evidence of BPH.

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- **Hormonal Influence**: The development of BPH is dependent on androgens. Testosterone is converted to **Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)** by the enzyme **5-alpha-reductase** within the prostate. DHT is the primary mediator of prostatic growth.
- **Mechanism of Symptoms**:
  - **Static Component**: Physical enlargement of the prostate directly compressing the prostatic urethra.
  - **Dynamic Component**: Increased smooth muscle tone in the prostate stroma and bladder neck, mediated by alpha-1 adrenergic receptors, causing functional obstruction.
  - **Detrusor Changes**: Chronic obstruction leads to detrusor muscle hypertrophy, instability (overactivity), and eventually decompensation (underactivity and retention).

## 3. Clinical Features (LUTS - 下尿路症狀)
Symptoms are broadly divided into voiding (obstructive) and storage (irritative) symptoms.
- **Voiding Symptoms (Obstructive)**:
  - Hesitancy (difficulty initiating the urine stream).
  - Weak or poor stream.
  - Intermittency (stream stops and starts).
  - Straining to void.
  - Terminal dribbling.
  - Sensation of incomplete emptying.
- **Storage Symptoms (Irritative)**: Often more bothersome to the patient.
  - Frequency (needing to urinate often during the day).
  - Nocturia (waking up at night to urinate).
  - Urgency (sudden, compelling urge to urinate).
  - Urge incontinence.

## 4. Assessment & Investigations (評估與檢查)
- **History & Symptom Scoring**:
  - Use the **International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS)** to quantify severity (0-7 Mild, 8-19 Moderate, 20-35 Severe) and assess quality of life.
  - Ask about medications (anticholinergics, sympathomimetics can worsen symptoms).
- **Physical Examination**:
  - Abdominal exam: Check for a palpable, distended bladder (chronic retention).
  - **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE - 肛門指檢)**: Essential to assess prostate size, consistency, and symmetry. A benign prostate is typically smooth, firm, and elastic (rubbery). Hard nodules or asymmetry suggest Prostate Cancer.
- **Investigations**:
  - **Urinalysis**: To rule out UTI or hematuria (which requires further workup for bladder/renal cancer).
  - **Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA)**: Blood test. Elevated in BPH, but also in prostate cancer, prostatitis, and after instrumentation. Used to screen for cancer and to estimate prostate volume (higher PSA correlates with larger volume and higher risk of progression).
  - **Renal Function (U&Es)**: Check creatinine to rule out obstructive uropathy (renal failure due to chronic retention).
  - **Uroflowmetry**: Measures peak urinary flow rate (Qmax). Qmax < 10 mL/s suggests significant obstruction.
  - **Post-Void Residual (PVR) Volume**: Measured via bladder ultrasound. High PVR (>100-200 mL) indicates poor emptying and higher risk of retention.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
### 1. Watchful Waiting & Lifestyle Modifications
- Indicated for mild symptoms (IPSS ≤ 7) or moderate symptoms that are not bothersome.
- Fluid management (restrict fluids before bedtime to reduce nocturia).
- Avoid caffeine and alcohol (diuretic and irritant effects).
- Double voiding technique.
- Avoid medications that exacerbate symptoms (e.g., decongestants containing pseudoephedrine).

### 2. Pharmacological Therapy
- **Alpha-1 Blockers (甲型阻斷劑)**: First-line for rapid symptom relief.
  - *Mechanism*: Relax smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck (addressing the dynamic component).
  - *Examples*: Tamsulosin (坦索羅辛 - uroselective), Alfuzosin, Doxazosin, Terazosin.
  - *Onset*: Fast (days to weeks). Does *not* reduce prostate size or prevent disease progression.
  - *Side effects*: Postural hypotension, dizziness, retrograde ejaculation, Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS - important if patient needs cataract surgery).
- **5-Alpha-Reductase Inhibitors (5-ARIs - 5-α還原酶抑制劑)**:
  - *Mechanism*: Block conversion of testosterone to DHT. Induces apoptosis of prostate epithelial cells, reducing prostate volume by ~20-30% (addressing the static component).
  - *Examples*: Finasteride (非那雄胺), Dutasteride.
  - *Indications*: Best for men with significantly enlarged prostates (>30-40g).
  - *Onset*: Slow (takes 3-6 months for maximum effect). **Prevents disease progression** (reduces risk of acute urinary retention and need for surgery).
  - *Side effects*: Decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, gynecomastia.
  - *Note*: 5-ARIs artificially lower serum PSA by ~50%. You must double the measured PSA value when screening for prostate cancer.
- **Combination Therapy**: Alpha-blocker + 5-ARI. Recommended for men with moderate/severe symptoms AND an enlarged prostate, as it provides rapid symptom relief and long-term risk reduction.
- **Anticholinergics / Mirabegron**: Can be added if storage symptoms (overactive bladder) persist despite alpha-blockers, provided PVR is low.

### 3. Surgical Treatment
- *Indications*: Refractory symptoms despite medical therapy, recurrent UTIs, recurrent gross hematuria, bladder stones, renal impairment due to BPH, or Acute Urinary Retention (AUR) failing trial without catheter.
- **Transurethral Resection of the Prostate (TURP - 經尿道前列腺切除術)**: The historical gold standard.
- **Minimally Invasive Therapies**: Laser enucleation (HoLEP), Photoselective vaporization (PVP/GreenLight), UroLift, Water vapor thermal therapy (Rezum).

## 6. Complications (併發症)
- **Acute Urinary Retention (AUR)**: Sudden, painful inability to void. Requires urgent urethral catheterization.
- Chronic Urinary Retention (painless, leading to overflow incontinence).
- Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections (due to stasis).
- Bladder Calculi (stones).
- Obstructive Uropathy leading to Chronic Kidney Disease (Post-renal failure).
