# Acne Vulgaris (暗瘡 / 尋常痤瘡)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A chronic inflammatory disease of the pilosebaceous unit (hair follicle and its associated sebaceous gland).
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common, affecting ~85% of adolescents and young adults. Can persist into or begin in adulthood (especially in women). Significant psychosocial impact (low self-esteem, depression, anxiety).

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
Acne development involves four key interrelated factors:
1. **Follicular Hyperkeratosis**: Abnormal shedding of skin cells (keratinocytes) within the hair follicle. The cells stick together and plug the follicle, forming a microcomedone.
2. **Increased Sebum Production**: Driven by androgens (e.g., testosterone). The sebaceous glands enlarge and produce excess sebum (oil).
3. **Proliferation of *Cutibacterium acnes* (*C. acnes*)**: A commensal anaerobic bacterium that thrives in the lipid-rich, anaerobic environment of the plugged follicle.
4. **Inflammation**: *C. acnes* metabolizes sebum into inflammatory free fatty acids and triggers an immune response, leading to inflammatory lesions (papules, pustules, nodules).

## 3. Clinical Features & Classification (臨床特徵與分類)
Acne lesions are divided into non-inflammatory and inflammatory types. The severity dictates the treatment approach.

### Types of Lesions
- **Non-inflammatory (Comedones - 粉刺)**: The hallmark of acne.
  - *Open comedones (Blackheads)*: The follicular plug is open to the air; melanin oxidizes and turns black.
  - *Closed comedones (Whiteheads)*: The follicle is completely blocked.
- **Inflammatory Lesions**:
  - *Papules*: Small, red, tender bumps.
  - *Pustules*: Papules topped with visible pus.
  - *Nodules/Cysts*: Deep, large, painful, solid or fluid-filled lumps. High risk of scarring.

### Severity Grading
- **Mild**: Mostly comedones, with a few scattered papules/pustules. No nodules.
- **Moderate**: Many comedones, prominent papules and pustules. Mild scarring may be present.
- **Severe**: Extensive inflammatory lesions, multiple deep nodules/cysts, significant scarring.

## 4. Assessment & Differential Diagnosis (評估與鑑別診斷)
- **History**: Age of onset, menstrual history in women (flares pre-menstrually, signs of hyperandrogenism like hirsutism/irregular periods suggest Polycystic Ovary Syndrome - PCOS), current skincare routine, medication history (corticosteroids, lithium, and androgens can cause acne).
- **Differential Diagnosis**:
  - *Rosacea*: Erythema, telangiectasia, papules/pustules primarily on the central face (cheeks, nose). **No comedones**. Often triggered by heat, spicy food, alcohol.
  - *Perioral Dermatitis*: Small papules/pustules around the mouth, sparing the vermillion border. Often related to topical steroid use.
  - *Folliculitis*: Infection of hair follicles (often *Staph aureus* or *Malassezia*), presents as itchy pustules, often on the trunk.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
Treatment requires patience; it takes 6-8 weeks to see significant improvement. Therapy targets the four pathogenic factors.

### 1. Topical Therapy (First-line for Mild to Moderate Acne)
- **Topical Retinoids (外用維他命A酸)**: The foundation of acne therapy.
  - *Mechanism*: Normalizes follicular desquamation (comedolytic - clears pores) and has anti-inflammatory properties.
  - *Examples*: Adapalene (Differin), Tretinoin, Tazarotene.
  - *Counseling*: Apply a pea-sized amount to the entire face (not just spots) at night. Causes initial irritation, redness, and peeling (start every other night, use moisturizer). Causes photosensitivity (use sunscreen). **Teratogenic (avoid in pregnancy)**.
- **Benzoyl Peroxide (BPO - 過氧化苯甲酰)**:
  - *Mechanism*: Antimicrobial (kills *C. acnes* via oxidative stress) and mildly comedolytic. **No bacterial resistance develops.**
  - *Counseling*: Can bleach hair, towels, and clothing. Can cause skin irritation.
- **Topical Antibiotics**:
  - *Examples*: Clindamycin, Erythromycin.
  - *Rule*: **Never use as monotherapy** due to the rapid development of bacterial resistance. Always combine with BPO or a retinoid.
- **Combination Therapy**: Highly effective (e.g., Epiduo = Adapalene + BPO; Duac = Clindamycin + BPO).

### 2. Systemic Therapy (For Moderate to Severe, or Scarring Acne)
- **Oral Antibiotics**:
  - *Indications*: Moderate-to-severe inflammatory acne, or acne involving the chest/back.
  - *First-line*: Tetracyclines (e.g., **Doxycycline**, Minocycline). Lymecycline is also common.
  - *Counseling (Doxycycline)*: Photosensitivity (sunburn risk), GI upset, pill esophagitis (take with a full glass of water, do not lie down for 30 mins). Contraindicated in pregnancy and children < 8 years (stains developing teeth).
  - *Rule*: Limit use to 3-4 months to minimize antibiotic resistance. Always use alongside topical BPO or retinoids.
- **Hormonal Therapy (For Women)**:
  - *Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs)*: Reduce ovarian androgen production, decreasing sebum. Useful if acne flares with menstruation or if PCOS is present.
  - *Spironolactone*: An androgen receptor antagonist. Highly effective for adult female acne (often presents on the jawline/neck). Monitor potassium levels. Teratogenic.
- **Oral Isotretinoin (口服維他命A酸 / Roaccutane)**:
  - *Indications*: Severe nodulocystic acne, acne causing severe scarring, or acne refractory to other treatments.
  - *Mechanism*: The only drug that targets all four pathogenic factors. Highly effective, often curative.
  - *Severe Side Effects & Monitoring*:
    - **Highly Teratogenic**: Strict pregnancy prevention is mandatory (two forms of contraception, regular pregnancy tests).
    - Mucocutaneous: Severe dryness of lips (cheilitis), skin, and eyes.
    - Systemic: Elevated liver enzymes, hypertriglyceridemia, myalgia.
    - Psychiatric: Possible link to depression/suicidal ideation (controversial, but must monitor mood).
    - Requires regular blood tests (LFTs, Lipids, Pregnancy) and specialist prescription.

## 6. Complications (併發症)
- **Scarring**: Atrophic (ice-pick, boxcar, rolling scars) or hypertrophic/keloid scars. Early, aggressive treatment of inflammatory acne is the only way to prevent scarring.
- **Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)**: Dark spots left after lesions heal. Very common in darker skin types. Fades slowly over months. Topical retinoids and sun protection help.
