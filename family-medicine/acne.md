# Acne Vulgaris

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Pathogenesis: Follicular hyperkeratosis, excess sebum, Cutibacterium acnes, inflammation.
**Answer**: 發病機制：毛囊角化過度、皮脂過多、痤瘡丙酸桿菌、發炎。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Lesions: Comedones (open/closed), papules, pustules, nodules, cysts.
**Answer**: 病灶：粉刺（開放/閉合）、丘疹、膿疱、結節、囊腫。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Topical Rx: Benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids (Adapalene), topical antibiotics (Clindamycin).
**Answer**: 外用治療：過氧化苯甲酰、外用維A酸（阿達帕林）、外用抗生素（克林黴素）。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Systemic Rx: Oral antibiotics (Doxycycline), oral contraceptives (females), oral Isotretinoin (severe/scarring - requires specialist).
**Answer**: 全身治療：口服抗生素（多西環素）、口服避孕藥（女性）、口服異維A酸（嚴重/留疤 - 需要專科醫生）。

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: A 16-year-old boy presents with worsening acne on his face and back for the past 6 months. He feels very self-conscious and avoids social events.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
PHYSICAL (4P):
- Presenting complaint: Worsening acne on face and back.
- Precipitating factors: Puberty, hormonal changes.
- Pattern: Gradual worsening over 6 months.
- Palliation/Provocation: Squeezing spots makes it worse.

BATHE:
- Background: "You've been dealing with this acne for 6 months. How are you managing it day-to-day?"
- Affect: "How does having this acne make you feel about yourself?"
- Trouble: "What is the most difficult part? Is it the physical discomfort or how it looks?"
- Handling: "What have you tried so far? Any over-the-counter creams or face washes?"
- Empathy: "It's completely understandable that this is affecting your confidence. Acne can be really tough to deal with at your age."

ICE:
- Ideas: "What do you think is causing the acne to get worse?"
- Concerns: "Are you worried about scarring?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to help clear your skin?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Acne Vulgaris (moderate to severe, involving face and back). Need to assess for scarring and current skincare routine.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Low self-esteem, social withdrawal, potential for depression.

SOCIAL:
- Avoiding social events and peer interactions.

BARRIERS:
- Impatience with treatment (acne treatments take weeks to show results).

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"Acne is very common in teenagers due to hormonal changes. It is not caused by poor hygiene or your diet, and we have very effective treatments for it."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Wash your face twice a day with a gentle, non-comedogenic cleanser. Avoid scrubbing your skin."
- "Do not squeeze or pick at the spots, as this can cause permanent scarring and spread the infection."
- "Use oil-free or 'non-comedogenic' moisturizers and sunscreens."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe a topical retinoid cream (e.g., Adapalene) to use at night, and a topical antibiotic (e.g., Clindamycin) for the morning."
- "If it's severe, we may need to start a course of oral antibiotics (e.g., Doxycycline) for a few months."

REFER:
- Refer to a dermatologist if the acne is severe, nodulocystic, causing significant scarring, or not responding to primary care treatments (for consideration of oral Isotretinoin).

INVESTIGATE:
- Usually a clinical diagnosis. No routine investigations needed.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor for improvement in acne lesions and any side effects of medications (e.g., dry skin, sun sensitivity).

PLAN:
- Review in 6-8 weeks, as treatments take time to work.

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain how to use the creams, then ask: "Just to make sure I explained it clearly, how will you use the two different creams I prescribed?"

ICE:
Directly address his self-consciousness by validating his feelings and emphasizing that the treatment will improve his skin's appearance over time.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know it's frustrating that it won't clear up overnight, but if we stick to this plan, we will see improvement."

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about a good daily skincare routine, and then we will discuss the prescription medications."
