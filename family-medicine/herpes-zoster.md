# Herpes Zoster (Shingles) (帶狀疱疹 / 生蛇)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A localized, blistering, painful rash caused by the reactivation of the Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV), which has remained dormant in the sensory dorsal root ganglia or cranial nerve ganglia since a primary infection of chickenpox (varicella).
- **Epidemiology**: Lifetime risk is ~30%. Incidence and severity increase significantly with age (especially >50 years) and immunosuppression.

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- **Primary Infection**: VZV causes chickenpox, usually in childhood. The virus then travels retrogradely up sensory nerves to establish lifelong latency in the dorsal root ganglia (spinal nerves) or trigeminal ganglion (cranial nerve V).
- **Reactivation**: Occurs when cell-mediated immunity to VZV wanes (due to aging, stress, illness, or immunosuppressive drugs/conditions like HIV or chemotherapy). The virus travels anterogradely down the sensory nerve to the skin, causing inflammation of the nerve (neuritis) and a characteristic rash in the corresponding dermatome.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Prodrome**: Precedes the rash by 2-3 days (sometimes up to a week). Characterized by pain, burning, tingling, itching, or hyperesthesia (increased sensitivity to touch) in the affected dermatome. May be accompanied by malaise, headache, or low-grade fever. The pain can mimic pleurisy, myocardial infarction, or biliary colic depending on the dermatome.
- **Rash**:
  - Starts as erythematous macules and papules.
  - Rapidly evolves into clusters of clear **vesicles** (blisters).
  - Vesicles become pustular, then rupture and crust over within 7-10 days. Complete healing takes 2-4 weeks.
- **Distribution**:
  - **Unilateral**: Strictly respects the midline.
  - **Dermatomal**: Confined to the sensory distribution of one (or occasionally two adjacent) spinal or cranial nerves.
  - Most common sites: Thoracic (50%), Trigeminal (face), Cervical, Lumbar.

## 4. Special Presentations & Complications (特殊表現與併發症)
- **Herpes Zoster Ophthalmicus (HZO)**: Reactivation in the ophthalmic division (V1) of the trigeminal nerve.
  - *Hutchinson's Sign*: Vesicles on the tip or side of the nose. Indicates involvement of the nasociliary branch, predicting a high risk of ocular involvement (keratitis, uveitis, acute retinal necrosis), which can lead to blindness. **Requires urgent ophthalmology referral.**
- **Ramsay Hunt Syndrome (Herpes Zoster Oticus)**: Reactivation in the geniculate ganglion (Facial nerve, CN VII).
  - Triad: Ipsilateral facial paralysis (LMN lesion, like Bell's palsy), ear pain, and vesicles in the auditory canal or auricle. May also involve CN VIII (tinnitus, hearing loss, vertigo).
- **Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN - 帶狀疱疹後遺神經痛)**: The most common complication.
  - Defined as significant pain persisting for **> 90 days** after the onset of the rash.
  - Characterized by severe, constant burning/aching pain, intermittent sharp/stabbing pain, and **allodynia** (pain provoked by a non-painful stimulus, like light touch from clothing).
  - Risk factors: Older age (>60), severe acute pain, severe rash, ophthalmic involvement.
- **Secondary Bacterial Infection**: Usually *Staphylococcus aureus* or *Streptococcus pyogenes* infecting the scratched vesicles.

## 5. Diagnosis (診斷)
- Diagnosis is almost always **clinical**, based on the characteristic unilateral, dermatomal vesicular rash and pain.
- Laboratory testing (PCR of vesicle fluid or viral culture) is rarely needed, reserved for atypical presentations (e.g., disseminated zoster in immunocompromised patients) or diagnostic uncertainty.

## 6. Management (治療與管理)
### 1. Antiviral Therapy
- **Goal**: Reduce the severity and duration of the acute rash, decrease viral shedding, and potentially reduce the risk/severity of PHN.
- **Timing**: Most effective if started **within 72 hours** of rash onset. (Can be considered after 72 hours if new vesicles are still actively forming, or in immunocompromised patients, or in HZO).
- **Medications**:
  - **Acyclovir (阿昔洛韋)**: 800mg 5 times daily for 7 days. (Requires frequent dosing, poor bioavailability).
  - **Valacyclovir (伐昔洛韋)**: 1g TDS for 7 days. (Prodrug of acyclovir, better bioavailability, preferred).
  - **Famciclovir**: 500mg TDS for 7 days.
  - *Note*: Adjust dose in renal impairment.

### 2. Analgesia (Pain Control)
- Acute pain can be severe and requires a step-wise approach.
- Mild/Moderate: Paracetamol, NSAIDs.
- Severe: Weak opioids (Tramadol) or short course of stronger opioids.
- Neuropathic agents: Amitriptyline, Pregabalin, or Gabapentin can be started early if acute pain is severe or neuropathic in nature, to help prevent PHN.

### 3. Local Care & Infection Control
- Keep the rash clean and dry to prevent secondary bacterial infection.
- Avoid topical creams/ointments (like calamine) which can cause crusting and trap bacteria.
- **Infection risk**: The patient is contagious (can transmit VZV to non-immune individuals, causing chickenpox, NOT shingles) until all lesions have crusted over. Avoid contact with pregnant women (who haven't had chickenpox), premature infants, and immunocompromised individuals.

### 4. Management of Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN)
- Difficult to treat. Standard analgesics (NSAIDs) are ineffective.
- **First-line**:
  - Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs): Amitriptyline, Nortriptyline. (Caution in elderly due to anticholinergic side effects).
  - Anticonvulsants: Pregabalin, Gabapentin.
  - Topical agents: Lidocaine 5% patches, Capsaicin cream.
- **Second-line**: SNRIs (Duloxetine), Opioids, referral to a pain clinic.

## 7. Prevention (預防)
- **Zoster Vaccine**: Highly recommended for adults **≥ 50 years old** to prevent shingles and PHN.
  - **Shingrix (Recombinant Zoster Vaccine - RZV)**: Two doses, 2-6 months apart. Highly efficacious (>90%) and protection wanes slowly. Safe for immunocompromised patients (as it is not a live virus). This is the preferred vaccine.
  - *Zostavax (Live Attenuated Zoster Vaccine)*: Older vaccine, less efficacious, contraindicated in immunocompromised individuals. Largely replaced by Shingrix.
