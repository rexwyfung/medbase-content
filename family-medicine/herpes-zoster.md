# Herpes Zoster (Shingles)

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Reactivation of dormant VZV in dorsal root ganglion; unilateral, dermatomal distribution, does not cross midline
**Answer**: 潛伏在背根神經節的水痘帶狀疱疹病毒（VZV）再激活；單側，皮節分佈，不越過中線

**Examiner's Pearl**: Prodrome: 1–5 days of pain/burning/tingling in dermatomal distribution BEFORE rash appears
**Answer**: 前驅期：皮疹出現前1–5天皮節分佈區域疼痛/灼燒/刺痛

**Examiner's Pearl**: Antiviral (aciclovir/valaciclovir/famciclovir): within 72 hours of rash onset → reduces severity, duration, PHN risk
**Answer**: 抗病毒藥（阿昔洛韋/伐昔洛韋/泛昔洛韋）：皮疹出現後72小時內→減輕嚴重程度、持續時間、PHN風險

**Examiner's Pearl**: Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN): pain persisting >3 months after rash; incidence rises steeply with age; Rx: TCA, gabapentin, pregabalin, capsaicin
**Answer**: 帶狀疱疹後神經痛（PHN）：皮疹後疼痛持續>3個月；發生率隨年齡急劇上升；治療：TCA、加巴噴丁、普瑞巴林、辣椒素

**Examiner's Pearl**: High-risk complications: HZ ophthalmicus (rash on nose tip = Hutchinson sign → eye involvement), Ramsay Hunt syndrome (ear, CN VII palsy)
**Answer**: 高風險併發症：眼部帶狀疱疹（鼻尖皮疹=Hutchinson徵→眼睛受累），Ramsay Hunt綜合症（耳部、第VII對顱神經麻痺）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Shingrix vaccine (recombinant, 2-dose): >90% efficacy against HZ; recommended all adults ≥50; especially immunocompromised
**Answer**: Shingrix疫苗（重組，2劑）：>90%對抗HZ有效；推薦所有≥50歲成年人；尤其是免疫功能受損者

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Chan, 65M. Presents with a 3-day history of a painful, blistering rash on the right side of his chest, wrapping around to his back. The pain is severe, burning, and started 2 days before the rash appeared. He is worried it is contagious to his newborn grandson.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Age 65 (waning immunity to VZV), past history of chickenpox.
- Precipitating: Reactivation of the Varicella Zoster Virus in the dorsal root ganglion.
- Perpetuating: Severe neuropathic pain causing sleep deprivation.
- Protective: Rash is localized to one dermatome, no eye involvement, presented within 72 hours of rash onset (optimal for antivirals).

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You mentioned this painful, blistering rash on your chest started 3 days ago. How is this severe burning pain affecting your sleep and daily activities?"
- Affect: "How do you feel about this sudden, intense pain and the rash?"
- Trouble: "What is the most difficult part of dealing with this right now? Is it the pain itself, or the worry about your grandson?"
- Handling: "How have you been trying to manage the pain so far? Have you put any creams on the rash?"
- Empathy: "Shingles pain is notoriously severe and exhausting. I completely understand why you are feeling so distressed and worried about your family."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you think has caused this rash to appear in this specific band around your chest?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned being worried about your newborn grandson. Are you afraid he might catch this from you?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to help with the pain and the rash?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Herpes Zoster (Shingles) affecting a thoracic dermatome.
- Acute neuropathic pain.
- Must rule out Herpes Zoster Ophthalmicus (if rash is on the face/nose) and disseminated zoster.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Anxiety regarding transmission to vulnerable family members (newborn).
- Distress from acute severe pain.

SOCIAL:
- Need to isolate from the newborn grandson.

BARRIERS:
- Misconception about how the virus is transmitted (it causes chickenpox in the unexposed, not shingles).

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I have examined your rash. This is Herpes Zoster, commonly known as Shingles. It is not a new infection you caught from someone else, but a reactivation of the chickenpox virus you had as a child. We have medications to help."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Regarding your grandson: You cannot give someone Shingles. However, the fluid in your blisters contains the chickenpox virus. If your newborn grandson, who hasn't had the chickenpox vaccine yet, touches the fluid, he could catch chickenpox. You must keep the rash completely covered with a dressing and wash your hands frequently. Do not hold the baby until the blisters have fully crusted over and dried up."
- "Keep the rash clean and dry. Do not scratch or pop the blisters, as this can cause a bacterial infection."

PRESCRIBE:
- "Because you came in within 72 hours of the rash starting, I will prescribe an antiviral medication (e.g., Acyclovir or Valacyclovir). This will speed up healing and reduce the severity of the attack."
- "I will also prescribe strong painkillers. Shingles causes nerve pain, so regular painkillers like Paracetamol might not be enough. I will add a medication specifically for nerve pain (e.g., Amitriptyline, Pregabalin, or a strong NSAID/opioid combination depending on patient profile)."

REFER:
- Not required currently, as there is no eye involvement.

INVESTIGATE:
- Clinical diagnosis; no investigations needed.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor for Post-Herpetic Neuralgia (PHN) - pain lasting more than 3 months after the rash heals.

PLAN:
- Follow up in 1-2 weeks to check rash healing and pain control.

SAFETY NET:
"If the rash spreads to your face, especially near your eye or on the tip of your nose, go to the A&E immediately, as it can threaten your vision. Also return if the blisters become filled with yellow pus, indicating a bacterial infection."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the difference between transmitting shingles vs. chickenpox, then ask, "Does it make sense why you need to avoid holding your grandson until the blisters are completely dry?"

ICE:
Directly address his fear of contagion by providing clear, actionable infection control advice (covering the rash, hand hygiene).

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"The nerve pain medication might make you a little drowsy. Are you comfortable taking it at night to help you sleep through the pain?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about how to protect your grandson, and then we will discuss the antiviral and pain medications you need to start today."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: Patient presents with a painful, blistering rash on one side of his chest. He is in severe pain and worried about infecting his family.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned having a painful rash on your chest. Can you tell me exactly when it started and what it feels like?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Allow the patient to describe the rash and pain without interrupting.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
ASSESS SHINGLES SYMPTOMS:
- "Did the pain start before the rash appeared?"
- "Is the pain sharp, burning, or shooting?"
- "Is the rash strictly on one side of your body, or does it cross the middle?"
- "Have you ever had chickenpox as a child?"

SCREEN FOR RED FLAGS (OPHTHALMICUS / DISSEMINATED):
- "Do you have any rash on your face, especially near your eye or on the tip of your nose?"
- "Has your vision changed, or is your eye red and painful?"
- "Do you have a fever or feel generally unwell?"
- "Do you have any medical conditions that weaken your immune system, like cancer or HIV?"

PHYSICAL EXAM:
- Inspect the rash: Look for grouped vesicles (blisters) on an erythematous (red) base, strictly following a dermatome and not crossing the midline.
- Check for signs of bacterial superinfection (yellow crusts, pus).
- If on the face, perform a thorough eye exam (fluorescein stain if indicated) and look for Hutchinson's sign (vesicles on the tip of the nose).

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"What do you think is causing this painful rash?"

CONCERNS:
"You mentioned being worried about your family. Are you concerned about passing this on to someone specific?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What were you hoping we could do to help with the pain today?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"Based on the look of the rash and your symptoms, you have Herpes Zoster, which is commonly known as Shingles."

EXPLANATION:
"Shingles is caused by the chickenpox virus. After you had chickenpox as a child, the virus went to sleep in your nerves. Now, years later, it has woken up and traveled down the nerve to your skin, causing this painful rash."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does that explanation make sense to you?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"This is very common and we have good treatments for it. You cannot give someone Shingles directly."

INVESTIGATE:
"We do not need any blood tests; the diagnosis is clear from examining the rash."

PLAN:
"I will prescribe an antiviral medication to stop the virus from multiplying, which helps it heal faster. I will also prescribe specific painkillers for nerve pain."

ADVICE:
"Keep the rash covered with a dressing. The fluid in the blisters contains the chickenpox virus, so avoid contact with pregnant women, newborns, or anyone with a weak immune system until the blisters have completely dried and crusted over."

SAFETY NET:
"If the rash spreads to your face, especially near your eye, or if the blisters become filled with yellow pus, please go to the A&E or come back immediately."

## Station 4 — Records & Prescription: Herpes Zoster

### Subjective (S)
Mrs Ho, 68F. Herpes zoster T5–T6 right, presented day 2 of rash. On methotrexate (RA).
Prodrome: 3 days burning pain right chest before rash.
Rash: right chest/back, does not cross midline, vesicular. No eye involvement, no ear symptoms.
Pain VAS 7/10. Anxious about duration and "nerve damage".

### Objective (O)
Afebrile (36.8°C). HR 76. BP 136/82
Right T5–T6 dermatome: erythematous base with clustered vesicles (some pustular), extending from chest to back, not crossing midline. No skin break/secondary infection.
Eyes: visual acuity normal bilaterally, no periorbital rash, no conjunctivitis. Hutchinson sign: NEGATIVE.
Ears: no vesicles in ear canal or pinna. No facial weakness. CN VII: intact bilaterally.
Renal function: Cr 72, eGFR 82 (good — safe for valaciclovir).

### Assessment (A)
Herpes zoster, T5–T6 right (uncomplicated — no ophthalmic, no Ramsay Hunt). Immunosuppressed (MTX). Treat with antivirals regardless of 72h window due to immunosuppression. High PHN risk: age 68, immunosuppressed, high pain score.

### Plan (P)
1. Valaciclovir 1g TDS × 7 days (antiviral — immunosuppressed → treat regardless of >72h)
2. Paracetamol 1g QDS regularly for pain
3. Gabapentin 300mg ON × 1 week, then 300mg BD × 1 week, then 300mg TDS if tolerated
   (prophylactic neuropathic pain management — prevent/reduce PHN severity)
4. Rash care: calamine lotion, non-adherent dressing, keep dry
5. Contagious precautions until fully crusted: avoid pregnant contacts, immunocompromised
6. Shingrix vaccination counselling: second dose in future (not during acute episode)
7. MONITOR: PHN at 3 months — if pain persists, treat actively
8. Inform rheumatology re: MTX during VZV reactivation
9. Review in 2 weeks (or sooner if: fever, eye symptoms, ear symptoms, worsening)

### Prescription
- **Drug**: Valaciclovir
- **Preparation**: Film-coated tablet 500mg
- **Dose**: 1000 mg (2 × 500mg tablets)
- **Route**: Oral
- **Frequency**: Three times daily (every 8 hours)
- **Duration**: 7 days
- **Quantity**: 42 tablets

**Notes**: Take with or without food. Ensure adequate fluid intake (≥1.5L/day) — risk of nephrotoxicity if dehydrated. Most effective if started within 72 hours of rash onset. In immunosuppressed patients: treat regardless of timing. Complete full 7-day course even if rash improving. SE: headache, nausea, renal impairment (monitor if CKD).

**Tips**:
- Valaciclovir 1g TDS is preferred over aciclovir 800mg 5x/day (compliance and bioavailability)
- Specify "every 8 hours" — not just TDS, for consistent antiviral levels
- In immunosuppressed: treat even after 72-hour window
- Check renal function before prescribing — reduce dose if eGFR <50
- Antiviral does NOT prevent PHN — it reduces risk and severity; gabapentin early may also help
