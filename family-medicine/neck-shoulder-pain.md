# Neck / Shoulder Pain

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Common causes: Cervical spondylosis, muscle strain, frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis), rotator cuff tendinopathy.
**Answer**: 常見原因：頸椎病、肌肉勞損、五十肩（粘連性肩關節囊炎）、肩袖肌腱病變。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Red flags (Neck): Trauma, neurological deficit (weakness, numbness, bowel/bladder changes), fever, weight loss.
**Answer**: 危險信號（頸部）：創傷、神經功能缺損（無力、麻木、大小便改變）、發燒、體重減輕。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Frozen shoulder: Global restriction of active AND passive movement. Diabetic patients are at higher risk.
**Answer**: 五十肩：主動和被動運動全面受限。糖尿病患者風險較高。

**Examiner's Pearl**: Rotator cuff: Painful arc (60-120 degrees), weakness in abduction/external rotation.
**Answer**: 肩袖：疼痛弧（60-120度），外展/外旋無力。

## Station 1: Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: A 35-year-old IT worker complains of neck and shoulder pain for 2 months. It worsens after long hours at the computer. He is worried about a "slipped disc".

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
PHYSICAL (4P):
- Presenting complaint: Neck and shoulder pain for 2 months.
- Precipitating factors: Prolonged computer use, poor posture.
- Pattern: Worsens at the end of the workday, relieved by rest.
- Palliation/Provocation: Stretching helps slightly.

BATHE:
- Background: "You've had this neck and shoulder pain for 2 months. How is it affecting your work?"
- Affect: "How does this constant pain make you feel?"
- Trouble: "What is the most difficult part of dealing with this pain?"
- Handling: "What have you been doing to manage the pain so far?"
- Empathy: "It must be very distracting and uncomfortable to work with this pain every day."

ICE:
- Ideas: "What do you think is causing the pain?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned a slipped disc. Are you experiencing any numbness or tingling in your arms?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to help you?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Mechanical neck and shoulder pain, likely due to muscle strain and poor ergonomics. Need to rule out cervical radiculopathy (slipped disc).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Anxiety about severe spinal problems.

SOCIAL:
- Occupational hazard (prolonged sitting, computer use).

BARRIERS:
- Difficulty changing work habits or ergonomics.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"Based on your symptoms, this sounds like muscle strain from your posture at work, rather than a slipped disc. There are no signs of nerve damage."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "It's important to take regular breaks. Try to stand up and stretch every 30-40 minutes."
- "Check your workstation setup. Your screen should be at eye level, and your chair should support your lower back."
- "Apply a warm compress to your neck and shoulders to help relax the muscles."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe some painkillers (e.g., Paracetamol or NSAIDs) and a muscle relaxant to help ease the discomfort."

REFER:
- Refer to physiotherapy for posture correction, strengthening exercises, and ergonomic advice.

INVESTIGATE:
- X-rays or MRI are NOT routinely needed for mechanical neck pain without red flags.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor response to physiotherapy and medication.

PLAN:
- Review in 4-6 weeks if symptoms do not improve.

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the ergonomic advice, then ask: "Just to make sure I explained it clearly, what changes will you try to make at your desk tomorrow?"

ICE:
Directly address his fear of a slipped disc by explaining the absence of neurological symptoms (like numbness or weakness).

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know it's hard to change your work habits. Let's focus on small changes first, like taking short breaks."

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about how to improve your workspace, and then we will discuss physiotherapy and medications."
