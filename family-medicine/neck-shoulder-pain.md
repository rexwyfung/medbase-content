# Neck and Shoulder Pain (頸肩痛)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: Pain localized to the cervical spine, the shoulder girdle, or the soft tissues connecting them. It is often a symptom complex rather than a single diagnosis.
- **Epidemiology**: Extremely common, especially in modern society due to prolonged computer/smartphone use ("text neck"). Lifetime prevalence is up to 70%.

## 2. Etiology & Classification (病因與分類)
The pain can originate from the neck, the shoulder, or be referred.

### Cervical Spine Causes (Neck-predominant)
- **Mechanical/Postural Neck Pain (Most common)**: Muscle strain (e.g., trapezius, levator scapulae), ligament sprain, often due to poor ergonomics, prolonged static posture, or sleeping awkwardly.
- **Cervical Spondylosis (頸椎退化)**: Osteoarthritis of the cervical spine. Degeneration of intervertebral discs and facet joints, leading to osteophyte formation. Common in older adults.
- **Cervical Radiculopathy (頸神經根病變)**: Compression or irritation of a cervical nerve root (usually C5, C6, or C7) by a herniated disc or osteophyte. Presents with sharp, shooting pain radiating down the arm, often with numbness, tingling, or weakness in the specific dermatome/myotome.
- **Whiplash Injury**: Acceleration-deceleration injury to the neck, typically from a rear-end motor vehicle collision.

### Shoulder Causes (Shoulder-predominant)
- **Rotator Cuff Disorders (肩袖病變)**: Tendinopathy, impingement syndrome, or tears. Pain is typically worse with overhead activities and at night.
- **Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder - 五十肩)**: Global restriction of active and passive shoulder movement, associated with severe pain initially. Common in diabetics.
- **Acromioclavicular (AC) Joint Osteoarthritis**: Pain localized to the top of the shoulder, worse with cross-body adduction.

### Referred Pain
- **Cardiac**: Ischemic heart disease (angina/MI) can refer pain to the left shoulder/neck.
- **Diaphragmatic Irritation**: Gallbladder disease (right shoulder), subphrenic abscess, ruptured ectopic pregnancy (Kehr's sign).
- **Apical Lung Tumor (Pancoast Tumor)**: Can compress the brachial plexus, causing severe shoulder/arm pain and Horner's syndrome.

## 3. Clinical Evaluation & Red Flags (臨床評估與紅旗症狀)
- **History**: Onset (trauma vs. insidious), character of pain, radiation (arm vs. local), exacerbating/relieving factors, occupational history, ergonomics.
- **Red Flags (Require urgent investigation)**:
  - **Trauma**: Major trauma, or minor trauma in the elderly/osteoporotic.
  - **Neurological Deficits**: Progressive weakness, sensory loss, or signs of **Cervical Myelopathy** (spinal cord compression: clumsy hands, gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, Babinski sign, bowel/bladder dysfunction).
  - **Systemic Symptoms**: Unexplained weight loss, fever, night sweats (suspect malignancy or infection).
  - **History of Cancer**.
  - **Unremitting Night Pain**.
- **Physical Examination**:
  - Inspection: Posture, muscle atrophy.
  - Palpation: Tenderness over spinous processes (suggests bony injury/infection) vs. paraspinal muscles (spasm).
  - Range of Motion (ROM): Active and passive ROM of the neck and shoulder.
  - Neurological Exam: Test dermatomes, myotomes, and reflexes of the upper limbs.
  - Special Tests: Spurling's test (for cervical radiculopathy), Neer's/Hawkins tests (for shoulder impingement).

## 4. Investigations (檢查)
- **Usually NOT required** for acute, non-specific mechanical neck/shoulder pain without red flags.
- **X-rays (C-spine or Shoulder)**: Indicated if history of trauma, suspicion of osteoarthritis, or lack of improvement after 4-6 weeks.
- **MRI**: The modality of choice if there are red flags, progressive neurological deficits (radiculopathy/myelopathy), or if surgery is being considered.

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
### Acute Mechanical Neck/Shoulder Pain
- **Reassurance**: Explain the benign nature of the condition.
- **Activity Modification**: Avoid prolonged static postures. Take frequent breaks. Ergonomic adjustments at work (screen height, chair support).
- **Pharmacotherapy**:
  - First-line: NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen) or Paracetamol.
  - Muscle relaxants (e.g., Diazepam, Baclofen) for short-term use (few days) if severe muscle spasm is present.
  - Topical NSAIDs or counterirritants.
- **Physical Therapy**: Heat/ice applications. Gentle stretching and range-of-motion exercises.

### Chronic Pain or Specific Pathologies
- **Physical Therapy**: Core component. Strengthening exercises for the neck and shoulder girdle (scapular stabilizers). Postural training.
- **Cervical Radiculopathy**: Most cases improve with conservative management (NSAIDs, physical therapy, time). Gabapentin or Pregabalin can be used for neuropathic pain. Epidural steroid injections or surgery (e.g., ACDF) are considered for refractory pain or progressive weakness.
- **Rotator Cuff Tendinopathy**: Physical therapy, NSAIDs, subacromial corticosteroid injection.
- **Frozen Shoulder**: NSAIDs, intra-articular corticosteroid injection (early phase), aggressive physical therapy to restore ROM.
