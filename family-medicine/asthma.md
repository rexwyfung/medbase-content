# Asthma (哮喘)

## 1. Definition & Epidemiology (定義與流行病學)
- **Definition**: A heterogeneous disease, usually characterized by chronic airway inflammation. It is defined by the history of respiratory symptoms (wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough) that vary over time and in intensity, together with variable expiratory airflow limitation.
- **Epidemiology**: Common chronic disease worldwide. Often begins in childhood but can develop at any age. Strongly associated with atopy (allergic rhinitis, eczema).

## 2. Pathophysiology (病理生理學)
- **Airway Inflammation**: Eosinophilic, neutrophilic, or mixed inflammation involving mast cells, T-helper 2 (Th2) cells, and IgE.
- **Airway Hyperresponsiveness (AHR)**: Exaggerated bronchoconstrictor response to various stimuli (allergens, cold air, exercise, viral infections).
- **Airway Remodeling**: Long-term structural changes including smooth muscle hypertrophy, subepithelial fibrosis, and mucus gland hyperplasia, leading to irreversible airflow limitation in severe cases.

## 3. Clinical Features (臨床特徵)
- **Classic Symptoms**:
  - Wheezing (喘鳴 - high-pitched whistling sound, usually expiratory).
  - Shortness of breath (呼吸急促).
  - Chest tightness (胸悶).
  - Cough (咳嗽 - often worse at night or early morning).
- **Symptom Patterns**:
  - Variable over time and intensity.
  - Often triggered by: Viral respiratory infections (colds), allergens (dust mites, pollen, pet dander), exercise, cold air, smoke/strong smells, stress, medications (NSAIDs, beta-blockers).
- **Physical Examination**:
  - May be completely normal between exacerbations.
  - During exacerbation: Widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze, prolonged expiratory phase, tachypnea, tachycardia, use of accessory muscles (in severe cases).

## 4. Diagnosis & Investigations (診斷與檢查)
Diagnosis is based on a characteristic history PLUS evidence of variable expiratory airflow limitation.
- **Spirometry (肺功能測試)**:
  - Demonstrates obstructive pattern: FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70 (or below lower limit of normal).
  - **Reversibility testing**: Increase in FEV1 by > 12% AND > 200 mL after administration of a short-acting beta2-agonist (SABA, e.g., Salbutamol). This is the gold standard.
- **Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) Monitoring**:
  - Excessive diurnal variability (> 20%) over 1-2 weeks confirms variable airflow limitation.
- **Other Tests (if diagnosis uncertain)**:
  - Bronchoprovocation tests (e.g., Methacholine challenge) to demonstrate AHR.
  - Allergy testing (Skin prick test or specific IgE) to identify triggers.
  - Chest X-ray: Usually normal; done to rule out other conditions (e.g., infection, pneumothorax).

## 5. Management (治療與管理)
Based on GINA (Global Initiative for Asthma) guidelines. The goal is symptom control and risk reduction (preventing exacerbations, lung function decline, and medication side effects).

### Pharmacological Therapy (Stepwise Approach)
**Crucial Paradigm Shift (GINA 2019 onwards)**: SABA-only treatment is NO LONGER recommended for adults/adolescents due to the risk of severe exacerbations. All patients should receive inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)-containing therapy.

- **Track 1 (Preferred)**: Low-dose ICS-Formoterol (a fast-acting LABA) as the reliever (MART - Maintenance and Reliever Therapy).
  - *Step 1 & 2*: As-needed low-dose ICS-Formoterol (e.g., Symbicort).
  - *Step 3*: Low-dose maintenance ICS-Formoterol + as-needed ICS-Formoterol.
  - *Step 4*: Medium-dose maintenance ICS-Formoterol + as-needed ICS-Formoterol.
  - *Step 5*: Add-on LAMA (e.g., Tiotropium), refer for phenotypic assessment (biologics like anti-IgE Omalizumab, anti-IL5).
- **Track 2 (Alternative)**: Regular ICS with as-needed SABA (e.g., Salbutamol).
  - *Step 1*: Take ICS whenever SABA is taken.
  - *Step 2*: Regular low-dose ICS + as-needed SABA.
  - *Step 3*: Low-dose ICS-LABA maintenance + as-needed SABA.
  - *Step 4*: Medium/high-dose ICS-LABA + as-needed SABA.

### Inhaler Technique & Adherence
- **CRITICAL**: Poor asthma control is most commonly due to poor inhaler technique or non-adherence. Always check technique at every visit. Use a spacer with Metered Dose Inhalers (MDIs).

### Non-Pharmacological Management
- **Trigger Avoidance**: Dust mite covers, removing pets from bedroom, smoking cessation (essential).
- **Vaccinations**: Annual influenza vaccine, pneumococcal vaccine.
- **Asthma Action Plan**: Provide a written plan detailing how to adjust medications based on symptoms/PEF and when to seek emergency care.

## 6. Acute Asthma Exacerbation (急性哮喘發作)
- **Assessment of Severity**:
  - *Mild/Moderate*: Talks in phrases, RR < 30, HR < 120, SpO2 ≥ 90%, PEF > 50% predicted.
  - *Severe*: Talks in words, sits hunched forward, RR > 30, HR > 120, SpO2 < 90%, PEF ≤ 50%.
  - *Life-threatening*: Drowsy, confused, silent chest (no wheeze due to minimal airflow), cyanosis, bradycardia.
- **Management (O SHIT)**:
  - **O**xygen: Target SpO2 93-95%.
  - **S**albutamol: Nebulized or via spacer (high dose).
  - **H**ydrocortisone (IV) or Prednisolone (Oral): Systemic corticosteroids are crucial to reduce inflammation and prevent relapse.
  - **I**pratropium bromide: Nebulized (add-on to Salbutamol in severe cases).
  - **T**heophylline / Magnesium Sulfate (IV): For severe/life-threatening cases not responding to initial therapy.
