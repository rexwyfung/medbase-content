# Asthma

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Diagnosis: variable airflow obstruction — spirometry FEV1/FVC <0.7 with ≥12% reversibility with bronchodilator, or PEFR variability >20%
**Answer**: 診斷：可變性氣流阻塞——肺活量計FEV1/FVC <0.7，支氣管擴張劑後改善≥12%，或PEFR變異性>20%

**Examiner's Pearl**: GINA steps: Step 1 SABA PRN → Step 2 Low ICS+SABA → Step 3 Medium ICS+LABA → Step 4 High ICS+LABA ± add-on → Step 5 specialist
**Answer**: GINA步驟：第1步按需SABA → 第2步低劑量ICS+SABA → 第3步中劑量ICS+LABA → 第4步高劑量ICS+LABA±附加 → 第5步專科

**Examiner's Pearl**: Inhaler technique is CRITICAL — poor technique = treatment failure. Always demonstrate + check back at every visit
**Answer**: 吸入器技術至關重要——技術差=治療失敗。每次就診都要示範並回查

**Examiner's Pearl**: Asthma ACTION PLAN: GREEN zone (well controlled); AMBER (worsening — start/increase reliever); RED (PEFR <50% — emergency treatment + seek help)
**Answer**: 哮喘行動計劃：綠色區域（控制良好）；黃色（惡化——開始/增加緩解劑）；紅色（PEFR <50%——緊急治療+尋求幫助）

**Examiner's Pearl**: Triggers: house dust mites, pet dander, pollen, exercise, cold air, NSAIDs/aspirin, beta-blockers, viral URTIs, occupational allergens
**Answer**: 誘發因素：塵蟎、寵物皮屑、花粉、運動、冷空氣、非甾體抗炎藥/阿司匹林、β受體阻滯劑、病毒性上呼吸道感染、職業性變應原

**Examiner's Pearl**: Acute severe: O2 to keep SpO2 94-98%, nebulised salbutamol 5mg Q20min, ipratropium 500mcg, systemic steroids (prednisolone 40mg OD ×5 days)
**Answer**: 急性重度：吸氧保持SpO2 94-98%，霧化沙丁胺醇5mg每20分鐘，異丙托溴銨500mcg，全身皮質類固醇（潑尼松龍40mg每日×5天）

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Ms Cheung, 25F, known asthma. Uses Salbutamol inhaler 4-5 times a week. Wakes up coughing twice a week. Reluctant to use steroid inhaler because she fears weight gain and side effects.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: History of atopy (eczema, allergic rhinitis), family history of asthma.
- Precipitating: Dust mites, weather changes, viral infections, exercise, stress.
- Perpetuating: Over-reliance on SABA (Salbutamol), steroid phobia, poor inhaler technique, non-compliance with preventer.
- Protective: Young, otherwise healthy, seeks medical advice, non-smoker.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "I see you have been needing your blue inhaler quite often lately. How has your asthma been affecting your daily life?"
- Affect: "How do you feel when you wake up coughing in the middle of the night?"
- Trouble: "What bothers you the most about your asthma right now?"
- Handling: "How are you managing when you get breathless? Are you using the blue inhaler more than prescribed?"
- Empathy: "It must be exhausting and frightening to have your sleep interrupted so often by coughing."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "Why do you think your asthma has been acting up so much recently?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned you prefer not to use the preventer inhaler. What are your main worries about it? Are you worried about steroids?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to help you breathe easier and sleep better?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Poorly controlled asthma (GINA guidelines: daytime symptoms >2/week, night waking).
- Risk of severe exacerbation due to over-reliance on SABA.

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Steroid phobia (fear of systemic side effects like weight gain, stunted growth, or dependence).

SOCIAL:
- Impact on sleep leading to daytime fatigue and reduced productivity at work or school.

BARRIERS:
- Misunderstanding the fundamental difference between reliever (symptom control) and preventer (treating underlying inflammation) inhalers.

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"It is very common to worry about steroids, but the inhaled type is extremely safe and works completely differently from steroid pills."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "Asthma is essentially inflammation and swelling in the airways. The blue inhaler only relaxes the muscle temporarily, but it does NOT treat the underlying swelling."
- "The preventer (steroid) inhaler treats the swelling. Because it goes straight into your lungs, the dose is tiny—measured in micrograms. It will NOT cause weight gain or affect your body like steroid pills do."
- "Remember to rinse your mouth and spit out the water after using the preventer to prevent oral thrush."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I strongly recommend starting a low-dose Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) daily to prevent the attacks."
- (Or MART therapy if appropriate: ICS-Formoterol as both maintenance and reliever).

REFER:
- Asthma nurse for comprehensive inhaler technique check and to provide a written Asthma Action Plan.

INVESTIGATE:
- Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) in clinic today to establish a baseline.

OBSERVE:
- Check inhaler technique using a placebo device right now in the clinic.

PLAN:
- Follow up in 4 weeks to review asthma control using the Asthma Control Test (ACT).

SAFETY NET:
"If your blue inhaler is not helping, if you are too breathless to speak in full sentences, or if your chest feels very tight, do not wait. Go to the Accident and Emergency department immediately."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the difference between the two inhalers, then ask, "Can you explain back to me when you would use the blue inhaler and when you would use the brown preventer inhaler?"

ICE:
Directly address the fear of weight gain and validate that it is a common concern, but factually incorrect for inhaled steroids.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"Are you willing to try the preventer inhaler every day for just one month to see if it stops you from waking up coughing?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's check exactly how you use your inhaler, then we can talk about the medications."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: Patient presents with episodic wheezing, chest tightness, and a dry cough, especially at night and after exercise.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned having a cough and a tight chest. Can you tell me more about when these symptoms usually happen?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Listen for triggers like cold air, exercise, or nighttime worsening without interrupting.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
ASSESS ASTHMA SYMPTOMS:
- "Do you ever hear a whistling or wheezing sound when you breathe out?"
- "Does the cough wake you up in the middle of the night?"
- "Do you feel breathless after exercise, or even after laughing?"

IDENTIFY TRIGGERS:
- "Have you noticed anything specific that makes it worse? Like house dust, pets, pollen, or cold weather?"

SCREEN FOR ATOPY:
- "Do you have a history of eczema, hay fever, or a constantly runny nose?"

RED FLAGS (SEVERE ATTACK):
- "Have you ever been so breathless you could not speak in full sentences, or noticed your lips turning blue?"

PHYSICAL EXAM:
- Auscultate chest for widespread polyphonic wheeze. Check PEFR.

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"What do you think is causing this wheezing and coughing?"

CONCERNS:
"Are you worried this might affect your ability to exercise or do your job?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What were you hoping we could do to help your breathing today?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"Based on your symptoms of wheezing, nighttime cough, and the triggers you mentioned, it is highly likely you have Asthma."

EXPLANATION:
"Asthma is a condition where the small breathing tubes in your lungs become sensitive and inflamed. When they are irritated by things like cold air or dust, the tubes narrow and tighten, making it hard to breathe and causing that wheezing sound."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does that explanation make sense to you?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"Asthma is very common and can be controlled extremely well with inhalers, allowing you to live a completely normal, active life."

PLAN:
"We will start you on an inhaler. Depending on how severe it is, we may use a reliever for when you feel tight, and a preventer to use every day to stop the inflammation."

DEMONSTRATE:
Show exactly how to use the inhaler (and spacer if needed). Ask the patient to demonstrate it back to you.

ADVICE:
"Try to identify and avoid your triggers. For example, washing bedsheets in hot water can help kill dust mites."

SAFETY NET:
"If your inhaler is not working and you are struggling to breathe, call an ambulance or go to the Accident and Emergency department immediately."

## Station 4 — Medical Record + Prescription

### Subjective (S)
Miss Lam Siu Wan (F/24), university student, presents with 6-month history of episodic wheeze, nocturnal cough (waking twice/week), and chest tightness on exercise. Using salbutamol 4-5 times/week. PMH: Childhood eczema (mild). Allergic rhinitis (not treated). NKDA. Social: Non-smoker. Lives in hall of residence (potentially dusty). Cat at home. FH: Mother has asthma. Spirometry (today): FEV1/FVC 0.68 (obstructed); FEV1 74% predicted. Post-salbutamol: FEV1 improved by 380mL (15.4%) → reversible airflow obstruction.

### Objective (O)
Vitals: HR 82 bpm, RR 16/min, SpO2 98% RA. 
Appearance: No respiratory distress at rest.
Chest: No accessory muscle use. Percussion resonant. Auscultation: mild expiratory wheeze bilaterally on forced expiration. Air entry equal.
ENT: Pale, boggy inferior turbinates (allergic rhinitis). No polyps.
Skin: No active eczema.
PEFR: 360 L/min (76% predicted for height/age/sex).

### Assessment (A)
PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS: Bronchial Asthma (partially controlled, GINA Step 1→2)
Evidence: Classic symptoms (nocturnal cough, wheeze, chest tightness); spirometry confirms reversible airflow obstruction (FEV1/FVC 0.68; FEV1 +15.4% post-BD); personal atopic history (eczema, allergic rhinitis); maternal history.
Severity: Partially controlled (salbutamol use >3×/week; nocturnal symptoms ×2/week).

COMORBIDITY: Allergic rhinitis (untreated — "one airway" relationship; treating rhinitis improves asthma control).

### Plan (P)
1. STEP 2 THERAPY: Beclomethasone dipropionate 100mcg/dose pMDI — 1 puff twice daily (regular preventer). Continue salbutamol 100mcg/dose PRN.
2. SPACER: Provide Volumatic spacer device — demonstrate technique; request patient demonstrate back.
3. ALLERGIC RHINITIS: Mometasone furoate intranasal spray 50mcg — 2 sprays each nostril once daily.
4. ASTHMA ACTION PLAN: Written personalised plan (green/amber/red zones based on symptoms + PEFR).
5. TRIGGER AVOIDANCE: Dust mite covers, minimise cat exposure (or re-home if possible), no smoking environments.
6. LIFESTYLE: Regular exercise (swimming preferred — warm, humid environment); weight monitoring.
7. SAFETY NET: Emergency action plan — if PEFR <50% or symptoms severe despite 10 puffs salbutamol → call 999.
8. FOLLOW-UP: 4-6 weeks (technique check, symptom review, PEFR diary review, ACT score).

### Prescription
- **Drug**: Beclomethasone dipropionate
- **Preparation**: Pressurised metered-dose inhaler (pMDI)
- **Dose**: 100 micrograms per actuation
- **Route**: Inhalation
- **Frequency**: One puff twice daily (morning and evening)
- **Duration**: 28 days (review)
- **Quantity**: 1 inhaler (200 doses)

**Notes**: Use with Volumatic spacer. Shake before use. Rinse mouth and gargle with water after each use (prevents oral candidiasis). Regular preventer — do NOT use as reliever.

**Tips**:
- Write "100 micrograms" NOT "100mcg" or "100μg"
- "Inhalation" route — NOT "inhale" or "per mouth"
- "Beclomethasone dipropionate" (generic) NOT "Clenil Modulite" alone
- State "per actuation" — important for inhalers
- "Twice daily" NOT "BD"
- Always specify spacer device use in notes
- For LABA+ICS combinations: NEVER prescribe LABA (salmeterol) without ICS — risk of LABA-related asthma death
