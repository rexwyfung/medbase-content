# Ischaemic Heart Disease / Angina

## General

**Examiner's Pearl**: Stable angina: exertional CP, relieved by rest/GTN <5min; CCS Classification I–IV
**Answer**: 穩定性心絞痛：勞力性胸痛，休息/GTN<5分鐘緩解；加拿大心血管協會（CCS）分類I–IV

**Examiner's Pearl**: Secondary prevention essentials: aspirin 80–100mg, statin (high-intensity), ACEI/ARB if HF/DM, beta-blocker
**Answer**: 二級預防要點：阿司匹林80–100mg、他汀類（高強度）、如有心衰/糖尿病加ACEI/ARB、β受體阻滯劑

**Examiner's Pearl**: GTN protocol: 1 spray SL → sit → if no relief after 5 min → 2nd spray → if no relief after 5 min → call 999
**Answer**: GTN方案：1噴舌下→坐下→5分鐘後無緩解→第2噴→5分鐘後無緩解→致電999

**Examiner's Pearl**: Atypical angina more common in women, DM, elderly: dyspnoea, fatigue, nausea (not classic chest pain)
**Answer**: 非典型心絞痛在女性、糖尿病患者、老年人中更常見：呼吸困難、疲勞、噁心（不是典型胸痛）

**Examiner's Pearl**: ACS red flags: rest pain >20min, pain at rest/minimal exertion, new-onset severe, accelerating → emergency
**Answer**: ACS紅旗：靜息痛>20分鐘、靜息/輕微活動痛、新發嚴重、加重→緊急就醫

**Examiner's Pearl**: Risk modification: LDL target <1.8 mmol/L (or >50% reduction) for established CAD
**Answer**: 風險控制：已確立CAD的LDL目標<1.8 mmol/L（或降低>50%）

## Station 1 — Management Interview

**Scenario**: Mr Kwok, 60M, smoker. Presents with central chest tightness radiating to his left arm when walking uphill. The pain stops after 5 minutes of rest. He is terrified he is having a heart attack right now. ECG in clinic is normal.

### Section 1 — Assessment (20%): 4P + BATHE + ICE
4P FRAMEWORK:
- Predisposing: Age 60, male, smoker (major cardiovascular risk factors).
- Precipitating: Physical exertion (walking uphill).
- Perpetuating: Continued smoking, potential underlying untreated hypertension/dyslipidaemia.
- Protective: Pain is relieved by rest, normal resting ECG, seeking immediate medical help.

BATHE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Background: "You mentioned getting this chest tightness when walking uphill. How long has this been happening, and how is it affecting your daily activities?"
- Affect: "How did you feel emotionally when the pain radiated to your arm?"
- Trouble: "What is the most frightening part of these episodes for you?"
- Handling: "When the pain happens, what exactly do you do to make it go away?"
- Empathy: "Chest pain is incredibly scary, especially when it goes down your arm. It is completely understandable that you are terrified of a heart attack."

ICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- Ideas: "What do you think is happening in your heart when you walk uphill?"
- Concerns: "You mentioned being terrified of a heart attack. Are you worried that your life is in immediate danger right now?"
- Expectations: "What were you hoping we could do today to figure out what is going on and keep you safe?"

### Section 2 — Problem Identification (10%)
PHYSICAL:
- Stable Angina (Ischaemic Heart Disease).
- Must explicitly rule out Acute Coronary Syndrome / Unstable Angina (pain at rest, pain lasting >20 mins, autonomic symptoms like sweating).

PSYCHOLOGICAL:
- Severe anxiety and fear of imminent death (myocardial infarction).

SOCIAL:
- Potential impact on occupation if it involves physical labor.
- Lifestyle factors: Smoking.

BARRIERS:
- Denial of the severity of smoking as a risk factor.
- Fear of invasive procedures (like angiograms).

### Section 3 — RAPRIOP Management (50%)
REASSURE:
"I have checked your ECG and your heart right now. You are NOT having a heart attack at this moment. You are safe right now. However, the symptoms you described are a warning sign from your heart."

ADVICE (EXACT SCRIPTS):
- "What you are experiencing is called Angina. The blood vessels supplying your heart muscle have become narrowed, likely due to smoking and cholesterol. When you rest, enough blood gets through. But when you walk uphill, your heart pumps harder, demands more oxygen, and the narrowed pipes can't deliver enough blood. That lack of oxygen causes the pain."
- "The most important thing you can do to stop this from turning into a real heart attack is to stop smoking immediately."
- "Avoid strenuous exertion like heavy lifting or running uphill until we get this fully investigated."

PRESCRIBE:
- "I will prescribe a GTN spray (Nitroglycerin) to put under your tongue. If you get the pain, stop walking, sit down, and use the spray. It opens up the blood vessels quickly."
- "I will also start you on a low-dose Aspirin to thin your blood and a Statin to stabilize the plaques in your arteries."

REFER:
- Urgent referral to Cardiology for further investigation (e.g., Treadmill Exercise Tolerance Test or CT Coronary Angiogram).

INVESTIGATE:
- Blood tests today: Fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile, renal function, and a baseline Troponin to be absolutely safe.

OBSERVE:
- Monitor frequency of angina attacks.

PLAN:
- Follow up in 1 week to review blood tests and ensure the Cardiology referral is processed.

SAFETY NET:
"This is extremely important: If you get chest pain while you are resting, if the pain lasts more than 15 minutes, or if the GTN spray does not take the pain away after 2 doses, call an ambulance immediately. That could be a heart attack."

### Section 4 — CIPS Communication Skills (20%)
CHUNK AND CHECK:
Explain the "narrowed pipes" analogy for angina, then ask, "Does it make sense why the pain only happens when you walk uphill and goes away when you rest?"

ICE:
Directly address his terror of a heart attack by clearly distinguishing between his current stable angina and an actual myocardial infarction.

PATIENT-CENTRED:
"I know this is scary news. Are you comfortable starting the Aspirin and Statin today to protect your heart while we wait for the specialist?"

SIGNPOSTING:
"First, let's talk about what the ECG shows and why you are safe right now, and then we will discuss the plan to investigate this further."

## Station 3 — FM Primary Care Consultation

**Scenario**: Patient presents with chest tightness when exerting himself (e.g., walking uphill). The pain radiates to his left arm but goes away with rest.

### Find Reason (×0.5)
OPEN QUESTION:
"You mentioned getting chest tightness when you walk uphill. Can you tell me exactly what it feels like when it happens?"

ACTIVE LISTENING:
Allow the patient to describe the angina episode without interrupting.

### Define Problem + Systems Review (×2.5)
SOCRATES FOR CHEST PAIN:
- Site: "Where exactly is the pain? Can you point to it?" (Usually central).
- Character: "Is it a sharp pain, or more like a heavy pressure or tightness?"
- Radiation: "Does the pain travel anywhere, like your jaw, neck, or left arm?"
- Time: "How long does it last? Does it stop as soon as you rest?"
- Exacerbating: "Does cold weather or eating a heavy meal make it happen faster?"

SCREEN FOR RED FLAGS (UNSTABLE ANGINA / MI - MUST ASK):
- "Have you ever had this pain while you were just sitting down or resting?"
- "Has the pain ever lasted longer than 15 or 20 minutes?"
- "Do you break out in a cold sweat, feel nauseous, or feel faint during the pain?"

SCREEN FOR RISK FACTORS:
- "Do you smoke?"
- "Do you have high blood pressure, high cholesterol, or diabetes?"
- "Is there a family history of heart disease?"

PHYSICAL EXAM:
- Check Blood Pressure and pulse.
- Auscultate heart for murmurs.
- Check peripheral pulses and for ankle edema.

### ICE (Ideas, Concerns, Expectations) (×0.5)
IDEAS:
"What do you think is causing this tightness in your chest?"

CONCERNS:
"Are you worried that you are having a heart attack?"

EXPECTATIONS:
"What were you hoping we could do to investigate this today?"

### Diagnosis & Explanation (×0.5)
DIAGNOSIS:
"Based on your description, you are experiencing Stable Angina. This is a warning sign from your heart."

EXPLANATION:
"The blood vessels supplying your heart have become narrowed. When you rest, enough blood gets through. But when you exert yourself, your heart needs more oxygen, and the narrowed pipes can't deliver it fast enough. That lack of oxygen causes the tightness."

CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
"Does that explanation make sense to you?"

### Management + RICE (×1.0)
REASSURE:
"Because the pain stops when you rest, you are not having a heart attack right now. But we need to take this seriously to prevent one in the future."

INVESTIGATE:
"I will do an ECG right now to check your heart rhythm, and arrange blood tests for cholesterol and diabetes. I will also refer you to a cardiologist for a treadmill test."

PLAN:
"I will prescribe a GTN spray. If you get the pain, stop, sit down, and spray it under your tongue to open the blood vessels. I will also start you on Aspirin to thin your blood."

ADVICE:
"You must stop smoking immediately—it is the best thing you can do for your heart. Avoid heavy lifting or strenuous exercise until you see the specialist."

SAFETY NET:
"If you get chest pain while resting, or if the GTN spray does not work after 5 minutes, call an ambulance immediately."
