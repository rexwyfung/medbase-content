# Anaphylaxis (過敏性休克)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology
### Clinical Definition of Anaphylaxis
* **Severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity** reaction
* Characterised by rapid onset with life-threatening airway, breathing, OR circulatory problems
* Usually associated with skin/mucosal changes

### HK ED Triage Category
* **Category 1 (Critical):** If active airway compromise, severe stridor, or unrecordable BP/shock
* **Category 2 (Emergency):** Stable airway/BP but clear systemic features needing immediate IM Adrenaline

### Local Epidemiology & Common Triggers
* **Food:** Seafood (shellfish highly common in HK), peanuts, tree nuts
* **Drugs:** **Penicillin (青黴素)**, cephalosporins, NSAIDs, contrast media
* **Venom:** Bee/wasp stings (膜翅目)
* **Idiopathic:** ~20% of adult cases in local clinics

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)
### IgE-Mediated Pathophysiology (Type I Hypersensitivity)
* Prior sensitisation to allergen → allergen cross-links **IgE antibodies** on mast cells/basophils
* Massive sudden degranulation → release of **histamine, tryptase, leukotrienes, prostaglandins**

### Systemic Pathophysiological Effects
* **Vasculature:** Profound vasodilation + ↑ capillary permeability → massive fluid shift → **distributive + hypovolaemic shock**
* **Respiratory:** Smooth muscle contraction → **bronchospasm**
* **Mucosal:** ↑ mucus secretion + submucosal edema → **angioedema (血管性水腫)**

### Non-IgE Mediated (Anaphylactoid) Pathophysiology
* Direct mast cell degranulation without IgE involvement
* Triggers: Opiates, radiocontrast media, vancomycin, physical stimuli (cold, exercise)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)
### Airway Findings
* **Swelling:** Lip, tongue, uvula, pharynx
* **Sounds:** Stridor, hoarseness, altered voice
* **Symptoms:** Sensation of "lump in throat", difficulty swallowing

### Breathing Findings
* **Respiratory effort:** Tachypnoea, accessory muscle use
* **Sounds:** Expiratory wheeze, silent chest (if severe bronchospasm)
* **Signs:** Cyanosis, ↓ SpO2, respiratory arrest

### Circulation Findings
* **Haemodynamics:** Tachycardia, profound hypotension
* **Perfusion:** Sweaty, clammy skin, prolonged CRT >2s
* **Conscious level:** Altered mental status secondary to ↓ cerebral perfusion, feeling of impending doom

### Skin & Mucosal Findings
* **Rash:** **Urticaria (蕁麻疹)** (hives), widespread erythema
* **Swelling:** Angioedema (periorbital, perioral)
* **Symptoms:** Severe pruritus (itching)

### Gastrointestinal Findings
* **Symptoms:** Crampy abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, severe diarrhoea

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)
### Table of Anaphylaxis Differentials
| Condition | Key Differentiating Features from Anaphylaxis |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Severe Asthma Exacerbation** | Wheeze +ve, but NO skin changes/urticaria, BP usually normal/high |
| **Septic Shock** | Gradual onset, fever/infection focus +ve, skin warm/flushed (early) |
| **Vasovagal Syncope** | Bradycardia, pallor, NO rash, rapidly resolves when supine |
| **ACEI-Induced Angioedema** | Isolated bradykinin-mediated swelling (lips/tongue), NO urticaria/wheeze |
| **Panic Attack** | Tachypnoea, normal SpO2, ↑ BP/HR, NO rash or true wheeze |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)
### Point-of-Care Bedside Tests
* **ECG:** Rule out myocardial ischaemia (Kounis syndrome), assess for arrhythmias
* **VBG/ABG:** Assess pH, lactate (↑ in shock), pO2, pCO2 (↑ if tiring/fatigue)
* **CBE (Bedside Glucose):** Rule out hypoglycaemia if altered mental status

### Formal Laboratory Investigations
* **Serum Mast Cell Tryptase (血清類胰蛋白酶):**
  * Sample 1: ASAP post-resuscitation
  * Sample 2: 1-2 hours post-onset (peak levels)
  * Sample 3: 24 hours post-resolution (baseline)
* **Routine Bloods:** FBC, U&E, LFT (baseline to assess multi-organ perfusion/AKI)

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms
### Immediate First-Line Drugs
* **Adrenaline (腎上腺素) (1:1000):** 0.5mg (0.5mL) IM stat
* Repeat IM Adrenaline q5mins if no clinical improvement

### Fluid Resuscitation Therapy
* **Crystalloid:** 0.9% NaCl 1-2L IV rapidly under pressure bag
* Repeat fluid boluses titrated to MAP >65 and CRT <2s

### Second-Line Adjunct Medications (Post-Stabilisation)
* **Antihistamine (H1):** IV/IM Chlorpheniramine 10mg
* **Corticosteroid:** IV Hydrocortisone 200mg OR IV Dexamethasone 8mg
* **Bronchodilator:** Nebulised Salbutamol 5mg (if ongoing wheeze)

### Refractory Shock & Special Scenarios Management
* **Refractory Hypotension:** IV Adrenaline infusion (1:10,000 or 1:1,000,000 conc.) titrated to MAP
* **Beta-Blocker Toxicity/Resistance:** IV Glucagon 1-2mg stat over 5 mins, followed by continuous infusion

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)
### IM Adrenaline Injection Technique
* **Site:** Mid-anterolateral thigh (Vastus lateralis muscle)
* **Mechanics:** 90-degree angle, long needle (blue/green) to reach muscle layer, inject deeply, hold for 10 seconds, massage site locally

### Advanced Airway Intubation Mechanics
* **Preparation:** Call for senior ED/Anaesthetics/ENT immediately
* **Equipment:** Prepare smaller ETT sizes (e.g., 6.0, 6.5) due to laryngeal edema, Video Laryngoscopy (VL) mandatory
* **Backup:** Surgical cricothyroidotomy kit open and on standby
* **Mechanics:** Early intubation before total airway closure; avoid blind instrumentation

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring
### World Allergy Organization (WAO) Diagnostic Criteria for Anaphylaxis
| Criterion | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Criterion 1** | Acute onset (mins-hrs) with skin/mucosal involvement **AND ≥1 of:** <br>a) Respiratory compromise<br>b) ↓ BP or associated symptoms (syncope) |
| **Criterion 2** | ≥2 of following post-exposure to *likely* allergen (mins-hrs):<br>a) Skin/mucosal tissue involvement<br>b) Respiratory compromise<br>c) ↓ BP or associated symptoms<br>d) Persistent GI symptoms (cramps, vomiting) |
| **Criterion 3** | ↓ BP post-exposure to *known* allergen for that patient (mins-hrs) |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications
### Observation & Disposition Criteria
* **Short Observation (6-8 hours):** Minor reactions responding rapidly to 1x IM Adrenaline
* **Admission Criteria (EMW/ICU):** 
  * Required >1 dose IM Adrenaline
  * Required IV fluid boluses >1L
  * History of severe asthma
  * High risk of biphasic reaction
  * Late evening/night presentation or poor social support

### Discharge Planning & Prescriptions
* **Auto-injector:** Prescribe **EpiPen (腎上腺素注射筆)** 0.3mg (adults), educate on technique
* **Meds:** PO Prednisolone (3-5 days) + PO Antihistamine (Cetirizine/Loratadine)
* **Follow-up:** Urgent referral to HA Allergy Clinic (免疫及過敏病科)
* **Advice:** Written anaphylaxis action plan, strict allergen avoidance

### Acute Complications
* **Biphasic Reaction (雙相反應):** Recurrence of symptoms without re-exposure (up to 72 hours later, peak at ~10 hours)
* **Kounis Syndrome:** Allergic myocardial infarction secondary to coronary vasospasm from mast cell degranulation
* **End-Organ Damage:** Hypoxic brain injury, cardiac arrest

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: Why do we give Adrenaline IM in the anterolateral thigh instead of the deltoid? Answer: Anterolateral thigh (vastus lateralis) has significantly higher blood flow, yielding faster peak plasma adrenaline concentrations compared to the deltoid or subcutaneous routes.
* Trap: Waiting for a rash to appear before diagnosing anaphylaxis. Pearl: 10-20% of severe anaphylaxis cases present with profound hypotension or bronchospasm without ANY cutaneous manifestations.
* Pitfall: Giving IV Adrenaline push (1:1000) for stable anaphylaxis. This can cause lethal ventricular arrhythmias or hypertensive crisis. IV Adrenaline is strictly reserved for cardiac arrest or refractory shock via controlled infusion.
* Viva Question: What is the mechanism of Glucagon in anaphylaxis patients on beta-blockers? Answer: Glucagon bypasses the beta-receptor, directly activating adenylate cyclase to ↑ intracellular cAMP, restoring inotropy and chronotropy independent of beta-adrenergic stimulation.
