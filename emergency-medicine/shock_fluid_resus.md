# Shock & Fluid Resuscitation (休克與輸液急救)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Shock
* **Shock (休克)**: State of acute circulatory failure → inadequate tissue oxygen delivery (DO2) to meet cellular oxygen consumption (VO2) → cellular hypoxia & dysoxia. 

### HK HA Triage Categories
* **Triage Category 1 (第一類危殆)**: Unstable vitals, actively crashing (e.g., unrecordable BP, extreme tachycardia). Target: Immediate.
* **Triage Category 2 (第二類危急)**: Borderline haemodynamics (e.g., HR >120, SBP <90) but maintaining airway/breathing. Target: <15 mins.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Most Common**: Septic shock (敗血性休克) (pneumonia, UTI, intra-abdominal infections).
* **2nd Most Common**: Cardiogenic shock (心因性休克) [Acute Myocardial Infarction (急性心肌梗塞), arrhythmias].
* **3rd Most Common**: Hypovolaemic shock (低血容量性休克) (GI bleeds, trauma).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Haemodynamic Equation & DO2
* **BP = CO x SVR** (Cardiac Output x Systemic Vascular Resistance)
* **CO = HR x SV** (Heart Rate x Stroke Volume)
* **DO2 Equation**: DO2 = CO x [(1.34 x Hb x SaO2) + (0.003 x PaO2)].

### The 4 Pillars of Shock
| Type | Primary Defect | CO | SVR | CVP/PCWP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypovolaemic** | ↓ Intravascular volume (↓ SV) | ↓ | ↑ | ↓ |
| **Cardiogenic** | Pump failure (↓ contractility) | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ |
| **Distributive** | Severe vasodilation (↓ SVR) | ↑ (early) / ↓ (late) | ↓↓ | ↓ |
| **Obstructive** | Extracardiac flow blockage | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ |

### Cellular Pathophysiology
* **Metabolic Shift**: Aerobic metabolism → anaerobic glycolysis.
* **Byproducts**: ↑ Lactic acid production → metabolic acidosis.
* **End Stage**: Failure of Na+/K+ ATP pump → cell membrane breakdown → apoptosis → Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Airway & Breathing Findings
* **Tachypnoea**: Rapid, shallow breathing (early compensatory sign).
* **Hypoxia**: ↓ SpO2 (especially in cardiogenic/septic/obstructive).

### Circulation & Perfusion Findings
* **Tachycardia**: HR >100 bpm (early compensation).
* **Hypotension**: SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg (LATE sign).
* **Capillary Refill Time (CRT)**: >2 seconds (prolonged).
* **Skin Findings**: Cold, clammy, pale (Hypovolaemic/Cardiogenic) vs Warm, flushed (Early Distributive).
* **Mottling**: Patchy skin discolouration (usually starts at knees, indicates severe microcirculatory failure).

### Disability & Neurological Findings
* **Altered Mental Status (AMS)**: Restlessness, anxiety, agitation (early) → confusion, lethargy, obtundation (late).

### Exposure & Renal Findings
* **Oliguria**: Urine output (UO) <0.5 ml/kg/hr.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Hypovolaemic Differentials
* **Haemorrhagic**: GI bleeding (variceal/peptic ulcer), major trauma, ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy (宮外孕), ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA).
* **Non-Haemorrhagic**: Severe Gastroenteritis (腸胃炎), Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA), burns, third-spacing (pancreatitis).

### Cardiogenic Differentials
* **Myopathic**: Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) (especially anterior wall or RV infarct), acute myocarditis.
* **Mechanical**: Acute mitral regurgitation (papillary muscle rupture), ventricular septal defect, severe aortic stenosis.
* **Arrhythmic**: Ventricular Tachycardia (VT), complete heart block, Atrial Fibrillation (AF) with Rapid Ventricular Response.

### Distributive Differentials
* **Septic Shock**: Sepsis (敗血症) with refractory hypotension.
* **Anaphylaxis**: Severe Type 1 hypersensitivity.
* **Neurogenic**: High cervical/thoracic spinal cord injury (loss of sympathetic tone).
* **Endocrine**: Adrenal crisis.

### Obstructive Differentials
* **Pulmonary**: Massive Pulmonary Embolism (肺栓塞).
* **Cardiac**: Cardiac Tamponade (心包填塞).
* **Thoracic**: Tension Pneumothorax (張力性氣胸).

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care & Bedside Tests
* **VBG/ABG**: Hallmark ↑ Lactate (乳酸) (>2 mmol/L), severe base deficit (BD < -4), metabolic acidosis.
* **ECG**: Ischaemia, arrhythmias, right heart strain (S1Q3T3).
* **RUSH Exam (Rapid Ultrasound in Shock)**: 
  * *Pump*: Pericardial effusion, RV strain, LV contractility.
  * *Tank*: IVC collapsibility (volume status), FAST (free fluid), Pleural fluid/pneumothorax.
  * *Pipes*: AAA, DVT.

### Formal Laboratory Tests
* **Haematology**: CBC (↑ WBC in sepsis, ↓ Hb in bleeding), Coagulation profile/INR/APTT (DIC screening), Type & Screen / Crossmatch (urgent).
* **Biochemistry**: RFT (AKI, electrolyte shifts), LFT (ischaemic hepatitis/"shock liver"), Trop/CK (AMI).
* **Microbiology**: Blood cultures x 2 sets (prior to antibiotics in sepsis).

### Imaging
* **CXR**: Cardiomegaly/pulmonary oedema (cardiogenic), widened mediastinum (aortic dissection), pneumothorax, pneumonia.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Fluid Resuscitation Strategy
* **Crystalloids First-Line**: Balanced solutions [Plasmalyte (勃脈力) or Hartmann's] preferred over Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) to avoid hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis.
* **Fluid Challenge**: Bolus 250-500ml rapid IV stat, re-assess response (CRT, HR, BP, IVC on POCUS).
* **Haemorrhagic Protocol**: Limit crystalloids (max 1L) → switch early to Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP) [1:1:1 ratio of PRBC : FFP : Platelets] to prevent dilutional coagulopathy.

### Vasopressors & Inotropes
* **Target**: MAP ≥ 65 mmHg. Administer via Central Line if possible (peripheral acceptable in emergencies temporarily).
* **1st Line Distributive/Septic**: Noradrenaline (去甲腎上腺素) (0.01 - 3 mcg/kg/min) → pure alpha-1 vasoconstriction with some beta-1.
* **1st Line Cardiogenic**: Dobutamine (多巴酚丁胺) (inotrope) OR Noradrenaline if severe hypotension.
* **1st Line Anaphylactic**: Adrenaline (腎上腺素) 0.5mg (1:1000) IM stat.
* **2nd Line Septic**: Add Vasopressin (血管加壓素) 0.03 U/min to spare Noradrenaline dose.

### Endpoints of Resuscitation
* **Macro-haemodynamics**: MAP ≥65, HR <100, UO ≥0.5 ml/kg/hr.
* **Micro-haemodynamics**: Normalising lactate (lactate clearance >20% in 2 hrs), normal CRT.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Large-Bore Peripheral IV Cannulation (PIVC)
* **Mechanics**: Insert 14G (Orange) or 16G (Grey) cannula into antecubital fossa. Ensure brisk flashback. 
* **Consent**: Verbal consent for emergent resuscitation.

### Intraosseous (IO) Access
* **Mechanics**: Locate proximal tibia (2cm medial, 1cm distal to tibial tuberosity) or proximal humerus. Prep with chlorhexidine. Use EZ-IO drill at 90 degrees. Drill until sudden "give". Remove stylet, flush with 10ml Normal Saline (painful, use 2% lignocaine flush in conscious patient).
* **Consent**: Implied consent in life-threatening emergencies.

### Central Venous Catheter (CVC) Insertion
* **Mechanics**: Internal Jugular (IJ) or Femoral vein preferred in ED. Ultrasound-guided. Seldinger technique: needle entry → guidewire insertion → remove needle → dilate tract over wire → insert triple-lumen catheter over wire → remove wire, flush lines, suture in place.
* **Consent**: Written consent if feasible; implied if critically crashing.

### Arterial Line Insertion
* **Mechanics**: Radial artery most common. Palpate pulse or use ultrasound. Angled needle insertion → flash of bright red pulsatile blood → advance wire → feed catheter → connect to transducer setup.
* **Consent**: Verbal or implied.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Shock Index (SI)
* **Calculation**: HR / SBP.
* **Normal**: 0.5 - 0.7.
* **Significance**: >0.9 suggests significant occult volume loss. >1.0 correlates with higher mortality and need for massive transfusion in trauma.

### qSOFA Score (Quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment)
* **Calculation**: 1 point each for:
  * Altered mental status (GCS <15)
  * Systolic blood pressure ≤100 mmHg
  * Respiratory rate ≥22 breaths/min
* **Significance**: Score ≥ 2 indicates high risk of poor outcome in suspected infection.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition 
* **Admission**: 100% admit. Immediate transfer to Intensive Care Unit (ICU) or High Dependency Unit (HDU) (深切治療部/加護病房) for invasive monitoring and vasopressor support.

### Acute Complications
* **Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS)**: Sequential failure of lungs, kidneys, liver.
* **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) (Acute Tubular Necrosis due to hypoperfusion).
* **Pulmonary**: Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) (capillary leak).
* **Haematological**: Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the earliest clinical sign of hypovolaemic shock? Answer: Tachycardia and narrowed pulse pressure; hypotension is a late and decompensated sign.
* Trap: A normal blood pressure means the patient is not in shock. Pearl: Look out for "Cryptic Shock" — normal BP but elevated lactate, oliguria, and altered sensorium. Compensatory mechanisms maintain BP initially.
* Pitfall: Vigorously pushing crystalloids in acute traumatic haemorrhage. Dilutional coagulopathy and hypothermia will worsen the "Lethal Triad". Switch to MTP (1:1:1 blood products) early and aim for permissive hypotension (SBP 80-90) until surgical control.
* Trap: Ordering a plain chest X-ray to diagnose a tension pneumothorax in a crashing shock patient. Pearl: Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis (tracheal deviation, distended neck veins, absent breath sounds); treat immediately with needle decompression/finger thoracostomy before any imaging.
* Viva Question: Why do we target a MAP of 65 mmHg? Answer: It is the minimum pressure required to maintain adequate autoregulation and perfusion to the brain and kidneys.
