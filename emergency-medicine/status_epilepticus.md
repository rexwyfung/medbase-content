# Status Epilepticus (癲癇重積狀態)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Status Epilepticus
* **Operational Definition:** Continuous clinical or electrographic seizure activity lasting **≥5 mins**, OR **≥2 discrete seizures** without complete recovery of consciousness in between.
* **Refractory Status Epilepticus (RSE):** Continued seizures despite adequate doses of initial benzodiazepine + 1 second-line antiepileptic drug (AED).
* **Super-Refractory Status Epilepticus:** Continues or recurs ≥24 hours after onset of anesthetic therapy.

### ED Triage Category for Status Epilepticus
* **Hong Kong Triage System:** **Category 1 (Critical - 危殆)**.
* Requires immediate zero-minute resuscitation room (R-room) activation.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* Bimodal distribution: Peaks in children <1 year and adults >60 years.
* Most common triggers in HA EDs: AED non-compliance, alcohol withdrawal, acute CNS infection (中樞神經系統感染), acute stroke (中風).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiology of Prolonged Seizures
* **Phase 1 (Compensation):** ↑ catecholamine release → ↑ HR, ↑ BP, ↑ cerebral blood flow (CBF) to match ↑ cerebral metabolic rate of oxygen (CMRO2).
* **Phase 2 (Decompensation - after ~30 mins):** Failure of autoregulation → ↓ BP, ↓ CBF → mismatch between O2 supply/demand → neuronal ischemia.
* **Receptor Failure:** Downregulation of inhibitory **GABA-A receptors**, upregulation of excitatory **NMDA/Glutamate receptors** → self-sustaining seizure loop.

### Systemic Physiological Consequences
* **Metabolic:** Severe lactic acidosis (乳酸性酸中毒), hyperkalemia (高鉀血症).
* **Respiratory:** Hypoxia, hypercapnia (due to chest wall muscle spasm/apnea).
* **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis (橫紋肌溶解症), hyperthermia (from intense muscle activity).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary Survey (ABCDE) Findings
* **Airway:** Trismus (牙關緊閉), pooling of secretions/blood (tongue bite).
* **Breathing:** Apnea, cyanosis, shallow respirations, noisy breathing (stridor/gurgling).
* **Circulation:** Tachycardia, initial hypertension (Phase 1) → hypotension (Phase 2).
* **Disability:** GCS <15 (usually unresponsive), continuous tonic-clonic movements, nystagmus (眼球震顫), pupillary dilation.
* **Exposure:** Hyperthermia, signs of trauma, medic alert bracelets, track marks (IVDU).

### Secondary Survey & Neurological Signs
* **Convulsive Signs:** Rhythmic jerking (clonic), sustained rigidity (tonic), focal twitching (e.g., facial).
* **Non-Convulsive Signs:** Subtle eye deviation, lip smacking, unresponsiveness without gross motor movements.
* **Physical Clues to Etiology:** Meningism (nuchal rigidity), hemiparesis (Todd's paralysis vs Stroke), lateral tongue bite (high specificity for true seizure), urinary/fecal incontinence.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Differential Diagnosis Table for Seizure-Like Activity
| Condition | Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Syncope (暈厥)** | Prodrome (sweating, pallor), rapid return to baseline, brief myoclonus (<10s). |
| **Hypoglycemia (低血糖)** | Sweating, tremors, rapid resolution with glucose. |
| **PNES (Psychogenic Non-Epileptic Seizure)** | Asynchronous movements, pelvic thrusting, eyes closed firmly, resistance to eye opening, normal pupillary light reflex. |
| **Arrhythmia (心律不整)** | Sudden collapse, absent pulses, preceded by palpitations. |
| **Eclampsia (子癇)** | Pregnant >20 weeks or <4 weeks postpartum, hypertension, proteinuria. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care Testing (POCT) & Bedside Adjuncts
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG):** Mandatory first test to rule out hypoglycemia.
* **Arterial/Venous Blood Gas (ABG/VBG):** Look for profound metabolic acidosis, hypoxia, hypercapnia, lactate clearance.
* **ECG:** Rule out pro-arrhythmic conditions (long QT, TCAs toxicity - widened QRS).

### Laboratory Investigations
* **Renal & Liver:** U&E (Na+, K+, Ca2+, Mg2+), Cr (rule out uremia), LFTs.
* **Muscle Enzymes:** CK (rule out rhabdomyolysis).
* **Toxicology & Pharmacology:** Salicylate, paracetamol levels, urine toxicology, serum AED levels (e.g., Phenytoin, Valproate).
* **Infection:** CBP, CRP, blood cultures (if febrile).

### Neuroimaging & Specialized Tests
* **CT Brain (Non-Contrast):** Rule out acute intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), mass effect, large stroke.
* **EEG (腦電圖):** Essential for diagnosing Non-Convulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE) and titrating anesthetic drips in ICU (not usually available in ED R-room).
* **Lumbar Puncture (LP):** If CNS infection suspected (post-CT to rule out ↑ ICP).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Timeline-Based Resuscitation Algorithm (0-5 mins)
* Secure ABCs, establish IV access.
* Administer empirical Thiamine 100mg IV + 50ml 50% Dextrose if malnourished/alcoholic + hypoglycemic.
* **1st Line (Benzodiazepines):** 
  * Lorazepam (勞拉西泮) IV 4mg (push over 2 mins) → repeat once at 5 mins if continuing.
  * *OR* Diazepam (地西泮) IV 10-20mg.
  * *OR* Midazolam (咪達唑侖) IM 10mg (if no IV access).

### Second-Line Antiepileptic Therapy (20-40 mins)
* Indicated if seizures persist after BZD doses. 
* **Levetiracetam (Keppra):** IV 60mg/kg (max 4500mg) over 15 mins. (Preferred in HA due to safety profile).
* **Phenytoin (苯妥英):** IV 20mg/kg at ≤50mg/min (requires cardiac monitoring for arrhythmias/hypotension).
* **Sodium Valproate:** IV 40mg/kg (max 3000mg) over 10 mins. (Avoid in women of childbearing age or liver failure).

### Refractory Status Epilepticus Medical Management (>40 mins)
| Drug | Dosing Strategy | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Propofol (異丙酚)** | Bolus 1-2mg/kg → Infusion 2-10 mg/kg/hr | Risk of Propofol Infusion Syndrome (PRIS), hypotension. |
| **Midazolam** | Bolus 0.2mg/kg → Infusion 0.05-2 mg/kg/hr | Tachyphylaxis, prolonged clearance in renal/liver failure. |
| **Thiopentone** | Bolus 2-7mg/kg → Infusion 3-5 mg/kg/hr | Hypotension, immunosuppression, accumulates in fat. |

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Airway Management & Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) Mechanics
* **Preparation:** Pre-oxygenate with BVM/NRB. Prepare vasopressors (push-dose adrenaline/metaraminol) as induction will cause profound hypotension.
* **Induction:** Propofol or Thiopentone (adds burst suppression properties).
* **Paralysis:** Rocuronium 1-1.2 mg/kg. *Note: Suxamethonium contraindicated if prolonged seizure >48h (risk of lethal hyperkalemia from rhabdomyolysis).*
* **Mechanics:** Direct/Video laryngoscopy. Place ETT. Inflate cuff. Confirm capnography (ETCO2).
* **Post-Intubation:** Ensure continuous sedation infusion (Propofol/Midazolam). Paralysis masks physical seizures; continuous EEG monitoring is mandatory once in ICU.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Status Epilepticus Severity Score (STESS)
| Variable | Criteria | Score |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Age** | <65 years <br> ≥65 years | 0 <br> 1 |
| **History** | Previous seizures <br> No previous seizures | 0 <br> 1 |
| **Seizure Type** | Simple partial, complex partial, absence <br> Generalized convulsive <br> Non-convulsive in coma | 0 <br> 1 <br> 2 |
| **Consciousness** | Alert, somnolent, or confused <br> Stuporous or comatose | 0 <br> 1 |
* **Interpretation:** Score ≥3 indicates high risk of poor outcome/mortality. Warrants aggressive ICU management.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### ED Disposition Criteria
* **Discharge:** Patients with brief, self-terminating breakthrough seizures, return to baseline GCS 15, known epilepsy, normal labs. Outpatient follow-up.
* **Admit Medical Ward:** First presentation seizure, prolonged post-ictal state, complex partial status.
* **Admit ICU:** Refractory status epilepticus requiring intubation, continuous anesthetic infusions, or hemodynamic instability.

### Acute Neurological & Systemic Complications
* **Neurological:** Permanent hypoxic-ischemic brain injury, cerebral edema, recurrent seizures.
* **Pulmonary:** Aspiration pneumonitis/pneumonia, neurogenic pulmonary edema.
* **Renal:** Acute kidney injury (AKI) 2° to rhabdomyolysis and myoglobinuria.
* **Orthopedic:** Posterior shoulder dislocations, vertebral compression fractures (due to severe muscle contractions).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls 

* Viva Question: How fast can you push IV Phenytoin and why? Answer: Maximum 50mg/min. Faster administration causes severe hypotension and lethal arrhythmias due to the propylene glycol diluent.
* Trap: Treating a pregnant female with eclamptic seizures using standard Benzodiazepines. Pearl: The definitive first-line treatment for eclampsia is IV Magnesium Sulfate (4g over 10-15 mins), followed by urgent delivery of the fetus.
* Pitfall: Paralysing an un-sedated status epilepticus patient to stop the shaking. This only stops the physical manifestation (motor unit activity) but the brain continues to seize, leading to permanent neuronal damage (Non-Convulsive Status). Always ensure deep sedation alongside paralysis.
* Viva Question: What is the most reliable clinical sign distinguishing a true seizure from a psychogenic non-epileptic seizure (PNES)? Answer: A lateral tongue bite has near 100% specificity for true generalized tonic-clonic seizures, whereas anterior tongue bites or no bites suggest PNES.
