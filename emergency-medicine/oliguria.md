# Oliguria (少尿)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Oliguria & Anuria
* **Oliguria (少尿):** Urine output (UO) < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6 consecutive hours OR < 400 mL/day in adults
* **Anuria (無尿):** UO < 50 mL/day
* **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI - 急性腎損傷):** Sudden ↓ in renal function, marked by ↑ serum creatinine (Cr) or ↓ UO

### Hong Kong HA ED Triage Categories
* **Category 1 (Critical):** Cardiac arrest, peri-arrest, severe shock, severe respiratory distress (e.g., flash pulmonary edema 2° to anuric renal failure)
* **Category 2 (Emergency):** Hyperkalemia (高鉀血症) with ECG changes, fluid overload with respiratory compromise, severe sepsis
* **Category 3 (Urgent):** Stable hemodynamics but anuria/oliguria, urinary retention with severe pain
* **Category 4/5 (Semi-urgent/Non-urgent):** Mild oliguria, normal vitals, usually ↓ oral intake

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Common Demographics:** Elderly, nursing home residents (OAH), poorly controlled DM/HTN
* **Common Triggers:** Sepsis (UTI, chest infection), polypharmacy (NSAIDs, ACEi/ARBs, diuretics), Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH - 良性前列腺增生)

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pre-Renal Pathophysiology
* **Mechanism:** ↓ renal perfusion pressure → ↓ glomerular filtration rate (GFR)
* **Pathophysiology:** Baroreceptors detect ↓ stretch → ↑ Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) & sympathetic tone → Na+/H2O retention (concentrated urine, low urine Na+)
* **Causes:** Hypovolemia (hemorrhage, GI loss), hypotension (sepsis, cardiogenic shock), renal artery stenosis

### Intrinsic Renal Pathophysiology
* **Mechanism:** Direct damage to renal parenchyma (tubules, glomerulus, interstitium, vasculature)
* **Pathophysiology:** Tubular necrosis/obstruction by casts → back-leak of filtrate → ↓ GFR
* **Causes:** Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) (ischemic or nephrotoxic), Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN), Glomerulonephritis (GN), Rhabdomyolysis (橫紋肌溶解症)

### Post-Renal Pathophysiology
* **Mechanism:** Obstruction of urinary outflow tract
* **Pathophysiology:** Back-pressure transmits to Bowman's capsule → ↑ hydrostatic pressure within Bowman's space → opposes capillary filtration pressure → ↓ GFR
* **Causes:** Urethral stricture, BPH, neurogenic bladder, bilateral ureteric calculi, pelvic malignancy

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Airway & Breathing Findings
* **Tachypnea:** Kussmaul breathing (deep, rapid) indicating severe metabolic acidosis
* **Auscultation:** Bibasal fine crepitations indicating pulmonary edema
* **Oxygenation:** Hypoxia 2° to fluid overload

### Circulation & Fluid Status Findings
* **Hypovolemia (Pre-renal):** Tachycardia, hypotension, dry mucous membranes, ↓ capillary refill, flat jugular venous pressure (JVP)
* **Hypervolemia (Renal/Post-renal):** ↑ JVP, peripheral pitting edema, S3 gallop, hypertension
* **Arrhythmias:** Irregular pulse (bradycardia, sine wave) 2° to severe hyperkalemia

### Abdominal & Genitourinary Findings
* **Palpation:** Palpable, tender suprapubic mass (distended bladder)
* **Kidneys:** Ballotable flank masses (polycystic kidneys, severe hydronephrosis)
* **Genitalia:** Phimosis, paraphimosis, meatal stenosis
* **DRE (Digital Rectal Exam):** Enlarged prostate, hard nodular prostate, fecal impaction, saddle anesthesia (Cauda Equina)

### Neurological & Cutaneous Findings
* **Uremia:** Asterixis (flapping tremor), altered mental status (uremic encephalopathy), myoclonus
* **Skin:** Uremic frost, palpable purpura (vasculitis), livedo reticularis (cholesterol emboli), petechiae

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Differential Diagnosis Matrix

| Category | Primary Pathologies | Critical Life Threats to Rule Out |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-Renal** | Dehydration, GI loss, 3rd spacing | Hemorrhagic shock, Septic shock, Cardiogenic shock |
| **Intrinsic** | ATN, AIN, Glomerulonephritis | Rhabdomyolysis, TTP/HUS, Severe systemic vasculitis |
| **Post-Renal** | BPH, stricture, blocked catheter | Cauda Equina Syndrome (馬尾綜合症), Ruptured AAA |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Bedside / Point-of-Care (POC) Tests
* **ECG (12-lead):** Look for hyperkalemia (peaked T waves, flat P waves, wide QRS, sine wave)
* **Bladder Scan:** Quantify post-void residual (PVR) volume (>300-400ml indicates retention)
* **VBG/ABG:** Acid-base status (metabolic acidosis), rapid K+, lactate, ionized Ca2+

### Laboratory Blood Investigations
* **Renal Function:** U&E, Cr, Urea (monitor ↑ Cr and ↑ K+)
* **Hematology:** CBC (sepsis, anemia in HUS/TTP), Clotting profile
* **Special Tests (if intrinsic suspected):** CK (Rhabdomyolysis), Troponin, Bone profile (Ca/PO4)

### Urine Investigations
* **Urinalysis (Dipstick):** Blood (stones, GN), Leukocytes/Nitrites (UTI), Protein (GN)
* **Microscopy:** Muddy brown casts (ATN), RBC casts (GN), WBC casts (AIN)
* **Chemistry:** Spot Urine Na+ (< 20 mmol/L = pre-renal, > 40 mmol/L = ATN), Urine osmolality

### Formal Imaging
* **Ultrasound KUB:** Assess for bilateral hydronephrosis, renal size, structural anomalies
* **CT KUB (Non-contrast):** Identify exact location of obstructing ureteric calculi

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Severe Hyperkalemia (高鉀血症) Resuscitation
* **Myocardial Stabilization:** 10ml 10% Calcium Gluconate IV over 2-5 mins
* **Intracellular Shift:**
* **Insulin/Dextrose:** 10 units Actrapid + 50ml 50% Dextrose IV stat
* **Salbutamol:** 10-20mg nebulized
* **Elimination:** Resonium A (calcium polystyrene sulfonate) 15g PO/PR, consider urgent hemodialysis

### Pre-Renal Volume Resuscitation
* **Fluid Challenge:** 250-500ml Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) or Hartmann's IV rapid bolus
* **Reassessment:** Monitor UO response, lung bases, JVP
* **Shock Protocol:** If unresponsive to fluids, initiate Vasopressors (Norepinephrine)

### Fluid Overload / Pulmonary Edema Management
* **Respiratory Support:** Sit patient up, high-flow O2, CPAP / BiPAP
* **Vasodilators:** IV/SL Nitroglycerin (if BP tolerates)
* **Diuretics:** IV Furosemide (Lasix) 40-80mg (ONLY if post-renal obstruction ruled out and patient is euvolemic/hypervolemic)

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Urethral Catheterization (Foley) Mechanics
* **Preparation:** Aseptic technique, sterile drapes, instill 11ml Lignocaine 2% gel into urethra (wait 3-5 mins)
* **Insertion:** Hold penis at 90°, insert 16F/18F Foley catheter gently until Y-junction reaches meatus
* **Confirmation:** Observe urine flashback
* **Anchoring:** Inflate balloon with 10ml sterile water, gently retract until resistance felt
* **Consent/Risks:** Trauma/false passage, infection (CAUTI), hematuria, post-obstructive diuresis

### Suprapubic Catheterization (SPC) Mechanics
* **Preparation:** Confirm distended bladder (clinically/US). Clean skin, inject local anesthetic 2 finger-breadths above symphysis pubis
* **Incision/Puncture:** Small scalpel stab incision, insert SPC trocar vertically or angled slightly inferiorly
* **Insertion:** Remove trocar inner stylet, pass catheter, confirm urine flow, inflate balloon
* **Consent/Risks:** Bowel perforation, hematuria, infection

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### KDIGO Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) Staging Criteria

| Stage | Serum Creatinine (Cr) Criteria | Urine Output (UO) Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | ↑ Cr ≥ 26.5 µmol/L OR 1.5–1.9x baseline | < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6–12 hrs |
| **2** | ↑ Cr 2.0–2.9x baseline | < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for ≥ 12 hrs |
| **3** | ↑ Cr 3.0x baseline OR Cr ≥ 353.6 µmol/L OR Initiation of RRT | < 0.3 mL/kg/hr for ≥ 24 hrs OR Anuria ≥ 12 hrs |

### Criteria for Urgent Dialysis (AEIOU)
| Acronym | Indication |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | **A**cidosis (Severe metabolic acidosis, pH < 7.1 refractory to medical tx) |
| **E** | **E**lectrolytes (Severe hyperkalemia refractory to medical tx) |
| **I** | **I**ntoxications (Toxins: Salicylates, Lithium, Isopropanol, Methanol, Ethylene glycol) |
| **O** | **O**verload (Fluid overload / Pulmonary edema refractory to diuretics) |
| **U** | **U**remia (Uremic pericarditis, uremic encephalopathy) |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Admission Criteria
* **General Medical (M&G) Ward:** Stable AKI, mild electrolyte derangement responding to therapy, UTIs requiring IV Abx
* **Surgical / Urology Ward:** Obstructive uropathy, blocked SPC/nephrostomy, intractable hematuria
* **ICU/HDU:** Hemodynamic instability, requirement for vasopressors, refractory hyperkalemia/acidosis requiring continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), severe pulmonary edema

### Acute Life-Threatening Complications
* **Cardiac Arrest:** 2° to hyperkalemia (VF/Asystole)
* **Post-Obstructive Diuresis:** Massive polyuria following relief of obstruction → severe dehydration and hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia
* **Uremic Pericarditis:** Can progress to cardiac tamponade

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* **Viva Question:** What is the first investigation in an anuric patient? Answer: Bladder scan. Always rule out post-renal obstruction first as it is instantly reversible with a catheter.
* **Viva Question:** How do you differentiate pre-renal from intrinsic AKI based on urine? Answer: Urine Na+ <20 mmol/L suggests pre-renal (kidneys conserving Na+/fluid), while Urine Na+ >40 mmol/L suggests intrinsic ATN (tubules damaged, cannot reabsorb Na+).
* **Trap:** Giving IV Furosemide to "kickstart" the kidneys in a hypotensive, oliguric patient. Pearl: Diuretics are harmful in pre-renal AKI. First-line is fluid resuscitation to restore renal perfusion. Only use Furosemide for overt volume overload.
* **Pitfall:** Decompressing a chronically distended bladder rapidly without monitoring. Post-obstructive diuresis can lead to profound hypovolemia and electrolyte collapse. Admit for strict fluid balance and replace matched losses (e.g., 0.45% Saline).
* **Pitfall:** Waiting for formal lab potassium results before treating a patient with sine wave ECG. The ECG is a real-time monitor of potassium toxicity; treat immediately with IV Calcium Gluconate.
