# Sepsis & Septic Shock (敗血症與敗血性休克)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Sepsis-3 Definition Criteria
* **Sepsis (敗血症):** Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection.
* **Septic Shock (敗血性休克):** Sepsis + vasopressor requirement to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg + serum lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation.
* **Organ Dysfunction:** Defined as acute ↑ in SOFA score ≥2 points.

### Hong Kong ED Triage Categories
* **Category 1 (Critical - Resuscitation):** Septic shock with unrecordable BP, profound altered mental status (AMS), or severe respiratory distress.
* **Category 2 (Emergency):** Sepsis with abnormal vitals (SBP <90, HR >120, SpO2 <92%) but not peri-arrest.
* **Category 3 (Urgent):** Stable vitals but high-risk features (e.g., elderly, immunocompromised, fever with rigors).

### Local Epidemiology & Common Pathogens
* **Prevalence:** Major cause of ICU admission and ED mortality in HK.
* **Common Sources:** Respiratory (Pneumonia - 肺炎), Genitourinary (UTI - 尿道炎), Intra-abdominal (Biliary/Bowel - 腹腔感染), Skin/Soft Tissue (Cellulitis - 蜂窩組織炎).
* **Common Pathogens (HK):** *E. coli* (ESBL prevalence ↑), *K. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus* (MRSA), *S. pneumoniae*.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Immune Cascade & Cytokine Storm
* **Trigger:** Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (PAMPs) bind to Toll-Like Receptors (TLRs) on macrophages/monocytes.
* **Cascade:** Massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6).
* **Result:** Systemic inflammatory response out of proportion to local infection.

### Endothelial Dysfunction & Distributive Shock
* **Vasodilation:** ↑ Nitric oxide (NO) production → smooth muscle relaxation → profound severe systemic vasodilation (↓ SVR).
* **Capillary Leak:** Endothelial tight junctions separate → intravascular fluid shifts to interstitium → profound absolute and relative hypovolemia.

### Microcirculatory Failure & Coagulopathy
* **Pro-coagulant State:** Cytokines induce Tissue Factor (TF) expression + ↓ natural anticoagulants (Protein C, Antithrombin).
* **Impaired Fibrinolysis:** ↑ PAI-1 → ↓ breakdown of clots.
* **Microvascular Thrombosis:** Fibrin deposition in microvasculature → Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC - 彌漫性血管內凝血) → tissue hypoxia (Type A Lactic Acidosis).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Airway & Breathing Assessment Findings
* **Airway:** Usually patent unless altered sensorium causes collapse.
* **Breathing:** Tachypnea (earliest sign of impending shock), accessory muscle use, hypoxemia, focal crepitations/bronchial breath sounds (pneumonia source).

### Circulation Assessment Findings
* **Early (Warm) Shock:** Tachycardia, bounding pulses, warm/flushed extremities, widened pulse pressure.
* **Late (Cold) Shock:** Hypotension, thready pulses, cold/clammy extremities, mottled skin (Livedo reticularis - 網狀青斑), delayed capillary refill (>2s).

### Disability & Neurological Findings
* **Consciousness:** Confusion, agitation, lethargy, coma (Septic Encephalopathy).
* **Localizing Signs:** Neck stiffness, photophobia, positive Kernig/Brudzinski signs (Meningitis source).

### Exposure & Source-Specific Survey Findings
* **Temperature:** High fever with rigors OR hypothermia (poor prognostic sign, especially in elderly).
* **Abdomen:** Tenderness, guarding, rigidity, Murphy's sign +ve, renal angle tenderness.
* **Skin/Joints:** Erythema, fluctuance, purpura fulminans / petechiae (Meningococcemia), hot swollen joint.
* **Devices:** Erythema or pus at CVC sites, urinary catheters, surgical wounds.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Shock Differentiation Table
| Shock Type | Classic History/Exam | Hemodynamic Profile |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Septic (Distributive)** | Fever, infection source, bounding pulses early | ↓ SVR, ↑ or N CO, ↓ CVP, ↓ PCWP |
| **Cardiogenic (心源性休克)** | Chest pain, JVP ↑, basal crackles, cold extremities | ↑ SVR, ↓ CO, ↑ CVP, ↑ PCWP |
| **Hypovolemic (低血容量性休克)** | Trauma, GI bleed, severe vomiting/diarrhea, flat JVP | ↑ SVR, ↓ CO, ↓ CVP, ↓ PCWP |
| **Obstructive (阻塞性休克)** | Pleuritic pain, sudden SOB, muffled heart sounds | ↑ SVR, ↓ CO, ↑ CVP, Variable PCWP |
| **Anaphylactic (過敏性休克)** | Allergen exposure, stridor, wheeze, urticaria | ↓ SVR, ↑ or N CO, ↓ CVP, ↓ PCWP |

### Sepsis Mimics (Systemic Inflammatory States)
* **Acute Pancreatitis (急性胰臟炎):** Severe epigastric pain radiating to back, ↑ Amylase/Lipase.
* **Thyroid Storm (甲狀腺風暴):** Goiter, exophthalmos, hyperreflexia, out-of-proportion tachycardia.
* **Toxic Shock Syndrome (中毒性休克綜合症):** Desquamating rash, tampons/wound packing history.

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care Testing (POCT) & Bedside Scans
* **Blood Gas (VBG/ABG):** ↑ Lactate (>2 mmol/L = tissue hypoperfusion), metabolic acidosis, respiratory alkalosis (compensation).
* **Glucometer:** Hyperglycemia (stress response) or Hypoglycemia (liver failure/depletion).
* **ECG:** Sinus tachycardia, rule out STEMI.
* **POCUS (RUSH Exam):** Hyperdynamic LV (early) or globally ↓ EF (late septic cardiomyopathy), flat IVC (hypovolemia), rule out B-lines/effusions/DVT/AAA.

### Formal Laboratory Investigations
* **Hematology:** CBC (leukocytosis >12 or leukopenia <4, left shift, ↓ platelets), Coagulation profile (↑ PT/APTT, ↓ Fibrinogen, ↑ D-dimer in DIC).
* **Biochemistry:** LRFT (↑ Cr/Urea, ↑ Bilirubin/ALT/AST), CRP, Procalcitonin (↑ specific to bacterial infection).
* **Microbiology (Pan-culture before Abx):** Blood C/ST (2 sets from different sites), Urine C/ST, Sputum C/ST, Wound swab.

### Formal Imaging Studies
* **Chest X-ray (CXR):** Consolidation, pleural effusion, ARDS (bilateral infiltrates).
* **CT Abdomen/Pelvis:** Visceral abscess, bowel ischemia, obstructive uropathy, perforated viscus.
* **CT Brain:** Rule out intracranial mass before LP if AMS/focal signs present.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Surviving Sepsis Campaign "Hour-1 Bundle" (Sepsis Six)
* **Measure:** Serum lactate (remeasure if initial >2 mmol/L).
* **Obtain:** Blood cultures x2 sets BEFORE antibiotics.
* **Administer (Abx):** Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics.
* **Administer (Fluid):** Rapid crystalloid bolus (30 mL/kg) for hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L.
* **Administer (Vaso):** Vasopressors if hypotensive during/after fluid resuscitation to maintain MAP ≥65.
* **Oxygenate:** Titrate high-flow O2 to maintain SpO2 94-98%.

### Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation Regimen
* **Agent:** Balanced crystalloids (Plasmalyte/Hartmann's) preferred over 0.9% Normal Saline (prevents hyperchloremic non-anion gap metabolic acidosis).
* **Dose:** 30 mL/kg actual body weight IV rapidly within first 3 hours.
* **Assessment:** Dynamic fluid responsiveness testing (Passive Leg Raise test or IVC ultrasound variability) before further boluses.

### Vasoactive Drug Therapy Algorithms
* **1st Line:** Noradrenaline (去甲腎上腺素) IV infusion 0.01-3.0 mcg/kg/min (Primary α-1 agonist).
* **2nd Line:** Vasopressin (血管加壓素) IV infusion 0.03 units/min (Add if escalating Noradrenaline fails to achieve MAP >65).
* **Inotropic Support:** Adrenaline (腎上腺素) or Dobutamine (多巴酚丁胺) ONLY if evidence of myocardial dysfunction (↓ CO) with adequate preload.
* **Corticosteroids:** IV Hydrocortisone 200mg/day (continuous or 50mg Q6H) ONLY if shock is refractory to adequate fluids + vasopressors.

### Hong Kong HA Empirical Antibiotic Protocols (Adults)
* **Unknown Source / Community Acquired:** IV Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) 1.2g Q8H.
* **Suspected Pseudomonas / Hospital Acquired:** IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin) 4.5g Q8H.
* **Penicillin Allergy:** IV Cefuroxime (if mild) or IV Levofloxacin / Aztreonam.
* **Suspected MRSA:** Add IV Vancomycin 15-20mg/kg.
* **Suspected Anaerobes (GI/Pelvic):** Add IV Metronidazole 500mg Q8H.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Central Venous Catheter (CVC) Insertion Mechanics
* **Setup:** Strict aseptic technique (gown, mask, cap, full drape).
* **Access:** Ultrasound-guided internal jugular or femoral vein.
* **Technique (Seldinger):** 
  * Infiltrate local anesthetic.
  * Puncture vein with introducer needle under dynamic US guidance.
  * Insert guidewire through needle.
  * Remove needle, make skin nick, advance dilator over wire.
  * Remove dilator, advance multi-lumen catheter over wire.
  * Remove wire, flush ports, suture to skin.
* **Consent Elements:** Risk of pneumothorax (if IJ/Subclavian), arterial puncture, bleeding, infection, arrhythmias.

### Arterial Line Insertion Mechanics
* **Setup:** Aseptic technique, wrist extended on roll.
* **Access:** Radial artery.
* **Technique:**
  * Palpate pulse or use US guidance.
  * Insert 20G catheter-over-needle at 30-45 degree angle until pulsatile blood return.
  * Drop angle to 10 degrees, advance wire/cannula into artery.
  * Remove needle, connect to rigid pressure tubing and transducer system.
* **Consent Elements:** Risk of bleeding, hematoma, thrombosis, distal ischemia, pseudoaneurysm.

### Source Control Principles (General Mechanics)
* **Abscess:** Incision and drainage (I&D) with loculation breakdown and packing.
* **Infected Devices:** Physical removal of CVC, urinary catheters, or pacemakers.
* **Surgical Referrals:** Laparotomy for perforated viscus, ureteric stenting/nephrostomy for pyonephrosis.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### qSOFA Score (Quick SOFA) - ED Bedside Triage Tool
| Criteria | Value | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Respiratory Rate** | ≥ 22 breaths/min | 1 |
| **Mental Status** | Altered (GCS <15) | 1 |
| **Systolic BP** | ≤ 100 mmHg | 1 |
* **Interpretation:** Score ≥2 predicts poor outcome/high mortality. Does NOT define sepsis, but prompts intensive workup/monitoring.

### SOFA Score Components (ICU Mortality Prediction)
| Organ System | Parameter Measured |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Respiratory** | PaO2/FiO2 ratio (↓) |
| **Coagulation** | Platelets (↓) |
| **Liver** | Bilirubin (↑) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypotension / Vasopressor requirement |
| **CNS** | GCS score (↓) |
| **Renal** | Creatinine (↑) or Urine Output (↓) |
* **Interpretation:** Sepsis-3 defined as acute change of ≥2 points from baseline.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### ED Disposition Criteria
* **ICU/HDU Admission:** Septic shock requiring vasopressors, respiratory failure requiring intubation/NIV, multi-organ failure.
* **Medical/Surgical Ward:** Hemodynamically stable sepsis responding rapidly to initial ED fluid challenge, single organ involvement.
* **Discharge:** RARE. Only if entirely stable, clear source (e.g., simple UTI), tolerant of oral fluids/Abx, reliable follow-up, and normal lactate.

### Acute Pathophysiological Complications
* **ARDS (Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome):** Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema requiring low tidal volume ventilation.
* **AKI (Acute Kidney Injury):** Acute tubular necrosis secondary to hypoperfusion.
* **DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation):** Bleeding + thrombosis.
* **MODS (Multiple Organ Dysfunction Syndrome):** Progressive, sequential failure of ≥2 organ systems.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate early septic shock from hypovolemic shock? Answer: Early septic shock features bounding pulses, warm extremities, and widened pulse pressure due to systemic vasodilation (low SVR). Hypovolemic shock presents with thready pulses, cold extremities, and narrow pulse pressure due to compensatory vasoconstriction (high SVR).
* Viva Question: What is the mechanism behind elevated lactate in sepsis? Answer: Type A lactic acidosis occurs due to tissue hypoxia from microvascular thrombosis and hypotension. Type B occurs due to catecholamine-driven stimulation of the Na+/K+ pump increasing glycolysis, and impaired hepatic clearance.
* Trap: Waiting for blood culture results or lumbar puncture before giving antibiotics. Pearl: Every hour delay in antibiotics in septic shock increases mortality by ~8%. Draw blood cultures rapidly, but if difficult, administer empiric IV antibiotics immediately regardless.
* Pitfall: Using Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) for massive fluid resuscitation. Explanation: Large volumes of 0.9% NaCl cause hyperchloremic non-anion gap metabolic acidosis, which can worsen hemodynamics and renal perfusion. Use balanced crystalloids (Plasmalyte/Hartmann's) instead.
* Pitfall: Assuming a normal body temperature rules out sepsis. Explanation: Elderly, immunocompromised, or uremic patients often fail to mount a fever and may present with hypothermia, which actually carries a higher mortality risk than fever.
