# CPR, BLS & ACLS (心肺復甦、基本及進階生命支援術)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Cardiac Arrest (心搏驟停)
* **Definition:** Abrupt cessation of effective cardiac mechanical activity → absence of circulating blood flow → global ischemia.
* **Clinical Triad:** Unresponsiveness, absent normal breathing (apnea/gasping), absent central pulses.

### Hong Kong ED Triage Category
* **Triage:** Category 1 (Critical/Resuscitation) - Immediate (0 minutes) assessment & intervention.

### Local Epidemiology & Demographics
* **Incidence:** ~6,000-8,000 Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrests (OHCA) annually in HK.
* **Survival to Discharge:** Low (~2-3% overall in HK); ↑ survival for witnessed arrest, bystander CPR, & initial shockable rhythm (VF/pVT).
* **Common Causes:** Coronary artery disease (most common in adults), asphyxia, trauma, drowning (pediatrics/young adults).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiology of Cardiac Arrest
* **Ischemic Cascade:** Zero blood flow → ↓ cellular ATP → failure of Na+/K+ pumps → cellular depolarization → intracellular Ca2+ influx → cell death.
* **Three-Phase Model of Resuscitation:**
  * **Electrical Phase (0-4 mins):** Heart maintains adequate ATP for rhythmic electrical activity; highly responsive to defibrillation.
  * **Circulatory Phase (4-10 mins):** ATP depletion → myocardium shifts to anaerobic metabolism → lactic acidosis; requires CPR to replenish myocardial O2 before shock is effective.
  * **Metabolic Phase (>10 mins):** Global ischemia → profound cellular injury, apoptosis, reperfusion injury (if ROSC achieved); ↓ survival probability.

### Pathological Mechanisms of Arrest Rhythms
* **Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) (心室顫動):** Disorganized, chaotic electrical re-entry circuits → no coordinated ventricular contraction.
* **Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (pVT) (無脈搏心室頻脈):** Rapid, wide QRS ectopic pacemaker activity overriding SA node → ↓ ventricular filling time (diastole) → zero cardiac output.
* **Asystole (心室靜止):** Complete failure of myocardial pacing systems.
* **Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA) (無脈搏電氣活動):** Organized electrical activity without mechanical coupling (often secondary to profound hypovolemia, hypoxia, or obstruction).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Initial BLS Recognition Findings
* **Responsiveness:** Absent response to tactile/verbal stimuli (shake & shout).
* **Airway/Breathing:** Apnea or agonal respirations (gasping, snorting, laboured breathing).
* **Circulation:** Absent carotid/femoral pulse (assessed for ≤10 seconds).

### Primary ABCDE Assessment Findings (Peri-Arrest)
* **Airway:** Pooling of secretions, vomitus, blood, flaccid tongue obstructing oropharynx.
* **Breathing:** Central cyanosis (發紺), absent chest rise/fall, absent breath sounds.
* **Circulation:** Profound pallor, mottling, capillary refill time (CRT) >4s, unrecordable BP.
* **Disability:** GCS 3 (E1V1M1), pupils fixed & dilated (late sign of brainstem ischemia).
* **Exposure:** Dialysis fistulas, pacemaker scars, transdermal medication patches, signs of trauma/exsanguination.

### Specific Arrhythmia ECG Findings
* **VF:** Chaotic, irregular, polymorphic deflections; absent P, QRS, T waves.
* **pVT:** Regular, wide QRS (>0.12s), rate >150 bpm.
* **Asystole:** Flatline (must confirm in 2 leads to rule out fine VF).
* **PEA:** Any organized rhythm (except VF/VT) with absent central pulse.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Reversible Causes: The 5 Hs
| Cause | Key Clinical Clues (History/Exam) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypoxia** | Choking, asthma, drowning, central cyanosis |
| **Hypovolemia** | Trauma, hematemesis, melena, ruptured AAA, ruptured ectopic |
| **Hydrogen Ion (Acidosis)** | DKA, renal failure, sepsis, toxin ingestion |
| **Hypo-/Hyperkalemia** | ESRF/dialysis (↑K+), diuretics (↓K+), peaked T waves / sine wave |
| **Hypothermia** | Environmental exposure, drowning, cold skin |

### Reversible Causes: The 5 Ts
| Cause | Key Clinical Clues (History/Exam) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Toxins** | Empty pill bottles, toxidromes, track marks, pinpoint pupils |
| **Tamponade (Cardiac)** | Chest trauma, malignancy, electrical alternans, narrow pulse pressure |
| **Tension Pneumothorax** | Trauma, asthma, deviated trachea, absent unilateral breath sounds |
| **Thrombosis (Coronary)** | Known IHD, chest pain preceding arrest, ST elevation |
| **Thrombosis (Pulmonary)** | DVT history, prolonged immobilization, recent surgery, RV strain |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care Testing (Intra-Arrest)
* **Waveform Capnography (ETCO2):** 
  * Normal: 35-45 mmHg.
  * Inadequate CPR: <10 mmHg.
  * ROSC Indicator: Abrupt, sustained ↑ ETCO2 (typically >40 mmHg).
* **Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS) / ECHO:**
  * Subxiphoid view during rhythm checks (<10s).
  * Findings: Cardiac standstill, pericardial effusion/tamponade, RV dilation (massive PE), sliding lung (rule out tension pneumothorax).
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG):** Rule out profound hypoglycemia.

### Immediate Post-ROSC Investigations
* **12-Lead ECG:** Crucial to identify STEMI / new LBBB requiring immediate PCI.
* **Arterial / Venous Blood Gas (ABG/VBG):** Assess pH, pCO2, pO2, lactate, K+, ionized Ca2+.
* **Bedside CXR:** Confirm endotracheal tube (ETT) depth, rule out pneumothorax, pulmonary edema, rib fractures.

### Formal Laboratory Tests (Post-ROSC)
* **Blood Profile:** CBC, U&E, LFT, Coagulation (PT/APTT).
* **Cardiac Biomarkers:** Troponin I/T.
* **Toxicology:** Urine/Serum tox screen (paracetamol, salicylate levels).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### BLS Resuscitation Algorithm
* **Ratio:** 30 compressions : 2 ventilations (before definitive airway).
* **Continuous CPR:** If advanced airway placed, continuous compressions + 1 breath every 6 seconds (10 breaths/min).

### ACLS: Shockable Rhythm (VF / pVT) Algorithm
* **Step 1:** Initiate CPR → attach monitor/defibrillator.
* **Step 2:** Rhythm check → VF/pVT identified.
* **Step 3:** Defibrillate (Shock 1) → immediately resume CPR for 2 mins.
* **Step 4:** Rhythm check → Defibrillate (Shock 2) → resume CPR.
* **Step 5 (Drugs):** Adrenaline (Epinephrine) 1mg IV/IO q3-5 mins (start after 2nd shock).
* **Step 6:** Rhythm check → Defibrillate (Shock 3) → resume CPR.
* **Step 7 (Anti-arrhythmics):** Amiodarone 300mg IV/IO bolus (after 3rd shock); 2nd dose 150mg if refractory.
  * *Alternative:* Lidocaine 1-1.5 mg/kg IV/IO.

### ACLS: Non-Shockable Rhythm (Asystole / PEA) Algorithm
* **Step 1:** Initiate CPR → attach monitor/defibrillator.
* **Step 2:** Rhythm check → Asystole/PEA identified.
* **Step 3 (Drugs):** Adrenaline 1mg IV/IO ASAP, then q3-5 mins.
* **Step 4:** CPR for 2 mins → continuous search for 5Hs & 5Ts.
* **Step 5:** Rhythm check. (If converts to shockable → switch to VF/pVT algorithm).

### Target Temperature Management (TTM) & Post-ROSC Care
* **Airway/Breathing:** Target SpO2 92-98%; Target PaCO2 35-45 mmHg; Avoid hyperventilation.
* **Circulation:** Target MAP >65 mmHg (use IV fluids, Noradrenaline/Adrenaline infusions).
* **Neurological (TTM):** For comatose adults post-ROSC; target 32-36°C for 24 hours to ↓ cerebral metabolic demand and prevent reperfusion injury.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### High-Quality Chest Compressions Mechanics
* **Position:** Lower half of sternum.
* **Rate:** 100-120 compressions per minute.
* **Depth:** At least 5 cm (2 inches), maximum 6 cm (2.4 inches) in adults.
* **Recoil:** Allow complete chest recoil (do not lean on chest).
* **Interruptions:** Minimize to <10 seconds.
* **Rotations:** Switch compressor every 2 minutes or when fatigued.

### Defibrillation (去顫) Mechanics
* **Pad Placement:** Right anterosuperior chest (infraclavicular) & left inferolateral chest (mid-axillary line, V6 position).
* **Energy Setting (Biphasic):** Manufacturer recommendation (typically 120-200 J); if unknown, use maximum dose (e.g., 200 J).
* **Safety Step:** Announce "Clear!", visually sweep patient, ensure no contact with bed/patient before discharging.

### Intraosseous (IO) Access Mechanics
* **Indications:** IV access impossible/delayed >2 attempts or >90 seconds.
* **Sites:** Proximal tibia (anteromedial flat surface, 2cm distal & medial to tibial tuberosity), Proximal humerus, Distal tibia.
* **Technique:** Clean skin → insert IO needle perpendicular to bone using driver → drill until sudden "give" (loss of resistance) → unscrew stylet → aspirate marrow to confirm → rapid flush with 10ml normal saline (essential to clear marrow cavity) → secure.

### Endotracheal Intubation (氣管插管) Mechanics (During CPR)
* **Technique:** Direct or Video Laryngoscopy. MUST NOT interrupt chest compressions (pass tube during compressions).
* **Confirmation:** Primary: Direct visualization passing cords. Secondary: Continuous waveform capnography (Gold Standard).
* **Consent:** Implied consent (life-saving emergency).

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Universal Termination of Resuscitation (TOR) Rule
Used to guide termination of efforts in OHCA. Consider termination if ALL criteria met:
* Unwitnessed arrest (by EMS or bystanders).
* No bystander CPR provided.
* No shock administered by AED/EMS.
* No return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) prior to transport.

### 5Hs and 5Ts Checklist
* **5Hs:** Hypoxia, Hypovolemia, Hydrogen ion, Hypo-/Hyperkalemia, Hypothermia.
* **5Ts:** Toxins, Tamponade, Tension pneumothorax, Thrombosis (pulmonary), Thrombosis (coronary).

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Post-Arrest Disposition
* **Coronary Care Unit (CCU) / Cath Lab:** If STEMI on ECG or high suspicion of acute coronary occlusion → immediate coronary angiography.
* **Intensive Care Unit (ICU):** All post-ROSC patients requiring mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, or Target Temperature Management (TTM).
* **Mortuary:** If TOR criteria met and declared dead in ED.

### CPR-Related Traumatic Complications
* Sternal and multiple rib fractures.
* Pneumothorax / Hemothorax.
* Liver/Spleen laceration (from low hand placement).
* Gastric distension and aspiration of gastric contents.

### Post-Cardiac Arrest Syndrome (PCAS)
* **Post-arrest brain injury:** Seizures, myoclonus, coma (leading cause of morbidity/mortality).
* **Post-arrest myocardial dysfunction:** Global hypokinesis, requiring inotropic support.
* **Systemic ischemia/reperfusion response:** Systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), vasoplegia, immunosuppression.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most reliable method to confirm ETT placement and monitor CPR quality? Answer: Continuous waveform capnography (ETCO2).
* Viva Question: When should Amiodarone be given in the ACLS algorithm? Answer: After the 3rd shock in refractory VF/pVT.
* Trap: Pausing chest compressions for 20 seconds to palpate a pulse during a rhythm check. Pearl: Rhythm and pulse checks must be completed simultaneously in strictly under 10 seconds. If in doubt, resume compressions.
* Trap: Administering Adrenaline immediately upon identifying VF. Pearl: For shockable rhythms, Adrenaline is only given *after* the 2nd shock. For non-shockable rhythms, it is given immediately.
* Pitfall: Hyperventilating the patient post-ROSC or during CPR. ↑ intrathoracic pressure → ↓ venous return to the heart → ↓ cardiac output and ↓ coronary perfusion pressure. Rate should be strictly 10 breaths/min.
* Pitfall: Flushing IO access slowly. IO flow requires a pressurized flush (push 10mL Normal Saline briskly) to open the marrow sinusoid network; otherwise, fluids/drugs will not infuse.
