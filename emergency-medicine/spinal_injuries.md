# Suspected Spinal Injuries (懷疑脊椎損傷)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition
* **Definition**: Structural damage to the vertebral column (脊椎) or **Spinal Cord (脊髓)** causing temporary or permanent disruption of motor, sensory, or autonomic functions.
* **SCIWORA**: Spinal Cord Injury Without Radiographic Abnormality (無骨折脫位型脊髓損傷), common in pediatric/geriatric populations.

### HK HA Triage Categories
* **Cat 1 (Critical)**: Apnea, respiratory failure (C3-C5 lesions), profound **Neurogenic Shock (神經性休克)** (↓BP, ↓HR), complete paralysis.
* **Cat 2 (Emergency)**: High-risk MOI with stable vitals, acute focal neurological deficits, severe spinal pain.
* **Cat 3 (Urgent)**: Stable patient, isolated spinal pain, normal neurology.

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong)
* **Most common MOI**: Falls in the elderly (geriatric trauma), industrial/construction accidents, motor vehicle accidents (MVA).
* **Common segments**: Cervical (most vulnerable) > Thoracolumbar junction (T11-L1) > Thoracic.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Primary vs Secondary Injury
* **Primary Injury (原發性損傷)**: Immediate mechanical disruption via shear, compression, or distraction forces on neural tissue.
* **Secondary Injury (繼發性損傷)**: Delayed cascade starting minutes to days post-injury.
* **Pathways**: Vasospasm → ischemia → edema → glutamate excitotoxicity → lipid peroxidation → apoptosis of neurons/astrocytes.

### Spinal Shock vs Neurogenic Shock
* **Spinal Shock (脊髓休克)**: Physiological/neurological phenomenon. Transient loss of all reflex, motor, and sensory function below injury level. Flaccid paralysis, absent bowel/bladder tone.
* **Neurogenic Shock (神經性休克)**: Hemodynamic phenomenon (distributive shock). Disruption of descending sympathetic pathways (T1-L2) → unopposed vagal tone. 
* **Shock Physiology**: Severe peripheral vasodilation (↓SVR) → ↓BP + ↓HR + warm, dry extremities.

### Common Mechanisms of Injury (MOI)
* **Axial Loading**: Jefferson fracture (C1), burst fractures (diving into shallow water).
* **Hyperflexion**: Flexion teardrop, bilateral facet dislocation.
* **Hyperextension**: Hangman's fracture (C2), central cord syndrome (elderly with spondylosis).
* **Penetrating Trauma**: Stabbings, projectiles (rare in HK).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary Survey Findings (ABCDE)
* **A/B**: Paradoxical abdominal breathing, diaphragmatic breathing (C3-C5 phrenic nerve impairment), weak cough, apnea.
* **C**: Bradycardia, hypotension, warm/flushed peripheries (neurogenic shock).
* **D**: Altered GCS (concomitant TBI), **Priapism (陰莖異常勃起)**, flaccid areflexia.
* **E**: Hypothermia (loss of sympathetic thermoregulation).

### Neurological Examination Findings
* **Motor**: Flaccid weakness or paralysis, grading 0-5 (MRC scale).
* **Sensory**: Loss of pinprick (spinothalamic), light touch (dorsal column), defined by exact dermatomal sensory level.
* **Reflexes**: Absent deep tendon reflexes (acute), absent **Bulbocavernosus Reflex (球海綿體反射)** (indicates spinal shock phase).
* **Autonomic**: Urinary retention (palpable bladder), patulous/flaccid anal sphincter on DRE.

### Incomplete Cord Syndromes Presentation
* **Central Cord Syndrome (中央脊髓症候群)**: Motor weakness Upper Extremities > Lower Extremities, cape-like pain/temperature loss.
* **Brown-Séquard Syndrome (布朗-塞卡症候群)**: Ipsilateral motor + proprioception/vibration loss; Contralateral pain/temperature loss.
* **Anterior Cord Syndrome (前脊髓症候群)**: Bilateral motor + pain/temperature loss; Preserved proprioception/vibration.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Major Trauma & Shock Differentials
| Condition | Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hemorrhagic Shock (出血性休克)** | ↑HR, ↓BP, cold/clammy skin, collapsed neck veins. |
| **Neurogenic Shock (神經性休克)** | ↓HR, ↓BP, warm/dry skin, wide pulse pressure. |
| **Severe Traumatic Brain Injury** | Altered GCS, pupillary changes, Cushing's triad (↑BP, ↓HR). |

### Non-Traumatic Spinal Emergencies
| Diagnosis | Key Assessment Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Aortic Dissection** | Tearing chest/back pain, pulse deficits, acute cord ischemia (Artery of Adamkiewicz involvement). |
| **Epidural Abscess (硬脊膜外膿腫)** | Fever, progressive back pain, IVDU history, localized tenderness. |
| **Cauda Equina Syndrome (馬尾症候群)** | Saddle anesthesia, asymmetric leg weakness, bowel/bladder incontinence. |
| **Transverse Myelitis** | Rapid onset bilateral motor/sensory deficits, sensory band, often post-viral. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care & Bedside Tests
* **eFAST Ultrasound**: Rule out intra-abdominal/thoracic hemorrhage (critical before attributing shock solely to neurogenic).
* **ECG**: Assess for severe bradycardia, AV blocks.
* **VBG/ABG**: Assess ventilation adequacy (pCO2), base deficit (perfusion).
* **Bladder Scan**: Assess urinary retention (neurogenic bladder).

### Formal Imaging Modalities
* **CT Spine (C/T/L)**: Gold standard for bony injuries in trauma. Sens/Spec >99% for fractures. 
* **MRI Spine (磁力共振掃描)**: Gold standard for cord parenchyma, ligaments, epidural hematoma, intervertebral discs, and SCIWORA.
* **Plain Radiographs**: Largely obsolete for major trauma in HK. May be used in low-risk ambulatory patients (AP, Lateral, Odontoid views).
* **CT Angiography Neck**: Indicated if suspected blunt cerebrovascular injury (e.g., C1-C3 fractures, foramen transversarium fractures).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Hemodynamic Resuscitation (Neurogenic Shock)
* **Fluid Therapy**: Warm isotonic crystalloids (Normal Saline or Plasmalyte) bolus 10-20mL/kg. Maximize preload before pressors.
* **Vasopressors**: 1st line **Norepinephrine (去甲腎上腺素)** (α1 and β1 activity) or **Dopamine**.
* **Hemodynamic Target**: Maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) > 85-90 mmHg for 5-7 days to perfuse ischemic penumbra.
* **Bradycardia Management**: **Atropine (阿托品)** 0.5-1mg IV, transcutaneous pacing if refractory/symptomatic.

### Corticosteroids & Neuroprotection
* **Methylprednisolone (甲潑尼龍)**: High-dose protocol (NASCIS). Highly controversial. 
* **HK HA Context**: NOT recommended for routine ED administration in traumatic spinal cord injury due to ↑ rates of sepsis, GI bleed, and pneumonia without proven functional benefit. Discuss with Orthopaedics/Neurosurgery first.
* **TXA (Tranexamic Acid)**: 1g IV over 10 mins if polytrauma + significant bleeding risk < 3 hrs post-injury.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Spinal Immobilization Techniques
* **Rigid C-Collar Application**: Measure distance from trapezius to bottom of chin; size collar appropriately. Slide back piece behind neck, secure front piece. Ensure no jugular venous compression.
* **Manual In-Line Stabilization (MILS)**: Operator stands at head, grasps mastoid processes/occiput, prevents rotation/flexion/extension without applying traction.
* **Log-Roll (4-Person)**: Operator 1 controls head (MILS) and commands. Operators 2, 3, 4 cross arms over chest/pelvis/legs. Roll patient as a single unit on command. Palpate entire spine, perform DRE, remove spine board.

### Advanced Airway Management
* **Intubation in C-Spine Injury**: Remove front of C-collar while assistant maintains MILS from below/side.
* **Technique**: Videolaryngoscopy (e.g., C-MAC, McGrath) strictly preferred over direct laryngoscopy. Avoid neck extension (sniffing position is contraindicated).
* **Adjuncts**: Use gum elastic bougie; fiberoptic awake intubation if difficult airway anticipated and patient is cooperative.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### NEXUS Criteria (C-Spine Clearance)
* **N**: Neurological deficit (focal)
* **S**: Spinal tenderness (midline)
* **A**: Altered mental status (GCS <15)
* **I**: Intoxication (alcohol/drugs)
* **D**: Distracting injury (e.g., long bone fracture)
* *Rule*: If ALL 5 are ABSENT, C-spine can be cleared clinically without imaging.

### Canadian C-Spine Rule
* **High-Risk Factors (Mandates CT)**: Age ≥ 65, dangerous mechanism (fall >1m/5 stairs, axial load, high-speed MVA, bicycle struck), paresthesias in extremities.
* **Low-Risk Factors (Allows ROM Assessment)**: Simple rear-end MVA, sitting position in ED, ambulatory at any time, delayed onset neck pain, absence of midline C-spine tenderness.
* **Range of Motion**: If low-risk criteria met, patient rotates neck 45° left and right. If able → clear clinically. If unable → CT.

### ASIA Impairment Scale (AIS)
* **A**: Complete. No sensory or motor function preserved in sacral segments S4-S5.
* **B**: Sensory Incomplete. Sensory but no motor function preserved below neurological level.
* **C**: Motor Incomplete. Motor preserved, >50% of key muscles below level have grade < 3.
* **D**: Motor Incomplete. Motor preserved, ≥50% of key muscles below level have grade ≥ 3.
* **E**: Normal.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition & Referral Pathways (HK System)
* **ICU/HDU**: All patients with high cervical lesions (C5 and above), respiratory compromise, or established neurogenic shock.
* **Orthopaedics / Spine Surgery Team**: Primary admitting specialty in most HK HA hospitals for bony fractures, cord contusions, and surgical fixations.
* **Neurosurgery**: Depending on local cluster protocols, may manage complex cord decompression.
* **Discharge**: Low-risk MOI, negative imaging, normal neurology, adequate analgesia. Provide strict return advice for delayed neurological symptoms.

### Acute Complications
* **Autonomic Dysreflexia (自主神經反射異常)**: Occurs later (T6 lesions or higher). Overactive sympathetic response to noxious stimuli (e.g., full bladder) → severe ↑BP, headache, flushing above lesion.
* **Respiratory**: Ventilator-associated pneumonia, atelectasis, mucous plugging.
* **VTE**: Extremely high risk of DVT/PE due to stasis.
* **Skin**: Decubitus ulcers (can form within hours on hard spine boards).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate between spinal shock and neurogenic shock?
  * Answer: Spinal shock is a neurological deficit (flaccid paralysis, areflexia, loss of bulbocavernosus reflex). Neurogenic shock is a hemodynamic instability (hypotension, bradycardia, warm skin).
* Viva Question: What is the hemodynamic target for a patient with an acute spinal cord injury?
  * Answer: MAP > 85-90 mmHg using IVF and vasopressors (Norepinephrine) to maintain spinal cord perfusion and minimize secondary ischemic injury.
* Trap: Attributing hypotension entirely to neurogenic shock in a polytrauma patient.
  * Pearl: Hemorrhagic shock must be assumed and ruled out first (via eFAST, CT) before confirming neurogenic shock, as treatment pathways differ significantly.
* Trap: Relying on plain X-rays to clear the C-spine in elderly patients following a fall.
  * Pearl: Osteoarthritic changes and osteopenia severely reduce X-ray sensitivity. Elderly patients (≥65) automatically trigger high-risk criteria on the Canadian C-Spine Rule and mandate a CT scan.
* Pitfall: Prolonged immobilization on a hard spine board in the ED.
  * Extrication boards cause severe tissue ischemia and pressure ulcers within 1-2 hours. Remove the board via a log-roll immediately after ED transfer while maintaining C-spine precautions.
