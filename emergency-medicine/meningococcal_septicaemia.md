# Meningococcal Septicaemia (腦膜炎雙球菌敗血症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Meningococcal Septicaemia
* **Pathogen:** Fulminant systemic infection by *Neisseria meningitidis* (Gram -ve diplococcus).
* **Core Pathology:** Rapidly progressive septic shock (敗血性休克) + Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) (彌漫性血管內凝血).
* **Spectrum:** Can occur with or without concurrent Meningococcal Meningitis (腦膜炎).

### Hong Kong HA Triage Category
* **Triage Category 1 (Critical):** If presenting with apnea, pulselessness, or profound shock.
* **Triage Category 2 (Emergency):** If presenting with fever + purpuric rash, lethargy, or ↑ heart rate (time target <15 mins).

### Local HK Epidemiology & Public Health Status
* **Incidence:** Rare but catastrophic mortality (up to 20-30% if shock develops).
* **Demographics:** Bimodal peak (infants/children <5 years, young adults/teens).
* **Seasonality:** Peaks in winter and spring in Hong Kong.
* **Serogroups:** Serogroup B is predominantly endemic in HK.
* **Statutory Status:** Notifiable Infectious Disease to HK Centre for Health Protection (CHP) (衞生防護中心).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Transmission & Colonisation Pathophysiology
* **Route:** Droplet spread via respiratory secretions.
* **Initial Phase:** Asymptomatic colonisation of nasopharyngeal mucosa via pili.
* **Invasion:** Penetrates mucosal barrier → enters bloodstream (bacteraemia).

### Endotoxin-Mediated Shock & Capillary Leak
* **Trigger:** Bacterial autolysis releases massive amounts of **Lipooligosaccharide (LOS)** (endotoxin).
* **Cytokine Storm:** LOS hyperstimulates macrophages → massive release of TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6.
* **Endothelial Dysfunction:** Loss of tight junctions → profound capillary leak syndrome → hypovolaemic + distributive shock.
* **Myocardial Depression:** Direct toxic effect of cytokines ↓ cardiac contractility.

### Coagulopathy & Ischaemic Pathophysiology
* **DIC Activation:** Tissue factor up-regulation + suppression of Protein C/fibrinolysis.
* **Microvascular Thrombosis:** Widespread fibrin clots → Purpura fulminans + digital/limb gangrene.
* **Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome:** Bilateral adrenal haemorrhage → acute secondary adrenal insufficiency → refractory shock.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary Survey (ABCDE) Findings
* **Airway:** Typically patent unless severe ↓ GCS.
* **Breathing:** Tachypnoea (Kussmaul breathing if severe metabolic acidosis), ↓ SpO2.
* **Circulation:** Tachycardia, severe hypotension, prolonged capillary refill time (CRT >2s), cold mottled extremities, weak/thready pulses.
* **Disability:** Altered mental status, lethargy, irritability (infants), ↓ GCS.
* **Exposure:** Hyperthermia (發燒) or profound hypothermia (poor prognostic sign).

### Dermatological Findings (Classic Rash)
* **Early Rash:** Non-specific maculopapular or urticarial rash.
* **Classic Rash:** Petechiae (瘀點) rapidly evolving into purpura (瘀斑).
* **Glass Tumbler Test:** Non-blanching under applied pressure.
* **Late Rash:** Purpura fulminans (large necrotic ecchymoses, commonly on extremities/pressure points).

### Neurological & Musculoskeletal Findings
* **Meningism (if concurrent meningitis):** Nuchal rigidity, +ve Kernig’s sign, +ve Brudzinski’s sign, bulging fontanelle (infants).
* **Musculoskeletal:** Severe myalgia (especially legs), arthralgia.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Differential Diagnoses Table
| Diagnostic Category | Specific Condition | Key Differentiating Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bacterial Sepsis** | Pneumococcal / Group A Strep Sepsis | Often lacks fulminant purpura; usually evident primary source (pneumonia, necrotising fasciitis). |
| **Viral Infections** | Dengue Fever (登革熱) | Travel history, retro-orbital pain, +ve tourniquet test, blanching rash initially. |
| **Viral Infections** | Enterovirus (e.g., EV71) | HFMD lesions (vesicles on hands/feet/mouth); shock is cardiogenic/neurogenic, not purpuric. |
| **Haematological** | Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP) | Purpura present, but patient is **non-toxic** and afebrile. |
| **Vasculitic** | Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (HSP) | Rash strictly gravitationally distributed (buttocks/legs); normal PLT; joint/abdo pain. |
| **Tick-borne** | Scrub Typhus (叢林斑疹傷寒) | Eschar present; hiking history in HK; maculopapular rash. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care & Bedside Diagnostics
* **VBG/ABG:** ↑ Lactate (tissue hypoperfusion), profound severe metabolic acidosis (↓ pH, ↓ HCO3).
* **Bedside Glucose:** Hypoglycaemia (especially in paediatric sepsis) or stress hyperglycaemia.
* **ECG:** Sinus tachycardia, signs of myocardial ischaemia (demand).

### Urgent Laboratory Bloods
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** ↑ WBC (neutrophilia) or ↓ WBC (severe sepsis exhaustion), ↓ PLT (consumptive).
* **Coagulation Profile:** ↑ PT, ↑ APTT, ↓ Fibrinogen, ↑ D-Dimer (confirms DIC).
* **Renal & Liver Function:** ↑ Cr/Urea (AKI), ↑ AST/ALT (hypoxic hepatitis).
* **Inflammatory Markers:** Markedly ↑ CRP and ↑ Procalcitonin.

### Microbiological Investigations
* **Blood Cultures:** x2 sets (draw **BEFORE** antibiotics if immediately available, do not delay >15 mins).
* **Meningococcal PCR:** EDTA blood sample for *N. meningitidis* DNA (crucial as cultures often negative post-antibiotics).
* **Throat/Nasopharyngeal Swab:** For bacterial culture/PCR.

### Diagnostic Imaging
* **CXR:** Rule out concurrent pneumonia.
* **CT Brain:** Indicates only if focal neuro signs or prolonged ↓ GCS; **must not delay antibiotics**.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Immediate Isolation & Resuscitation Protocol
* **Isolation:** Strict droplet and contact precautions immediately.
* **Oxygenation:** High-flow O2 via Non-Rebreather Mask (15L/min) ± early endotracheal intubation.
* **IV Fluids:** Aggressive crystalloid bolus (Plasma-Lyte or Normal Saline).
* **Fluid Dosing:** 10-20 ml/kg rapid push; reassess after each bolus; watch for fluid overload/myocardial depression.

### Empirical Antibiotic Protocol (HK HA Guidelines)
* **First Line (IV):** IV Ceftriaxone (頭孢曲松) 2g Q12H (Adults) or 50-100 mg/kg/day (Paeds).
* **Pre-hospital/GP Setting:** IM Benzylpenicillin (if IV access impossible).
* **Penicillin/Cephalosporin Anaphylaxis:** IV Meropenem OR IV Chloramphenicol (consult local micro).
* **Timing:** Administer within ONE hour of triage. DO NOT wait for cultures/LP.

### Vasoactive & Adjunctive Corticosteroid Therapy
* **First-line Vasopressor:** IV Noradrenaline (peripheral initially, then via CVC) to target MAP > 65 mmHg.
* **Inotrope Addition:** IV Adrenaline or Dobutamine if cold shock / myocardial dysfunction persists.
* **Corticosteroids (Shock):** IV Hydrocortisone 50mg Q6H (if fluid/vasopressor-refractory shock suspected Waterhouse-Friderichsen).
* **Corticosteroids (Meningitis):** IV Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg Q6H (must be given just before or with first antibiotic dose).

### Blood Product Correction (DIC Management)
* **Platelets:** Transfuse if PLT <50 x 10^9/L with active bleeding.
* **Cryoprecipitate:** Transfuse to keep Fibrinogen >1.5 g/L.
* **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP):** Transfuse if PT/APTT >1.5x normal with bleeding.

### Public Health & Prophylaxis Protocol
* **Notification:** Urgent statutory notification to CHP.
* **Close Contact Prophylaxis (Adults):** PO Ciprofloxacin 500mg single dose OR PO Rifampicin 600mg BD for 2 days.
* **Close Contact Prophylaxis (Paeds/Pregnancy):** IM Ceftriaxone single dose.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Intraosseous (IO) Cannulation Mechanics
* **Indications:** Failure to obtain peripheral IV access within 90 seconds in shock.
* **Site:** Proximal tibia (1-2 cm medial to tibial tuberosity) or proximal humerus.
* **Steps:**
  * Clean with Chlorhexidine.
  * Stabilise limb; insert needle at 90 degrees to bone.
  * Drill until sudden loss of resistance (cortical penetration).
  * Unscrew stylet, aspirate marrow to confirm, flush with 10ml Normal Saline.
  * Secure dressing.

### Central Venous Catheter (CVC) Insertion Mechanics
* **Site Selection:** Internal Jugular or Femoral Vein (Subclavian avoided in DIC/severe coagulopathy due to non-compressible bleeding risk).
* **Steps:**
  * Strict aseptic non-touch technique (full barrier precautions).
  * Ultrasound guidance to locate vein.
  * Seldinger technique: Insert introducer needle → advance guidewire → remove needle → dilate tract → advance catheter over wire → remove wire.
  * Flush all lumens, suture to skin, apply sterile dressing.

### Lumbar Puncture (LP) Contraindications
* **Absolute Contraindications in this context:** Coagulopathy (DIC), severe shock, rapidly falling GCS, focal neurological signs, local infection at insertion site.
* **Protocol:** Defer LP entirely in the ED for suspected Meningococcal Septicaemia.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### qSOFA (Quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) Score
* **Criteria (1 point each):**
  * Respiratory rate ≥ 22 breaths/min
  * Altered mentation (GCS < 15)
  * Systolic blood pressure ≤ 100 mmHg
* **Interpretation:** Score ≥ 2 indicates high risk of poor outcome/ICU stay in suspected infection.

### Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS) Criteria
* **Criteria (Need ≥ 2):**
  * Temperature > 38°C or < 36°C
  * Heart rate > 90 beats/min
  * Respiratory rate > 20 breaths/min or PaCO2 < 32 mmHg
  * WBC > 12,000/mm³, < 4,000/mm³, or > 10% bands

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### ED Disposition
* **Destination:** Immediate admission to Intensive Care Unit (ICU) via designated resuscitation lift.
* **Transport:** Doctor escort required with full monitoring, transport ventilator, and running vasoactive infusions.

### Systemic Acute Complications
* **Dermatological/Surgical:** Purpura fulminans leading to auto-amputation or requiring surgical amputation of digits/limbs.
* **Renal:** Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN) requiring continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT).
* **Endocrine:** Permanent adrenal insufficiency (Waterhouse-Friderichsen).
* **Neurological:** Sensorineural hearing loss, cognitive impairment (if concurrent meningitis).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* **Viva Question:** What is the most critical first step in managing a child with a fever and non-blanching rash? Answer: Immediate administration of empirical IV/IM antibiotics (Ceftriaxone) without waiting for investigations.
* **Trap:** Attempting to perform a Lumbar Puncture to confirm meningitis in a patient with a purpuric rash. Pearl: Never perform an LP in suspected meningococcal septicaemia due to the massive risk of bleeding (DIC) and coning; treat clinically.
* **Pitfall:** Delaying antibiotics to obtain a CT brain. Always give IV antibiotics and steroids prior to transferring the patient to the scanner.
* **Viva Question:** Why might a patient with meningococcal septicaemia become suddenly refractory to adrenaline/noradrenaline infusions? Answer: Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome (adrenal haemorrhage), requiring immediate IV Hydrocortisone.
* **Trap:** Prescribing prophylactic antibiotics to the healthcare workers who triaged the patient. Pearl: Prophylaxis is strictly for close contacts (household members, kissing contacts) or healthcare workers directly exposed to respiratory secretions (e.g., during intubation without a mask).
