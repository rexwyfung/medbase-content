# Airway Management & Oxygenation (呼吸道管理與氧氣治療)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Airway & Oxygenation Emergencies
* **Airway Compromise**: Inability to maintain patency of respiratory tract allowing for adequate gas exchange.
* **Respiratory Failure**: Inability of cardiopulmonary system to maintain adequate gas exchange (Type 1 = Hypoxia; Type 2 = Hypoxia + Hypercapnia).
* **Definitive Airway**: A cuffed tube placed in the trachea below the vocal cords (e.g., **Endotracheal Tube (氣管插管)**, tracheostomy) connected to an oxygen-enriched assisted ventilation system.

### HK AED Triage Categorization
* **Triage Category 1 (Critical)**: Imminent airway loss, respiratory arrest, profound **Hypoxia (缺氧)** (SpO2 <90% despite high-flow O2), severe stridor. Requires immediate resuscitation room (R-room) management.
* **Triage Category 2 (Emergency)**: Moderate respiratory distress, SpO2 90-94% on room air, stable airway but at risk of deterioration.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiology of Upper Airway Obstruction
* **Mechanical Obstruction**: Pharyngeal tone loss (tongue falls back) in unconscious state (most common).
* **Foreign Body**: Food bolus (e.g., steakhouse syndrome), dentures, toys (pediatrics).
* **Inflammatory/Edema**: Fluid shifts into loose areolar tissue of supraglottic area → swelling compromises lumen (e.g., Anaphylaxis, burns, Ludwig's angina).

### Pathophysiology of Oxygenation Failure
* **V/Q Mismatch**: Perfusion without ventilation (shunt, e.g., pneumonia, pulmonary edema) or ventilation without perfusion (dead space, e.g., Pulmonary Embolism).
* **Hypoventilation**: ↓ Respiratory drive (e.g., opioid toxidrome, brain injury) → ↑ PaCO2 + ↓ PaO2.
* **Diffusion Impairment**: Thickened alveolar-capillary membrane (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Visual Assessment (Look)
* **Agitation/Obtundation**: Hypoxia → agitation; Hypercapnia → obtundation/lethargy.
* **Cyanosis (發紺)**: Central (lips/tongue) indicates severe hypoxemia (requires ~5g/dL of deoxygenated Hb).
* **Work of Breathing**: Accessory muscle use (SCM, scalenes), intercostal retractions, nasal flaring, tracheal tug.
* **Paradoxical Breathing**: "See-saw" chest/abdomen movement (sign of complete upper airway obstruction).

### Auditory Assessment (Listen)
* **Normal**: Quiet, effortless breathing.
* **Snoring (鼾聲)**: Pharyngeal obstruction (tongue base).
* **Gurgling**: Fluid in airway (blood, vomitus, secretions).
* **Stridor (喘鳴)**: Harsh, high-pitched inspiratory sound indicating laryngeal/tracheal obstruction (narrowed by >50%).
* **Hoarseness**: Vocal cord involvement/edema.
* **Silence**: Complete airway obstruction or impending respiratory arrest.

### Tactile Assessment (Feel)
* **Breath Flow**: Diminished or absent exhaled air flow over mouth/nose.
* **Crepitus**: Subcutaneous emphysema indicating laryngeal/tracheal tear or pneumothorax.
* **Tracheal Deviation**: Shifted away from tension pneumothorax or massive effusion.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Airway Obstruction Differentials
| Category | Differential Diagnoses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Infectious** | **Epiglottitis (會厭炎)**, Retropharyngeal abscess, Croup, Ludwig's Angina. |
| **Traumatic** | Maxillofacial smash, Laryngeal crush, Inhalation burns/thermal injury, Neck hematoma. |
| **Allergic/Immunologic** | **Anaphylaxis (過敏性休克)**, Angioedema (ACE-inhibitor induced). |
| **Mechanical/Neuro** | Foreign Body Airway Obstruction (FBAO), Coma (GCS ≤8), Vocal cord paralysis. |

### Oxygenation Failure Differentials
| Category | Differential Diagnoses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pulmonary** | Severe Asthma, COPD exacerbation, Tension Pneumothorax, Pulmonary Edema. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Acute Myocardial Infarction, Massive Pulmonary Embolism (PE), Cardiac Tamponade. |
| **Toxicologic** | Opioid/Sedative overdose, CO poisoning, Methemoglobinemia. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care Airway Investigations
* **Pulse Oximetry (SpO2)**: Rapid assessment of oxygenation. Unreliable in shock, hypothermia, CO poisoning.
* **Waveform Capnography (ETCO2)**: Gold standard to confirm ETT placement in trachea. Normal 35-45 mmHg. Assesses ventilation and cardiac output during CPR.
* **Bedside Ultrasound (POCUS)**: Rule out pneumothorax (sliding sign), assess for endotracheal vs esophageal tube placement.

### Formal Laboratory Investigations
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Quantifies PaO2, PaCO2, pH, base excess.
* **Venous Blood Gas (VBG)**: Acceptable alternative for pH and pCO2 in urgent settings.
* **Carboxyhemoglobin (COHb)**: Co-oximetry for suspected carbon monoxide poisoning.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Oxygenation Escalation Algorithm
* **Step 1 (Low Flow)**: Nasal Cannula (1-6 L/min, ~24-44% FiO2).
* **Step 2 (Moderate Flow)**: Simple Face Mask (6-10 L/min, ~40-60% FiO2).
* **Step 3 (High Flow/Emergency)**: **Non-Rebreather Mask (NRBM)** (15 L/min, ~80-90% FiO2). Fill reservoir bag prior to application.
* **Step 4 (Positive Pressure)**: **Bag-Valve-Mask (BVM)** (15 L/min, near 100% FiO2). High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) or Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV - CPAP/BiPAP).

### Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) Pharmacology Algorithm
* **Pre-oxygenation**: 100% O2 for 3 mins via NRBM or 8 vital capacity breaths.
* **Induction Agents**:
  * **Ketamine**: 1-2 mg/kg IV. Preserves respiratory drive, bronchodilator. (Preferred in shock/asthma).
  * **Propofol**: 1-2.5 mg/kg IV. Rapid onset. (Avoid in hypotension).
  * **Etomidate**: 0.3 mg/kg IV. Hemodynamically stable. (Risk of adrenal suppression).
* **Paralytic Agents**:
  * **Suxamethonium (Succinylcholine)**: 1.5 mg/kg IV. Depolarizing. Onset 45s, duration 10 mins. (Contraindicated in hyperkalemia, burns >72h, crush injury).
  * **Rocuronium**: 1.2 mg/kg IV. Non-depolarizing. Onset 60s, duration 40-60 mins.

### Post-Intubation Sedation
* **Propofol infusion**: 5-50 mcg/kg/min.
* **Midazolam infusion**: 0.05-0.1 mg/kg/hr.
* **Fentanyl**: 1-2 mcg/kg for analgesia.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Basic Airway Maneuvers & Adjuncts Mechanics
* **Head-Tilt/Chin-Lift**: Place one hand on forehead to extend head, use fingers of other hand under bony part of mandible to lift chin forward. (Contraindicated in C-spine injury).
* **Jaw Thrust**: Grasp angles of lower jaw and lift forward with both hands. (Safe for C-spine injury).
* **Oropharyngeal Airway (OPA)**: Measure from mouth corner to earlobe. Insert upside down, rotate 180° when hitting soft palate. (Only for unconscious pt, triggers gag reflex).
* **Nasopharyngeal Airway (NPA)**: Measure from nostril to earlobe. Lubricate. Insert perpendicular to face with bevel facing septum. (Contraindicated in severe base of skull fractures).

### Bag-Valve-Mask (BVM) Ventilation Mechanics
* **C-E Grip (Single Rescuer)**: Thumb and index finger form "C" around mask, middle/ring/pinky form "E" under mandible lifting jaw into mask.
* **Two-Person Technique**: Rescuer 1 uses two hands to maintain mask seal and jaw thrust. Rescuer 2 squeezes bag. (Most effective).
* **Ventilation Rate**: Squeeze bag every 5-6 seconds (10-12 breaths/min). Volume: Just enough to produce visible chest rise.

### Endotracheal Intubation (ETT) Mechanics
* **Preparation**: Assemble laryngoscope (Mac 3/4), check light, insert stylet into ETT, prepare suction.
* **Positioning**: "Sniffing position" (neck flexed, head extended) by placing pad under occiput.
* **Insertion**: Hold laryngoscope in LEFT hand. Insert blade into right side of mouth, sweeping tongue to left. Advance into vallecula.
* **Lifting**: Lift laryngoscope upwards and forwards (45° angle) to expose vocal cords. Do NOT lever back on teeth.
* **Placement**: Pass ETT through vocal cords until cuff passes 1-2 cm beyond cords.
* **Confirmation**: Remove stylet, inflate cuff. Attach ETCO2. Auscultate epigastrium (silent) and lung fields (bilateral breath sounds). Secure tube.

### Surgical Cricothyroidotomy (環甲膜切開術) Mechanics
* **Indication**: "Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate" (CICO) scenario.
* **Landmark**: Palpate cricothyroid membrane between thyroid cartilage and cricoid cartilage.
* **Incision**: Vertical midline incision through skin, then horizontal incision through cricothyroid membrane.
* **Dilation**: Insert scalpel handle and rotate 90°, or use tracheal hook/bougie.
* **Insertion**: Pass small cuffed tube (e.g., size 6.0 ETT or tracheostomy tube) through membrane. Inflate cuff, ventilate, confirm with ETCO2.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### LEMON Rule for Difficult Intubation
| L-E-M-O-N | Clinical Parameter & Scoring |
| :--- | :--- |
| **L**ook externally | Facial trauma, large incisors, beard/moustache, short neck. |
| **E**valuate (3-3-2) | 3 fingers between incisors; 3 fingers mentum to hyoid; 2 fingers hyoid to thyroid notch. |
| **M**allampati | Class III/IV indicates high risk of poor glottic view. |
| **O**bstruction | Epiglottitis, hematoma, malignancy, foreign body. |
| **N**eck Mobility | Limited extension (e.g., cervical collar, ankylosing spondylitis). |

### MOANS Rule for Difficult Bag-Mask Ventilation
| M-O-A-N-S | Clinical Parameter |
| :--- | :--- |
| **M**ask Seal | Beards, altered facial anatomy, massive trauma. |
| **O**besity/Obstruction | BMI >26, 3rd trimester pregnancy, supraglottic obstruction. |
| **A**ge | Age > 55 years (loss of facial tissue elasticity). |
| **N**o Teeth | Edentulous patients (leave dentures in for BVM). |
| **S**tiff Lungs/Chest | Severe asthma, COPD, ARDS. |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition Pathways
* **ICU/HDU Admission**: ALL patients requiring intubation, continuous CPAP/BiPAP, or highly unstable airways.
* **Medical/Surgical Ward**: Stable patients resolving from mild airway compromise (e.g., resolved anaphylaxis post-observation).
* **Operation Theater (OT)**: Immediate transfer for complex airway trauma, deep neck space abscess drainage, or rigid bronchoscopy.

### Peri-Intubation Complications (DOPE Mnemonic for Post-Intubation Deterioration)
* **D - Displacement**: Tube migrated into right mainstem bronchus or dislodged into esophagus.
* **O - Obstruction**: Secretions, blood, mucous plug, or kinking of the tube.
* **P - Pneumothorax**: Tension pneumothorax from barotrauma/positive pressure.
* **E - Equipment Failure**: Ventilator disconnect, empty oxygen cylinder, cuff leak.

### Hemodynamic Complications
* **Post-Intubation Hypotension**: Caused by loss of sympathetic drive (sedatives), decreased venous return from positive pressure ventilation, or underlying hypovolemia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most reliable clinical method to confirm endotracheal tube placement in the emergency department? Answer: Continuous waveform capnography (ETCO2).
* Trap: Assuming oxygen saturation of 100% means the patient is ventilating adequately. Pearl: SpO2 only measures oxygenation, not ventilation. A patient can have normal SpO2 while accumulating lethal levels of CO2 (hypercapnia) if they are hypoventilating.
* Pitfall: Removing cervical collar to perform head-tilt chin-lift in a trauma patient. Maintain C-spine immobilization and exclusively use the jaw-thrust maneuver to open the airway.
* Viva Question: What are the absolute contraindications to using Suxamethonium for rapid sequence intubation? Answer: Hyperkalemia, major burns/crush injuries >72 hours old, history of malignant hyperthermia, and neuromuscular denervating diseases (e.g., muscular dystrophy).
* Trap: Waiting for central cyanosis to appear before initiating airway interventions. Pearl: Cyanosis is a very late and pre-terminal sign of hypoxia. Act early on work of breathing and altered mental status.
* Pitfall: Using an Oropharyngeal Airway (OPA) in a semi-conscious patient. An OPA can trigger a gag reflex, causing vomiting and massive aspiration. Use a Nasopharyngeal Airway (NPA) instead if the gag reflex is intact.
