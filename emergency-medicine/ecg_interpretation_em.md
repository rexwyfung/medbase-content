# 3- and 12-lead ECG Interpretation (三及十二導聯心電圖詮釋)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition
* **Electrocardiogram (ECG) (心電圖):** Non-invasive transcutaneous recording of cardiac electrical activity over time.
* **3-Lead:** Continuous rhythm monitoring (bipolar limbs).
* **12-Lead:** Static multi-angle snapshot (6 limb, 6 precordial leads) for vectors, ischaemia, and structural abnormalities.

### HK HA ED Triage Categories
* **Cat 1 (Critical):** Cardiac arrest, Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) (心室顫動), haemodynamic instability + arrhythmias.
* **Cat 2 (Emergency):** Chest pain suspicious for Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) (急性心肌梗塞), symptomatic tachy/bradycardia, syncope.
* **Cat 3 (Urgent):** Atypical chest pain, stable palpitations, asymptomatic AF.

### Local Epidemiology
* **Ischaemic Heart Disease (缺血性心臟病):** Top 3 causes of mortality in HK.
* **Time-to-ECG:** HK HA standard mandates 12-lead ECG within 10 mins of ED arrival for all chest pain patients.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Cardiac Electrophysiology Basics
* **Depolarisation (去極化):** Na+/Ca2+ influx → myocardial contraction (QRS complex).
* **Repolarisation (復極化):** K+ efflux → myocardial relaxation (ST segment, T wave).
* **Electrical Vector:** Wave moving TOWARDS +ve electrode = upward deflection; AWAY = downward.

### Coronary Artery Territories & ECG Leads
| Territory (壁) | ECG Leads | Culprit Artery | Reciprocal Changes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inferior** | II, III, aVF | RCA (80%) / LCx (20%) | I, aVL |
| **Anterior / Septal** | V1-V4 | LAD | Inferior leads |
| **Lateral** | I, aVL, V5, V6 | LCx or Diagonal branch of LAD | Inferior leads |
| **Posterior** | V7-V9 | RCA or LCx | V1-V3 (ST↓, tall R, upright T) |
| **Right Ventricular**| V4R | Proximal RCA | None |

### Ischaemia-Infarction Cascade (心肌缺血級聯反應)
* **Hyperacute T waves:** Localized subendocardial ischaemia → K+ derangement.
* **ST Elevation (STE):** Transmural injury currents.
* **Q Waves:** Necrosis / electrically silent dead myocardium.
* **T wave Inversion (TWI):** Late ischaemia/reperfusion.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Cardiovascular Symptoms
* **Classic Angina:** Central, crushing chest pain, radiation to jaw/L arm.
* **Angina Equivalents:** Dyspnoea, diaphoresis (出汗), nausea, profound weakness (elderly/diabetic/female).
* **Arrhythmia Symptoms:** Palpitations (心悸), syncope (暈厥), presyncope, dizziness.

### Clinical Signs
* **Vital Signs:** Tachycardia, bradycardia, hypotension (cardiogenic shock), tachypnoea.
* **Pump Failure:** Raised JVP, bibasal crepitations, S3/S4 gallop rhythm.
* **Valvular:** New systolic murmur (papillary muscle rupture).

### 12-Lead ECG Interpretation Framework (The Rule of 5s)
* **Rate:** 300 / number of large squares between R-R (if regular), or total QRS in 10 sec × 6 (if irregular).
* **Rhythm:** P wave before every QRS? Constant PR interval?
* **Axis:** Lead I & aVF (Both +ve = Normal; I +ve/aVF -ve = LAD; I -ve/aVF +ve = RAD).
* **Intervals:** 
  * PR: 0.12-0.20s (3-5 small squares).
  * QRS: < 0.12s (< 3 small squares).
  * QTc: < 440ms (men), < 460ms (women).
* **Ischaemia/Infarct:** STE, ST depression (STD), TWI, pathological Q waves (>1 small square wide, >25% depth of R wave).

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### ST-Segment Elevation Differentials (STE)
| Diagnosis | Key ECG Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **STEMI (急性心肌梗塞)** | Convex STE, reciprocal STD, contiguous leads. |
| **Pericarditis (心包炎)** | Widespread concave STE, PR depression, Spodick's sign. |
| **Benign Early Repolarisation**| Concave STE in V2-V5, prominent J-point notching, stable over time. |
| **Brugada Syndrome (布魯格達氏症候群)** | Coved STE >2mm in V1-V3 followed by -ve T wave. |
| **Left Ventricular Aneurysm** | Persistent STE weeks post-MI, deep Q waves, no reciprocal changes. |

### Wide Complex Tachycardia (WCT) Differentials
* **Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) (心室頻脈):** AV dissociation, capture/fusion beats, extreme RAD, QRS >0.14s.
* **SVT with Aberrancy (BBB):** Typical BBB morphology, previous similar ECG.
* **Antidromic WPW:** Delta waves in sinus rhythm, slurred QRS upstroke.

### Toxicological & Metabolic Differentials
* **Hyperkalaemia (高血鉀):** Tall tented T waves → flat P wave → wide QRS → sine wave.
* **TCA Overdose:** Sinus tachycardia, RAD, widened QRS, dominant terminal R wave in aVR.

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care (PoC) Tests
* **PoC Troponin T/I:** Rapid screening (less sensitive than high-sensitivity lab assays).
* **VBG / ABG:** K+, Ca2+, pH, lactate.
* **Bedside Echocardiography (POCUS):** Regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA), pericardial effusion, RV strain.

### Formal Laboratory Tests
* **hs-Troponin T/I (高敏肌鈣蛋白):** 0h / 1h or 0h / 2h serial pathways in HK HA.
* **Renal Function Test (RFT):** Baseline Cr before angiography/PCI.
* **Coagulation Profile (PT/APTT/INR):** Baseline prior to thrombolysis or heparinization.
* **CXR:** Rule out aortic dissection (widened mediastinum) or pulmonary oedema.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) Pharmacotherapy
* **Antiplatelets:** Aspirin 300mg PO + Ticagrelor 180mg PO (or Clopidogrel 300-600mg PO if high bleeding risk/thrombolysis).
* **Anticoagulation:** Enoxaparin 1mg/kg SC or Heparin IV bolus + infusion (HK HA local protocol dependent).
* **Vasodilator:** Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN) 0.5mg SL. 
* **Contraindications to GTN:** Inferior STEMI + RV infarction, SBP < 90, recent PDE5 inhibitor use.

### HK HA STEMI Reperfusion Pathway
* **Primary PCI (PPCI):** First-line if symptom onset < 12h AND door-to-balloon time < 90 mins.
* **Thrombolysis:** Tenecteplase (TNK) IV bolus weight-adjusted if PPCI unavailable within 120 mins.

### Tachyarrhythmia Drugs (ALS Guidelines)
* **Narrow Complex (SVT):** Adenosine 6mg IV rapid push → 12mg IV → 12mg IV.
* **Broad Complex (Stable VT):** Amiodarone 300mg IV over 20-60 mins.
* **Atrial Fibrillation (Rate Control):** Metoprolol 2.5-5mg IV OR Amiodarone (if structural heart disease/HF).

### Bradyarrhythmia Drugs (ALS Guidelines)
* **1st Line:** Atropine 0.5mg IV (repeat max 3mg).
* **2nd Line:** Adrenaline infusion (2-10 mcg/min) or Isoprenaline infusion.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### 12-Lead ECG Placement Mechanics
* **V1:** 4th intercostal space (ICS), R sternal edge.
* **V2:** 4th ICS, L sternal edge.
* **V3:** Midway between V2 and V4.
* **V4:** 5th ICS, L mid-clavicular line.
* **V5:** 5th ICS, L anterior axillary line.
* **V6:** 5th ICS, L mid-axillary line.
* **Right-sided (V4R):** Mirror image of V4 on right side (suspected RV infarct).
* **Posterior (V7-V9):** 5th ICS continuing to L posterior axillary line, mid-scapular line, paraspinal edge.

### Synchronised DC Cardioversion (同步直流電復律)
* **Indication:** Unstable tachyarrhythmias (SVT, AF, VT with pulse).
* **Mechanics:** 
  1. Apply antero-apical or antero-posterior pads.
  2. Select "SYNC" mode on defibrillator (must see markers on R waves).
  3. Provide procedural sedation (e.g., Propofol/Ketamine) if patient conscious.
  4. Select energy (e.g., 120-150J biphasic for AF, 70-120J for SVT).
  5. Charge, clear area, press & hold SHOCK button until discharge.
* **Consent:** Explain risk of failure, skin burns, induction of VF, stroke (if AF > 48h).

### Transcutaneous Pacing (TCP) (體外心臟起搏)
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Apply pads (antero-posterior preferred).
  2. Select "PACER" mode.
  3. Set rate (e.g., 60-70 bpm).
  4. Dial up output (mA) until electrical capture (wide QRS after every pacer spike) AND mechanical capture (palpable femoral pulse).

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### HEART Score (For ED Chest Pain)
| Parameter | 0 Points | 1 Point | 2 Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **H**istory | Slightly suspicious | Moderately suspicious | Highly suspicious |
| **E**CG | Normal | Non-specific repolarization | Significant STD |
| **A**ge | < 45 | 45-64 | ≥ 65 |
| **R**isk Factors | No known RFs | 1-2 RFs | ≥ 3 RFs or prior MI/PCI |
| **T**roponin | ≤ Normal limit | 1-3x Normal limit | > 3x Normal limit |
* **Interpretation:** 0-3 = Low risk (discharge); 4-6 = Moderate risk (admit/obs); 7-10 = High risk (early invasive strategy).

### Modified Sgarbossa Criteria (For LBBB or Paced Rhythms)
* **Score ≥ 3 is highly specific for AMI:**
  1. Concordant STE ≥ 1mm in ≥ 1 lead (5 points).
  2. Concordant STD ≥ 1mm in V1-V3 (3 points).
  3. Proportionally excessive discordant STE (STE / S-wave depth > 0.25) (2 points).

### TIMI Risk Score (For NSTEMI / Unstable Angina)
* **Parameters (1 point each):** Age ≥65, ≥3 CAD risk factors, known CAD (stenosis ≥50%), ASA use in past 7 days, severe angina (≥2 episodes in 24h), ST changes ≥0.5mm, +ve cardiac marker.
* **Use:** Stratifies risk of 14-day mortality/new MI/urgent revascularization.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Ward vs CCU Admission Criteria
* **Coronary Care Unit (CCU):** STEMI post-PCI, high-risk NSTEMI, cardiogenic shock, life-threatening arrhythmias (VT/VF, 3rd degree AV block), unstable angina requiring IV infusions.
* **Medical Ward:** Low-to-moderate risk chest pain, stable AF, resolved SVT, HEART score 4-6 pending 2nd troponin.
* **ED Discharge:** HEART score 0-3, normal serial ECGs & troponins, non-cardiac etiology identified, arrange SOPD follow-up.

### Acute Complications of AMI (Mechanical & Electrical)
* **Electrical:** VT, VF, AV blocks, asystole (highest risk first 24h).
* **Mechanical (Days 3-14):** 
  * Ventricular free wall rupture → Tamponade / PEA arrest.
  * Papillary muscle rupture → Acute severe mitral regurgitation.
  * Ventricular septal rupture (VSR) → Left-to-right shunt.
* **Cardiogenic Shock:** Extensive LV failure.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a pacing spike from an artifact on an ECG? Answer: A true pacing spike is immediately followed by a wide QRS complex (ventricular pacing) or a P wave (atrial pacing), indicating electrical capture.
* Trap: Treating all ST elevations as STEMI and giving thrombolytics. Pearl: Always rule out benign early repolarization and pericarditis; look for reciprocal ST depressions which strongly confirm true ischaemia.
* Pitfall: Giving GTN to a patient with an inferior STEMI without checking right-sided leads. You risk causing severe, refractory hypotension if they have a right ventricular infarct (preload dependent).
* Viva Question: What is the significance of a dominant R wave in V1? Answer: RV hypertrophy, Posterior MI (check V7-V9), WPW (Type A), RBBB, or dextrocardia.
* Trap: Discharging a syncope patient with a "normal" ECG without checking for hidden killers. Pearl: Systematically screen every syncope ECG for Brugada, WPW, Long/Short QT, HOCM, and Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC) (epsilon waves).
* Pitfall: Synchronised cardioverting a patient but forgetting to press the "SYNC" button. The shock may land on the T-wave (R-on-T phenomenon), precipitating Ventricular Fibrillation (VF).
