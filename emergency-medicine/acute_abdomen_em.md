# Acute Abdomen (急腹症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology
### Clinical Definition of Acute Abdomen
* **Definition**: Sudden onset of severe abdominal pain <24-72h requiring urgent evaluation for potential life-threatening surgical or medical pathology.
### HK HA Triage Categories for Acute Abdomen
* **Category 1 (Critical - 0 min)**: Shock, impending arrest, suspected ruptured AAA.
* **Category 2 (Emergency - 15 min)**: Severe pain (pain score ≥8/10), overt peritonism, suspected ectopic pregnancy with stable vitals.
* **Category 3 (Urgent - 30 min)**: Moderate pain, stable vitals, unable to walk due to pain.
### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Elderly Patients**: Carry highest mortality risk; frequently present atypically with blunted physiological responses (absence of fever/tachycardia).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)
### Visceral vs Somatic Pain Pathways
* **Visceral Pain**: Stretching of autonomic nerve fibers (vague, poorly localized, dull, central).
* **Somatic Pain**: Irritation of parietal peritoneum (sharp, localized, ↑ severity with movement).
* **Referred Pain**: Shared central neural pathways (e.g., diaphragmatic irritation → shoulder tip pain).
### Pathological Mechanisms of the Acute Abdomen
* **Inflammation/Infection**: Release of inflammatory mediators → focal peritonitis (e.g., appendicitis).
* **Obstruction**: Bowel distension → venous congestion → bowel wall edema → compromised arterial flow.
* **Ischemia**: SMA occlusion/hypoperfusion → mucosal sloughing → transmural necrosis.
* **Perforation**: Spillage of gastric acid or feces → severe chemical or bacterial peritonitis.
* **Hemorrhage**: Intravascular volume depletion → hemorrhagic shock (e.g., ruptured AAA, ectopic).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)
### Primary Survey Findings (ABCDE)
* **Airway**: Aspiration risk if actively vomiting or depressed GCS.
* **Breathing**: Tachypnea (compensatory for metabolic acidosis), shallow breathing (splinting).
* **Circulation**: Tachycardia, hypotension, delayed capillary refill, mottled cool skin.
* **Disability**: Altered mental status (sepsis, hypoxia, hypoperfusion).
* **Exposure**: Visible bruising, abdominal distension, surgical scars, hernial orifices.
### Secondary Survey: Abdominal Inspection & Auscultation
* **Inspection**: Distension (obstruction/ascites), visible peristalsis, caput medusae.
* **Auscultation (Absent bowel sounds)**: Indicates paralytic ileus or diffuse peritonitis.
* **Auscultation (Tinkling/high-pitched sounds)**: Indicates mechanical bowel obstruction.
### Secondary Survey: Palpation & Percussion Signs
* **Rigidity**: Involuntary abdominal muscle spasm (severe peritonitis).
* **Rebound Tenderness (Blumberg's sign)**: Pain elicited upon sudden release of palpation pressure.
* **Percussion**: Tympany (bowel gas/obstruction), loss of liver dullness (pneumoperitoneum).
### Specific Eponymous Clinical Signs
* **Murphy’s Sign**: Inspiratory arrest upon deep palpation of RUQ (Acute Cholecystitis).
* **Rovsing’s Sign**: RIF pain elicited upon deep palpation of LIF (Acute Appendicitis).
* **Cullen’s Sign**: Periumbilical ecchymosis (Retroperitoneal hemorrhage / Severe pancreatitis).
* **Grey-Turner’s Sign**: Flank ecchymosis (Retroperitoneal hemorrhage).

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)
### Life-Threatening Differentials by Region
| Quadrant / Region | Critical Differential Diagnosis | Key Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Epigastric** | **Perforated Peptic Ulcer** (消化性潰瘍穿孔) | Sudden onset, board-like rigidity, +ve NSAID/steroid history. |
| **Epigastric** | **Acute Myocardial Infarction** (急性心肌梗塞) | Atypical presentation (inferior STEMI), diaphoresis, dyspnea. |
| **RUQ** | **Ascending Cholangitis** (急性膽管炎) | Charcot's Triad (fever, RUQ pain, jaundice), shock. |
| **Diffuse / Central** | **Ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm** (腹主动脉瘤破裂) | Pulsatile expansile mass, severe back pain, hypotension, syncope. |
| **Diffuse / Central** | **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia** (急性腸繫膜缺血) | Pain entirely out of proportion to physical signs, +ve AFib history. |
| **Diffuse / Central** | **Bowel Obstruction** (腸梗阻) | Colicky pain, absolute constipation, bilious/feculent vomiting. |
| **Lower Quadrants** | **Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy** (宮外孕破裂) | Female reproductive age, missed menses, syncope, peritonism. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)
### Point-of-Care Testing (POCT)
* **VBG**: pH, Lactate (ischemia/sepsis marker), Base Excess, Hemoglobin.
* **ECG**: Rule out inferior STEMI and AFib.
* **Urine hCG**: Mandatory in ALL females of reproductive age.
* **Urine Dipstick**: Leukocytes, nitrites, microscopic hematuria (renal colic vs UTI).
### Bedside Ultrasonography (POCUS)
* **FAST Scan**: Detect free intraperitoneal fluid (hemoperitoneum / ruptured ectopic).
* **Abdominal Aorta**: Measure maximal AP diameter (abnormal >3cm).
* **Biliary / Gallbladder**: Assess for gallstones, wall thickening (>3mm), pericholecystic fluid.
### Formal Laboratory Investigations
* **CBC**: Leukocytosis (infection), acute Hb drop (hemorrhage).
* **Renal Function Test (RFT)**: Urea, Creatinine (AKI, severe dehydration).
* **Liver Function Test (LFT)**: Bilirubin, ALP, ALT, AST (biliary obstruction).
* **Pancreatic Enzymes**: Serum Lipase (highly sensitive/specific for acute pancreatitis).
* **Type & Screen / Crossmatch**: Essential for surgical optimization.
### Emergency Imaging Modalities
* **Erect CXR**: Detect free subdiaphragmatic gas (pneumoperitoneum).
* **Abdominal X-Ray (AXR)**: Dilated bowel loops, multiple air-fluid levels (obstruction).
* **CT Abdomen & Pelvis (Triple Contrast vs IV Only)**: Gold standard for diagnosing obscure acute abdominal pathology (requires hemodynamically stable patient).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms
### Immediate Hemodynamic Resuscitation
* **Intravenous Access**: Secure 2x large-bore (14G/16G) peripheral IV lines.
* **Fluid Resuscitation**: Rapid bolus 500ml-1L Plasmalyte / Normal Saline (titrate to MAP ≥65 mmHg).
* **Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)**: Activate for suspected ruptured AAA/ectopic; transfuse O-ve uncrossmatched blood immediately if unstable.
### Analgesia & Antiemetic Regimens
* **Opioid Analgesia**: IV Fentanyl 1-2 mcg/kg OR IV Morphine 0.1 mg/kg (titrate to effect).
* **Antiemetics**: IV Metoclopramide 10mg OR IV Ondansetron 4-8mg.
### Empirical Antibiotic Therapy (HK HA Guidelines)
| Suspected Source | First-Line IV Antibiotic Regimen (HK HA) | Alternative for Penicillin Allergy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Biliary Tract** | Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin) 1.2g | Cefuroxime + Metronidazole |
| **Lower GI / Perforated Viscus** | Amoxicillin-Clavulanate 1.2g + Metronidazole 500mg | Cefuroxime + Metronidazole |
| **Severe Sepsis / Undifferentiated** | Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin) 4.5g | Meropenem |
### Vasopressor Support
* **First-Line Agent**: IV Noradrenaline (titrate 0.01 - 3 mcg/kg/min) via central line or large peripheral vein if refractory to fluid challenge.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)
### Nasogastric Tube (NGT) Insertion Mechanics
* **Positioning**: High Fowler's position (45-90 degrees).
* **Measurement**: Measure length from tip of nose → earlobe → xiphisternum.
* **Insertion**: Lubricate tip; advance horizontally along nasal floor. Ask patient to swallow water; advance tube in sync with swallowing.
* **Confirmation**: Aspirate gastric contents and test pH (must be ≤5.5), OR confirm via CXR.
### Urinary Catheterization Mechanics
* **Preparation**: Strict aseptic non-touch technique (ANTT), clean urethral meatus.
* **Anesthesia**: Instill 2% Lignocaine gel into urethra; wait 3-5 mins.
* **Insertion**: Advance Foley catheter until bifurcation; observe for immediate urine return.
* **Securing**: Inflate balloon with 10ml sterile water; retract gently to anchor at bladder neck.
### POCUS FAST Scan Mechanics
* **Probe Selection**: Curvilinear or phased array probe.
* **RUQ (Morison's Pouch)**: Mid-axillary line 8th-11th rib spaces; visualize liver, kidney interface.
* **LUQ (Splenorenal Recess)**: Posterior axillary line 7th-10th rib spaces; visualize spleen, kidney interface.
* **Pelvic (Pouch of Douglas)**: Suprapubic transverse and sagittal planes; visualize area posterior to bladder.
* **Subxiphoid**: Visualize pericardial space using liver as an acoustic window.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring
### Alvarado Score for Acute Appendicitis
| Clinical Feature | Score |
| :--- | :--- |
| Migratory RIF pain | 1 |
| Anorexia | 1 |
| Nausea / Vomiting | 1 |
| Tenderness in RIF | 2 |
| Rebound tenderness | 1 |
| Elevated Temperature (≥37.3°C) | 1 |
| Leukocytosis | 2 |
| Shift to the left of neutrophils | 1 |
* **Interpretation**: ≤3 (Appendicitis unlikely), 4-6 (Observe/Image), ≥7 (High probability, refer surgery).
### qSOFA Score (Quick Sepsis Related Organ Failure Assessment)
| Clinical Parameter | Score |
| :--- | :--- |
| Altered Mental Status (GCS <15) | 1 |
| Respiratory Rate ≥ 22 breaths/min | 1 |
| Systolic Blood Pressure ≤ 100 mmHg | 1 |
* **Interpretation**: Score ≥2 indicates high risk of poor outcome/mortality in patients with suspected infection.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications
### ED Disposition Criteria
* **Immediate Resuscitation Room / Emergency Theatre**: Ruptured AAA, ruptured ectopic pregnancy, tension pneumoperitoneum, unstable mesenteric ischemia.
* **Surgical Ward / ICU Admission**: Appendicitis, cholecystitis, bowel obstruction requiring IV therapy, optimization, or strict observation.
* **Discharge with ED Return Warning**: Uncomplicated renal colic (pain controlled, tolerating PO intake), uncomplicated gastroenteritis.
### Acute Complications of Abdominal Emergencies
* **Septic Shock**: Massive systemic vasodilation secondary to perforated viscus.
* **Hemorrhagic Shock**: Rapid exsanguination from vascular rupture.
* **Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS)**: Consequence of severe systemic inflammatory response (e.g., severe acute pancreatitis).
* **Aspiration Pneumonitis**: Chemical injury secondary to emesis and depressed airway reflexes.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the most sensitive bedside test to rule out a ruptured ectopic pregnancy in a reproductive-aged female? Answer: Point-of-care urine hCG test.
* Trap: Withholding opioid analgesia in the acute abdomen obscures physical signs and hinders surgical assessment. Pearl: Administer early, titrated IV opioid analgesia; it relieves distress and does NOT negatively impact diagnostic accuracy or mask peritonism.
* Pitfall: Discharging an elderly patient with vague abdominal pain and normal vital signs. Elderly patients frequently lack classic peritoneal signs or fever despite catastrophic pathology (e.g., mesenteric ischemia); maintain a high index of suspicion and low threshold for early CT imaging.
* Viva Question: What is the classic finding on an erect CXR in a patient with a perforated peptic ulcer? Answer: Free gas under the diaphragm (pneumoperitoneum).
* Trap: Assuming a normal serum lactate strictly rules out acute mesenteric ischemia. Pearl: A normal serum lactate occurs in up to 25% of acute mesenteric ischemia cases; diagnosis ultimately relies on high clinical suspicion and CT angiography.
