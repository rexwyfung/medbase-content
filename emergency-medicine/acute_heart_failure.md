# Acute Heart Failure (急性心臟衰竭)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Acute Heart Failure
* **Acute Heart Failure (急性心臟衰竭)**: Rapid onset or worsening of symptoms/signs of heart failure requiring urgent medical attention and hospital admission.
* **De Novo AHF**: First presentation of heart failure.
* **Acute Decompensated Heart Failure (ADHF)**: Exacerbation of chronic HF.

### HK Hospital Authority (HA) ED Triage Category
* **Triage 1 (Critical)**: Cardiac arrest, respiratory arrest, or severe respiratory distress with **SpO2 < 90%** despite high-flow O2.
* **Triage 2 (Emergency)**: Severe dyspnea, diaphoresis, unable to speak in full sentences, hemodynamically unstable (e.g., cardiogenic shock).
* **Triage 3 (Urgent)**: Mild-to-moderate dyspnea, stable vitals.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* High burden in HK AEDs, closely linked to aging population and prevalent **Coronary Artery Disease (冠心病)**.
* High seasonal variation (↑ winter admissions).
* Leading cause of acute medical admissions in HA hospitals.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiological Triggers (CHAMP)
* **C**: **Acute Coronary Syndrome (急性冠心症)** (Ischemia → ↓ contractility/papillary muscle rupture).
* **H**: **Hypertensive Emergency (高血壓急症)** (↑↑ Afterload → LV failure).
* **A**: **Arrhythmia (心律不正)** (AF with RVR, VT, Bradycardia).
* **M**: **Acute Mechanical Cause** (Valvular rupture, aortic dissection, tamponade).
* **P**: **Pulmonary Embolism (肺栓塞)** (RV strain/failure).

### Hemodynamic Derangements
* **↑ Preload**: Volume overload / sodium retention.
* **↑ Afterload**: Peripheral vasoconstriction / systemic hypertension.
* **↓ Contractility**: Ischemia / cardiomyopathy.
* **Result**: ↑ Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Pressure (LVEDP) → backward failure into pulmonary venous system → **Pulmonary Edema (肺水腫)**.

### Neurohormonal Activation
* ↓ Cardiac Output (CO) sensed by baroreceptors.
* **Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS)** activation: ↑ HR, ↑ contractility, ↑ vasoconstriction.
* **Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)** activation: ↑ Na+/H2O retention.
* Vicious cycle: Compensatory mechanisms ultimately worsen myocardial work and volume overload.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Airway & Breathing Findings
* **Tachypnea**: Rapid, shallow breathing.
* **Hypoxia**: ↓ SpO2.
* **Orthopnea / Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND)**.
* **Auscultation**: Bilateral basal fine crepitations (crackles).
* **Cardiac Asthma**: Expiratory wheeze due to peribronchial edema.
* **Frothy Sputum**: Pink/blood-tinged in severe pulmonary edema.

### Circulation Findings
* **Tachycardia**: Sympathetic overdrive.
* **Blood Pressure**: Variable. HTN common (sympathetic drive). Hypotension suggests **Cardiogenic Shock (心源性休克)**.
* **Elevated JVP (頸靜脈怒張)**.
* **Heart Sounds**: S3 gallop (volume overload), S4 gallop (stiff ventricle), new murmurs.
* **Peripheries**: Cold/clammy (hypoperfusion) or warm (vasodilated).
* **Peripheral Edema**: Pitting sacral or pedal edema.

### Disability & Exposure Findings
* **Altered Mental Status (AMS)**: Agitation, confusion, or lethargy (hypoxia / ↓ cerebral perfusion).
* **Skin**: Profuse diaphoresis, central cyanosis.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Primary Respiratory vs Cardiac Differentials
| Condition | Key Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Heart Failure** | Bilateral crackles, ↑ JVP, S3 gallop, peripheral edema, orthopnea. |
| **Exacerbation of COPD / Asthma** | Prolonged expiratory phase, widespread wheeze, hyperinflated chest, pursed-lip breathing. |
| **Severe Pneumonia** | Fever, purulent sputum, localized bronchial breathing, dullness to percussion, unilateral crackles. |
| **Pulmonary Embolism (PE)** | Pleuritic chest pain, clear lungs, hemoptysis, unilateral leg swelling (DVT), sudden onset. |

### Life-Threatening Cardiac Differentials
* **Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)** (Can be trigger or alternative diagnosis).
* **Cardiac Tamponade** (Beck's Triad: Hypotension, ↑ JVP, muffled heart sounds).
* **Aortic Dissection** (Tearing back pain, unequal pulses, widened mediastinum).

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care (POC) Tests
* **12-Lead ECG (心電圖)**: Ischemia/infarction (ST changes), arrhythmias (AF), LVH, low voltage (tamponade).
* **Arterial/Venous Blood Gas (ABG/VBG)**: Hypoxemia (Type 1 respiratory failure) or hypercapnia (Type 2, exhaustion). Met/Resp Acidosis.

### Bedside Ultrasound (POCUS) Findings
* **Lung Ultrasound**: ≥3 B-lines per rib space bilaterally (Interstitial syndrome / alveolar edema).
* **Cardiac Ultrasound (ECHO)**: ↓ Ejection Fraction (EF), regional wall motion abnormalities, severe valvular regurgitation, pericardial effusion.
* **IVC Scan**: Plethoric, dilated (>2cm), non-collapsing (<50% sniff variation) → ↑ CVP.

### Formal Laboratory Investigations
* **Cardiac Biomarkers**: High-sensitivity Troponin (hs-TnI/T).
* **Heart Failure Peptides**: NT-proBNP or BNP. (Rule-out cutoffs: NT-proBNP <300 pg/mL highly unlikely; >450 [age<50], >900 [age 50-75], >1800 [age>75] suggestive of AHF).
* **Renal Function (RFT)**: BUN, Creatinine, Electrolytes (K+, Na+) to check baseline before diuresis and assess cardiorenal syndrome.
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Anemia (trigger), leukocytosis (infection trigger).

### Radiological Imaging
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR) Findings (ABCDE)**:
  * **A**: **A**lveolar edema (bat-wing appearance).
  * **B**: Kerley **B** lines (interstitial edema).
  * **C**: **C**ardiomegaly (Cardiothoracic ratio >0.5).
  * **D**: **D**ilated prominent upper lobe vessels (Cephalization).
  * **E**: Pleural **E**ffusions (blunted costophrenic angles).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Vasodilator Therapy (1st Line for Hypertensive AHF)
* **Nitroglycerin (GTN)** (IV/SL):
  * Indication: SBP > 110 mmHg.
  * Mechanism: Venodilation (↓ preload), arterial dilation at higher doses (↓ afterload).
  * Dose: SL 0.4mg-0.6mg stat. IV infusion 10-200 mcg/min, titrate to BP.

### Diuretic Therapy (Volume Management)
* **Intravenous Loop Diuretics (利尿劑)**:
  * Drug: **Furosemide (Lasix)**.
  * Dose (Naïve): 20-40 mg IV bolus.
  * Dose (Chronic User): 1x to 2.5x their usual daily oral dose given as IV stat.
  * Mechanism: Rapid venodilation (within mins) followed by diuresis (within 30-60 mins) → ↓ preload.

### Inotropic / Vasopressor Support (Cardiogenic Shock - SBP < 90 mmHg)
* **Norepinephrine (Noradrenaline)**: 1st line vasopressor to maintain MAP > 65.
* **Dobutamine**: Inotrope to ↑ contractility (use carefully, can cause hypotension and arrhythmias).
* **Avoid**: Nitrates, beta-blockers, and aggressive diuresis until hemodynamically stable.

### Anticoagulation / Antiplatelet
* Only if concurrent ACS (Aspirin, Ticagrelor/Clopidogrel, Heparin) or PE/AF (Heparin/LMWH).

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV - BiPAP/CPAP) Mechanics
* **Indications**: Resp distress, SpO2 < 90%, use of accessory muscles despite O2 therapy.
* **Mechanics**:
  * Apply tight-fitting face mask.
  * CPAP starting pressure: 5-10 cmH2O.
  * BiPAP starting pressure: IPAP 10-12 cmH2O / EPAP 5 cmH2O.
  * Titrate FiO2 to maintain SpO2 > 94%.
  * Mechanism: Positive pressure pushes fluid out of alveoli, ↑ intrathoracic pressure → ↓ venous return (↓ preload) and ↓ LV transmural pressure (↓ afterload).
* **Consent**: Verbal consent, explain mask will feel tight and blow air to help breathing.

### Endotracheal Intubation (Invasive Ventilation) Steps
* **Indications**: Failure of NIV, AMS, exhaustion, cardiogenic shock.
* **Mechanics/Modifications**:
  * High-risk intubation due to poor cardiopulmonary reserve.
  * Pre-oxygenate with head-up position / NIV.
  * Cardiostable induction agents: Ketamine or Etomidate. Avoid Propofol (causes profound hypotension).
  * Paralytic: Rocuronium or Suxamethonium.

### Urinary Catheterization (Foley Catheter)
* **Mechanics**: Aseptic technique, insertion of 14-16 Fr silicone/latex catheter into bladder, inflate balloon with 10cc sterile water.
* **Purpose**: Strict hourly urine output monitoring to assess diuretic response.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### KILLIP Classification (For AHF in setting of AMI)
| Class | Clinical Features | Mortality Risk (approx.) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | No clinical signs of heart failure. | 6% |
| **II** | Crackles in < 50% lung fields, S3 gallop, ↑ JVP. | 17% |
| **III** | Frank pulmonary edema (crackles > 50% lung fields). | 38% |
| **IV** | Cardiogenic shock (SBP < 90, hypoperfusion). | 81% |

### NYHA Functional Classification (Baseline Chronic HF)
| Class | Symptom Severity |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | No limitation of physical activity. |
| **II** | Slight limitation (symptoms with ordinary activity). |
| **III** | Marked limitation (symptoms with less than ordinary activity). |
| **IV** | Symptoms at rest. |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Admission Criteria
* **Intensive Care Unit (ICU) / Coronary Care Unit (CCU)**:
  * Cardiogenic shock requiring vasopressors/inotropes.
  * Respiratory failure requiring intubation or prolonged NIV.
  * High-risk ACS triggering AHF.
* **General Medical Ward**:
  * Hemodynamically stable, symptomatic relief achieved with ED treatment, transitioning to oral therapies.

### Safe Discharge Criteria (Rare directly from ED in HA setting)
* Back to baseline NYHA class.
* Resolving pulmonary edema on clinical exam.
* Normal vital signs, no ischemia on ECG.
* Clear outpatient follow-up plan and medication compliance review.

### Acute Complications
* **Cardiogenic Shock**: Progressive pump failure.
* **Type 1 Respiratory Failure**: Refractory hypoxia requiring mechanical ventilation.
* **Arrhythmias**: VT/VF leading to cardiac arrest.
* **Cardiorenal Syndrome**: Worsening renal failure due to venous congestion and ↓ renal perfusion.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the physiological basis of using CPAP in acute pulmonary edema? Answer: CPAP increases intrathoracic pressure, which decreases both venous return (preload) and left ventricular transmural pressure (afterload), while stenting open fluid-filled alveoli to improve oxygenation.
* Trap: Treating acute heart failure automatically with Morphine (part of the old "LMNOP" mnemonic). Pearl: Morphine is no longer recommended routinely; it causes respiratory depression, reduces the efficacy of antiplatelets in ACS, and is associated with increased mortality and ICU admission rates.
* Pitfall: Giving massive fluid boluses to a hypotensive patient without checking for cardiac function. Point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) is critical to differentiate hypovolemia (requires fluids) from cardiogenic shock (requires inotropes/vasopressors), as fluids will rapidly worsen pulmonary edema.
* Viva Question: A patient presents with acute heart failure and a blood pressure of 220/110 mmHg. What is the most critical first-line pharmacological agent? Answer: High-dose intravenous nitrates (GTN). In sympathetic crashing acute pulmonary edema (SCAPE), afterload reduction is more urgent than diuresis.
* Pitfall: Waiting for formal chest X-ray to initiate treatment in severe respiratory distress. Clinical diagnosis (history, crackles, POCUS B-lines) is sufficient to start NIV and vasodilators immediately in the resuscitation room.
