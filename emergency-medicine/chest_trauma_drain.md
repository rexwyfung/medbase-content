# Chest Trauma & Chest Drain (胸部創傷與胸腔引流)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Chest Trauma Definition
* **Major Thoracic Trauma (嚴重胸部創傷):** Injury to chest wall, lungs, heart, great vessels, or diaphragm causing physiologic compromise (hypoxia, hypovolaemia, obstructive shock).
* Includes **Blunt Thoracic Trauma (鈍性胸部創傷)** and **Penetrating Thoracic Trauma (穿透性胸部創傷)**.

### HK Hospital Authority (HA) Triage Categories
* **Category 1 (Critical):** Cardiac arrest, tension pneumothorax, massive haemothorax, cardiac tamponade.
* **Category 2 (Emergency):** Flail chest, severe respiratory distress (SpO2 <90%), penetrating chest wound (haemodynamically stable).
* **Category 3 (Urgent):** Isolated rib fractures with moderate pain, normal vitals.

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong)
* Predominantly **blunt trauma** (MVCs, falls from height in elderly, industrial accidents).
* Penetrating trauma is rare but typically involves stab wounds (domestic/triad violence).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Tension Pneumothorax (張力性氣胸) Pathophysiology
* **One-way valve leak:** Air enters pleural space but cannot exit.
* ↑ Intrapleural pressure → lung collapse.
* Mediastinal shift to contralateral side → kinking of SVC/IVC.
* ↓ Venous return → ↓ Cardiac Output (CO) → **Obstructive Shock**.

### Massive Haemothorax (大量血胸) Pathophysiology
* Rapid accumulation of blood in pleural space (typically >1500mL or >200mL/hr for 2-4 hrs).
* Sources: Intercostal arteries, internal mammary artery, lung parenchyma, great vessels.
* Leads to combined **Hypovolaemic Shock** (blood loss) and **Obstructive Shock** (lung compression).

### Cardiac Tamponade (心包填塞) Pathophysiology
* Blood fills pericardial sac (usually penetrating trauma).
* Pericardium is inelastic → small volume (60-100mL) rapidly ↑ intrapericardial pressure.
* Restricts right heart filling (diastole) → ↓ stroke volume → **Obstructive Shock**.

### Flail Chest (連枷胸) & Pulmonary Contusion Pathophysiology
* ≥2 contiguous ribs fractured in ≥2 places → free-floating chest wall segment.
* **Flail segment:** Moves opposite to normal mechanics (drawn in during inspiration, pushed out during expiration).
* Primary morbidity = underlying **Pulmonary Contusion (肺挫傷)** (alveolar haemorrhage/oedema) → V/Q mismatch → hypoxia.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary Survey (ABC) General Chest Findings
* **Airway:** Tracheal deviation.
* **Breathing:** Tachypnoea, paradoxical chest wall movement, unequal expansion, open sucking wounds, absent/decreased breath sounds, hyperresonance, dullness to percussion.
* **Circulation:** Tachycardia, hypotension, narrowed pulse pressure, distended neck veins (JVP/JVD), muffled heart sounds.

### Specific Tension Pneumothorax Signs
* Severe respiratory distress.
* Hyperresonance to percussion (ipsilateral).
* Absent breath sounds (ipsilateral).
* Tracheal deviation (contralateral - late sign).
* Distended neck veins (+ve JVD).

### Specific Massive Haemothorax Signs
* Dullness to percussion (ipsilateral).
* Absent breath sounds (ipsilateral).
* Flat neck veins (-ve JVD) due to hypovolaemia.

### Specific Cardiac Tamponade Signs (Beck's Triad)
* Hypotension.
* Distended neck veins (+ve JVD).
* Muffled heart sounds.

### Specific Flail Chest Signs
* Paradoxical chest wall motion (visible during breathing).
* Subcutaneous emphysema.
* Bony crepitus on palpation.

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care Ultrasound (eFAST)
* **eFAST (急診床邊超聲波):** Essential primary survey adjunct.
* **Pleural views:** Absence of lung sliding, presence of "Barcode sign" on M-mode (pneumothorax). Anechoic fluid at costophrenic angle (haemothorax).
* **Cardiac views (Subxiphoid):** Anechoic fluid in pericardial space, right ventricular diastolic collapse (tamponade).

### Formal Imaging
* **Portable Chest X-Ray (CXR):** 
  * Identifies: Haemothorax (blunting of CP angle), pneumothorax (absent lung markings), widened mediastinum (>8cm), rib fractures.
* **CT Thorax with IV Contrast:** 
  * Gold standard for stable patients.
  * Detects occult pneumothoraces, aortic transections, pulmonary contusions.

### Bedside Bloods & ECG
* **ABG/VBG:** Assess pO2 (hypoxia) and pCO2 (ventilation failure).
* **ECG:** Arrhythmias (PVCs, AF) indicating blunt cardiac injury, low voltage QRS/electrical alternans (tamponade).
* **Bloods:** Group & Screen/Crossmatch (urgent), Troponin, Lactate (tissue perfusion).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Immediate ATLS Resuscitation Principles
* High-flow Oxygen (15L/min via Non-Rebreather Mask).
* Establish 2x Large-bore IV access (14G/16G).
* Permissive hypotension (Target SBP ~90mmHg) until haemorrhage control, EXCEPT in TBI.

### Pharmacological & Fluid Resuscitation
* **Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP):** 1:1:1 ratio of PRBC:FFP:Platelets for haemorrhagic shock.
* **Tranexamic Acid (TXA):** 1g IV over 10 mins, then 1g infusion over 8 hrs (administer within 3 hrs of injury).
* Limit crystalloids to <1L to prevent dilutional coagulopathy and worsening of pulmonary contusion.

### Analgesia Protocol for Chest Trauma
* Essential to prevent hypoventilation, atelectasis, and pneumonia.
* **1st Line (Multimodal):** IV Paracetamol 1g + NSAID (e.g., IV Ketorolac 30mg) if no contraindications.
* **2nd Line (Opioids):** IV Morphine or Fentanyl titration.
* **3rd Line (Regional):** Thoracic Epidural Analgesia (TEA) or Paravertebral Block (gold standard for severe flail chest).

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Needle Thoracostomy / Decompression (針刺減壓) Mechanics
* **Location:** 5th intercostal space, mid-axillary line (modern ATLS recommendation) OR 2nd intercostal space, mid-clavicular line.
* **Equipment:** Long (8cm) 14G angiocatheter.
* **Steps:** Insert needle over the superior rib margin. Listen for hiss of escaping air. Remove needle, leave catheter. IMMEDIATELY follow with chest tube.

### Tube Thoracostomy / Chest Drain (胸腔引流) Mechanics
* **Location:** "Triangle of Safety" (5th intercostal space, mid-axillary line, above nipple line, anterior to latissimus dorsi, lateral to pectoralis major).
* **Consent:** Verbal emergent consent if possible; implied consent for life-threatening shock.
* **Steps:**
  * Clean & drape. Infiltrate local anaesthetic (Lidocaine) down to pleura.
  * 3-4cm transverse incision.
  * Blunt dissection with Kelly forceps over the superior edge of the lower rib.
  * Puncture parietal pleura (feel the "pop").
  * Insert finger, sweep to clear adhesions and confirm intrapleural placement.
  * Insert chest tube (28-32 Fr for haemothorax, 24-28 Fr for pneumothorax) directed apically (air) or basally (fluid).
  * Connect to underwater seal drain. Suture (purse-string/mattress) and dress.

### 3-Sided Dressing (開放性氣胸處理) Mechanics
* For Open Pneumothorax.
* **Steps:** Apply occlusive dressing (e.g., Asherman Chest Seal or sterile plastic). Tape on 3 sides only. Allows air to escape during expiration (flutter valve effect) but prevents entry during inspiration.

### Resuscitative Thoracotomy (開胸急救) Criteria & Mechanics
* **Indications:** Penetrating thoracic trauma with witnessed loss of pulse/CPR <15 mins. (Blunt trauma CPR <10 mins is a relative/rare indication).
* **Steps:** Left anterolateral thoracotomy → open pericardium → relieve tamponade → cross-clamp descending aorta → direct cardiac massage/control haemorrhage.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### NEXUS Chest Rule (For blunt trauma CXR clearance)
* Validated tool to safely rule out significant intrathoracic injury without imaging.
* **Criteria (CXR NOT needed if ALL absent):**
  * Age >60
  * Rapid deceleration MOI (>40 mph, fall >20 ft)
  * Chest pain
  * Intoxication
  * Abnormal alertness/mental status
  * Distracting painful injury
  * Tenderness to chest wall palpation

### PIC Score (Predicting Complications in Chest Trauma)
| Variable | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Age >65 | +1 |
| ≥3 Rib Fractures | +1 |
| Chronic Lung Disease / Heart Failure | +1 |
| Flail Chest / Pulmonary Contusion | +1 |
| **Score ≥3** | Indicates high risk of pneumonia/mortality, warrants ICU/HDU admission. |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition & Admission Criteria
* **Discharge:** Isolated 1-2 rib fractures, normal SpO2, adequate pain control, normal serial CXR, safe home environment.
* **General Ward:** >2 rib fractures, mild hypoxia requiring low-flow O2, minor pneumothorax treated conservatively.
* **HDU/ICU:** Flail chest, severe pulmonary contusion, elderly with multiple rib fractures, intubated patients.
* **Operating Theatre (Cardiothoracic Surgery):** Massive haemothorax (>1500mL initial output or >200mL/hr for 2-4 hrs), cardiac tamponade, massive air leak.

### Acute Complications
* **Immediate (<24h):** Exsanguination, tension pneumothorax, hypoxic cardiac arrest.
* **Early (1-7 days):** ARDS, pneumonia (secondary to splinting/poor cough from rib fractures), empyema (infected haemothorax).
* **Late (>7 days):** Retained haemothorax (fibrothorax), persistent chronic pain.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of a massive haemothorax in blunt trauma? Answer: Rupture of the intercostal or internal mammary arteries, not the lung parenchyma itself.
* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate between cardiac tamponade and tension pneumothorax in a shocked patient? Answer: Tension pneumothorax will have hyperresonance and absent breath sounds; tamponade will have equal breath sounds and normal percussion.
* Viva Question: What are the absolute indications for urgent thoracotomy after chest drain insertion for haemothorax? Answer: Initial drainage >1500mL of blood, or ongoing bleeding >200mL/hour for 2-4 consecutive hours.
* Trap: Relying on a chest x-ray to diagnose a tension pneumothorax. Pearl: Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis; you must perform needle decompression or finger thoracostomy immediately before getting an x-ray.
* Trap: Placing a chest drain directly under the inferior margin of a rib. Pearl: The neurovascular bundle runs along the inferior margin. Always dissect and insert the tube over the superior margin of the lower rib.
* Trap: Diagnosing a widened mediastinum on a supine portable AP chest x-ray. Pearl: Supine AP films artificially widen the mediastinum. Confirm with a CT Thorax if the patient is haemodynamically stable.
* Pitfall: Giving copious IV crystalloids to a patient with a flail chest. This worsens underlying pulmonary contusion and causes rapid respiratory failure due to fluid extravasation into the damaged lung tissue.
* Pitfall: Waiting for tracheal deviation to diagnose tension pneumothorax. Tracheal deviation is a very late and inconsistent sign; rely on hypotension, respiratory distress, and absent ipsilateral breath sounds.
