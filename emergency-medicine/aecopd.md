# Acute Exacerbation of COPD (慢性阻塞性肺病急性發作)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition
* **AECOPD:** Acute worsening of respiratory symptoms beyond normal day-to-day variations requiring change in medication.

### ED Triage Category (Hong Kong)
* **Category 1 (Critical):** Respiratory arrest, profound shock, SpO2 < 80%.
* **Category 2 (Emergency):** Severe respiratory distress, altered mental state, severe hypoxemia/hypercapnia.
* **Category 3 (Urgent):** Moderate ↑ dyspnea, normal mental state, stable vitals.

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong)
* Peak incidence during winter/spring (influenza & cold weather surges).
* Leading cause of acute medical admissions in HK Hospital Authority (HA) hospitals.
* High 1-year mortality rate post-severe exacerbation in elderly populations.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiological Cascade
* Trigger → Airway inflammation → ↑ mucus production + mucosal edema → bronchoconstriction.
* Expiratory airflow limitation → **Dynamic Hyperinflation (氣體滯留)** → auto-PEEP.
* ↑ Work of breathing (WOB) → respiratory muscle fatigue (呼吸肌疲勞) → alveolar hypoventilation.
* **V/Q Mismatch (通氣與血流比例失調)** → hypoxemia (↓ PaO2) & hypercapnia (↑ PaCO2).

### Common Triggers
* **Infections (70-80%):**
  * Viral: Rhinovirus, Influenza A/B, RSV.
  * Bacterial: *H. influenzae*, *S. pneumoniae*, *M. catarrhalis*, *P. aeruginosa* (in severe COPD).
* **Environmental:** Air pollution (↑ PM2.5), cold exposure.
* **Iatrogenic:** Inappropriate high-dose oxygen (→ O2-induced hypercapnia), non-compliance, sedatives/opiates.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary Survey (ABCDE) Findings
* **A:** Secretion pooling, stridor (rare, implies alternative dx).
* **B:** Tachypnea, ↑ WOB, accessory muscle use (SCM, scalenes), pursed-lip breathing, paradoxical abdominal motion (diaphragmatic fatigue).
  * Auscultation: Prolonged expiratory phase, widespread polyphonic wheeze, silent chest (critical).
* **C:** Tachycardia, bounding pulse (CO2 retention), signs of **Cor Pulmonale (肺心病)** (↑ JVP, right ventricular heave, peripheral edema).
* **D:** Altered mental status (AMS), lethargy, confusion, flapping tremor / asterixis (CO2 narcosis).
* **E:** **Central Cyanosis (中樞性發紺)**.

### Secondary Survey & Cardinal Symptoms
* ↑ Dyspnea (氣促).
* ↑ Sputum purulence (膿痰).
* ↑ Sputum volume.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Life-Threatening Differentials Table
| Diagnosis | Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Pulmonary Edema (急性肺水腫)** | Fine basal crepitations, orthopnea, PND, S3 gallop, absence of fever/purulence. |
| **Acute Severe Asthma (急性哮喘)** | Younger age, completely reversible between attacks, personal/family history of atopy. |
| **Pulmonary Embolism (肺栓塞)** | Sudden onset pleuritic chest pain, hemoptysis, DVT signs, no ↑ sputum purulence. |
| **Pneumothorax (氣胸)** | Sudden unilateral pleuritic pain, unilateral ↓ breath sounds, hyperresonant percussion. |
| **Pneumonia (肺炎)** | Localized crepitations, bronchial breathing, high fever, dullness to percussion. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Bedside Investigations
* **Arterial / Venous Blood Gas (ABG/VBG):** Assess for **Type II Respiratory Failure (第二型呼吸衰竭)** (pH < 7.35, PaCO2 > 6.0 kPa / 45 mmHg) and hypoxemia.
* **ECG:** Rule out ischemia (ACS), arrhythmias (Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia, AF), right heart strain (P pulmonale, RAD, RVH).
* **Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS):** Rule out pneumothorax (absence of lung sliding) and pleural effusion; assess RV size/function.

### Laboratory Investigations
* **CBP:** Leukocytosis (infection), polycythemia (chronic hypoxia).
* **RFT / Electrolytes:** Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia (secondary to beta-agonists).
* **Cardiac Biomarkers:** Troponin (rule out concurrent MI / demand ischemia).
* **Microbiology:** Sputum C/ST, NPA for Respiratory Multiplex PCR (Influenza/RSV/COVID-19).

### Radiological Investigations
* **CXR:** Hyperinflation (flattened hemidiaphragms, > 10 posterior ribs visible), tubular heart, hyperlucency. Exclude consolidation, pneumothorax, pulmonary edema.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Oxygen Therapy Algorithm
* **Target SpO2:** Strictly 88-92%.
* Use **Venturi mask** (24% or 28% O2) to deliver precise FiO2.
* Avoid 100% O2 via non-rebreather unless in peri-arrest state.

### First-Line Pharmacotherapy
* **Bronchodilators:**
  * Salbutamol 2.5-5 mg + Ipratropium Bromide 0.25-0.5 mg via nebulizer. Repeat Q4H to Q6H.
* **Systemic Corticosteroids:**
  * Prednisolone 30-40 mg PO daily for 5 days.
  * If unable to tolerate PO: Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV Q6H.

### Antibiotic Protocol (HK HA Guidelines)
* Indicated if 3 cardinal symptoms present, OR 2 symptoms (if one is ↑ purulence), OR requires invasive/non-invasive ventilation.
* **1st Line:** Amoxicillin/Clavulanate 1g PO BD OR Amoxicillin 500mg PO TDS for 5-7 days.
* **Penicillin Allergic:** Clarithromycin 500mg PO BD or Azithromycin 500mg PO daily.
* **Suspected Pseudomonas (Severe/Recurrent):** Levofloxacin 500mg PO/IV daily.

### Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP) Algorithm
* **Indications:** Respiratory acidosis (pH 7.25 - 7.35, PaCO2 > 6.0 kPa) despite maximal medical therapy.
* Recheck ABG 1 hour post-NIV initiation. If pH < 7.25 or deteriorating → consider intubation.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### BiPAP Setup & Mechanics
* **Patient Position:** Upright at 45-90 degrees.
* **Interface:** Oronasal mask or full face mask. Ensure tight seal (prevent air leak) but avoid excessive pressure on nasal bridge.
* **Initial Settings:**
  * IPAP: 10-12 cmH2O (Target: adequate tidal volume and CO2 clearance).
  * EPAP: 4-5 cmH2O (Target: overcome intrinsic auto-PEEP).
  * FiO2: Titrate to SpO2 88-92%.
* **Titration:** ↑ IPAP by 2 cmH2O increments based on clinical response and 1-hour ABG.

### Endotracheal Intubation & Mechanical Ventilation Mechanics
* **Tube Selection:** Largest possible ETT (≥ 8.0 mm) to ↓ airway resistance and facilitate thick secretion suctioning.
* **Ventilator Strategy (Avoid Auto-PEEP):**
  * Mode: Volume Control (VC) or Pressure Control (PC).
  * Respiratory Rate: Low (8-10 breaths/min).
  * Tidal Volume: 6-8 ml/kg Ideal Body Weight.
  * I:E Ratio: Prolonged expiratory phase (1:3 to 1:5).
  * PEEP: Set to match 80% of measured auto-PEEP (usually 3-5 cmH2O).

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Anthonisen Criteria (Antibiotic Indication)
| Type | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Type I (Severe)** | All 3 cardinal symptoms (↑ dyspnea, ↑ sputum volume, ↑ sputum purulence) |
| **Type II (Moderate)** | 2 cardinal symptoms |
| **Type III (Mild)** | 1 cardinal symptom + 1 minor feature (URTI in past 5 days, fever, ↑ wheeze/cough, ↑ HR/RR) |

### BAP-65 Score (Severity/Mortality Risk)
| Parameter | Value | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **B**UN | > 9.0 mmol/L (> 25 mg/dL) | 1 |
| **A**ltered Mental Status | Present (GCS < 15) | 1 |
| **P**ulse | > 109 beats/min | 1 |
| Age > **65** | Present | 1 |
* *Interpretation:* 0 = Class I (Low risk), 1-2 = Class II-III (Moderate), 3-4 = Class IV-V (High risk, consider HDU/ICU).

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition Criteria
* **Home / OPD Follow-up:** Mild exacerbation, rapid response to ED bronchodilators, can walk comfortably, adequate social support, normal mental status.
* **EMW / General Medical Ward:** Failure to maintain SpO2 > 88% on room air, significant comorbidities (e.g., IHD, DM), poor home support.
* **HDU / ICU:** Impending respiratory arrest, severe acidemia (pH < 7.25), AMS, hemodynamic instability, failure of NIV.

### Acute Complications
* **Pneumothorax (氣胸):** Secondary to ruptured bullae from positive pressure ventilation (auto-PEEP or BiPAP/Invasive).
* **Cor Pulmonale / Right Heart Failure:** Acute worsening due to severe hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction.
* **Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP):** High risk if invasively intubated.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why do you target SpO2 88-92% in AECOPD? Answer: To avoid oxygen-induced hypercapnia caused by the Haldane effect, loss of hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (worsening V/Q mismatch), and reduced hypoxic ventilatory drive.
* Viva Question: What is the primary ventilator strategy when intubating a severe COPD patient? Answer: Provide a long expiratory time (I:E ratio 1:3 or 1:4) and low respiratory rate to prevent dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP) and subsequent cardiovascular collapse.
* Trap: Assuming wheezing always equals asthma or COPD. Pearl: Always rule out "cardiac wheeze" (acute pulmonary edema) and silent chest (critical airway obstruction where airflow is too poor to generate wheeze).
* Pitfall: Giving a severe AECOPD patient high-flow 100% oxygen and leaving them unmonitored. This leads to rapid CO2 retention, obtundation, respiratory arrest, and the need for emergent intubation.
* Trap: Setting BiPAP pressures backwards or with too narrow a gap. Pearl: IPAP must always be significantly higher than EPAP; the pressure support (IPAP minus EPAP) is what dictates the tidal volume and clears the CO2.
