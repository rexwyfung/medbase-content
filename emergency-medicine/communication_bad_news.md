# Breaking Bad News (宣佈壞消息)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology
### Clinical Definition
* **Bad News:** Any information that drastically and negatively alters a patient's view of their future.
* **ED Context:** Usually sudden, unanticipated, and catastrophic events leaving families completely unprepared.

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong)
* **Common ED Scenarios:** Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest (院外心臟停頓), Major Trauma (嚴重創傷), SIDS (嬰兒猝死症), Catastrophic Stroke.
* **Triage Category:** Cat 1 (Resuscitation) for the patient; family requires immediate diversion to a private space with a Medical Social Worker (醫務社工).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)
### Psychological MOI
* **Information Overload:** Acute stress response → ↑ sympathetic tone → amygdala hijacking → cognitive block (inability to process further information).
* **Acute Grief Response:** Denial → Anger → Bargaining → Depression → Acceptance (Kübler-Ross model).

### Biological Response to Emotional Shock
* **Vasovagal Syncope (血管迷走神經性昏厥):** Intense emotional distress → parasympathetic overtone → profound bradycardia & hypotension.
* **Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy (章魚壺心肌症):** Massive stress-induced catecholamine surge → apical ballooning of left ventricle.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)
### Assessing the Recipient (Family/Patient)
* **A/B/C:** Tachypnea (hyperventilation), marked pallor, diaphoresis.
* **D (Neurological/Psychiatric):** Acute agitation, catatonia, extreme emotional withdrawal, psychogenic non-epileptic seizures (pseudoseizures).
* **Secondary Survey (Perception & Body Language):**
	 * **Posture:** Crossed arms/legs (defensive/closed).
	 * **Eye Contact:** Intensely engaged vs completely avoidant.
	 * **Affect:** Uncontrollable crying, shouting, or completely flat/numb affect.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)
### Differentials for Recipient's Acute Reaction
| Differential | Key Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute MI / Takotsubo** | Chest pain, SOB, diaphoresis immediately following news delivery |
| **Vasovagal Syncope** | Transient LOC, pallor, rapid spontaneous recovery supine |
| **Acute Psychosis/Mania** | Bizarre behavior, hallucinations, grandiose statements |
| **Normal Acute Grief** | Crying, shock, numbness, anger directed at ED staff |
| **Panic Attack** | Hyperventilation, carpopedal spasm, fear of impending doom |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)
### Information Gathering (Pre-Meeting)
* **Verify Patient ID:** Strictly match patient identity with correct Next-of-Kin (NOK) ID.
* **Chart Review:** Confirm exact time of death, clinical sequence, and resuscitation efforts.
* **Inter-professional Huddle:** Brief nursing, MSW, and treating physicians before entering the family room.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms
### Algorithmic Approach to Breaking Bad News
| Protocol | Key Steps & Objectives |
| :--- | :--- |
| **SPIKES** (General) | **S**etting, **P**erception, **I**nvitation, **K**nowledge, **E**mpathy, **S**ummary |
| **GRIEV_ING** (Death) | **G**ather, **R**esources, **I**dentify, **E**ducate, **V**erify (Use "Dead"), **_** (Silence), **I**nquire, **N**uts & Bolts, **G**ive info |

### Resuscitating the Conversation (Managing Emotions)
* **Acknowledge:** "I can see this is a huge shock."
* **Validate:** "Anyone would be devastated by this news."
* **Silence:** Stop talking; wait for them to process.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)
### The Procedure of Delivering the News
* **Step 1 (Setting):** Secure private room, arrange chairs (sit down at eye level), mute all pagers/phones.
* **Step 2 (Warning Shot):** State clearly: "I'm afraid I have some very bad news."
* **Step 3 (Delivery):** Deliver facts in 1-2 sentences. Avoid medical jargon. Use the definitive D-word: "Despite our best efforts, he has **died (死亡)**."
* **Step 4 (The Pause):** Maintain absolute silence for 10-20 seconds. Do not interrupt their initial reaction.

### Telephone Notification (Exceptional Circumstances Only)
* **Step 1:** Verify identity of receiver and current environment (e.g., "Are you driving? Pull over.").
* **Step 2:** Direct delivery. Do not build false hope.
* **Step 3:** Request they attend the ED safely. Explicitly instruct them not to rush.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring
### Risk Factors for Complicated Grief Reactions
* Sudden/violent death (Trauma/Suicide/Homicide).
* Death of a pediatric patient (Paediatric Code).
* Lack of social support / isolated NOK.
* Language barrier / requirement for official interpreter.

### Capacity & Surrogate Decision Making
* **Mental Capacity Ordinance (精神健康條例):** Assess if patient has capacity to receive news or designate a surrogate.
* **Consent:** Only the patient can consent to sharing information with family unless they completely lack capacity.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications
### Family Disposition & Aftercare
* **Body Viewing:** Prepare family for visual state (ET tube, IV lines, traumatic injuries).
* **Referral:** Immediate Medical Social Worker (醫務社工) or Clinical Psychologist referral.
* **Logistics:** Issue death documents, arrange mortuary (殮房) transfer, return belongings.

### Acute Complications
* **Family:** PTSD, complicated grief, formal hospital complaints, self-harm.
* **Staff:** Burnout, compassion fatigue, moral injury.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the most critical word to use when notifying a family of a death? Answer: "Dead" or "Died". Never use ambiguous euphemisms like "passed away" or "gone".
* Trap: Delivering bad news over the telephone to save time. Pearl: Only do it if absolutely unavoidable (e.g., family overseas). Always attempt to bring them into the ED first.
* Pitfall: Rushing to fill the silence after delivering the news. Silence is a therapeutic tool; you must allow the family time to process the shock before explaining clinical details.
