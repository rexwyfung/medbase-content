# Pneumonia (肺炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Pneumonia Subtypes
* **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (社區獲得性肺炎) (CAP):** Infection of lung parenchyma acquired outside hospital setting (or <48h post-admission)
* **Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (醫院獲得性肺炎) (HAP):** Occurs ≥48h after admission, excludes incubating infections
* **Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (呼吸機相關肺炎) (VAP):** Occurs >48h after endotracheal intubation

### Hong Kong HA Triage Categories for Pneumonia
* **Cat 1 (Critical):** Respiratory arrest, SpO2 <80%, profound shock
* **Cat 2 (Emergency):** SpO2 <90% on high-flow O2, severe work of breathing, altered mental state (AMS), sepsis syndrome
* **Cat 3 (Urgent):** SpO2 90-94% on room air, stable vitals, pleuritic chest pain
* **Cat 4 (Semi-urgent):** Normal vitals, mild cough/fever

### Local Hong Kong Epidemiology
* Peak incidence during winter flu seasons
* High prevalence in elderly care home residents (OAH)
* High endemicity of **Tuberculosis (結核病) (TB)** → always consider as differential
* Prevailing pathogens: *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *Mycoplasma*, *Klebsiella* (endemic in Asia/alcoholics), viral (Influenza A/B, COVID-19)

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiological Cascade
* Microaspiration of oropharyngeal flora → overwhelms alveolar macrophages
* Pro-inflammatory cytokine release (IL-1, TNF-α) → capillary leak
* Alveolar exudate formation (pus/fluid filling alveoli)
* Impaired gas exchange → intra-pulmonary shunting → **V/Q mismatch (通氣/血流比例失調)** → hypoxemia
* Systemic spillover → **Sepsis (敗血症)** → vasodilatation → shock

### Pathogen-Specific Mechanisms
* **Typical (Strep pneumo):** Lobar consolidation, rapid onset, intense inflammatory response
* **Atypical (Mycoplasma/Legionella):** Interstitial infiltrates, intracellular replication, systemic/extrapulmonary symptoms
* **Aspiration:** Chemical pneumonitis + mixed anaerobes in dependent lung segments (R lower lobe)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary ABCDE Assessment Findings
* **Airway:** Sputum pooling, obstructed by secretions
* **Breathing:** Tachypnea (RR >30), use of accessory muscles, tracheal tug, paradoxical breathing, cyanosis
* **Circulation:** Tachycardia, hypotension (systolic BP <90), delayed capillary refill, bounding pulse (early sepsis)
* **Disability:** Confusion, agitation, lethargy (especially in elderly), GCS drop
* **Exposure:** Hyperthermia (>38.5°C) or hypothermia (<36°C)

### Secondary Survey Respiratory Signs
* **Inspection:** Asymmetrical chest expansion, splinting
* **Palpation:** ↑ tactile vocal fremitus over consolidation
* **Percussion:** Dullness over affected lobes
* **Auscultation:** 
* Bronchial breath sounds
* Focal coarse **crepitations (肺囉音)**
* Pleural rub
* ↓ breath sounds (if associated effusion)

### Classic Symptomatology
* Productive cough (purulent/rust-colored sputum)
* Pleuritic chest pain (sharp, worse on inspiration)
* Rigors and chills
* Dyspnea
* Atypical symptoms (Elderly): Falls, confusion, anorexia, ↓ mobility

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Life-Threatening Cardiopulmonary Differentials Table

| Differential Diagnosis | Key Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Pulmonary Embolism (急性肺栓塞)** | Sudden onset pleuritic pain, clear CXR, calf swelling, risk factors (immobility, active cancer) |
| **Acute Coronary Syndrome (急性冠心症)** | Crushing central chest pain, radiation to jaw/arm, diaphoresis, ECG changes |
| **Congestive Heart Failure (心臟衰竭)** | Bilateral basal fine crepitations, elevated JVP, bilateral leg edema, orthopnea |
| **Pneumothorax (氣胸)** | Sudden sharp pain, hyperresonant percussion, absent breath sounds unilaterally |
| **Aortic Dissection (主動脈剝離)** | Tearing/ripping chest pain radiating to back, pulse deficits, widened mediastinum |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Bedside Investigations & Point-of-Care
* **ECG:** Rule out ACS, check for cor pulmonale (S1Q3T3), AF (sepsis trigger)
* **VBG/ABG:** Assess pO2, pCO2 (hypercapnic failure), lactate (tissue hypoperfusion), pH (acidosis)
* **Urinalysis:** Legionella/Pneumococcal urinary antigens
* **POCUS (Lung Ultrasound):** 
* Focal B-lines
* Hepatization of lung tissue (tissue-like appearance)
* Shred sign (irregular pleural line)
* Dynamic air bronchograms

### Laboratory Tests
* **CBP:** Leukocytosis (left shift) or leukopenia (poor prognosis), ↓ Platelets (sepsis)
* **LRFT:** Urea ↑ (dehydration/CURB-65), Creatinine ↑ (AKI), transaminitis (Legionella)
* **Biomarkers:** CRP ↑, Procalcitonin ↑ (bacterial vs viral differentiation)
* **Cultures:** Blood cultures x2 (before Abx), sputum culture/AFB smear
* **Virology:** Multiplex PCR swab (Influenza, COVID-19, RSV, Mycoplasma)

### Radiological Investigations
* **CXR (胸部X光) (PA/AP):** Focal lobar consolidation, patchy infiltrates, cavitations (TB/Staph), pleural effusion
* **CT Thorax:** Rarely 1st line in ED. Reserved for undifferentiated sepsis, suspect empyema, hidden abscess, or resolving PE vs Pneumonia

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Oxygen Therapy & Airway Control
* **Target SpO2:** 94-98% (healthy), 88-92% (known COPD/Type II Respiratory Failure)
* **Delivery:** Nasal cannula → Venturi mask → Non-rebreather (NRB) mask 15L/min
* **Bronchodilators:** Salbutamol/Ipratropium nebulizers ONLY if concurrent bronchospasm (Asthma/COPD overlap)

### Empiric Antibiotic Therapy (HK HA Guidelines)
* **Mild CAP (Outpatient/Ward):**
* Oral Amoxicillin/Clavulanate 1g BD + Oral Clarithromycin 500mg BD
* Penicillin allergy: Oral Levofloxacin 500mg QD
* **Severe CAP (ICU/Resus):**
* IV Amoxicillin/Clavulanate 1.2g Q8H + IV Clarithromycin 500mg BD
* Penicillin allergy: IV Levofloxacin 500mg QD
* **HAP / Suspected Pseudomonas:**
* IV Piperacillin/Tazobactam (Tazocin) 4.5g Q8H OR IV Cefepime 2g Q8H
* **Suspected MRSA:**
* Add IV Vancomycin (weight-based dosing) or IV Linezolid

### Fluid Resuscitation & Sepsis Pathway
* **Fluid Challenge:** IV 0.9% NaCl or Plasmalyte (250-500ml boluses), max 30ml/kg if hypotensive
* **Vasopressors:** Noradrenaline (norepinephrine) via central line if MAP <65mmHg despite fluid resuscitation
* **Steroids:** IV Hydrocortisone 50mg Q6H ONLY if refractory septic shock

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV) Setup Mechanics
* **Position:** Upright 45-90 degrees
* **Equipment:** Tight-fitting oronasal mask, ventilator circuit
* **Settings (BiPAP):** Start IPAP 10-12 cmH2O, EPAP 5 cmH2O. Titrate IPAP for tidal volume, EPAP for oxygenation
* **Consent:** Explain claustrophobia risk, pressure sores on nasal bridge, need for immediate removal if vomiting

### Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) Mechanics
* **Position:** Sniffing position (unless C-spine suspect)
* **Pre-oxygenation:** Flush rate O2 via BVM with PEEP valve for 3 minutes
* **Drugs:** Push induction agent (e.g., Ketamine 1-2mg/kg) immediately followed by paralytic (e.g., Rocuronium 1.2mg/kg)
* **Mechanics:** 
* Perform direct/video laryngoscopy
* Identify vocal cords
* Pass endotracheal tube (ETT) under direct vision
* Inflate cuff, confirm with ETCO2 (waveform capnography)
* **Consent:** Implied in life-threatening emergency

### Pleural Fluid Aspiration (Thoracentesis) Mechanics
* **Position:** Sitting upright, leaning forward on a table
* **Mechanics:**
* POCUS to identify largest fluid pocket (usually posterior hemithorax)
* Sterile prep and drape
* Local anesthetic (Lidocaine 1%) infiltration down to parietal pleura
* Insert needle exactly OVER the superior rib margin (avoid neurovascular bundle)
* Aspirate 20-50ml for biochemical/microbiological testing
* **Consent:** Explain risks of pneumothorax, bleeding, infection, liver/spleen puncture

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### CURB-65 Score Components
| Parameter | Definition | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **C**onfusion | AMTS ≤8 or new disorientation | 1 |
| **U**rea | >7 mmol/L | 1 |
| **R**espiratory Rate | ≥30 breaths/min | 1 |
| **B**lood Pressure | SBP <90 mmHg or DBP ≤60 mmHg | 1 |
| **65** | Age ≥ 65 years | 1 |

### CURB-65 Interpretation
* **0-1:** Low risk (Outpatient management)
* **2:** Moderate risk (Short-stay admission or supervised outpatient)
* **3-5:** High risk (Severe pneumonia, requires inpatient admission, consider ICU if ≥4)

### qSOFA (Quick SOFA) Score Components
* **Respiratory Rate:** ≥22 breaths/min (1 point)
* **Altered Mentation:** GCS <15 (1 point)
* **Systolic BP:** ≤100 mmHg (1 point)
* *Score ≥2 indicates high risk of poor outcome in suspected infection.*

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition Criteria
* **Discharge:** CURB-65 0-1, normal vitals (SpO2 >94% on room air), tolerating oral intake, reliable social support
* **General Medical Ward:** CURB-65 2, requiring IV fluids, O2 therapy <4L/min, failed outpatient Abx, multiple comorbidities
* **ICU/HDU:** CURB-65 3-5, requiring invasive mechanical ventilation, persistent shock requiring vasopressors, severe acidosis

### Acute Complications
* **Respiratory:** ARDS, Parapneumonic effusion, Empyema, Lung abscess
* **Cardiovascular:** Septic shock, Myocardial Infarction (心肌梗塞) (demand ischemia), new-onset Atrial Fibrillation
* **Systemic:** Multi-organ failure (MOF), Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), Delirium

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of community-acquired pneumonia and its classic symptom? Answer: Streptococcus pneumoniae, classically presenting with rust-colored sputum.
* Trap: Assuming bilateral interstitial infiltrates on CXR in an elderly patient is always an atypical pneumonia. Pearl: Bilateral infiltrates in the elderly with a background of cardiac disease often represent acute heart failure. Always check POCUS and clinical fluid status.
* Pitfall: Withholding antibiotics in a hemodynamically unstable pneumonia patient to wait for CXR confirmation. Do not delay life-saving antibiotics; in suspected sepsis, administer broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour of presentation.
* Viva Question: Which atypical pneumonia is associated with hyponatremia and deranged liver function tests? Answer: Legionella pneumophila.
* Trap: Discharging a young patient with normal vital signs but an unrecorded respiratory rate. Pearl: Tachypnea is often the earliest sign of respiratory deterioration; never discharge a patient without a documented and normal respiratory rate.
* Pitfall: Inserting a chest drain blindly for a parapneumonic effusion without ultrasound guidance. Always use point-of-care ultrasound to locate the largest pocket and rule out loculations to avoid visceral injury.
