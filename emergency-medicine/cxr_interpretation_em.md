# Chest X-Ray Interpretation (胸部X光解讀)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Role of ED Chest X-Ray
* **Primary Modality**: First-line thoracic imaging in **Hospital Authority (HA)** A&E departments for cardiopulmonary & trauma presentations
* **Utility**: Rapid, low-dose radiation (≈0.1 mSv), high specificity for gross pathology [e.g., **Pneumothorax (氣胸)**, **Consolidation (肺實變)**]
* **Projections**: 
* **PA (Posteroanterior)**: Standard, upright, accurate cardiac silhouette
* **AP (Antero-posterior)**: Resuscitation room/ward, portable, magnifies heart
* **Lateral**: Localizes lesions, assesses posterior mediastinum & retrocardiac space (less common in acute ED trauma)

### HA Triage Categories for Urgent CXR Ordering
* **Cat 1 (Critical)**: Tension PTX, massive haemothorax (clinical dx first, portable CXR post-intervention or during active resus)
* **Cat 2 (Emergency)**: Suspected **Aortic Dissection (主動脈夾層)**, severe respiratory distress, query pulmonary oedema
* **Cat 3 (Urgent)**: Stable chest pain, CAP, rib fractures w/ mild distress

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Radiographic Densities Pathophysiology
* **Air (Black/Lucent)**: ↓ X-ray attenuation. Pathological in pleural space (PTX), bowel gas under diaphragm
* **Fat (Dark Grey)**: Pericardial fat pads, lipomas
* **Soft Tissue/Fluid (Light Grey/Opaque)**: Heart, diaphragm, blood, pus. Pathological in alveoli (oedema/exudate) or pleural space (**Pleural Effusion (胸腔積液)**)
* **Bone (White)**: Ribs, clavicles, spine
* **Metal (Bright White)**: Pacemakers, prosthetic valves, bullets, ECG leads

### Pathophysiology of Silhouette Sign (剪影徵)
* **Mechanism**: Loss of normal radiological border between two structures of identical radiographic density contiguous to each other
* **Significance**: Localizes anatomical pathology
* **Right Heart Border**: Right middle lobe pathology
* **Right Hemidiaphragm**: Right lower lobe pathology
* **Left Heart Border**: Lingula pathology
* **Left Hemidiaphragm**: Left lower lobe pathology

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Image Quality Assessment (RIP)
* **Rotation**: Medial clavicular ends equidistant to thoracic spinous processes
* **Inspiration**: Visiblity of **5-6 anterior ribs** or **9-10 posterior ribs** above diaphragm
* **Penetration**: Thoracic intervertebral disc spaces faintly visible behind cardiac silhouette

### Airway & Mediastinum Findings (A)
* **Tracheal Deviation**: Pushed away (Tension PTX, massive effusion) or pulled towards (lobar collapse, pneumonectomy)
* **Carina**: Normal angle <90°. Widening suggests Left Atrial Enlargement or subcarinal lymphadenopathy
* **Mediastinal Widening**: >8cm at aortic knob. Loss of aortopulmonary window. Suggests **Aortic Dissection**, mediastinitis, or mass

### Breathing & Lungs Findings (B)
* **Lung Markings**: Absence extending to periphery → **Pneumothorax**
* **Alveolar Pattern**: Fluffy, confluent opacities, **air bronchograms (支氣管氣像)** → Consolidation, APO, haemorrhage
* **Interstitial Pattern**: Reticular/nodular lines (Kerley B lines) → Pulmonary fibrosis, interstitial oedema
* **Meniscus Sign**: Blunting of costophrenic angles → **Pleural Effusion** (>200ml fluid)

### Cardiac Findings (C)
* **Cardiothoracic Ratio (CTR)**: >0.5 on **PA film** indicates **Cardiomegaly (心臟肥大)**
* **Heart Borders**: Loss of definition (Silhouette sign)
* **Water Bottle Sign**: Globular heart → **Pericardial Effusion (心包積液)**

### Diaphragm Findings (D)
* **Height**: Right normally 1-2cm higher than Left (due to liver)
* **Pneumoperitoneum (氣腹)**: Free air under diaphragm (crescent-shaped lucency) → Perforated viscus
* **Elevation**: Phrenic nerve palsy, eventration, lower lobe collapse

### Everything Else Findings (E)
* **Fractures**: Ribs (flail segment?), clavicles, scapulae, humeri
* **Subcutaneous Emphysema**: Radiolucent striations in soft tissues
* **Iatrogenic Tubes/Lines**: 
* **ETT**: Tip 3-5cm above carina
* **CVC**: Tip at cavoatrial junction
* **NGT**: Bisects carina, courses below diaphragm, tip in stomach bubble

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Differential Diagnosis of Complete Hemithorax Opacity (White-Out)
| Diagnosis | Tracheal Deviation | Key CXR Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Massive Pleural Effusion** | **Pushed AWAY** from opacity | Uniform opacity, meniscus if partial |
| **Total Lung Collapse** | **Pulled TOWARDS** opacity | Crowded ribs, elevated diaphragm |
| **Consolidation (Whole Lung)** | **Central** (No deviation) | Air bronchograms visible |
| **Pneumonectomy** | **Pulled TOWARDS** opacity | Surgical clips, missing ribs |

### Differential Diagnosis of Hyperlucent Hemithorax (Black-Out)
| Diagnosis | Key CXR Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pneumothorax** | Visible visceral pleural edge, lack of peripheral lung markings |
| **Tension Pneumothorax** | Tracheal/mediastinal shift *away*, depressed hemidiaphragm |
| **Severe Emphysema** | Bilateral hyperinflation, flattened diaphragms, bullae |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | **Westermark Sign** (focal oligemia distal to embolus) |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Adjunct Imaging for Equivocal CXR
* **Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS)**:
* **eFAST (Lung)**: Seashore sign (normal) vs Barcode/Stratosphere sign (PTX). Lung point = 100% specific for PTX
* **B-Lines**: ≥3 per rib space bilaterally → **Acute Pulmonary Oedema (急性肺水腫)**
* **CT Thorax (Contrast)**: Gold standard for suspected Aortic Dissection, PE (CTPA), traumatic aortic injury
* **CT Thorax (Non-Contrast)**: Occult pneumothorax, undifferentiated interstitial lung disease

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Pharmacological Management of Acute Pulmonary Oedema (APO)
* **Oxygenation**: High-flow O2 → Titrate to SpO2 94-98%
* **Vasodilator**: **GTN (Nitroglycerin) SL** 0.4mg-0.6mg, followed by **GTN IV Infusion** (start 10-20 mcg/min, titrate up to 200 mcg/min to ↓ preload/afterload)
* **Diuretic**: **Frusemide IV** 40-80mg (or match patient's usual daily oral dose)
* **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV)**: BiPAP/CPAP if persistent hypoxia or respiratory fatigue

### Pharmacological Management of Severe Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)
* **HA Antibiotic Guidelines (Severe/ICU admission)**:
* **Amoxicillin/Clavulanate IV** 1.2g Q8H + **Azithromycin PO/IV** 500mg daily
* If Penicillin allergic: **Levofloxacin IV** 500mg daily or **Ceftriaxone IV** 2g daily + **Azithromycin**
* **Fluid Resuscitation**: **Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl)** or **Plasmalyte** boluses (250-500ml) if hypotensive/septic, re-evaluate response

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Mechanics of Needle Thoracostomy (針刺胸腔穿刺術)
* **Indication**: Emergency decompression of Tension PTX
* **Site**: 2nd Intercostal Space (ICS) Mid-Clavicular Line (MCL) OR 5th ICS Mid-Axillary Line (MAL)
* **Mechanics**:
	* Clean site with Chlorhexidine
	* Insert 14G/16G long IV cannula perpendicular to skin, just **SUPERIOR** to rib margin (avoid neurovascular bundle)
	* Advance until sudden "give" and hiss of air
	* Remove needle, leave plastic cannula in situ, secure. Proceed immediately to formal tube thoracostomy

### Mechanics of Intercostal Tube Insertion (胸腔引流管置入術)
* **Site**: "Triangle of Safety" (5th ICS, anterior to mid-axillary line, lateral to pec major)
* **Consent**: Explain risk of bleeding, infection, organ perforation (lung, liver, spleen), failure
* **Mechanics**:
* Position pt supine w/ arm behind head
* Aseptic technique + Local Anaesthesia (Lidocaine 1% infiltration to pleura)
* Make 2-3cm transverse skin incision
* Blunt dissection w/ Kelly forceps over superior rib border into pleural space
* Insert gloved finger to sweep track, confirm lung down/no adhesions
* Insert chest tube (28-32 Fr for trauma/fluid, smaller for simple PTX) directed apically (PTX) or basally (effusion)
* Connect to underwater seal drain, secure w/ heavy silk suture (purse-string/mattress)

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Canadian C-Spine & NEXUS Chest Rules (CXR Ordering)
* **NEXUS Chest Rule** (Validates safe omission of CXR in blunt trauma):
* Age <60
* No rapid deceleration mechanism
* No chest pain
* No intoxication
* No abnormal alertness/mental status
* No distracting painful injury
* No chest wall tenderness

### HEART Score (Risk Stratification for Chest Pain + Normal CXR)
| Component | Criteria (Score 0 / 1 / 2) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **History** | Slightly / Moderately / Highly suspicious |
| **ECG** | Normal / Non-specific repolarization / Significant ST depression |
| **Age** | <45 / 45-64 / ≥65 |
| **Risk Factors** | None / 1-2 / ≥3 or atherosclerotic disease |
| **Troponin** | ≤Normal limit / 1-3x limit / >3x limit |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition Based on CXR Findings
* **Resuscitation/ICU**: Tension PTX, massive haemothorax, aortic dissection, multi-lobar pneumonia w/ septic shock
* **Surgical/Medical Ward Admission**: Large simple PTX requiring drain, CAP needing IV antibiotics, undiagnosed pleural effusion, rib fractures in elderly (high risk of pneumonia)
* **Discharge w/ OPD Follow-Up**: Normal CXR w/ musculoskeletal chest pain, clear viral bronchitis, isolated simple rib fracture (young, healthy, good pain control)

### Acute Complications Visible on Post-Procedural CXR
* **Malposition**: ETT in Right Mainstem Bronchus (causes left lung collapse)
* **Iatrogenic PTX**: Post-CVC insertion (Subclavian/Internal Jugular)
* **Subcutaneous Emphysema**: Leaking chest drain or displaced tube port outside pleural space

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate anterior versus posterior ribs on a CXR? Answer: Posterior ribs are strictly horizontal and more prominent; anterior ribs run obliquely downward toward the sternum.
* Trap: Assuming a normal sized cardiac silhouette on an AP film means no cardiomegaly. Pearl: AP films magnify the heart due to divergence of the X-ray beam; only use PA films to accurately calculate a CTR >0.5.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing a Tension Pneumothorax solely based on CXR findings. Tension PTX is a clinical diagnosis (hypotension, hypoxia, distended neck veins); if suspected, decompress immediately before awaiting the X-ray.
* Viva Question: What causes the "Deep Sulcus Sign" on a supine CXR? Answer: Air collecting anteriorly and basally in a pneumothorax in a supine patient, causing a deep, lucent costophrenic angle.
* Trap: Confusing a skin fold for a pneumothorax visceral pleural line. Pearl: Skin folds appear as thick white lines that extend beyond the rib cage, whereas a pleural line is a thin, sharp line with absence of lung markings distal to it.
* Pitfall: Inserting a chest drain blindly without a finger sweep. Always sweep the pleural space with your finger to lyse adhesions and ensure the lung is deflated to avoid intraparenchymal tube placement.
