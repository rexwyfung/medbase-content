# Altered Consciousness & Coma (意識改變及昏迷)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Definitions of Impaired Consciousness
* **Altered Mental Status (AMS)**: Broad term for decline in arousal (wakefulness) or cognition (awareness)
* **Delirium (譫妄)**: Acute, fluctuating alteration in attention & cognition
* **Coma (昏迷)**: Profound state of unarousable unresponsiveness; eyes closed, no sleep-wake cycles
* **Vegetative State (植物人狀態)**: Return of sleep-wake cycles but complete lack of cognitive awareness

### HK Hospital Authority (HA) Triage Categories
* **Category 1 (Critical)**: GCS ≤ 9, actively seizing, unrecordable BP, extreme hypoglycemia
* **Category 2 (Emergency)**: GCS 10-13, acute severe confusion, sudden severe headache, suspected acute stroke within therapeutic window
* **Category 3 (Urgent)**: GCS 14-15 with mild acute confusion, normal vital signs

### Local Epidemiology & Presentation
* **Major HK ED etiologies**: Intracranial hemorrhage (腦出血), acute ischemic stroke (缺血性中風), sepsis (敗血症) (especially pneumonia/UTI in elderly), hypoglycemia (低血糖)
* **Toxicological**: Ethanol intoxication, paracetamol OD, CO poisoning (charcoal burning suicide)

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Reticular Activating System (RAS) Dysfunction
* **Anatomy**: RAS located in upper pons & midbrain, projects to thalami & bilateral cerebral cortices
* **Coma MOI**: Requires either bilateral diffuse cortical dysfunction OR focal destruction/compression of brainstem RAS

### Major Pathophysiological Categories
* **Toxic-Metabolic Encephalopathy**: Symmetrical, diffuse interruption of energy substrate delivery or cellular metabolism (e.g., hypoglycemia, hypoxia, toxins)
* **Supratentorial Mass Effect**: Expanding lesion (tumor, EDH, SDH, ICH) → ↑ ICP → midline shift → downward uncal herniation (小腦幕切跡疝) → RAS compression
* **Infratentorial Destructive Lesion**: Direct brainstem destruction (e.g., pontine hemorrhage, basilar artery occlusion) → immediate coma

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Vital Sign & Breathing Pattern Abnormalities
* **Cushing Reflex (庫興氏反射)**: ↑ BP, ↓ HR, irregular respirations (late sign of ↑ ICP)
* **Cheyne-Stokes Respiration**: Crescendo-decrescendo breathing with apneas (bilateral hemispheric or diencephalic lesion)
* **Central Neurogenic Hyperventilation**: Sustained rapid deep breathing (midbrain/pontine lesion)
* **Apneustic Breathing**: Prolonged inspiratory pauses (lower pontine lesion)
* **Ataxic (Biot's) Breathing**: Completely irregular pattern (medullary lesion, impending arrest)

### Neurological & Pupillary Assessment
* **Pinpoint Pupils**: Opioid toxidrome, pontine hemorrhage (橋腦出血)
* **Unilateral Fixed & Dilated Pupil**: Ipsilateral CN III palsy due to uncal herniation
* **Bilateral Fixed & Dilated Pupils**: Severe midbrain damage, massive anoxia, sympathomimetic toxidrome
* **Conjugate Gaze Deviation**:
    * Looks *towards* hemispheric destructive lesion (e.g., MCA stroke)
    * Looks *away* from hemispheric irritative lesion (seizure) or pontine lesion

### Motor Responses & Posturing
* **Decorticate Posturing (去皮質僵直)**: Abnormal flexion of upper limbs, extension of lower limbs (lesion *above* red nucleus / thalamus)
* **Decerebrate Posturing (去大腦僵直)**: Abnormal extension of all limbs (lesion *below* red nucleus / midbrain/pons) → worse prognosis
* **Oculocephalic Reflex (Doll's Eyes)**: Intact brainstem = eyes move opposite to head turning. Absent = eyes remain fixed (severe brainstem dysfunction)

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Immediate Bedside / Point-of-Care (POC)
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG)**: Mandatory for ANY altered patient (Rule out hypoglycemia)
* **Venous Blood Gas (VBG)**: Rapid pH, pCO2 (narcosis), Lactate (sepsis/ischemia), COHb (Carbon Monoxide)
* **12-Lead ECG**: QTc prolongation (toxins), STEMI (cardiogenic shock), arrhythmias
* **POC Ultrasound (POCUS)**: Cardiac output, IVC status, eFAST (if trauma suspected)

### Acute Laboratory Investigations
* **Routine Bloods**: CBC, LRFT (U&E, Cr, LFTs), Bone Profile (Ca2+, Mg2+)
* **Coagulation Profile**: PT/APTT/INR (crucial for ICH workup/reversal)
* **Toxicology**: Paracetamol/Salicylate levels (standard screen), Serum Osmolality (for osmolar gap)
* **Septic Workup**: Blood cultures x2, Urinalysis, CXR

### Definitive Neuroimaging & Diagnostics
* **Non-Contrast CT Brain (CTB)**: 1st line to rule out acute hemorrhage, mass effect, large infarct, hydrocephalus
* **CT Angiogram (CTA) Brain/Neck**: If basilar thrombosis or large vessel occlusion (LVO) suspected
* **Lumbar Puncture (LP)**: CSF analysis for WBC, RBC, Protein, Glucose, C/ST, viral PCR (post-CT if mass effect ruled out)
* **EEG**: Rule out non-convulsive status epilepticus (NCSE) (usually ordered in ward/ICU)

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### "Coma Cocktail" & Empirical Toxidrome Reversals
* **Hypoglycemia (CBG < 4.0 mmol/L)**: 50ml of 50% Dextrose IV OR 250ml 10% Dextrose IV
* **Wernicke's Encephalopathy Prevention**: IV Thiamine 500mg (administer *before* or *with* glucose in chronic alcoholics/malnourished)
* **Opioid Overdose (RR < 12, pinpoint pupils)**: IV/IM Naloxone 0.4mg - 2.0mg (titrate to respiratory rate, not GCS)

### Elevated Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Management
* **Head Elevation**: Prop head of bed 30° to ↑ venous drainage
* **Osmotic Diuresis**: IV Mannitol 20% (0.5 - 1.0 g/kg) OR IV 3% Hypertonic Saline (250ml over 10-15 mins)
* **Hyperventilation**: Temporizing measure only! Titrate vent to pCO2 30-35 mmHg to cause cerebral vasoconstriction (avoid <30 mmHg to prevent ischemia)
* **Seizure Prophylaxis**: IV Levetiracetam 1-2g OR IV Phenytoin 20mg/kg (if SAH/TBI with active seizures)

### Empirical Antimicrobial Therapy (Suspected CNS Infection)
* **Bacterial Meningitis (細菌性腦膜炎)**: IV Ceftriaxone 2g (local HK protocol) + IV Dexamethasone 10mg (given before/with 1st dose antibiotics)
* **Listeria Coverage (>50yo, immunocompromised)**: Add IV Ampicillin 2g
* **Viral Encephalitis (HSV)**: IV Acyclovir 10mg/kg

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) Mechanics
* **Preparation**: 100% O2 via BVM/Non-rebreather. Position in sniffing position (if no trauma)
* **Induction**: Push rapidly (e.g., IV Ketamine 1-2mg/kg OR Propofol 1-2mg/kg)
* **Paralysis**: Push rapidly (e.g., IV Rocuronium 1.2mg/kg OR Suxamethonium 1.5mg/kg)
* **Execution**: Laryngoscopy (direct/video), pass endotracheal tube (ETT) through vocal cords, inflate cuff
* **Confirmation**: End-tidal CO2 (ETCO2) waveform color change + auscultation + CXR
* **Consent**: Implied consent (Emergency life-saving procedure)

### Lumbar Puncture (LP) Mechanics
* **Positioning**: Lateral decubitus with hips/knees maximally flexed to chest (opens interspinous spaces)
* **Site Preparation**: Aseptic technique, locate L3/L4 or L4/L5 interspace (Tuffier's line)
* **Needle Insertion**: Insert spinal needle (with stylet) aiming towards umbilicus. Remove stylet once past ligamentum flavum/dura to check for CSF flow
* **Manometry**: Attach manometer to measure opening pressure (normal < 20 cmH2O)
* **Collection**: Collect 3-4 separate tubes (1-2ml each) for micro/chem/path
* **Consent**: Require proxy/NOK written consent if patient is comatose, citing risks (infection, bleeding, headache, rare herniation)

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) (格拉斯哥昏迷指數)

| Category | Score & Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Eye Opening (E)** | 4 = Spontaneous, 3 = To speech, 2 = To pain, 1 = None |
| **Verbal Response (V)** | 5 = Oriented, 4 = Confused, 3 = Inappropriate words, 2 = Incomprehensible sounds, 1 = None (T = Intubated) |
| **Motor Response (M)** | 6 = Obeys commands, 5 = Localizes pain, 4 = Withdraws from pain, 3 = Abnormal flexion (Decorticate), 2 = Abnormal extension (Decerebrate), 1 = None |

### AVPU Assessment Tool
| Score | Clinical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Alert & awake |
| **V** | Responds to Verbal stimuli |
| **P** | Responds to Painful stimuli |
| **U** | Unresponsive |

### FOUR Score (Full Outline of UnResponsiveness)
| Component | Grading (0 to 4 max per category) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Eye Response** | 4 = tracking, 3 = open/not tracking, 2 = closed/open to voice, 1 = closed/open to pain, 0 = remains closed |
| **Motor Response** | 4 = thumbs up/fist, 3 = localizes, 2 = flexion, 1 = extension, 0 = no response/status myoclonus |
| **Brainstem Reflexes** | 4 = pupil/corneal present, 3 = one pupil wide/fixed, 2 = pupil OR corneal absent, 1 = both absent, 0 = absent + absent cough |
| **Respiration** | 4 = regular, 3 = Cheyne-Stokes, 2 = irregular, 1 = breathes above vent, 0 = breathes at vent rate/apnea |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Triage & Disposition Pathways
* **Intensive Care Unit (ICU)**: GCS ≤ 8 (intubated), requiring ongoing mechanical ventilation, vasopressor support, or refractory status epilepticus
* **Neurosurgery (NS)**: Acute ICH, SDH, EDH, SAH, or structural mass requiring external ventricular drain (EVD) or craniotomy
* **Emergency Medicine Ward (EMW) / Medical Ward**: Readily reversible toxic/metabolic causes (e.g., cleared hypoglycemia, resolved post-ictal state, mild drug intoxication observing washout)

### Acute Complications of Coma
* **Aspiration Pneumonitis / Pneumonia (吸入性肺炎)**: Loss of gag reflex → gastric contents in lungs
* **Anoxic Brain Injury**: Prolonged downtime → irreversible cortical necrosis
* **Secondary Traumatic Injuries**: Undetected cervical spine injuries, corneal abrasions (loss of blink reflex), pressure injuries (decubitus ulcers)

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Relying on Flumazenil as part of the routine "coma cocktail" for suspected benzodiazepine overdose.
* Pearl: Flumazenil is heavily contraindicated in undifferentiated coma, chronic benzo users, or suspected TCA overdose due to the high risk of precipitating intractable status epilepticus. Support airway and wait for drug washout instead.
* Viva Question: What is the significance of the Cushing Reflex in a comatose patient?
* Answer: It indicates critically elevated Intracranial Pressure (ICP) causing brainstem ischemia and impending uncal/tonsillar herniation. The systemic hypertension is a reflex to maintain cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP).
* Pitfall: Administering large doses of IV dextrose to a malnourished/alcoholic comatose patient without prior thiamine.
* Pearl: Dextrose drives cellular metabolism, abruptly depleting marginal thiamine stores. This rapidly precipitates irreversible Wernicke's Encephalopathy (ataxia, ophthalmoplegia, confusion). Always give IV Thiamine before or concurrently with glucose.
* Viva Question: At what GCS score is definitive airway management (intubation) strictly indicated, and why?
* Answer: GCS ≤ 8 ("Less than 8, intubate"). At this level, airway reflexes (gag, swallow, cough) are statistically likely to be profoundly impaired or absent, rendering the patient unable to protect their airway from aspiration.
