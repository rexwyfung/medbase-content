# Common Poisoning & Toxidromes (常見中毒與毒性症候群)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Poisoning
* **Poisoning (中毒)**: Exposure (ingestion, inhalation, injection, absorption) to a substance in amounts causing acute physiological harm or death.
* **Toxidrome (毒性症候群)**: Constellation of clinical signs/symptoms diagnostic of specific toxin classes.

### Hong Kong ED Triage Categories (HA Guidelines)
* **Category 1 (Resuscitation - 0 min)**: Cardiac arrest, shock, GCS ≤8, actively seizing, severe respiratory distress.
* **Category 2 (Emergency - <15 min)**: GCS 9-13, unstable vitals, known lethal dose ingestion (e.g., severe corrosive, massive paracetamol, cyclic antidepressants).
* **Category 3 (Urgent - <30 min)**: Stable vitals, asymptomatic but history of potentially toxic exposure, intentional self-harm requiring observation.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Most Common Overdoses**: **Paracetamol (撲熱息痛)**, Benzodiazepines (BDZ), cough mixtures (Dextromethorphan/Codeine).
* **Most Lethal**: **Carbon Monoxide (一氧化碳)** (charcoal burning suicide), herbicides (Paraquat), corrosive household agents.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiology of Anticholinergic Toxidrome
* **MOI**: Competitive antagonism of muscarinic acetylcholine receptors.
* **Target Organs**: CNS, smooth muscle, secretory glands.
* **Result**: ↓ parasympathetic tone → ↑ sympathetic override.

### Pathophysiology of Cholinergic Toxidrome
* **MOI**: Inhibition of acetylcholinesterase (AChE) (e.g., Organophosphates).
* **Result**: Accumulation of ACh at muscarinic/nicotinic synapses → continuous receptor overstimulation → eventual depolarization block.

### Pathophysiology of Opioid Toxidrome
* **MOI**: Agonism of mu (μ), kappa (κ), delta (δ) opioid receptors.
* **Result**: CNS depression, ↓ brainstem respiratory center sensitivity to CO2 → respiratory failure.

### Pathophysiology of Sympathomimetic Toxidrome
* **MOI**: ↑ catecholamine release, ↓ reuptake, or direct alpha/beta receptor agonism (e.g., Cocaine, Amphetamines).
* **Result**: Massive sympathetic surge → hypermetabolic state, vasospasm.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary ABCDE Findings in Poisoning
* **Airway**: Gurgling, stridor (corrosive injury), vomitus blocking airway.
* **Breathing**: ↓ RR (opioids), Kussmaul breathing (toxic alcohols/salicylates).
* **Circulation**: Bradycardia/hypotension (beta-blockers, CCBs), tachycardia/hypertension (sympathomimetics).
* **Disability**: Altered GCS, seizures, agitation.
* **Exposure**: Needle tracks, medical patches, chemical burns, distinct breath odors (bitter almond = cyanide, fruity = DKA/isopropanol).

### Anticholinergic Physical Findings
* **Mental Status**: Delirium, hallucinations, "mad as a hatter".
* **Skin/Mucosa**: Dry, flushed, hot, "dry as a bone", "red as a beet", "hot as a hare".
* **Pupils**: Mydriasis (dilated), "blind as a bat".
* **GI/GU**: Absent bowel sounds, urinary retention.

### Cholinergic Physical Findings (SLUDGE / DUMBELS)
* **Muscarinic**: Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, GI cramping, Emesis.
* **Nicotinic**: Muscle fasciculations, weakness, paralysis.
* **Vitals**: Bradycardia (muscarinic) OR tachycardia (nicotinic).
* **Pupils**: Miosis (pinpoint).

### Opioid Physical Findings
* **Respiration**: RR < 12/min, shallow breathing.
* **CNS**: Sedation to deep coma.
* **Pupils**: Miosis (pinpoint).
* **Skin**: Cyanosis (if hypoxic).

### Sympathomimetic Physical Findings
* **CNS**: Agitation, paranoia, hyperreflexia.
* **Skin/Mucosa**: Diaphoresis (sweaty) (distinguishes from anticholinergic).
* **Pupils**: Mydriasis (dilated).
* **Vitals**: Severe tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Non-Toxicological Causes of Altered Mental Status
| Category | Differential Diagnosis | Key Distinguishing Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolic** | Hypoglycemia | Differentiate via BGT; mimics sympathomimetic or alcohol intoxication. |
| **Neurological** | Intracranial Hemorrhage / Stroke | Focal neurological deficits, asymmetrical pupils, signs of trauma. |
| **Infectious** | Meningitis / Encephalitis / Sepsis | Fever (mimics anticholinergic/sympathomimetic), nuchal rigidity, gradual onset. |
| **Psychiatric** | Acute Psychosis | Normal vitals (usually), chronic history, no typical toxidrome pupils/skin changes. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care Testing (POCT) in Toxicology
* **Blood Glucose Test (BGT)**: Rule out hypoglycemia (mandatory for altered GCS).
* **ECG (12-Lead)**:
  * **QRS prolongation (>100ms)**: TCA toxicity (sodium channel blockade).
  * **QTc prolongation**: Antipsychotics, methadone, macrolides (risk of Torsades).
  * **Bradycardia/AV Block**: Beta-blockers, CCBs, Digoxin.
* **VBG/ABG**: Assess pH, pCO2, lactate, Co-oximetry (carboxyhemoglobin for CO, methemoglobin).

### Urgent Laboratory Investigations
* **Toxicology Screens**:
  * **Paracetamol (撲熱息痛) level**: Done at 4 hours post-ingestion.
  * **Salicylate level**: Check if unexplained metabolic acidosis.
  * **Ethanol/Toxic Alcohol levels**: If high osmolar gap.
* **Blood Chemistries**:
  * **Renal Function (RFT)**: U&E, creatinine (clearance of toxins).
  * **Liver Function (LFT)**: ALT/AST baseline (essential for paracetamol overdose).
  * **CK level**: Rule out rhabdomyolysis (sympathomimetics, prolonged coma).
* **Urine**: Pregnancy test (UPT) for females of childbearing age, urine toxicology screen (limited acute value, mainly for psych disposition).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Universal Toxicology Resuscitation Principles
* **Airway/Breathing**: Supplemental O2.
* **Circulation**: IV fluid resuscitation (Normal Saline 1-2L bolus for hypotension).
* **Seizure Control**: IV Diazepam or Lorazepam (Do NOT use phenytoin for toxin-induced seizures).
* **Toxin Decontamination**:
  * **Activated Charcoal (活性碳)**: 1g/kg PO/NG tube within 1 hour of ingestion (Contraindicated if ↓ GCS without protected airway, corrosives, metals, alcohols).
  * **Whole Bowel Irrigation (WBI)**: Polyethylene glycol (PEG) for slow-release meds, body packers, iron/lithium.

### Key Antidotes for Specific Toxins
| Toxin | Antidote | Dosing / Endpoints |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Opioids** | **Naloxone (納洛酮)** | 0.4 - 2 mg IV; titrate to respiratory rate > 12/min (not to full wakefulness). |
| **Paracetamol** | **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** | 21-hour IV protocol; started if level crosses nomogram line. |
| **Benzodiazepines** | **Flumazenil (氟馬西尼)** | Rarely used in ED (risk of intractable seizures); supportive care preferred. |
| **Organophosphates** | **Atropine** + **Pralidoxime** | Atropine: titrate to clear lung secretions. Pralidoxime: removes enzyme block. |
| **TCA** | **Sodium Bicarbonate** | IV boluses; indicated if QRS >100ms or wide-complex dysrhythmias. |
| **Digoxin** | **Digoxin-specific Fab** | Given for life-threatening hyperkalemia or dysrhythmias. |

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Mechanics of Gastric Lavage (洗胃)
* **Indications restriction**: Only for life-threatening ingestion <1 hour, no antidote available, fails charcoal.
* **Consent**: Verbal consent; explain risks of aspiration, esophageal perforation, hypoxia.
* **Patient Positioning**: Left lateral decubitus with head down 20 degrees.
* **Insertion**: Measure 36-40F large-bore orogastric tube (mouth to ear to xiphisternum). Lubricate and insert.
* **Confirmation**: Aspirate gastric contents; check pH or auscultate over epigastrium while injecting air.
* **Lavage**: Instill 200-250 mL warm 0.9% NaCl aliquots. Drain by gravity. Repeat until effluent is completely clear.

### Mechanics of Endotracheal Intubation for Overdose
* **Purpose**: Protect airway prior to gastric lavage or activated charcoal in unconscious patient.
* **Pre-oxygenation**: 100% O2 via BVM.
* **RSI Drugs Selection**: Avoid succinylcholine if hyperkalemia suspected/organophosphate poisoning (delayed metabolism). Use Rocuronium.
* **Positioning**: Sniffing position, cricoid pressure if aspiration risk high.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Rumack-Matthew Nomogram for Paracetamol
* **Purpose**: Determines need for NAC therapy.
* **Parameters**: Plots Serum Paracetamol Concentration (µg/mL or mmol/L) against Time since ingestion (hours).
* **Application Rule**:
  * Only valid for single acute ingestions.
  * Start measuring at exactly **4 hours** post-ingestion.
  * If level falls on or above the treatment line (usually 100 mg/L at 4 hrs in HK/UK guidelines) → start NAC.
  * If presentation is >8 hours and suspected toxic dose, start NAC immediately pending levels.

### Anion Gap Formula
* **Purpose**: Identifies metabolic acidosis caused by unmeasured toxins (MUDPILES: Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Paraldehyde, Iron/Isoniazid, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates).
* **Formula**: `AG = [Na+] - ([Cl-] + [HCO3-])`
* **Normal Range**: 8 - 12 mmol/L.

### Osmolar Gap Formula
* **Purpose**: Identifies presence of toxic alcohols (Ethanol, Methanol, Ethylene Glycol).
* **Formula**: `Calculated Osmolarity = 2[Na+] + [Glucose] + [BUN]` (all in mmol/L).
* **Gap**: `Measured Osmolality - Calculated Osmolarity`.
* **Pathological Threshold**: Gap > 10 mOsm/kg.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Admission & Discharge Criteria
* **Discharge**: Asymptomatic after 4-6 hours observation + non-toxic levels + psychiatric clearance (if intentional).
* **General Medical Ward**: Symptomatic but stable, requiring short-term monitoring or IV antidotes (e.g., NAC for paracetamol).
* **ICU/HDU Admission**: Needs intubation, vasopressors, HD, continuous naloxone infusion, or exhibiting severe toxidrome (e.g., refractory seizures, extreme hyperthermia, widening QRS).

### Acute Complications of Severe Poisoning
* **Neurological**: Hypoxic brain injury, persistent seizures.
* **Cardiopulmonary**: Aspiration pneumonitis, ARDS, ventricular arrhythmias, cardiac arrest.
* **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) 2° to rhabdomyolysis or direct tubular toxicity (e.g., ethylene glycol).
* **Hepatic**: Fulminant hepatic failure (Paracetamol).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate anticholinergic vs sympathomimetic toxidromes clinically? Answer: Check the skin. Anticholinergic is dry (no sweating), sympathomimetic is diaphoretic (sweaty).
* Viva Question: What is the target endpoint for Atropine resuscitation in Organophosphate poisoning? Answer: Clearing of respiratory secretions and resolution of bronchospasm, NOT normalization of heart rate or pupils.
* Trap: Giving Flumazenil to reverse a comatose patient with suspected benzodiazepine overdose. Pearl: Flumazenil can precipitate intractable seizures in patients with chronic BDZ use or co-ingestion of TCAs. Manage with airway support instead.
* Trap: Using pupillary size alone to rule out opioid overdose. Pearl: Co-ingestions (e.g., opioids + cocaine) or specific opioids like Tramadol/Meperidine may not present with classic pinpoint pupils.
* Pitfall: Stopping NAC therapy early just because liver enzymes are normal at 12 hours. The protocol must be completed or guided by local toxicologist advice, as ALT/AST rise lags behind cellular damage.
* Pitfall: Administering Activated Charcoal to a patient who drank bleach. Corrosives are not absorbed by charcoal, and the charcoal obscures endoscopic evaluation of mucosal burns.
