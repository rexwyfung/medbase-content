# Primary Survey & Resuscitation (初步評估與復甦)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Primary Survey
* **Primary Survey (初步評估):** Systematic `<c-A-B-C-D-E>` algorithmic approach to rapidly identify & immediately treat life-threatening conditions.
* **Resuscitation (復甦):** Concurrent physiological restoration of tissue perfusion, oxygenation & metabolic function.

### HK HA Triage Categories for Resuscitation
* **Category 1 (Critical / 危殆):** Immediate (0 mins) → Cardiac arrest, major trauma (GCS <9), severe shock.
* **Category 2 (Emergency / 危急):** <15 mins → Severe resp distress, massive hematemesis, major fractures.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong EDs
* **Trauma:** Leading cause of mortality in HK adults <40 yrs.
* **Blunt Trauma:** Most common MOI in HK (falls from height, MVCs).
* **Penetrating Trauma:** Less common than Western countries (mostly industrial accidents, self-harm).

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Pathophysiology of Global Tissue Hypoxia (Shock)
* ↓ O2 delivery (DO2) relative to tissue demand (VO2).
* Aerobic → Anaerobic metabolism.
* ↑ Lactic acid production → Severe metabolic acidosis.
* Failure of Na+/K+ ATPase pump → Cellular swelling → Apoptosis & cell death.

### The Trauma Lethal Triad (創傷致死三角)
* **Hypothermia (低溫症):** Impairs coagulation cascade enzyme function.
* **Acidosis (酸中毒):** ↓ Myocardial contractility, ↓ response to catecholamines.
* **Coagulopathy (凝血障礙):** Dilutional, consumptive (DIC), & hypothermia-induced.
* Core concept: Each factor exacerbates the others → Irreversible shock.

### Mechanism of Injury (MOI) Bio-kinetics
* **Blunt Trauma:** Energy transfer over broad area → Tearing/shearing of solid organs (spleen/liver), hollow viscus rupture.
* **Penetrating Trauma:** High-velocity (bullets) → Cavitation. Low-velocity (knives) → Direct tissue transection.
* **Deceleration Injury:** Aortic shearing at Ligamentum Arteriosum.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Catastrophic Hemorrhage Assessment
* Arterial spurting.
* Massive pooling of blood.
* Blood-soaked clothing.

### Airway (A) Clinical Findings
* **Patency:** Vocalization (speaking = patent).
* **Signs of Obstruction:** Stridor, gurgling, snoring.
* **Visual Inspection:** Foreign bodies, massive facial trauma, soot/burns, expanding neck hematoma.

### Breathing (B) Clinical Findings
* **Inspection:** RR, work of breathing, accessory muscle use, paradoxical movement (flail chest), asymmetrical expansion.
* **Palpation:** Tracheal deviation, surgical emphysema (crepitus).
* **Auscultation:** Absent/diminished breath sounds (PTX/hemothorax), dullness to percussion vs hyper-resonance.

### Circulation (C) Clinical Findings
* **Perfusion:** Capillary Refill Time (CRT) >2s, cold/clammy extremities, pallor.
* **Hemodynamics:** Tachycardia (earliest sign), hypotension (late sign), narrow pulse pressure.
* **Inspection/Palpation:** Muffled heart sounds, distended neck veins (JVP), active bleeding sources, rigid abdomen.

### Disability (D) Clinical Findings
* **Consciousness:** Altered GCS.
* **Pupils:** Asymmetry, unreactive, blown pupil (uncal herniation).
* **Motor:** Lateralizing signs, posturing (decorticate/decerebrate).
* **Metabolic:** Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG) derangements.

### Exposure (E) & Secondary Survey Findings
* Core body temperature abnormalities.
* Hidden injuries: Back/spine contusions (log roll findings), axillary/perineal wounds.
* Rashes: Petechiae, purpura, urticaria.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### The "Lethal Six" Thoracic Traumas
| Condition | Key Clinical Features (ABCDE) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Airway Obstruction** | Stridor, apnea, paradoxical chest movement |
| **Tension Pneumothorax** | ↑ JVP, ↓ breath sounds, tracheal deviation, shock |
| **Open Pneumothorax** | Sucking chest wound, hypoxia |
| **Massive Hemothorax** | Flat JVP, ↓ breath sounds, dull percussion, profound shock |
| **Flail Chest** | Paradoxical chest segment movement, hypoxia |
| **Cardiac Tamponade** | Beck's Triad: ↓ BP, ↑ JVP, muffled heart sounds |

### Medical Arrest: Reversible Causes (4Hs & 4Ts)
| 4Hs | 4Ts |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypoxia** | **Tension Pneumothorax** |
| **Hypovolemia** | **Tamponade (Cardiac)** |
| **Hypo-/Hyperkalemia** | **Toxins** |
| **Hypothermia** | **Thrombosis (Pulmonary/Coronary)** |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care (POC) Bedside Tests
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG):** R/O hypoglycemia.
* **VBG / ABG:** Lactate, base excess (BE), pH, pCO2, pO2, Hb.
* **12-Lead ECG:** R/O STEMI, arrhythmias.
* **eFAST Ultrasound:** Assess for pericardial, hepatorenal, splenorenal, pelvic free fluid & pneumothorax.

### Standard Trauma & Resuscitation Blood Panel
* **T&S / Cross-match:** Priority #1 for hemorrhagic shock.
* **CBC:** Baseline Hb/Hct, Plt.
* **Coagulation:** PT, aPTT, INR, Fibrinogen, TEG/ROTEM (if available).
* **Renal/Liver:** U&E (K+), LFT.
* **Cardiac/Muscle:** Trop I/T, CK.
* **Tox Screen:** Paracetamol/Salicylate levels (suspected overdose), EtOH.

### Emergent Radiological Imaging
* **Portable CXR:** R/O wide mediastinum, hemothorax, PTX.
* **Portable Pelvic XR:** R/O open book fracture.
* **Trauma Pan-CT (Trauma Gram):** Non-con Brain/C-Spine + Contrast Chest/Abdo/Pelvis (Only if hemodynamically stable).

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Catastrophic Hemorrhage & Circulation Fluids/Drugs
* **Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP):** 1:1:1 ratio of PRBCs : FFP : Platelets.
* **Permissive Hypotension:** Target SBP 80-90 mmHg (avoid popping clots) until surgical hemostasis. *Exception: TBI (Target SBP >110).*
* **Tranexamic Acid (TXA):** 1g IV over 10 mins, then 1g IV infusion over 8 hrs (Give within 3 hrs of injury).
* **Reversal Agents:** Vitamin K / Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) for Warfarin; Idarucizumab for Dabigatran.
* **Crystalloids:** Max 1L warm Plasmalyte/Normal Saline (minimize to avoid dilutional coagulopathy).

### Airway & Breathing Resuscitation Algorithms
* **Oxygen Therapy:** 15L/min via Non-Rebreather Mask (NRM).
* **BVM Ventilation:** 10-12 breaths/min. Avoid hyperventilation (↓ venous return).

### Medical Cardiac Arrest Drug Algorithms (HK Resuscitation Council)
* **VF/pVT (Shockable):**
  * Adrenaline 1mg IV every 3-5 mins (after 2nd shock).
  * Amiodarone 300mg IV (after 3rd shock) → 150mg IV (after 5th shock).
* **PEA/Asystole (Non-Shockable):**
  * Adrenaline 1mg IV immediately, then every 3-5 mins.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Mechanics of Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI / 快速順序插管)
* **Pre-oxygenation:** 3 mins 100% O2 via BVM/NRM.
* **Positioning:** Sniffing position (unless C-spine immobilized).
* **Laryngoscopy:** Insert blade right side of mouth, sweep tongue left.
* **Visualization:** Identify epiglottis, vallecula, and vocal cords.
* **Placement:** Advance ETT through cords until cuff passes.
* **Confirmation:** Inflate cuff. EtCO2 waveform (Gold standard).

### Mechanics of Finger Thoracostomy (指探胸腔造口術)
* **Site:** 5th Intercostal Space (ICS), mid-axillary line, triangle of safety.
* **Incision:** 3cm horizontal incision just above the rib.
* **Dissection:** Blunt dissection with curved forceps through intercostal muscles and pleura.
* **Sweep:** Insert finger, sweep 360 degrees to clear adhesions and confirm intrapleural placement. (Follow with chest tube insertion).

### Mechanics of Pelvic Binder Application
* **Landmarks:** Identify bilateral greater trochanters.
* **Placement:** Slide binder under patient, centered directly over greater trochanters (NOT iliac crests).
* **Tightening:** Pull straps firmly until click/resistance is felt. Tie/clip securely.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)
| Eye Opening (E) | Verbal Response (V) | Motor Response (M) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 4 - Spontaneous | 5 - Oriented | 6 - Obeys commands |
| 3 - To speech | 4 - Confused | 5 - Localizes pain |
| 2 - To pain | 3 - Inappropriate words | 4 - Withdraws from pain |
| 1 - None | 2 - Incomprehensible sounds | 3 - Abnormal flexion (Decorticate) |
| | 1 - None | 2 - Abnormal extension (Decerebrate) |
| | | 1 - None |

### Assessment of Blood Consumption (ABC) Score
* Predicts need for Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP). Score ≥ 2 indicates MTP. Variable (1 point each).
	* **Penetrating mechanism**
	* **Positive FAST ultrasound**
	* **Systolic BP < 90 mmHg in ED**
	* **Heart Rate > 120 bpm in ED**

### Shock Index (SI)
* Calculation: Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP.
* Normal: 0.5 - 0.7.
* Score > 1.0: Indicates severe occult shock / high transfusion requirement.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Emergency Disposition & Transfer Criteria
* **OT / Damage Control Surgery:** Hemodynamically unstable despite resuscitation (+ve FAST, rigid abdomen, massive hemothorax).
* **ICU Admission:** Intubated, requiring inotropic support, severe acid-base derangements.
* **Angio / Interventional Radiology (IR):** Pelvic bleeding with stable enough vitals for transfer, solid organ solid injuries with contrast blush on CT.

### Acute Resuscitation Complications
* **Over-Resuscitation (Fluids):** ARDS, abdominal compartment syndrome, dilutional coagulopathy.
* **Procedural Complications:** ETT esophageal intubation, right mainstem bronchus intubation, iatrogenic lung laceration (chest drain).
* **Transfusion Complications:** TRALI, TACO, hypocalcemia (citrate toxicity), hyperkalemia.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of shock in a trauma patient? Answer: Hypovolemic (hemorrhagic) shock until proven otherwise.
* Trap: Relying on blood pressure to assess early shock. Pearl: Tachycardia, narrowed pulse pressure, and delayed CRT precede hypotension; BP drops only after ~30% blood volume loss.
* Pitfall: Performing a secondary survey before stabilizing ABCs. Always stop and treat identified life-threats in the primary survey before moving on.
* Viva Question: Why do we give TXA empirically in major trauma? Answer: To inhibit fibrinolysis by competitively blocking the lysine binding sites on plasminogen.
* Trap: Placing a pelvic binder over the iliac crests. Pearl: It must be placed over the greater trochanters to effectively reduce an open-book pelvic ring volume.
* Pitfall: Hyperventilating a patient with suspected traumatic brain injury (TBI) routinely. Prophylactic hyperventilation worsens cerebral ischemia by causing cerebral vasoconstriction; only use briefly for acute herniation.
* Viva Question: What is the single best physiological marker of adequate resuscitation and tissue perfusion? Answer: Clearance of serum lactate and normalization of base excess.
