# Pain Control (疼痛控制)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of ED Pain Control
* **Acute Pain (急性疼痛):** Unpleasant sensory/emotional experience reflecting actual/potential tissue damage
* **Oligoanalgesia (鎮痛不足):** Historic systemic undertreatment of pain in EDs
* **Goal:** Rapid, safe ↓ of pain to tolerable levels (NRS < 4), not necessarily absolute zero

### HK AED Triage Categories for Pain
* **Category 1 (Critical):** Pain with immediate airway/hemodynamic collapse (e.g., ruptured AAA)
* **Category 2 (Emergency):** Severe pain (NRS 8-10/10) with high-risk features (e.g., **Ischemic Chest Pain (缺血性胸痛)**, severe chemical eye burn)
* **Category 3 (Urgent):** Moderate-severe pain (NRS 5-7/10) needing early relief (e.g., renal colic, major fractures)
* **Category 4 (Semi-urgent):** Mild-moderate pain, stable vitals

### Local Epidemiology
* **Most common symptom:** Accounts for >70% of ED visits
* **Vulnerable groups:** Elderly, pediatric, cognitively impaired, non-Cantonese speaking → highest risk of oligoanalgesia

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Nociceptive Pain Pathophysiology
* **Somatic (軀體性):** Skin, muscle, bone damage
* **Pathway:** A-delta fibers (fast, myelinated) → sharp, well-localized
* **Visceral (內臟性):** Organ ischemia, distension, capsule stretch
* **Pathway:** C-fibers (slow, unmyelinated) → dull, aching, poorly localized, often referred

### Neuropathic Pain Pathophysiology
* **Mechanism:** Direct nerve injury/dysfunction (e.g., sciatica, post-herpetic neuralgia)
* **Characteristics:** Burning, shooting, "electric shock"
* **Associated phenomena:** Hyperalgesia (↑ response to pain), Allodynia (pain from non-painful stimuli)

### Central Pain Pathways
* **Transduction:** Nociceptor activation via prostaglandins/bradykinin
* **Transmission:** Spinothalamic tract → Thalamus → Somatosensory cortex
* **Modulation:** Descending inhibitory pathways (serotonin, norepinephrine, endogenous opioids)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Clinical History of Pain (SOCRATES)
* **S**ite: Exact location
* **O**nset: Sudden (vascular/rupture) vs Gradual (infective/inflammatory)
* **C**haracter: Tearing, crushing, colicky, burning
* **R**adiation: Classic patterns (e.g., back → AAA; shoulder → diaphragmatic irritation)
* **A**ssociated symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, diaphoresis, syncope
* **T**ime course: Constant vs Intermittent
* **E**xacerbating/relieving factors: Movement, posture, eating
* **S**everity: Subjective score (0-10)

### Objective Signs of Acute Pain
* **Vital signs:** Tachycardia, ↑ BP, tachypnea (sympathetic drive)
* **Skin:** Diaphoresis, pallor
* **Musculoskeletal:** Guarding, rigidity, wincing, reluctance to move
* **Neurological:** Agitation, delirium (especially in elderly)

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Pain Disproportionate to Physical Signs
* **Mesenteric Ischemia (腸繫膜缺血):** Severe abdo pain, soft abdomen
* **Necrotizing Fasciitis (壞死性筋膜炎):** Severe limb pain, minimal early skin changes
* **Compartment Syndrome (骨筋膜室綜合症):** Extreme pain on passive stretch

### Life-Threatening Causes by Region
| Region | Critical "Rule-Out" Diagnoses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Head/Neck** | SAH, Meningitis, Temporal Arteritis, Cervical Dissection |
| **Chest** | AMI, Aortic Dissection, Tension Pneumothorax, Pulmonary Embolism |
| **Abdomen** | Ruptured AAA, Perforated Viscus, Ectopic Pregnancy |
| **Pelvis/Scrotum** | Testicular Torsion, Ovarian Torsion |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### POC Investigations for Analgesia Safety
* **Vitals & SpO2:** Baseline before opioid/sedation administration
* **ECG:** Mandatory for chest/epigastric pain
* **CBG (Capillary Blood Glucose):** Baseline for altered mental status from pain
* **VBG (Venous Blood Gas):** Check lactate if mesenteric ischemia/sepsis suspected

### Diagnostic Investigations for Underlying Cause
* **Blood tests:** Renal function (Cr) mandatory before NSAID administration
* **X-ray:** Targeted to trauma/pain site
* **CT Scan:** Definitive imaging for visceral/vascular catastrophic pain
* **Ultrasound (FAST/POCUS):** Detect AAA, hemoperitoneum, gallstones

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### ED Analgesia Stepladder Approach
| Pain Severity | 1st Line Options | Adjuncts/Rescue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild (1-3)** | Paracetamol PO ± NSAID PO/Topical | Heat/Cold packs, splinting |
| **Moderate (4-6)** | Weak Opioid (Tramadol, DF-118) + Paracetamol | NSAID IM, Entonox |
| **Severe (7-10)** | Morphine IV or Fentanyl IV/IN | Ketamine IV, Regional Blocks |

### Non-Opioid Pharmacotherapy
* **Paracetamol (撲熱息痛):** 1g PO/IV Q6H (Max 4g/day). Safe, synergistic with opioids.
* **NSAIDs (非類固醇消炎藥):** Ibuprofen 400mg PO, Diclofenac (Voltaren) 75mg IM/PO.
* **Topical:** Diclofenac gel, Lignocaine patches.

### Opioid Pharmacotherapy & Titration
* **Morphine (嗎啡):** 0.1mg/kg IV ideal. ED practice: 2-2.5mg IV boluses every 5-10 mins titrated to effect.
* **Fentanyl (芬太尼):** 1-2mcg/kg IV/IN. Rapid onset, short duration. Preferred in renal failure/hypotension.
* **Tramadol (曲馬多):** 50-100mg PO/IV. Risk of serotonin syndrome, lowers seizure threshold.

### Adjuncts & Sedation Analgesia
* **Entonox (笑氣):** 50% N2O / 50% O2. Patient-administered via demand valve.
* **Ketamine (氯胺酮):** Sub-dissociative dose (0.1-0.3mg/kg IV over 15 mins). Excellent for refractory pain, spares respiratory drive.
* **Antiemetics:** Metoclopramide 10mg IV or Ondansetron 4mg IV prophylactically with IV opioids.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Fascia Iliaca Compartment Block (FICB) Mechanics
* **Target:** Femoral, obturator, lateral cutaneous nerves
* **Position:** Supine
* **Landmarks:** 1/3 distance from ASIS to pubic tubercle, 1cm inferior to inguinal ligament
* **Technique:** Insert blunt needle → feel 2 "pops" (fascia lata, then fascia iliaca) → aspirate (-ve for blood) → inject 30-40mL local anesthetic (e.g., 0.25% Levobupivacaine)
* **Ultrasound-guided:** Visualize needle deep to fascia iliaca, observe LA spreading laterally/medially.

### Hematoma Block Procedure (e.g., Colles' Fracture)
* **Preparation:** Sterile prep over fracture hematoma site
* **Technique:** Insert needle directly into fracture hematoma → aspirate (+ve for fracture blood/fat globules) → inject 10-15mL 1% Lignocaine
* **Wait time:** Allow 10-15 mins for block to take effect before manipulation.

### Procedural Consent for Regional Anesthesia
* **Bleeding/Hematoma:** Risk ↑ in coagulopathy
* **Infection:** Rare but serious
* **Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST):** Perioral numbness, tinnitus, seizures, cardiac arrest
* **Nerve injury:** Temporary or permanent neuropathy

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Adult Pain Scoring Systems
| Scale | Description | Target Population |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **NRS (Numerical Rating Scale)** | "Rate pain 0 to 10" | Cognitively intact adults |
| **VAS (Visual Analogue Scale)** | Mark on a 10cm continuous line | Adults |
| **FACES Scale** | Point to facial expressions | Language barriers, mild cognitive impairment |

### FLACC Behavioral Pain Scale (Paediatrics)
* **F**ace: 0 (Smile) to 2 (Frequent frown, clenched jaw)
* **L**egs: 0 (Normal) to 2 (Kicking/drawn up)
* **A**ctivity: 0 (Quiet) to 2 (Arched, rigid, jerking)
* **C**ry: 0 (None) to 2 (Steady crying, screams)
* **C**onsolability: 0 (Content) to 2 (Difficult to console)
* **Score:** 0-10 total

### PAINAD Scale (Advanced Dementia)
* **Parameters:** Breathing, Negative Vocalization, Facial Expression, Body Language, Consolability
* **Scoring:** 0-2 per parameter (Total 0-10). ≥2 indicates potential need for analgesia.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Disposition & Discharge Criteria
* **Discharge:** Pain well-controlled (NRS < 4) on oral analgesia, no life threats, clear diagnosis/follow-up. Provide sufficient PO meds for 3-5 days.
* **Admit:** Intractable pain, requirement for ongoing IV analgesia, unstable vitals, underlying surgical/critical pathology.

### Acute Complications of Analgesia
* **Opioids:** Respiratory depression (RR < 8), hypoxia, hypotension, nausea/vomiting, histamine release (urticaria).
* **NSAIDs:** AKI, peptic ulcer exacerbation/GI bleed, bronchospasm in susceptible asthmatics.
* **Paracetamol:** Hepatotoxicity (if massive overdose).
* **Entonox:** Expansion of gas-filled spaces (contraindicated in pneumothorax, bowel obstruction).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you reverse severe opioid-induced respiratory depression in the ED? Answer: Naloxone (納洛酮) 0.4-2mg IV/IM, repeated every 2-3 mins as needed.
* Trap: Withholding analgesia in acute abdominal pain to avoid "masking signs" for the surgeon. Pearl: Early IV morphine does NOT obscure diagnosis and improves patient cooperation for the physical exam. Provide analgesia immediately.
* Pitfall: Giving NSAIDs to trauma patients who are hypotensive. They are highly reliant on renal prostaglandins for afferent arteriole dilation; NSAIDs will precipitate acute kidney injury. Use opioids/ketamine instead.
* Viva Question: What is the maximum safe dose of Lignocaine for local infiltration? Answer: 3 mg/kg without adrenaline, 7 mg/kg with adrenaline.
* Trap: Discharging an elderly patient with rib fractures and "tolerable" pain at rest. Pearl: Rib fractures require aggressive multimodal analgesia (e.g., regional block) because hypoventilation due to pain leads to atelectasis and deadly pneumonia in the elderly.
