# Acute Asthma (急性哮喘)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology
### Clinical Definition of Acute Asthma
* **Definition:** Acute exacerbation of chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airflow obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness. 
* **Key Triad:** Bronchospasm (支氣管痙攣) + Airway inflammation + Mucus plugging.

### HK ED Triage Category for Asthma
* **Category 1 (Critical):** Cardiac/Respiratory arrest or impending arrest (e.g., altered mental state, profound cyanosis).
* **Category 2 (Emergency):** Severe/Life-threatening exacerbation (e.g., SpO2 <92%, HR >110, RR >25, silent chest).
* **Category 3 (Urgent):** Moderate exacerbation with stable vitals (SpO2 >92%, PEFR 50-75%).

### Local Epidemiology & Triggers
* **Peak Seasons:** Winter and Spring in Hong Kong.
* **Common Triggers:** Viral Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (上呼吸道感染), air pollution, cold air, allergens (dust mites), medication non-compliance, NSAIDs/Aspirin, Beta-blockers.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)
### Pathophysiology of Bronchospasm
* **Trigger Exposure:** Allergen/virus cross-links IgE on mast cells.
* **Degranulation:** Release of histamine, leukotrienes, and prostaglandins.
* **Smooth Muscle Contraction:** → Acute bronchospasm (支氣管痙攣) → ↑ airway resistance.

### Airway Remodeling & Inflammation
* **Inflammatory Infiltrate:** Eosinophils, neutrophils, T-lymphocytes migrate to airway mucosa.
* **Edema:** Capillary leakage → mucosal edema → narrowed airway lumen.
* **Mucus Plugging:** Goblet cell hyperplasia → thick, tenacious mucus exudate.

### Respiratory Mechanics & Auto-PEEP
* **Dynamic Hyperinflation:** Prolonged expiratory phase + narrowed airways → incomplete exhalation.
* **Air Trapping:** Gas accumulation in alveoli → ↑ intra-thoracic pressure.
* **Auto-PEEP (Intrinsic PEEP):** ↑ intrathoracic pressure → ↓ venous return → ↓ cardiac output → **Pulsus Paradoxus** (奇脈) (fall in SBP >10mmHg during inspiration).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)
### ABCDE Primary Survey Findings
* **A:** Vocalization (complete vs incomplete sentences), stridor (-ve).
* **B:** Tachypnea, use of accessory muscles, tripod positioning, bilateral expiratory wheeze (哮鳴音), ↓ air entry.
* **C:** Tachycardia, pulsus paradoxus, cyanosis (cyanosis = late sign).
* **D:** Altered mental status (agitation → lethargy = hypoxia/hypercapnia).
* **E:** Diaphoresis, exhaustion.

### Classification of Severity (Symptoms/Signs)
| Grade | Clinical Signs | Vitals / PEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Moderate** | Normal speech, no accessory muscle use | HR <110, RR <25, SpO2 >92%, PEFR 50-75% |
| **Severe** | Incomplete sentences, accessory muscle use | HR >110, RR >25, SpO2 ≥92%, PEFR 33-50% |
| **Life-Threatening** | Silent chest (靜止胸), exhaustion, altered LOC | Bradycardia/Hypotension, SpO2 <92%, PEFR <33% |
| **Near-Fatal** | Coma, respiratory arrest | Unrecordable PEFR, apnea |

### Silent Chest Phenomenon
* **Definition:** Complete absence of wheeze on auscultation in a severely breathless patient.
* **Significance:** Indicates critical airway narrowing where airflow is insufficient to generate a wheeze. Sign of impending respiratory arrest (呼吸衰竭).

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)
### Life-Threatening Differentials for Wheeze
| Differential | Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Pulmonary Edema / Cardiac Asthma (心源性哮喘)** | Bibasal crepitations, JVP ↑, peripheral edema, history of IHD/Heart Failure. |
| **Anaphylaxis (過敏性休克)** | Urticaria, angioedema, hypotension, abrupt onset post-exposure. |
| **Tension Pneumothorax (張力性氣胸)** | Unilateral ↓ breath sounds, hyperresonance, tracheal deviation. |
| **Acute Exacerbation of COPD (慢阻肺病急性惡化)** | Older age, smoking history, chronic productive cough, barrel chest. |
| **Foreign Body Aspiration (異物吸入)** | Sudden onset, stridor, localized focal wheeze, child/elderly patient. |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)
### Point-of-Care (POC) Testing
* **Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) (最高呼氣流速):** Baseline measurement prior to treatment, repeat post-treatment. Use best of 3 attempts.
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) (動脈血氣分析):** 
    * *Early:* Hypocapnia (↓ pCO2) + Respiratory alkalosis (due to hyperventilation).
    * *Late:* Normocapnia/Hypercapnia (↑ pCO2) = Fatigue & impending respiratory failure.
* **ECG:** Rule out ACS. Look for sinus tachycardia, P-pulmonale, Right Axis Deviation (RAD), RV strain pattern (S1Q3T3).

### Laboratory Investigations
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** ↑ Eosinophils (allergic), ↑ WCC (infection/steroid-induced).
* **Urea & Electrolytes (U&E):** Hypokalemia (↓ K+) secondary to frequent high-dose Salbutamol nebulization.
* **Cardiac Biomarkers (Troponin):** Only if concomitant chest pain, ECG changes, or elderly with IHD risk.

### Radiological Investigations
* **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** NOT routinely indicated unless suspecting complications.
* **CXR Indications:** Suspected pneumothorax, pneumonia (consolidation), failure to respond to standard therapy, life-threatening asthma.
* **Typical Asthma CXR Findings:** Hyperinflation, flattened hemidiaphragms, horizontal ribs.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms
### Oxygen Therapy & Target SpO2
* **Target:** Titrate supplemental O2 to achieve SpO2 94-98%.
* **Delivery:** Nasal cannula → Venturi mask → Non-Rebreather Mask (NRBM) depending on severity.

### 1st Line Pharmacotherapy (Inhaled & Systemic)
* **Short-Acting Beta-Agonist (SABA):** Salbutamol (沙丁胺醇) 5mg NEB or 4-10 puffs via MDI+Spacer, repeat every 15-30 mins.
* **Short-Acting Muscarinic Antagonist (SAMA):** Ipratropium Bromide (異丙托溴銨) 0.5mg NEB (added to Salbutamol in severe asthma).
* **Systemic Corticosteroids (皮質類固醇):** Hydrocortisone 100-200mg IV OR Prednisolone 40-50mg PO (give early, takes 4-6 hours to peak).

### 2nd Line / Refractory Pharmacotherapy (IV)
* **IV Magnesium Sulfate (硫酸鎂):** 2g IV over 20 mins (bronchodilator via calcium channel blockade).
* **IV Aminophylline:** 5mg/kg loading dose over 20 mins, followed by infusion (requires cardiac monitoring, narrow therapeutic index).
* **IV/IM Adrenaline (腎上腺素):** 0.3-0.5mg IM (1:1000) or IV infusion for near-fatal asthma/anaphylaxis crossover.

### Ventilatory Support (NIV/Invasive)
* **Heliox:** Helium-Oxygen mixture (70:30 or 80:20) (↓ turbulent airflow).
* **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV):** BiPAP / CPAP to ↓ work of breathing and overcome Auto-PEEP.
* **Intubation:** Last resort for coma, respiratory arrest, or exhaustion. High risk of barotrauma and cardiac arrest upon induction.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)
### Setup for Nebulization & MDI-Spacer
* **MDI+Spacer Mechanics:** Shake inhaler → attach to spacer → 1 puff into spacer → patient takes 5 normal breaths (or 1 deep breath with 10s hold) → repeat.
* **Nebulizer Mechanics:** Attach fluid chamber to 6-8 L/min O2 wall source until mist generated. 

### Non-Invasive Ventilation (BiPAP) Mechanics in Asthma
* **Indications:** Severe work of breathing, failure of medical therapy, bridging to avoid intubation.
* **Mechanics:** 
    * Set low IPAP (e.g., 10-12 cmH2O) to assist ventilation.
    * Set low EPAP (e.g., 3-5 cmH2O) to stent airways but avoid exacerbating Auto-PEEP.
    * Ensure prolonged expiratory time is permitted by the machine.

### Intubation Mechanics for Status Asthmaticus
* **Consent:** Implied (life-saving emergency).
* **Induction Agent:** Ketamine (氯胺酮) 1-2mg/kg IV (provides direct bronchodilation).
* **Paralytic:** Rocuronium 1.2mg/kg IV (avoids histamine release seen with atracurium).
* **Tube Size:** Use largest possible Endotracheal Tube (ETT) (≥8.0mm in adults) to ↓ airway resistance and facilitate suctioning of mucus plugs.
* **Ventilator Settings post-intubation:** 
    * Rate: Low (8-10 breaths/min).
    * I:E Ratio: 1:4 or 1:5 (allow prolonged exhalation time to prevent Auto-PEEP).
    * Mode: Volume Control, low tidal volume (6-8 ml/kg).
    * Goal: Permissive hypercapnia (accept ↑ pCO2 as long as pH >7.20).

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring
### Peak Expiratory Flow (PEFR) Grading Criteria
| Measurement | Value against Best/Predicted | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild/Moderate** | > 50-75% | Safe for ED discharge if sustained >60 min |
| **Severe** | 33-50% | Requires aggressive ED management, likely admission |
| **Life-Threatening** | < 33% | Resuscitation room management |

### Near-Fatal Asthma Risk Factors
* **History:** Previous intubation or ICU admission for asthma.
* **Medications:** ≥3 classes of asthma meds, heavy use of SABA (>1 canister/month), recent oral steroids.
* **Compliance:** Poor adherence to Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS), defaults HA outpatient clinics.
* **Psychosocial:** Psychiatric illness, substance abuse, low socioeconomic status.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications
### ED Discharge Criteria & Plan
* **Criteria:** Clinically stable, PEFR >75% of best/predicted, SpO2 >94% on room air, asymptomatic for >60 mins post-treatment.
* **Medications:** PO Prednisolone 40-50mg daily for 5-7 days. Continue SABA PRN. Ensure ICS is prescribed.
* **Follow-up:** GOPC (General Outpatient Clinic) or AED follow-up clinic within 2-4 days. Provide written Asthma Action Plan.

### Admission & ICU Referral Criteria
* **General Medical Ward:** Incomplete response, PEFR 50-75% despite treatment, pregnant, poor social support, recurrent ED visits within 48h.
* **ICU/HDU:** PEFR <33%, altered mental state, requiring NIV, impending exhaustion (normal/↑ pCO2 on ABG), failed IV therapies.

### Acute Complications of Severe Asthma
* **Barotrauma:** Pneumothorax (氣胸), Pneumomediastinum.
* **Cardiac:** Dysrhythmias (hypoxia/hypokalemia), Cardiac Arrest.
* **Pulmonary:** Respiratory arrest, mucus plugging leading to lobar atelectasis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: An asthmatic patient has an ABG showing a pCO2 of 40 mmHg (normal range). Are you reassured? Answer: No, a "normal" pCO2 in an acutely dyspneic, tachypneic asthmatic is a highly dangerous sign of impending respiratory failure and muscle fatigue. They should be hypocapnic from hyperventilation.
* Trap: Prescribing anxiolytics or sedatives to calm an agitated asthmatic patient. Pearl: Never give sedatives to an asthmatic in acute exacerbation. Agitation is a sign of hypoxia. Sedation depresses respiratory drive and precipitates respiratory arrest.
* Pitfall: Intubating an asthmatic with standard I:E ratios (1:2) and normal respiratory rates (14-16). You will cause massive dynamic hyperinflation (Auto-PEEP) leading to cardiovascular collapse (pulseless electrical activity). Keep the respiratory rate very low (8-10) and extend the expiratory phase (I:E 1:4).
* Viva Question: What is the primary mechanism of hypotension immediately following intubation of a severe asthmatic? Answer: Breath stacking leading to severe Auto-PEEP, which increases intrathoracic pressure, collapsing the vena cava, abolishing venous return, and eliminating cardiac output. Disconnect the ventilator and push on the chest to exhale.
