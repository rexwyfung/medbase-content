# Tension Pneumothorax (張力性氣胸)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Tension Pneumothorax
* **Tension Pneumothorax (張力性氣胸):** Life-threatening condition where air enters pleural space but cannot escape (one-way valve)
* Accumulating intrapleural pressure → mediastinal shift → cardiopulmonary compromise

### HK AED Triage Category for Tension Pneumothorax
* **Triage Category:** Cat 1 (Critical / 危殆) 
* Requires immediate bedside resuscitation (0 mins)

### Local Epidemiology & Risk Factors
* **Trauma:** Blunt/penetrating chest injury, rib fractures
* **Iatrogenic:** Central venous catheterization (CVC), positive pressure ventilation (PPV), post-thoracentesis
* **Medical:** Severe COPD, ruptured bullae, Marfan syndrome, PCP pneumonia

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### One-Way Valve Mechanism
* Pleural tear acts as one-way valve
* Inspiration → air pulled into pleural space
* Expiration → valve closes → trapped air ↑
* Intrapleural pressure rapidly exceeds atmospheric pressure

### Hemodynamic Collapse Pathophysiology
* ↑ Intrapleural pressure → compresses ipsilateral lung
* Mediastinal shift to contralateral side → compresses contralateral lung (hypoxia)
* Compression of superior/inferior vena cava (SVC/IVC) → ↓ venous return (preload)
* ↓ Preload → ↓ cardiac output → **Obstructive Shock (阻塞性休克)** → Cardiac arrest (PEA)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Primary Survey (Airway & Breathing) Findings
* Severe respiratory distress / tachypnea
* Ipsilateral absent or ↓ breath sounds
* Ipsilateral hyperresonance to percussion
* Asymmetrical chest wall movement
* **Tracheal deviation (氣管偏移)** (late sign, away from affected side)
* **Subcutaneous emphysema (皮下氣腫)** (crepitus on palpation)

### Primary Survey (Circulation) Findings
* Tachycardia
* Hypotension
* **Jugular Venous Distension (JVD / 頸靜脈怒張)** (absent if concurrent severe hypovolemia)
* Pulsus paradoxus
* Cyanosis (late sign)
* Altered mental status (cerebral hypoperfusion)

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Major Trauma Differentials (The "Fatal Five" Table)
| Diagnosis (English) | Diagnosis (Chinese) | Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiac Tamponade** | 心包填塞 | Bilateral equal breath sounds, +ve JVD, muffled heart sounds, normal percussion |
| **Massive Hemothorax** | 大量血胸 | Dullness to percussion, flat neck veins (hypovolemia), ↓ breath sounds |
| **Simple Pneumothorax** | 單純性氣胸 | Normal hemodynamics, no mediastinal shift |
| **Flail Chest** | 連枷胸 | Paradoxical chest wall movement, palpable rib fractures |
| **Right Mainstem Intubation** | 右主支氣管插管 | ↓ Left breath sounds post-intubation, ET tube deep on CXR |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Diagnostic Principle
* **Clinical diagnosis!** Never delay treatment for imaging if hemodynamically unstable.

### Point-of-Care Ultrasound (eFAST) Findings
* **Lung Sliding (肺滑動徵):** Absent on affected side
* **B-lines:** Absent
* **Barcode/Stratosphere Sign:** Seen on M-mode (indicates pneumothorax)
* **Lung Point (肺點):** 100% specific (transition between sliding and non-sliding lung)

### Chest X-Ray (CXR) Findings (If stable enough/post-decompression)
* Absent lung markings on affected side
* Deep sulcus sign (supine position)
* Mediastinal shift away from affected side
* Ipsilateral hemidiaphragm depression

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Immediate Resuscitation Algorithm
* **Step 1:** Call for help (Trauma Team / ICU)
* **Step 2:** High-flow O2 via non-rebreather mask (15L/min)
* **Step 3:** Immediate Needle/Finger Thoracostomy (Do NOT wait for X-ray)
* **Step 4:** IV fluid resuscitation (Warm crystalloids) for concurrent hypovolemia
* **Step 5:** Definitive Tube Thoracostomy
* **Step 6:** Continuous cardiac & SpO2 monitoring

### Pharmacological Support
* **Analgesia:** IV Morphine or Fentanyl (titrated, careful with hypotension)
* **Antibiotics:** Prophylactic IV Cefazolin (for traumatic open wounds/chest tube insertion per local HA protocol)
* **Inotropes:** NOT 1st line (fix the mechanical obstruction first)

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Needle Thoracostomy (針刺胸腔減壓術) Mechanics
* **Site (Adult ATLS 10th Ed):** 5th intercostal space (ICS), mid-axillary line (MAL) OR 2nd ICS, mid-clavicular line (MCL)
* **Equipment:** Large bore cannula (14G or 16G, >5cm length)
* **Steps:** 
* Cleanse skin with chlorhexidine
* Insert needle perpendicular to skin, just above the lower rib margin (avoid neurovascular bundle)
* Listen for "hiss" of escaping air
* Remove needle, leave cannula in place
* Secure cannula

### Finger Thoracostomy (手指胸腔造口術) Mechanics
* **Indication:** Preferred in cardiac arrest or impending arrest (more reliable than needle)
* **Site:** 5th ICS, anterior to mid-axillary line
* **Steps:**
* Incise skin (3-4cm) with scalpel
* Blunt dissect through subcutaneous tissue and intercostal muscles with curved Kelly forceps
* Puncture parietal pleura just above the lower rib
* Insert finger to sweep lung away and confirm intrapleural placement
* Air should rush out

### Tube Thoracostomy (胸腔引流管置入術) Mechanics & Consent
* **Consent:** Implied if life-threatening; written emergency consent if time permits (Risks: bleeding, infection, organ injury)
* **Equipment:** Chest tube (28-32 Fr for trauma), underwater seal drain
* **Steps:**
* Follow Finger Thoracostomy steps above
* Clamp proximal end of chest tube
* Guide tube into pleural space via tract, directing posteriorly and apically
* Connect to underwater seal drainage system
* Suture in place (heavy silk/nylon) and apply occlusive dressing

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Physiologic Trauma Scoring Table
| Score Name | Components | Relevance to Tension PTX |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Revised Trauma Score (RTS)** | GCS, Systolic BP, Respiratory Rate | Identifies physiological deterioration (score <11 indicates severe injury) |
| **Shock Index (SI)** | Heart Rate / Systolic BP | SI >1.0 indicates severe hemodynamic compromise (obstructive/hypovolemic) |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Post-Resuscitation Disposition
* **OT:** If persistent massive air leak or massive hemothorax (>1500ml or >200ml/hr for 2-4 hrs) indicating tracheobronchial/vascular injury
* **ICU/HDU:** All tension pneumothorax patients require intensive monitoring post-decompression

### Acute Complications of Interventions
* **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (REPE / 復張性肺水腫):** Due to rapid lung re-expansion
* **Bleeding/Hemothorax:** From lacerated intercostal artery during tube insertion
* **Infection:** Empyema (膿胸) from contaminated field
* **Malposition:** Subcutaneous, intra-abdominal, or intraparenchymal tube placement

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate clinically between tension pneumothorax and massive hemothorax in a trauma patient? Answer: Percussion (hyperresonant in tension PTX vs. dull in hemothorax) and neck veins (distended in tension PTX vs. flat in hemothorax).
* Trap: Waiting for a portable chest X-ray to confirm the diagnosis in a hypotensive, hypoxic trauma patient. Pearl: Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis; you must perform immediate needle or finger decompression based on physical signs alone.
* Pitfall: Inserting the needle or chest tube directly below the rib. You risk lacerating the intercostal neurovascular bundle. Always insert just above the superior margin of the lower rib.
* Viva Question: Why has the recommended site for needle decompression shifted to the 5th ICS mid-axillary line in recent ATLS guidelines? Answer: The chest wall thickness at the 2nd ICS mid-clavicular line often exceeds the length of a standard 5cm angiocatheter, leading to a high failure rate.
* Trap: Assuming bilateral breath sounds rules out a tension pneumothorax in a noisy trauma bay. Pearl: Breath sounds are notoriously unreliable in noisy environments; rely on hemodynamics, JVD, tracheal deviation, and eFAST.
