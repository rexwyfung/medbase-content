# Diabetic Emergencies (糖尿病急症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of DKA (糖尿病酮症酸中毒)
* **Triad**: Hyperglycemia (CBG >11.1 mmol/L) + Ketosis (Blood ketones >3.0 mmol/L or urine ++/+++) + Acidosis (pH <7.3 or HCO3 <15 mmol/L)
* **Variant**: Euglycemic DKA (CBG <11.1 mmol/L, often due to SGLT-2 inhibitors or starvation).

### Definition of HHS (高滲性高血糖狀態)
* **Features**: Marked hyperglycemia (CBG >33.3 mmol/L) + Hyperosmolality (Serum osmolality >320 mOsm/kg) + No significant ketosis/acidosis (pH >7.3, HCO3 >15 mmol/L).

### Definition of Hypoglycemia (低血糖)
* **Whipple's Triad**: CBG <3.9 mmol/L + symptomatic + symptom resolution after glucose administration.
* **Severe**: Requires 3rd-party assistance to reverse.

### HK AED Triage Categories
* **Category 1 (Critical)**: Unresponsive, GCS ≤8, impending airway compromise (e.g., severe hypoglycemia/DKA).
* **Category 2 (Emergency)**: GCS 9-14, severe dehydration, Kussmaul breathing.
* **Category 3 (Urgent)**: Stable vital signs but CBG >20 mmol/L or <4 mmol/L with mild symptoms.

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong)
* **DKA**: More common in Type 1 DM (1型糖尿病), younger patients, rising incidence due to SGLT-2i use.
* **HHS**: More common in elderly Type 2 DM (2型糖尿病), high mortality (up to 20%).
* **Hypo**: Extremely common in AED, mostly elderly on Sulfonylureas (SU) or erratic insulin use.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### DKA Pathophysiology
* Absolute insulin deficiency + ↑ Counter-regulatory hormones (Glucagon, Cortisol, Catecholamines).
* **Hyperglycemia** → Osmotic diuresis → Intravascular volume depletion + electrolyte loss (Na, K, PO4).
* **Ketogenesis** → Lipolysis (breaking down fat) → Free fatty acids → Liver converts to ketoacids (Acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate) → High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA).

### HHS Pathophysiology
* Relative insulin deficiency: Enough insulin to suppress lipolysis/ketogenesis but NOT enough to suppress hepatic gluconeogenesis.
* Massive fluid loss (Osmotic diuresis) → Profound dehydration (avg fluid deficit 8-10L) → Hyperosmolality.

### Hypoglycemia Pathophysiology
* Absolute or relative excess of insulin vs glucose supply.
* Prompts sympathetic nervous system surge (Epinephrine release) → Neuroglycopenia (brain starvation).

### Precipitating Triggers (The "6 I's")
* **Infection** (肺炎 Pneumonia, 尿道炎 UTI)
* **Infarction** (AMI, Stroke, Bowel ischemia)
* **Infraction** (Non-compliance with insulin)
* **Ignorance** (Poor sick-day rules)
* **Intoxication** (Alcohol, Drugs)
* **Iatrogenic** (Steroids, Thiazides, SGLT2i)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### DKA & HHS Primary Survey (ABCDE)
* **Airway**: Unprotected if comatose (HHS > DKA). Fruity acetone breath (DKA).
* **Breathing**: Kussmaul respirations (Deep, rapid breathing to blow off CO2 in DKA). Normal/shallow in HHS.
* **Circulation**: Tachycardia, hypotension, delayed capillary refill, dry mucous membranes, sunken eyes, poor skin turgor.
* **Disability**: Altered mental status, GCS ↓ (correlates with ↑ osmolality in HHS). Focal neurology possible in HHS.
* **Exposure**: Hypothermia (poor prognostic sign), hidden infection sites (examine feet, perineum for Fournier's).

### Hypoglycemia Primary Survey (ABCDE)
* **Airway**: Aspiration risk if seizing or comatose.
* **Breathing**: Usually normal unless aspiration pneumonia present.
* **Circulation**: Tachycardia, diaphoresis, pallor (Sympathetic response).
* **Disability**: Agitation, confusion, coma, seizures, transient focal neurological deficits (stroke mimic).

### Secondary Survey Findings
* **GI**: Diffuse abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting (Very common in DKA, mimics surgical abdomen).
* **Neuro**: Absent reflexes, lethargy.
* **Skin**: Cold sweats (Hypo), flushed & dry (DKA/HHS), signs of insulin injection lipohypertrophy.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Diabetic Emergency Differentiations
| Feature | DKA | HHS | Alcoholic Ketoacidosis (AKA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Glucose** | >11.1 mmol/L | >33.3 mmol/L | Normal / Low / Slightly ↑ |
| **Ketones** | High (+++) | Trace / Negative | High (+++) |
| **Acidosis** | Severe (pH <7.3) | None (pH >7.3) | Mild to Severe |
| **Osmolality** | Variable | >320 mOsm/kg | Normal / Variable |

### High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA) Differentials
* **MUDPILES / CATMUDPILES**:
* **C**: Cyanide / CO
* **A**: Alcoholic Ketoacidosis
* **T**: Toluene
* **M**: Methanol / Metformin
* **U**: Uremia
* **D**: DKA
* **P**: Paracetamol / Propylene glycol
* **I**: Iron / Isoniazid
* **L**: Lactic acidosis (Sepsis, Ischemia)
* **E**: Ethylene glycol
* **S**: Salicylates

### Hypoglycemia Mimics
* **Acute Stroke (急性中風)**: Exclude via point-of-care CBG.
* **Seizure Disorder**: Post-ictal state mimics neuroglycopenia.
* **Intoxication**: Alcohol, Opioids causing depressed GCS.

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Point-of-Care Testing (POCT)
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG)**: Immediate triage tool.
* **Capillary Blood Ketones (β-hydroxybutyrate)**: >3.0 mmol/L highly specific for DKA.
* **VBG (Venous Blood Gas)**: pH, pCO2, HCO3, Lactate.
* **Urine Dipstick**: Glucose, Ketones, Leukocytes/Nitrites (Infection screen).

### Blood Laboratory Investigations
* **Renal Function Test (RFT)**: Urea, Cr, Na, K, Cl (Calculate Anion Gap).
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: ↑ WBC (stress response vs true infection).
* **HbA1c**: Assess chronic glycemic control.
* **Blood Cultures**: If septic etiology suspected.
* **Cardiac Enzymes (hs-Trop I)**: If silent MI suspected as trigger.

### Imaging & ECG
* **ECG**: Look for ↑/↓ K+ changes (peaked T waves vs U waves/flat T), STEMI, ischemia.
* **CXR**: Exclude pneumonia, pulmonary edema.
* **CT Brain**: Exclude stroke if focal signs persist after hypoglycemia reversal or suspect cerebral edema.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### DKA & HHS Fluid Resuscitation Protocol
* **Goal**: Restore circulating volume & clear ketones. (Fluid deficit: DKA ~6L, HHS ~9L).
* **1st Line Fluid**: 0.9% NaCl (Normal Saline).
* **Rate**: 1L over 1st hour → 1L over next 2 hours → 1L over next 2-4 hours. (Adjust for heart failure/ESRF).
* **Glucose Switch**: Once CBG <14 mmol/L, ADD 5% or 10% Dextrose infusion to prevent hypoglycemia while continuing insulin to clear ketones.

### Potassium Replacement Algorithm
* **Rule**: NEVER start insulin if K+ <3.3 mmol/L.
* **K+ >5.2 mmol/L**: Do not add K+. Recheck in 2 hours.
* **K+ 3.3 - 5.2 mmol/L**: Add 20-40 mmol KCl per liter of IV fluid.
* **K+ <3.3 mmol/L**: HOLD insulin. Give 40 mmol KCl per liter of IV fluid until K+ >3.3.

### Insulin Therapy Protocol
* **Route**: IV Actrapid (Short-acting insulin) infusion.
* **Dose**: Fixed rate 0.1 units/kg/hr.
* **Goal**: ↓ CBG by 3-4 mmol/L/hr, ↑ HCO3 by 3 mmol/L/hr, ↓ Ketones by 0.5 mmol/L/hr.
* **Resolution**: Continue IV insulin until ketones <0.6 mmol/L, pH >7.3, and patient can eat. Overlap with Subcutaneous (SC) insulin by 1-2 hours before stopping IV.

### Hypoglycemia Reversal Algorithm
* **Conscious & able to swallow**: Oral fast-acting carbohydrates (e.g., 15-20g glucose gel/juice).
* **Unconscious / Nil by mouth (NBM)**:
	* **1st Line IV**: 50ml of 50% Dextrose (D50) OR 250ml of 10% Dextrose (D10) IV bolus.
	* **Alternative (No IV access)**: 1mg Glucagon IM (Ineffective in malnourished/alcoholics due to empty glycogen stores).
	* **Maintenance**: Start D10W infusion if SU-induced to prevent rebound hypoglycemia.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Intubation in Severe Acidosis (Mechanics & Caveats)
* **Mechanics**: RSI protocol. Requires extreme caution in DKA.
* **Caveat**: Patient is maximizing respiratory compensation (Kussmaul). Paralysis stops this → PaCO2 rapidly rises → deadly drop in pH → Cardiac Arrest.
* **Step-by-step modification**: Pre-oxygenate heavily, use Ketamine (hemodynamically stable), set ventilator immediately to match prior high minute ventilation (high RR, high Tidal Volume).

### Central Venous Catheter (CVC) Insertion Mechanics
* **Setup**: Ultrasound-guided Internal Jugular or Femoral vein approach. Full sterile barrier.
* **Steps**: Seldinger technique (Needle → Guidewire → Dilator → Catheter). Flush all ports. Secure with sutures.
* **Consent Considerations**: Risk of pneumothorax (if IJ/Subclavian), arterial puncture, bleeding, infection.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### DKA Severity Classification Score
| Severity | pH | HCO3 (mmol/L) | Mental Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild** | 7.25 - 7.30 | 15 - 18 | Alert |
| **Moderate** | 7.00 - 7.24 | 10 - <15 | Alert / Drowsy |
| **Severe** | <7.00 | <10 | Stupor / Coma |

### Corrected Sodium Calculation
* **Formula**: Measured Na + [0.3 × (Glucose - 5.5)]
* **Purpose**: Hyperglycemia causes osmotic fluid shift into vessels, diluting serum Na. Use corrected Na to guide fluid choice (switch to 0.45% NaCl if corrected Na is high/normal).

### Anion Gap Calculation
* **Formula**: [Na+] - ([Cl-] + [HCO3-])
* **Normal**: 8 - 12 mmol/L.
* **Significance**: Closure of AG (<12) signifies resolution of DKA.

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Admission Criteria (Disposition)
* **ICU/HDU (深切治療部)**: Severe DKA (pH <7.0), HHS with coma/shock, airway risk, extremes of age.
* **Medical Ward**: Mild/Moderate DKA, resolving HHS, Hypoglycemia on oral agents (SU) requiring observation for rebound.
* **Discharge**: Accidental insulin-induced hypoglycemia fully reversed, tolerating diet, reliable home support, and clear precipitant identified.

### Lethal Complications
* **Cerebral Edema (腦水腫)**: Mostly in children/young adults. Associated with dropping glucose/osmolality too fast. Signs: headache, bradycardia, vomiting. Treatment: IV Mannitol or 3% Hypertonic Saline.
* **Hypokalemic Cardiac Arrest**: From insulin-driven intracellular K+ shift.
* **ARDS / Non-cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema**: From aggressive fluid resuscitation, especially in HHS/elderly.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Why do we continue the insulin infusion even when the blood glucose drops to normal levels in DKA? Answer: Insulin is required to switch off ketogenesis and clear the acidosis. We add IV Dextrose to prevent hypoglycemia while insulin completes the metabolic clearance.
* Trap: Giving a bolus of Sodium Bicarbonate to correct severe acidosis in DKA. Pearl: Routine bicarbonate is contraindicated. It worsens intracellular acidosis, promotes hypokalemia, and delays ketoacid clearance. Only consider if pH < 6.9 and causing life-threatening arrhythmias.
* Pitfall: Discharging an elderly patient with Sulfonylurea-induced hypoglycemia immediately after their CBG normalizes with IV Dextrose. Sulfonylureas have a long half-life and will cause rebound profound hypoglycemia once the IV dextrose wears off. They mandate admission and serial CBG monitoring.
* Viva Question: A DKA patient complains of severe abdominal pain. What must you consider? Answer: Abdominal pain is a common symptom of DKA itself, but you must rule out an underlying surgical abdomen (e.g., acute pancreatitis, appendicitis, bowel ischemia) which could be the *precipitating* cause of the DKA.
* Trap: Assuming a normal measured serum sodium means the patient in HHS is normonatremic. Pearl: The massive hyperglycemia draws water out of cells, artificially diluting the serum sodium. You must always calculate the corrected sodium to reveal the true, profound hypernatremic dehydration.
* Pitfall: Intubating a severe DKA patient without matching their pre-intubation minute ventilation. Apnea during RSI allows CO2 to accumulate rapidly, causing a fatal crash in pH and subsequent asystole.
