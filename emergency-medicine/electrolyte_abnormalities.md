# Life-Threatening Dysnatremias and Dyskalemias (致命性鈉鉀代謝異常)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Severe Hyponatraemia Definition
* **Definition:** Serum Na < 120 mmol/L
* **Acute vs Chronic:** Acute if onset < 48h (higher risk of cerebral edema); Chronic if > 48h (higher risk of osmotic demyelination upon rapid correction)

### Severe Hypernatraemia Definition
* **Definition:** Serum Na > 160 mmol/L
* **Hallmark:** Absolute or relative free water deficit

### Severe Hypokalaemia Definition
* **Definition:** Serum K < 2.5 mmol/L

### Severe Hyperkalaemia Definition
* **Definition:** Serum K > 6.5 mmol/L (or > 6.0 mmol/L with ECG changes)

### Hong Kong ED Triage Categories
* **Cat 1 (Critical):** Seizures/coma (Hyponatraemia); Cardiac arrest/arrhythmias (Hyper/Hypokalaemia)
* **Cat 2 (Emergency):** Altered mental state; severe weakness; known K > 6.5 from POCT
* **Cat 3 (Urgent):** Incidental lab abnormalities without critical signs/symptoms

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Sodium (Na) Pathophysiology
* Na is the primary extracellular fluid (ECF) osmole
* **Hyponatraemia:** ↓ ECF osmolality → H2O shifts INTO brain cells → **Cerebral Edema (腦水腫)** → ↑ Intracranial pressure → brain herniation
* **Hypernatraemia:** ↑ ECF osmolality → H2O shifts OUT of brain cells → Cellular shrinkage → tearing of bridging veins → intracranial hemorrhage

### Potassium (K) Pathophysiology
* K controls resting membrane potential (RMP) of myocytes/neurons
* **Hyperkalaemia:** ↓ Concentration gradient → RMP becomes less negative (closer to threshold) → initially ↑ excitability → eventually Na channels fail to reset → **Depolarization block (去極化阻滯)** → cardiac standstill
* **Hypokalaemia:** ↑ Concentration gradient → RMP becomes hyperpolarized → ↓ excitability, delayed repolarization → early afterdepolarizations → **Torsades de Pointes (多型性心室性心搏過速)**

### Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS) Pathogenesis
* **Mechanism:** Rapid correction of chronic hyponatraemia → brain cells lack time to re-synthesize intracellular osmoles → rapid H2O efflux → astrocyte apoptosis → destruction of myelin sheaths (especially in pons)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Severe Hyponatraemia Signs & Symptoms
* **CNS:** Agitation, delirium, lethargy, coma
* **Motor:** Generalized tonic-clonic seizures, focal neurological deficits, Cheyne-Stokes respiration
* **GI:** Nausea, intractable vomiting

### Severe Hypernatraemia Signs & Symptoms
* **Vitals:** Tachycardia, hypotension (if hypovolaemic)
* **Skin/Mucosa:** Dry mucous membranes, decreased skin turgor
* **CNS:** Irritability, high-pitched cry (pediatrics), hyperreflexia, muscle twitching, lethargy

### Severe Hyperkalaemia Signs & Symptoms
* **Cardiovascular:** Irregular pulse, severe bradycardia, absent pulses (arrest)
* **Neuromuscular:** Ascending flaccid paralysis, generalized weakness, paresthesias, hyporeflexia

### Severe Hypokalaemia Signs & Symptoms
* **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle cramps, severe weakness, rhabdomyolysis, respiratory muscle paralysis
* **GI:** Decreased bowel sounds, paralytic ileus

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### Hyponatraemia Diagnostic Differentials
| Volume Status | Key Differentials |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypovolaemic** | Diuretics, GI loss (D/V), Burns, Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's) |
| **Euvolaemic** | SIADH, Hypothyroidism, Polydipsia, MDMA (Ecstasy) intoxication |
| **Hypervolaemic** | Congestive Heart Failure (CHF), Liver Cirrhosis, Nephrotic Syndrome |

### Hyperkalaemia Diagnostic Differentials
| Mechanism | Key Differentials |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Spurious (假性)** | Hemolysis (poor venipuncture), prolonged tourniquet, extreme leukocytosis |
| **Decreased Excretion** | Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), ACEi/ARB, Spironolactone |
| **Cellular Shift** | Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA), Rhabdomyolysis, Tumor Lysis Syndrome, Suxamethonium |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Immediate Point-of-Care Testing (POCT)
* **VBG/ABG:** Rapid screening for Na, K, pH, and iCa
* **Capillary Blood Glucose (CBG):** Rule out hyperglycemia-induced pseudohyponatraemia or DKA

### Hyperkalaemia ECG Findings
* **Early:** Tall, peaked, symmetrical T waves (tenting)
* **Moderate:** Prolonged PR interval, flattened/absent P waves
* **Severe:** Widened QRS complex, slurred S wave
* **Critical:** Sine wave pattern (正弦波), Ventricular Fibrillation (VF), Asystole

### Hypokalaemia ECG Findings
* **Classic:** U waves (positive deflection after T wave), flattened/inverted T waves
* **Advanced:** ST depression, prolonged QU interval, Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs)

### Formal Laboratory Panel
* **Hyponatraemia W/U:** Paired Serum + Urine Osmolality, Urine Na
* **Renal Profile:** Urea, Creatinine
* **Others:** CK (Rhabdomyolysis), Cortisol/TFTs (if euvolemic hyponatraemia suspected)

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### Severe Symptomatic Hyponatraemia (Seizures/Coma)
* **Target:** Rapidly ↑ serum Na by 4-6 mmol/L to stop seizures
* **Drug:** **3% Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl)**
* **Dose:** 150 ml IV bolus over 20 mins
* **Re-dose:** Repeat 150 ml x2 if seizures persist
* **Max Limit:** DO NOT exceed ↑ 8 mmol/L in 24 hours (prevent ODS)

### Severe Hyperkalaemia (K > 6.5 or ECG Changes)
| Action | Drug & Dose (HK HA Protocol) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Membrane Stabilization** | **10% Calcium Gluconate:** 10 ml IV over 3-5 mins (protects heart, does NOT lower K). Repeat after 5 mins if ECG unchanged. |
| **Intracellular Shift** | **Insulin-Glucose:** Actrapid 10 units IV + 50 ml D50 (or 500 ml D10). <br>**Salbutamol:** 10-20 mg nebulized. |
| **Elimination** | **Calcium Resonium:** 15g PO or 30g PR. <br>**Hemodialysis:** For refractory hyperK or severe AKI/oligoanuria. |

### Severe Hypokalaemia Replacement
* **IV Route:** **KCl** infusion. STRICT max rate = 10-20 mmol/hour (via peripheral line) with continuous cardiac monitoring.
* **Co-factor:** Always check and replace Magnesium (IV MgSO4 1-2g); K cannot be corrected if Mg is low.
* **Oral Route:** Slow-K tablets (preferred if patient tolerates PO and K > 2.5)

### Hypernatraemia Management
* **Hypovolaemic:** Resuscitate with IV 0.9% Normal Saline first until hemodynamically stable
* **Euvolaemic/Post-resus:** Replace free water deficit with IV 5% Dextrose (D5W) or Enteral Water
* **Rate:** Correct Na no faster than 0.5 mmol/L/hour

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Central Venous Catheter (CVC) Insertion (Seldinger Technique)
* **Indications:** Administration of 3% NaCl (prevent phlebitis), rapid highly concentrated KCl infusion (>20 mmol/hr), emergency hemodialysis (vascath)
* **Mechanics:**
  * Landmark or US-guided identification of Internal Jugular or Femoral vein
  * Infiltrate local anesthetic (1% Lignocaine)
  * Puncture vessel with introducer needle; aspirate dark, non-pulsatile blood
  * Advance guidewire through needle
  * Remove needle, make small skin stab incision
  * Advance dilator over wire, remove dilator
  * Feed CVC over wire to 15cm (Right IJV), remove wire, flush and suture
* **Consent / Complications:**
  * Arterial puncture / hematoma
  * Pneumothorax / Haemothorax (if IJV/Subclavian)
  * Infection / CLABSI
  * Arrhythmia (guidewire inducing ectopics)

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Free Water Deficit Formula (Hypernatraemia)
* **Formula:** Total Body Water (TBW) × [(Actual Na / 140) - 1]
* **TBW:** 0.6 × Body Weight (kg) for men; 0.5 × Body Weight (kg) for women

### Fractional Excretion of Sodium (FENa)
* **Formula:** (Urine Na × Serum Cr) / (Serum Na × Urine Cr) × 100%
* **Interpretation:**
  * FENa < 1% = Pre-renal etiology (e.g., Hypovolaemia)
  * FENa > 2% = Intrinsic renal etiology (e.g., Acute Tubular Necrosis)

### Pseudohyponatraemia Correction (Hyperglycemia)
* **Rule:** Add 1.6 mmol/L to measured Na for every 5.5 mmol/L increase in glucose above 5.5 mmol/L
* **Formula:** Corrected Na = Measured Na + [1.6 × (Glucose - 5.5) / 5.5]

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### ICU / HDU Admission Criteria
* Seizures or coma related to hyponatraemia requiring 3% NaCl
* Hyperkalaemia with unstable arrhythmias or requiring emergency dialysis
* Need for central CVC administration of concentrated electrolytes

### Acute Complications
* **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS):** Locked-in syndrome, quadriplegia, dysarthria (Onset 2-6 days post-correction)
* **Cardiac Arrest:** VF/Asystole (hyperkalaemia), Torsades (hypokalaemia)
* **Fluid Overload:** From aggressive 0.9% NaCl resuscitation in heart/renal failure patients

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the first and most critical drug to give in hyperkalaemia with a widened QRS? Answer: IV 10% Calcium Gluconate to stabilize the cardiac membrane.
* Trap: Giving Sodium Bicarbonate routinely for hyperkalaemia. Pearl: Bicarbonate is only effective if there is concomitant severe metabolic acidosis; it is slow and unreliable for pure potassium shifting.
* Pitfall: Chasing normal serum potassium levels in a hypokalaemic patient without checking magnesium. Always check and aggressively replace Magnesium, as hypomagnesemia causes refractory hypokalaemia via renal K wasting.
* Viva Question: How fast can you safely correct chronic severe hyponatraemia? Answer: Maximum of 8 mmol/L in the first 24 hours to prevent Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome.
* Trap: Confusing 10% Calcium Gluconate with 10% Calcium Chloride. Pearl: Calcium Chloride has 3x more elemental calcium and causes severe tissue necrosis if it extravasates; it should ideally only be given via a central line.
* Pitfall: Relying solely on the ECG to rule out hyperkalaemia. A normal ECG does not exclude life-threatening hyperkalaemia; always treat the lab value if it is critically high (> 6.5) and matches the clinical picture.
