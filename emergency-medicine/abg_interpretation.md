# Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) Analysis (動脈血氣分析)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Triage Category & Local Epidemiology

### Definition & Primary Indications
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) (動脈血氣分析)**: Diagnostic test measuring arterial oxygenation, ventilation (CO2 clearance), and acid-base status.
* **Indications**: Undifferentiated shock (休克), acute respiratory failure (急性呼吸衰竭), severe metabolic derangements (e.g., DKA, sepsis), toxicology (e.g., CO poisoning).

### Hong Kong HA Triage & Epidemiology
* **HK Triage Category (香港分流類別)**: Patients requiring ABG are almost universally **Cat 1 (Critical)** or **Cat 2 (Emergency)**.
* **Epidemiology**: Most frequent ED triggers include COPD exacerbation, severe pneumonia, sepsis, and cardiac arrest.

## 2. Acute Pathophysiology & Mechanism of Injury (MOI)

### Acid-Base Homeostasis & Buffering
* **Normal Blood pH**: Tightly regulated between 7.35 – 7.45 via the bicarbonate-carbonic acid buffer system.
* **Respiratory Regulation**: Lungs control **PaCO2** (acid). Altered within minutes via ↑/↓ alveolar ventilation.
* **Metabolic Regulation**: Kidneys control **HCO3-** (base) and H+ excretion. Altered slowly over hours to days (2-3 days for max compensation).
* **Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation**: pH ∝ [HCO3-] / PaCO2.

### Hypoxaemia & Hypercapnia Pathophysiology
* **Type I Respiratory Failure (Hypoxaemic)**: V/Q mismatch, shunt, alveolar hypoventilation, or diffusion defect → ↓ PaO2.
* **Type II Respiratory Failure (Hypercapnic)**: Alveolar hypoventilation (e.g., COPD, neuromuscular weakness, CNS depression) → ↑ PaCO2 + ↓ PaO2.
* **Tissue Hypoxia**: Inadequate O2 delivery → anaerobic metabolism → ↑ Lactic acid (Lactate) → High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (ABCDE & Secondary Survey Findings ONLY)

### Signs of Acute Acidemia & Alkalemia
* **Severe Acidemia (pH < 7.20)**: Kussmaul respirations (deep, rapid breathing), depressed conscious state, peripheral vasodilation, poor myocardial contractility.
* **Severe Alkalemia (pH > 7.60)**: Tetany, carpopedal spasm, Chvostek's/Trousseau's signs, hyperreflexia, arrhythmias.

### Signs of Hypercapnia (CO2 Retention)
* **Neurological**: Confusion, drowsiness, coma (CO2 narcosis), papilloedema.
* **Vascular**: Bounding pulse, flushed/warm extremities, asterixis (flapping tremor).

### Signs of Hypoxaemia
* **Vital Signs**: Tachycardia (bradycardia late/peri-arrest), tachypnoea.
* **Physical Findings**: Central cyanosis (blue tongue/lips), peripheral cyanosis, agitation, altered mental status.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses (Rule-Out Life Threats)

### High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA) Causes
| Mnemonic (GOLDMARK) | Specific Aetiology |
| :--- | :--- |
| **G** | Glycols (Ethylene/Propylene glycol) |
| **O** | Oxoproline (Paracetamol toxicity) |
| **L** | L-Lactate (Shock, sepsis, ischemia) |
| **D** | D-Lactate (Short bowel syndrome) |
| **M** | Methanol |
| **A** | Aspirin (Salicylate) toxicity |
| **R** | Renal failure (Uraemia) |
| **K** | Ketoacidosis (DKA, Alcoholic, Starvation) |

### Normal Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (NAGMA) Causes
| Category | Specific Aetiology |
| :--- | :--- |
| **GI Loss of HCO3-** | Severe diarrhoea, fistulas. |
| **Renal Loss of HCO3-** | Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA). |
| **Iatrogenic** | Normal saline (0.9% NaCl) massive resuscitation (hyperchloraemic acidosis). |

### Respiratory Derangements Causes
| Type | Common Aetiologies |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Respiratory Acidosis** | COPD, asthma (late/exhaustion), opiate overdose, neuromuscular disease (e.g., Guillain-Barré). |
| **Respiratory Alkalosis** | Pain, anxiety, pulmonary embolism, early asthma, salicylate toxicity (early). |

## 5. Emergency Investigations (Point-of-Care to Formal)

### Normal ABG Reference Ranges (Hong Kong HA Standards)
* **pH**: 7.35 – 7.45
* **PaO2**: 10.7 – 13.3 kPa (80 – 100 mmHg)
* **PaCO2**: 4.7 – 6.0 kPa (35 – 45 mmHg)
* **HCO3-**: 22 – 26 mmol/L
* **Base Excess (BE)**: -2 to +2 mmol/L
* **Lactate**: < 2.0 mmol/L

### VBG vs ABG Considerations
* **VBG (Venous Blood Gas)**: Excellent screening tool. pH is ~0.03-0.05 lower; pCO2 is ~5-8 mmHg (0.7-1.1 kPa) higher than ABG.
* **Constraint**: VBG cannot accurately assess PaO2 (oxygenation).

### Co-Oximetry Panel (Point-of-Care)
* **Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb)**: Normal <3% (Smokers up to 10%). Critical in suspected carbon monoxide poisoning.
* **Methaemoglobin (MetHb)**: Normal <1%. Elevated in dapsone, local anaesthetic toxicity.

## 6. Acute Resuscitation & Medical Management Algorithms

### 5-Step ABG Interpretation Algorithm
* **Step 1: Assess pH**: < 7.35 = Acidemia; > 7.45 = Alkalemia.
* **Step 2: Identify Primary Process**: Determine if PaCO2 or HCO3- matches the pH derangement.
* **Step 3: Calculate Anion Gap (AG)**: Apply to ALL metabolic acidoses.
* **Step 4: Assess Compensation**: Use Winter's formula (for metabolic acidosis) or respiratory compensation rules to check for mixed disorders.
* **Step 5: Calculate Delta Ratio**: Only if HAGMA is present to detect co-existing NAGMA or metabolic alkalosis.

### Severe Acidemia Resuscitation Strategy
* **Target**: Correct underlying cause (e.g., insulin for DKA, fluids/abx for sepsis).
* **Bicarbonate Therapy (NaHCO3)**: Highly controversial. Generally reserved for pH < 6.9-7.0 in specific contexts (TCA overdose, severe hyperkalaemia, profound NAGMA).
* **Ventilation**: If mechanical ventilation is initiated for severe metabolic acidosis, the ventilator settings MUST match the patient's intrinsic hyperventilation (high minute ventilation) to prevent rebound fatal acidemia.

## 7. Key ED Procedures & Technical Interventions (Mechanics & Consent)

### Modified Allen's Test (Prerequisite Assessment)
* **Step 1**: Elevate hand, ask patient to clench fist.
* **Step 2**: Occlude BOTH radial and ulnar arteries simultaneously.
* **Step 3**: Ask patient to open hand (palm should be blanched).
* **Step 4**: Release ulnar artery only.
* **Step 5**: Observe for capillary refill (Normal ≤ 5-7 seconds indicates adequate collateral ulnar supply).

### Radial Artery Puncture Procedure
* **Consent**: Verbal consent, explain sharp pain.
* **Position**: Wrist hyperextended over a rolled towel.
* **Prep**: Clean with 2% chlorhexidine. Consider local anaesthetic (1% lignocaine sc).
* **Puncture**: Palpate radial pulse. Insert heparin-coated ABG syringe (23G needle) at a 45° angle, bevel up.
* **Collection**: Observe for spontaneous pulsatile flash of bright red blood. Draw 1-2 mL.
* **Post-Procedure**: Remove needle, immediately apply firm manual pressure for minimum 5 mins (10 mins if coagulopathic).
* **Handling**: Expel air bubbles, cap syringe, roll between palms to mix heparin, send on ice immediately.

## 8. Clinical Decision Rules & Risk Scoring

### Anion Gap (AG) Formula & Correction
* **Formula**: `AG = Na+ - (Cl- + HCO3-)` (Note: K+ is routinely omitted).
* **Normal Range**: 8 – 12 mmol/L.
* **Albumin Correction Rule**: For every 10g/L decrease in albumin below normal (40g/L), add 2.5 mmol/L to the calculated AG.

### Winter's Formula (Metabolic Acidosis Compensation)
* **Rule**: Calculates expected PaCO2 to determine if there is a concurrent respiratory disorder.
* **Formula**: `Expected PaCO2 = (1.5 × HCO3-) + 8 (± 2) [mmHg]`
* **Interpretation**:
  * Measured PaCO2 = Expected: Simple metabolic acidosis.
  * Measured PaCO2 > Expected: Concomitant respiratory acidosis.
  * Measured PaCO2 < Expected: Concomitant respiratory alkalosis.

### Delta Ratio (Δ/Δ) Assessment (For HAGMA only)
* **Formula**: `(Calculated AG - Normal AG [12]) / (Normal HCO3 [24] - Measured HCO3)`
| Delta Ratio | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **< 0.4** | Pure NAGMA |
| **0.4 - 0.8** | Mixed HAGMA + NAGMA |
| **1.0 - 2.0** | Pure HAGMA |
| **> 2.0** | Mixed HAGMA + Metabolic Alkalosis (or compensated respiratory acidosis) |

### Respiratory Compensation Rules
| Disturbance | Expected HCO3- Change per 10 mmHg altered PaCO2 |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Resp Acidosis** | ↑ HCO3- by 1 |
| **Chronic Resp Acidosis** | ↑ HCO3- by 4 |
| **Acute Resp Alkalosis** | ↓ HCO3- by 2 |
| **Chronic Resp Alkalosis** | ↓ HCO3- by 5 |

## 9. Disposition (Admit/Discharge) & Acute Complications

### Arterial Puncture Complications
* **Local**: Haematoma, local site pain, infection.
* **Vascular**: Arterial spasm, thrombosis (causing distal ischaemia), pseudoaneurysm formation, A-V fistula.
* **Neurological**: Direct nerve injury (median or radial nerve).

### Disposition Guidelines
* **ICU / Resus Admission**: Refractory acidosis (pH < 7.20), uncompensated hypoxia (PaO2 < 8 kPa despite high-flow O2), hyperlactatemia (>4 mmol/L) with shock.
* **General Medical Ward**: COPD patients with chronic compensated hypercapnia returned to their baseline ABG status.
* **Discharge**: ABG normalises, underlying pathology reversed (e.g., resolved panic attack causing transient respiratory alkalosis).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of an isolated, unexplained lactate elevation in an asthma patient? Answer: Beta-agonist use (e.g., salbutamol nebulisers).
* Trap: Relying on standard pulse oximetry (SpO2) to rule out hypoxia in a fire victim. Pearl: SpO2 will falsely read normal (often 100%) in carbon monoxide poisoning; you must request an ABG with co-oximetry to measure COHb.
* Pitfall: Delaying the processing of an un-iced ABG sample. Samples must be analysed immediately (or placed on ice); ongoing leukocyte metabolism within the syringe will artificially ↓ PaO2 and ↑ PaCO2, simulating hypoventilation.
* Viva Question: Why do we correct the Anion Gap for albumin? Answer: Albumin is negatively charged (a major unmeasured anion). Hypoalbuminaemia falsely lowers the Anion Gap, potentially masking a HAGMA.
* Pitfall: Intubating a severe salicylate toxicity or DKA patient without planning for ventilation strategy. Pausing ventilation for intubation or setting the ventilator rate too low will cause a rapid spike in PaCO2, leading to catastrophic intracellular acidosis and peri-intubation cardiac arrest.
