# Hypertension in Pregnancy (妊娠高血壓)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Diagnostic Criteria for Hypertension
* **Hypertension (高血壓):** BP $\ge$ 140/90 mmHg (2 readings $\ge$ 4 hrs apart)
* **Severe Hypertension:** BP $\ge$ 160/110 mmHg
* **Significant Proteinuria (蛋白尿):**
  * Urine protein:creatinine ratio (PCR) $\ge$ 30 mg/mmol OR
  * Albumin:creatinine ratio (ACR) $\ge$ 8 mg/mmol OR
  * 24-hr urine protein > 300 mg/day

### Epidemiology & Context
* **Incidence:** 2-8% of all pregnancies
* **Significance:** Major cause of maternal & perinatal morbidity/mortality
* **High-Risk Threshold:** PlGF-based testing rules out **Pre-eclampsia (先兆子癇症)** between 20-35 weeks GA

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Endothelial Dysfunction & Placentation
* Abnormal trophoblast invasion → impaired spiral artery remodelling → uteroplacental ischemia
* Ischemic placenta releases anti-angiogenic factors → systemic maternal endothelial dysfunction
* Endothelial damage → ↑ capillary permeability (oedema/proteinuria), ↑ vasoconstriction (HTN), microangiopathic haemolysis

### Physiological Hemodynamic Shifts
* Normal pregnancy: ↓ peripheral vascular resistance (PVR) via progesterone & prostaglandins
* **Pre-eclampsia (先兆子癇症):** Refractory to normal vasodilators → exaggerated response to catecholamines → ↑ PVR → systemic HTN

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Symptoms of Severe Pre-eclampsia
* **Asymptomatic:** Often picked up on routine antenatal BP check
* **Severe PET Symptoms:**
  * Severe frontal/occipital headache
  * Visual disturbances (blurring, flashing lights/scotomata)
  * Epigastric/RUQ pain (hepatic capsule stretching)
  * Nausea & vomiting
  * Sudden excessive swelling (face, hands, feet)

### Clinical Findings in Severe Pre-eclampsia
* **Vital Signs:** BP $\ge$ 140/90 mmHg or $\ge$ 160/110 mmHg
* **Neurological:** 
  * Hyper-reflexia (+ve patellar reflex)
  * Clonus
  * Altered mental status/Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) ↓
* **Respiratory:** Basal lung crepitations (pulmonary oedema)
* **Abdominal:** 
  * RUQ/Epigastric tenderness
  * Uterine tenderness/firmness (suspect **Placental abruption (胎盤早剝)**)
  * Symphyseal-fundal height (SFH) lagging (suspect FGR)

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging 

### Disease Classification Table
| Diagnosis | Onset GA | HTN Present | Proteinuria Present |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chronic Hypertension** | < 20 weeks | Yes | No |
| **Chronic Renal Disease** | < 20 weeks | +/- | Yes |
| **Superimposed PET** | < 20 weeks (HTN) | Yes | New onset > 20 weeks |
| **Gestational Hypertension** | > 20 weeks | Yes | No |
| **Gestational Proteinuria** | > 20 weeks | No | Yes |
| **Pre-eclampsia (PET)** | > 20 weeks | Yes | Yes |

### Severe Variants
* **Eclampsia (子癇症):** Convulsive condition associated with PET
* **HELLP Syndrome:** Haemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelet count

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Maternal Investigations in Hypertensive Pregnancy
* **Urine:** Mid-stream urine (MSU) for PCR/ACR, routine microscopy
* **Haematology:** CBC (↓ platelets, ↓ Hb/haemoconcentration), Blood smear (schistocytes = haemolysis)
* **Biochemistry:** 
  * LRFT (↑ ALT/AST, ↑ urate, ↑ creatinine)
  * Clotting profile (PT, aPTT, Fibrinogen for DIC)
  * Serum Magnesium (if urine output < 30 ml/hr on MgSO4)
* **Biomarkers:** PlGF-based testing (rules out PET)

### Fetal Surveillance in Hypertensive Pregnancy
* **Ultrasound:**
  * Fetal biometry (EFW) → FGR
  * Amniotic fluid index (AFI) → Oligohydramnios
  * Umbilical artery Doppler → ↑ Resistance index (RI), absent/reversed end-diastolic flow (REDF)
* **CTG Tracings:**
  * Baseline tachycardia/bradycardia
  * ↓ Variability
  * Late decelerations (uteroplacental insufficiency)

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Stabilization of Severe Hypertensive Pregnancy
* Admit for inpatient observation if severe HTN or high PIERS/PREP-S score
* Strict fluid balance: Restrict to **80 ml/hour** total input
* Foley catheter: Hourly urine output monitoring (target > 20 ml/hr)
* Continuous SaO2 monitoring (target > 95%)

### Antihypertensives in Hypertensive Pregnancy
* **Target BP:** 135/85 mmHg (Chronic/Gestational), < 150/80-100 mmHg (Acute Severe)
* **Oral 1st Line:**
  * **Labetalol:** 100 mg q45min (max 1200 mg/day)
  * **Nifedipine:** 5 mg 1st dose, then 10 mg q30m (avoid if aortic stenosis)
  * **Methyldopa:** 3rd line alternative
* **IV Acute Rescue:**
  * **Labetalol IV:** 20 mg loading, 20-80 mg q30m (max 300 mg)
  * **Hydralazine IV:** 5-10 mg q30m (max 20 mg)

### Seizure Prophylaxis: Magnesium Sulphate (硫酸鎂)
* **Indications:** Severe PET features, planned birth < 24 hrs, or Eclampsia
* **Regimen:** 
  * Loading: 4g IV over 5-15 mins
  * Maintenance: 1g/hr IV infusion for 24 hrs post-delivery/last fit
* **Recurrent Seizure:** Further 2-4g IV loading OR IV Diazepam 10 mg

### Steroids for Fetal Lung Maturity
* **Betamethasone:** 12 mg IM q24h x 2 doses (if birth planned < 37w and < 7 days)

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Timing of Delivery in Hypertensive Pregnancy
* **< 37 weeks GA:** Expectant management UNLESS:
  * Refractory severe BP (despite $\ge$ 3 meds)
  * Maternal SaO2 < 90%
  * Deteriorating labs (HELLP, renal failure)
  * Placental abruption
  * Ongoing neurological features
  * Fetal distress (REDF on Doppler, abnormal CTG)
* **> 37 weeks GA:** Recommend delivery within 24-48 hours once PET diagnosed

### Mode of Delivery & Intrapartum Care
* **Vaginal Delivery:** Preferred. Shorten 2nd stage with instrumental delivery (ventouse/forceps) to prevent maternal pushing-induced severe HTN
* **Caesarean Section (LSCS):** Indicated for standard obstetric reasons or acute fetal/maternal compromise
* **Anaesthesia:** Avoid IV fluid preload before epidural (prevents pulmonary oedema)
* **3rd Stage Management:** 
  * USE **Oxytocin (催產素)**
  * STRICTLY AVOID **Ergometrine/Syntometrine** (causes severe vasoconstriction)

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Immediate Postpartum Care After Hypertensive Pregnancy
* Continue **MgSO4** for 24 hours post-delivery
* Monitor SaO2 and strict fluid balance for 24-48 hours
* Target BP: < 150/100 mmHg

### Postpartum Pharmacological Adjustments
* **Cease Methyldopa:** Risk of post-partum depression
* **Safe Breastfeeding Antihypertensives:** Labetalol, Nifedipine, Enalapril, Captopril, Atenolol, Metoprolol
* Avoid diuretics / ARBs during breastfeeding

### Follow-up & Future Pregnancy Risk
* Medical review at 6-8 weeks post-partum (check proteinuria/BP)
* **Recurrence Risk:**
  * Gestational HTN: ~22%
  * PET: ~16%
  * Severe PET/HELLP < 34 weeks: ~33%
* **Prophylaxis in Future Pregnancy:** Low-dose Aspirin (75-150mg) from 12-36 weeks

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications of Hypertensive Pregnancy
* **Neurological:** Eclampsia (seizures), Cerebral haemorrhage
* **Cardiopulmonary:** Pulmonary oedema
* **Hepatic/Haematological:** HELLP syndrome, DIC
* **Renal:** Acute kidney injury (AKI)
* **Obstetric:** Placental abruption (胎盤早剝)

### Fetal & Neonatal Complications of Hypertensive Pregnancy
* Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR)
* Iatrogenic Prematurity (Respiratory Distress Syndrome)
* Intrauterine Hypoxia / Asphyxia
* Intrauterine Death (IUD)

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the first-line medication to prevent pre-eclampsia in high-risk women, and when should it be initiated? Answer: Low dose aspirin (75-150 mg) started between 12 and 16 weeks gestation, continued until 36 weeks.
* Trap: Administering Syntometrine for active management of the third stage in a patient with pregnancy-induced hypertension. Pearl: Strictly avoid ergometrine-containing agents as they cause severe vasoconstriction and hypertensive crisis; use Oxytocin alone.
* Pitfall: Pre-loading a pre-eclamptic patient with IV fluids prior to epidural insertion. This severely increases the risk of iatrogenic pulmonary oedema due to endothelial leakiness and impaired renal excretion. Maintain strict fluid restriction of 80 ml/hour.
* Viva Question: What are the clinical signs of Magnesium Sulphate toxicity and how is it monitored? Answer: Loss of patellar reflex, respiratory depression (RR < 12), and oliguria. Monitor urine output hourly and check serum Mg if urine output drops below 30 ml/hr.
