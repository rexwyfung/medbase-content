# Subfertility (不孕症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition
* **Subfertility:** failure to conceive after **12 months** of regular unprotected intercourse
* Earlier investigation if:
  * Female age ≥35
  * Oligomenorrhoea/amenorrhoea
  * Known tubal disease
  * Endometriosis
  * Severe male factor

### Epidemiology and Clinical Burden
* Affects ~10-15% of couples
* In Hong Kong, ART is regulated and generally offered to **legally married couples**
* Causes:
  * Ovulatory ~25%
  * Tubal/peritoneal ~20%
  * Male factor ~40%
  * Unexplained ~25%

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Female Factors
* **Ovulatory dysfunction:**
  * PCOS
  * Hypothalamic amenorrhoea
  * Hyperprolactinaemia
  * POI
* **Tuboperitoneal disease:**
  * PID
  * Endometriosis
  * Previous surgery
* **Uterine factors:**
  * Fibroids
  * Polyps
  * Adhesions
* **Cervical factors:** post-coital test now rarely used

### Male Factors
* Spermatogenesis disorder
* Obstruction
* Ejaculatory dysfunction
* Varicocele
* Genetic causes: Klinefelter, Y microdeletion

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Female History
* Menstrual cycle regularity
* Dysmenorrhoea or dyspareunia
* Previous pelvic infection or surgery
* Cervical surgery
* Age and smoking status
* Previous pregnancies
* Treatment for endometriosis/fibroids

### Male History
* Previous mumps, trauma, or surgery
* Heat exposure
* Smoking/alcohol/anabolic steroids
* Sexual dysfunction

### Examination
* Female:
  * BMI
  * Hirsutism/acne
  * Thyroid enlargement
  * Galactorrhoea
  * Uterine size, mobility, adnexal mass
* Male:
  * Testicular volume
  * Varicocele
  * Cryptorchidism

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Cause Classification
| Cause | Examples |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ovulatory** | PCOS, hypothalamic, hyperprolactinaemia |
| **Tubal** | Hydrosalpinx, PID, sterilisation |
| **Uterine** | Fibroid, polyp, Asherman |
| **Male** | Oligozoospermia, azoospermia |
| **Unexplained** | Normal basic workup |

### Semen Analysis Categories
* **Normozoospermia:** normal parameters
* **Oligozoospermia:** low concentration
* **Asthenozoospermia:** poor motility
* **Teratozoospermia:** abnormal morphology
* **Azoospermia:** no sperm in ejaculate

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Female Baseline Tests
* Day 2-3 FSH, LH, oestradiol
* AMH for ovarian reserve
* TSH, prolactin if cycle abnormal
* Testosterone/SHBG if PCOS
* Rubella and hepatitis status
* HIV and syphilis screening

### Tubal and Uterine Assessment
* **Hysterosalpingography:** tubal patency
* **Laparoscopy with dye test:** gold standard for tubal disease
* **Hysteroscopy:** intrauterine pathology
* Transvaginal ultrasound:
  * Antral follicle count
  * Fibroids/polyps
  * Hydrosalpinx
  * PCO morphology

### Male Tests
* Two semen analyses 6-12 weeks apart
* FSH, LH, testosterone if azoospermia
* Karyotype and Y microdeletion if severe

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Ovulation Induction
* **Letrozole:** first-line for PCOS
* **Clomiphene citrate:** alternative
* **Metformin:** insulin resistance adjunct
* **Gonadotropins:** if oral agents fail

### Adjuncts
* Lifestyle modification and weight loss
* Treat thyroid/prolactin abnormalities
* Treat endometriosis if pain coexists

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Assisted Reproduction
* **IUI:**
  * Mild male factor
  * Unexplained infertility
  * Cervical factor
* **IVF:**
  * Tubal disease
  * Severe endometriosis
  * Severe male factor
  * Failed ovulation induction/IUI
* **ICSI:**
  * Severe male factor
  * Previous fertilisation failure

### Surgical Fertility Treatment
* Laparoscopic adhesiolysis or salpingostomy
* Hysteroscopic polypectomy/myomectomy
* Varicocelectomy in selected men
* Surgical sperm retrieval if obstructive azoospermia

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After Fertility Treatment
* Early pregnancy scan to confirm intrauterine pregnancy
* Multiple pregnancy surveillance
* Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome monitoring

### Psychological Follow-up
* Counsel about stress and expectation
* Consider fertility preservation if age or POI
* Discuss gamete donation if indicated

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome
* Ectopic pregnancy
* Multiple pregnancy
* Pregnancy complications from advanced maternal age

### Fetal and Gynaecological Complications
* Preterm birth
* Low birthweight
* Congenital anomaly risk remains age-related
* Miscarriage
* Psychological distress

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Investigate earlier if female age ≥35, amenorrhoea, or known tubal/male factor
* Repeat semen analysis if first sample abnormal
* Use letrozole first-line for anovulatory PCOS
* Refer for IVF after failed ovulation induction/IUI or tubal disease
* Monitor OHSS risk if >20 follicles, raised oestradiol, or abdominal distension

* Investigate earlier if female age ≥35 or known male/tubal factor
* Repeat semen analysis if first sample abnormal
* Use letrozole first-line for anovulatory PCOS
* Refer for IVF after failed ovulation induction/IUI or tubal disease
* Monitor for OHSS with ultrasound and oestradiol during gonadotropin therapy
* Offer early pregnancy scan after ART because ectopic risk is increased

* Check AMH before ovarian stimulation if ovarian reserve is uncertain
* Interpret semen analysis against WHO reference ranges
* Use hysteroscopy if HSG suggests intrauterine adhesion or polyp
* Consider genetic testing if azoospermia or severe oligozoospermia is found
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: When should subfertility investigation begin earlier than 12 months? Answer: Female age ≥35, amenorrhoea, known tubal disease, or severe male factor.
* Trap: Starting gonadotropins without monitoring. Pearl: Ultrasound monitoring reduces multiple pregnancy and OHSS risk.
* Pitfall: Assuming azoospermia excludes biological fatherhood. Consider surgical sperm retrieval.
* Viva Question: Which tubal assessment is gold standard? Answer: Laparoscopy with dye test.
* Pitfall: Forgetting to repeat semen analysis. Single abnormal result is insufficient.
