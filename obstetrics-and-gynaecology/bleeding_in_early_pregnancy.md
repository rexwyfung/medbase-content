# Bleeding in Early Pregnancy (早孕期出血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition and Gestational Context
* **Bleeding in early pregnancy:** vaginal bleeding before **24 weeks’ gestation**
* Commonest presentation:
  * Threatened miscarriage
  * Inevitable miscarriage
  * Incomplete miscarriage
  * Complete miscarriage
  * Silent miscarriage
  * Ectopic pregnancy
  * Molar pregnancy

### Local Epidemiology
* Miscarriage occurs in ~10-20% of recognised pregnancies
* Most occur in the first trimester
* Majority are due to chromosomal abnormality

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Miscarriage Mechanisms
* **Aneuploidy** is the commonest cause
* Other causes:
  * Uterine anomaly
  * Antiphospholipid syndrome
  * Uncontrolled diabetes/thyroid disease
  * Cervical weakness
  * Infection

### Recurrent Miscarriage
* Defined as **3 or more consecutive miscarriages**
* Consider:
  * Parental karyotype
  * Antiphospholipid antibodies
  * Uterine anomaly
  * Thyroid dysfunction
  * PCOS

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### History
* Gestational age
* Painful or painless bleeding
* Passage of tissue
* Shoulder tip pain or syncope → ectopic
* Hyperemesis or uterus large for dates → molar pregnancy
* Previous miscarriage or ectopic
* Fertility treatment

### Examination
* Pallor, tachycardia, hypotension
* Abdominal tenderness or peritonism
* Uterine size
* Speculum:
  * Cervical os open or closed
  * Products of conception
  * Local bleeding source
* Bimanual:
  * Cervical excitation
  * Adnexal mass

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Miscarriage Classification Table
| Type | Pain | Cervical Os | Uterine Size |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Threatened** | Mild | Closed | Corresponding to dates |
| **Inevitable** | Crampy | Open | Corresponding |
| **Incomplete** | Crampy | Open | Small for dates |
| **Complete** | Mild/none | Closed | Small |
| **Silent** | None | Closed | Small |

### Critical Differential Diagnoses
* Ectopic pregnancy
* Molar pregnancy
* Cervical polyp or cancer
* Implantation bleeding
* Retained products

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Maternal Tests
* Urine pregnancy test
* Serum hCG
* CBC, blood group, Rh status
* Progesterone if ectopic suspected
* TSH, TPO antibodies, antiphospholipid screen if recurrent loss

### Ultrasound Criteria
* **Threatened miscarriage:** intrauterine sac with fetal heartbeat
* **Silent miscarriage:**
  * CRL ≥7 mm with no heartbeat
  * MSD ≥25 mm with no embryo
* **Incomplete miscarriage:** heterogeneous cavity echoes >15 mm
* **Complete miscarriage:** thin, regular endometrial line
* **Ectopic:** empty uterus with adnexal mass or free fluid
* **Molar:** snowstorm appearance, absent fetus, enlarged uterus

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Haemodynamically Unstable Patient
* ABC approach
* IV access and cross-match
* Urgent theatre if ectopic rupture or heavy bleeding
* Anti-D if Rh negative

### Medical Management of Miscarriage
* **Threatened:** observe, avoid heavy exertion
* **Progestogen** may be considered if recurrent early miscarriage
* **Incomplete:** misoprostol or expectant management
* **Silent/inevitable:** expectant, medical, or surgical evacuation

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Miscarriage Treatment Options
* **Expectant:** suitable if clinically stable, small retained products, compliant patient
* **Medical:** misoprostol ± mifepristone
* **Surgical:** suction evacuation if:
  * Heavy bleeding
  * Infection
  * Large retained products
  * Failed medical/expectant management

### Recurrent Miscarriage
* Investigate with karyotype, antiphospholipid antibodies, pelvic ultrasound
* Aspirin + heparin if antiphospholipid syndrome
* Refer to recurrent miscarriage clinic

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up
+
### After Miscarriage
* Monitor bleeding and fever
* Anti-D if Rh negative
* Histology of products
* Psychological support
* Contraception as desired
* Review after 2-6 weeks

### Future Pregnancy
* Early dating scan
* Pre-pregnancy optimisation of diabetes/thyroid disease
* Consider aspirin/heparin if indicated

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Heavy bleeding
* Sepsis
* Retained products
| Asherman’s syndrome after curettage
* Ectopic rupture
* Gestational trophoblastic neoplasia after molar pregnancy

### Fetal and Gynaecological Complications
* Recurrent miscarriage
* Stillbirth risk
* Psychological trauma
* Subfertility after uterine damage

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Diagnose silent miscarriage only if CRL ≥7 mm or MSD ≥25 mm without heartbeat
* Repeat scan ≥7 days later if criteria not met
* Offer surgical evacuation if heavy bleeding, infection, or retained products >15 mm
* Give anti-D if Rh negative after surgical or medical management
* Investigate recurrent miscarriage after three consecutive losses

* Use TVS and hCG before diagnosing miscarriage type
* Repeat scan ≥7 days later if viability uncertain
* Offer surgical evacuation if bleeding is heavy or infection present
* Give anti-D if Rh negative after surgical or medical management
* Investigate after 3 consecutive miscarriages
* Exclude ectopic pregnancy even if miscarriage seems complete

* Suspect ectopic if pain is unilateral and hCG rises abnormally
* Consider molar pregnancy if uterus is large for dates or hCG is disproportionately high
* Consider cervical polyp or ectropion if bleeding is bright red and painless with closed os
* Do not diagnose complete miscarriage without excluding ectopic
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What ultrasound criteria diagnose silent miscarriage? Answer: CRL ≥7 mm without cardiac activity or MSD ≥25 mm without embryo.
* Trap: Prescribing progestogen for every threatened miscarriage. Pearl: Benefit is mainly in women with recurrent early loss.
* Pitfall: Assuming complete miscarriage without excluding ectopic pregnancy.
* Viva Question: When should anti-D be given? Answer: Rh-negative women after miscarriage, ectopic, or invasive procedure.
* Pitfall: Missing recurrent miscarriage workup. Define as 3 or more consecutive losses.
