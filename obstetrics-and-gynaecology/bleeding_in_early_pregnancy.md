# Bleeding in Early Pregnancy (懷孕初期出血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Early Pregnancy Bleeding
* **Definition:** Vaginal bleeding occurring <24 weeks GA.
* **Context:** Includes miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, molar pregnancy, and local tract lesions.

### Local Epidemiology (Hong Kong)
* **Incidence:** Occurs in ~20% of all pregnancies.
* **Miscarriage (流產):** 15-20% of clinically recognized pregnancies.
* **Ectopic Pregnancy (宮外孕):** ~1-2% of all pregnancies.
* **Molar Pregnancy (葡萄胎):** ~1 in 1000 pregnancies in SE Asia.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Miscarriage Pathophysiology
* **Primary Cause:** >70% due to fetal chromosomal aneuploidy.
* **Mechanism:** Failure of trophoblastic invasion → ↓hCG production → corpus luteum breakdown → ↓progesterone → decidual necrosis & sloughing → uterine contractions & bleeding.

### Ectopic Pregnancy Pathophysiology
* **Anatomy:** 95% implant in Fallopian tube (輸卵管) ampulla.
* **Mechanism:** Embryo implants in tubal mucosa → trophoblast invades muscularis (lacks decidual resistance) → tubal distension → eventual transmural rupture → intra-abdominal haemorrhage.

### Gestational Trophoblastic Disease Pathophysiology
* **Complete Mole:** 46,XX or 46,XY (empty ovum fertilized by 1-2 sperm) → diffuse trophoblastic hyperplasia, absent fetal tissue.
* **Partial Mole:** Triploidy (69,XXX/XXY/XYY; 1 ovum + 2 sperm) → focal hyperplasia, fetal tissue present.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History Findings
* **Menstrual:** LMP → calculate EDD/GA.
* **Bleeding:** Spotting (brown/pink) vs heavy bleeding with clots/tissue passage.
* **Pain:** Midline suprapubic cramping (miscarriage) vs unilateral iliac fossa pain (ectopic).
* **Alarm Symptoms:** Syncope, dizziness, shoulder tip pain (diaphragmatic irritation from haemoperitoneum).

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* **General:** Soft, non-tender (threatened miscarriage).
* **Peritonism:** Rebound tenderness, guarding, rigidity (+ve in ruptured ectopic).
* **Uterine Size:** Palpable above symphysis if large (molar pregnancy → size > dates).

### Speculum Examination Findings
* **Volume:** Active bleeding, pooling in fornices.
* **Cervical Os:** Closed (threatened/missed) vs Open (inevitable/incomplete).
* **Tissue:** POC visible at external os (+ve in incomplete miscarriage).
* **Local Lesions:** Cervical ectropion, polyps, trauma.

### Bimanual Examination Findings
* **Cervix:** Cervical excitation / Chandelier sign (+ve in ectopic/PID).
* **Adnexa:** Palpable mass, unilateral tenderness (+ve in ectopic).
* **Uterus:** Size correlation with GA, tenderness.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnoses of Early Pregnancy Bleeding
| Category | Conditions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pregnancy-Related** | Miscarriage, Ectopic Pregnancy, Molar Pregnancy, Implantation bleeding |
| **Lower Genital Tract** | Cervical ectropion, Cervical polyp, Cervicitis/Vaginitis, Cervical malignancy |
| **Systemic/Trauma** | Coagulopathy, Post-coital trauma |

### Clinical Staging of Miscarriage
| Stage | Bleeding/Pain | Cervical Os | Ultrasound Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Threatened** | Mild | Closed | Viable IUP (+ve FH) |
| **Inevitable** | Heavy/Severe | Open | Viable or non-viable IUP |
| **Incomplete** | Heavy with tissue | Open | Retained POC in cavity |
| **Complete** | Subsiding | Closed | Empty cavity |
| **Missed** | Nil/Spotting | Closed | Non-viable embryo (no FH) |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Laboratory Investigations
* **Urine:** UPT (+ve confirms pregnancy).
* **Serum β-hCG:** Quantitative serial tracking (48h interval). Normal IUP → ↑ by >66%. Failing IUP/Ectopic → suboptimal ↑ or plateau.
* **Blood:** CBP (Hb ↓ in haemorrhage), Type & Screen, Rh status (crucial for Anti-D).
* **Serum Progesterone:** <20 nmol/L suggests failing pregnancy; >60 nmol/L suggests viable IUP.

### Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVS) Criteria for Miscarriage
* **Missed Miscarriage Diagnostic Criteria (HK/RCOG):**
	* **CRL:** ≥7.0 mm with absent FH.
	* **MSD:** ≥25.0 mm with absent fetal pole.
	* **Timeline:** Absence of FH ≥14 days after scan showing GS without yolk sac, OR ≥11 days after scan showing GS with yolk sac.

### TVS Criteria for Ectopic & Molar Pregnancy
* **Ectopic:** Empty uterine cavity + inhomogeneous adnexal mass ("bagel sign" / tubal ring) + free fluid in Pouch of Douglas.
* **Pregnancy of Unknown Location (PUL):** +ve UPT but empty cavity and no adnexal mass on TVS.
* **Molar:** Heterogeneous echogenic mass in cavity ("snowstorm" appearance) + bilateral theca lutein cysts.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization

### Acute Stabilization & Resuscitation
* **Airway/Breathing:** High-flow O2 if shocked.
* **Circulation:** x2 large-bore IV access (14G/16G).
* **Fluids:** Rapid crystalloid infusion (Normal Saline/Hartmann's).
* **Blood:** Crossmatch 4-6 units packed RBCs → transfuse if haemodynamically unstable.

### Pharmacological Management of Miscarriage
* **Misoprostol (米索前列醇):** Prostaglandin E1 analogue.
* **Dose:** 800mcg vaginally, sublingually, or orally (single dose for missed/incomplete miscarriage).
* **Anti-D Immunoglobulin:** Administer 250 IU to non-sensitized Rh -ve women presenting with significant bleeding or requiring surgical intervention.

### Pharmacological Management of Ectopic Pregnancy
* **Methotrexate (甲氨蝶呤):** Folic acid antagonist, inhibits DNA synthesis in trophoblasts.
* **Dose:** 50 mg/m² IM single dose.
* **Strict HA Criteria:** Haemodynamically stable, unruptured, adnexal mass <3.5cm, no FH, baseline β-hCG <1500-5000 IU/L, compliant for follow-up.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surgical Management of Miscarriage
* **Suction Evacuation of Uterus (吸宮手術):**
* **Indications:** Haemodynamic instability, heavy bleeding, failed medical management, patient preference.
* **Procedure:** Cervical priming (Misoprostol 400mcg) → mechanical dilation → vacuum aspiration of POC under GA or LA.

### Surgical Management of Ectopic Pregnancy
* **Laparoscopic Salpingectomy (腹腔鏡輸卵管切除術):** Definitive removal of affected tube. Standard for ruptured ectopic or if contralateral tube is healthy.
* **Laparoscopic Salpingotomy:** Linear incision to remove ectopic tissue, preserving tube. Used if contralateral tube is absent/diseased. Requires serial β-hCG to rule out persistent trophoblastic disease.

### Surgical Management of Molar Pregnancy
* **Suction Evacuation:** Definitive treatment. Avoid medical management due to risk of uterine contractions causing trophoblastic embolization to lungs.
* **Oxytocin:** Run IV infusion *during/after* evacuation (not before) to control bleeding.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-operative Care & Counseling
* **Monitoring:** Vital signs, vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain.
* **Discharge:** Usually same-day for uncomplicated suction evacuation.
* **Counseling:** Reassure patient (1 miscarriage does not significantly ↑ risk for next pregnancy).

### Follow-up Protocol
* **Medical Miscarriage:** UPT at 3 weeks to confirm complete resolution.
* **Ectopic (MTX):** Check β-hCG on Day 4 & Day 7 (expect >15% drop between D4-D7). Weekly β-hCG until <5 IU/L.
* **Molar Pregnancy:** Strict MCHC/HA follow-up. Weekly β-hCG until normal x3, then monthly for 6 months (complete mole) or until normal (partial mole).

### Contraception
* **Miscarriage/Ectopic:** Ovulation returns in ~2 weeks. Resume contraception immediately if pregnancy not desired.
* **Molar:** Strict avoidance of pregnancy during β-hCG surveillance period to prevent confusing a new pregnancy with GTN. COCP or barrier methods recommended; avoid IUCD until β-hCG normal (perforation risk).

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal & Surgical Complications
* **Acute:** Haemorrhagic shock, DIC.
* **Surgical:** Uterine perforation (子宮穿孔), cervical tear, haemorrhage requiring laparotomy.
* **Infectious:** Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID), endometritis, sepsis.
* **Psychological:** Anxiety, depression, grief.

### Future Gynaecological Complications
* **Post-Ectopic:** Tubal factor infertility, 10-15% recurrence risk of ectopic pregnancy.
* **Post-Evacuation:** Asherman's syndrome (intrauterine adhesions causing secondary amenorrhoea).
* **Post-Molar:** Progression to Gestational Trophoblastic Neoplasia (GTN) / Choriocarcinoma (絨毛膜癌) in ~15% of complete moles.
* **Recurrent Miscarriage:** Defined as ≥3 consecutive first-trimester losses (warrants autoimmune/karyotype screening).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic ultrasound triad for a molar pregnancy? Answer: Snowstorm appearance, absent fetal parts, and bilateral theca lutein cysts.
* Trap: Reassuring a patient with bleeding that an empty uterus on ultrasound rules out ectopic pregnancy. Pearl: An empty uterus with a positive pregnancy test is a Pregnancy of Unknown Location (PUL) and requires serial β-hCG tracking until ectopic is definitively excluded.
* Pitfall: Administering Methotrexate for an ectopic pregnancy without checking liver and renal function first. Methotrexate is nephrotoxic and hepatotoxic, and contraindicated in patients with significant organ dysfunction.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a threatened miscarriage from an inevitable miscarriage clinically? Answer: In a threatened miscarriage, the cervical os is closed; in an inevitable miscarriage, the cervical os is open.
* Trap: Giving misoprostol to a patient presenting with an open cervical os and heavy bleeding causing shock. Pearl: Haemodynamically unstable patients require immediate surgical suction evacuation and active resuscitation, not medical management.
* Pitfall: Failing to administer Anti-D immunoglobulin to a Rhesus-negative woman undergoing surgical evacuation for a miscarriage. This risks alloimmunization and haemolytic disease of the newborn in future pregnancies.
