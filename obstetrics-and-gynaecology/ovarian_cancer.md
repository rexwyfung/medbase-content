# Ovarian Cancer (卵巢癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Ovarian Cancer
* **Malignant Neoplasm** originating from the ovary (卵巢), fallopian tube, or primary peritoneum.
* **Epithelial Ovarian Cancer (EOC)**: Comprises ~90% of cases (e.g., serous, mucinous, endometrioid, clear cell).
* **Non-Epithelial Tumours**: Germ cell tumours, sex cord-stromal tumours (typically affect younger demographic).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Incidence**: 6th most common cancer among women in Hong Kong (HK).
* **Mortality**: Most lethal gynaecological malignancy locally due to late presentation.
* **Median Age**: Peak incidence ~50-60 years (EOC predominantly postmenopausal).
* **Genetic Predisposition**: ~10-15% of EOC cases in HK linked to **BRCA1/2** mutations (↑ risk in familial breast/ovarian cancer syndromes).

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Pathophysiology & Carcinogenesis
* **Incessant Ovulation Theory**: Repeated surface epithelial trauma & repair cycles → ↑ genetic mutation risk.
* **Fallopian Tube Origin Theory**: Majority of high-grade serous carcinomas originate from **Secretory Intraepithelial Carcinoma (STIC)** in the distal fimbriae (傘端).
* **Metastatic Spread Pathway**: Exfoliation leading to transcoelomic spread (peritoneal seeding) → ↑ ascites production. Lymphatic spread → pelvic & para-aortic nodes.

### Relevant Pelvic Anatomy & Vascular Supply
* **Ovarian Blood Supply**: Ovarian artery (direct branch from abdominal aorta) & ovarian branch of uterine artery.
* **Venous Drainage**: Right ovarian vein → IVC; Left ovarian vein → Left renal vein.
* **Lymphatic Drainage**: Follows ovarian vessels to **Para-aortic Nodes** (L1 level), traversing alongside the infundibulopelvic ligament (骨盆漏斗韌帶).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Patient History & Symptomatology
* **Early Stage Disease**: Often entirely asymptomatic ("Silent Killer").
* **Gastrointestinal Symptoms**: Early satiety, bloating, dyspepsia, ↓ appetite, altered bowel habits.
* **Pelvic & Abdominal Symptoms**: ↑ abdominal girth, vague dull aching pelvic pain, urinary frequency/urgency.
* **High-Risk Factors**: Nulliparity, early menarche, late menopause, positive family history.

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* **Distension**: ↑ abdominal girth (due to massive ascites or large pelvic mass).
* **Palpable Mass**: Firm, irregular, fixed mass arising from the pelvis.
* **Percussion**: +ve **Shifting Dullness** (ascites).
* **Fluid Thrill**: May be +ve in profound ascites.

### Speculum & Bimanual Examination Findings
* **Speculum Exam**: Usually normal (required to rule out coexisting cervical pathology/bleeding).
* **Bimanual Exam**: Hard, irregular, fixed adnexal mass independent of the uterus.
* **Pouch of Douglas (POD) Exam**: Nodularity felt on rectovaginal examination (strongly suggests peritoneal metastasis).

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging 

### Critical Differential Diagnoses
* **Benign Ovarian Mass**: Endometrioma (子宮內膜異位瘤), Dermoid cyst, Functional cyst.
* **Gynaecological Malignancy / Tumours**: Degenerating Uterine Fibroid (子宮肌瘤) / Leiomyosarcoma, Tubo-ovarian abscess (TOA).
* **Gastrointestinal Pathology**: Colorectal carcinoma, Appendiceal tumour, Diverticular mass.

### FIGO Clinical Staging of Ovarian Cancer
| Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage I** | Tumour confined strictly to ovaries (I) or fallopian tubes (FT). |
| **Stage IA** | 1 ovary/FT involved; capsule intact; -ve washings. |
| **Stage IB** | Both ovaries/FT involved; capsules intact; -ve washings. |
| **Stage IC** | Tumour spill (IC1), capsule ruptured before surgery (IC2), or +ve washings/ascites (IC3). |
| **Stage II** | Tumour involves 1 or both ovaries/FT with **pelvic extension** (below pelvic brim). |
| **Stage III** | Micro/macroscopic **peritoneal metastasis** beyond pelvis OR +ve retroperitoneal nodes. |
| **Stage IIIC** | Macroscopic peritoneal metastasis >2cm. |
| **Stage IV** | **Distant metastasis** excluding peritoneal metastasis. |
| **Stage IVA** | Pleural effusion with +ve cytology. |
| **Stage IVB** | Hepatic/splenic parenchymal metastases or extra-abdominal organ involvement. |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Laboratory Investigations & Tumour Markers
* **CA-125**: ↑ in ~80% of EOC (especially serous type). Crucial baseline for monitoring.
* **CEA**: Indicated to rule out bowel primary (associated with mucinous tumours).
* **Young Patients (<40)**: Must check AFP, $\beta$-hCG, LDH (rule out germ cell tumours).
* **Baseline Bloods**: CBP, LFT, RFT baseline mapping prior to major surgery/chemotherapy.

### Risk of Malignancy Index (RMI) Criteria
* **Formula**: RMI = U $\times$ M $\times$ CA-125
* **Ultrasound Score (U)**: 0, 1, or 3. (Points given for: multilocular cyst, solid areas, bilateral lesions, ascites, intra-abdominal metastases).
* **Menopausal Status (M)**: Pre-menopausal = 1; Post-menopausal = 3.
* **Clinical Threshold**: RMI >200 indicates high risk of malignancy → mandatory referral to Gynae-Oncology.

### Specific Ultrasound Findings for Malignancy
* **Solid Components**: Presence of papillary projections, thick septations (>3mm).
* **Doppler Flow**: ↑ vascularity within solid areas demonstrating ↓ resistance index.
* **Bilateral Involvement**: Suspicious complex masses in both adnexa.
* **Extra-ovarian Disease**: Significant free fluid (ascites), visible peritoneal nodules.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Stabilization for Complications
* **Malignant Bowel Obstruction**: NPO, Nasogastric (NG) tube decompression, IV fluid resuscitation, IV anti-emetics (e.g., Metoclopramide), IV Octreotide (↓ GI secretions).
* **Massive Ascites / Pleural Effusion**: Therapeutic paracentesis / thoracentesis for acute symptomatic relief (dyspnoea/abdominal compartment).
* **Thromboembolism Prophylaxis**: LMWH (e.g., Enoxaparin) & compression stockings initiated immediately (high VTE risk in pelvic malignancy).

### Systemic Pharmacology & Chemotherapy Principles
* **First-line Chemotherapy**: IV **Carboplatin** + **Paclitaxel** (3-weekly cycle, typically 6 cycles total).
* **Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy (NACT)**: 3 cycles NACT → Interval Debulking Surgery (IDS) → 3 cycles adjuvant. Utilized if primary debulking is not feasible or poor maternal performance status.
* **Targeted Therapy**: **Bevacizumab** (anti-VEGF) for advanced stage disease; **PARP Inhibitors** (e.g., Olaparib) as maintenance therapy for **BRCA** mutated cases.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surgical Staging & Primary Cytoreduction
* **Primary Goal**: Maximal effort to achieve no macroscopic residual disease (R0 resection).
* **Incision**: Midline Laparotomy (wide access from pubic symphysis to xiphisternum).
* **Standard Staging Procedure**: Total Abdominal Hysterectomy (TAH) + Bilateral Salpingo-Oophorectomy (BSO) + Infracolic Omentectomy + Peritoneal washings/biopsies.
* **Lymphadenectomy**: Pelvic and para-aortic lymph node sampling/dissection if indicated by HA guidelines.

### Fertility-Sparing Surgery
* **Indications**: Young patients desiring to conceive, strict Stage IA/IC1 (Grade 1/2 only), non-epithelial tumours.
* **Procedure**: Unilateral Salpingo-Oophorectomy (USO) + careful surgical staging of the contralateral ovary, omentum, and peritoneum.
* **Post-Childbearing Strategy**: Completion surgery (TAH + BSO) strongly recommended for EOC once family is complete.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Immediate Post-Operative Care
* **VTE Prophylaxis**: Prolonged LMWH strictly for 28 days post-op.
* **Bowel Function**: Early mobilization, strict monitoring for paralytic ileus.
* **Fluid Balance**: Strict I/O monitoring; beware of massive third-spacing fluid loss following ascites drainage/surgery.

### Long-term Follow-Up Protocol
* **Schedule**: Every 3 months for years 1-2, every 6 months for years 3-5, then annually.
* **Clinical Assessment**: Symptom review, pelvic examination, CA-125 serial monitoring.
* **Imaging**: CT/PET-CT Scan indicated ONLY if CA-125 ↑ or new clinical symptoms develop.
* **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT)**: Generally safe for symptomatic relief post-BSO in EOC (avoid entirely in endometrioid or granulosa cell tumours).

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Gynaecological & Disease Complications
* **Tumour Rupture / Torsion**: Causes acute abdomen requiring emergency operative intervention.
* **Ascites & Cachexia**: Profound malnutrition, sarcopenia, and systemic protein depletion.
* **Malignant Bowel Obstruction**: Terminal complication due to advanced peritoneal carcinomatosis.

### Treatment-Related Complications
* **Chemotherapy Toxicity**: Alopecia, bone marrow suppression (neutropenia), peripheral neuropathy (Paclitaxel), hypersensitivity reactions (Carboplatin).
* **Surgical Complications**: Bowel/ureteric injury, pelvic lymphocyst formation, wound dehiscence/infection.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the significance of the Risk of Malignancy Index (RMI)? Answer: It triages patients; RMI >200 warrants mandatory referral to a tertiary gynae-oncology centre for specialized staging surgery.
* Trap: Assuming a raised CA-125 always means ovarian cancer. Pearl: CA-125 can be significantly elevated in benign conditions like endometriosis, PID, pregnancy, and fibroids; it must be interpreted alongside ultrasound and clinical picture.
* Pitfall: Performing percutaneous biopsy of a suspicious early-stage ovarian cyst. Biopsy can cause tumour spill and upstage the disease from Stage IA to IC; primary surgical excision intact is preferred.
