# Ovarian Cancer (卵巢癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Disease Definition and Histological Subtypes
* **Ovarian cancer:** primary malignant tumour of the ovary, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneum
* **Epithelial ovarian cancer:** 85% of cases
  * High-grade serous carcinoma is the commonest
* **Germ cell tumours:** usually younger women <30 years
* **Sex-cord stromal tumours:** often hormone-secreting
* Many high-grade serous cancers now originate from distal fallopian tube epithelium

### Local Epidemiology
* In Hong Kong, ovarian cancer is a leading gynaecological malignancy
* Most women present at advanced stage because early disease is often asymptomatic
* Lifetime risk ~1-2% in the general population

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Ovulation-Related Carcinogenesis
* Repeated ovulation causes:
  * Surface epithelial injury
  * Inclusion cysts
  * Genetic instability
* Risk increases with:
  * Nulliparity
  * Early menarche
  * Late menopause
* Risk decreases with:
  * Combined oral contraceptive use
  * Multiparity
  * Breastfeeding
  * Tubal ligation or salpingectomy

### Genetic Basis
* **BRCA1:** up to 40-45% lifetime ovarian cancer risk
* **BRCA2:** up to 15-20% risk
* **Lynch syndrome:** endometrial and colorectal cancer predisposition
* TP53 mutations are common in high-grade serous carcinoma

### Pattern of Spread
* Transcoelomic spread throughout peritoneum
* Omental cake
* Diaphragmatic deposits
* Bowel serosa
* Pelvic and para-aortic lymph nodes
* Haematogenous spread to liver/lung is late

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Early Symptoms
* Often vague and non-specific
* Persistent bloating
* Early satiety
* Abdominal discomfort
* Urinary urgency or frequency
* Constipation
* Dyspareunia
* Fatigue

### Advanced Symptoms
* Abdominal distension/ascites
* Weight loss
* Anorexia
* Bowel obstruction
* Pleural effusion and breathlessness
* Leg swelling from venous or lymphatic obstruction

### Examination Findings
* Ascites
* Solid, irregular, fixed pelvic mass
* Nodularity in pouch of Douglas
* Omental mass
* Supraclavicular or inguinal lymphadenopathy
| Pleural effusion
* Signs of bowel obstruction

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
* Functional ovarian cyst
* Endometrioma
* Benign cystadenoma
* Dermoid cyst
* Fibroid
* Tubo-ovarian abscess
* Metastatic gastrointestinal cancer
* Primary peritoneal cancer
* Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome

### FIGO Epithelial Ovarian Cancer Staging
| Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Confined to ovaries |
| **II** | Pelvic extension |
| **III** | Peritoneal implants outside pelvis ± nodes |
| **IV** | Distant metastasis |
* Stage is surgically assigned

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Imaging
* **Transvaginal and transabdominal ultrasound**
* Suspicious features:
  * Multilocular cyst
  * Solid areas
  * Papillary projections
  * Thick irregular septa
  * High vascularity
  * Ascites
  * Peritoneal deposits
* **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis**
* PET-CT in selected cases
* MRI if ultrasound indeterminate

### Tumour Markers
| Marker | Associated Tumour |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CA125** | Epithelial ovarian cancer |
| **CEA** | Gastrointestinal metastasis |
| **CA19-9** | Mucinous tumour |
| **AFP** | Yolk sac tumour |
| **hCG** | Choriocarcinoma |
| **LDH** | Dysgerminoma |
| **Inhibin** | Granulosa cell tumour |

### Risk Assessment
* **RMI** = ultrasound score × menopausal status × CA125
* RMI >250 → refer to gynaecological oncology
* Test CA125, CEA, CA19-9 in postmenopausal pelvic mass

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Complications
* Ascites:
  * Paracentesis for symptom relief
  * Send fluid for cytology
* Pleural effusion:
  * Drain if breathless
* Bowel obstruction:
  * NBM, NG tube, IV fluids
  * Consider stent or palliative surgery
* VTE:
  * LMWH therapeutic anticoagulation

### Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy
Consider if:
* Stage III/IV disease
* Poor performance status
* Unresectable disease
* Massive ascites or pleural effusion
* Not fit for immediate cytoreductive surgery
Regimen:
* Carboplatin + paclitaxel
* Usually 3 cycles before interval debulking

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Early-Stage Surgery
* Total hysterectomy
* Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
* Omental biopsy
* Peritoneal biopsies
* Pelvic and para-aortic lymphadenectomy
* Appendicectomy if mucinous tumour

### Advanced-Stage Surgery
* Primary cytoreductive surgery aiming for **complete macroscopic resection**
* Includes:
  * Total hysterectomy and BSO
  * Omentectomy
  * Peritoneal stripping
  * Bowel resection if needed
  * Splenectomy in selected cases
* If complete resection not possible, consider neoadjuvant chemotherapy first

### Adjuvant Chemotherapy
* Stage IC/II: chemotherapy usually offered
* Stage III/IV: carboplatin + paclitaxel 6 cycles
* Anti-angiogenic therapy:
  * Bevacizumab in selected advanced disease
* PARP inhibitors:
  * BRCA-positive or homologous recombination deficient tumours

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-operative Care
* ICU/high-dependency care after major cytoreduction
* Monitor:
  * Blood loss
  * Sepsis
  * Anastomotic leak
  * Thromboembolism
* Thromboprophylaxis is essential
* Nutritional support if bowel surgery

### Follow-up
* Clinical review every 2-3 months initially
* CA125 trend if initially raised
| CT if recurrence suspected
* Genetic counselling for all high-grade serous ovarian cancer and BRCA-associated tumours

### Family Risk Management
* BRCA carriers:
  * Risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy after family completion, usually around 40-45 years
* Consider opportunistic salpingectomy during benign pelvic surgery in average-risk women who have completed family

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal and Surgical Complications
* Bowel obstruction
* Fistula
* Anastomotic leak
* Ureteric injury
* Massive haemorrhage
* Sepsis
* VTE
* Death

### Gynaecological and Fertility Complications
* Infertility after bilateral oophorectomy
* Premature menopause
* Osteoporosis
| Cardiovascular disease risk
* Hormone replacement therapy may be needed after risk-reducing surgery

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Which tumour marker is most useful in epithelial ovarian cancer? Answer: CA125, although it can also rise in benign disease.
* Trap: Assuming a normal CA125 excludes ovarian cancer. Pearl: Early-stage and mucinous tumours may have normal CA125.
* Pitfall: Using RMI >250 as diagnostic. Pearl: It is a referral threshold, not a definitive diagnosis.
* Viva Question: Which patients are offered PARP inhibitor maintenance? Answer: Those with BRCA mutation or homologous recombination deficiency, particularly after platinum-sensitive disease.
* Pitfall: Missing inherited cancer risk. Test BRCA in high-grade serous ovarian cancer.
* Trap: Offering primary debulking when disease is clearly unresectable. Pearl: Consider neoadjuvant chemotherapy.
