# Cervical Cancer and Screening (子宮頸癌與篩查)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Disease Definition and Precursor Spectrum
* **Cervical cancer:** malignant epithelial tumour of the uterine cervix
* Precursor lesions:
  * **CIN1:** low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion
  * **CIN2/3:** high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion
  * **CGIN:** cervical glandular intraepithelial neoplasia
* Nearly all cervical cancers are caused by persistent high-risk **HPV** infection, especially types **16 and 18**

### Local Epidemiology and Screening Context
* In Hong Kong, cervical cancer remains an important preventable cancer
* Department of Health Family Health Service and HA provide cytology-based screening
* Current Hong Kong practice commonly starts at **25 years** if ever sexually active
* Screening interval:
  * Consecutive normal smears → every **3 years**
  * HPV-based strategies may use 5-year intervals where implemented
* HPV vaccination is available and most effective before sexual debut

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### HPV-Driven Carcinogenesis
* High-risk HPV infects basal cervical epithelial cells
* E6 and E7 oncoproteins inactivate:
  * **p53**
  * **Rb**
* Persistent infection → integration → CIN → invasive carcinoma over years
* Transformation zone is the highest-risk site

### Local Spread Pattern
* Direct extension to:
  * Vagina
  * Parametrium
  * Bladder
  * Rectum
* Lymphatic spread:
  * Pelvic nodes first
  * Para-aortic nodes later
* Haematogenous spread to lung, liver, bone is late

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Early Symptoms
* Often asymptomatic and detected by screening
* **Postcoital bleeding**
* Intermenstrual bleeding
* Abnormal vaginal discharge
* Abnormal cervical cytology

### Advanced Symptoms
* Heavy or persistent bleeding
* Offensive discharge
* Pelvic or back pain
* Leg swelling from lymphatic obstruction
* Urinary symptoms, haematuria
| Rectal bleeding or tenesmus
* Weight loss and fatigue

### Examination Findings
* Early:
  * Normal cervix
  * Ectropion
  * Contact bleeding
* Advanced:
  * Friable, ulcerated, exophytic cervical tumour
  * Bleeding on contact
  * Firm, irregular cervix
* Bimanual and rectal examination:
  * Parametrial induration
  * Fixed cervix
  * Nodular pelvic side wall
  * Hydronephrosis if ureteric obstruction

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
* Cervical ectropion
* Cervicitis
* Cervical polyp
* CIN/CGIN
* Cervical cancer
* Endometrial cancer involving cervix
* Vaginal cancer
* Metastatic tumour

### FIGO 2018 Cervical Cancer Staging
| Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Confined to cervix |
| **IA1** | Stromal invasion <3 mm |
| **IA2** | Invasion ≥3 mm but <5 mm |
| **IB1** | ≥5 mm and <2 cm |
| **IB2** | ≥2 cm but <4 cm |
| **IB3** | ≥4 cm |
| **IIA** | Upper 2/3 vagina without parametrium |
| **IIB** | Parametrial involvement |
| **IIIA** | Lower 1/3 vagina |
| **IIIB** | Pelvic wall/hydronephrosis |
| **IIIC** | Pelvic or para-aortic lymph nodes |
| **IVA** | Bladder/rectal invasion |
| **IVB** | Distant metastasis |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Screening and Triage Tests
* **Cervical cytology**
* **HPV DNA testing**
* Reflex HPV typing if cytology abnormal
* Colposcopy if:
  * ASC-US with high-risk HPV
  * LSIL persistent >12 months
  * HSIL, AGC, AIS
  * Postcoital bleeding with abnormal cervix

### Colposcopy Findings
* Acetowhite epithelium
* Punctation
* Mosaic pattern
* Atypical vessels
* Ulceration
* Glandular abnormality

### Diagnostic and Staging Tests
* Punch biopsy
* **LLETZ** for excision and treatment
* **Cone biopsy** if:
  * Glandular lesion
  * Need to assess invasion depth
  * Inadequate colposcopy
* MRI pelvis for stromal/parametrial invasion
* CT/PET-CT for nodal and distant disease
| Cystoscopy/protoscopy if bladder or rectal involvement suspected
* Renal function and ultrasound if hydronephrosis

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Abnormal Smear Management
* CIN1:
  * Often observe
  * Treat if persistent or colposcopically suspicious
* CIN2/3:
  * LLETZ excision
  * Ensure clear margins
* CGIN:
  * Excisional biopsy essential
  * Exclude invasive adenocarcinoma

### Acute Heavy Bleeding
* Vaginal packing
* Tranexamic acid
* Blood transfusion if significant anaemia
* Urgent radiotherapy or surgical assessment if uncontrolled

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Early-Stage Disease
* **Stage IA1 without LVSI:** cone biopsy may suffice if margins clear and fertility desired
* **Stage IA2-IB1:** radical hysterectomy with pelvic lymphadenectomy
* **Fertility-sparing trachelectomy** in selected small early tumours

### Locally Advanced Disease
* **Stage IIB-IVA:** concurrent chemoradiotherapy
  * External beam radiotherapy
  * Brachytherapy
  * Cisplatin chemotherapy
* Consider para-aortic node treatment if involved

### Recurrent or Metastatic Disease
* Pelvic exenteration if isolated central recurrence
* Chemotherapy:
  * Cisplatin/paclitaxel
  * Pembrolizumab if PD-L1 positive
* Palliative radiotherapy for bleeding or pain

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After LLETZ
* Avoid intercourse, tampons, and swimming ~2-4 weeks
* Monitor bleeding and infection
* Histology review
* Repeat cytology/HPV at 6 months
* Preterm birth risk slightly increased after excision; document depth of specimen

### After Radical Surgery
* Bladder training and residual volume monitoring
* Lymphoedema assessment
* Thromboprophylaxis
* Sexual function and menopause support if oophorectomy

### Surveillance
* Cytology/HPV every 3-6 months initially
* Continue at least 10 years
* Imaging if symptoms or abnormal findings

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Heavy bleeding
* Renal failure from ureteric obstruction
* Fistula formation
* Radiotherapy bowel/bladder injury
* Lymphoedema
* Sexual dysfunction

### Gynaecological and Obstetric Complications
* Cervical stenosis
* Premature ovarian insufficiency if radiotherapy/oophorectomy
* Mid-trimester loss
* Cervical incompetence after excision
* Preterm prelabour rupture of membranes

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Which cytology result mandates colposcopy? Answer: HSIL, AGC/AIS, or ASC-US with high-risk HPV.
* Trap: Treating CIN1 without histological confirmation. Pearl: Many CIN1 lesions regress.
* Pitfall: Using cytology alone after treatment for CIN2/3. Combine cytology and HPV testing.
* Viva Question: Which stage requires chemoradiotherapy rather than radical surgery? Answer: Stage IIB or above.
* Pitfall: Missing glandular disease. Use excisional cone biopsy rather than destructive treatment.
* Trap: Assuming HPV vaccination replaces screening. Pearl: Continue screening according to local protocol.
