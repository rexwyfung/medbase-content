# Fetal Growth Restriction (胎兒生長受限)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of SGA & FGR
* **Small for Gestational Age (SGA) (小於孕齡)**: Estimated Fetal Weight (**EFW**) or Abdominal Circumference (**AC**) < 10th percentile for Gestational Age (**GA**).
* **Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR) (胎兒生長受限)**: Failure of a fetus to reach its pre-determined biological growth potential, typically driven by placental insufficiency and abnormal fetoplacental perfusion. Severe FGR is classified as EFW or AC < 3rd percentile.

### Local Epidemiology & Gestational Context
* **Incidence**: Occurs in approximately 3-5% of all pregnancies locally.
* **High-Risk Gestational Context**: Significantly ↑ prevalence in multiple pregnancies (affecting up to 20%), advanced maternal age, and maternal vascular comorbidities including Pre-eclampsia (先兆子癇), Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), and Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS).

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Maternal Vascular Pathophysiology
* Defective trophoblastic invasion of spiral arteries → ↓ uteroplacental blood flow and high-resistance circulation.
* Chronic hypoxia → maternal endothelial cell dysfunction, thrombophilia [e.g., APS, SLE (系統性紅斑狼瘡)].
* Associated maternal hypoxemia conditions (e.g., cyanotic heart disease, severe restrictive lung disease).

### Fetal-Placental Pathophysiology
* **Placental**: Infarction, circumvallate placenta, confined placental mosaicism, abnormal umbilical cord insertion.
* **Fetal**: Chromosomal aneuploidies, structural anomalies, congenital infections (TORCH, primarily Cytomegalovirus, Rubella).
* **Hemodynamic Adaptation**: Fetal hypoxia → redirection of cardiac output → preferential blood flow to the brain, myocardium, and adrenal glands (**Brain-sparing effect**) → ↓ splanchnic and renal blood flow → ↓ fetal urine production → Oligohydramnios (羊水過少).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History Assessment
* **Previous Obstetric Hx**: Prior FGR, previous stillbirth (死產), early-onset pre-eclampsia.
* **Current Pregnancy Hx**: Subjective ↓ fetal movements (胎動), inaccurate dating, inadequate maternal weight gain.
* **Medical Hx**: Chronic HTN, chronic renal disease, autoimmune disorders (SLE, APS).
* **Social Hx**: Smoking, alcohol consumption, illicit drug use.

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* **Symphyseal Fundal Height (SFH)**: Lagging > 2 cm behind GA in weeks (detectable after 24 weeks).
* **Palpation**: Clinically small fetal size on Leopold maneuvers.
* **Liquor Volume Assessment**: Uterus may feel tightly wrapped around the fetus, with easily palpable fetal parts (suggestive of oligohydramnios).

### Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings
* **Speculum Exam**: Conduct to rule out ruptured membranes if vaginal fluid leaking is suspected (can mimic oligohydramnios).
* **Bimanual Exam**: Usually unremarkable unless evaluating for co-existing cervical incompetence or preterm labour.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnoses
* **Constitutionally Small Fetus**: SGA without underlying pathology, normal amniotic fluid, normal Dopplers, structurally normal, ↑ parental likelihood of being small.
* **Wrong Dates**: Incorrect LMP or delayed presentation for dating scan.
* **Oligohydramnios from Pre-labour Rupture of Membranes (PROM)**.

### Clinical Staging: Symmetrical vs Asymmetrical FGR
| Feature | Symmetrical FGR | Asymmetrical FGR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Early (< 28 weeks) | Late (> 28 weeks) |
| **Aetiology** | Intrinsic (Chromosomal, TORCH) | Extrinsic (Placental insufficiency, Pre-eclampsia) |
| **Cellular Defect** | ↓ Cell number (Hyperplastic phase) | ↓ Cell size (Hypertrophic phase) |
| **Growth Pattern** | Head & Body proportionately small | Head-sparing (Normal HC, ↓ AC) |
| **HC/AC Ratio** | Normal | Elevated (↑) |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Specific Ultrasound Criteria (Biometry & Morphology)
* **Biometry**: EFW < 10th percentile, AC < 10th percentile.
* **Amniotic Fluid**: Deepest Vertical Pocket (DVP) < 2 cm or Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI) < 5 cm.
* **Morphology**: Detailed structural anomaly scan required to rule out congenital defects.

### Specific Ultrasound Criteria (Doppler Studies)
* **Umbilical Artery (UA) Doppler**: ↑ Pulsatility Index (PI) > 95th percentile, Absent End-Diastolic Flow (**AEDF**), or Reversed End-Diastolic Flow (**REDF**).
* **Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) Doppler**: ↓ PI < 5th percentile (indicates brain-sparing vasodilation).
* **Cerebroplacental Ratio (CPR)**: Calculated as MCA PI / UA PI. CPR < 5th percentile indicates severe redistribution.
* **Ductus Venosus (DV) Doppler**: Absent or reversed a-wave indicates impending fetal acidemia and right heart failure.

### Cardiotocography (CTG) Tracings
* **Baseline Fetal Heart Rate**: May demonstrate tachycardia or bradycardia.
* **Variability**: ↓ baseline variability (< 5 bpm).
* **Decelerations**: Late decelerations (胎心晚期減速) due to uteroplacental insufficiency and hypoxia.

### Laboratory Investigations
* **Maternal Blood**: Karyotype/Microarray (if severe/early FGR), TORCH serology (CMV, Toxoplasmosis, Syphilis), Thrombophilia screen (Lupus anticoagulant, Anticardiolipin antibodies, Anti-β2GP1) for APS.
* **Pre-eclampsia Screen**: Complete Blood Picture (CBP), Renal Function Test (RFT), Liver Function Test (LFT), Uric acid, Urine protein-creatinine ratio.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization

### Pharmacological Prevention & Maintenance
* **Low-Dose Aspirin (阿士匹靈)**: 75–150 mg PO daily starting at 12–16 weeks until 36 weeks for women with high-risk factors (e.g., previous FGR, chronic HTN, SLE).
* **Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH)**: Prophylactic or therapeutic dosing if concomitant APS.

### Acute Stabilization & Fetal Neuroprotection
* **Antenatal Corticosteroids (皮質類固醇)**: Betamethasone 12 mg IM every 24 hours for 2 doses (or Dexamethasone) if delivery is anticipated < 34-36 weeks to promote fetal lung maturity.
* **Magnesium Sulphate (MgSO4) (硫酸鎂)**: 4g IV loading dose, then 1g/hr IV infusion for 24 hours if delivery is imminent < 32 weeks for fetal neuroprotection (reduces risk of cerebral palsy).

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Timing of Delivery Algorithm
| Doppler / Clinical Finding | Recommended Timing of Delivery |
| :--- | :--- |
| **SGA with Normal UA Doppler** | 37 - 38 weeks |
| **FGR with ↑ UA PI (End-diastolic flow present)** | 37 weeks |
| **FGR with AEDF (Absent End-Diastolic Flow)** | 32 - 34 weeks (post-steroids) |
| **FGR with REDF (Reversed End-Diastolic Flow)** | 30 - 32 weeks (post-steroids) |
| **Abnormal DV Doppler (Reversed a-wave) OR Abnormal CTG** | Deliver IMMEDIATELY via LSCS regardless of GA |

### Mode of Delivery Principles
* **Vaginal Delivery**: Appropriate for uncomplicated FGR with normal Doppler, cephalic presentation, and reactive CTG. Requires continuous electronic fetal heart rate monitoring (CFHM).
* **Lower Segment Caesarean Section (LSCS) (剖腹產)**: Indicated for AEDF, REDF, abnormal DV Doppler, abnormal CTG, severe pre-eclampsia, or malpresentation (e.g., breech).
* **Intrapartum Care**: Avoid aggressive induction if fetal reserves are poor. Ensure strict fluid balance and avoid intrapartum hypoxia.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Neonatal Resuscitation & Care
* Paediatrician / Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) standby required at delivery.
* Immediate clamping of cord may be needed if the neonate requires urgent resuscitation; otherwise, practice delayed cord clamping.
* Strict monitoring for neonatal hypoglycemia, hypothermia, polycythemia, and jaundice.

### Maternal Post-partum Care & Follow-up
* **Placental Pathology**: Send placenta for histopathology (evaluate for infarctions, thrombosis, infection).
* **Thromboprophylaxis**: Early mobilization, graduated compression stockings, +/- LMWH depending on maternal VTE risk score.
* **Pre-conception Counseling**: Optimize underlying medical conditions (BP control, SLE remission) before subsequent pregnancies.

### Contraception
* Avoid estrogen-containing combined oral contraceptives if underlying HTN or thrombophilia (APS, SLE with positive APLA) is present.
* Progestogen-only pills, implant, or Levonorgestrel Intrauterine System (Mirena) are safe and effective alternatives.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Fetal & Neonatal Complications
* **Antenatal**: Intrauterine fetal death (IUFD), hypoxia, acidosis.
* **Intrapartum**: Fetal distress, meconium aspiration syndrome.
* **Neonatal**: Hypoglycemia (due to ↓ glycogen stores), hypothermia (due to ↓ subcutaneous fat), polycythemia, hyperbilirubinemia, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), respiratory distress syndrome (RDS).
* **Long-term (Barker Hypothesis)**: ↑ risk of adult-onset metabolic syndrome, Type 2 DM, HTN, cardiovascular disease.

### Maternal Complications
* Pre-eclampsia (先兆子癇) / HELLP Syndrome.
* Operative delivery complications (LSCS risks, PPH).
* Psychological impact of prematurity, NICU admission, or perinatal loss.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical ultrasound Doppler vessel for deciding the timing of delivery in early-onset severe FGR? Answer: The Ductus Venosus (DV). An absent or reversed a-wave indicates impending fetal right heart failure and acidemia.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate symmetrical from asymmetrical FGR on ultrasound? Answer: Symmetrical FGR shows global reduction in biometry with a normal HC/AC ratio, whereas asymmetrical FGR shows a brain-sparing effect with an elevated HC/AC ratio.
* Trap: Assuming isolated oligohydramnios is always due to placental insufficiency. Pearl: Always exclude ruptured membranes (PROM) and fetal renal anomalies (e.g., renal agenesis, obstructive uropathy) as alternative causes of oligohydramnios.
* Pitfall: Recommending vaginal delivery for a fetus with Reversed End-Diastolic Flow (REDF). Fetuses with REDF have extremely poor physiological reserves and will not tolerate the hypoxic stress of uterine contractions; an immediate LSCS is indicated.
