# Fetal Growth Restriction (胎兒生長受限)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition and Diagnostic Thresholds
* **Fetal growth restriction (FGR):** pathological failure of a fetus to achieve its growth potential
* Antenatal proxy: **estimated fetal weight <10th centile**
* **Severe FGR:** EFW <3rd centile or abnormal Doppler
* **Small for gestational age (SGA):** EFW or birthweight <10th centile; may be constitutional

### Gestational-Onset Classification
* **Early-onset FGR:** <32 weeks
  * Usually placental
  * High risk of abnormal Doppler
* **Late-onset FGR:** ≥32 weeks
  * Often less marked Doppler abnormality
  * May present near term with stillbirth risk

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Uteroplacental Insufficiency Mechanism
* Abnormal trophoblast invasion → poor spiral artery remodelling
* Reduced uteroplacental perfusion → chronic fetal hypoxaemia
* Fetus redistributes perfusion to:
  * Brain
  * Heart
  * Adrenals
* Result: **brain-sparing effect**

### Maternal and Fetal Causes
* **Maternal:**
  * Hypertension/pre-eclampsia
  * Chronic kidney disease
  * SLE
  * Antiphospholipid syndrome
  * Severe anaemia
  * Malnutrition
* **Placental:**
  * Infarction
  * Abruption
  * Velamentous cord insertion
* **Fetal:**
  * Aneuploidy
  * Congenital infection
  * Structural anomaly
  * Multiple pregnancy

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### History Suggesting FGR
* Hypertension or proteinuria
* Chronic renal disease, SLE, or antiphospholipid syndrome
* Smoking, alcohol, or substance misuse
* Previous FGR, stillbirth, or pre-eclampsia
* Reduced fetal movements
* Vaginal bleeding or abdominal pain

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* **Symphysiofundal height less than gestational age**
* Clinically suspected oligohydramnios
* Malpresentation due to small fetal size
* Uterine tenderness or hypertonus if abruption

### Speculum and Bimanual Findings
* Usually normal
* Assess for:
  * Cervical dilation
  * Vaginal bleeding
  * Signs of preterm labour

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis of Suspected FGR
| Diagnosis | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Constitutional SGA** | Normal growth velocity, normal Doppler |
| **Placental FGR** | Falling growth centile, abnormal Doppler |
| **Aneuploidy** | Early onset, structural anomaly, polyhydramnios |
| **Congenital infection** | Multiple anomalies, hepatosplenomegaly |
| **Inaccurate dating** | Dating not confirmed early |

### Clinical Severity Staging
* **Mild:** EFW 3rd-10th centile, normal Doppler
* **Moderate:** EFW <3rd centile or oligohydramnios
* **Severe:** EFW <3rd centile with abnormal Doppler or CTG

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Maternal Laboratory Tests
* CBC
* Renal and liver function
* Uric acid
* Urine PCR
* Blood glucose/HbA1c
* Infection screen if congenital infection suspected
* Thrombophilia and antiphospholipid screen if recurrent or severe

### Ultrasound Assessment
* Accurate gestational dating
* Serial growth scan every 2 weeks
* **EFW <10th centile**
* Abdominal circumference often lags first
* Oligohydramnios: AFI <5 cm
* Umbilical artery Doppler:
  * Increased pulsatility index
  * Absent end-diastolic flow
  * Reversed end-diastolic flow
* Middle cerebral artery Doppler:
  * Reduced PI suggests brain sparing
* Cerebroplacental ratio:
  * MCA PI / UA PI
  * Low CPR suggests redistribution

### CTG Findings in FGR
* Reduced variability
* Late decelerations
* Baseline tachycardia
* Sinusoidal pattern if fetal anaemia
* Bradycardia in terminal compromise

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Maternal Disease Optimization
* Treat pre-eclampsia and hypertension
* Optimise chronic disease control
* Smoking cessation
* Nutritional support
* Aspirin prophylaxis in future pregnancy if high risk

### Fetal Lung Maturity and Neuroprotection
* **Betamethasone** if delivery likely before 34-37 weeks
* **Magnesium sulphate** for neuroprotection if delivery before 30 weeks
* Admit if severe FGR or abnormal Doppler

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surveillance Strategy
* Fetal movement chart
* Serial ultrasound and Doppler every 1-2 weeks
* CTG at least weekly if severe or late-onset
* Admit if severe Doppler abnormality

### Timing of Delivery
* Normal Doppler and normal growth velocity: continue surveillance to 38-40 weeks
* Severe FGR or abnormal Doppler: deliver earlier
* Reversed end-diastolic flow: usually deliver by 30-32 weeks
* Absent end-diastolic flow: usually deliver by 32-34 weeks
* Abnormal CTG: deliver irrespective of gestation

### Mode of Delivery
* Caesarean section if severe Doppler abnormality or abnormal CTG
* Vaginal delivery possible if stable and monitored

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Neonatal Care Priorities
* Neonatal team at delivery
* Watch for:
  * Hypoglycaemia
  * Hypothermia
  * Polycythaemia
  * Necrotising enterocolitis
  * Respiratory distress

### Maternal Follow-up and Future Pregnancy
* Placental histology
* Review cause of FGR
* Pre-pregnancy counselling
* Aspirin prophylaxis in next pregnancy if placental cause

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Pre-eclampsia
* Placental abruption
* Caesarean delivery
* Anxiety and psychological distress

### Fetal and Neonatal Complications
* Stillbirth
* Hypoxia and acidosis
* Preterm birth
* Neonatal hypoglycaemia
* Necrotising enterocolitis
* Neurodevelopmental impairment
* Adult cardiovascular and metabolic disease risk

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Which Doppler abnormality most strongly predicts fetal acidaemia in early-onset FGR? Answer: Absent or reversed end-diastolic flow in the umbilical artery, especially with abnormal ductus venosus.
* Trap: Using estimated fetal weight alone to time delivery. Pearl: Timing depends on gestation, Doppler trend, CTG, growth velocity, and maternal factors.
* Pitfall: Missing late-onset FGR. A low cerebroplacental ratio or abnormal middle cerebral artery Doppler may identify risk even when estimated fetal weight is near normal.
* Viva Question: When would you deliver rather than continue surveillance in early-onset FGR? Answer: If there is absent/reversed end-diastolic flow, abnormal ductus venosus, abnormal CTG, reduced movements, or failing growth.
* Trap: Assuming all small-for-gestational-age fetuses require delivery at 37 weeks. Pearl: Constitutional SGA with normal growth velocity and Doppler may continue with surveillance.
