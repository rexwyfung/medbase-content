# Contraception & Family Planning (避孕與家庭計劃)

## 1. Contraceptive Counselling & Efficacy

### The GATHER Approach
* **G**: Greet clients
* **A**: Ask about themselves (medical/obstetric history, STI risk)
* **T**: Tell about all available methods
* **H**: Help choose a method matching their needs
* **E**: Explain how to use it correctly
* **R**: Return for follow-up

### Efficacy & Failure Rates (Typical Use)
* **Very Effective (<1%)**: Implant (0.05%), Vasectomy (0.15%), Female Sterilization (0.5%), LNG-IUD (0.5%), Cu-IUD (0.8%).
* **Effective (1-9%)**: Injectables (DMPA), Lactational Amenorrhoea Method (LAM) (for 6 months), Combined Oral Contraceptives (COC), Progestogen-only pills (POP).
* **Moderately Effective (10-19%)**: Male Condoms (13%), Standard Days Method, Withdrawal.
* **Less Effective (20+)**: Female Condoms (21%), Spermicides.


## 2. Natural Family Planning (自然避孕法)

### Fertility Awareness Methods
* **Lifespan**: Sperm viable up to 7 days; ovum viable for 1 day. Fertile window lasts ~8-9 days.
* **Standard Days Method**: Abstain Days 8-19 (requires regular 26-32 day cycles).
* **Basal Body Temperature (BBT)**: 0.5°C rise sustained for 3 days indicates ovulation. Abstain from Day 1 to 3rd day of elevated temp.
* **Cervical Secretions**: Peak day = last day of thin, clear, stretchy mucus. Fertile period ends on 4th day after peak.
* **Symptothermal Method**: Combines BBT + Cervical secretions.

### Lactational Amenorrhoea Method (LAM / 哺乳期閉經避孕法)
* **Mechanism**: Suckling ↑ prolactin → inhibits GnRH/ovulation.
* **Criteria for 98% efficacy (Must meet ALL 3)**:
  1. Fully or nearly fully breastfeeding (on demand, day and night).
  2. Amenorrhoeic.
  3. < 6 months postpartum.
* *Pearl*: If any criterion is unmet (e.g., menses return or formula introduced), an alternative contraceptive is immediately required.


## 3. Barrier Methods & Spermicides (屏障避孕法)

### Male & Female Condoms (避孕套)
* **Benefits**: Immediate effect, only method protecting against STIs (HIV, HPV, chlamydia).
* **Usage**: Water-based/silicone lubricants ONLY (oil-based degrades latex).
* **Female Condom (FC2)**: Nitrile sheath, can be inserted before coitus. Maximal wear time 8 hours.

### Spermicides (殺精劑)
* **Nonoxynol-9 (N-9)**: Inactivates sperm. Max duration ~60 mins.
* **Risks**:
	* Does NOT protect against STIs.
	* Frequent use disrupts vaginal epithelium → ↑ risk of HIV/STI transmission.
	* Contraindicated for anal sex.


## 4. Combined Hormonal Contraceptives (混合荷爾蒙避孕法)

### Formulations & Mechanism of COCPs
* **Types**: Combined Oral Contraceptives (COC), Patch, Vaginal Ring, Combined Injectable (CIC).
* **Mechanism**:
	* Suppresses FSH/LH → inhibits ovulation.
	* Progestogen thickens cervical mucus and thins endometrium.

### Non-Contraceptive Benefits of COCPs
* ↓ Menstrual blood loss & primary dysmenorrhoea.
* ↓ Risk of ovarian, endometrial, and colorectal cancers (protection persists >30 years).
* Improves acne, hirsutism, and PCOS symptoms (especially drospirenone/Yaz).
* Treats endometriosis-related pain.

### Risks & Side Effects of COCPs
* **Vascular**:
	* ↑ Risk of VTE (highest in first 4 months or restarting after >1 month break).
	* Drospirenone/desogestrel have higher VTE risk than levonorgestrel.
	* ↑ Risk of MI/stroke in smokers and hypertensive patients.
* **Oncological**: Transient ↑ risk of breast and cervical cancer (returns to baseline ~10 years post-cessation).

### Absolute Contraindications (WHO MEC 4) of COCPs
* Postpartum < 6 weeks if breastfeeding, or < 21 days if not breastfeeding (+ VTE risk).
* Smokers ≥ 35 years old (≥ 15 cigarettes/day).
* BP ≥ 160/100 mmHg or hypertension with vascular disease.
* Current/past VTE, IHD, Stroke, or thrombogenic mutations.
* Migraine with aura (any age).
* Current breast cancer.
* SLE with positive/unknown antiphospholipid antibodies.

### COC Missed Pill Rules (21-Day Pack)
* **1 Pill Missed (<72 hrs)**: Take missed pill immediately. Continue pack. No backup needed.
* **≥ 2 Pills Missed (≥72 hrs)**: Take most recent missed pill. Discard others. Continue pack. Use condoms x 7 days.
  * *If in Week 1*: Consider EC if UPSI occurred in HFI or Week 1.
  * *If in Week 3*: Omit the Hormone-Free Interval (HFI) and start next pack immediately.


## 5. Progestogen-Only Contraceptives (單一荷爾蒙避孕法)

### Progestogen-Only Pills (POP / 單一荷爾蒙避孕丸)
* **Mechanism**: Thickens cervical mucus (traditional POP) + inhibits ovulation (desogestrel 75µg).
* **Benefits**: Safe for breastfeeding, avoids estrogenic cardiovascular/VTE risks.
* **Missed Pill**: For traditional POP, if >3 hours late (or >12 hours for desogestrel), take immediately + use condoms x 48 hours.

### Depot Medroxyprogesterone Acetate (DMPA / 避孕針)
* **Regimen**: 150mg IM every 3 months (12 weeks).
* **Side Effects**: Unpredictable bleeding initially, transitioning to amenorrhoea (68% at 2 years). Weight gain (especially in BMI >30 or <18 yo).
* **Bone Health**: Small loss of BMD with long-term use. Do DXA if >45yo on long-term DMPA.
* **Return to Fertility**: Delayed up to 1 year post-cessation.

### Subdermal Implant (Etonogestrel / 植入式避孕劑)
* **Efficacy**: >99.9% effective for 3 years. Subdermal insertion in non-dominant upper arm.
* **Bleeding Profile**: Highly variable. 20% frequent/prolonged bleeding, 20% amenorrhoea.
* **Return to Fertility**: Rapid (ovulation resumes within 1 month post-removal).


## 6. Intrauterine Contraception (子宮環)

### Types & Mechanisms of IUD
* **Copper-bearing IUD (Cu-IUD)**: Foreign body sterile inflammation. Copper is toxic to sperm/ovum.
* **Levonorgestrel-releasing IUD (LNG-IUD / Mirena)**: Local LNG release → thickens cervical mucus, causes endometrial atrophy.

### Benefits & Risks of IUD
* **LNG-IUD Benefits**: First-line for heavy menstrual bleeding, adenomyosis, endometriosis. Protects endometrium during HRT or tamoxifen use.
* **Complications**: Uterine perforation (2/1000), expulsion (~5%), insertion-related PID (first 20 days).
* **Pregnancy with IUD**: High risk of miscarriage, preterm labour, septic abortion. *Must remove in 1st trimester if strings are visible*. High risk of ectopic pregnancy.

### Absolute Contraindications of IUD
* Known/suspected pregnancy, unexplained vaginal bleeding.
* Current PID, purulent cervicitis, pelvic TB, active STI.
* Distorted uterine cavity (e.g., submucosal fibroids).
* Current breast cancer (LNG-IUD only).


## 7. Emergency Contraception (EC / 事後避孕)

### Types & Mechanisms of Emergency Contraception
* **Copper IUD**:
	* Most effective (Failure 0.09%).
	* Insert up to 120 hours post-UPSI.
	* Provides ongoing contraception.
* **Ulipristal Acetate (UPA 30mg)**:
	* SPRM.
	* Delays ovulation.
	* Take within 120 hours.
	* *Interaction*: Wait 5 days before starting routine hormonal contraception (UPA competes with progestogens).
* **Levonorgestrel (LNG 1.5mg)**:
	* Inhibits ovulation.
	* Take within 72 hours.
	* Can start regular hormonal contraception immediately.


## 8. Sterilization (結紮手術)

### Female Sterilization
* **Methods**: Laparoscopic (clips/rings) or Mini-laparotomy (modified Pomeroy) postpartum.
* **Failure Rate**: 2-5 per 1000 at 10 years (mostly due to recanalization, fistula, or luteal phase pregnancy). ↑ Risk of ectopic if failure occurs.

### Male Sterilization (Vasectomy)
* **Methods**: Incisional or No-scalpel vasectomy.
* **Failure Rate**: 1 in 2000.
* **Efficacy**:
	* NOT immediate.
	* Must use alternate contraception until 2 consecutive semen analyses confirm azoospermia (~3 months post-op).


## 9. Contraception in Special Medical Conditions

### Contraception in Special Medical Conditions
* **Hypertension / VTE Hx / IHD**: Avoid COCs. POP, Implant, or IUDs are preferred.
* **Epilepsy**: Enzyme-inducing anti-epileptic drugs (phenytoin, carbamazepine) reduce efficacy of COCs and POPs. DMPA or Cu-IUD/LNG-IUD are preferred.
* **SLE / Antiphospholipid Syndrome**: COCs absolutely contraindicated if APL antibodies present. Cu-IUD or progestogen-only methods preferred.
* **Gestational Diabetes / DM**: All methods generally safe unless vascular complications exist (>20 years duration, nephropathy, retinopathy), then avoid COCs.


## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you manage a patient who gets pregnant with an IUD in situ? Answer: Exclude ectopic pregnancy immediately. If the pregnancy is intrauterine and the patient wishes to continue, the IUD should be removed in the first trimester if the strings are visible to prevent septic abortion and prematurity.
* Trap: Assuming Ulipristal Acetate (UPA) is just another form of Plan B and treating follow-up contraception identically. Pearl: UPA is an SPRM. You must wait at least 5 days after taking UPA before initiating routine hormonal contraception, whereas with LNG EC, you can start immediately.
* Pitfall: Prescribing combined oral contraceptives to a 36-year-old woman who smokes 20 cigarettes a day. Pearl: This is a WHO MEC Category 4 (absolute contraindication) due to the unacceptably high risk of myocardial infarction and VTE. Offer progestogen-only methods or an IUD instead.
* Viva Question: What is the most effective form of emergency contraception? Answer: Copper IUD insertion within 120 hours of unprotected sexual intercourse.
* Trap: Believing a family history of breast cancer strictly contraindicates COC use. Pearl: A positive family history or BRCA mutation is WHO Category 1 for COCs. It does not further increase their already high breast cancer risk, but it significantly reduces their risk of ovarian cancer.
