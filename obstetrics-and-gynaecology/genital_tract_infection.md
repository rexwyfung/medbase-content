# Genital Tract Infection (生殖道感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology
### Definition of Genital Tract Infections in Pregnancy
* **Definition**: Pathogenic microbial colonization of the lower or upper genital tract during the gestational period.
* **Context**: Often asymptomatic but can trigger severe obstetric sequelae including chorioamnionitis and preterm birth.
### Local Epidemiology of Group B Streptococcus
* **Prevalence**: 21.8% maternal colonization rate in Hong Kong.
* **Policy**: Universal culture-based screening is implemented across Hospital Authority (HA) public obstetric units.
### Epidemiology of Bacterial Vaginosis
* **Recurrence**: Up to 30% of treated cases relapse within one month.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)
### Vaginal pH & Endocrine Changes
* **Hormonal Shift**: ↑ **Estrogen** and ↑ **Progesterone** stimulate ↑ glycogen deposition in the vaginal epithelium.
* **Flora Metabolism**: Normal lactobacilli break down glycogen into lactic acid, maintaining an acidic pH (3.5-6.0).
* **Pathophysiology**: The acidic environment protects against bacterial infection but directly ↑ susceptibility to **Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (念珠菌陰道炎)**.
### Pathophysiology of Bacterial Vaginosis & STIs
* **Bacterial Vaginosis (BV)**: Overgrowth of anaerobic organisms (**Gardnerella vaginalis**, **Mycoplasma hominis**, **Bacteroides** species) replacing normal lactobacilli flora.
* **Transplacental Transmission**: Pathogens like **Syphilis (梅毒)** and **HIV (愛滋病毒)** cross the placental barrier, leading to direct fetal infection.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)
### Clinical History Findings
* **Candidiasis**: Severe vulval itching (pruritus) and redness.
* **Trichomoniasis (滴蟲)**: Pruritus, redness, and dysuria.
* **Bacterial Vaginosis**: Foul-smelling (fishy) odor, explicitly with an absence of inflammatory symptoms.
### Speculum Examination Findings
* **Candidiasis**: Curdy, thick white discharge with an inflamed vaginal wall.
* **Trichomoniasis**: Frothy, yellowish/greenish discharge.
* **Bacterial Vaginosis**: Thin, white or yellow discharge without surrounding vaginal inflammation.
* **Cervicitis/STI**: Purulent discharge originating directly from the endocervical canal.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging
### Differential Diagnoses of Vaginal Discharge in Pregnancy
| Pathology | Discharge Characteristics | Associated Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physiological** | Thin, white or milky | Nil inflammation |
| **Candidiasis** | Curdy, thick | Vulval redness, itching, inflamed vaginal wall |
| **Bacterial Vaginosis** | Foul-smelling, white/yellow, frothy | Absence of inflammation |
| **Trichomoniasis** | Yellowish/greenish | Itching, redness, dysuria |
| **Labour / Show** | Blood-stained mucus | Uterine contractions, cervical dilatation |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)
### Key Microbiological Swabs
* **High Vaginal Swab (HVS)**: Cultured specifically for Candida and Trichomonas.
* **Endocervical Swab**: Targeted for **Gonorrhoea** and **Chlamydia**.
* **Low Vaginal & Rectal Swab**: Specific screening for **Group B Streptococcus (GBS) (B型鏈球菌)** performed at 35-37 weeks of gestation.
### Diagnostic Microscopy & Bedside Testing
* **Vaginal pH**: > 4.5 confirms an abnormal alkaline shift (suggestive of BV).
* **Gram Stain Microscopy**: Reveals **Clue Cells** (vaginal epithelial cells with fuzzy borders due to adherent small bacteria) indicating BV.
### Maternal Antenatal Blood Screening
* **Routine Serology**: **VDRL/RPR** (Syphilis), **Anti-HIV**, and **HBsAg** (Hepatitis B).

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)
### Pharmacological Management of Vaginal Infections
* **Candidiasis**: Topical antifungals (**Nystatin** or **Clotrimazole** vaginal pessaries/cream).
* **Bacterial Vaginosis**: **Metronidazole** or **Clindamycin** (oral or vaginal route).
* **Trichomoniasis**: Oral **Metronidazole** (Requires caution: potential teratogenicity in the 1st trimester).
### Intrapartum Antibiotic Prophylaxis (IAP) for GBS
* **Indication**: Positive GBS screening swab, previous GBS-infected neonate, or documented GBS bacteriuria during the current pregnancy.
* **Unknown Status Indication**: Ruptured membranes >18 hours, preterm labour, or maternal fever >38°C.
* **First-line**: IV **Penicillin**.
* **Allergy Protocol**: IV **Clindamycin** or **Erythromycin** (if susceptibilities confirmed), OR IV **Vancomycin** (if resistant or unconfirmed).
### Systemic Medical Management for Viral/Spirochete
* **Syphilis**: IM **Penicillin**.
* **HIV**: Antiretroviral therapy (ART) continued strictly through antenatal, intrapartum, and early neonatal periods.
* **Hepatitis B**: **Tenofovir** (FDA Category B antiviral) initiated from 28 weeks if maternal viral load is high.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)
### Mode of Delivery Guidelines
* **Uncomplicated Vaginal Infections**: Proceed with standard spontaneous vaginal delivery; no surgical intervention required.
* **Viral Infections (HIV/HBV)**: Vaginal delivery is safe if the maternal viral load is adequately suppressed; otherwise, elective Lower Segment Caesarean Section (LSCS) may be indicated to prevent vertical transmission.
### Neonatal Preventive Management
* **GBS/STI Exposure**: Immediate paediatric consultation and close monitoring post-delivery.
* **HBV Positive Mother**: Administer **Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin (HBIG)** concurrently with the **HBV Vaccine** to the neonate immediately after birth.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up
### Post-partum Infection Control & Contact Tracing
* **STI Protocol**: Mandatory screening and concurrent treatment of all sexual partner(s) to prevent reinfection.
* **Neonatal Monitoring**: Observe the neonate for signs of early-onset sepsis (e.g., lethargy, poor feeding, temperature instability).
### Contraception Considerations
* **Barrier Methods**: Strictly emphasized prior to resuming sexual intercourse to prevent recurrent STIs and ascending pelvic infections.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications
### Maternal & Obstetric Complications
* **Bacterial Vaginosis**: Strongly associated with ↑ risk of 2nd-trimester miscarriage, preterm birth, and chorioamnionitis.
* **Untreated STIs**: Ascending infection leading to chronic Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) and secondary subfertility.
### Fetal & Neonatal Complications
* **Group B Streptococcus**: Severe early-onset neonatal sepsis, neonatal meningitis, and high neonatal mortality.
* **Syphilis / HIV / HBV**: Unchecked vertical transmission resulting in lifelong chronic infection or severe congenital anomalies.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of curdy vaginal discharge with severe pruritus in pregnancy, and what is its pathophysiology? Answer: Vulvovaginal Candidiasis, secondary to an acidic vaginal pH shift caused by pregnancy-induced increases in estrogen and progesterone.
* Trap: Assuming asymptomatic bacterial vaginosis (BV) does not require intervention in a patient with a history of preterm labour. Pearl: BV is strongly associated with 2nd-trimester miscarriage and preterm birth; it must be aggressively screened and treated in patients with a high-risk obstetric history.
* Pitfall: Prescribing oral metronidazole indiscriminately for Trichomoniasis in early pregnancy. Always evaluate the gestational age carefully, as metronidazole carries potential teratogenic risks during the 1st trimester.
* Viva Question: What are the clinical criteria for initiating intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis for GBS in a patient with an unknown swab status? Answer: Prolonged rupture of membranes >18 hours, active preterm labour, or a maternal fever >38°C.
* Trap: Overlooking alternative antibiotic choices for GBS prophylaxis in penicillin-allergic patients. Pearl: Use IV Clindamycin or Erythromycin only if sensitivities confirm susceptibility; otherwise, default to IV Vancomycin.
