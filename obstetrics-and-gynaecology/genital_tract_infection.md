# Genital Tract Infection (生殖道感染)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Infection Categories
* **Non-sexually transmitted:**
  * Candidiasis
  * Bacterial vaginosis
* **Sexually transmitted infections (STIs):**
  * Chlamydia
  * Gonorrhoea
  * Trichomoniasis
  * Syphilis
  * Herpes simplex virus
  * HIV
* **Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID):** upper genital tract infection

### Local Epidemiology
* Vaginal discharge is one of the commonest gynaecology complaints
* STIs often coexist — screen comprehensively when one is identified

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Vaginal Microenvironment
* Normal pH 3.8-4.5
* **Lactobacilli** maintain acidity
* Oestrogen promotes glycogen-rich epithelium
* Altered pH or flora predisposes to infection

### PID Mechanism
* Ascending infection from cervix to:
  * Endometrium
  * Fallopian tubes
  * Pelvic peritoneum
* Common organisms:
  * **Chlamydia trachomatis**
  * **Neisseria gonorrhoeae**
  * Anaerobes
  * Mycoplasma genitalium

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Vaginal Discharge History
* Colour, odour, consistency
* Itching or burning
* Dysuria
* Dyspareunia
* Sexual history
* Previous STI
* Contraceptive use
* Diabetes or immunosuppression

### Examination Findings
* **Candidiasis:** curdy white discharge, vulval erythema
* **BV:** grey offensive discharge
* **Trichomoniasis:** frothy yellow-green discharge, strawberry cervix
* **PID:**
  * Lower abdominal tenderness
  * Cervical excitation
  * Adnexal tenderness
  * Fever

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Discharge Differential Table
| Diagnosis | Discharge | pH | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Candidiasis** | Curdy white | Normal | Itch, soreness |
| **BV** | Grey, fishy | >4.5 | Clue cells |
| **Trichomoniasis** | Frothy yellow | >4.5 | Strawberry cervix |
| **Chlamydia** | Mucopurulent | Variable | Often asymptomatic |
| **Gonorrhoea** | Purulent | Variable | Cervicitis |

### PID Severity
* **Mild-moderate:** pelvic pain ± discharge
* **Severe:** fever, peritonism, tubo-ovarian abscess
* Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome: perihepatitis with RUQ pain

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Vaginal and Cervical Tests
* High vaginal swab:
  * Candida culture
  * Gardnerella/anaerobes
  * Trichomonas
* Endocervical swab:
  * Gonorrhoea culture
  * Chlamydia NAAT
* Urine NAAT for chlamydia/gonorrhoea
* Wet mount:
  * Clue cells
  * Pseudohyphae
  * Motile trichomonads

### Coinfection and Systemic Screening
* HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B screening
* Pregnancy test
* CBC, CRP, ESR if PID suspected
* Pelvic ultrasound if abscess suspected

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Specific Antimicrobial Therapy
* **Candidiasis:**
  * Clotrimazole 500 mg pessary once
  * Fluconazole 150 mg PO if non-pregnant
* **BV:**
  * Metronidazole 400 mg BD 7 days
  * Clindamycin cream alternative
* **Trichomoniasis:**
  * Metronidazole 2 g PO once
* **Chlamydia:**
  * Azithromycin 1 g PO once or doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days
* **Gonorrhoea:**
  * Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once + chlamydia cover

### PID Antibiotics
* Mild-moderate:
  * Ceftriaxone + doxycycline + metronidazole
* Severe:
  * IV therapy or admission

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Outpatient PID Management
* Oral ceftriaxone, doxycycline, and metronidazole
* Review within 72 hours
* Treat partner
* Avoid intercourse until treated

### Inpatient PID Management
* IV antibiotics
* Analgesia
* Drainage of tubo-ovarian abscess
* Surgery if rupture or failed medical therapy

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Follow-up and Partner Treatment
* Test of cure if persistent symptoms or pregnancy
* Treat sexual partners
* Contact tracing
* Condom advice
* Screen for other STIs

### Contraception
* Avoid IUCD insertion during active PID
* Hormonal contraception can continue if treated

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Chronic pelvic pain
* Ectopic pregnancy
* Infertility
| Tubo-ovarian abscess
* Sepsis
* Perihepatitis

### Pregnancy and Neonatal Complications
* Preterm labour
* PPROM
* Neonatal conjunctivitis
* Vertical transmission of HIV/syphilis/herpes

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Admit if severe PID, tubo-ovarian abscess, sepsis, or pregnancy
* Review within 72 hours of outpatient PID therapy; escalate if no improvement
* Offer test of cure if pregnant, symptoms persist, or reinfection suspected
* Treat partners before resuming intercourse
* Screen for HIV, syphilis, and hepatitis B whenever an STI is confirmed

* Take high vaginal, endocervical, and urine samples before empirical therapy when feasible
* Use ceftriaxone, doxycycline, and metronidazole for clinically diagnosed PID
* Admit if fever >38°C, vomiting, signs of sepsis, or suspected abscess
* Screen for HIV, syphilis, and hepatitis B in every confirmed STI
* Perform contact tracing and partner treatment to prevent reinfection
* Consider pregnancy test in all women with pelvic pain and abnormal discharge

* Do not use oral metronidazole in the first trimester unless clearly necessary
* Consider pelvic tuberculosis in chronic pelvic pain with infertility and adnexal mass
* Reassess diagnosis if symptoms persist after one course of therapy
* Use NAAT for chlamydia and gonorrhoea where available
* Examine and treat the male partner in all confirmed STIs
* Screen for HIV, syphilis, and hepatitis B when PID is diagnosed
* Consider Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome if right upper quadrant pain accompanies PID
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are clue cells? Answer: Vaginal epithelial cells studded with bacteria, indicating bacterial vaginosis.
* Trap: Treating only Candida in abnormal discharge. Pearl: Always consider BV, trichomonas, chlamydia, and gonorrhoea.
* Pitfall: Missing PID in a woman with mild pain. Have a low diagnostic threshold.
* Viva Question: What is the classic PID antibiotic combination? Answer: Ceftriaxone + doxycycline + metronidazole.
* Pitfall: Forgetting partner treatment and contact tracing. Reinfection is common.
