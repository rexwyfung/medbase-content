# Postnatal Mood Problems (產後情緒問題)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Spectrum and Diagnostic Labels
* **Postnatal blues:** transient mood disturbance in the first week postpartum
* **Postnatal depression:** major depressive episode within the first 6 months postpartum
* **Postnatal psychosis:** rare psychiatric emergency with hallucinations, delusions, or disorganised behaviour
* Most cases occur within the first 4 weeks to 6 months

### Local Epidemiology
* **Postnatal blues:** ~50%
* **Postnatal depression:** ~10%
* **Postnatal psychosis:** ~0.1-0.2%
* Increased risk with:
  * Previous psychiatric illness
  * Family history
  * Poor support
  * Relationship conflict
  * Obstetric complications

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Hormonal Withdrawal and Neurobiology
* Rapid fall in:
  * Oestrogen
  * Progesterone
  * Oxytocin
  * Thyroid hormones
* Altered:
  * Serotonergic pathways
  * HPA axis
  * Sleep-wake cycle

### Risk Factors
* Previous depression or psychosis
* Bipolar disorder
* Previous postnatal depression
* Severe premenstrual syndrome
* Sleep deprivation
* Poor social support
* Recent stressful life events

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Key History Features
* Low mood or tearfulness
* Anhedonia
* Sleep disturbance beyond baby care
* Fatigue
* Poor concentration
* Feelings of guilt or worthlessness
* Difficulty bonding with baby
* Suicidal ideation
* Thoughts of harming baby
* Psychotic symptoms

### Examination Findings
* General appearance and self-care
* Psychomotor retardation or agitation
* Eye contact and affect
* Breast and wound examination if relevant
* Assess mother-baby interaction

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
| Diagnosis | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Postnatal blues** | Transient, mild, resolves <2 weeks |
| **Postnatal depression** | Persistent low mood, functional impairment |
| **Postnatal psychosis** | Delusions, hallucinations, disorganisation |
| **Thyroid dysfunction** | Fatigue, weight change, tachycardia |
| **Anaemia** | Fatigue, pallor |
| **Sheehan’s syndrome** | Failure to lactate, amenorrhoea, hypopituitarism |

### Risk Stratification
* **Low risk:** mild symptoms, no suicidal or infanticidal thoughts
* **Moderate risk:** functional impairment, poor bonding
* **High risk:** suicidal ideation, thoughts of harming baby, psychotic features

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Screening Tools
* **Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS)**
* Chinese version validated in Hong Kong
* Score ≥11 suggests possible depression
* Ask directly about item 10: self-harm

### Baseline Tests
* CBC for anaemia
* TSH and free T4
* Renal and liver function if medication planned
* Vitamin B12 and folate if fatigue or malnutrition

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Postnatal Blues
* Reassurance
* Practical support
* Sleep optimisation
* Review after 1-2 weeks

### Postnatal Depression
* Psychological therapy:
  * CBT
  * Interpersonal therapy
* SSRIs:
  * **Sertraline** preferred if breastfeeding
  * Fluoxetine if not breastfeeding
* Monitor response and side effects

### Postnatal Psychosis
* Psychiatric emergency
* Admission if risk to mother or baby
* Consider:
  * Atypical antipsychotic
  * Mood stabiliser
  * Benzodiazepine if agitation
* Avoid lithium if breastfeeding, or monitor carefully

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Management by Severity
* **Mild:** watchful waiting, support, follow-up
* **Moderate:** psychological therapy ± SSRI
* **Severe:** SSRI plus psychiatric referral
* **Psychosis:** urgent psychiatric admission

### Mother and Baby Unit
* Prefer admission with baby if:
  * Mother not actively suicidal
  * Infant safety can be supervised
* Promotes bonding
* Requires 24-hour observation

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Ongoing Care
* Regular psychiatric follow-up
* Monitor medication adherence
* Sleep and social support
* Couple and family counselling
* Assess bonding and infant development

### Contraception and Future Pregnancy
* Discuss contraceptive preferences
* Plan future pregnancy with psychiatric review
* Prophylactic treatment if previous postnatal psychosis

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Suicide
* Self-harm
* Relapse of bipolar disorder
* Chronic depression
* Substance misuse
* Marital breakdown

### Infant and Family Complications
* Impaired bonding
* Poor emotional development
* Neglect
* Infanticide risk
* Domestic stress

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Use EPDS ≥11 in Chinese women as a positive screen
* Ask about thoughts of self-harm and harm to infant at every review
* Admit urgently if psychotic features or active suicidal ideation
* Prefer sertraline if breastfeeding and medication is needed
* Offer prepregnancy psychiatric planning after postnatal psychosis

* Use EPDS ≥11 as the positive cut-off in Chinese women
* Ask specifically about thoughts of self-harm and harming the baby
* Admit urgently if psychosis or active suicidal ideation
* Use sertraline as preferred SSRI if breastfeeding
* Involve psychiatric team and medical social worker early
* Offer close follow-up in next pregnancy after previous postnatal psychosis

* Distinguish blues from depression by duration, severity, and functional impact
* Check thyroid function and anaemia before attributing fatigue to depression
* Ask about bonding, feeding, and maternal safety at every contact
* Involve family if risk to mother or infant is present
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What EPDS cut-off is commonly used in Chinese women? Answer: 11.
* Trap: Treating postnatal blues as depression. Pearl: Blues usually resolve within 2 weeks without pharmacotherapy.
* Pitfall: Failing to ask about thoughts of harming the baby. This is essential in every postnatal mood assessment.
* Viva Question: Which SSRI is preferred if breastfeeding? Answer: Sertraline.
* Pitfall: Missing postnatal psychosis as a medical emergency. Urgent psychiatric referral and admission are required.
