# Labour and Delivery (分娩)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Labour
* **Labour (臨產/作動):** Process of birth driven by regular **uterine contractions** → progressive effacement and dilatation of cervix + descent of fetus through pelvis.

### The Three "P's" of Labour
* **Power:** Regular uterine contractions (3-4 per 10 mins) → retraction of muscle fibers, thinning of lower segment, thickening of fundal myometrium.
* **Passage:** Birth canal (vagina, bony pelvis, pelvic floor muscles, perineum).
* **Passenger:** The fetus + position of its presenting part.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Endocrine Initiation of Labour
* **Fetal Axis:** ↑ fetal production of **cortisol**.
* **Placental Shift:** ↓ progesterone production, ↑ oestrone and oestradiol production.
* **Prostaglandins:** ↑ production by placenta and myometrium.
* **Pituitary:** Release of **Oxytocin (催產素)** from posterior lobe.

### Normal Mechanisms of Labour (Vertex Presentation)
* **Descent:** Fetal head enters pelvis.
* **Flexion:** Presents smaller diameter to pelvis.
* **Internal Rotation:** Occiput meets pelvic floor resistance → rotates anteriorly towards pubic symphysis (or occasionally posterior to sacrum).
* **Extension:** Base of occiput contacts inferior rami of pubis → **crowning** at perineum.
* **Restitution:** Head rotates back to original position aligning with shoulders.
* **External Rotation:** Shoulders reach pelvic floor → rotate into anteroposterior diameter.
* **Lateral Flexion:** Anterior shoulder delivers under pubis → posterior shoulder disengages.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History & Symptoms
* **Contractions:** Regular, painful tightening of uterus.
* **Show:** Passage of blood-stained mucus plug.
* **Leaking Liquor:** Suggestive of rupture of membranes.

### Abdominal Examination
* **Palpation:** Assess fetal lie, presentation (e.g., vertex, breech), and engagement (station relative to pelvic brim).
* **Uterus:** Palpate contraction strength, frequency, and resting tone.

### Vaginal Examination (Bimanual & Speculum)
* **Cervix:** Assess effacement (thinning) and dilatation (cm).
* **Membranes:** Intact vs ruptured (pooling of liquor in posterior fornix on speculum).
* **Presenting Part:** Identify fetal part (e.g., cephalic, breech), position (e.g., Left Occipito-Anterior [LOA]), and station (relationship to ischial spines, e.g., S-1 = 1cm above spines).
* **Pelvis:** Assess clinical adequacy (Cephalopelvic disproportion).

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging 

### Stages of Labour Classification
| Stage            | Definition & Boundaries                            | Sub-Phases                                                                                                                 |
| :--------------- | :------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **First Stage**  | Onset of labour to full (10cm) cervical dilatation | - **Latent:** Regular contractions + effacement/dilatation <3cm. <br>- **Active:** Full effacement + $\ge$ 3cm dilatation. |
| **Second Stage** | Full cervical dilatation to delivery of fetus      | - Nulliparous: Prolonged if active pushing >2 hrs. <br>- Multiparous: Prolonged if active pushing >1 hr.                   |
| **Third Stage**  | Delivery of fetus to delivery of placenta          | Prolonged if >30 mins without placental separation.                                                                        |

### Dystocia Differentials
| Category      | Clinical Entities                                                                                                             |
| :------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Power**     | Uterine inertia (weak/incoordinate contractions)                                                                              |
| **Passenger** | - Macrosomia<br>- Malpresentation (Breech, Face, Brow, Transverse)<br>- Malposition (Occipito-posterior, Occipito-transverse) |
| **Passage**   | Cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD)                                                                                             |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Fetal Heart Rate Monitoring Protocols
* **Intermittent Auscultation:**
	* Every 30 mins in active 1st stage.
	* Every 5 mins in 2nd stage (immediately after contractions to detect late decelerations).
* **Continuous Fetal Heart Monitoring (CFHM / CTG):** Indicated for:
	* **Meconium-stained liquor (胎糞)**
	* **Abnormal HR on auscultation**
	* **High-risk:**
		* Induction of labour.
		* Fetal growth restriction (FGR).
		* Previous uterine scar.
		* Preterm.
		* Twins.
		* Maternal DM/Hypertension.

### Invasive Fetal Assessment
* **Fetal Scalp Blood Sampling:** Indicated for abnormal CTG when delivery is not imminent to assess fetal pH/lactate.

### Progress Monitoring
* **Partogram:** Graphical record of cervical dilatation and fetal head descent against time to detect abnormal progress early.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Analgesia & Pain Relief
* **Inhalational:**
	* **Entonox** (50% $N_2O$ / 50% $O_2$).
	* Safe, but causes drowsiness.
* **Intramuscular:**
	* **Pethidine**.
	* Strong narcotic; risks nausea and newborn respiratory depression (antidote may be required).
* **Regional:**
	* **Epidural Analgesia** (L3-L4 space).
	* Highly effective; risks hypotension, motor weakness, urinary retention, maternal fever.

### Labour Augmentation & Medical Induction
* **Oxytocin (催產素):** IV infusion for uterine inertia/dystocia or medical induction (e.g., PROM).
* **Prostaglandin E2:** Used for cervical priming if Bishop score <7.
* **Acid Aspiration Prophylaxis:** **Famotidine** and oral **Sodium Citrate** given if high risk for emergency Caesarean.

### Tocolysis (Acute Preterm Labour <34 weeks)
* **Steroids:** **Betamethasone/Dexamethasone** IM to accelerate fetal lung maturity.
* **Tocolytics:** **Nifedipine** (oral), **Atosiban** (IV oxytocin inhibitor), or **Terbutaline** (IV $\beta$-agonist) for 48 hrs to allow steroids to work.
* **Neuroprotection:** IV **Magnesium Sulphate** (24+0 to 29+6 weeks) to ↓ cerebral palsy risk.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Normal Delivery & Episiotomy
* **Delivery:** Immediate uninterrupted skin-to-skin contact for $\ge$ 1 hour to establish breastfeeding.
* **Restrictive Episiotomy (會陰側切):** Mediolateral cut *only* if severe tear likely or instrumental delivery required.
![image|200](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/d4bb5fe408e519273161eeadfa4627cb.png)

### Assisted / Instrumental Vaginal Delivery
* **Prerequisites:**
	* Fully dilated cervix.
	* CPD excluded.
	* Engaged head.
* **Ventouse (胎頭吸引):**
	* Vacuum extraction.
	* Risks: Cephalohaematoma, subaponeurotic haemorrhage.
* **Forceps (產鉗助產):**
	* Risks: Maternal genital tract injury, fetal facial nerve palsy.
![image|300](https://pub-f0606dedf6d94e4bb5b1999cbd065914.r2.dev/e0fa05402c88b73979180dabf6ea1182.png)

### Active Management of Third Stage
* **Step 1:** Uterotonic (**Syntocinon** or **Syntometrine**) within 1 minute of fetal delivery.
* **Step 2:** Delayed cord clamping ($\ge$ 1 minute) to ↓ newborn anaemia.
* **Step 3:** Controlled cord traction once signs of separation appear.

### Caesarean Section (剖宮產)
* **Lower Segment (LSCS):** 
	* Transverse incision.
	* Standard of care for CPD, malpresentation (transverse/breech), fetal distress, placenta praevia.
* **Classical:**
	* Vertical upper segment incision.
	* Reserved for extreme preterm without lower segment or specific morbidly adherent placentas.
	* Future vaginal birth contraindicated.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Immediate Postpartum Care
* **Monitoring:**
	* Vital signs.
	* Uterine tone.
	* Lochia (vaginal discharge).
	* Voiding (ensure passage of urine within 6-8 hrs).
* **Lactation:** Encourage early breastfeeding.

### Puerperium (產褥期) Changes
* **Uterine Involution:**
	* Fundus descends 1 finger-breadth/day.
	* Not palpable abdominally by 2 weeks; normal size by 6 weeks.
* **Lochia (惡露):** Red (0-5 days) → Brown (5-10 days) → Light brown/yellow (after 10 days).
* **Menses Return:**
	* Earliest 3-5 weeks (non-breastfeeding).
	* Breastfeeding delays ovulation/menses.
	* Contraception needed by 3 weeks post-partum.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Third Stage & Postpartum Complications
* **Retained Placenta:**
	* No separation >30 mins.
	* Requires manual removal under anaesthesia.
* **Primary Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH - 產後出血):**
	* Blood loss $\ge$ 500ml within 24 hrs. 
	* *70% due to Uterine Atony (子宫收缩乏力).* 
	* *Management:* Resuscitation, Bimanual compression, IV Syntocinon, IM Carboprost, PR Misoprostol, Tranexamic Acid.
* **Secondary PPH:**
	* Excessive bleeding 24 hrs to 6 weeks postpartum.
	* Usually due to endometritis or retained products.
* **Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injuries (OASI):**
	* 3rd degree (involves sphincter) or 4th degree (involves anal epithelium).
	* Requires OT repair; ↑ risk of faecal incontinence.

### Intrapartum Emergencies
* **Shoulder Dystocia (肩難產):**
	* Failure of shoulder delivery after head.
	* Requires McRoberts manoeuvre (flexion/abduction of hips) + suprapubic pressure.
	* Risks: Brachial plexus injury, fractures, asphyxia.
* **Cord Prolapse (臍帶脫垂):**
	* Cord descends past presenting part with ruptured membranes.
	* Requires immediate Caesarean.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are the three classic signs of placental separation during the third stage of labour? Answer: Lengthening and descent of the umbilical cord, rising of the uterine fundus (becomes harder/globular), and a gush of fresh blood.
* Trap: Assuming heavy red bleeding 14 days postpartum is a normal return of menses. Pearl: Normal lochia should be brown or light yellow by day 10; persistent heavy red bleeding beyond day 5 or a sudden increase indicates Secondary Postpartum Haemorrhage (often due to retained products or endometritis).
* Pitfall: Rushing to perform artificial rupture of membranes (amniotomy) without checking the fetal station. Pearl: If the presenting part is high and unengaged, amniotomy severely increases the risk of umbilical cord prolapse.
* Viva Question: What are the prerequisites for performing an instrumental (ventouse or forceps) delivery? Answer: The cervix must be fully dilated, the fetal head must be engaged, and cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD) must be clinically excluded.
* Trap: Continuing oxytocin infusion despite a hyperstimulated uterus (>5 contractions/10 min) or prolonged fetal decelerations. Pearl: Immediately stop the oxytocin and consider administering a subcutaneous tocolytic like Terbutaline to relax the myometrium.
