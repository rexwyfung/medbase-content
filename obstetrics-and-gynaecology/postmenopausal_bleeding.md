# Postmenopausal Bleeding (絕經後出血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology
### Clinical Definition of Postmenopausal Bleeding
* **Postmenopausal Bleeding (PMB)**: Vaginal bleeding occurring after menopause (cessation of menses for ≥12 months).

### Epidemiology & Common Causes
* **Atrophic Vaginitis (萎縮性陰道炎)**: Commonest cause of PMB.
* **Malignancy Risk**: Crucial to strictly exclude **Endometrial Cancer (子宮內膜癌)** in all presentations.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)
### Pathophysiology of Atrophic Vaginitis
* ↓ Estrogen postmenopause → thinning of vaginal epithelium → fragile tissue susceptible to microtrauma & bleeding.
* Loss of rugae & ↓ vaginal secretions → vaginal dryness & irritation.

### Pathophysiology of Endometrial Hyperplasia & Malignancy
* **Endometrial Hyperplasia (子宮內膜增生)** / Cancer: Driven by unopposed exogenous/endogenous estrogen.
* **Estrogen-Secreting Ovarian Cancer**: e.g., **Granulosa Cell Tumour (顆粒細胞瘤)** → ↑ endogenous estrogen → endometrial proliferation → PMB.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)
### Maternal History for Postmenopausal Bleeding
* **Bleeding Pattern**: Frequency, duration, amount.
* **Precipitating Factors**: Assess for postcoital bleeding (性交後出血).
* **Constitutional Symptoms**: Weight loss, lethargy (warning signs for malignancy).
* **Alternative Sources**: Must exclude gross haematuria (血尿) or rectal bleeding.

### Past Medical & Drug History
* **Risk Factors for Endometrial Cancer**: HTN, DM, **Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) (多囊卵巢綜合症)**, chronic anovulation.
* **Family History**: Endometrial cancer, **Hereditary Nonpolyposis Colorectal Cancer (HNPCC)**.
* **Medications**: Exogenous estrogen/HRT, herbs, anti-coagulants, **Tamoxifen (他莫昔芬)**.

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* **General Assessment**: Pallor (貧血), palpable cervical/groin lymph nodes.
* **Abdomen**: Palpable mass, presence of ascites (腹水) → suspect advanced/ovarian malignancy.

### Speculum & Bimanual Examination Findings
* **Vaginal Epithelium**: Pale, smooth, shiny, petechiae, loss of rugae (suggests atrophy).
* **Cervix**: Assess for **Cervical Polyp (子宮頸息肉)** or visible cervical cancer.
* **Uterus/Adnexa**: Assess uterine size, mobility, presence of adnexal mass.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging
### Differential Diagnoses of Postmenopausal Bleeding
| Anatomical Source | Specific Conditions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Vaginal** | Atrophic vaginitis, Vaginal cancer |
| **Cervical** | Cervical polyp, Cervical cancer |
| **Endometrial** | Endometrial polyp, Endometrial hyperplasia, Endometrial cancer |
| **Ovarian** | Estrogen-secreting ovarian cancer (Granulosa cell tumour) |
| **Iatrogenic/Systemic** | Intake of exogenous estrogen, Anti-coagulants |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)
### Laboratory Investigations
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Assess for anaemia.
* **Cervical Smear (子宮頸塗片)**: Rule out cervical dysplasia/malignancy.

### Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) Criteria
* **Endometrial Thickness (ET)**: Measured as max anterior-posterior distance on longitudinal axis.
* **ET ≤4 mm**: High -ve predictive value (99.4-100%); endometrial carcinoma highly unlikely.
* **ET >4 mm**: Abnormal, mandates further endometrial sampling.
* **Inadequate View**: Endometrial thickness obscured/cannot be assessed → mandates further investigation.

### Endometrial Sampling & Hysteroscopy
* **Endometrial Aspiration (Pipelle)**: Outpatient tissue sampling. Requires prior pelvic exam to determine uterine size/direction.
* **Hysteroscopy (宮腔鏡檢查)**: Gold standard. Allows direct visualization + targeted biopsy. Safe, well-tolerated without general anaesthesia.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)
### Medical Management of Atrophic Vaginitis
* **Topical Estrogen (局部雌激素)**: Local application of estrogen cream effectively treats thin, friable epithelium and relieves symptoms.

### Management of Acute Bleeding & Anaemia
* **Haemodynamic Stabilization**: Large bore IV access, fluid resuscitation, blood transfusion if profound anaemia/shock present.
* **Iron Supplementation**: Oral/IV iron to correct chronic anaemia secondary to recurrent PMB.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)
### Indications for Hysteroscopy Algorithm
* **Persistent PMB** despite normal endometrial aspirate.
* **Abnormal Endometrial Aspirate**: e.g., atypia, hyperplasia, malignancy.
* **Abnormal TVUS**: ET >4 mm, obscured ET, or suspected focal lesion (polyp/submucosal fibroid).

### Definitive Surgical Management for Focal Lesions
* **Therapeutic Hysteroscopy**: Targeted resection of **Endometrial Polyp (子宮內膜息肉)** or submucosal fibroids.
* **Hysterectomy (子宮切除術)**: Definitive tx for endometrial cancer. Route: Vaginal, laparoscopic, or abdominal depending on staging.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up
### Post-procedural Care for Hysteroscopy/Sampling
* **Observation**: Monitor for signs of pelvic infection (fever, purulent discharge) or heavy bleeding.
* **Analgesia**: Simple NSAIDs for mild post-procedural cramping.

### Follow-up for High-Risk Endometrial Lesions
* **Benign Atrophy**: Reassure patient, continue topical estrogen if symptomatic, advise urgent return if bleeding recurs.
* **Hyperplasia/Malignancy**: Fast-track referral to gynae-oncology for definitive staging, surgery, and surveillance.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications
### Complications of Untreated Endometrial Pathology
* **Missed Malignancy**: Delay in diagnosing endometrial/cervical cancer → disease progression, poorer overall prognosis.
* **Chronic Anaemia**: Persistent slow bleeding leading to symptomatic anaemia (fatigue, dyspnea).

### Procedural Complications
* **Uterine Perforation (子宮穿孔)**: Higher risk during sampling/hysteroscopy in postmenopausal atrophic uteri.
* **Pelvic Infection (盆腔感染)**: Ascending infection post-instrumentation.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of postmenopausal bleeding, and what critical condition must always be excluded? Answer: Atrophic vaginitis is the most common cause, but endometrial cancer must strictly be excluded.
* Viva Question: What is the cut-off for endometrial thickness on transvaginal ultrasound to rule out endometrial carcinoma? Answer: An endometrial thickness of ≤4 mm has a 99.4-100% negative predictive value for endometrial cancer.
* Trap: Assuming PMB is due to atrophic vaginitis purely based on physical exam findings without performing investigations. Pearl: Always perform a TVUS or endometrial sampling to objectively rule out endometrial pathology before attributing bleeding solely to atrophy.
* Pitfall: Relying only on a blind endometrial aspirate in a patient with a suspected focal lesion on ultrasound. Proceed directly to hysteroscopy for direct visualization and targeted biopsy to avoid missing small focal malignancies or polyps.
