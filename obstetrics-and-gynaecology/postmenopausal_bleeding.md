# Postmenopausal Bleeding (停經後出血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition and Clinical Significance
* **Postmenopausal bleeding:** any vaginal bleeding ≥12 months after the last menstrual period
* Commonest cause: **atrophic vaginitis**
* Most important cause to exclude: **endometrial cancer**
* Requires urgent investigation if:
  * Heavy bleeding
  * Persistent bleeding
  * Endometrial thickness >4 mm
  * Failed initial sampling

### Local Epidemiology
* Endometrial cancer is the **4th commonest female cancer in Hong Kong**
* Most women present with early-stage disease due to postmenopausal bleeding
* Median age ~57 years

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Atrophic Vaginitis Mechanism
* Postmenopausal oestrogen deficiency → thin vaginal epithelium
* Fragile mucosa → spontaneous or contact bleeding
* Loss of rugae and lubrication

### Endometrial Cancer Mechanism
* Unopposed oestrogen → endometrial hyperplasia → carcinoma
* Risk factors:
  * Obesity
  * Nulliparity
  * PCOS
  * Tamoxifen
  * Diabetes
  * Hypertension
  * Lynch syndrome
  * Oestrogen-secreting ovarian tumour

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### History
* Time since menopause
* Bleeding pattern
* Postcoital bleeding
* Hormone or herbal supplement use
* Tamoxifen therapy
* Weight change or bloating
* Family history of colorectal/endometrial cancer
* Systemic symptoms

### Examination
* Pallor
* Abdominal mass
* Ascites
* Atrophic vagina:
  * Pale
  * Smooth
  * Shiny
  * Loss of rugae
  * Petechiae
* Cervical lesion or polyp
* Enlarged uterus
* Adnexal mass

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
| Cause | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Atrophic vaginitis** | Commonest benign cause |
| **Endometrial polyp** | Focal lesion on scan |
| **Endometrial hyperplasia** | Thickened endometrium |
| **Endometrial cancer** | Must exclude |
| **Cervical cancer** | Contact bleeding or abnormal cervix |
| **Oestrogen-secreting tumour** | Endometrial stimulation |
| **Tamoxifen effect** | Polyp/hyperplasia risk |
| **Vaginal trauma or foreign body** | Local cause |

### Endometrial Cancer Risk Stratification
* **High risk:**
  * Age >60
  * Diabetes/obesity
  * Nulliparity
  * Lynch syndrome
  * Family history
* **Low risk:**
  * Endometrium ≤4 mm
  * Atrophic changes only
  * No focal lesion

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Initial Investigations
* Transvaginal ultrasound
* Endometrial sampling
* Cervical smear if due or cervix abnormal
* CBC if bleeding
* Renal and liver function if surgery planned

### Ultrasound Criteria
* Measure endometrial thickness on sagittal view
* **≤4 mm:** endometrial cancer unlikely
* **>4 mm:** needs sampling/hysteroscopy
* Focal lesions require hysteroscopy
* Look for ovarian mass if oestrogen-secreting tumour suspected

### Hysteroscopy Indications
* Failed outpatient sampling
* Thickened endometrium >4 mm
* Focal lesion on scan
* Persistent bleeding despite normal aspirate
* Tamoxifen users with abnormal bleeding

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Atrophic Vaginitis
* Topical oestrogen cream or pessary
* Vaginal moisturisers
* Avoid irritants

### Endometrial Hyperplasia
* **Without atypia:** progestogen or LNG-IUS
* **With atypia:** hysterectomy preferred due to cancer risk
* If fertility desired: LNG-IUS + surveillance

### Heavy Bleeding
* Tranexamic acid
* Progestogen
* Transfusion if severe anaemia

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Hysteroscopy and Biopsy
* Gold standard for focal intrauterine lesion
* Allows:
  * Direct visualisation
  * Targeted biopsy
  * Polypectomy
  * Myomectomy if submucosal fibroid

### Endometrial Cancer Surgery
* Total hysterectomy
* Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
* Lymphadenectomy if high-risk features
* Adjuvant radiotherapy/chemotherapy depending on stage

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After Hysteroscopy
* Observe for bleeding and infection
* Avoid sexual intercourse for 1-2 weeks
* Histology follow-up
* Reassess if bleeding recurs

### After Endometrial Cancer Treatment
* Surveillance clinic
* Vault cytology if indicated
* Monitor for recurrence
* Hormone replacement therapy only after oncology advice

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Anaemia
* Endometrial cancer
* Surgical complications
* Lymphoedema after lymphadenectomy
* Radiation complications

### Gynaecological Complications
* Endometrial hyperplasia recurrence
* Uterine perforation during biopsy/hysteroscopy
| Asherman’s syndrome after curettage
* Chronic pelvic pain

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Investigate every episode of postmenopausal bleeding
* Endometrial cancer is unlikely if thickness ≤4 mm and no focal lesion
* Proceed to hysteroscopy if sample inadequate, bleeding persists, or lesion seen
* Treat atypical hyperplasia with hysterectomy if fertility complete
* Screen for Lynch syndrome if endometrial cancer occurs <50 years or family history is suggestive

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What endometrial thickness cut-off is used to exclude endometrial cancer? Answer: ≤4 mm on transvaginal ultrasound.
* Trap: Assuming all postmenopausal bleeding is atrophic vaginitis. Pearl: Endometrial cancer must be excluded.
* Pitfall: Accepting an insufficient endometrial sample. Arrange hysteroscopy if sampling is inadequate.
* Viva Question: What is the gold standard for diagnosing focal endometrial lesions? Answer: Hysteroscopy with directed biopsy.
* Pitfall: Forgetting Lynch syndrome in women with endometrial and colorectal cancer family history.
