# Adenomyosis (子宮腺肌病)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Adenomyosis
* **Adenomyosis** (子宮腺肌病) is a benign structural gynaecological condition characterised by the presence of ectopic endometrial glands and stroma deep within the **myometrium** (子宮肌層).
* Induces surrounding smooth muscle hypertrophy and hyperplasia → globally enlarged uterus.

### Local Epidemiology & Demographic Profile
* Typically affects multiparous women aged 35–50 years.
* Often coexists with **Leiomyoma** (子宮肌瘤) and **Endometriosis** (子宮內膜異位症).
* Prevalence is rising in younger women due to delayed childbearing and improved imaging.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Pathophysiology of Myometrial Invasion
* Downward invagination of the stratum basalis of the **endometrium** (子宮內膜) into the myometrium.
* Associated with tissue injury and repair (TIAR) mechanisms → often triggered by prior uterine trauma (e.g., Caesarean section, curettage, multiparity).
* Ectopic tissue lacks a submucosal layer → direct contact with myometrial fibres.

### Endocrine Axis & Hormonal Dependence
* Estrogen-dependent condition → ↑ local aromatase expression in adenomyotic tissue → locally synthesised estrogen promotes ongoing tissue growth.
* Ectopic glands are primarily composed of basal endometrium → do NOT typically undergo cyclical bleeding like endometriosis, but trigger severe inflammatory response and prostaglandins release → ↑ dysmenorrhoea.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Patient History in Adenomyosis
* Severe secondary **Dysmenorrhoea** (經痛) → classically progressive and cramping.
* **Heavy Menstrual Bleeding** (經血過多) → prolonged flow often with clots.
* Chronic pelvic pain and pressure symptoms (urinary frequency, bowel dysfunction).
* Deep **Dyspareunia** (性交疼痛).

### Abdominal & Bimanual Examination Findings
* Abdominal exam: Lower abdominal mass may be palpable if uterus is significantly enlarged.
* Bimanual exam: **Symmetrically enlarged, globular, boggy (soft) uterus** (球狀子宮).
* Uterine size usually corresponds to <12 weeks gestation (unlike massive fibroids).
* Exquisite uterine tenderness during premenstrual or menstrual phase.
* Speculum exam: Usually unremarkable; cervix appears normal unless coexisting pathology.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging (e.g., FIGO if applicable)

### FIGO PALM-COEIN Classification
* Classified under the **"A"** in the **PALM** (Structural) category for Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB-A).

### Key Differential Diagnoses of Bulky Uterus
| Feature | Adenomyosis (子宮腺肌病) | Leiomyoma (子宮肌瘤) | Endometriosis (子宮內膜異位症) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uterine Contour** | Symmetrical, globular, boggy | Irregular, firm, nodular | Normal or fixed/retroverted |
| **Pain** | Severe progressive dysmenorrhoea | Often painless unless degenerating | Deep dyspareunia, chronic pelvic pain |
| **Menorrhagia** | Very common | Very common (submucosal) | Less common |
| **Mobility** | Mobile | Mobile | Often fixed (adhesions/frozen pelvis) |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVS) Diagnostic Criteria
* Asymmetrical thickening of the myometrium (usually posterior wall > anterior wall).
* Myometrial cysts (anechoic lacunae representing cystic glands).
* Linear echogenic striations radiating from the endometrium.
* Loss of a clear endomyometrial junction (indistinct borders).
* Diffuse vascularity on Doppler (unlike peripheral flow in fibroids).

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) Criteria
* MRI Pelvis is the gold standard for equivocal cases.
* **Junctional Zone** thickening >12 mm is highly diagnostic.
* High signal intensity myometrial spots on T1/T2-weighted images (haemorrhage).

### Laboratory Investigations
* **CBP** (Complete Blood Picture) → Check for iron-deficiency anaemia (↓ Hb, ↓ MCV).
* **Pregnancy test** → Mandatory to exclude pregnancy in women of reproductive age with AUB.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Non-Hormonal Pharmacological Management
* **NSAIDs** (e.g., Mefenamic acid) → ↓ prostaglandins → 1st-line for dysmenorrhoea.
* **Tranexamic acid** (傳明酸) → Antifibrinolytic → ↓ HMB acutely during menses.

### Long-Term Hormonal Suppression Therapies
* **Levonorgestrel-Releasing Intrauterine System (LNG-IUS)** (曼月樂/Mirena) → 1st-line medical therapy in HK HA protocols → continuous local progestin causes endometrial atrophy & ↓ uterine size.
* **Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs)** → continuous or cyclical use to suppress axis.
* **Oral Progestogens** (e.g., Norethisterone, Dienogest) → induces decidualisation and atrophy.
* **GnRH Agonists** (e.g., Goserelin/Zoladex) → induces temporary menopausal state → used for 3–6 months pre-operatively to shrink uterus and correct anaemia.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Definitive Gynaecological Surgery
* **Total Hysterectomy** (全子宮切除術) → The *only* definitive cure.
* Indicated for women who have completed childbearing and failed medical management.
* Can be performed via Laparoscopic, Vaginal, or Abdominal route depending on uterine size.

### Uterine-Sparing Surgical Options (For women desiring fertility)
* **Adenomyomectomy** → Excision of localized adenomyoma. Technically difficult due to poorly defined margins; high risk of uterine rupture in future pregnancies.
* **Endometrial Ablation** → Destroys endometrium. High failure rate in adenomyosis because glands penetrate deep into myometrium beyond ablation depth.
* **Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)** → Interventional radiology option; ↓ blood supply causes necrosis, but impacts future ovarian reserve and placentation.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-Operative Care (Hysterectomy)
* Standard ERAS (Enhanced Recovery After Surgery) protocols.
* Venous thromboembolism (VTE) prophylaxis, early mobilization, catheter removal by post-op Day 1.

### Contraception & Longitudinal Follow-up
* If treated medically with LNG-IUS, device must be replaced every 5 years for ongoing symptom control and contraception.
* Regular CBP monitoring to ensure resolution of anaemia.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Gynaecological & Systemic Complications
* Severe iron-deficiency anaemia → fatigue, palpitations, cardiac compromise.
* Chronic pelvic pain → severe negative impact on mental health and quality of life.

### Obstetric Complications (If co-existing with pregnancy)
* ↑ risk of spontaneous miscarriage.
* ↑ risk of preterm labour (PTL) and preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM).
* ↑ risk of abnormal placentation and post-partum haemorrhage (PPH).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How does the TVS appearance of adenomyosis differ from a leiomyoma? Answer: Adenomyosis presents with asymmetrical thickening, ill-defined borders, and central vascularity, whereas a leiomyoma is a well-circumscribed mass with peripheral vascular flow.
* Trap: Assuming endometrial ablation is a good definitive treatment for heavy bleeding caused by adenomyosis. Pearl: Ablation often fails and can worsen pain (causing Post-Ablation Tubal Sterilization Syndrome) because adenomyotic glands reside deep in the myometrium beyond the reach of surface ablation.
* Pitfall: Recommending adenomyomectomy as a standard fertility-sparing option without extensive counselling. Unlike fibroids, adenomyosis lacks a pseudo-capsule, making complete excision nearly impossible and drastically increasing the risk of uterine rupture in subsequent pregnancies.
