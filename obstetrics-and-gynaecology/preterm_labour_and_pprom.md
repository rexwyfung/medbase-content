# Preterm Labour and PPROM (早產與未足月胎膜早破)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definitions
* **Preterm labour:** regular contractions with cervical change before **37+0 weeks**
* **Preterm prelabour rupture of membranes (PPROM):** membrane rupture before labour onset and before 37 weeks
* **PROM:** membrane rupture before labour at term

### Epidemiology and Clinical Importance
* Preterm birth is the leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality
* Risk increases with:
  * Previous preterm birth
  * Multiple pregnancy
  * Cervical shortening
  * Infection
  * Bleeding
  * Uterine overdistension

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Pathways to Preterm Labour
* **Infection/inflammation:** chorioamnionitis, BV, UTI
* **Uterine overdistension:** multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios
* **Cervical insufficiency:** short or dilated cervix
* **Placental pathology:** abruption, ischaemia
* **Maternal stress:** cortisol and inflammatory mediators

### PPROM Mechanism
* Membrane weakening due to:
  * Infection
  * Inflammation
  * Collagen degradation
* Loss of amniotic fluid increases:
  * Cord compression
  * Infection risk
  * Pulmonary hypoplasia if very early
  * Limb deformity

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### History
* Regular painful contractions
* Vaginal fluid loss
* Bleeding or show
* Fever or malaise
* UTI or genital infection symptoms
* Previous preterm birth
* Cervical surgery

### Abdominal and Speculum Findings
* Uterine contractions on palpation
* Fluid pooling in posterior fornix
* Cervical dilation or effacement
* Funic presentation or cord prolapse
* Fever, tachycardia, or uterine tenderness if chorioamnionitis

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
* Braxton Hicks contractions
* UTI
* Placental abruption
* Cervical insufficiency
* Uterine irritation from fibroids
* False fluid loss: urine, vaginal discharge

### Clinical Staging
* **Threatened preterm labour:** contractions without cervical change
* **Established preterm labour:** contractions with cervical dilatation/effacement
* **PPROM with latency:** membranes ruptured but labour not yet started
* **PPROM with chorioamnionitis:** delivery indicated

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Maternal Tests
* CBC, CRP
* High vaginal and endocervical swabs
* Midstream urine culture
* Blood cultures if sepsis suspected
* GBS screening

### Ultrasound Findings
* Cervical length:
  * <25 mm is high risk
* Amniotic fluid volume:
  * Oligohydramnios after PPROM
* Fetal presentation and wellbeing
* Placental site
* Signs of abruption

### CTG Findings
* Contractility pattern
* Fetal tachycardia if infection
* Reduced variability
* Decelerations from cord compression

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Corticosteroids for Fetal Lung Maturity
* **Betamethasone 12 mg IM 24-hourly × 2 doses**
* Indicated if delivery likely before 34-37 weeks
* Reduces:
  * RDS
  * IVH
  * NEC
  * Neonatal death

### Neuroprotection
* **Magnesium sulphate** if delivery before 30 weeks
* Reduces cerebral palsy risk

### Tocolysis
* Options:
  * Nifedipine
  * Atosiban
  * Indomethacin <32 weeks
* Aim: delay labour for steroid completion and in-utero transfer
* Contraindications:
  * Chorioamnionitis
  * Severe abruption
  * Fetal distress
  * Severe pre-eclampsia

### Antibiotics
* GBS prophylaxis if positive or unknown with risk factors
* Broad-spectrum antibiotics if chorioamnionitis
* Latency antibiotics for PPROM before 34 weeks

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Expectant Management of PPROM
* Admit if <34 weeks
* Monitor for chorioamnionitis
* Avoid digital examination
* Serial ultrasound and CTG
* Continue pregnancy until 34 weeks if stable

### Indications for Delivery
* Chorioamnionitis
* Placental abruption
* Fetal distress
* Severe pre-eclampsia
* Advanced labour
* PPROM at ≥34 weeks

### Mode of Delivery
* Vaginal delivery usually preferred
* Caesarean for:
  * Breech if very preterm
  * Fetal distress
  * Standard obstetric indications

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Neonatal Care
* Neonatal team at delivery
* Watch for:
  * RDS
  * Sepsis
  * Hypoglycaemia
  * Jaundice
  * Necrotising enterocolitis
  * IVH

### Maternal Follow-up
* Treat underlying infection
* Screen for retained products
* Discuss future preterm birth risk
* Consider cervical length surveillance and progesterone in next pregnancy

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Chorioamnionitis
* Sepsis
* Postpartum haemorrhage
* Psychological distress
* Future preterm birth

### Fetal and Neonatal Complications
* RDS
* Sepsis
* IVH
* NEC
* Cord prolapse
* Stillbirth
* Neurodevelopmental impairment

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Give steroids if delivery likely before 34-37 weeks
* Add magnesium sulphate before 30 weeks for neuroprotection
* Avoid digital examination after PPROM
* Deliver if chorioamnionitis, abruption, fetal distress, or labour advanced
* Continue expectant management until 34 weeks if PPROM stable

* Admit women with PPROM before 34 weeks for surveillance
* Use sterile speculum only; avoid digital examination
* Give corticosteroids if delivery is likely before 34-37 weeks
* Add magnesium sulphate if delivery before 30 weeks
* Continue pregnancy until 34 weeks if maternal and fetal status stable
* Deliver immediately if chorioamnionitis, abruption, fetal distress, or advanced labour

* Distinguish true preterm labour from Braxton Hicks contractions using cervical change
* Use fetal fibronectin or cervical length only if the result will change management
* Consider chorioamnionitis if maternal tachycardia, fetal tachycardia, or uterine tenderness
* Avoid repeated digital examination after PPROM
* Confirm neonatal cot availability before antenatal transfer
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the main benefit of antenatal corticosteroids? Answer: Reduced neonatal death, RDS, IVH, and NEC.
* Trap: Using tocolysis in chorioamnionitis. Pearl: Delivery and antibiotics are required.
* Pitfall: Digital vaginal examination in PPROM. Use sterile speculum only.
