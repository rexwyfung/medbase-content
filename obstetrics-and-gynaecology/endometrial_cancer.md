# Endometrial Cancer (子宮內膜癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition
* **Endometrial Cancer (子宮內膜癌)**: Primary malignant neoplasm arising from the epithelial lining of the uterine cavity. 

### Local HK Epidemiology
* Most common gynaecological malignancy in Hong Kong.
* Peak incidence: 50-60 years (postmenopausal).
* Incidence rapidly $\uparrow$ locally due to $\uparrow$ obesity rates & $\downarrow$ parity.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Type 1 vs Type 2 Pathophysiology
* **Type 1 (Endometrioid)**: 
 * ~80% of cases.
 * Estrogen-dependent ($\uparrow$ unopposed estrogen).
 * Precursor: **Endometrial Hyperplasia (子宮內膜增生)**.
 * Associated mutations: PTEN, KRAS, MSI.
 * Favorable prognosis.
* **Type 2 (Non-endometrioid/Serous/Clear Cell)**:
 * ~10-20% of cases.
 * Estrogen-independent.
 * Arises in atrophic endometrium.
 * Associated mutation: p53.
 * Highly aggressive, poor prognosis.

### Endocrine Axis & Risk Factors
* $\uparrow$ Unopposed estrogen drives endometrial proliferation $\to$ atypia $\to$ carcinoma.
* Major Risk Factors: 
 * **Obesity (肥胖)** (peripheral aromatization of androgens $\to$ estrone).
 * Nulliparity, early menarche, late menopause.
 * **Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) (多囊卵巢綜合症)** (chronic anovulation).
 * **Tamoxifen (他莫昔芬)** use (partial estrogen agonist in endometrium).
 * **Lynch Syndrome (HNPCC)** (genetic predisposition).

### Pelvic Anatomy & Spread Patterns
* **Direct Extension**: Into myometrium, cervical stroma, vagina, parametrium.
* **Lymphatic**: Pelvic nodes (obturator, internal/external iliac) $\to$ Para-aortic nodes.
* **Hematogenous**: Lungs, liver, bone (late stage).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History
* **Postmenopausal Bleeding (PMB) (絕經後出血)**: Classic presentation (10% of PMB is cancer).
* Irregular perimenopausal bleeding or intermenstrual bleeding.
* Heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) in younger anovulatory patients (e.g., PCOS).
* Purulent vaginal discharge (pyometra).
* Constitutional symptoms: Unexplained weight loss, lethargy (late stage).

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* Often unremarkable in early stages.
* Palpable pelvic/abdominal mass (advanced disease with uterine enlargement or synchronous ovarian tumour).
* Ascites (abdominal distension, shifting dullness).
* Hepatomegaly (metastatic disease).

### Speculum Examination Findings
* Blood visualizing at external cervical os.
* Co-existing **Cervical Polyp (子宮頸息肉)**.
* Gross cervical or vaginal involvement (tumour extending to ectocervix/vagina).
* **Atrophic Vaginitis (萎縮性陰道炎)** (pale, smooth, shiny mucosa with petechiae - common co-existing benign cause of bleeding).

### Bimanual Examination Findings
* Bulky, enlarged, or globular uterus.
* Fixed, immobile uterus (advanced parametrial invasion).
* Adnexal mass (metastasis or primary ovarian pathology).
* Nodularity in Pouch of Douglas.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Critical Differential Diagnoses
| Condition | Differentiating Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Atrophic Vaginitis (萎縮性陰道炎)** | Pale, friable vaginal mucosa; lack of rugae; low estrogen state. |
| **Endometrial Polyp (子宮內膜息肉)** | Focal structural lesion; bleeding pattern variable. |
| **Endometrial Hyperplasia (子宮內膜增生)** | Precursor lesion; identical clinical presentation to carcinoma. |
| **Cervical Cancer (子宮頸癌)** | Gross cervical lesion; postcoital bleeding prominent; abnormal smear. |
| **Submucosal Leiomyoma (黏膜下肌瘤)** | Enlarged firm uterus; heavy regular menses rather than irregular. |

### FIGO Clinical Staging (2023 Revision)
| Stage | Extent of Tumour Spread |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stage I** | Confined to corpus uteri or ovaries (synchronous). |
| *IA* | Non-aggressive histology confined to endometrium/polyps or <50% myometrium. |
| *IB* | Non-aggressive histology invading $\ge$50% myometrium. |
| *IC* | Aggressive histology (e.g., serous) confined to polyp/endometrium. |
| **Stage II** | Tumour extension to cervical stroma. |
| **Stage III** | Local/regional spread (serosa, adnexa, vagina, parametrium, lymph nodes). |
| **Stage IV** | Distant metastasis (bladder/bowel mucosa, extra-abdominal). |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Pelvic Ultrasound Criteria (Transvaginal - TVS)
* **Endometrial Thickness (ET)**: ET $\le$ 4mm implies endometrial carcinoma is highly unlikely (Negative Predictive Value 99.4-100%). ET > 4mm warrants biopsy.
* Irregular endometrial-myometrial junction.
* Heterogeneous echogenicity within the cavity.
* Pathological intra-cavitary vascular flow on color Doppler.

### Laboratory Investigations
* CBP: $\downarrow$ Hb, $\downarrow$ MCV (iron deficiency anemia secondary to chronic bleeding).
* RFT, LFT: Baseline pre-operative assessment & check for metastasis.
* Tumour markers: CA-125 (may be $\uparrow$ in advanced/extrauterine disease; not for primary screening).

### Pathological & Tissue Sampling
* **Endometrial Aspiration (e.g., Pipelle)**: First-line outpatient sampling.
* **Hysteroscopy + Directed Biopsy**: Gold standard. Indicated if TVS >4mm, focal lesion suspected, or Pipelle failed/inconclusive.

### Imaging for Staging
* **MRI Pelvis (with contrast)**: Modality of choice for assessing myometrial invasion depth and cervical stromal involvement.
* **CT Thorax/Abdomen/Pelvis**: To assess retroperitoneal lymph nodes and distant metastasis.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization

### Acute Haemorrhage Stabilization
* IV access, fluid resuscitation (crystalloids).
* Type & Screen / Crossmatch.
* **Tranexamic Acid (傳明酸)**: 1g IV or PO Q8H to promote clot stability.
* Vaginal packing if catastrophic bleeding (rare in primary endometrial, more common in cervical).

### Medical Pharmacology (Non-Surgical Candidates / Fertility Sparing)
* Target population: Extremely high surgical risk or young women with Grade 1 Stage IA disease strongly desiring fertility preservation.
* **High-Dose Oral Progestogens**: Medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) 400-600mg daily or Megestrol acetate 160-320mg daily.
* **Levonorgestrel-Releasing Intrauterine System (LNG-IUS / Mirena)**: Local high-dose progestogen therapy.
* *Note: Requires strict hysteroscopic monitoring every 3-6 months.*

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Primary Surgical Management
* **Standard Procedure**: Total Hysterectomy + Bilateral Salpingo-oophorectomy (TH+BSO) (全子宮及雙側輸卵管卵巢切除術).
* **Approach**: Minimally invasive (Laparoscopic or Robotic) is preferred over laparotomy due to $\downarrow$ morbidity and faster recovery.
* **Lymph Node Assessment**:
 * **Sentinel Lymph Node (SLN) Mapping**: Using Indocyanine Green (ICG). Preferred for early-stage disease to reduce lymphedema risk while ensuring accurate staging.
 * **Pelvic & Para-aortic Lymphadenectomy**: Reserved for high-risk histology (Type 2), deep myometrial invasion, or clinically suspicious nodes.
* Peritoneal washings obtained for cytology (prognostic, no longer dictates staging).

### Adjuvant Therapy Indications
* **Observation**: Stage IA, Grade 1-2.
* **Vaginal Vault Brachytherapy (VBT)**: High-intermediate risk (e.g., Stage IB, Grade 1-2; Stage IA, Grade 3) to $\downarrow$ local vault recurrence.
* **External Beam Radiotherapy (EBRT)**: Stage II or Stage III (pelvic control).
* **Systemic Chemotherapy**: Carboplatin + Paclitaxel for advanced stages (Stage III/IV) or high-risk histologies (serous/clear cell).

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-operative Ward Care
* Strict VTE prophylaxis (LMWH, TED stockings, early mobilization) due to high baseline risk (obesity, malignancy, pelvic surgery).
* Urinary catheter care (remove Post-Op Day 1-2 depending on radicality and mobility).
* Monitor vaginal vault bleeding.

### Follow-up Protocol
* Objective: Detect early vault recurrence (highly salvageable with RT if naive).
* **Schedule**: Every 3-4 months for first 2 years, then every 6 months up to 5 years.
* **Clinical**: Speculum exam to inspect vaginal vault, bimanual/rectovaginal exam to detect nodularity.
* Vault cytology (smear) routinely performed but physical inspection is paramount.

### Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) & Menopause Management
* Premature surgical menopause induced by BSO.
* Systemic HRT is generally **contraindicated** in endometrial cancer survivors (estrogen-dependent tumour).
* Manage genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) with non-hormonal vaginal moisturizers/lubricants.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Disease-Related Complications
* Chronic severe anemia.
* Pyometra or hematometra secondary to cervical stenosis.
* Ureteric obstruction leading to hydronephrosis and renal failure.

### Treatment-Related Surgical Complications
* Intraoperative ureteric, bladder, or bowel injury.
* Vaginal vault dehiscence.
* Lower limb lymphedema (secondary to systemic lymphadenectomy).
* Pelvic lymphocyst formation.

### Radiotherapy/Chemotherapy Complications
* **Radiation Proctitis (放射性直腸炎)** / Radiation Cystitis: Diarrhea, PR bleeding, hematuria, strictures.
* Vaginal stenosis and dyspareunia (prevented with regular vaginal dilator use).
* Chemotherapy toxicity: Peripheral neuropathy, alopecia, myelosuppression.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical initial investigation for a 55-year-old presenting with postmenopausal bleeding? Answer: Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVS) to measure endometrial thickness; if $\le$4mm, cancer is highly unlikely.
* Trap: Assuming negative TVS rules out all malignancies in PMB. Pearl: TVS only assesses the endometrium; meticulous speculum exam is mandatory to exclude cervical or vaginal malignancies.
* Pitfall: Prescribing unopposed estrogen HRT to a woman with an intact uterus. This will drive endometrial hyperplasia and significantly increase the risk of Type 1 endometrial cancer.
* Viva Question: What is the gold standard investigation for diagnosing endometrial cancer if outpatient Pipelle sampling fails or is inconclusive? Answer: Outpatient hysteroscopy with directed biopsy.
* Trap: Performing a standard subtotal hysterectomy for endometrial cancer. Pearl: The standard surgical procedure must be a Total Hysterectomy with Bilateral Salpingo-oophorectomy (TH+BSO) to remove the entire target organ and estrogen-producing sources.
* Pitfall: Ignoring irregular menses in an obese 35-year-old with PCOS. Chronic anovulation leads to unopposed estrogen exposure; these patients require cyclical progestogens to protect the endometrium.
