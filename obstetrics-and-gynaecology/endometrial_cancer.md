# Endometrial Cancer (子宮內膜癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Disease Definition and Histological Types
* **Endometrial cancer:** malignant epithelial tumour arising from endometrial glands
* Commonest gynaecological malignancy in high-income settings
* Hong Kong:
  * **4th commonest female cancer**
  * Annual incidence ~29/100,000
  * Median age ~57 years
* Types:
  * **Type I / endometrioid:** oestrogen-dependent, better prognosis
  * **Type II:** serous, clear cell, carcinosarcoma; oestrogen-independent, poorer prognosis

### Key Risk Groups
* Obesity
* Nulliparity
* PCOS
* Diabetes
* Hypertension
| Tamoxifen therapy
* Lynch syndrome
* Oestrogen-secreting ovarian tumour
* Unopposed oestrogen therapy

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Type I Oestrogen-Driven Pathway
* Unopposed oestrogen → endometrial proliferation
* Progression:
  * Hyperplasia
  * Atypical hyperplasia
  * Endometrioid carcinoma
* PTEN, PIK3CA, KRAS, and mismatch repair gene abnormalities are common

### Type II Pathway
* Often arises in atrophic endometrium
* p53 mutation is common
* More aggressive:
  * Deep myometrial invasion
  * Lymphovascular space involvement
  * Early nodal spread

### Spread Pattern
* Direct:
  * Myometrium
  * Cervix
  * Adnexa
* Lymphatic:
  * Pelvic nodes
  * Para-aortic nodes
* Haematogenous:
  * Lung
  * Liver
  * Bone

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Presenting Symptoms
* **Postmenopausal bleeding** is the commonest presentation
* Persistent intermenstrual bleeding
* Heavy menstrual bleeding in perimenopause
* Abnormal glandular cervical cytology
* Pelvic pain if advanced
* Weight loss and bowel/bladder symptoms are late

### Examination Findings
* Often normal in early disease
* Obesity and metabolic syndrome features
* Abdominal mass or ascites in advanced disease
* Vaginal or cervical lesion if tumour prolapses
* Enlarged uterus
* Fixed pelvic mass if extrauterine spread

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
* Endometrial polyp
* Endometrial hyperplasia with or without atypia
* Atrophic vaginitis
* Cervical cancer
| Submucosal fibroid
* Hormone therapy effect
* Coagulopathy
* Oestrogen-secreting ovarian tumour

### FIGO 2009 Endometrial Cancer Staging
| Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Confined to corpus |
| **IA** | No or <50% myometrial invasion |
| **IB** | ≥50% myometrial invasion |
| **II** | Cervical stromal invasion |
| **IIIA** | Serosa/adnexa involvement |
| **IIIB** | Vaginal/parametrial spread |
| **IIIC1** | Pelvic lymph nodes |
| **IIIC2** | Para-aortic lymph nodes |
| **IVA** | Bladder/bowel mucosa |
| **IVB** | Distant metastasis |

### Prognostic Factors
* Stage
* Histological type and grade
* Myometrial invasion depth
* Lymphovascular space invasion
* Nodal involvement
* ER/PR status

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Initial Tests
* **Transvaginal ultrasound**
  * Endometrial thickness **≤4 mm** has high negative predictive value for cancer
  * **>4 mm** requires sampling
  * Assess:
    * Focal lesion
    * Myometrial invasion
    * Cervical extension
    * Adnexal mass
    * Fluid in cavity

### Endometrial Sampling
* **Pipelle aspiration**
* Dilatation and curettage
* **Hysteroscopy with targeted biopsy**
  * Preferred if focal lesion suspected
  * Also allows polypectomy

### Pre-operative and Staging Tests
* CBC
* Renal and liver function
* CA125 if advanced disease suspected
* Chest X-ray
* MRI pelvis for myometrial invasion and nodal disease
* CT chest/abdomen/pelvis if high-risk histology or advanced clinical stage
* Mismatch repair immunohistochemistry and Lynch screening in selected cases

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Pre-operative Optimisation
* Optimise diabetes and hypertension
* Assess cardiovascular and anaesthetic risk
* Correct anaemia:
  * Oral iron if mild
  * Transfusion if symptomatic or severe
* VTE risk assessment:
  * Obesity and cancer are major risk factors
  * Consider preoperative prophylactic LMWH

### Conservative Hormonal Therapy
Only if:
* Well-differentiated endometrioid cancer
* Stage IA presumed
* Fertility desired
* Strong compliance
* No contraindication
* Use:
  * Levonorgestrel intrauterine system
  * Oral progestogen
* Requires hysteroscopic biopsy and close surveillance

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Standard Surgical Management
* **Total hysterectomy**
* **Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy**
* Peritoneal washings
* Surgical staging with lymphadenectomy if:
  * Deep myometrial invasion
  * Grade 3
  * Serous/clear cell/carcinosarcoma
  * Cervical stromal invasion
  * Suspected nodes on imaging

### Surgical Approach
* Laparoscopic or robotic preferred if technically suitable
* Open surgery if:
  * Bulky disease
  * Uterine enlargement preventing safe laparoscopy
  * Severe adhesions
* Sentinel lymph node mapping increasingly used in selected cases

### Adjuvant Therapy
* **Vaginal brachytherapy** for intermediate-risk early disease
* **External beam radiotherapy** if high intermediate or node-positive risk
* **Chemotherapy** for:
  * Stage III/IV
  * Serous/clear cell histology
  * Recurrence risk
* Combined chemoradiotherapy for high-risk advanced disease

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Immediate Post-operative Care
* VTE prophylaxis
* Monitor:
  * Bleeding
  * Infection
  * Bladder function
  * Bowel recovery
* Early mobilisation
* Histology review at multidisciplinary meeting

### Hormonal and Survivorship Care
* HRT may be considered after low-risk disease but requires oncology input
* Avoid HRT in serous/clear cell tumours unless benefits outweigh risks
* Manage menopausal symptoms and bone health
* Lynch syndrome cascade testing if positive

### Surveillance
* Clinical review every 3-6 months initially
* Vault cytology only if indicated
* Investigate new bleeding or pelvic pain
| CT/MRI if recurrence suspected

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal and Oncological Complications
* Anaemia
* Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism
* Lymphoedema
* Bowel or bladder injury
* Radiotherapy enteritis/cystitis
* Recurrence
* Distant metastasis

### Fertility and Gynaecological Complications
* Infertility after hysterectomy
* Premature menopause if oophorectomy
* Osteoporosis and cardiovascular risk after oophorectomy
* Asherman’s syndrome after repeated curettage
* Psychological distress

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What endometrial thickness threshold usually excludes endometrial cancer? Answer: ≤4 mm on transvaginal ultrasound.
* Trap: Treating atypical hyperplasia conservatively without confirming no invasive cancer. Pearl: Occult carcinoma risk is significant.
* Pitfall: Assuming all endometrial cancers are oestrogen-driven. Pearl: Serous and clear cell tumours often occur in atrophic endometrium.
* Viva Question: Which histologies require adjuvant chemotherapy even in early stage? Answer: Serous, clear cell, and carcinosarcoma.
* Pitfall: Forgetting Lynch syndrome screening in endometrial cancer diagnosed before 50 years or with suggestive family history.
* Trap: Offering HRT routinely after endometrial cancer. Pearl: Individualise based on stage, histology, and symptoms.
