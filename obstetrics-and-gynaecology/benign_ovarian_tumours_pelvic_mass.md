# Benign Ovarian Tumours and Pelvic Mass (良性卵巢腫瘤與盆腔腫塊)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definitions
* **Ovarian cyst:** fluid-filled ovarian structure
* **Functional cyst:** physiological follicular or corpus luteum cyst
* **Benign ovarian tumour:** neoplastic cyst or solid ovarian mass without invasion
* **Pelvic mass:** any ovarian, tubal, uterine, or other pelvic lesion

### Local Epidemiology
* Ovarian cysts are found in up to **20%** of asymptomatic women
* Most premenopausal cysts are functional and resolve spontaneously
* Malignancy risk rises after menopause

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Functional Cysts
* **Follicular cyst:** failed ovulation/follicle persistence
* **Corpus luteum cyst:** haemorrhagic cyst after ovulation
* Usually resolve within **2-3 menstrual cycles**

### Benign Neoplasms
* **Epithelial:**
  * Serous cystadenoma
  * Mucinous cystadenoma
* **Germ cell:**
  * Mature teratoma/dermoid
* **Sex-cord stromal:**
  * Fibroma
  * Thecoma
* **Endometrioma:** endometriotic cyst

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Symptoms
* Asymptomatic
* Pelvic pain or pressure
* Bloating
* Dyspareunia
* Urinary frequency
| Constipation
* Acute pain, vomiting, or dizziness suggests torsion/rupture
* Postmenopausal bleeding if hormone-secreting tumour

### Examination
* Palpable pelvic mass
* Mobile smooth mass if benign cyst
* Fixed irregular mass suggests malignancy/endometriosis
* Ascites or nodularity suggests malignancy
* Acute abdomen if torsion or rupture

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
* Functional cyst
* Serous/mucinous cystadenoma
* Dermoid cyst
* Endometrioma
| Fibroid
* Tubo-ovarian abscess
* Ectopic pregnancy
* Ovarian cancer
* Diverticular/appendiceal mass

### Risk of Malignancy Index
RMI = U × M × CA125
* **U score:** 1, 3, or 5 based on ultrasound features
* **M:** 1 premenopausal, 3 postmenopausal
* **CA125:** absolute value
* RMI <25 = low risk
* RMI 25-250 = intermediate
* RMI >250 = high risk

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Ultrasound Features
* Simple unilocular cyst:
  * Anechoic
  * Thin wall
  * No solid areas
* Dermoid:
  * Rokitansky nodule
  * Shadowing
  * Fat/fluid level
* Endometrioma:
  * Ground-glass echoes
* Suspicious features:
  * Solid areas
  * Papillary projections
  * Thick septa
  * Ascites
  * High vascularity

### Tumour Markers
* CA125 for epithelial cancer
* AFP in germ cell tumour/yolk sac
* hCG in choriocarcinoma/dysgerminoma
* LDH in dysgerminoma
* Inhibin in granulosa cell tumour

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Expectant Management
* Premenopausal simple cyst <5 cm
* No symptoms
* Normal CA125
* Repeat ultrasound 4-6 weeks

### Acute Torsion or Rupture
* NBM, IV fluids, analgesia
* Urgent laparoscopy
* Ovarian preservation if viable

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surgical Indications
* Persistent cyst >5 cm
* Solid or complex cyst
* Suspicious RMI
* Symptoms
* Torsion/rupture
* Postmenopausal cyst with raised RMI

### Surgical Approach
* **Laparoscopy:** preferred for presumed benign cyst
* **Cystectomy:** preserve ovary in premenopausal women
* **Salpingo-oophorectomy:** postmenopausal women or high malignancy risk
* Refer to gynaecological oncologist if RMI >250

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After Cystectomy
* Histology review
* Monitor ovarian reserve if bilateral
* Follow-up ultrasound if endometrioma recurs

### After Oophorectomy
* Counsel fertility impact
* HRT if premenopausal bilateral oophorectomy
* Long-term bone health assessment

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Torsion
* Rupture
| Haemorrhage
* Ovarian cancer
* Loss of ovarian tissue

### Gynaecological Complications
* Recurrence
* Adhesions
* Infection
* Intraoperative bladder/bowel injury
* Premature ovarian insufficiency after bilateral surgery

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Admit for urgent laparoscopy if sudden severe pain, vomiting, or peritonism suggests torsion
* Repeat ultrasound 4-6 weeks for premenopausal simple cyst <5 cm
* Refer to gynaecological oncology if RMI >250 or suspicious solid/papillary areas
* Offer salpingo-oophorectomy rather than cystectomy in postmenopausal women
* Check AFP, hCG, LDH, and inhibin if solid tumour in a young woman

* Use RMI to triage to general gynaecology or oncology
* Repeat ultrasound at 4-6 weeks for premenopausal simple cysts <5 cm
* Offer laparoscopy for symptomatic persistent cysts
* Consider torsion even if ultrasound Doppler is present
* Use salpingo-oophorectomy for postmenopausal cysts rather than cystectomy
* Cross-match blood if large cyst, haemoperitoneum, or suspected malignancy

* Consider torsion even when Doppler flow is present because dual blood supply can preserve perfusion
* Repeat ultrasound after the next menstrual cycle in premenopausal women if cyst is simple and <5 cm
* Use MRI when ultrasound cannot distinguish dermoid, endometrioma, or fibroid
* Check CA125, CEA, CA19-9, AFP, hCG, LDH, and inhibin if the mass is complex or solid
* Avoid cystectomy in postmenopausal women because malignancy risk is higher
* Refer to oncology before surgery if RMI >250 or imaging suggests peritoneal spread
* Suspect ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome in women receiving fertility treatment who develop ascites and enlarged ovaries
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How is RMI calculated? Answer: Ultrasound score × menopausal score × CA125.
* Trap: Operating immediately on all premenopausal simple cysts. Pearl: Most <5 cm functional cysts can be observed.
* Pitfall: Performing cystectomy in postmenopausal women. Salpingo-oophorectomy is preferred due to malignancy risk.
* Viva Question: Which marker is associated with dysgerminoma? Answer: LDH.
* Pitfall: Missing torsion in sudden severe pelvic pain with vomiting. Urgent laparoscopy is required.
