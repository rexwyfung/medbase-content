# Benign Ovarian Tumours & Pelvic Mass (良性卵巢腫瘤與盆腔腫塊)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition
* **Definition:** Non-cancerous cystic or solid growths of the ovary
* **Functional Cysts:**
    * Follicular cysts
    * Corpus luteum cysts
* **Benign Neoplasms:**
    * Mature Cystic Teratoma / Dermoid (成熟囊性畸胎瘤)
    * Cystadenoma (囊腺瘤)
    * Endometrioma (子宮內膜異位囊腫)

### Local Epidemiology
* **Prevalence:** Common in reproductive age group
* **Surgical Frequency:** ~10% of women undergo surgery for an ovarian mass in their lifetime
* **Malignancy Risk:**
    * Low in reproductive age
    * Significantly ↑ in postmenopausal women

### Gestational Context (Ovarian Cyst in Pregnancy)
* **Detection:** Mostly found incidentally during 1st-trimester dating ultrasound
* **Most Common Type:** Corpus luteum cyst
* **Natural History:** Typically resolve spontaneously by 14-16 weeks
* **Persistent Mass Risks:**
    * ↑ Risk of torsion (especially at 14-16 weeks as the uterus rises into the abdomen)
    * Obstructed labour if located in the pelvis at term

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Functional Cysts Pathophysiology
* **Follicular Cyst:** 
    * Failure of dominant follicle rupture
    * Incomplete resorption of follicular fluid
    * Lined by granulosa cells
* **Corpus Luteum Cyst:** 
    * Failure of corpus luteum regression
    * Thin-walled
    * Prone to internal hemorrhage (hemorrhagic cyst)
* **Theca Lutein Cyst:** 
    * Bilateral
    * Multiloculated
    * Driven by ↑↑ hCG levels (e.g., Molar pregnancy, Multiple gestation, OHSS)

### Benign Epithelial Tumours (60-80%)
* **Serous Cystadenoma (漿液性囊腺瘤):** 
    * Unilocular
    * Clear serous fluid
    * Ciliated epithelium lining
* **Mucinous Cystadenoma (黏液性囊腺瘤):** 
    * Multilocular
    * Can grow to massive sizes
    * Thick mucinous content

### Germ Cell & Sex-Cord Stromal Tumours
* **Mature Cystic Teratoma (Dermoid Cyst):** 
    * Derived from totipotent germ cells
    * Contains ectodermal/mesodermal/endodermal tissue
    * Hair, sebum, teeth, bone present
    * May contain functional thyroid tissue (Struma ovarii)
* **Fibroma:** 
    * Sex-cord stromal origin
    * Solid benign tumour
    * Associated with Meigs' syndrome (Fibroma + Ascites + Pleural effusion)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History & Symptoms
* **Classic Symptoms:**
    * Asymptomatic (Incidental finding)
    * Dull aching lower abdominal pain
* **Pressure Symptoms:**
    * Urinary frequency
    * Constipation
    * Early satiety
* **Acute Symptoms (Suspected Complication):**
    * Acute severe localized pain
    * Nausea and vomiting
    * Fainting or dizziness
* **Menstrual History:**
    * LMP
    * Regularity of cycles
    * Dysmenorrhoea (Suggests endometrioma)
    * Menorrhagia

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* **Palpation:**
    * Mass palpable per abdomen if >12 weeks gestation equivalent size
    * Smooth surface (Suggests benign)
* **Peritoneal Signs:**
    * Guarding (Suggests rupture/torsion)
    * Rebound tenderness
* **Fluid Assessment:**
    * Shifting dullness +ve (Suggests ascites)

### Speculum & Bimanual Examination Findings
* **Speculum Exam:**
    * Usually visually normal
    * Look for lateral deviation of the cervix
* **Bimanual Exam:**
    * Palpable adnexal mass independent of the uterus
* **Assessment of Mass Characteristics:**
    * Size
    * Consistency (Cystic, firm, or solid)
    * Mobility (Mobile = simple cyst/dermoid; Fixed = endometrioma/PID/malignancy)
    * Tenderness (Tender = inflammation/torsion)
* **Pouch of Douglas (POD):**
    * Nodularity (Suggests endometriosis or malignancy)

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Gynaecological Differential Diagnoses
* **Pregnancy-related:**
    * Ectopic pregnancy (宮外孕)
* **Uterine Pathology:**
    * Subserosal Fibroid (漿膜下肌瘤)
    * Broad ligament fibroid
* **Infective:**
    * Tubo-ovarian abscess (TOA)
    * Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)
* **Malignant:**
    * Ovarian carcinoma
    * Borderline ovarian tumour

### Non-Gynaecological Differential Diagnoses
* **Gastrointestinal:**
    * Acute appendicitis
    * Appendiceal mucocele
    * Diverticular abscess
    * Colorectal carcinoma
* **Genitourinary:**
    * Distended bladder
    * Pelvic kidney

### Risk of Malignancy Index (RMI)
| Parameter | Diagnostic Criteria | Assigned Score |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **U (Ultrasound Features)** | Multilocular cyst<br>Solid areas<br>Bilateral lesions<br>Ascites<br>Intra-abdominal metastases | 0 features = 0<br>1 feature = 1<br>$\ge$ 2 features = 3 |
| **M (Menopausal Status)** | Premenopausal<br>Postmenopausal | Premenopausal = 1<br>Postmenopausal = 3 |
| **CA-125** | Absolute serum value (U/mL) | Exact Value |
* **Calculation:** RMI = U x M x CA-125
* **Threshold:** RMI > 200 indicates High Risk (Warrants gynaecological oncologist referral)

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Laboratory Investigations
* **Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT):** Mandatory for all women of reproductive age
* **Premenopausal Tumour Markers (If Suspicious/Solid):**
    * CA-125 (Low specificity, ↑ in endometriosis/fibroids)
    * AFP (Germ cell)
    * $\beta$-hCG (Germ cell / Choriocarcinoma)
    * LDH (Dysgerminoma)
    * Inhibin (Granulosa cell tumour)
* **Postmenopausal Tumour Markers:**
    * CA-125 (High specificity)
    * HE4
* **Pre-operative Labs:**
    * Complete Blood Picture (CBP)
    * Type & Screen

### Specific Ultrasound Criteria (Benign vs Malignant)
* **IOTA Simple Rules (Benign Features):**
    * Unilocular cyst
    * Presence of solid components < 7mm
    * Presence of acoustic shadows
    * Smooth multilocular tumour < 100mm
    * No blood flow (Color Doppler score 1)
* **Pathology-Specific Patterns:**
    * **Simple Cyst:** Anechoic, thin smooth wall, posterior acoustic enhancement
    * **Dermoid Cyst:** Hyperechoic Rokitansky nodule, echogenic lines/dots, dense acoustic shadowing
    * **Endometrioma:** Homogeneous ground-glass echogenicity, thick walls, absent internal flow
    * **Hemorrhagic Cyst:** Reticular "fishnet" or "cobweb" pattern, retracting clot with concave margins

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Stabilization (Suspected Torsion or Rupture)
* **Resuscitation Steps:**
    * ABCDE approach
    * Secure large-bore IV access x2
* **Fluid Management:**
    * IV 0.9% NaCl bolus if hypotensive or hemorrhagic shock
* **Pharmacological Support:**
    * IV Paracetamol
    * IV NSAIDs
    * IV Opiates (if severe pain)
* **Pre-operative Preparation:**
    * Keep patient NPO
    * Prepare for emergency laparoscopy

### Medical & Conservative Management
* **Asymptomatic Simple Cysts < 5cm (Premenopausal):**
    * No follow-up required
    * Regarded as physiological
* **Simple Cysts 5-7cm (Premenopausal):**
    * Repeat USG in 6-8 weeks
    * Often resolve spontaneously
* **Endometrioma Medical Therapy:**
    * NSAIDs for analgesia
    * Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCP)
    * Progestogens (e.g., Dienogest)

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Indications for Gynaecological Surgery
* **Emergency Indications:**
    * Ovarian torsion
    * Cyst rupture with hemodynamic instability
* **Elective Indications:**
    * Suspicion of malignancy (Complex features, ↑ CA-125, RMI >200)
    * Persistent symptomatic mass causing pressure/pain
    * Mass > 8-10cm (↑ risk of torsion)
    * Bilateral complex masses

### Operative Principles
* **Surgical Approach:**
    * **Laparoscopy (腹腔鏡):** Gold standard for benign masses (↓ morbidity, ↓ adhesions)
    * **Laparotomy (開腹手術):** Reserved for massive cysts, hemodynamic instability, or high suspicion of malignancy
* **Procedural Options:**
    * **Ovarian Cystectomy:** Preferred in reproductive age to conserve ovarian reserve. Use endoscopic retrieval bag to prevent spillage (especially dermoids to avoid chemical peritonitis).
    * **Salpingo-oophorectomy (BSO/USO):** Standard for postmenopausal women, or if the ovary is completely infarcted/destroyed by disease.
* **Ovarian Torsion Management:**
    * Emergency detorsion (untwisting)
    * Observe for reperfusion
    * Perform cystectomy
    * Strictly avoid immediate oophorectomy in young women unless tissue is frankly necrotic and friable post-detorsion

### Timing of Surgery in Pregnancy
* **Elective Timing:**
    * If persistent/solid >6-8cm, perform surgery at **14-16 weeks** (Early 2nd Trimester)
    * Minimizes risk of abortion and avoids technical difficulty from a large gravid uterus
* **Progesterone Support:**
    * If corpus luteum is excised < 10 weeks GA
    * Give exogenous progesterone until placental takeover at 10-12 weeks

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-operative Care
* **Immediate Care:**
    * Early mobilization
    * Adequate oral analgesia
* **VTE Prophylaxis:**
    * Mechanical thrombo-embolic deterrent (TED) stockings
    * LMWH if high-risk factors present
* **Monitoring:**
    * Watch for signs of internal hemorrhage
    * Watch for signs of chemical peritonitis (if dermoid spillage occurred)

### Contraception & Follow-up
* **Histology Review:**
    * Confirm benign pathology
* **Functional Cysts Follow-up:**
    * COCP does not accelerate resolution of existing cysts
    * COCP prevents formation of *new* functional cysts
* **Postmenopausal Conservative Follow-up (Simple cyst < 5cm, normal CA-125):**
    * Repeat USG and CA-125 every 4-6 months for 1 year
    * Discharge if stable or resolved

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Gynaecological Complications
* **Ovarian Torsion (卵巢扭轉):**
    * Mass twists on infundibulopelvic ligament
    * Venous/lymphatic occlusion → edema → arterial occlusion → ischemic necrosis
* **Cyst Rupture:**
    * **Simple cyst:** Mild self-limiting pain
    * **Corpus luteum:** Massive hemoperitoneum
    * **Dermoid cyst:** Severe acute chemical peritonitis from sebaceous material
* **Tubo-Ovarian Abscess:**
    * Secondary infection of an existing cyst

### Maternal-Fetal Complications
* **Preterm Labour:**
    * Triggered by acute abdomen from torsion or rupture
* **Obstructed Labour:**
    * Occurs if a large ovarian cyst is impacted in the Pouch of Douglas during descent of the fetal head

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the most critical initial investigation for a reproductive-age woman presenting with an adnexal mass and lower abdominal pain? Answer: Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT) to rule out an ectopic pregnancy.
* Trap: Ordering CA-125 routinely for all young women with simple ovarian cysts. Pearl: CA-125 is highly non-specific in premenopausal women and easily elevated by menstruation, endometriosis, or fibroids; it should be reserved for suspicious or complex masses.
* Pitfall: Performing a rapid oophorectomy for a blue/black, twisted ovary in a young nulliparous patient. Detorsion (untwisting) must be performed first to allow observation of reperfusion; ovarian conservation is the priority unless the tissue is completely necrotic and crumbling.
* Viva Question: What specific parameters are used to calculate the Risk of Malignancy Index (RMI)? Answer: Ultrasound score (U), Menopausal status (M), and absolute serum CA-125 level.
