# Amenorrhoea & Oligomenorrhoea (閉經與月經稀發)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Diagnostic Criteria for Amenorrhoea (閉經)
* **Primary Amenorrhoea (原發性閉經):** Absence of menarche by age 16 in presence of normal secondary sexual characteristics.
* **Secondary Amenorrhoea (繼發性閉經):** Absence of menses for >6 months in a patient with previously established menstruation.
* **Physiological Amenorrhoea (生理性閉經):**
  * **Pregnancy (妊娠)**
  * **Lactation (哺乳)**
  * **Menopause (絕經)**

### Diagnostic Criteria for Oligomenorrhoea (月經稀發)
* **Definition:** Infrequent menstruation occurring at intervals from 39 days to 6 months.
* **Pathological Spectrum:** Represents a continuum; ranges from normal physiological variation to conditions matching strict amenorrhoea aetiologies (e.g., early-stage endocrine disruption).

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis (下丘腦-垂體軸) Pathophysiology
* **Hypothalamic Dysfunction:** ↓ **Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH)** pulsatility → ↓ **FSH/LH** → chronic anovulation.
  * **Triggers:** Stress, severe weight loss, **Anorexia Nervosa (神經性厭食症)**.
  * **Kallman's Syndrome:** Congenital absence of **GnRH** neurons + anosmia.
* **Pituitary Dysfunction:** 
  * **Hyperprolactinaemia (高泌乳素血症):** ↑ **Prolactin** directly suppresses **GnRH** secretion.
  * **Sheehan's Syndrome (席漢氏綜合症):** Ischaemic necrosis of anterior pituitary secondary to massive post-partum haemorrhage (PPH).

### Ovarian & Outflow Tract (卵巢與流出道) Pathophysiology
* **Primary Ovarian Insufficiency (原發性卵巢功能不全):** Depletion of ovarian follicles before age 40 → ↑ **FSH/LH** (loss of negative feedback) → ↓ **Oestrogen (雌激素)**.
* **Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (多囊卵巢綜合症):** Hyperandrogenism + insulin resistance → altered **LH:FSH** ratio → follicular arrest & anovulation.
* **Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (雄激素不敏感綜合症):** **46,XY** karyotype → testes produce **Testosterone (睾酮)** → peripheral receptor resistance → conversion to **Oestrogen** via aromatase → breast development but absent **Uterus (子宮)**.
* **Müllerian Agenesis / Outflow Obstruction:** Functional **Endometrium (子宮內膜)** sheds but blood accumulates proximally → **Haematocolpos (陰道積血)** / **Haematometra (子宮積血)**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Targetted Gynaecological History
* **Menstrual Hx:** Age of menarche, cycle length, flow volume, sudden vs gradual cessation.
* **Oestrogen Deficiency Sx:** Hot flushes, vaginal dryness, dyspareunia.
* **Hyperandrogenism Sx:** Hirsutism, acne, deepening of voice.
* **Pituitary Sx:** Galactorrhoea, visual field defects, chronic headaches.
* **Metabolic Hx:** Sudden weight loss/gain, intense athletic training, eating disorders.
* **Past Hx:** Previous uterine instrumentation (curettage), pelvic irradiation, chemotherapy, massive PPH.
* **Obstetric/Sexual Hx:** Coital history, contraception use.
* **Pain Sx:** Cyclical lower abdominal pain (suggests outflow obstruction).

### General & Systemic Physical Examination
* **Vital Signs:** BMI (emaciation vs central obesity), BP.
* **Secondary Sexual Characteristics:** Tanner staging (breast development, axillary/pubic hair distribution).
* **Endocrine Signs:** 
  * **Thyroid:** Goitre.
  * **Skin:** Hirsutism, acne, acanthosis nigricans.
  * **Breasts:** Galactorrhoea upon expression.
  * **Neurological:** Anosmia (Kallman's).

### Abdominal & Pelvic Examination Findings
* **Abdomen:** Palpable lower abdominal mass (distended uterus/haematometra).
* **Vulval Inspection:** 
  * **Imperforate Hymen (處女膜閉鎖):** Bulging bluish membrane at introitus.
  * **Clitoris:** Clitoromegaly (severe hyperandrogenism).
* **Speculum Exam:** Vaginal atrophy (smooth, pale mucosa with loss of rugae), absent/short blind-ending vagina.
* **Bimanual Exam:** Absent uterus, cervical anomalies, fixed adnexal masses.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Aetiological Classification Table
| Anatomical Level | Condition | Key Distinguishing Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physiological** | **Pregnancy (妊娠)** | Most common secondary cause; enlarged uterus |
| **Hypothalamic** | Anorexia, Excessive Exercise | Emaciation, normal/low FSH, low Oestrogen |
| **Hypothalamic** | Kallman's Syndrome | Primary amenorrhoea, anosmia, no secondary sexual traits |
| **Pituitary** | Prolactinoma | Galactorrhoea, bitemporal hemianopia |
| **Pituitary** | Sheehan's Syndrome | Hx of severe PPH, failure to lactate post-partum |

| **Ovarian** | **PCOS (多囊卵巢綜合症)** | Obesity, hirsutism, irregular menses |

| **Outflow Tract** | **Asherman Syndrome (阿舍曼綜合症)** | Hx of vigorous curettage/infection, secondary amenorrhoea |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Baseline Laboratory Profiling
* **Pregnancy Test:** Urine/Serum β-hCG (Mandatory 1st line).
* **Pituitary/Ovarian Axis:**
  * **FSH & LH:** ↑ in POI/gonadal dysgenesis; ↓/normal in hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction.
  * **Prolactin:** ↑ in prolactinoma or drug-induced hyperprolactinaemia.
  * **TSH:** ↑/↓ to exclude thyroid dysfunction.
* **Hyperandrogenism Screen:**
  * **Total Testosterone:** ↑ heavily in androgen-secreting tumours.
  * **17-Hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP):** ↑ in Late-Onset Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH).

### Dynamic Hormonal Challenge Tests
* **Progestogen Withdrawal Test:** Oral **Medroxyprogesterone Acetate** (5mg daily x 10 days).
  * **+ve Bleed:** Confirms adequate endogenous oestrogen + patent outflow tract (e.g., PCOS, mild anovulation).
  * **-ve Bleed:** Indicates low oestrogen OR outflow tract obstruction.
* **Oestrogen-Progestogen Challenge Test:** Oral **Premarin** 1.25mg x 21d + **Provera** 5mg (last 10d).
  * **+ve Bleed:** Confirms low endogenous oestrogen; localizes defect to HPO axis.
  * **-ve Bleed:** Confirms uterine/outflow tract defect (e.g., Asherman's).

### Imaging & Cytogenetic Studies
* **Pelvic Ultrasound (USG):**
  * **PCOS Criteria:** $\ge$ 20 antral follicles (2-9mm) per ovary and/or ovarian volume >10mL.
  * **Anatomical:** Confirm presence/absence of uterus, measure endometrial thickness, detect haematocolpos.
* **MRI Pituitary:** Indicated if ↑ Prolactin OR +ve E+P withdrawal with low/normal FSH.
* **Karyotyping:**
  * **45,X or 45,X/46,XX:** Turner Syndrome / Mosaicism.
  * **46,XY:** Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome / Gonadal Dysgenesis.
  * Indicated for POI < 40 yrs or primary amenorrhoea.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization

### Hormone Replacement & Menstrual Regulation
* **Oestrogen Replacement Therapy (ERT):** Essential for hypothalamic amenorrhoea, POI, or gonadal dysgenesis to prevent osteoporosis and maintain cardiovascular/urogenital health.
* **Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COCP):** 
  * Indicated for **PCOS** for cycle regulation and endometrial protection.
  * Cyproterone acetate-containing COCP preferred if severe hirsutism present.
* **Progestogen Therapy:** Periodic oral progestogen (e.g., medroxyprogesterone) for chronic anovulation (PCOS) to induce withdrawal bleeds and prevent endometrial hyperplasia.

### Endocrine-Specific Pharmacology
* **Dopamine Agonists:** Bromocriptine or Cabergoline for Prolactinomas → shrinks tumour, normalizes prolactin, restores menses.
* **Thyroid Hormone Replacement:** Thyroxine for hypothyroidism.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surgical Principles for Outflow Tract Obstruction
* **Imperforate Hymen:** Cruciate incision (hymenotomy) to drain haematocolpos.
* **Transverse Vaginal Septum:** Surgical excision and end-to-end vaginal anastomosis.
* **Asherman Syndrome:** Hysteroscopic adhesiolysis (division of intrauterine synechiae) followed by post-op oestrogen therapy/IUD insertion to prevent recurrence.

### Prophylactic & Reconstructive Gynaecological Surgery
* **Gonadectomy:** Mandatory for patients with **46,XY** karyotype (e.g., AIS, gonadal dysgenesis) due to ↑ risk of gonadoblastoma.
  * *Timing:* Delayed until after complete breast development in complete AIS.
* **Vaginal Agenesis:** Vaginal dilation therapy (1st line) → Operative vaginoplasty if dilation fails.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Contraception & Family Planning
* **Anovulatory Patients:** Do NOT assume sterility. COCP provides dual benefit of contraception + endometrial protection.
* **Hypothalamic Amenorrhoea:** Tailored counseling; if contraception needed, COCP is viable. If fertility desired, lifestyle modification (weight restoration) prior to medical induction.

### Fertility Planning & Ovulation Induction
* **PCOS:** Weight loss (1st line) → Ovulation induction (Letrozole/Clomiphene citrate).
* **Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism:** Requires pulsatile GnRH or exogenous gonadotropins (FSH/LH injections).
* **POI / Turner Syndrome / Gonadal Dysgenesis:** Require **Oocyte Donation (卵子捐贈)** and IVF if pregnancy is desired.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Long-Term Gynaecological & Metabolic Sequelae
* **Unopposed Oestrogen (e.g., PCOS):** Chronic anovulation → Endometrial Hyperplasia (子宮內膜增生) → ↑ risk of **Endometrial Cancer (子宮內膜癌)**.
* **Prolonged Hypoestrogenism (e.g., POI, Anorexia):** → Osteopenia/Osteoporosis, premature cardiovascular disease, urogenital atrophy.
* **Outflow Obstruction (Untreated):** Retrograde menstruation → severe pelvic **Endometriosis (子宮內膜異位症)** → chronic pelvic pain & structural infertility.
* **Gonadal Tumours:** ↑ incidence of dysgerminoma/gonadoblastoma in intra-abdominal dysgenetic gonads (46,XY).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most common cause of secondary amenorrhoea in a reproductive-aged woman? Answer: Pregnancy, which must be excluded first with a β-hCG test.
* Trap: Assuming a patient with primary amenorrhoea and normal breast development has a normal uterus. Pearl: Normal breast development with absent pubic hair and primary amenorrhoea strongly suggests Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (46,XY); always perform a pelvic USG to check for a uterus.
* Pitfall: Prescribing unopposed oestrogen to an amenorrhoeic patient with an intact uterus. Always add cyclical progestogen to prevent endometrial hyperplasia and malignancy.
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate between POI and hypothalamic amenorrhoea using baseline labs? Answer: POI presents with elevated FSH (hypergonadotropic), whereas hypothalamic amenorrhoea presents with low or normal FSH (hypogonadotropic).
* Trap: Delaying gonadectomy indefinitely in patients with Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome. Pearl: Gonads must be surgically removed after the completion of breast development to eliminate the high risk of malignant gonadoblastoma.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing primary ovarian insufficiency based on a single FSH reading. FSH must be elevated to menopausal levels on at least two occasions in a woman under 40 years old.
