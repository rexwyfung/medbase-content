# Amenorrhoea and Oligomenorrhoea (閉經與月經過少)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition and Types
* **Primary amenorrhoea:** no menstruation by age 15 with normal secondary sexual characteristics, or no secondary sexual characteristics by age 13
* **Secondary amenorrhoea:** absence of menstruation >6 months in previously menstruating woman
* **Oligomenorrhoea:** infrequent menstruation, cycle length >38 days

### Epidemiology and Clinical Burden
* Commonest causes:
  * Pregnancy
  * PCOS
  * Hypothalamic amenorrhoea
  * Thyroid dysfunction
  * Hyperprolactinaemia
* Always exclude pregnancy first

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Ovarian Axis
* Hypothalamus releases **GnRH**
* Pituitary releases **FSH and LH**
* Ovarian follicles produce **oestrogen and inhibin**
* Endometrium proliferates under oestrogen
* Progesterone from corpus luteum stabilises endometrium

### Causes by Anatomical Level
| Level | Causes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypothalamus** | Stress, weight loss, excessive exercise, anorexia |
| **Pituitary** | Prolactinoma, Sheehan syndrome, drugs |
| **Ovary** | POI, Turner, chemotherapy, surgery |
| **Uterus/outflow** | Asherman, imperforate hymen, absent uterus |
| **Systemic** | Thyroid disease, diabetes, chronic illness |

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Key History
* Age of menarche and last menstrual period
* Pregnancy, lactation, contraception
* Weight change, diet, exercise
* Galactorrhoea
* Hot flushes
* Acne or hirsutism
* Stress and chronic illness
* Previous uterine surgery
* Family history of POI

### Examination
* BMI
* Hirsutism or acne
* Thyroid enlargement
* Visual field defects
* Breast development and galactorrhoea
* Ambiguous genitalia
* Uterine size and outflow tract
* Clitoromegaly

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Common Differential Diagnoses
* Pregnancy
* PCOS
* Primary ovarian insufficiency
* Functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea
* Hyperprolactinaemia
* Thyroid dysfunction
* Asherman syndrome
* Müllerian anomaly
* Androgen insensitivity syndrome

### PCOS Diagnostic Criteria
Rotterdam criteria: **2 of 3**
* Oligo/anovulation
* Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism
* Polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Essential Laboratory Tests
* Pregnancy test
* FSH
* LH
* Oestradiol
* Prolactin
* TSH
* Testosterone/SHBG if hyperandrogenism
* 17-OHP if congenital adrenal hyperplasia suspected

### Imaging
* Pelvic ultrasound:
  * PCO morphology
  * Uterine anomaly
  * Ovarian cyst
* MRI pituitary if:
  * Prolactin elevated
  * Visual field defect
  * Headache

### Karyotype Indications
* Primary amenorrhoea
* Primary ovarian insufficiency <30 years
* Short stature or absent uterus
* Suspected Turner or androgen insensitivity

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Pregnancy
* Confirm intrauterine pregnancy
* Exclude ectopic if pain or bleeding

### Hormonal Therapy
* **PCOS:** combined oral contraceptive or cyclical progestogen
* **Hypothalamic:** weight restoration, reduce exercise
* **Hyperprolactinaemia:** cabergoline or bromocriptine
* **POI:** hormone replacement therapy until age 50
* **Asherman:** hysteroscopic adhesiolysis + oestrogen therapy

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Management by Cause
* **PCOS:** lifestyle, OCP, fertility treatment if desired
* **Prolactinoma:** dopamine agonist; surgery if resistant
* **POI:** HRT and fertility counselling
* **Asherman:** hysteroscopic adhesiolysis
* **Imperforate hymen:** surgical incision
* **Androgen insensitivity:** gonadectomy after breast development

### Fertility Considerations
* PCOS: letrozole or clomiphene
* POI: donor oocyte
* Asherman: adhesiolysis followed by oestrogen
* Hypothalamic: weight gain; gonadotropins if needed

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After Hormonal Treatment
* Monitor bleeding pattern
* Assess side effects
* Regular BP and BMI
* Bone density if prolonged hypoestrogenism

### After Surgery
* Adhesiolysis: oestrogen therapy and follow-up hysteroscopy
* Gonadectomy: HRT after surgery
* Pituitary surgery: monitor hormone function

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Infertility
* Osteoporosis
* Cardiovascular risk in POI
* Endometrial hyperplasia from unopposed oestrogen
* Diabetes in PCOS
* Psychological distress

### Gynaecological Complications
* Recurrent adhesions
| Uterine perforation during adhesiolysis
* Ovarian failure after surgery/chemotherapy
* Gonadal tumour in androgen insensitivity

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Check pregnancy before any endocrine testing
* Diagnose POI if FSH raised on two occasions before age 40
* Arrange karyotype for primary amenorrhoea, absent uterus, or POI <30
* Use HRT until age 50 in POI if no contraindication
* Refer to fertility clinic if pregnancy desired after 12 months of anovulation

* Exclude pregnancy before endocrine testing
* Diagnose POI if FSH is raised on two occasions before age 40
* Check karyotype for primary amenorrhoea, absent uterus, or POI <30
* Use HRT until age 50 in POI if no contraindication
* Refer to fertility clinic if pregnancy desired and cycles remain anovulatory
* Assess bone density if amenorrhoea >6 months

* Check prolactin if galactorrhoea or headaches are present
* Arrange MRI if prolactin is raised or visual field defect is found
* Check 17-OHP if hirsutism, oligomenorrhoea, or clitoromegaly suggests CAH
* Consider hypothalamic amenorrhoea if BMI <18.5 or excessive exercise
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are Rotterdam criteria for PCOS? Answer: 2 of 3: oligo/anovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovarian morphology.
* Trap: Forgetting pregnancy as commonest cause of secondary amenorrhoea.
* Pitfall: Missing primary ovarian insufficiency before age 40. Check FSH and karyotype if <30.
* Viva Question: When should karyotype be performed? Answer: Primary amenorrhoea, POI <30, short stature, or absent uterus.
* Pitfall: Ignoring bone health in prolonged hypoestrogenism. Assess BMD and consider HRT.
