# Malpresentation (胎位不正)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition and Key Presentations
* **Malpresentation:** any presentation other than cephalic vertex
* Common forms:
  * **Breech:** buttocks or feet presenting
  * **Transverse lie:** fetal long axis perpendicular to maternal long axis
  * **Oblique lie:** fetal long axis oblique to maternal long axis
  * **Unstable lie:** presentation changes repeatedly after 36 weeks
  * **Face or brow presentation**

### Gestational Context and Incidence
* Breech is common before 28-30 weeks
* Most fetuses spontaneously convert to cephalic by term
* At term, breech occurs in ~3-4% of singleton pregnancies
* Persistent malpresentation increases risk of:
  * Cord prolapse
  * Obstructed labour
  * Birth injury
  * Emergency caesarean section

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Maternal Factors predisposing to Malpresentation
* Uterine anomalies
* Fibroids distorting cavity
* Placenta praevia
* Pelvic contraction
* Multiparity with lax uterus
* Previous caesarean section

### Fetal Factors predisposing to Malpresentation
* Preterm gestation
* Multiple pregnancy
* Polyhydramnios
* Oligohydramnios
* Fetal anomaly
* Macrosomia
* Short cord or cord around neck

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Abdominal Findings in Malpresentation
* Fundal palpation: head or breech at fundus
* Pelvic palpation: presenting part may be soft/irregular
* **Breech:** head felt in fundus, buttocks above pelvis
* **Transverse lie:** fetus lies across abdomen; shoulder may present
* **Unstable lie:** presentation changes between visits

### Speculum and Bimanual Findings
* Cervix may be uneffaced and high
* Presenting part may not be engaged
* Cord or limbs may be visible if membranes ruptured
* Assess for:
  * Cervical dilation
  * Cord prolapse
  * Bulging membranes

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis of Abnormal Lie or Presentation
* Breech presentation
* Transverse or oblique lie
* Face or brow presentation
* Compound presentation
* Cord prolapse
* Placenta praevia causing high head
* Fibroid preventing engagement

### Breech Classification
| Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Frank breech** | Hips flexed, knees extended |
| **Complete breech** | Hips and knees flexed |
| **Incomplete/footling breech** | One or both feet presenting |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Ultrasound Assessment
* Confirm presentation
* Assess type of breech
* Estimated fetal weight
* Amniotic fluid volume
* Placental site
* Fetal head flexion
* Cord position
* Uterine anomalies or fibroids

### CTG Findings
* Normal if uncomplicated
* Abnormal if:
  * Cord prolapse
  * Placental abruption
  * Obstructed labour

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Immediate Management if Malpresentation in Labour
* Call senior obstetrician
* Continuous CTG
* Left lateral position
* Avoid artificial rupture of membranes if presenting part not engaged
* Prepare for emergency caesarean if:
  * Cord prolapse
  * Fetal distress
  * Obstructed labour
  * Transverse lie

### Tocolysis and Steroids
* Nifedipine or atosiban if urgent caesarean transfer needed
* Corticosteroids if preterm delivery anticipated

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### External Cephalic Version
* Offer at **36-37 weeks** for term singleton breech
* Contraindications:
  * Placenta praevia
  * Multiple pregnancy
  * Severe FGR
  * Ruptured membranes
  * Previous caesarean section is relative

### Mode of Delivery
* **Planned caesarean** usually preferred for:
  * Term frank/complete breech if EFW abnormal or head extension
  * Footling breech
  * Transverse or oblique lie
  * Unstable lie at term
* **Vaginal breech delivery** may be considered if:
  * Frank breech
  * Adequate pelvis
  * Normal growth
  * Flexed head
  * No other contraindication

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Postpartum Care after Vaginal Breech
* Watch for:
  * Birth trauma
  * Hip dysplasia
  * Cord injury
  * Perineal trauma

### Postoperative Care after Caesarean
* Standard caesarean care
* Thromboprophylaxis
* Support breastfeeding
* Discuss contraception

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Obstructed labour
* Uterine rupture
* Emergency caesarean section
* Postpartum haemorrhage
* Perineal trauma

### Fetal and Neonatal Complications
* Cord prolapse
* Birth asphyxia
* Brachial plexus injury
* Hip dysplasia
* Intracranial haemorrhage
* Preterm birth

### Escalation and Decision Thresholds
* Offer external cephalic version at 36-37 weeks if singleton breech and no contraindication
* Avoid ARM until presenting part is engaged
* Prepare for caesarean if footling breech, transverse lie, or cord prolapse
* Assess head extension and estimated fetal weight before planned vaginal breech birth
* Recheck presentation at 36-37 weeks in unstable lie

* Confirm presentation and placental site before offering external cephalic version
* Book ECV at 36-37 weeks with CTG and theatre available
* Avoid ARM if presenting part is unengaged
* Offer planned caesarean for footling breech, transverse lie, or hyperextended head
* Prepare for shoulder dystocia if macrosomia or previous difficult delivery
* Reassess presentation at 36-37 weeks in unstable lie

* Confirm placental site and amniotic fluid volume before external cephalic version
* Avoid version if previous caesarean section, fetal compromise, or major fetal anomaly is present
* Monitor CTG before and after version
* Suspect cord prolapse if membranes rupture with a high or mobile presenting part
* Consider uterine anomaly if malpresentation is persistent or recurrent
* Check fetal head extension before planning vaginal breech birth
* Prepare for emergency caesarean during external cephalic version
## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the optimal timing for external cephalic version? Answer: 36-37 weeks.
* Trap: All breech presentations require caesarean section. Pearl: Selected frank breech cases may deliver vaginally.
* Pitfall: Performing ARM before engagement. This risks cord prolapse.
