# Malpresentation (胎位不正)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Malpresentation
* **Definition**: Any fetal presentation other than **Vertex (頂先露)**.
* **Types**: **Breech (臀先露)**, **Shoulder (肩先露)**, **Face (面先露)**, **Brow (眉頭先露)**, **Compound (複合先露)**.

### Gestational Context & Epidemiology of Malpresentation
* **Breech Incidence**: ~3% at term.
* **Shoulder Incidence**: 1:200 deliveries at term.
* **Gestational Effect**: Fetal position unstable before term; prematurity is the most common association.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Maternal & Pelvic Causes of Malpresentation
* **Lax Abdominal Wall**: High parity → ↓ uterine tone → persistent unstable lie.
* **Pelvic Abnormalities**: Contracted pelvis, pelvic tumours (e.g., cervical fibroids) preventing normal head engagement.
* **Uterine Abnormalities**: Bicornuate/septate uterus restricting fetal turning.

### Fetal & Placental Causes of Malpresentation
* **Prematurity**: Fetus too small relative to amniotic fluid volume to maintain longitudinal cephalic lie.
* **Multiple Pregnancy (多胞胎)**: Restricted spatial accommodation.
* **Fetal Abnormality**: Hydrocephalus, neck masses, or neuromuscular conditions preventing neck flexion.
* **Amniotic Fluid Extremes**: **Polyhydramnios (羊水過多)** (excessive mobility) or **Oligohydramnios (羊水過少)** (restricted mobility).
* **Placenta Praevia (胎盤前置)**: Physically blocks pelvic inlet, preventing head engagement.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History & Symptoms of Malpresentation
* **History**: Multiparity, previous malpresentation, known uterine fibroids/anomalies.
* **Symptoms**: Often asymptomatic; may present with subcostal discomfort (fetal head in fundus).

### Abdominal Examination Findings in Breech Presentation
* **Inspection**: Longitudinal lie.
* **Palpation (Fundus)**: Hard, round, ballotable mass (fetal head) palpable in upper uterine pole.
* **Palpation (Pelvis)**: Softer, irregular mass (buttocks) at or above pelvic brim.

### Abdominal Examination Findings in Shoulder/Transverse Presentation
* **Inspection**: Uterus appears unusually wide; fundal height < expected for GA.
* **Palpation (Fundus & Pelvis)**: Both poles empty.
* **Palpation (Flanks)**: Fetal head palpable in maternal flank or iliac fossa.

### Intrapartum Vaginal Examination Findings in Malpresentation
* **Breech**: Sacrum, ischial tuberosities, or fetal feet felt upon cervical dilatation.
* **Face**: Facial features (nose, mouth, malar eminences, mentum) palpable.
* **Brow**: Frontal sutures, anterior fontanelle, orbital ridges palpable.
* **Compound**: Fetal hand/arm prolapsed alongside presenting head or breech.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Categorization of Malpresentations
| Presentation | Lie | Presenting Part | Distinguishing Clinical Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Breech** | Longitudinal | Buttocks/Feet | Head ballotable in fundus |
| **Shoulder** | Transverse/Oblique | Scapula/Acromion | Head in flank, empty fundus/pelvis |
| **Face** | Longitudinal | Mentum (Chin) | Neck fully extended; mentum guides delivery |
| **Brow** | Longitudinal | Frontal bone | Neck partially extended; widest diameter (mentovertical) |
| **Compound** | Varies | Extremity + Head/Breech | Prolapsed limb beside main presenting part |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Antenatal Ultrasound Criteria for Malpresentation
* **Presentation Confirmation**: Direct visualization of presenting part at internal os.
* **Etiological Screening**: Assess placental localization (exclude **Placenta Praevia**), amniotic fluid index (exclude poly/oligohydramnios).
* **Fetal Anomaly Screening**: Detailed morphology to exclude structural defects causing malpresentation.

### Intrapartum Cardiotocography (CTG) Tracings
* **Baseline & Variability**: Usually normal unless complications arise.
* **Variable Decelerations**: High risk for cord compression, especially in compound or unengaged breech/shoulder presentations with ruptured membranes.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Stabilization for Cord Prolapse
* **Maternal Positioning**: Knee-chest position or extreme Trendelenburg.
* **Manual Elevation**: Elevate presenting part digitally via vagina to offload cord compression.
* **Tocolysis**: Consider subcutaneous **Terbutaline** to halt contractions temporarily pending emergency LSCS.

### Pharmacological Preparation for External Cephalic Version (ECV)
* **Uterine Relaxation**: Tocolysis (e.g., **Terbutaline** or **Nifedipine**) may be administered prior to **External Cephalic Version (ECV)** to relax myometrium and ↑ success rate.
* **Anti-D Immunoglobulin**: Mandatory post-ECV for Rh-ve women to prevent isoimmunization.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Definitive Management Algorithm for Breech Presentation
* **GA 37 Weeks**: Counsel for **External Cephalic Version (ECV)**.
* **Successful ECV**: Await spontaneous labour.
* **Failed/Declined ECV**: Plan **Elective Lower Segment Caesarean Section (LSCS) (擇期剖宮產)**.
* **Vaginal Breech Delivery**: Highly selected cases; carries ↑ risk of neonatal complications.

### Definitive Management Algorithm for Shoulder/Transverse Presentation
* **GA 37 Weeks**: Mandatory admission due to high risk of **Cord Prolapse (臍帶脫垂)** upon spontaneous rupture of membranes.
* **GA 39 Weeks**: Attempt ECV if no contraindications.
* **Successful ECV**: Immediate **Stabilizing Induction (穩定催產)** (Artificial Rupture of Membranes + Oxytocin infusion) to fix longitudinal lie.
* **Failed ECV / Persistent Transverse Lie**: Proceed to **Elective LSCS**.

### Mode of Delivery for Face, Brow & Compound Presentations
* **Face Presentation**: Mento-anterior position → Vaginal delivery possible. Mento-posterior position → **LSCS**.
* **Brow Presentation**: Vaginal delivery usually impossible due to mentovertical diameter → **LSCS**.
* **Compound Presentation**: Vaginal delivery usually not possible → **LSCS**.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-partum Care Following Malpresentation Delivery
* **PPH Prevention**: Active management of 3rd stage; ↑ risk of uterine atony post-LSCS or prolonged labour.
* **Neonatal Assessment**: Paediatric review for birth trauma (e.g., brachial plexus injury, fractures) following complex vaginal deliveries.
* **VTE Prophylaxis**: LMWH per risk stratification, especially post-LSCS.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications of Malpresentation
* **Operative Morbidity**: ↑ rates of emergency LSCS, leading to ↑ risk of haemorrhage, infection, and uterine scar for future pregnancies.
* **Genital Tract Trauma**: ↑ risk of severe perineal/cervical lacerations during difficult vaginal breech or instrumental deliveries.

### Fetal & Neonatal Complications of Malpresentation
* **Cord Prolapse (臍帶脫垂)**: Especially critical in transverse lie or footling breech.
* **Birth Trauma**: Fractures (clavicle, humerus), brachial plexus injury, or hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) during difficult extraction.

### Complications of External Cephalic Version (ECV)
* **Fetal Distress / Demise**: Requires emergency LSCS.
* **Preterm Labour**: Triggered by uterine manipulation.
* **Abruptio Placentae (胎盤早剝)**: Shearing forces during version causing placental separation.
* **Fetomaternal Haemorrhage**: Evident by vaginal bleeding.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the management of a persistent transverse lie at 37 weeks?
  Answer: Admit to hospital due to high risk of cord prolapse upon membrane rupture, plan ECV at 39 weeks.
* Trap: Assuming all face presentations require LSCS.
  Pearl: Face presentation with mento-anterior position can deliver vaginally; only mento-posterior explicitly requires LSCS.
* Pitfall: Attempting stabilizing induction in a breech presentation.
  Stabilizing induction (ARM + oxytocin) is specifically used for unstable/transverse lie AFTER successful ECV to fix the longitudinal cephalic pole in the pelvis.
