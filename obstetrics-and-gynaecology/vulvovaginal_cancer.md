# Vulvovaginal Cancer (外陰與陰道癌)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definitions and Primary Sites
* **Vulvar cancer:** malignant tumour of the vulva, including:
  * Labia majora/minora
  * Clitoris
  * Vestibule
  * Perineum
* **Vaginal cancer:** primary malignant tumour of vaginal epithelium
* Vaginal cancer is rare and often secondary to cervical, endometrial, or rectal cancer

### Local Epidemiology
* Most vulvar cancers occur in postmenopausal women
* Two main pathways:
  * HPV-associated, particularly type 16
  * Non-HPV-associated via vulvar dystrophy/lichen sclerosus
* Primary vaginal cancer is rare
* Most vaginal malignancies are metastatic

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Falf Physiology)

### Vulvar Carcinogenesis Pathways
* **HPV pathway:**
  * High-risk HPV infection
  * VIN usual type
  * Basaloid or warty carcinoma
* **Lichen sclerosus pathway:**
  * Chronic inflammation and vulvar dystrophy
  * Differentiated VIN
  * Keratinising squamous cell carcinoma

### Vaginal Carcinogenesis
* Usually squamous cell carcinoma
* Associated with:
  * HPV infection
  * Prior cervical intraepithelial neoplasia
  * Pelvic radiotherapy
  * Chronic irritation
* Clear cell adenocarcinoma is associated with intrauterine diethylstilboestrol exposure, now rare

### Lymphatic Drainage
* Vulva:
  * Superficial inguinal nodes
  * Deep inguinal nodes
  * Pelvic nodes
* Upper vagina:
  * Pelvic nodes
* Lower vagina:
  * Inguinal nodes

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Vulvar Symptoms
* Persistent itch
* Vulvar pain or burning
* Lump or ulcer
* Bleeding or discharge
* Dysuria
* Dyspareunia
* Non-healing lesion despite treatment

### Vaginal Symptoms
* Painless or painful vaginal bleeding
* Postcoital bleeding
| Offensive discharge
* Pelvic pain
* Urinary or rectal symptoms in advanced disease

### Examination Findings
* Vulvar:
  * Raised plaque, ulcer, or fungating mass
  * Bleeding on contact
  * Lichen sclerosus features:
    * Thin white skin
    * Scarring
    * Labial fusion
  * Inguinal lymphadenopathy
* Vaginal:
  * Ulcerated or exophytic lesion
  * Fixed vaginal wall mass
  * Vesicovaginal or rectovaginal fistula if advanced
  * Bimanual tenderness or pelvic mass

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Vulvar Differential Diagnosis
* Lichen sclerosus
* Vulvar intraepithelial neoplasia
* Vulvar dermatitis
* Bartholin cyst/abscess
* Condyloma acuminatum
* Basal cell carcinoma
* Melanoma
| Paget disease of vulva

### Vaginal Differential Diagnosis
* Vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia
* Cervical cancer extension
* Endometrial cancer metastasis
* Rectal cancer invasion
* Vaginal polyp
| Vaginitis or ulcer
* Sarcoma

### FIGO Vulvar Cancer Staging
| Stage | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **I** | Tumour confined to vulva/perineum |
| **IA** | ≤2 cm and stromal invasion ≤1 mm |
| **IB** | >2 cm or invasion >1 mm |
| **II** | Extension to lower urethra/vagina/anus |
| **III** | Inguinofemoral lymph node metastasis |
| **IVA** | Fixed/ulcerated nodes or upper urethra/bladder/rectal invasion |
| **IVB** | Distant metastasis |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Biopsy
* **Incisional or excisional biopsy** is mandatory for vulvar lesions
* Include dermis to assess invasion depth
* Colposcopy and directed biopsy for vaginal lesions
* Biopsy any persistent vulvar lesion unresponsive to topical treatment

### Imaging
* Pelvic MRI:
  * Tumour size
  * Depth of invasion
  * Vaginal/urethral/anal involvement
  * Inguinal and pelvic nodes
* CT chest/abdomen/pelvis if node-positive or advanced disease
* PET-CT for recurrent or metastatic disease
* Cystoscopy/protoscopy if bladder or rectal involvement suspected

### Coinfection and Systemic Screening
* CBC and renal function
* HIV testing if HPV-associated risk factors
| HPV testing may inform aetiology
* Sentinal lymph node mapping is increasingly used in early vulvar cancer

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Pre-invasive Disease
* VIN:
  * Local excision
  * Laser ablation
  * Topical imiquimod in selected cases
* VAIN:
  * Observation for low-grade lesions
  * Laser or excision for high-grade lesions

### Symptom Control
* Analgesia
* Topical oestrogen if atrophic vaginitis coexists
* Treat secondary infection
* Manage bleeding with packing or tranexamic acid if severe

### Neoadjuvant Therapy
Consider if:
* Large tumour involving urethra/anus where primary surgery would cause major morbidity
* Selected locally advanced squamous cancers
* Chemoradiotherapy may shrink tumour before surgery

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Early Vulvar Cancer
* **Wide local excision** with at least 1-2 cm margin
* **Unilateral inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy** for lateral tumours
* **Bilateral lymphadenectomy** for midline lesions
* **Sentinel node biopsy** may be considered in experienced centres for small unifocal tumours without suspicious nodes

### Advanced Vulvar Cancer
* Radical vulvectomy with bilateral groin node dissection
* Pelvic exenteration in highly selected central recurrences
* Chemoradiotherapy for:
  * Unresectable disease
  * Fixed nodes
  * Bulky disease to preserve bladder/rectum

### Vaginal Cancer
* Upper vaginal tumours:
  * Surgery if early and superficial
  * Radiotherapy if deeper or extensive
* Lower vaginal tumours:
  * Often treated like vulvar cancer if localized
* Chemoradiotherapy for advanced disease

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-operative Vulvar Care
* Wound care and infection prevention
* Monitor:
  * Wound breakdown
  * Lymphocyst
  * Lymphoedema
  * Deep vein thrombosis
* Physiotherapy and pelvic floor rehabilitation
| Sexual function counselling

### Surveillance
* Clinical examination every 3 months initially
* Biopsy any new or persistent lesion
* Long-term follow-up for lichen sclerosus, VIN, or VAIN
* Smoking cessation and HPV vaccination counselling

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal and Surgical Complications
* Groin wound breakdown
* Lymphoedema
| Cellulitis
* DVT/PE
* Urethral or anal dysfunction
* Vesicovaginal/rectovaginal fistula
* Psychological and psychosexual distress

### Gynaecological Complications
* Recurrence
* Vaginal stenosis after radiotherapy
* Dyspareunia
* Loss of sexual function
| Chronic pelvic pain

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What defines stage IA vulvar cancer? Answer: Tumour ≤2 cm with stromal invasion ≤1 mm and no nodal spread.
* Trap: Treating a persistent vulvar ulcer as dermatitis without biopsy. Pearl: Biopsy any non-healing vulvar lesion.
* Pitfall: Performing unilateral groin dissection for a midline tumour. Pearl: Midline tumours drain bilaterally.
* Viva Question: Why is vulvar cancer associated with lichen sclerosus? Answer: Chronic inflammation predisposes to differentiated VIN and squamous carcinoma.
* Pitfall: Failing to examine inguinal nodes. Nodal status is the strongest prognostic factor.
* Trap: Assuming vaginal cancer is primary. Exclude cervical, endometrial, and rectal primary tumours first.
