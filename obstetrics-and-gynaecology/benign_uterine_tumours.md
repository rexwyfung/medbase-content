# Benign Uterine Tumours (良性子宮腫瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Main Tumour Types
* **Uterine fibroids (leiomyomas):** benign monoclonal smooth muscle tumours
* **Endometrial polyps:** localized overgrowth of endometrial glands and stroma
* **Adenomyosis:** endometrial glands and stroma within myometrium
* Fibroids occur in 20-40% of reproductive-age women
* More common with increasing age until menopause

### Clinical Significance
* Many women are asymptomatic
* Important causes of:
  * Heavy menstrual bleeding
  * Pelvic pressure
  | Infertility
  * Recurrent miscarriage
  * Pregnancy complications

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Fibroid Biology
* Monoclonal smooth muscle proliferation with extracellular matrix
* Oestrogen and progesterone responsive
* Usually shrink after menopause
* Karyotypic abnormalities include:
  * Translocation t(12;14)
  * Trisomy 12
  * MED12 mutations

### Fibroid Location
* **Submucosal:** bleeding and fertility impact
* **Intramural:** bulk symptoms and pain
* **Subserosal:** pressure effects
* **Pedunculated:** torsion risk
* **Intraligamentous:** ureteric compression

### Adenomyosis and Polyps
* Adenomyosis:
  * Endometrial tissue invades myometrium
  * Globular, tender uterus
  * Dysmenorrhoea and heavy bleeding
* Endometrial polyps:
  * Oestrogen-driven hyperplasia
  * Intermenstrual or postcoital bleeding

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Key Symptoms
* Heavy or prolonged menstruation
* Inter menstrual bleeding
* Postcoital bleeding
* Pelvic pain or pressure
* Urinary frequency or retention
* Constipation
* Backache
| Infertility or recurrent pregnancy loss
* Rapid postmenopausal growth is a red flag

### Examination Findings
* Palpable pelvic mass
* Enlarged, irregular uterus if fibroids
* Bulky, tender, symmetric uterus if adenomyosis
* Cervical polyp visible on speculum
* Anaemia from chronic blood loss

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
* Leiomyoma
* Adenomyosis
* Endometrial polyp
* Uterine sarcoma
* Endometrial cancer
* Ovarian tumour
* Pregnancy
* Adenomyosis

### FIGO Fibroid Classification
* **Type 0:** pedunculated submucosal
* **Type 1:** submucosal with <50% intramural extension
* **Type 2:** submucosal with ≥50% intramural extension
* **Types 3-8:** progressively deeper or serosal fibroids
* Type 0-2 most affect bleeding and fertility

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Ultrasound Findings
* Fibroids:
  * Well-defined hypoechoic round masses
  * Whorled appearance
  * Acoustic shadowing
  * Peripheral vascularity
* Adenomyosis:
  * Globular uterus
  * Myometrial cysts
  * Asymmetric wall thickening
  * Linear striations
* Polyps:
  * Focal endometrial thickening
  * Feeding vessel on Doppler
* MRI useful for complex mapping before surgery

### Biopsy
* Endometrial sampling if:
  * Age >45
  * Persistent abnormal bleeding
  * PCOS or obesity
  * Failed medical therapy
* Hysteroscopy and directed biopsy if focal lesion suspected

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Symptom Control
* Tranexamic acid
* NSAIDs
* Combined oral contraceptive
* Levonorgestrel intrauterine system
| Oral progestogen
* GnRH agonist for temporary shrinkage before surgery

### Acute Pain Management
* NSAIDs for degenerating fibroid
* Analgesia
* Exclude torsion or red degeneration in pregnancy
* Blood transfusion if severe anaemia

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surgical Options
* **Hysteroscopic myomectomy:** submucosal fibroids
* **Laparoscopic or open myomectomy:** fertility-sparing
* **Uterine artery embolisation:** selected fibroids
* **Hysterectomy:** definitive if family complete

### Fertility-Sparing Approach
* Hysteroscopic resection for FIGO Type 0-2 fibroids
* Laparoscopic myomectomy for intramural or subserosal fibroids
* Avoid embolisation if pregnancy desired
* Use GnRH agonist preoperatively only for short-term size reduction

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After Myomectomy
* Monitor bleeding and infection
* Avoid pregnancy for 3-6 months depending on surgical approach
* Consider caesarean delivery after deep myomectomy
* Assess adhesion risk

### After Hysterectomy
* Thromboprophylaxis
* Ovarian conservation if appropriate
* Histology review
* HRT if oophorectomy and symptoms

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Anaemia
* Pressure symptoms
* Infertility
* Pregnancy loss
* Red degeneration
* Torsion of pedunculated fibroid
* Uterine rupture after myomectomy

### Fetal Complications
* Malpresentation
* Preterm labour
* Placental abruption
* Fetal growth restriction

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: Which fibroids most affect fertility and bleeding? Answer: Submucosal fibroids, especially FIGO Type 0-2.
* Trap: Using uterine artery embolisation in women desiring pregnancy. Pearl: Myomectomy is preferred.
* Pitfall: Assuming rapid postmenopausal growth is benign. Consider sarcoma.
* Viva Question: What ultrasound features suggest adenomyosis? Answer: Globular uterus, myometrial cysts, asymmetric thickening, linear striations.
* Pitfall: Missing endometrial cancer in women over 45 with abnormal bleeding. Send tissue for histology.
