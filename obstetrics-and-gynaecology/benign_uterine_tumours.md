# Benign Uterine Tumours (良性子宮腫瘤)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Benign Uterine Tumours
* **Leiomyoma (子宮肌瘤)**: Benign monoclonal tumour of myometrial smooth muscle cells.
* Commonly referred to as uterine fibroids.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* Highly prevalent in Southern Chinese women.
* Peak incidence in late reproductive age (30-50 years).
* Spontaneous regression usually occurs post-menopause.

### Gestational Context & Pregnancy Interactions
* $\uparrow$ growth during pregnancy due to high **Estrogen (雌激素)** and **Progesterone (黃體酮)** levels.
* Highly susceptible to **Red Degeneration (紅色變性)** in mid-trimester.
* Can cause malpresentation, obstructed labour, or $\uparrow$ risk of postpartum haemorrhage.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Endocrine Axis & Hormone Dependence
* Tumour growth is strictly driven by **Estrogen (雌激素)** and **Progesterone (黃體酮)**.
* High local aromatase activity within the fibroid converts androgens to estrogens.
* Atrophy occurs in hypoestrogenic states (e.g., menopause, GnRHa therapy).

### Uterine Anatomy & Tumour Locations
* **Submucosal (黏膜下)**: Distorts the uterine cavity; most prone to bleeding.
* **Intramural (肌層內)**: Confined within the myometrium; causes global uterine enlargement.
* **Subserosal (漿膜下)**: Projects outward from the serosa into the pelvic cavity.
* **Pedunculated (帶蒂)**: Attached via a stalk; prone to torsion.
* **Cervical (子宮頸)**: Arises in the cervix; can obstruct the birth canal.

### Pathological Degenerative Changes
* **Hyaline Degeneration**: Most common; tissue becomes acellular, asymptomatic.
* **Red Degeneration (紅色變性)**: Haemorrhagic infarction during pregnancy due to rapid growth outstripping blood supply.
* **Calcific Degeneration**: Common post-menopause.
* **Sarcomatous Change**: Malignant transformation to leiomyosarcoma is extremely rare (<0.1%).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Menstrual & Gynaecological History
* **Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (經血過多)** 
* Passage of large clots
* Flooding sensation
* Secondary **Dysmenorrhoea (經痛)**
* Intermenstrual bleeding (less common, usually submucosal)

### Pressure Symptoms History
* Anterior pressure $\to$ Urinary frequency, urgency, retention.
* Posterior pressure $\to$ Constipation, tenesmus.
* Pelvic heaviness/dragging sensation.

### Acute Symptoms History
* Acute, severe, constant abdominal pain (indicates torsion of pedunculated fibroid or red degeneration).
* Low-grade fever (associated with red degeneration).

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* Firm, irregular, central lower abdominal mass.
* Arises from the pelvis (cannot get below the mass).
* Dull to percussion.
* Localized focal tenderness if undergoing active degeneration.

### Speculum Examination Findings
* Usually normal cervix and vaginal walls.
* May visualize a prolapsed pedunculated submucosal fibroid extruding through the external cervical os.

### Bimanual Examination Findings
* Enlarged, firm, irregularly contoured, non-tender uterus.
* Mass moves continuously with the cervix.
* Pedunculated fibroid may feel like an adnexal mass but moves synchronously with the uterus.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnoses of Pelvic Mass
| Diagnosis                      | Distinguishing Clinical Features                                    |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Leiomyoma (子宮肌瘤)**           | Firm, irregular, non-tender, moves with cervix                      |
| **Adenomyosis (子宮腺肌病)**        | Globally enlarged, tender, "boggy" uterus, severe dysmenorrhoea     |
| **Ovarian Tumour (卵巢腫瘤)**      | Distinct from uterus, does not move with cervix, adnexal origin     |
| **Pregnancy (妊娠)**             | Soft, symmetrically enlarged uterus, +ve amenorrhoea                |
| **Endometrial Cancer (子宮內膜癌)** | Irregular bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding, prominent in older age |

### FIGO Classification System for Leiomyomas
| FIGO Type | Anatomical Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Type 0 | Pedunculated intracavitary |
| Type 1 | Submucosal, <50% intramural |
| Type 2 | Submucosal, $\ge$50% intramural |
| Type 3 | 100% intramural, contacts endometrium |
| Type 4 | Intramural |
| Type 5 | Subserosal, $\ge$50% intramural |
| Type 6 | Subserosal, <50% intramural |
| Type 7 | Pedunculated subserosal |
| Type 8 | Other (e.g., cervical, parasitic) |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Laboratory Investigations
* Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT) $\to$ Exclude pregnancy.
* CBP $\to$ $\downarrow$ Hb, $\downarrow$ MCV (Iron-deficiency anaemia secondary to chronic blood loss).
* Iron profile $\to$ $\downarrow$ Ferritin.

### First-Line Pelvic Ultrasound (USG) Criteria
* Solid, well-circumscribed, hypoechoic myometrial mass(es).
* Peripheral vascularity on color Doppler.
* Calcifications casting acoustic shadows (especially in older women).

### Advanced Endometrial/Cavity Imaging
* Saline Infusion Sonohysterography (SIS) $\to$ Clearly visualizes submucosal fibroids projecting into a fluid-filled cavity.
* MRI Pelvis $\to$ Precise anatomical mapping required for complex surgery or UAE planning.

### Endometrial Assessment Criteria
* Endometrial Aspiration (Pipelle) $\to$ Mandatory if age $\ge$40 or AUB risk factors are present to exclude **Endometrial Hyperplasia (子宮內膜增生)** or carcinoma.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Bleeding Stabilization
* IV fluid resuscitation (Normal Saline).
* Packed Red Blood Cell transfusion if haemodynamically unstable or severe symptomatic anaemia.

### Non-Hormonal Pharmacological Therapy
* **Tranexamic Acid (氨甲環酸)** $\to$ Antifibrinolytic; $\downarrow$ menstrual blood loss by 40-50%.
* **Mefenamic Acid (甲芬那酸)** $\to$ NSAID; relieves coexisting dysmenorrhoea, $\downarrow$ blood loss by up to 35%.

### Hormonal Medical Therapy
* Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs) $\to$ Regulates bleeding cycle.
* Oral Progestins (e.g., **Norethisterone**) $\to$ Taken days 5-26 to induce endometrial atrophy.
* **Levonorgestrel-releasing Intrauterine System (LNG-IUS / Mirena)** $\to$ Local progestin delivery, causes profound endometrial atrophy, $\downarrow$ blood loss up to 97% at 12 months.

### Pre-Operative Pharmacological Shrinkage
* **GnRH Agonists (GnRHa)** (e.g., Goserelin)
* Creates a hypoestrogenic menopausal state by downregulating GnRH receptors.
* Shrinks fibroid volume pre-operatively and allows Hb to recover.
* Strict 6-month maximum use due to osteoporosis and severe vasomotor side effects.

### Management of Red Degeneration in Pregnancy
* Strict bed rest.
* IV fluids hydration.
* Simple analgesia (Paracetamol).
* Avoid NSAIDs in late pregnancy (risk of premature closure of ductus arteriosus).

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Indications for Surgical Intervention
* Failure of medical therapy for heavy bleeding.
* Severe symptomatic anaemia.
* Significant pressure symptoms (hydronephrosis, bowel obstruction).
* Rapid growth (to rule out sarcoma).
* Submucosal fibroid causing recurrent miscarriage or infertility.

### Minimally Invasive Endoscopic Surgery
* **Hysteroscopic Resection (Transcervical Resection of Fibroid - TCRF)** $\to$ Definitive for FIGO Type 0, 1, 2 submucosal fibroids.
* Laparoscopic **Myomectomy (子宮肌瘤切除術)** $\to$ Fertility-sparing for subserosal or accessible intramural fibroids.

### Major Definitive Surgery
* Open Abdominal **Myomectomy (子宮肌瘤切除術)** $\to$ Required for extremely large or multiple intramural fibroids where fertility is desired.
* **Hysterectomy (子宮切除術)** $\to$ Definitive cure if family is complete. Route (Vaginal, Laparoscopic, or Abdominal) dictated by uterine size, mobility, and surgical expertise.

### Radiological Interventions
* **Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)** $\to$ Induces ischemic necrosis. Contraindicated if future pregnancy is desired.

### Obstetric Delivery Algorithm for Fibroids
* Vaginal delivery preferred if the patient is uncompromised and the birth canal is clear.
* Elective LSCS indicated if a cervical or lower uterine segment fibroid physically obstructs the birth canal.
* Strict avoidance of concurrent myomectomy during LSCS due to intractable haemorrhage risk.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-Myomectomy Obstetric Care
* If the uterine cavity was breached during surgery $\to$ mandatory elective LSCS at 37-38 weeks in all future pregnancies to prevent uterine rupture.

### Post-Hysterectomy Care
* Strict VTE prophylaxis (early mobilization, TED hose, LMWH if high risk).
* Monitor closely for vault haematoma or infection.
* Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) counselling if bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy was performed in a pre-menopausal patient.

### Long-Term Contraception Strategy
* **LNG-IUS (Mirena)** $\to$ First-line option providing both highly effective contraception and excellent suppression of heavy menstrual bleeding.
* COCs $\to$ Safe alternative for simultaneous bleeding control and contraception in non-obese, normotensive women.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Primary Gynaecological Complications
* Severe iron-deficiency anaemia $\to$ cardiac compromise.
* Torsion of pedunculated fibroid $\to$ acute surgical emergency.
* Ureteric compression $\to$ Hydronephrosis $\to$ Chronic renal failure.

### Obstetric & Fetal Complications
* Early pregnancy miscarriage.
* Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR).
* Preterm labour and premature rupture of membranes.
* Malpresentation (e.g., Breech, Transverse lie).
* Placental abruption (if placenta implants over fibroid).

### Intrapartum & Postpartum Complications
* Obstructed labour.
* **Primary Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH)** $\to$ Secondary to uterine atony because fibroids physically impede normal myometrial contraction.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What is the most common degenerative change of a leiomyoma during pregnancy? Answer: Red degeneration (haemorrhagic infarction) due to rapid growth outstripping the localized blood supply.
* Trap: Attempting a myomectomy during a routine Caesarean section. Pearl: Fibroids are highly vascularized during pregnancy; removing them during LSCS carries a massive risk of intractable haemorrhage and should generally be avoided unless absolutely necessary for delivery.
* Pitfall: Recommending Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE) to a patient wishing to preserve fertility. UAE carries a risk of premature ovarian insufficiency and targeted uterine necrosis, making myomectomy the safer fertility-sparing option.
* Viva Question: What is the primary medical treatment to rapidly reduce the size of a fibroid prior to surgery? Answer: GnRH agonists (e.g., Goserelin) administered for a maximum of 3 to 6 months.
* Pitfall: Performing a blind endometrial biopsy (Pipelle) and falsely reassuring the patient complaining of heavy bleeding. Pipelle sampling frequently misses focal intracavitary lesions like submucosal fibroids or polyps; a saline infusion sonohysterogram or hysteroscopy is required for definitive evaluation of the uterine cavity.
