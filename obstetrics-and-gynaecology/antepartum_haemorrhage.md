# Antepartum Haemorrhage (產前出血)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Antepartum Haemorrhage
* **Antepartum Haemorrhage (APH)**: Vaginal bleeding occurring from **24 weeks GA** to delivery of baby.

### Local Context & Epidemiology
* Major cause of maternal/perinatal morbidity & mortality in HK.
* Warrants immediate HA hospital admission for fetal-maternal monitoring.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Pathophysiology of Placenta Praevia (胎盤前置)
* Abnormal placental implantation in **lower uterine segment**.
* Grading based on internal os relationship:
	* **Type I (Low-lying)**: Near internal os.
	* **Type II (Marginal)**: Reaching internal os.
	* **Type III (Partial)**: Partially covering internal os.
	* **Type IV (Complete)**: Completely covering internal os.

### Pathophysiology of Placental Abruption (胎盤早剝)
* Premature separation of normally sited placenta.
* → Rupture of maternal vessels in decidua basalis.
* → Retroplacental haematoma formation.
* Impairs maternal-fetal gas/nutrient exchange.

### Pathophysiology of Vasa Praevia & Uterine Rupture
* **Vasa Praevia**: Aberrant feto-placental vessels traversing fetal membranes across internal os → prone to avulsion upon membrane rupture.
* **Uterine Rupture (子宮破裂)**: Rare catastrophic tearing of uterine wall → usually at previous **LSCS** scar.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History Assessment
* **Current Gestation**: GA, fetal movements (↓ implies distress).
* **Bleeding Characteristics**:
	* Painless, unprovoked bleeding → heavily suggests **Placenta Praevia (胎盤前置)**.
	* Painful bleeding + uterine contractions → heavily suggests **Placental Abruption (胎盤早剝)**.
	* Small blood + mucus → **Show** (mucus plug dislodgement).
- **Previous Obstetric Hx**: Prior APH, previous uterine scar/LSCS.
- **Maternal Symptoms**: Dizziness, syncope (hypovolaemia).

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* **Vital Signs**: ↑ HR, ↓ BP, pallor (correlate with shock severity).
* **Uterus**:
	* Tender, woody/hard, irritable → **Placental Abruption**.
	* Soft, non-tender → **Placenta Praevia**.
* **Fetal Assessment**:
	* High presenting part / malpresentation → suggests **Placenta Praevia**.
	* Fetal HR auscultation.

### Pelvic Examination Rules
* **STRICT CONTRAINDICATION**: NO digital vaginal or rectal exam unless **Placenta Praevia** explicitly excluded (risk of catastrophic bleeding).
* **Speculum Exam**:
	* Assess cervical os (closed/dilated).
	* Quantify active bleeding.
	* Identify lower genital tract lesions (polyp, cervicitis, cancer, trauma).

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnoses of APH
| Pathology | Pain | Uterine Tone | Fetal Presentation | Fetal Distress |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Placenta Praevia** | Painless | Soft, non-tender | Often malpresentation / high | Late/Rare |
| **Placental Abruption** | Painful | Woody, hard, irritable | Normal engagement | Common / Severe |
| **Vasa Praevia** | Painless | Normal | Normal | Immediate bradycardia |
| **Lower Genital Tract** | Painless | Normal | Normal | None |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Maternal Laboratory Investigations
* **CBP**: Assess Hb baseline & ↓ platelets (DIC risk).
* **Type & Screen (T&S)** / Cross-match: Blood prep for immediate transfusion.
* **Coagulation Screen**: PT, APTT, Fibrinogen (crucial for severe abruption/DIC).
* **RFT / LFT**: Baseline for end-organ damage.
* **Kleihauer Test**: Mandatory if maternal blood group **Rh -ve**.

### Specific Ultrasound (USG) Criteria
* **Placental Localization**: Confirm/exclude **Placenta Praevia** (Type I-IV).
* **Retroplacental Clot**: Seen in ONLY ~25% of **Placental Abruption** cases. (Normal USG *never* excludes abruption).
* **Fetal Biometry & Liquor**: Assess fetal size and amniotic fluid volume.

### CTG Tracings
* **Baseline FHR & Variability**: Assess fetal hypoxia/distress (e.g., late decelerations, bradycardia).
* **Tocodynamometer**: Detects high-frequency, low-amplitude contractions (uterine irritability in abruption).

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Resuscitation Protocol
* **Admission**: Mandatory for all APH (except very mild lower tract bleeding).
* **ABC Resuscitation**: Secure wide-bore IV access.
* **Fluid/Blood Replacement**: Infuse crystalloids / packed cells to prevent/treat hypovolaemic shock.
* **Bladder Catheterization**: Foley catheter → strict fluid balance & hourly urine output monitoring.

### Pharmacological Management
* **Anti-D Immunoglobulin**: Administer immediately if maternal blood is **Rh -ve**.
* **Hematinics**: Iron supplementation for anaemia correction.
* **Corticosteroids**: IM Betamethasone/Dexamethasone if preterm (< 34 wks) to ↑ fetal lung maturity.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Timing of Delivery
* **Immediate Delivery**: Indicated for heavy life-threatening bleeding, acute fetal distress, or GA $\ge$ 37 weeks (for placental causes).
* **Conservative / Expectant**: For mild APH, GA < 37 weeks, stable maternal-fetal status.
* **Unexplained APH**: If recurrent, consider planned delivery before term.

### Mode of Delivery Selection
* **Lower Segment Caesarean Section (LSCS) (剖腹產)**:
	* **Placenta Praevia** (Type II-IV).
	* Fetal distress.
	* Moderate-to-severe **Placental Abruption**.
* **Vaginal Delivery (陰道分娩)**:
	* APH of unknown origin.
	* Lower genital tract lesions.
	* Mild **Placental Abruption** w/o fetal compromise.
	* **Placenta Praevia** Type I w/o active bleeding.
	* **Placental Abruption** with Intrauterine Death (IUD).

### Intrapartum Care
* **NPO** (Nil per os).
* Continuous IV drip & T&S active.
* Continuous electronic fetal heart monitoring (**CEFM**).

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Active Management of Third Stage
* Strict adherence to active management to prevent **Primary Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH) (原發性產後出血)**.
* Prophylactic IV oxytocin infusion.

### Postnatal Monitoring
* Vigilant monitoring of vital signs & lochia for **Secondary PPH**.
* Continue iron therapy; recheck CBP to confirm anaemia resolution.
* Standard postnatal contraceptive counseling.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Hypovolaemic shock.
* Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
* Acute renal failure.
* **Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH)**.
* Maternal death.

### Fetal Complications
* Fetal hypoxia / asphyxia.
* Prematurity (**Preterm Birth** / 早產).
* Perinatal mortality.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical physical examination constraint in a patient presenting with uninvestigated APH? Answer: Digital vaginal examination is strictly contraindicated until placenta praevia has been definitively excluded via ultrasound to avoid provoking catastrophic haemorrhage.
* Trap: Ultrasound is a highly sensitive diagnostic tool for confirming placental abruption. Pearl: A retroplacental clot is visualized on ultrasound in only about 25% of cases; therefore, a normal ultrasound scan can never exclude placental abruption. The diagnosis remains primarily clinical.
* Pitfall: Discharging a Rhesus-negative mother presenting with mild APH without immunological testing. Always perform a Kleihauer test and administer Anti-D immunoglobulin prophylaxis to prevent maternal alloimmunization.
