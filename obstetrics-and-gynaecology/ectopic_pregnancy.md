# Ectopic Pregnancy (宮外孕)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition and Sites
* **Ectopic pregnancy:** implantation outside the uterine cavity
* Commonest site: **fallopian tube**
* Other sites:
  * Ovary
  * Cervix
  * Interstitial cornual region
  * Caesarean scar
  * Abdominal cavity
* **Heterotopic pregnancy:** simultaneous intrauterine and ectopic pregnancy, especially after assisted reproduction

### Clinical Importance
* Occurs in ~1-2% of pregnancies
* Leading cause of first-trimester maternal death
* Must be excluded in every woman of reproductive age with:
  * Positive pregnancy test
  * Abdominal or pelvic pain
  * Abnormal vaginal bleeding

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Tubal Implantation and Rupture
* Trophoblast invades tubal mucosa
* Tubal wall cannot expand like myometrium
* Progressive distension causes:
  * Tubal pain
  * Intratubal bleeding
  * Rupture
  * Haemoperitoneum
* Rupture can occur before a gestational sac is visible on ultrasound

### hCG Dynamics and Pregnancy of Unknown Location
* hCG usually rises abnormally
* Patterns:
  * Plateau
  * Slow rise
  * Decline
* **Pregnancy of unknown location (PUL):** no intrauterine or ectopic pregnancy seen on initial scan
* Requires serial hCG and repeat ultrasound

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Key History
* Missed period or abnormal bleeding
* Lower abdominal or pelvic pain
* Shoulder tip pain
* Syncope or dizziness
* Risk factors:
  * Previous ectopic
  * PID
  * Tubal surgery
  * IVF
  * Smoking
  * IUCD in situ if conception occurs

### Examination Findings
* Pallor, tachycardia, hypotension if ruptured
* Lower abdominal tenderness
* Rebound tenderness or guarding if haemoperitoneum
* Vaginal bleeding
* **Cervical excitation**
* Adnexal tenderness or mass
| Uterus slightly enlarged but smaller than dates

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnosis
* Early intrauterine pregnancy
* Miscarriage
* Ovarian cyst torsion or rupture
* PID or tubo-ovarian abscess
* Appendicitis
* UTI or renal colic
* Corpus luteum haemorrhage

### Clinical Staging
* **Unruptured ectopic:** haemodynamically stable, pregnancy confined to tube
* **Ruptured ectopic:** shock, peritonism, haemoperitoneum
* **Chronic ectopic:** persistent pain, low-grade bleeding, pelvic mass
* **Heterotopic pregnancy:** intrauterine plus ectopic gestation

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Essential Maternal Tests
* Urine pregnancy test
* Serum hCG:
  * Repeat at 48 hours if scan inconclusive
  * Abnormal rise or plateau suggests ectopic or failing pregnancy
* CBC
* Blood group, Rh status, and cross-match
* Progesterone if clinically useful
* ALT, creatinine, and CBC before methotrexate

### Ultrasound Findings
* Empty uterus despite positive pregnancy test
* Adnexal mass separate from ovary
* Extrauterine gestational sac ± fetal pole
* Free fluid in pouch of Douglas
* Tubal ring sign
* Heterotopic pregnancy if IVF

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Resuscitation if Ruptured
* ABC approach
* Two large-bore IV cannulae
* Crystalloid resuscitation
* Urgent cross-match
* Immediate theatre
* Do not delay surgery for imaging if unstable

### Methotrexate Criteria
* Unruptured ectopic
* Adnexal mass <35 mm
* No fetal heartbeat
* hCG <5000 IU/L
* No significant pain
* Reliable follow-up
* Normal liver, renal, and haematological function
* No contraindication to methotrexate

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surgical Options
* **Laparoscopic salpingectomy:**
  * Preferred if tube damaged
  * Contralateral tube normal
  * Fertility not desired
* **Salpingotomy:**
  * Preserve tube if contralateral tube absent or damaged
  * Requires hCG follow-up
* **Laparotomy** if unstable or laparoscopy unavailable

### Choice of Management
* **Expectant:**
  * Falling hCG
  * Minimal symptoms
  * Small ectopic
  * Reliable follow-up
* **Medical:**
  * Meets methotrexate criteria
* **Surgical:**
  * Rupture
  * Significant pain
  * Large ectopic
  * Fetal heartbeat
  * Non-compliance with follow-up

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After Methotrexate
* Monitor hCG on days 1, 4, and 7
* Continue weekly until negative
* Watch for persistent trophoblast
* Avoid NSAIDs, alcohol, folate supplements, and pregnancy until hCG normalises
* Anti-D if Rh negative

### After Surgery
* Histology of specimen
* Anti-D if Rh negative
* Contraception discussion
* Early ultrasound in next pregnancy
* Psychological support and grief counselling

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Tubal rupture
* Haemoperitoneum
* Hypovolaemic shock
* Death
* Persistent trophoblast
| Future ectopic pregnancy
* Subfertility

### Gynaecological Complications
* Tubal damage or loss
| Recurrent ectopic
* Adhesions
* Psychological distress

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What hCG threshold is commonly used for methotrexate suitability? Answer: <5000 IU/L, with other criteria met.
* Trap: Excluding ectopic because an intrauterine sac is present after IVF. Pearl: Consider heterotopic pregnancy.
* Pitfall: Delaying surgery for ultrasound in an unstable patient. Take to theatre immediately.
* Viva Question: Which surgical option preserves fertility if the contralateral tube is abnormal? Answer: Salpingotomy.
* Pitfall: Losing methotrexate follow-up. Persistent trophoblast can occur after treatment.
