# Abnormal Labour (異常產程)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition of Abnormal Labour
* **Abnormal Labour (異常產程)**: Deviation from normal physiological progress of labour, characterized by slow/arrested cervical dilatation or lack of fetal descent.
* **Prolonged First Stage**: Failure to progress efficiently from latent to active phase, or arrest during active phase (defined locally at HKU/HA as >3cm dilatation + full effacement).
* **Prolonged Second Stage**: Active maternal pushing for >1 hr in multiparous women OR >2 hrs in nulliparous women without delivery.

### Gestational Context & Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Common indication for emergency intrapartum intervention in HK.
* **Context**: Often manifests at term (GA >37w). 
* **High-Risk Demographics**: Nulliparity, advanced maternal age, ↑ BMI, fetal macrosomia, multiple gestations, IOL (Induction of Labour).

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### The 3 "P"s of Labour Pathophysiology
| Factor | Component | Pathological Variant |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Power** | Uterine contractions | **Uterine Inertia (子宫收缩乏力)**: Weak/incoordinate activity, inadequate to efface/dilate cervix. |
| **Passage** | Bony pelvis & soft tissue | Contracted pelvis, soft tissue mass (e.g., lower segment fibroid), rigid perineum. |
| **Passenger** | Fetus | Macrosomia, structural anomalies. |

### Malposition & Malpresentation
* **Malposition (胎位不正)**: Abnormal position of fetal occiput relative to maternal pelvis.
* **Occipito-Posterior (OP)** & **Occipito-Transverse (OT)**: Present larger cephalic diameter to pelvic brim → deflexed head → delayed descent.
* **Malpresentation**: Cephalic variants (face/brow), **Breech Presentation (臀先露)**, Shoulder (transverse/oblique lie), Compound presentation. 

### Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD)
* **Definition**: True mechanical mismatch between fetal head size/position and maternal pelvic dimensions.
* **Consequence**: Arrest of descent despite adequate uterine contractions → fetal head moulding, severe caput succedaneum formation, risk of **Uterine Rupture (子宮破裂)**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Maternal History
* **Onset & Progress**: Time of onset of regular painful contractions, duration of active pushing.
* **Membrane Status**: Time of spontaneous rupture of membranes (PROM), liquor color (clear, meconium-stained, blood-stained).
* **Symptoms**: Maternal exhaustion, continuous severe abdominal pain (warning sign of rupture), absence of fetal movements.
* **Obstetric Hx**: G/P, previous prolonged labour, previous LSCS, previous shoulder dystocia or PPH.

### Abdominal Examination
* **Palpation (Leopold's Maneuvers)**: Assess fetal lie, presentation, estimated fetal weight (EFW).
* **Engagement**: High presenting part (e.g., 4/5 or 5/5 palpable above pelvic brim) indicates failure of descent.
* **Uterine Tone**: Palpate contractions for frequency/strength. Check for excessive tenderness or unyielding rigid uterus (Bandl's ring in impending rupture).

### Speculum & Bimanual Examination
* **Cervical Assessment**: Dilatation (cm), effacement (%), position, consistency.
* **Presenting Part**: Station (relative to ischial spines, e.g., S-1, S0, S+1), presence of severe caput or excessive cranial suture moulding.
* **Pelvic Architecture**: Prominent ischial spines, flat sacrum, narrow subpubic arch suggestive of contracted pelvis.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnoses for Arrest of Labour
| Diagnosis | Key Distinguishing Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Uterine Inertia** | Infrequent/weak contractions, head engaged, no severe moulding. |
| **Cephalopelvic Disproportion (頭盆不稱)** | Strong contractions but no descent, severe caput/moulding, high station. |
| **Malposition (e.g., OP)** | Deflexed head, prolonged 1st/2nd stage, backache prominent. |

### Clinical Staging of Normal vs Abnormal Labour
* **First Stage (Latent)**: Onset to 3cm. Prolonged if excessively long without active progress.
* **First Stage (Active)**: 3cm to 10cm. Abnormal if cervical dilatation crosses the alert/action lines on Partogram.
* **Second Stage**: 10cm to expulsion. Abnormal if >2h (nullip) or >1h (multip) of active pushing.

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Fetal Monitoring (CTG & Scalp Blood)
* **Continuous Fetal Heart Monitoring (CFHM)**: Mandatory for high-risk labour or abnormal auscultation.
* **Abnormal CTG Tracings**: 
  * Early decelerations (head compression).
  * Variable decelerations (cord compression).
  * Late decelerations (uteroplacental insufficiency).
* **Fetal Scalp Blood Sampling**: Indicated if CTG is abnormal/suspicious but delivery is not imminent. Checks fetal pH/lactate for hypoxia.

### Ultrasound & Imaging
* **Intrapartum USG**: Confirms fetal malpresentation (e.g., brow/face), assesses fetal heart rate if difficult to auscultate, rules out placenta praevia if APH co-occurs.

### Maternal Laboratory Investigations
* **Blood**: CBC (Hb/Plt), Type & Screen (T&S) or Crossmatch if operative delivery anticipated.
* **Coagulation Profile**: If placental abruption or severe pre-eclampsia suspected.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Maternal Stabilization
* **IV Access & Fluid Resuscitation**: Insert large-bore IV cannula. Administer crystalloids to treat dehydration/ketosis.
* **Nil By Mouth (NBM)**: Institute fasting if emergency operative delivery is anticipated.
* **Analgesia**: Ensure adequate pain relief (Entonox, IM Pethidine, or Epidural Analgesia) to prevent maternal exhaustion.

### Pharmacological Augmentation
* **Oxytocin (催產素) Infusion**: 
  * Indication: Uterine inertia/dystocia without CPD. 
  * Action: Titrated IV infusion to achieve 3-4 adequate contractions per 10 mins.

### Tocolysis & Pre-operative Medications
* **Terbutaline**: 0.25mg SC bolus indicated for uterine hyperstimulation (excessive contractions causing fetal bradycardia).
* **Acid Aspiration Prophylaxis**: Famotidine (IV/PO) + 0.3M Sodium Citrate (PO) immediately prior to emergency LSCS.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### First Stage Arrest Algorithm
* **Step 1**: Exclude CPD and malpresentation.
* **Step 2**: Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM) if intact.
* **Step 3**: Oxytocin augmentation if contractions are inadequate.
* **Step 4**: Emergency **Lower Segment Caesarean Section (LSCS) (剖腹產)** if no progress despite adequate augmentation, or if fetal distress/CPD diagnosed.

### Second Stage Arrest & Instrumental Delivery
* **Pre-requisites**: Fully dilated cervix, engaged head (station $\ge$ S0), ruptured membranes, CPD excluded, empty bladder.
* **Ventouse Delivery (胎頭吸引助產)**: Vacuum extraction. Avoid in prematurity <34w.
* **Forceps Delivery (產鉗助產)**: Used depending on operator experience and fetal head position.
* **Emergency LSCS**: Indicated if instrumental delivery fails or head is unengaged.

### Shoulder Dystocia (肩難產) Protocol
* **Step 1**: Call for help (senior obstetrician, midwife, paediatrician, anaesthetist).
* **Step 2**: Episiotomy (to increase vaginal access, does not release shoulder).
* **Step 3**: **McRoberts Maneuver**: Maternal lithotomy position with maximal flexion & abduction of hips.
* **Step 4**: **Suprapubic Pressure**: Apply firm pressure over anterior fetal shoulder.
* **Step 5**: **Internal Maneuvers**: Woods' corkscrew maneuver, delivery of posterior arm.

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Immediate Post-partum Care
* **Active Management of Third Stage**: Essential to prevent PPH. Administer Syntocinon or Syntometrine within 1 min of delivery.
* **Uterine Tone Assessment**: Continuous massage and monitoring of fundus. 
* **Perineal Repair**: Suture episiotomy or 3rd/4th degree tears (Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injury) under adequate regional/general anaesthesia.

### Long-Term Follow-up & Contraception
* **Physiotherapy**: Pelvic floor exercises to treat stress incontinence or anal sphincter weakness.
* **Debriefing**: Explain indications for emergency LSCS or instrumental delivery.
* **Subsequent Pregnancy**: Counsel on Trial of Labour After Caesarean (TOLAC) vs Elective Repeat LSCS (ERCS). Vaginal delivery is safe after 1 prior LSCS (72-76% success) but contraindicated after Classical LSCS.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* **Primary Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH) (原發性產後出血)**: ↑ risk due to uterine atony (from prolonged labour) or genital tract trauma.
* **Infection**: Chorioamnionitis, puerperal endometritis.
* **Genital Tract Trauma**: Cervical tears, high vaginal tears, 3rd/4th degree perineal tears.
* **Uterine Rupture**: Rare but catastrophic, ↑ risk with obstructed labour or previous scar.

### Fetal & Neonatal Complications
* **Hypoxic Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE)**: Due to prolonged fetal distress or cord compression.
* **Birth Trauma (產傷)**: 
  * Ventouse: Cephalohaematoma, subaponeurotic haemorrhage.
  * Forceps: Facial nerve palsy.
  * Shoulder Dystocia: Brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy), fractured clavicle/humerus.
* **Neonatal Sepsis**: ↑ risk secondary to prolonged rupture of membranes.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are the absolute prerequisites before attempting instrumental vaginal delivery? Answer: Fully dilated cervix, ruptured membranes, head engaged (at or below ischial spines), exact position known, CPD excluded, and empty maternal bladder.
* Trap: Prescribing oxytocin augmentation for prolonged labour without performing a vaginal exam. Pearl: Always perform a careful pelvic assessment to exclude Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD) and malpresentation before augmenting with oxytocin to prevent uterine rupture.
* Pitfall: Pulling excessively on the fetal head during shoulder dystocia. This stretches the brachial plexus leading to permanent Erb's palsy. Apply McRoberts maneuver and suprapubic pressure instead of fundal pressure or excessive downward traction.
