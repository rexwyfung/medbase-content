# Abnormal Labour (異常分娩)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Labour Progress Definitions
* **Abnormal labour:** deviation from expected cervical dilatation, descent, or fetal condition during labour
* **Prolonged latent phase:** >20 hours in nullipara, >14 hours in multipara
* **Protracted active phase:** dilatation <1 cm/hour in nullipara, <1.2 cm/hour in multipara
* **Arrest of dilatation:** no cervical change for 2 hours despite adequate contractions
* **Failure of descent:** no descent during active labour or second stage
* **Obstructed labour:** mechanical obstruction preventing delivery despite uterine activity

### Clinical Significance
* Common indication for emergency caesarean or instrumental delivery
* Main causes are classified by the **3 Ps**:
  * **Power:** uterine contractions
  * **Passenger:** fetal size, position, presentation
  * **Passage:** bony pelvis and soft tissues
* In HA practice, partogram use and escalation thresholds help standardise intrapartum decisions

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Uterine Power Abnormalities
* **Hypotonic contractions:**
  * <3 contractions in 10 minutes
  * Inadequate intensity
  * Poor cervical progress
* **Hypertonic contractions:**
  * Excessive frequency or resting tone
  * Reduced uteroplacental perfusion
  * Fetal hypoxia and labour arrest
* Oxytocin misuse can convert hypotonic to hypertonic dysfunction

### Passenger and Passage Abnormalities
* Cephalopelvic disproportion
* Persistent occiput-posterior position
* Deep transverse arrest
* Breech, brow, face, or compound presentation
* Macrosomia or shoulder dystocia
* Contracted pelvis, pelvic mass, or previous pelvic surgery

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Intrapartum History
* Onset, frequency, and strength of contractions
* Duration of ruptured membranes
* Meconium-stained liquor
* Analgesia and oxytocin use
* Previous labour pattern and caesarean section
* Maternal fever or tachycardia
* Fetal movement history before labour

### Abdominal Findings
* Uterine contraction frequency and strength
* Fetal lie, presentation, position, and engagement
* Estimated fetal size
* Fundal height and uterine tenderness
* Bandl’s ring in obstructed labour
* Loss of fetal heart variability on auscultation

### Vaginal Findings
* Cervical dilatation, effacement, and consistency
* Station, moulding, and caput
* Position of occiput or presenting part
* Membrane status and cord prolapse
* Cephalopelvic disproportion assessment
* Presence of meconium or blood

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Causes of Abnormal Labour
| Cause | Examples |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Power** | Hypotonic or hypertonic contractions |
| **Passenger** | Malposition, macrosomia, breech |
| **Passage** | Contracted pelvis, fibroid, pelvic mass |
| **Placenta** | Placenta praevia, abruption |
| **Maternal** | Exhaustion, dehydration, sepsis |

### Labour Arrest Patterns
* **Prolonged latent phase:** slow early dilatation
* **Protracted active phase:** slow dilatation despite adequate contractions
* **Arrest of dilatation:** no progress for 2 hours with adequate contractions
* **Arrest of descent:** no descent in second stage
* **Obstructed labour:** mechanical blockage with no progress

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Partogram and Monitoring
* Plot cervical dilatation and descent against time
* Record contraction frequency, maternal observations, liquor, and fetal heart pattern
* Escalate if dilatation crosses the action line or fetal heart becomes abnormal

### CTG Interpretation
* Normal baseline 110-160 bpm
* Reduced variability may indicate hypoxia or opioid effect
* Late decelerations suggest uteroplacental insufficiency
* Variable decelerations suggest cord compression
* Tachysystole is >5 contractions in 10 minutes
* Bradycardia or sinusoidal pattern requires urgent delivery

### Additional Tests
* CBC if bleeding or suspected sepsis
* Urinalysis for ketones and infection
* Ultrasound if malpresentation, fibroid, or fetal size is uncertain
* Fetal blood sampling if CTG is suspicious and labour is progressing

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Maternal Stabilization
* IV access and hydration
* Correct dehydration and ketoacidosis
* Analgesia, including epidural if requested
* Left lateral position
* Catheterise bladder if obstruction or retention
* Treat fever or sepsis if present

### Uterine Stimulation
* **Amniotomy** if membranes intact
* **Oxytocin infusion** for hypotonic contractions
  * Aim for 3-4 contractions in 10 minutes
  * Titrate to CTG and contraction pattern
* Stop oxytocin if tachysystole, fetal distress, or scar dehiscence suspected

### Hyperstimulation Reversal
* Stop oxytocin
* Left lateral position
* Consider tocolysis with terbutaline or nifedipine
* Urgent caesarean if fetal compromise persists

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### First Stage Management
* Use partogram for all labouring women
* Amniotomy + oxytocin for poor progress
* Reassess after 2 hours
* Caesarean if:
  * Arrest despite adequate contractions
  * Cephalopelvic disproportion
  * Fetal distress
  * Abnormal presentation
* Consider uterine rupture if previous scar plus abnormal CTG, pain, or loss of station

### Second Stage Management
* Nullipara: up to 2 hours, or 3 hours with epidural
* Multipara: up to 1 hour, or 2 hours with epidural
* If delay:
  * Assess contractions
  * Consider oxytocin
  * Instrumental delivery if head is engaged and station ≥+2
  * Caesarean if high station, CPD, or abnormal CTG

### Instrumental Delivery Criteria
* Cephalic presentation
* Fully dilated cervix
* Engaged head
* Station ≥+2
* No cephalopelvic disproportion
* Adequate analgesia
* Bladder empty

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### After Vaginal or Instrumental Delivery
* Active management of third stage
* Inspect for genital tract trauma
* Monitor bleeding and uterine tone
* Bladder care and voiding assessment
* Neonatal assessment for birth injury

### After Caesarean Section
* Thromboprophylaxis
* Monitor wound and lochia
* Early mobilisation
* Analgesia and breastfeeding support
* Discuss future birth plans

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal Complications
* Postpartum haemorrhage
* Uterine rupture
* Sepsis
* Genital tract trauma
* Obstetric fistula in neglected obstructed labour
* Psychological trauma

### Fetal and Neonatal Complications
* Birth asphyxia
* Cord prolapse
* Shoulder dystocia
* Brachial plexus injury
* Intracranial haemorrhage
| Neonatal acidosis

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are the 3 Ps of abnormal labour? Answer: Power, passenger, and passage.
* Trap: Augmenting with oxytocin in obstructed labour. Pearl: Caesarean section is required.
* Pitfall: Ignoring tachysystole on CTG. Stop oxytocin and reassess fetal wellbeing.
* Viva Question: When is instrumental delivery contraindicated? Answer: High station, unengaged head, CPD, or abnormal presentation.
* Pitfall: Failing to escalate after 2 hours without cervical change despite adequate contractions.
