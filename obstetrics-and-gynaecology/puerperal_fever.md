# Puerperal Fever (產褥熱)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Definition & Clinical Context
* **Puerperal Pyrexia** (產褥熱): Fever occurring during puerperium (產褥期), the 6-week period after childbirth.
* **Physiological Baseline**: Transient reactionary fever common on Day 1 due to tissue trauma and dehydration.

### Pathophysiological Timeline & Common Causes
* **Day 1**: Reactionary fever, atelectasis (post-GA).
* **Day 3-4**: Breast engorgement (乳脹).
* **Late Postpartum**: Mastitis (乳腺炎), breast abscess (乳房膿腫), endometritis (子宮內膜炎).
* **Any Time**: Wound infection (perineal/abdominal), UTI, deep vein thrombosis (DVT) (深層靜脈血栓), coincidental infections (e.g., chest infection).

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy

### Genital Tract Pathophysiology
* Ascending bacterial colonization post-delivery → infection of decidua/myometrium → **Endometritis** (子宮內膜炎).
* Impaired uterine contraction (subinvolution) secondary to retained products of conception (RPOC) → nidus for infection.

### Breast & Systemic Pathophysiology
* **Lactogenesis II** → Milk stasis from ineffective removal → inflammatory response (engorgement) → bacterial entry via nipple fissures → **Mastitis** → localized pus collection (**Breast Abscess**).
* Pregnancy hypercoagulability + venous stasis + endothelial injury → ↑ risk of **DVT**.

## 3. Clinical Assessment

### Maternal History Findings
* Labour/delivery history (mode of delivery, prolonged rupture of membranes).
* Fever onset timing and pattern.
* Associated symptoms: Foul-smelling **lochia** (惡露), ↑ vaginal bleeding.
* Localized pain: Breast, abdomen, perineum, or surgical wound.
* Urinary symptoms (dysuria, frequency).
* Leg swelling/pain.
* Breastfeeding status and use of lactation suppression drugs.

### General Physical Examination
* **Vitals**: ↑ Temp, ↓ BP, ↑ HR (assess for septic shock).
* **Chest**: Crepitations / decreased breath sounds.
* **Lower Limbs**: Unilateral swelling, erythema, tenderness.

### Abdominal & Breast Examination
* **Breasts**: Engorgement, localized erythema, heat, palpable fluctuant mass, cracked nipples.
* **Abdomen**: Uterine subinvolution (fundus higher than expected), severe uterine tenderness.
* **Wounds**: Erythema, induration, purulent discharge at **LSCS** or episiotomy (會陰側切) sites.

### Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings
* **Vagina/Cervix**: Foul-smelling discharge, open cervical os.
* **Bimanual**: Uterine tenderness, boggy/enlarged uterus, cervical excitation tenderness.

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging

### Differential Diagnoses of Postpartum Fever
| Differential Diagnosis | Key Clinical Distinguishing Features |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Reactionary Fever** | Transient, Day 1 post-delivery, related to dehydration/trauma, afebrile within 24h. |
| **Endometritis** (子宮內膜炎) | Foul-smelling lochia, uterine tenderness, secondary PPH. |
| **Mastitis** (乳腺炎) | Sudden onset, localized breast erythema/pain, chills, >Day 3. |
| **Breast Engorgement** | Bilateral, gradual onset Day 3-4, hard/tender breasts, relieved by milk removal. |
| **Deep Vein Thrombosis** | Unilateral leg swelling, pain, erythema. |
| **Wound Infection** | Erythema, discharge at LSCS or episiotomy site. |
| **Urinary Tract Infection** | Dysuria, frequency, loin pain, rigors. |

## 5. Key Investigations

### Maternal Laboratory Investigations
* **CBC**: ↑ WBC with left shift.
* **Serum Lactate**: Check within 6 hours if severe sepsis / septic shock suspected.
* **Blood Cultures**: Indicated if high fever / rigors present.

### Microbiological Swabs & Cultures
* **High Vaginal Swab (HVS)** & **Endocervical Swab**: For bacterial culture.
* **Midstream Urine (MSU)**: Microscopy & culture to exclude UTI.
* **Wound Swab**: If purulent discharge present at surgical sites.

### Imaging & Specific Criteria
* **USG Pelvis**: Assess for empty uterus (excludes RPOC) vs. retained tissue. Cannot definitively distinguish RPOC from retained blood clots/decidua.
* **Doppler USG Lower Limbs**: If DVT suspected clinically.
* **Chest X-ray**: If respiratory symptoms / atelectasis suspected.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization

### Acute Resuscitation & Sepsis Protocol
* Continuous monitoring of BP, HR, Temp, Urine Output.
* Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation for hypovolaemia / suspected septic shock.

### Pharmacological Management
* Empirical broad-spectrum IV **Antibiotics** (抗生素) immediately if infection suspected.
* **Analgesics & Antipyretics**: Paracetamol, NSAIDs.
* Uterotonics: **Syntocinon** or **Carboprost** if associated with secondary PPH.
* **Tranexamic Acid**: To control bleeding if secondary PPH co-exists.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm

### Surgical & Gynaecological Interventions
* **Retained Products of Gestation**: Evacuation of uterus (子宮刮宮術) under antibiotic cover.
* **Breast Abscess**: Surgical drainage.
* **Wound Infection**: Surgical debridement, opening of wound to drain pus.
* **DVT**: Therapeutic anticoagulation (e.g., LMWH).

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Post-partum Care & Monitoring
* Monitor lochia volume, colour, and odor.
* Monitor uterine involution (normally ↓ 1 finger-breadth/day, non-palpable by 2 weeks).
* Ensure adequate hydration and early mobilization.

### Infant Feeding Considerations
* **Mastitis**: Ensure effective milk removal, continue breastfeeding / expressing on affected side.
* **Breast Abscess**: Rest affected breast, continue breastfeeding on the unaffected side.

### Long-term Follow-up
* Assess for **Asherman Syndrome** (阿休曼綜合症) / intrauterine adhesions if uterine evacuation was performed.
* Contraception: Progesterone-only pills (POPs) compatible with breastfeeding.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Maternal & Gynaecological Complications
* **Septic Shock**: Major cause of maternal mortality.
* **Secondary PPH** (繼發性產後出血).
* Intrauterine adhesions (**Asherman Syndrome**).
* Thromboembolic events (PE).
* Chronic pelvic pain / pelvic inflammatory disease.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Trap: Assuming early day 1 fever is always infectious. Pearl: Reactionary fever on day 1 is common and physiological due to dehydration and tissue trauma.
* Viva Question: What is the definitive management of a breast abscess? Answer: Surgical drainage, antibiotics, and continuing feeding on the unaffected breast.
* Pitfall: Delaying antibiotics until swab culture results return. Always start empirical antibiotics early if postpartum infection is suspected to prevent rapid progression to septic shock.
* Trap: Relying completely on ultrasound to diagnose retained products of conception. Pearl: Ultrasound cannot reliably distinguish between retained products of gestation and retained blood clots or normal decidua.
* Viva Question: What is the most critical life-threatening complication of puerperal pyrexia to monitor for? Answer: Septic shock, which remains a major cause of maternal death.
