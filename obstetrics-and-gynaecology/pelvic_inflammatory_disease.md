# Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (盆腔炎)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Gestational Context & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Pelvic Inflammatory Disease
* **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (盆腔炎)**: Acute clinical syndrome of upper female genital tract inflammation.
* Encompasses spectrum:
	* **Endometritis (子宮內膜炎)**.
	* **Salpingitis (輸卵管炎)**.
	* **Parametritis (宮旁組織炎)**.
	* **Oophoritis (卵巢炎)**.
	* **Tubo-ovarian Abscess (輸卵管卵巢膿腫)**.
	* **Pelvic Peritonitis (盆腔腹膜炎)**.

### Local Epidemiology & Risk Factors
* Primarily affects young, sexually active women (peak age 15-25).
* Risk factors:
	* Multiple sexual partners.
	* Previous **Sexually Transmitted Infection (性病)**.
	* Non-barrier contraception.
	* Recent uterine instrumentation (e.g., TOP, IUCD insertion within 3 weeks).
* Protectives:
	* **Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (口服避孕藥)** (thickens cervical mucus).
	* Barrier methods.

### Gestational Context
* Rare in pregnancy (mucus plug prevents ascending infection).
* If occurs in T1 → high risk of **Miscarriage (小產)**.
* Postpartum/post-abortal endometritis is clinically distinct but shares pathophysiological pathways.

## 2. Pathophysiology, Endocrine Axis & Pelvic Anatomy (Maternal-Fetal Physiology)

### Microbiological Pathophysiology
* Polymicrobial infection driven by ascending migration of cervicovaginal flora.
* Primary initiators:
	* **Chlamydia trachomatis (沙眼衣原體)**.
	* **Neisseria gonorrhoeae (淋球菌)**.
* Secondary invaders (endogenous vaginal flora):
	* **Anaerobes**.
	* **Gardnerella vaginalis**.
	* **Haemophilus influenzae**.
	* **Streptococcus spp**.
	* **Mycoplasma genitalium**.
* Gonococcal/Chlamydial endotoxins → tubal ciliary destruction → tubal scarring & occlusion.

### Pelvic Anatomy Dissemination
* Direct contiguous spread: Cervix → Endometrium → Fallopian tubes → Ovaries → Pelvic peritoneum.
* Lymphatic/Haematogenous spread: Via broad ligament lymphatics to parametrium (common in non-STI causes like IUCD/post-procedure).
* Peritoneal spread: Purulent exudate tracking via right paracolic gutter to perihepatic capsule (Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (History, Abdominal, Speculum & Bimanual Exam Findings ONLY)

### Patient History & Symptoms
* Lower abdominal/pelvic pain (bilateral, dull, continuous).
* Deep **Dyspareunia (性交疼痛)**.
* Abnormal vaginal discharge (purulent, offensive).
* Irregular vaginal bleeding (intermenstrual, postcoital).
* Systemic symptoms: Fever, nausea, vomiting, chills.
* Urinary / Bowel symptoms: Dysuria, diarrhea (pervic peritonism).

### Abdominal Examination Findings
* Lower abdominal tenderness (usually bilateral).
* Rebound tenderness & guarding (+ve pelvic peritonism).
* Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) tenderness (suspicion of perihepatitis).

### Speculum Examination Findings
* Purulent endocervical discharge.
* **Cervicitis (子宮頸炎)**: Erythematous, friable cervix (contact bleeding).
* Visible IUCD strings (if present).

### Bimanual Examination Findings
* **Cervical Excitation / Chandelier Sign (子宮頸搖舉痛)**: Extreme pain on cervical motion.
* Uterine tenderness.
* Bilateral adnexal tenderness.
* Palpable adnexal mass (suspicion of **Tubo-ovarian Abscess**).

## 4. Critical Differential Diagnoses & Clinical Staging (e.g., FIGO if applicable)

### Critical Differential Diagnoses
| System | Differential Diagnosis | Key Distinguishing Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gynaecological** | **Ectopic Pregnancy (宮外孕)** | Unilateral pain, +ve UPT, amenorrhoea, shoulder tip pain. |
| | **Ovarian Cyst Complication (卵巢囊腫併發症)** | Sudden onset severe unilateral pain (torsion/rupture), vomiting. |
| | **Endometriosis (子宮內膜異位症)** | Cyclical pain, fixed retroverted uterus, nodular uterosacral ligaments. |
| **Surgical** | **Acute Appendicitis (急性盲腸炎)** | Migratory pain (umbilicus → RIF), +ve Rovsing's sign, anorexia. |
| | **Diverticulitis (憩室炎)** | Left lower quadrant pain, older age, altered bowel habit. |
| **Urological** | **Acute Pyelonephritis (急性腎盂腎炎)** | Loin pain, renal angle tenderness, rigors. |

### Severity Staging (Modified Monif Classification)
| Stage         | Clinical Description                                       |
| :------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stage I**   | Acute salpingitis without peritonitis.                     |
| **Stage II**  | Acute salpingitis with peritonitis.                        |
| **Stage III** | Salpingitis with **Tubo-ovarian Abscess (TOA)** formation. |
| **Stage IV**  | Ruptured TOA.                                              |

## 5. Key Investigations (Labs, Specific Ultrasound Criteria & CTG Tracings)

### Bedside & Microbiological Investigations
* **Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT)**: Mandatory to exclude ectopic pregnancy.
* **Endocervical Swab**: NAAT (Nucleic Acid Amplification Test) for Gonorrhea & Chlamydia.
* **High Vaginal Swab (HVS)**: Microscopy & culture for bacterial vaginosis, Trichomonas, standard flora.
* **Urine Dipstick & MSU**: To exclude UTI.

### Laboratory Blood Tests
* **Complete Blood Count (CBP)**: ↑ WBC (Leukocytosis with left shift).
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ↑ CRP, ↑ ESR.
* **Blood Cultures**: If temperature > 38.5°C or rigors.

### Specific Ultrasound Criteria (Tubo-ovarian Abscess)
* Thickened, fluid-filled fallopian tubes (cogwheel sign / incomplete septa).
* Free pelvic fluid in Pouch of Douglas.
* **Tubo-ovarian Abscess**: Complex, thick-walled, multilocular cystic adnexal mass with internal echoes.
* Hyperaemia on colour Doppler.

## 6. Medical Management & Acute Stabilization (Pharmacology, Steroids, Resuscitation)

### Acute Stabilization & Indications for Hospitalization
* Indications for IV therapy:
	* Surgical emergency cannot be ruled out (e.g., appendicitis).
	* Pregnancy.
	* Lack of response to PO antibiotics (48-72h).
	* Inability to tolerate PO (nausea/vomiting).
	* Severe illness (high fever, severe peritonism).
	* Suspected TOA.
* **Resuscitation**: IV fluids (Normal Saline / Hartmann's), strict input/output monitoring.
* Analgesia: Paracetamol, NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen), Opioids for severe pain.

### HA Pharmacological Regimen (Outpatient)
* Empirical therapy must cover CT, GC, and anaerobes.
* **IM Ceftriaxone (頭孢曲松)** 500mg single dose + **PO Doxycycline (多西環素)** 100mg BD x 14 days + **PO Metronidazole (甲硝唑)** 400mg BD x 14 days.
* Alternative (if Cephalosporin allergy): PO Ofloxacin 400mg BD + PO Metronidazole 400mg BD x 14 days.

### HA Pharmacological Regimen (Inpatient)
* **IV Ceftriaxone** 1g QD + **IV/PO Doxycycline** 100mg BD + **IV Metronidazole** 500mg TDS.
* Severe TOA / septic shock: **IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Tazocin)** or **IV Clindamycin** + **Gentamicin**.
* Switch to PO regimen after 24-48 hours of clinical improvement; complete 14-day total course.

## 7. Definitive Management Algorithm (Timing/Mode of Delivery OR Gynaecological Surgery)

### Surgical Indications
* Ruptured **Tubo-ovarian Abscess** (Surgical Emergency).
* Failure to respond to IV antibiotics within 48-72 hours.
* Diagnostic uncertainty (e.g., appendicitis vs. PID).
* TOA > 8cm (often refractory to conservative medical management).

### Surgical Principles & Techniques
* **Diagnostic Laparoscopy**:
	* Gold standard for diagnosis.
	* Allows visualization of hyperaemic, purulent tubes and perihepatic adhesions.
* **Laparoscopic Adhesiolysis & Drainage**:
	* Drainage of pyosalpinx/TOA.
	* Copious peritoneal lavage.
* **Salpingectomy / Salpingo-oophorectomy**:
	* Excision of necrotic tube/ovary if tissue is completely destroyed or actively hemorrhaging.
* **Ultrasound-guided percutaneous/transvaginal drainage**:
	* Alternative for TOA unamenable to primary surgery (high risk of bowel injury).

## 8. Post-partum/Post-operative Care, Contraception & Follow-up

### Clinical Follow-up Protocol
* Outpatient review at 72 hours: Assess response to empirical PO antibiotics (↓ pain, ↓ tenderness).
* Repeat bimanual exam & CBP/CRP at 2 weeks to confirm clinical resolution.
* TOA follow-up: Serial USG pelvis to ensure complete resolution of abscess.

### Partner Tracing & Contact Management
* **Partner Notification**: Trace and treat all sexual partners within the last 6 months.
* Empirical partner treatment for Gonorrhea/Chlamydia regardless of their test results.

### Contraception
* Abstinence until patient and partner have completed the 14-day antibiotic course and symptoms have fully resolved.
* **IUCD Management**:
	* Do NOT routinely remove IUCD initially.
	* Remove if no clinical improvement after 48-72 hours of antibiotics.
	* Ensure alternative contraception is provided.

## 9. Maternal, Fetal & Gynaecological Complications

### Acute & Life-Threatening Complications
* Sepsis & Septic Shock.
* Ruptured TOA → Generalized acute peritonitis.
* **Fitz-Hugh-Curtis Syndrome**: Perihepatitis characterized by RUQ pain and "violin-string" adhesions between liver capsule and anterior abdominal wall.

### Long-Term Gynaecological Sequelae
* **Infertility (不育)**: 10-20% risk after 1 episode, ↑ risk with multiple episodes due to tubal scarring.
* **Ectopic Pregnancy (宮外孕)**: 6-10x increased risk due to impaired tubal ciliary motility and strictures.
* **Chronic Pelvic Pain (慢性盆腔痛)**: 30% risk secondary to dense pelvic adhesions.
* **Recurrent PID**: High recurrence rate if partners untreated or non-barrier methods continued.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: What are the three cardinal signs of PID on bimanual examination? Answer: Cervical excitation, uterine tenderness, and bilateral adnexal tenderness.
* Trap: Assuming a negative endocervical swab rules out PID. Pearl: PID is a clinical diagnosis; upper genital tract infection can exist even if lower tract swabs are negative for CT/GC.
* Pitfall: Removing an IUCD immediately upon diagnosing PID. Explain: Routine removal is not indicated unless the patient fails to respond to 48-72 hours of appropriate IV/PO antibiotics. If removed, you must provide emergency contraception if she had unprotected intercourse in the prior 7 days.
* Viva Question: What is the classic surgical finding in Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome? Answer: "Violin-string" adhesions between the liver capsule and the parietal peritoneum/diaphragm.
* Trap: Using empirical Ofloxacin/Moxifloxacin as first-line outpatient therapy in Hong Kong. Pearl: Due to high rates of fluoroquinolone-resistant Neisseria gonorrhoeae in HK, Ceftriaxone + Doxycycline + Metronidazole is the mandatory HA first-line regimen.
