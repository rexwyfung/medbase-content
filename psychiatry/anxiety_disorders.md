# Anxiety Disorders (焦慮症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition (臨床定義)
* **Anxiety (焦慮)**: Normal physiological response to threat → becomes abnormal/pathological when severity is out of proportion to objective threat or outlasts the threat duration.
* **Core Components**: Psychological dread, somatic tension, autonomic overactivity, avoidance behaviors.

### Biopsychosocial Etiologies (生理心理社會成因)
* **Biological**: ↑ Genetic heritability (moderate).
* **Psychological**: Classical conditioning (fear acquisition) → operant conditioning (avoidance maintains fear).
* **Social**: Early childhood adversity, urban stress, major life events.

### Local Epidemiology (本地流行病學)
* **Prevalence**: ~4-5% point prevalence in Hong Kong (香港).
* **Demographics**: ♀ > ♂ (2:1 ratio).
* **Peak Onset**: Late adolescence to early adulthood.

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurotransmitter Pathways (神經傳導物質路徑)
* **GABA (伽瑪氨基丁酸)**: ↓ Inhibitory tone in amygdala/limbic system.
* **Serotonin (5-HT)**: Dysregulation in raphe nucleus projections → altered emotional processing.
* **Noradrenaline (NE)**: ↑ Locus coeruleus firing → autonomic arousal.

### Psychopathology (心理病理學)
* **Psychological**: Dread, apprehension, restlessness, narrowed attention, insomnia, irritability.
* **Autonomic**: ↑ Heart rate, sweating, dry mouth, urge to urinate/defecate.
* **Somatic**: ↑ Muscle tension, tremor, hyperventilation (→ dizziness/paraesthesia).
* **Behavioral**: Avoidance of danger/phobic stimuli (逃避行為), anticipatory anxiety.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History (精神科病歷)
* **Onset**: Abrupt (panic) vs. gradual (GAD).
* **Triggers**: Specific objects/situations (phobias) vs. pervasive worry.
* **Substance**: Caffeine, alcohol, illicit drugs (cocaine/amphetamines).
* **Impact**: Functional impairment (occupational, social handicap).

### Mental State Examination (精神狀態檢查)
* **Appearance & Behavior**: Restless, tremulous, edge of chair, ↑ muscle tension, widened palpebral fissures, dilated pupils.
* **Speech**: Rapid, tremulous voice.
* **Mood & Affect**: Subjectively "anxious/panicky"; objectively restricted, hyper-aroused affect.
* **Thoughts**: Worries about loss of control, fainting, death, or physical illness (e.g., heart attack).
* **Cognition**: ↓ Attention/concentration (easily distracted by internal cues/worry).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differentials (精神科鑑別診斷)
* **Depressive Disorders (抑鬱症)**: Often comorbid; anxiety can be secondary to depression.
* **Substance-Induced Anxiety**: Intoxication (stimulants) or withdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines).
* **Psychosis**: Anxiety secondary to persecutory delusions or hallucinations.

### Organic/Medical Rule-Outs (生理疾病排除)
* **Endocrine**: Hyperthyroidism (甲狀腺機能亢進), phaeochromocytoma, hypoglycemia.
* **Cardiovascular**: Arrhythmias (e.g., SVT), ischemic heart disease.
* **Respiratory**: Asthma, COPD, pulmonary embolism.
* **Neurological**: Temporal lobe epilepsy, vestibular dysfunction.

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### Diagnostic Framework (ICD-11 / DSM-5)
| Category (類別) | Core Feature (核心特徵) | Duration (時長) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **GAD (廣泛性焦慮症)** | Excessive, pervasive, uncontrollable worry | > 6 months |
| **Panic Disorder (恐慌症)** | Recurrent, unexpected panic attacks + anticipatory fear | > 1 month |
| **Agoraphobia (廣場恐懼症)** | Fear of spaces where escape is difficult (e.g., crowds, transport) | > 6 months |
| **Social Anxiety (社交焦慮症)** | Fear of negative evaluation in social/performance situations | > 6 months |
| **Specific Phobia (特定恐懼症)** | Irrational fear of specific object/situation (e.g., heights, spiders) | > 6 months |

### Standardized Assessment Tools (標準化評估工具)
* **GAD-7**: Self-rated 7-item scale; scores $\ge10$ indicate probable GAD.
* **HADS (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale)**: 7 items for anxiety, 7 for depression.

### Organic Rule-Out Investigations (生理排除檢查)
* **Bloods**: TFTs, FBC, U&E, fasting glucose.
* **Urine**: Toxicology screen.
* **ECG**: Rule out arrhythmias.

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Management (急性處理)
* **De-escalation**: Reassurance, calm environment.
* **Breathing**: Slow, controlled breathing for hyperventilation.
* **Pharmacology**: Short-course Benzodiazepines (e.g., Lorazepam) strictly for severe, acute distress (limit 1-2 weeks to prevent dependence).

### Risk Assessment (風險評估)
* **Suicide Risk (自殺風險)**: ↑ Risk if comorbid with depression or substance misuse.
* **Self-Medication**: High risk of alcohol/drug abuse to cope with symptoms.

### Medico-Legal Framework (醫療法律)
* **HK Mental Health Ordinance Cap 136 (香港精神健康條例)**: Involuntary admission (Section 2/3) is *rarely* indicated for primary anxiety disorders unless there is imminent, severe risk to self (e.g., suicidal secondary to extreme panic/comorbid depression).

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Pharmacotherapy (藥物治療)
* **First-line**: SSRIs (e.g., Sertraline, Escitalopram) or SNRIs (e.g., Venlafaxine). 
* **Initiation Principle**: "Start low, go slow" → anxiety can transiently ↑ upon initiation.
* **Beta-blockers**: Propranolol for somatic autonomic symptoms (e.g., tremor, palpitations) - especially in performance anxiety.

### Psychotherapy (心理治療)
* **CBT (認知行為治療)**: First-line psychological intervention.
* **Cognitive Restructuring**: Identifying and challenging catastrophic misinterpretations.
* **Exposure Therapy (暴露療法)**: Graded exposure with response prevention (essential for phobias/agoraphobia).
* **Applied Relaxation**: Progressive muscle relaxation.

### Social Management (社會管理)
* **Lifestyle**: ↓ Caffeine, smoking cessation, regular aerobic exercise.
* **Support**: Patient support groups via Hospital Authority (HA) or NGOs.

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Illness Trajectory (疾病進程)
* **Course**: Typically chronic, fluctuating, relapsing-remitting course exacerbated by stress.
* **Recovery Rate**: ~50% achieve full remission with adequate treatment.
* **Untreated**: Can lead to significant chronicity and secondary complications.

### Prognostic Factors (預後因素)
* **Poor Prognosis (-ve)**: Severe avoidance behaviors, severe somatic symptoms, personality disorder traits, long duration of untreated illness.
* **Good Prognosis (+ve)**: Good premorbid functioning, strong social support, compliance with CBT/medications.

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric Complications (精神科併發症)
* **Major Depressive Disorder (重度抑鬱症)**: Most common comorbidity.
* **Substance Use Disorders (物質使用障礙)**: Alcohol dependency secondary to self-medication.

### Social Complications (社會併發症)
* **Occupational**: Job loss, academic failure due to avoidance.
* **Interpersonal**: Marital breakdown, social isolation.

### Psychotropic Drug Side Effects (藥物副作用)
| Drug Class | Key Adverse Effects (主要副作用) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **SSRIs** | Initial ↑ anxiety, GI upset (nausea), sexual dysfunction (↓ libido, delayed ejaculation), hyponatremia (elderly). |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Tolerance, dependence, sedation, cognitive/memory impairment, falls in elderly. |
| **Beta-blockers** | Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm (contraindicated in asthma). |

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the defining difference between a panic attack and Panic Disorder? Answer: A panic attack is a discrete period of intense fear; Panic Disorder requires recurrent *unexpected* attacks followed by at least 1 month of anticipatory anxiety or maladaptive behavior changes.
* Trap: Prescribing a high-dose SSRI immediately for panic disorder. Pearl: Always start at half the typical depression dose for anxiety disorders, as SSRIs can cause a transient anxiogenic effect in the first 1-2 weeks.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing a specific phobia without checking for broader agoraphobia. Always confirm if the fear is isolated to a single object/situation or part of a broader pattern of escaping difficult environments.
