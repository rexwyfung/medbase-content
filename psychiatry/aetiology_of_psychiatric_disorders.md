# Aetiology of Psychiatric Disorders (精神疾病病因學)

## 1. Topic Definition, Scope & Relevance in Hong Kong

### Definition of Psychiatric Aetiology
* **Aetiology** (病因學): Study of causation of psychiatric disorders.
* Characterized by complex, multifactorial mechanisms rather than single pathogen models.
* Relies on continuous interaction between biological vulnerabilities and environmental exposures.

### Scope of Aetiological Study
* Bridges scientific disciplines: genetics, neurochemistry, sociology, psychology.
* Explores temporal relationships: from in utero exposures → adult psychopathology.
* Investigates **Epigenetics** (表觀遺傳學): environmental modulation of gene expression.

### Relevance in HK Clinical Practice
* Essential for drafting the comprehensive **Psychiatric Formulation** (精神科個案分析) required in HA (Hospital Authority) settings.
* Directs multidisciplinary interventions (e.g., targeting maintaining factors via Medical Social Workers).
* Addresses local sociodemographic stressors (e.g., high-density living, hyper-competitive education, immigrant/acculturation stress).

## 2. Core Theoretical Principles & Mechanisms (Psychological, Pharmacological, or Legal)

### The Biopsychosocial Model (生物心理社會模型)
* Proposed by Engel (1977); gold standard for HKUMed clinical formulations.
* Postulates disorders emerge from intersection of:
* **Biological**: Genetics, neurochemistry, neurodevelopment.
* **Psychological**: Coping mechanisms, personality traits, cognitive schemas.
* **Social**: Family dynamics, socioeconomic status, cultural idioms of distress.

### Jaspers' Concepts: Erklären vs. Verstehen
* **Erklären** (Explanation): Scientific/objective causation (e.g., brain tumour → aggression).
* **Verstehen** (Understanding): Intuitive/empathic grasp of psychological reactions (e.g., job loss → depressed mood).
* Both essential for holistic patient assessment.

### Neuroscience & Epigenetic Mechanisms
* Mental processes = brain operations; behavioural disorders = brain dysfunctions.
* Genes ↑/↓ vulnerability to environmental stressors.
* Social factors → epigenetic modifications → altered synaptic connectivity → modified behavioural phenotype.

### Alternative Aetiological Models
| Model | Core Concept | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Medical** (醫學模型) | Disease entity with specific causes/pathology | DA hypothesis in **Schizophrenia** (精神分裂症) |
| **Behavioural** (行為模型) | Maladaptive learned responses/reinforcement | Avoidance in **Phobias** (恐懼症) |
| **Psychoanalytic** (精神分析) | Unconscious conflicts/early trauma | Repressed grief → Depression |
| **Political/Critical** (批判性) | Illness as social construct/control mechanism | Szasz: Mental illness is a myth |

## 3. Key Classifications, Frameworks & Components

### The 3Ps Chronological Framework (3P時間框架)
| Factor Type | Timing | Function | HA Clinical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Predisposing** (易感) | Early life/Constitutional | Sets baseline vulnerability | +ve FHx of Bipolar, childhood abuse |
| **Precipitating** (誘發) | Proximal to onset | Triggers the acute episode | Recent bankruptcy, relationship breakdown |
| **Perpetuating** (維持) | Ongoing during illness | Prevents recovery / prolongs | Ongoing substance misuse, social isolation |

### Predisposing Factors (易感因素)
* **Genetics**: Polygenic inheritance; determines vulnerability.
* **In utero/Perinatal**: Maternal infection, birth trauma, prematurity.
* **Childhood**: Maltreatment, early parental loss, insecure attachment.
* **Personality** (性格): Core constitutional trait ↑ risk (e.g., anankastic traits → MDD).

### Precipitating Factors (誘發因素)
* **Physical**: CNS infections, head injury, illicit drugs.
* **Psychological/Social**: Major life events (bereavement, migration, exam failure).
* Magnitude of stressor required depends inversely on magnitude of predisposing vulnerability.

### Perpetuating (Maintaining) Factors (維持因素)
* **Secondary consequences**: Demoralization, job loss due to illness.
* **Maladaptive coping**: ↑ alcohol/substance use to self-medicate.
* **Systemic**: Lack of social support, poor treatment compliance, institutionalization.

## 4. Clinical Indications & Standardized Assessment Tools

### Aetiological Formulation in Clinical Assessment
* Synthesizes history + MSE into a coherent narrative.
* Essential for creating targeted management plans (e.g., treating perpetuating factors first).
* Requires evaluating patient's premorbid **Personality** (性格) to contextualize reactions.

### Evaluating Stressors vs. Stress Reactions
* **Stressors**: Objective events (e.g., DSE exams, housing eviction).
* **Stress Reaction**: Subjective physiological/psychological response (e.g., ↑ sympathetic tone, anxiety).
* Must differentiate true stressors from post-hoc patient rationalizations.

### Jaspers' Criteria for Psychological Reactions
* 1. Stressor must be adequate in severity & closely temporally related to onset.
* 2. Content of disorder reflects nature of stressor (e.g., preoccupation with lost loved one).
* 3. Symptoms ↓ when stressor is removed (unless perpetuating factors take over).

## 5. Evidence-Base, Efficacy & Limitations

### Limitations in Psychiatric Aetiological Research
* High reliance on retrospective recall (recall bias, especially in depressed/psychotic patients).
* Difficulty in long-term prospective cohort studies.
* Lack of definitive biomarkers for most functional psychiatric disorders.

### Causal Multiplicity & Indirect Mechanisms
* **Equifinality** (殊途同歸): Multiple different causes → single disorder (e.g., genetics OR trauma OR drugs → Psychosis).
* **Multifinality** (一因多果): Single cause → multiple different disorders (e.g., childhood abuse → MDD, PTSD, BPD, or Substance Misuse).
* Aetiological factors rarely direct; mediated via complex intervening mechanisms.

### Lack of Temporal Association
* Causes often remote from effects (e.g., early childhood separation → adult depression).
* Complicates establishing definitive causation vs. correlation.

## 6. Practical Execution, Guidelines & Local Protocols

### Structuring the HA Aetiological Formulation
* Strictly divide formulation into Biological, Psychological, and Social columns.
* Intersect with Predisposing, Precipitating, Perpetuating rows.
* Conclude with a synthesized summary driving the biopsychosocial management plan.

### Integrating Intuition & Scientific Evidence
* Combine epidemiological evidence (e.g., ↑ risk of MDD with +ve FHx) with empathic understanding (e.g., profound grief over partner leaving).
* Avoid superficial explanations; dig for constitutional vulnerabilities.
* Actively identify and target **Perpetuating Factors** (維持因素) for immediate intervention.

## 7. Medico-Legal Considerations & HK Statutory Frameworks (e.g., Cap 136)

### Aetiology & The Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136)
* Legal threshold for involuntary admission (Form 1/2/3) requires presence of **Mental Disorder** (精神紊亂).
* Aetiology (e.g., organic vs. functional) dictates whether treatment falls under psychiatric vs. general medical purview.
* Organic conditions causing behavioural disturbance (e.g., Delirium) must be ruled out/treated medically before psychiatric detention.

### Causation in Forensic Psychiatric Assessment
* Determining if mental illness (e.g., Schizophrenia) directly caused criminal behaviour (e.g., violence due to command AH).
* Crucial for establishing diminished responsibility or unfitness to plead in HK courts.

## 8. Special Populations (Lifespan, Intellectual Disability, Comorbidities)

### Aetiological Considerations in Childhood/Adolescence
* Heavy reliance on developmental models.
* Assessing mother-child separation, bullying, educational stress, family dynamics.
* Early temperamental traits heavily influence later psychiatric morbidity.

### Aetiology in Intellectual Disability (智力障礙)
* Higher biological loading (chromosomal abnormalities, perinatal hypoxia).
* ↑ vulnerability to psychosocial stressors due to ↓ cognitive coping reserves.
* Diagnostic overshadowing: misattributing new psychiatric symptoms to the underlying ID rather than a new precipitating factor.

## 9. Adverse Effects, Ethical Dilemmas & Systemic Challenges

### Ethical Pitfalls in Aetiological Attribution
* **Victim Blaming**: Misattributing illness purely to patient's poor coping/personality.
* **Mother-Blaming**: Outdated psychoanalytic models falsely attributing disorders (e.g., Autism, Schizophrenia) to "refrigerator mothers".
* Essential to maintain neutral, evidence-based stance.

### The "Antipsychiatry" Critique (反精神病學)
* Critics (e.g., Szasz, Foucault) argue psychiatric aetiology is a tool for social control.
* Argue definitions of illness are culturally bound value judgements.
* While largely rejected by mainstream psychiatry, highlights need for cultural sensitivity and strict diagnostic criteria to prevent political abuse.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the difference between multifinality and equifinality in psychiatric aetiology? Answer: Multifinality means one cause leads to many possible outcomes (e.g. trauma leading to MDD or PTSD); equifinality means many different causes can lead to the same outcome (e.g. genetics or drugs leading to psychosis).
* Trap: Assuming the precipitating event is the sole cause of the illness. Pearl: The precipitating event only triggers the illness; the underlying predisposing factors (genetics, personality) determine the vulnerability and must be elicited in the history.
* Pitfall: Focusing only on precipitating factors during management planning. Perpetuating factors (e.g., substance misuse, social isolation) are often the most modifiable targets for active clinical intervention and must be addressed.
* Viva Question: How does Jaspers differentiate Erklären from Verstehen? Answer: Erklären is objective, scientific explanation (e.g., neurochemical imbalance), while Verstehen is empathic, psychological understanding of a reaction to an event.
* Trap: Conflating a stressor with a stress reaction. Pearl: A stressor is the objective external event (e.g., losing a job); the stress reaction is the internal physiological/psychological response (e.g., anxiety, insomnia).
