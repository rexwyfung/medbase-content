# Misuse of Drugs (藥物濫用)

**Source Text:** Shorted Oxford Psychiatry Textbook.pdf

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Substance Misuse (物質濫用)
* **Substance Use Disorder (物質使用障礙):** Maladaptive pattern of substance use → clinically significant impairment/distress.
* **Dependence (依賴):** Physiological (Tolerance 耐受性 + Withdrawal 戒斷) & psychological (craving, loss of control) adaptation.

### Biopsychosocial Etiologies (生理心理社會病因)
* **Biological:** Genetic predisposition (heritability ~40-60%), DA D2 receptor down-regulation, altered prefrontal cortex function.
* **Psychological:** Operant conditioning (+ve reinforcement from high, -ve reinforcement from relieving withdrawal), classical conditioning (cue-induced craving), maladaptive coping.
* **Social:** Peer pressure, easy availability, low socioeconomic status, adverse childhood events (ACEs).

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong (香港流行病學)
* **Prevalence:** Heroin (海洛英) historically dominant, but recent shift → psychotropic substances.
* **Common Substances:** Ketamine (氯胺酮/K仔), Methamphetamine (冰毒), Cocaine (可卡因), Cannabis (大麻).
* **Demographics:** ↑ prevalence among young adults & adolescents; ↑ hidden abuse at home.

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### The Mesolimbic Dopamine Reward Pathway (中腦邊緣多巴胺獎賞路徑)
* **Core Mechanism:** VTA (Ventral Tegmental Area) → Nucleus Accumbens (NAc).
* **Action:** All drugs of abuse ↑ DA release in NAc → intense reward/euphoria.
* **Chronic Use:** Down-regulation of DA receptors → blunted baseline reward (anhedonia) & ↑ craving.

### Substance-Specific Neurobiology (特定物質神經生物學)
* **Opioids:** Bind µ-opioid receptors → disinhibit VTA DA neurons.
* **Stimulants (Cocaine/Amphetamines):** Block DA reuptake or stimulate DA release.
* **Ketamine:** NMDA receptor antagonist → dissociative state.
* **Cannabis:** THC binds CB1 receptors → modulates DA release.

### Psychopathology of Addiction (成癮精神病理學)
* **Salience:** Drug cues hijack attention → uncontrollable urges.
* **Executive Dysfunction:** Impaired prefrontal inhibitory control → impulsive drug seeking despite -ve consequences.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History in Substance Misuse (物質濫用病史)
* **Substance Profile:** Exact drugs, onset, frequency, route (IV/smoked/oral), last use, typical daily dose.
* **Dependence Symptoms:** Craving, tolerance, withdrawal history (seizures/DTs).
* **Impact:** Functional decline (occupational, social, financial, legal).
* **Treatment History:** Previous detox attempts, longest abstinent period, relapse triggers.

### Mental State Examination (MSE) Findings (精神狀態檢查)
* **Appearance/Behavior:** Poor hygiene, track marks (IV use), tremor, pupillary changes (constricted in opioids, dilated in stimulants), psychomotor agitation/retardation.
* **Speech:** Slurred (depressants), pressured (stimulants).
* **Mood/Affect:** Labile, irritable, depressed, or euphoric.
* **Thought Content:** Paranoia (stimulants/cannabis), cravings.
* **Perception:** VH/AH/tactile hallucinations (e.g., formication in cocaine/meth).
* **Cognition:** Impaired attention, memory deficits.
* **Insight:** Usually poor/partial (denial/minimization).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differential Diagnoses (精神科鑑別診斷)
* **Primary Psychotic Disorders:** Schizophrenia (精神分裂症) (must differentiate from substance-induced psychosis; timeline is key).
* **Primary Mood Disorders:** Bipolar Disorder (躁鬱症) (manic episode vs. stimulant intoxication), Major Depressive Disorder (重性抑鬱症) (vs. depressant intoxication/stimulant withdrawal).
* **Anxiety Disorders:** Generalized Anxiety Disorder (vs. stimulant intoxication/depressant withdrawal).

### Organic & Medical Rule-Outs (器質性及內科排除)
* **Neurological:** Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI), CNS infections (HIV/Syphilis), epilepsy.
* **Metabolic/Endocrine:** Hypo/Hyperthyroidism, hepatic encephalopathy, hypoglycemia.
* **Toxicity:** Heavy metal poisoning, iatrogenic delirium.

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 Criteria for Substance Use Disorder (DSM-5診斷標準)
| Domain | Criteria (Need $\ge$ 2 in 12 months) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impaired Control** | 1. Use larger amounts/longer time<br>2. Persistent desire/unsuccessful cut down<br>3. Much time spent obtaining/using/recovering<br>4. Craving |
| **Social Impairment** | 5. Failure to fulfill major obligations<br>6. Continued use despite social/interpersonal problems<br>7. Giving up important activities |
| **Risky Use** | 8. Use in physically hazardous situations<br>9. Continued use despite physical/psychological problem |
| **Pharmacological** | 10. Tolerance<br>11. Withdrawal |
* **Severity:** Mild (2-3), Moderate (4-5), Severe ($\ge$ 6).

### Screening & Rating Scales (篩查及評估量表)
* **CAGE / FAST:** Primarily for alcohol, easily adapted.
* **ASSIST:** WHO scale for general substance involvement.
* **DAST-10:** Drug Abuse Screening Test.

### Baseline Organic Investigations (基準器質性檢查)
* **Toxicology:** Urine drug screen (UDS), blood alcohol/drug levels.
* **Blood:** CBC, LFTs (hepatitis), RFTs, clotting, infective screen (HIV, HBV, HCV, Syphilis).
* **Imaging:** CXR (TB in IV users), CT/MRI Brain (if focal neuro signs).

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Intoxication & Overdose Management (急性中毒及過量處理)
* **ABCs:** Airway, Breathing, Circulation.
* **Opioid Overdose:** Naloxone (納洛酮) IV/IM; monitor for rebound.
* **Stimulant Toxicity:** Benzodiazepines for agitation/seizures; antipsychotics (e.g., Haloperidol) for severe acute psychosis.
* **General:** Hydration, cooling (if hyperthermic), safe environment.

### Risk Assessment (風險評估)
* **Self:** High suicide risk (especially during comedown/withdrawal), accidental overdose, self-neglect, BBVs (Blood-Borne Viruses).
* **Others:** Violence/aggression (stimulant intoxication), neglect of dependents (child protection).

### Medico-Legal Framework (HK Mental Health Ordinance Cap 136) (香港精神健康條例)
* **Section 2 / 3:** Substance misuse *alone* is NOT grounds for compulsory admission. However, substance-induced psychosis or severe mood disturbance causing risk to self/others → warrant involuntary admission.
* **Child Safeguarding:** Mandatory reporting to SWD if child neglect is suspected.

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Pharmacotherapy for Relapse Prevention (預防復發藥物治療)
| Substance | Pharmacotherapy Options |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Opioids** | Methadone (美沙酮) maintenance (CCPS in HK), Buprenorphine, Naltrexone. |
| **Stimulants** | No approved specific pharmacotherapy. Treat comorbid psychopathology. |

### Psychological & Psychosocial Therapies (心理及社會治療)
* **Motivational Interviewing (MI):** Enhance intrinsic motivation; move through Stages of Change (Prochaska & DiClemente).
* **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT):** Relapse prevention, identify triggers, coping strategies.
* **Contingency Management:** Reward-based abstinence.
* **Rehabilitation:** Voluntary agencies (e.g., SARDA in HK), halfway houses, peer support groups (Narcotics Anonymous).

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Natural History of Addiction (成癮自然史)
* **Course:** Chronic, relapsing remitting.
* **Relapse:** Extremely common; should be reframed as a learning opportunity rather than absolute failure.

### Prognostic Indicators (預後指標)
* **Good Prognosis:** Late onset, short duration of use, good premorbid functioning, strong social support, stable employment, absence of severe comorbid psychopathology.
* **Poor Prognosis:** Early onset, IV use, severe personality disorder (antisocial), severe cognitive deficits, homelessness.

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Medical Complications (精神及內科併發症)
* **Psychiatric:** Substance-induced psychotic disorder, mood disorders, anxiety, persistent cognitive impairment (Methamphetamine/Ketamine).
* **Medical:**
  * IV use: Endocarditis, HIV, Hepatitis B/C, abscesses, DVT.
  * Ketamine specific: Ketamine-induced cystitis (urological emergency), biliary dilatation.
  * Stimulants: Myocardial infarction, stroke, dental decay ("meth mouth").

### Social & Occupational Sequelae (社會及職業後遺症)
* **Social:** Family breakdown, divorce, child neglect.
* **Occupational:** Job loss, poverty, homelessness.
* **Legal:** Incarceration, debt.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a substance-induced psychotic disorder from primary schizophrenia? Answer: Substance-induced psychosis usually resolves within days to weeks of abstinence; primary schizophrenia persists long after acute intoxication and withdrawal have resolved.
* Trap: Discharging a patient immediately after reversing an opioid overdose with Naloxone. Pearl: Naloxone has a shorter half-life than many opioids (e.g., heroin, methadone); the patient is at high risk of re-sedation and fatal respiratory depression and must be observed.
* Pitfall: Attempting cognitive behavioral therapy during the acute intoxication or severe withdrawal phase. Wait until the patient is medically stable, detoxified, and cognitively clear before initiating structured psychological interventions.
* Viva Question: Under the Hong Kong Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136), can you compulsorily admit a patient solely because they are heavily addicted to heroin? Answer: No, drug dependence alone is explicitly excluded from the definition of mental disorder for compulsory admission purposes; there must be a comorbid certifiable psychiatric condition presenting a risk to self or others.
