# Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (強迫症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (強迫症)
* **Core feature**: Recurrent, persistent, intrusive thoughts (Obsessions / 強迫思想) + repetitive, stereotyped behaviours (Compulsions / 強迫行為).
* **Key characteristic**: Subjective sense of struggle/resistance → patient recognizes thoughts/impulses as own (not inserted) but senseless/unwanted.
* **Result**: ↑ distress, severe functional impairment (e.g., obsessional slowness).

### Biopsychosocial Etiologies of OCD
* **Biological**: ↑ genetic concordance in monozygotic twins. Dysregulation in cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) circuit. 5-HT (serotonin) system dysfunction. Autoimmune mechanism (PANDAS) in paediatric subsets.
* **Psychological**: Maladaptive cognitive schemas (inflated responsibility, overestimation of threat, thought-action fusion). Failure of habituation to anxiety-provoking stimuli.
* **Social**: Early childhood trauma, stressful life events acting as precipitating factors.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: Lifetime prevalence ~1-2% globally and locally in HK.
* **Demographics**: Equal male-to-female ratio in adulthood; male predominance in childhood-onset.
* **Onset**: Bimodal (childhood & early adulthood). Mean age ~19 years.

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurobiology & Neurotransmitter Pathways
* **CSTC Loop**: Hyperactivity in orbitofrontal cortex (OFC), anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), and head of caudate nucleus.
* **Neurotransmitters**: ↓ 5-HT availability. DA (dopamine) hyperactivity in striatum (target for antipsychotic augmentation). Glutamate dysregulation in CSTC pathways.

### Psychopathology of Obsessions (強迫思想)
* **Definition**: Intrusive thoughts, ruminations, doubts, or impulses entering mind despite efforts to exclude.
* **Insight**: Recognized by patient as self-generated (distinguishes from delusions of thought insertion).
* **Themes**: 
  * Dirt/contamination (harm to self/others).
  * Aggression (urge to stab, shout obscenities).
  * Orderliness (symmetry).
  * Illness/Somatic.
  * Sexual (shameful practices).
  * Religious (blasphemy/scruples).

### Psychopathology of Compulsions (強迫行為)
* **Definition**: Repetitive, purposeful, stereotyped behaviours performed to ↓ anxiety/distress triggered by obsessions.
* **Mechanism**: Subjective urge to act + resistance. Yields transient relief → negative reinforcement.
* **Types**: 
  * Checking (safety/doors).
  * Cleaning (washing/dermatitis risk).
  * Counting (often silent, to prevent dreaded event).
  * Dressing (strict sequence/laying out clothes).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History for OCD Assessment
* **HPC**: Onset, duration, fluctuation of obsessions/compulsions. Time spent on rituals (>1 hr/day). Functional impairment.
* **Symptom profiling**: Ask for specific examples of intrusive thoughts and corresponding rituals. Ask about avoidance behaviours (e.g., avoiding knives, public toilets).
* **Past Psychiatric History**: Screen for tic disorders, Tourette's, depression, eating disorders. 

### Mental State Examination (MSE) Findings in OCD
* **Appearance & Behaviour**: Obsessional slowness, restlessness, observable repetitive motor rituals (e.g., tapping, checking), signs of self-harm/dermatitis from overwashing.
* **Speech**: May be hesitant if distracted by internal counting/ruminations.
* **Mood**: Subjective & objective anxiety, secondary depressed mood.
* **Thoughts (Form/Content)**: Content dominated by obsessional themes. Form is logical but repetitive. MUST differentiate from delusions (assess resistance and insight).
* **Insight**: Usually preserved. Patient recognizes thoughts are senseless/excessive (unlike psychosis).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differential Diagnoses for OCD
* **Schizophrenia (精神分裂症)**: Delusions of thought insertion lack resistance; patient believes thoughts are external. OCD patient knows thoughts are their own.
* **Depressive Ruminations**: Themes are mood-congruent (guilt, pessimism, past failures) without active resistance. OCD themes are egodystonic (unwanted/alien to nature).
* **Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder (OCPD)**: Egosyntonic traits (perfectionism, rigidity) without true obsessions/compulsions.
* **Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)**: Real-life worries, not bizarre/egodystonic obsessions.
* **Tic Disorders**: Involuntary, sudden motor movements lacking the cognitive "purpose" of compulsions.

### Organic and Medical Rule-Outs for OCD
* **PANDAS / PANS**: Sudden onset OCD post-streptococcal infection in children.
* **Neurological**: Sydenham's chorea, Huntington's disease, Frontotemporal dementia, post-encephalitic states.
* **Substance-Induced**: Stimulant abuse (cocaine/amphetamines) causing formication/picking.

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria for OCD

| Criterion | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Presence of obsessions, compulsions, or both. |
| **B** | Symptoms are time-consuming (>1 hr/day) or cause clinically significant distress/impairment. |
| **C** | Not attributable to physiological effects of a substance or medical condition. |
| **D** | Disturbance not better explained by another mental disorder. |
| **Specifiers** | *Insight*: Good/fair, poor, or absent/delusional beliefs.<br>*Tic-related*: Current or past history of tic disorder. |

### ICD-11 Diagnostic Features for OCD
* Emphasizes temporal requirement: Symptoms present on most days for $\ge 2$ weeks.
* Categorized under "Obsessive-compulsive or related disorders" (moved away from anxiety disorders).

### Clinical Rating Scales for OCD
* **Y-BOCS (Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale)**: Gold standard clinician-rated scale. Assesses time, interference, distress, resistance, and control.
* **OCI-R (Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory - Revised)**: Self-report scale for symptom severity and subtypes.

### Baseline Organic Rule-Out Investigations for OCD
* **Bloods**: FBC, TFTs, CRP/ESR, ASOT (if PANDAS suspected).
* **Neuroimaging (CT/MRI Brain)**: If late-onset (>50 yrs), atypical presentation, or focal neurological signs +ve.

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Psychiatric Management & Risk Assessment in OCD
* **Risk to Self**: ↑ risk of secondary depression and suicide due to severe distress/demoralization. Risk of severe dermatological injury (bleeding/infection) from washing compulsions.
* **Risk to Others**: Aggressive obsessions (urge to harm) are extremely distressing to patient, but actual risk of acting on them is exceedingly low.
* **Acute Action**: Treat secondary depression. Provide psychoeducation to patient and family to stop accommodating rituals.

### Medico-Legal Application of HK Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136) in OCD
* **Voluntary vs Involuntary**: Overwhelming majority treated as voluntary patients.
* **Involuntary Admission (Form 1 / Form 2 / Form 3)**: Rarely applied unless severe comorbid psychotic depression, imminent suicide risk, or total self-neglect (e.g., inability to eat/drink due to contamination fears) where patient refuses care and meets criteria for "mental disorder" warranting detention for health/safety.

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Pharmacotherapy for Chronic OCD Management

| Drug Class | Examples | Clinical Pearls & Side Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SSRI (1st Line)** | Fluoxetine, Fluvoxamine, Sertraline | Require ↑ doses (e.g., Fluoxetine 60-80mg) and longer trial duration (8-12 weeks). SE: GI upset, sexual dysfunction, insomnia. |
| **TCA (2nd Line)** | Clomipramine | Highly serotonergic. SE: Anticholinergic (dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention), cardiotoxicity (prolonged QTc). Fatal in overdose. |
| **Atypical Antipsychotics** | Risperidone, Aripiprazole | Used as augmentation in SSRI-resistant cases or tic-related OCD. SE: Metabolic syndrome, EPS, akathisia. |

### Psychotherapy & Behavioural Interventions
* **CBT with ERP (Exposure and Response Prevention)**: Gold standard. 
  * *Exposure*: Graded exposure to feared stimuli (e.g., touching dirty surface).
  * *Response Prevention*: Active blocking of compulsion (e.g., no handwashing for 2 hours). Leads to habituation.
* **Cognitive Therapy**: Targets maladaptive appraisals (thought-action fusion, over-responsibility).

### Refractory OCD Management & Somatic Therapies
* **Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)**: For extreme, treatment-refractory cases. Targets include anterior limb of internal capsule or subthalamic nucleus.
* **Ablative Neurosurgery**: Anterior cingulotomy or capsulotomy (rarely performed).

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Natural Course of Illness
* **Onset**: Often insidious. Delays in seeking treatment are common (average 5-10 years) due to shame/stigma.
* **Trajectory**: Typically chronic and fluctuating. Exacerbated by psychosocial stress.
* **Remission**: Spontaneous complete remission is rare in adults (<20%). Mild episodic cases have better outcomes.

### Prognostic Indicators
* **Good Prognosis**: Mild symptoms, good premorbid social/occupational adjustment, episodic nature, prominent environmental precipitants.
* **Poor Prognosis**: Early (childhood) onset, male gender, yielding to compulsions, poor insight/delusional intensity, comorbid personality disorder (schizotypal), severe tics/Tourette's.

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Social Complications
* **Psychiatric Comorbidities**: Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) (>50%), Agoraphobia, Social Anxiety Disorder, Substance Use Disorder (self-medication).
* **Social/Occupational**: Marital breakdown (family accommodation of rituals causes severe strain), job loss due to obsessional slowness/time spent on compulsions, severe social isolation.

### Psychotropic Drug Side Effects in Treatment
* **Serotonin Syndrome**: Risk if SSRIs combined with Clomipramine or MAOIs (mental status changes, autonomic hyperactivity, neuromuscular abnormalities).
* **Clomipramine Toxicity**: Narrow therapeutic index. Risk of seizures and fatal arrhythmias in overdose. Monitor ECG.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate an obsession from a delusion? Answer: Obsessions are egodystonic, recognized as self-generated, and resisted by the patient; delusions are egosyntonic, firmly held as absolute truth, and often perceived as externally imposed.
* Trap: Confusing depressive ruminations with obsessions. Pearl: Depressive ruminations are mood-congruent, pessimistic themes that the patient does not actively resist, whereas obsessions are senseless, intrusive, and actively resisted.
* Pitfall: Under-dosing SSRIs in OCD management. SSRIs in OCD typically require significantly higher doses (e.g., Sertraline 200mg, Fluoxetine 80mg) and longer trials (12 weeks) compared to MDD management.
* Viva Question: What is the defining behavioural therapy for OCD? Answer: Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP), which breaks the negative reinforcement cycle by preventing the anxiety-reducing compulsion.
* Trap: Assuming aggressive obsessions mean the patient is dangerous. Pearl: Patients with OCD have an intense fear of acting on aggressive obsessions and almost never do; they are egodystonic, unlike the egosyntonic aggression seen in antisocial personality disorder.
