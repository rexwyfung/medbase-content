# Schizophrenia (精神分裂症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Schizophrenia
* **Primary Psychotic Disorder (原發性精神病)**
* **Characterized by:** Reality distortion (delusions, hallucinations), disorganized thought/behavior, and negative symptoms.
* **Duration:** >1 month active symptoms, >6 months continuous disturbance (DSM-5).

### Biopsychosocial Etiologies
* **Biological:**
* **Genetics:** ↑ concordance in monozygotic twins (~40-50%); polygenic (e.g., COMT, dysbindin).
* **Neurodevelopmental:** Obstetric complications, maternal influenza (2nd trimester), winter birth.
* **Neuroanatomical:** Ventricular enlargement, ↓ cortical volume (superior temporal gyrus, prefrontal cortex).
* **Psychological:**
* **Cognitive biases:** Jumping to conclusions, impaired theory of mind.
* **Social/Environmental:**
* **Urbanicity & Migration:** ↑ risk in urban areas and immigrant populations.
* **Cannabis use:** ↑ risk (especially COMT Val/Val carriers).
* **High Expressed Emotion (EE):** Hostility, criticism, over-involvement → ↑ relapse rates.

### Hong Kong Epidemiology
* **Lifetime risk:** ~1% (matches global average).
* **Peak onset:** Males 15-25; Females 25-35.
* **Local Initiatives:** **EASY (Early Assessment Service for Young People with Early Psychosis)** programme by HK Hospital Authority for targeted early intervention (aged 15-25).

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurobiology & Dopamine (DA) Pathways
* **Mesolimbic pathway:** ↑ DA (hyperactivity) → **Positive Symptoms** (+ve).
* **Mesocortical pathway:** ↓ DA (hypoactivity) → **Negative Symptoms** (-ve) & Cognitive deficits.
* **Nigrostriatal pathway:** Normal in disease; D2 blockade → **EPSE** (錐體外系症狀).
* **Tuberoinfundibular pathway:** Normal in disease; D2 blockade → **Hyperprolactinemia** (高泌乳素血症).
* **Other Receptors:** ↓ NMDA (Glutamate) activity, 5-HT2A abnormalities.

### Psychopathology: First-Rank Symptoms (Schneider)
* **Auditory Hallucinations (聽覺幻覺):**
* **3rd Person:** Voices discussing patient (e.g., "He is eating").
* **Running commentary:** Voices narrating actions.
* **Gedankenlautwerden / Écho de la pensée:** Hearing thoughts spoken aloud.
* **Thought Possession Delusions (思想妄想):**
* **Thought Insertion:** Alien thoughts put into mind.
* **Thought Withdrawal:** Thoughts extracted by external force.
* **Thought Broadcasting:** Thoughts audible to others (via telepathy/radio).
* **Delusions of Control / Passivity (被動妄想):** Actions, impulses, or affect controlled by external agency.
* **Delusional Perception (妄想性知覺):** Normal perception → instantaneous, bizarre delusional interpretation.

### Other Key Psychopathology
* **Negative Symptoms (-ve):** Avolition, Alogia (poverty of speech), Anhedonia, Affective flattening, Asociality.
* **Formal Thought Disorder (思維形式障礙):** Loosening of associations, Knight's move (derailment), Neologisms, Thought block.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History Key Elements
* **HPI:** Onset speed (acute vs insidious), duration of untreated psychosis (DUP).
* **Premorbid personality:** Schizoid or schizotypal traits (loner, eccentric).
* **Substance history:** Exclude amphetamines, cannabis, LSD.
* **Family history:** 1st-degree relatives with psychosis or bipolar disorder.
* **Social impact:** Decline in academic/occupational trajectory.

### MSE: Appearance & Behavior
* **General:** Self-neglect, bizarre attire (e.g., tin foil hat for 'rays').
* **Catatonia (緊張症):** Catalepsy (waxy flexibility), posturing, grimacing, negativism, echopraxia, ambitendence.
* **Motor:** Restlessness (akathisia), tardive dyskinesia (medication effects).

### MSE: Speech & Thought
* **Speech:** Poverty of speech (alogia), latency of response.
* **Thought Stream:** Pressure of thought vs poverty of thought, sudden thought block.
* **Thought Form:** Tangentiality, verbigeration (word salad), perseveration.
* **Thought Content:** Primary vs secondary delusions, persecutory, grandiose, bizarre.

### MSE: Perception & Insight
* **Perception:** Hallucinations (modality: auditory most common, somatic/haptic). Vis/olfactory suggest organic.
* **Insight:** Usually profoundly impaired (Reality distortion). Assess awareness of illness, need for treatment, and attribution of symptoms.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differentials
* **Schizoaffective Disorder:** Prominent mood episodes concurrent with active phase + >2 weeks delusions/hallucinations *without* mood symptoms.
* **Bipolar Disorder (with psychotic features):** Psychosis restricted to manic/depressive episodes.
* **Delusional Disorder:** Non-bizarre or circumscribed delusions, relatively preserved functioning, no prominent hallucinations/thought disorder.
* **Substance-Induced Psychotic Disorder:** Amphetamines, cannabis, cocaine, hallucinogens.

### Organic/Medical Rule-Outs
* **Neurological:** Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (TLE), space-occupying lesions, Huntington's disease, Wilson's disease.
* **Infectious/Autoimmune:** Neurosyphilis, HIV encephalopathy, Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis.
* **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Thyroid dysfunction (hyper/hypo), acute intermittent porphyria.
* **Toxic/latrogenic:** Steroid-induced psychosis, heavy metal poisoning.

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 vs ICD-11 Criteria Table

| Feature | DSM-5 (Schizophrenia) | ICD-11 (Schizophrenia) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | >1 month active symptoms, >6 months continuous signs. | >1 month of core symptoms. |
| **Core Symptoms (Need $\ge$2)** | Delusions, Hallucinations, Disorganized speech, Grossly disorganized/catatonic behavior, Negative symptoms. | Similar; explicitly includes passivity phenomena and thought disorder. |
| **Mandatory Symptom** | At least one must be Delusions, Hallucinations, or Disorganized speech. | At least one core symptom (delusions, hallucinations, passivity, thought disorder). |
| **Functional Decline** | Required (work, interpersonal, or self-care). | Usually associated with functional impairment (not strictly mandatory for dx). |
| **Subtypes (e.g., Paranoid)**| Removed in DSM-5 (uses dimensional severity). | Removed in ICD-11 (uses symptom qualifiers). |

### Rating Scales
* **PANSS (Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale):** Gold standard for research/clinical severity.
* **BPRS (Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale):** Quick clinical assessment.

### Investigations (Organic Rule-Out)
* **Bloods:** CBC, RFT, LFT, TFT, VDRL/syphilis, HIV, Vitamin B12/Folate, autoimmune panel (Anti-NMDA) if suspected.
* **Urine:** Toxicology screen (amphetamines, cannabis, cocaine).
* **Imaging:** CT/MRI Brain (rule out SOL, hydrocephalus, demyelination).
* **EEG:** If temporal lobe epilepsy or delirium suspected.

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Risk Assessment
* **Harm to Self:** Command hallucinations dictating self-harm, post-psychotic depression.
* **Harm to Others:** Persecutory delusions acting on specific targets, delusional jealousy.
* **Self-Neglect:** Failure to eat/drink due to delusions of poisoning.

### HK Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136) Framework
* **Form 1/2/3 Involuntary Admission (強制入院):**
* **Form 1:** Application by relative or registered medical practitioner (often Medical Social Worker/AMHP).
* **Form 2:** Medical recommendation by a registered medical practitioner.
* **Form 3:** Medical recommendation by an approved doctor (often a Psychiatrist).
* **Criteria:** Suffering from mental disorder + nature/degree warrants hospital treatment + necessary for health/safety of patient or protection of others + voluntary admission refused.
* **Duration:** Detention for observation $\le$7 days (Section 31).

### Acute Pharmacological Control
* **Rapid Tranquilization (HK Protocol):**
* **Oral:** Haloperidol 5mg + Lorazepam 1-2mg.
* **IM (if refused PO):** Haloperidol 5mg IM + Promethazine 50mg IM (reduces EPSE).
* **Alternative:** IM Olanzapine (do NOT mix with IM benzodiazepines due to respiratory depression risk).

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Pharmacotherapy (Antipsychotics)
* **First-Generation Antipsychotics (FGAs - Typical):** Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine. (D2 antagonists → ↑ EPSE).
* **Second-Generation Antipsychotics (SGAs - Atypical):** Risperidone, Olanzapine, Quetiapine, Aripiprazole. (5-HT2A/D2 antagonists → ↓ EPSE, ↑ Metabolic syndrome).
* **Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia (TRS):** Failure of $\ge$2 antipsychotics (at adequate dose/duration) → Start **Clozapine (氯氮平)**. Requires strict absolute neutrophil count (ANC) monitoring (risk of agranulocytosis).
* **Long-Acting Injections (LAI):** Depot Paliperidone, Risperidone, Flupentixol. Indicated for poor compliance.

### Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT)
* **Indications:** Severe catatonia, intractable severe psychosis, prominent suicidal drive, marked depressive overlay.

### Psychotherapy & Social Rehabilitation
* **CBT for Psychosis (CBTp):** Target delusion conviction, manage distress of auditory hallucinations.
* **Family Intervention:** Education, ↓ High Expressed Emotion (EE), conflict resolution.
* **Social Rehabilitation (HK HA Services):** Medical Social Worker (MSW) referral, Community Psychiatric Nursing (CPN), halfway houses, supported employment, Vocational Training Council (VTC) programs.

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Course of Illness
* **Prodromal Phase:** Social withdrawal, subtle cognitive/perceptual changes months/years before acute episode.
* **Active Phase:** Florid positive symptoms.
* **Residual Phase:** Predominant negative symptoms and cognitive blunting.
* **Relapsing/Remitting:** Most common pattern. Each relapse leads to further baseline cognitive/functional decline.

### Prognostic Factors (Good vs Poor)
* **Good Prognosis:** Female sex, older age of onset, acute onset, clear precipitants, married/good social support, prominent mood symptoms, absence of structural brain abnormalities.
* **Poor Prognosis:** Male sex, insidious onset, early age of onset, prolonged DUP, prominent negative symptoms, poor premorbid personality (schizoid), high family EE, substance abuse.

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Social Complications
* **Psychiatric:** ↑ Suicide risk (approx 5-10% lifetime), post-psychotic depression, comorbid substance use disorder (nicotine, cannabis).
* **Social:** Downward social drift, unemployment, homelessness, stigma, family caregiver burden.

### Psychotropic Side Effects Table

| Side Effect Category | Specific Presentation | Offending Agent / Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Extrapyramidal (EPSE)** | Dystonia (torticollis, oculogyric crisis). | Acute: IM Anticholinergic (Benztropine). |
| **EPSE** | Akathisia (motor restlessness). | ↓ Dose, add Propranolol or Clonazepam. |
| **EPSE** | Parkinsonism (tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia). | Add oral Anticholinergic (Benzhexol). |
| **EPSE** | Tardive Dyskinesia (oro-facial choreoathetosis). | Stop anticholinergics; switch to Clozapine. |
| **Metabolic Syndrome** | ↑ Weight, ↑ Blood Glucose, ↑ Lipids. | SGAs (Olanzapine, Clozapine). Diet, Metformin. |
| **Endocrine** | Hyperprolactinemia (galactorrhea, amenorrhea). | Risperidone, FGAs. Switch to Aripiprazole. |
| **Life-Threatening** | NMS (Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome). | Stop AP, IV fluids, Dantrolene, Bromocriptine. |
| **Life-Threatening** | Agranulocytosis (neutropenia). | Clozapine. Stop immediately, broad-spectrum Abx. |

## 10. Examiner's Pearls (High-yield viva questions and common pitfalls)

* Viva Question: What differentiates a primary delusion from a secondary delusion? Answer: A primary delusion (e.g., delusional perception) arises instantaneously without prior psychological context, whereas a secondary delusion is an understandable consequence of another morbid experience (e.g., paranoia due to hearing threatening voices).
* Trap: Assuming visual hallucinations are typical of Schizophrenia. Pearl: Visual hallucinations strongly suggest an organic etiology (e.g., delirium, Lewy body dementia, substance intoxication) and demand thorough medical workup.
* Pitfall: Ignoring the impact of negative symptoms. While positive symptoms lead to acute admission, it is the negative symptoms (avolition, anhedonia) and cognitive deficits that dictate the patient's long-term functional impairment and social drift.
* Viva Question: What is the most critical monitoring parameter when initiating Clozapine? Answer: Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) via regular blood tests, to detect life-threatening agranulocytosis.
* Trap: Prescribing anticholinergics (e.g., benzhexol) to treat tardive dyskinesia (TD). Pearl: Anticholinergics can *worsen* TD. The correct management is to taper the offending drug and consider switching to an SGA with lower TD liability, like Clozapine.
* Pitfall: Mistaking akathisia for worsening psychotic agitation. Increasing the antipsychotic dose for akathisia will worsen the restlessness; correct action is to lower the dose or add a beta-blocker (propranolol).
