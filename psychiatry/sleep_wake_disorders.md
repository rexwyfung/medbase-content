# Sleep-Wake Disorders (睡眠覺醒障礙)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Sleep-Wake Disorders (睡眠覺醒障礙)
* **Sleep-Wake Disorders (睡眠覺醒障礙)**: Syndromes involving clinically significant disruption in sleep quality, timing, or amount, resulting in daytime distress/impairment.
* **Key Subtypes**: **Insomnia (失眠)**, **Hypersomnolence (嗜睡)**, **Narcolepsy (發作性嗜睡病)**, **Breathing-Related Sleep Disorders (呼吸相關睡眠障礙)**, **Circadian Rhythm Disorders (生理時鐘睡眠障礙)**, **Parasomnias (異睡症)**.

### Biopsychosocial Etiologies of Sleep Pathology
* **Biological**: Genetic predisposition (e.g., HLA-DQB1*06:02 in Narcolepsy), hyperarousal states, hypocretin (orexin) deficiency, aging ↓ slow-wave sleep (SWS), medical comorbidities (pain, nocturia).
* **Psychological**: Maladaptive conditioning (bed = wakefulness locus), rumination, anticipatory anxiety regarding sleep loss.
* **Social**: Shift work, jet lag, poor **sleep hygiene (睡眠衛生)**, excessive screen time (blue light ↓ melatonin), high-stress HK work culture.

### Hong Kong Epidemiology of Insomnia & Sleep Apnea
* **Prevalence**: ~10-20% of HK adults experience chronic insomnia; higher in females & elderly.
* **OSA Prevalence**: ~4-5% in middle-aged HK males, strongly linked to ↑ BMI & craniofacial structure (retrognathia).
* **Comorbidity**: >50% of sleep disorders in HK HA psychiatric clinics present with comorbid **Major Depressive Disorder (重性抑鬱症)** or **Anxiety (焦慮症)**.

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurobiology of the Sleep-Wake Cycle
* **Two-Process Model**: Process C (Circadian rhythm via Suprachiasmatic Nucleus [SCN]) + Process S (Homeostatic sleep drive via adenosine accumulation).
* **Wakefulness**: Ascending Reticular Activating System (ARAS) projects to cortex.
* **Sleep**: Ventrolateral Preoptic Nucleus (VLPO) in hypothalamus inhibits ARAS.

### Neurotransmitter Pathways in Sleep Regulation
* **Wake-Promoting**: ↑ Orexin (Hypocretin), ↑ Histamine, ↑ DA, ↑ NA, ↑ ACh, ↑ 5-HT.
* **Sleep-Promoting**: ↑ GABA (via VLPO), ↑ Adenosine (inhibits basal forebrain), ↑ Melatonin (pineal gland, regulates SCN), ↑ Galanin.
* **REM Regulation**: Driven by ACh (pons); inhibited by NA (locus coeruleus) & 5-HT (raphe nuclei).

### Psychopathology of Sleep Architecture Disruption
* **Depression**: ↓ REM latency, ↑ REM density, ↓ SWS, early morning awakening (EMA).
* **Narcolepsy**: REM intrusion into wakefulness → cataplexy, **hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations (入睡前/醒後幻覺)**, sleep paralysis.
* **Anxiety**: ↑ Sleep latency (initial insomnia), frequent awakenings (middle insomnia).

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History for Sleep-Wake Disturbances
* **Sleep Pattern**: Bedtime, sleep latency (>30 min is +ve), number of awakenings, wake after sleep onset (WASO), final wake time, total sleep time (TST).
* **Daytime Impact**: Fatigue, ↓ concentration, excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS), naps.
* **Pre-sleep Cognitions**: Rumination, anxiety about not sleeping.
* **Psychiatric Screening**: Rule out mood/anxiety/psychotic features.
* **Substance Hx**: Caffeine, alcohol (causes sleep fragmentation), nicotine, hypnotics (rebound insomnia).

### Collateral History & Sleep Diary Assessment
* **Bed Partner Hx (CRITICAL)**: Snoring, witnessed apneas, gasping, periodic limb movements (PLMD), REM sleep behavior disorder (RBD) violence.
* **Sleep Diary/Actigraphy**: 2-week continuous log to establish baseline efficiency [Sleep Efficiency = (TST / Time in Bed) x 100]. Target >85%.

### MSE Findings in Sleep Deprivation
* **Appearance**: Yawning, periorbital dark circles, micro-sleeps during interview.
* **Affect/Mood**: Irritable, emotionally labile, subjective distress.
* **Cognition**: ↓ Sustained attention (poor serial 7s), sluggish psychomotor speed, transient ↓ short-term memory.
* **Perception**: Transient visual illusions or hypnagogic phenomena (if severely deprived).

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differential Diagnoses for Insomnia
* **Major Depressive Disorder**: Typically EMA, ↓ REM latency.
* **Generalized Anxiety Disorder / PTSD**: Initial insomnia, hyperarousal, nightmares.
* **Bipolar Mania**: ↓ Need for sleep (feels rested despite <3 hrs) vs insomnia (feels fatigued).
* **Schizophrenia**: Severe sleep fragmentation, circadian reversal during acute psychosis.

### Organic & General Medical Rule-Outs for Sleep Disturbance
* **Neurological**: Parkinson's (RBD), Alzheimer's (sundowning), restless legs syndrome (RLS).
* **Cardiopulmonary**: Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), CHF (orthopnea), COPD, asthma.
* **Endocrine/Metabolic**: Hyperthyroidism, menopause (hot flashes), GERD.
* **Pain Syndromes**: Osteoarthritis, neuropathic pain.

### Substance-Induced Sleep-Wake Disorders
* **Alcohol**: ↓ Sleep latency initially, but ↑ sympathetic drive → severe rebound WASO & ↓ REM.
* **Stimulants**: Cocaine, amphetamines, methylphenidate → severe initial insomnia.
* **Withdrawal**: Benzodiazepines/Z-drugs, opioids, cannabis → REM rebound, nightmares, extreme insomnia.

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 Criteria for Insomnia Disorder (失眠症)
| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A. Core Symptom** | Dissatisfaction with sleep quantity/quality with $\ge$ 1: difficulty initiating, difficulty maintaining, or early-morning awakening. |
| **B. Impairment** | Causes clinically significant distress or daytime impairment. |
| **C. Frequency** | Occurs $\ge$ 3 nights per week. |
| **D. Duration** | Present for $\ge$ 3 months. |
| **E. Opportunity** | Occurs despite adequate opportunity for sleep. |
| **F. Exclusions** | Not better explained by narcolepsy, OSA, parasomnia, substance, or coexisting medical/psychiatric disorder (though can be diagnosed concurrently if independent). |

### ICD-11 Classification of Sleep-Wake Disorders
* Now structurally separate in ICD-11 (moved out of Mental/Behavioral chapter).
* Focuses on phenotypic presentation (e.g., Chronic Insomnia, Hypersomnolence Disorder, Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders).

### Standardized Rating Scales for Sleep
* **Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS)**: Measures daytime sleepiness; score >10 indicates EDS.
* **Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI)**: Assesses sleep quality over 1 month; score >5 = poor sleep.
* **Insomnia Severity Index (ISI)**: 7-item scale evaluating insomnia severity and distress.

### Polysomnography (PSG) & Organic Rule-Out Investigations
* **PSG Indications**: Mandatory for suspected OSA, Narcolepsy (Multiple Sleep Latency Test [MSLT] shows sleep latency <8 min & $\ge$2 Sleep-Onset REM Periods [SOREMPs]), RBD, or PLMD. NOT routinely indicated for primary insomnia.
* **Labs**: Ferritin (rule out iron deficiency in RLS), TFTs (rule out hyper/hypothyroidism), UDS (substance screen).

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Pharmacological Management of Insomnia
* **Goal**: Rapid relief of severe distress to prevent transition to chronic insomnia; limit use to 2-4 weeks.
* **Z-Drugs**: Zolpidem (↑ GABA-A affinity). Fast onset, short half-life.
* **Short-acting BZDs**: Lorazepam.
* **Melatonin Agonists**: Ramelteon (MT1/MT2 agonist), useful for circadian rhythm resetting.

### Acute Risk Assessment in Sleep Deprivation
* **Suicide Risk**: Severe insomnia is an independent predictor of suicidal ideation and completed suicide.
* **Occupational/Driving Hazard**: ↑↑ Risk of MVAs and machinery accidents; mandate driving cessation if EDS/Narcolepsy is present until stabilized.
* **Psychosis Trigger**: Acute profound sleep deprivation can precipitate manic episodes or transient psychosis.

### HK Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136) & Involuntary Admission Limits
* **Cap 136**: Primary sleep disorders *alone* do NOT fulfill criteria for compulsory admission (Section 2/3) as they do not constitute severe mental illness warranting detention.
* **Exception**: If insomnia is a symptom of a severe psychotic or manic relapse posing imminent risk to self/others, detention is based on the primary Axis I disorder, not the insomnia.

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Pharmacotherapy for Chronic Sleep-Wake Disorders
| Drug Class | Examples | Indication & Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sedating Antidepressants** | Mirtazapine, Trazodone | Off-label for insomnia, useful if comorbid depression. Trazodone risk: priapism. |
| **DORA (Orexin Antagonists)** | Suvorexant, Lemborexant | Blocks wake-pathways. ↓ rebound insomnia. |
| **Dopamine Agonists** | Pramipexole, Ropinirole | 1st-line for Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS). |
| **Stimulants/Wake-Promoters**| Modafinil, Methylphenidate | 1st-line for Narcolepsy daytime sleepiness. |
| **Sodium Oxybate** | Xyrem (GHB) | Treats cataplexy & consolidates nighttime sleep in Narcolepsy. |

### Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)
* **Status**: Gold standard 1st-line treatment for chronic insomnia; superior to meds long-term.
* **Sleep Restriction**: Limit time in bed to match actual sleep time → ↑ sleep drive → gradual extension.
* **Stimulus Control**: Bed is for sleep/sex ONLY. Leave bed if awake >20 mins. No clock-watching.
* **Cognitive Restructuring**: Challenge catastrophic thoughts ("If I don't sleep 8 hours, I will fail").

### Sleep Hygiene & Social Interventions
* **Environment**: Cool, dark, quiet room.
* **Routine**: Strict consistent wake-time (anchors circadian rhythm), regardless of sleep duration.
* **Avoidance**: No caffeine >2PM, no heavy meals/alcohol before bed, ↓ blue light screens 2 hrs pre-sleep.

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Natural Course of Insomnia Disorder
* **Trajectory**: Highly prone to chronicity. ~40-50% of acute insomnia transitions to chronic.
* **Relapse**: Stress-induced relapse is common even after successful CBT-I.
* **Bidirectionality**: Chronic insomnia ↑ risk of developing new-onset major depression 2-fold.

### Prognostic Indicators in Sleep-Wake Disorders
* **Good Prognosis**: Identifiable/removable acute stressor, high compliance with CBT-I, good sleep hygiene.
* **Poor Prognosis**: Heavy reliance on BZDs/Z-drugs, persistent maladaptive cognitions, untreated medical comorbidities (e.g., chronic pain, OSA).

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Cardiometabolic Complications
* **Psychiatric**: ↑ Relapse rate for Mood Disorders, ↑ Suicidality, substance abuse (self-medication with alcohol).
* **Cardiometabolic (esp. OSA & short sleep duration)**: ↑ Hypertension, ↑ stroke, insulin resistance, ↑ obesity risk (↓ leptin, ↑ ghrelin).

### Social & Occupational Complications
* **Cognitive Impairment**: ↓ Executive function, poor memory consolidation.
* **Occupational**: ↑ Absenteeism, ↓ productivity, workplace accidents.
* **Interpersonal**: Bed partner distress (snoring, thrashing), marital conflict.

### Hypnotic & Sedative Drug Side Effects
* **Z-Drugs/BZDs**: Tolerance, dependence, withdrawal seizures (abrupt cessation), cognitive blunting, falls in the elderly (hip fractures).
* **Complex Sleep Behaviors**: Somnambulism (sleepwalking), sleep-eating, or sleep-driving with amnesia (FDA Black Box warning for Z-drugs).
* **Respiratory Depression**: Exacerbation of OSA with BZDs (contraindicated).

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the classic tetrad of narcolepsy symptoms? Answer: Excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations, and sleep paralysis.
* Trap: Prescribing benzodiazepines as a long-term solution for chronic insomnia. Pearl: CBT-I is the evidence-based 1st-line treatment for chronic insomnia; hypnotic medications should be restricted to short-term or intermittent use to prevent tolerance and dependence.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing primary insomnia in a middle-aged, overweight male without obtaining collateral history. Always ask the bed partner about snoring and witnessed apneas to rule out Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), as prescribing sedatives can worsen airway collapse.
* Viva Question: What is the mechanism of action of Suvorexant? Answer: It is a Dual Orexin Receptor Antagonist (DORA) that promotes sleep by blocking the wake-promoting neuropeptide orexin.
* Trap: Assuming early morning awakening is pathognomonic for depression. Pearl: Always differentiate between MDD and Advanced Sleep Phase Syndrome, which is common in the elderly where the entire circadian rhythm shifts earlier.
