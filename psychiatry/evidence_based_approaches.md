# Evidence-Based Approaches to Psychiatry (循證精神病學)

## 1. Topic Definition, Scope & Relevance in Hong Kong

### Definition of Evidence-Based Medicine in Psychiatry
* **Evidence-Based Medicine (循證醫學)**: Conscientious, explicit, and judicious use of current best evidence in making decisions about care of individual patients.
* **Core Goal**: ↓ reliance on intuition/unsystematic experience → ↑ objective, data-driven clinical decision-making.
* **Psychiatric Application**: Translating epidemiological data, neurobiological research, and clinical trial outcomes into individualized biopsychosocial care plans.

### Relevance in Hong Kong Hospital Authority (HA) Practice
* **Resource Allocation**: Guides **Hospital Authority (醫院管理局)** Drug Advisory Committee (DAC) in approving novel psychotropics (e.g., Esketamine for TRD) for the HA Formulary.
* **Standardization**: Drives development of local clinical protocols (e.g., HKCPsych guidelines for Schizophrenia and Major Depressive Disorder) → ↓ practice variation across HK clusters.
* **Cultural Context**: EBM must be adapted to local epidemiology (e.g., specific genetic polymorphisms in Han Chinese affecting CYP450 metabolism → adjusting psychotropic dosing).

## 2. Core Theoretical Principles & Mechanisms (Psychological, Pharmacological, or Legal)

### The Triad of Evidence-Based Practice (EBP)
* **Research Evidence (研究證據)**: Highest quality clinically relevant research (e.g., RCTs, Meta-analyses).
* **Clinical Expertise (臨床經驗)**: Clinician's accumulated experience, education, and clinical skills.
* **Patient Values (病人意願)**: Patient's unique preferences, cultural background (e.g., TCM beliefs), and expectations.

### Hierarchy of Clinical Evidence Mechanism
* **Principle**: Not all evidence is equal; methodological rigor determines validity.
* **Filtering Mechanism**: Higher levels of evidence effectively ↓ selection bias, ↓ confounding factors, and ↑ statistical power.

## 3. Key Classifications, Frameworks & Components

### Hierarchy of Evidence Classification
| Level | Study Design (研究設計) | Key Characteristics & Psychiatric Utility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | **Systematic Review (系統綜述) / Meta-Analysis** | Highest validity. Pools RCT data (e.g., Cochrane reviews on SSRI efficacy). |
| **II** | **Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) (隨機對照試驗)** | Blinding + Randomization → demonstrates causality (e.g., CBT vs. SSRI). |
| **III** | **Cohort Study (世代研究)** | Observational, longitudinal. Tracks exposed vs. unexposed (e.g., childhood trauma → adult depression). |
| **IV** | **Case-Control Study (病例對照研究)** | Retrospective. Good for rare outcomes (e.g., evaluating suicide risk factors). |
| **V** | **Cross-Sectional Study (橫斷面研究)** | Snapshot in time. Good for prevalence (e.g., HK Mental Morbidity Survey). |
| **VI** | **Case Series / Case Report (病例報告)** | Descriptive, high bias. Useful for novel adverse effects (e.g., clozapine-induced myocarditis). |
| **VII** | **Expert Opinion (專家意見)** | Lowest validity. Based on clinical experience without systematic critical appraisal. |

### PICO Framework for Formulating Clinical Questions
| Component | Description | Example in Psychiatry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P (Population/Patient)** | Target clinical demographic | Final-year HKDSE students with GAD |
| **I (Intervention)** | Proposed treatment/test | Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT) |
| **C (Comparison)** | Alternative/Standard of care | Standard SSRI therapy (e.g., Escitalopram) |
| **O (Outcome)** | Measurable clinical endpoint | ↓ HAM-A scores at 8 weeks |

## 4. Clinical Indications & Standardized Assessment Tools

### Clinical Indications for EBM Application
* **Diagnostic Uncertainty**: Evaluating sensitivity/specificity of screening tools (e.g., MoCA vs. MMSE for early dementia).
* **Treatment Selection**: Choosing 1st-line vs 2nd-line agents based on efficacy/tolerability profiles (e.g., selecting atypical antipsychotics).
* **Prognostication**: Estimating relapse rates or suicide risk based on validated epidemiological cohorts.

### Standardized Tools for Critical Appraisal
* **CASP (Critical Appraisal Skills Programme) Checklists**: Validates methodological quality of specific study designs (RCTs, SRs).
* **GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation)**: Standardized framework rating quality of evidence and strength of healthcare recommendations.
* **CONSORT Statement**: Guidelines for reporting parallel group RCTs → ↑ transparency.
* **PRISMA**: Minimum set of items for reporting systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

## 5. Evidence-Base, Efficacy & Limitations

### Efficacy & Statistical Measures in Psychiatric Trials
* **Number Needed to Treat (NNT)**: Number of patients needed to be treated to prevent 1 bad outcome ($1 / Absolute Risk Reduction$). 
  * *Clinical Goal*: Lower NNT = ↑ efficacy (e.g., NNT for SSRIs in MDD ~ 7).
* **Number Needed to Harm (NNH)**: Number of patients needed to be exposed to a risk factor to cause 1 harm ($1 / Absolute Risk Increase$).
  * *Clinical Goal*: Higher NNH = ↑ safety profile.
* **Effect Size (Cohen's d)**: Standardized measure of difference between two groups (0.2 = small, 0.5 = medium, 0.8 = large).
* **Confidence Interval (CI)**: Range of values containing the true population parameter (narrower CI = ↑ precision).
* **p-value**: Probability of obtaining results at least as extreme as observed, assuming null hypothesis is true (p < 0.05 considered +ve significance).

### Limitations of EBM in Psychiatric Populations
* **Diagnostic Heterogeneity**: DSM-5/ICD-11 categories (e.g., Schizophrenia) encompass vastly different biological/symptomatic profiles → limits generalizability of trial results.
* **Exclusion of Comorbidities**: RCTs typically exclude complex, multi-morbid patients (e.g., SMI + active substance use) → poor external validity for standard HK HA ward patients.
* **Subjective Outcomes**: Reliance on rating scales (e.g., HAM-D, PANSS) rather than objective biomarkers limits definitive measurement of treatment effect.

## 6. Practical Execution, Guidelines & Local Protocols

### Practical Execution: The 5 A's of EBP
* **Ask**: Formulate a clear, answerable clinical question (via PICO).
* **Acquire**: Search literature efficiently (PubMed, Cochrane Library, Ovid MEDLINE).
* **Appraise**: Critically evaluate validity, impact, and applicability (using CASP/GRADE).
* **Apply**: Integrate evidence with clinical expertise and patient values in the HK context.
* **Assess**: Evaluate effectiveness of the decision/intervention and identify areas for improvement.

### Guidelines & Local Protocols
* **NICE Guidelines (UK)**: Heavily referenced in HK due to rigorous EBM methodology.
* **Maudsley Prescribing Guidelines**: Standard EBM psychopharmacology reference in HK HA.
* **HK HA Corporate Guidelines**: Localized protocols (e.g., guidelines on prescribing Clozapine, Depot antipsychotics).

## 7. Medico-Legal Considerations & HK Statutory Frameworks (e.g., Cap 136)

### Medico-Legal Standard of Care
* **Bolam Test (波倫測試)**: Doctor is not negligent if acting in accordance with a practice accepted as proper by a responsible body of medical peers. EBM provides the defensive backbone for this standard.
* **Montgomery Test (蒙哥馬利測試)**: Shift towards patient autonomy. Duty to disclose material risks to patient. EBM data (e.g., specific NNH for Tardive Dyskinesia) must be communicated in understandable terms.

### HK Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136) & EBM
* **Involuntary Admission (強制入院)**: Cap 136 Section 31/36 requires evidence of "Mental Disorder" (精神紊亂) warranting detention for health/safety/protection of others.
* **Evidence-Based Justification**: Tribunals increasingly require EBM-backed rationales for compulsory interventions (e.g., citing epidemiological risk factors for violence or suicide to justify detention).

## 8. Special Populations (Lifespan, Intellectual Disability, Comorbidities)

### EBM in Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (CAP)
* **Ethical Constraints**: Scarcity of pediatric RCTs due to ethical barriers → heavy reliance on off-label prescribing extrapolated from adult EBM data.
* **Local Adaptation**: EBM interventions (e.g., CBT for adolescent depression) require significant cultural adaptation for HK school stress/family dynamics.

### EBM in Geriatric & Intellectual Disability (ID) Psychiatry
* **Polypharmacy & Frailty**: Geriatric RCTs often exclude frail elderly with severe comorbidities → EBM guidelines lack specific dosing/safety data for this critical HK demographic.
* **ID Challenges**: EBM relies on subjective self-reporting, which is unreliable/impossible in severe ID (智力障礙). Treatment often relies on Level VI/VII evidence.

## 9. Adverse Effects, Ethical Dilemmas & Systemic Challenges

### Systemic Challenges
* **Publication Bias (發表偏差)**: +ve trials are more likely to be published than -ve trials → overestimation of drug efficacy (e.g., "file drawer problem" in antidepressant trials).
* **Industry Sponsorship**: Trials funded by pharma companies have ↑ likelihood of reporting +ve outcomes.
* **Time Constraints**: HA outpatient clinics (e.g., 5-8 mins per follow-up) severely restrict capacity for physicians to perform the "5 A's" of EBM for individual patients.

### Ethical Dilemmas
* **Placebo Arms in Severe Mental Illness (SMI)**: Conducting RCTs with placebo arms for conditions like acute psychosis or severe suicidality poses significant ethical risks (non-maleficence violation).
* **EBM vs. Patient Autonomy**: Conflict occurs when EBM strongly supports an intervention (e.g., ECT for psychotic depression) but the patient’s values/fears dictate refusal.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the defining characteristic of a Systematic Review versus a Narrative Review? Answer: A Systematic Review uses predefined, explicit, and reproducible methodology to identify, select, and critically appraise all relevant research.
* Trap: Assuming that statistical significance (p < 0.05) guarantees clinical utility. Pearl: Always evaluate the Effect Size and Number Needed to Treat (NNT); a mathematically significant outcome in a massive trial may be clinically meaningless for the individual patient.
* Pitfall: Blindly applying international EBM guidelines without cultural translation. EBM must always be integrated with local HK epidemiological data, healthcare resource constraints, and traditional family structures to ensure compliance.
* Viva Question: How do you mathematically calculate Number Needed to Treat (NNT)? Answer: NNT is the inverse of the Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR); NNT = 1 / (Control Event Rate - Experimental Event Rate).
* Trap: Believing that RCTs are the gold standard for answering all clinical questions. Pearl: RCTs are ideal for therapeutic efficacy, but cohort or case-control studies are superior for investigating rare adverse effects or long-term prognostic risk factors.
