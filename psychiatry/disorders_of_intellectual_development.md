# Disorders of Intellectual Development (智力發展障礙)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Disorders of Intellectual Development
* **Definition:** Neurodevelopmental conditions marked by significant deficits in BOTH intellectual functioning (IQ < 70) AND adaptive behavior.
* **Onset:** Must manifest during the developmental period (childhood/adolescence).

### Biological Etiologies (遺傳與生理病因)
* **Chromosomal Abnormalities:** Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21) [唐氏綜合症] → Most common genetic cause.
* **Genetic Mutations:** Fragile X Syndrome [X染色體易裂症] → Most common inherited cause; Rett Syndrome, Phenylketonuria (PKU).
* **Prenatal Factors:** Congenital infections (TORCH: Toxoplasmosis, Rubella, CMV, Herpes), fetal alcohol spectrum disorder, teratogen exposure.
* **Perinatal Factors:** Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE), extreme prematurity, neonatal intracranial hemorrhage.
* **Postnatal Factors:** CNS infections (meningitis/encephalitis), severe head trauma, extreme chronic malnutrition.

### Psychosocial Etiologies (心理社會病因)
* **Environmental Deprivation:** Extreme lack of early cognitive/social stimulation.
* **Social Factors:** Severe maternal deprivation, abuse, institutionalization without early intervention.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong (香港本地流行病學)
* **Prevalence:** ~1-2% of general HK population.
* **Gender Ratio:** M > F (due to X-linked conditions like Fragile X).
* **Comorbidities:** High rates of comorbid Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) [自閉症譜系障礙] and ADHD.

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurobiological Mechanisms
* **Structural Anomalies:** Microcephaly, cortical malformations, agenesis of corpus callosum.
* **Microstructural Deficits:** Abnormal dendritic spine density/morphology (e.g., long, thin immature spines in Fragile X) → ↓ synaptic plasticity.
* **Metabolic Disruption:** Accumulation of toxic metabolites (e.g., phenylalanine in PKU) causing direct neurotoxicity.

### Psychopathological Behavioral Phenotypes
* **Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome:** Severe self-injurious behavior (biting lips/fingers).
* **Prader-Willi Syndrome:** Hyperphagia → extreme obesity, temper tantrums, obsessive-compulsive traits.
* **Williams Syndrome:** "Cocktail party" personality (hyper-sociable), marked visuospatial deficits.
* **Fragile X Syndrome:** Social anxiety, gaze avoidance, ADHD-like hyperactivity.

### Neurotransmitter Pathway Alterations
* **Dopaminergic System:** Dysregulation implicated in severe self-injurious and aggressive behaviors.
* **GABAergic/Glutamatergic Systems:** Excitatory/inhibitory imbalance → ↑ risk of co-occurring epilepsy and sensory processing deficits.
* **Serotonergic System:** Implicated in prominent affective lability and obsessive-compulsive symptoms in certain genetic syndromes.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History Focus in ID
* **Developmental History:** Milestone delays (motor, speech, social).
* **Pregnancy/Birth History:** Maternal substance use, perinatal distress, birth weight.
* **Family History:** Consanguinity, known genetic disorders, sibling developmental status.
* **Educational History:** Special school placement in HK (e.g., Mild/Moderate/Severe Grade Special Schools), need for Individualized Education Program (IEP).
* **Functional History:** ADLs (Activities of Daily Living), self-care, communication methods (verbal vs. alternative/augmentative).

### Mental State Examination (MSE) Adaptations
* **Appearance & Behavior:** Dysmorphic facial features, hypermotility, stereotypies (flapping/rocking), self-injury scars.
* **Speech & Language:** Echolalia, delayed articulation, non-verbal (use of Makaton/PECS).
* **Mood & Affect:** Subjective mood often difficult to elicit; rely on objective behavioral equivalents (agitation, sleep disruption, withdrawal).
* **Thoughts & Perceptions:** Hallucinations/delusions extremely difficult to assess in severe ID; suspect psychosis if sudden, unprovoked behavioral decline.
* **Cognition:** Standard tools (e.g., MMSE, MoCA) lack validity in moderate-severe ID; qualitative assessment of attention and basic orientation is required.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differential Diagnoses
* **Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD):** May have impaired social/adaptive function without global intellectual deficit (though highly comorbid).
* **Specific Learning Disorders:** Isolated deficits in reading/math with normal global IQ.
* **Communication Disorders:** Expressive/receptive language delays masking normal non-verbal intelligence.
* **Severe Neglect:** Mimics ID; cognitive function may partially rebound with environmental enrichment.

### Organic & Medical Rule-Outs
* **Sensory Impairments:** Occult deafness or blindness mimicking cognitive/social unresponsiveness.
* **Neurodegenerative Disorders:** Childhood disintegrative disorder, progressive metabolic diseases (e.g., Tay-Sachs, MPS) → features *loss* of previously acquired skills.
* **Acquired Brain Injury (ABI):** Trauma or hypoxia occurring *after* the developmental period (e.g., TBI at age 20 → Acquired cognitive impairment, not ID).
* **Severe Untreated Medical Conditions:** Hypothyroidism (cretinism), chronic poorly controlled epilepsy (epileptic encephalopathy).

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 & ICD-11 Diagnostic Criteria
| Criterion | Feature | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | **Intellectual Deficits** | Deficits in reasoning, problem-solving, abstract thinking, academic learning. Confirmed by clinical assessment & standardized IQ testing (IQ typically ≤ 70). |
| **B** | **Adaptive Deficits** | Failure to meet developmental/sociocultural standards for personal independence & social responsibility across environments (home, school, work). |
| **C** | **Onset** | Deficits manifest during the developmental period. |
| **Severity**| **Based on Adaptive Functioning** | Mild, Moderate, Severe, Profound (Severity is dictated by adaptive deficits, NOT IQ scores). |

### Psychometric Rating Scales & Tools
* **Intelligence:** Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS-IV) / WISC-V (for children), Stanford-Binet.
* **Adaptive Functioning:** Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales (VABS), Adaptive Behavior Assessment System (ABAS).
* **Behavior/Comorbidities:** Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC), Developmental Behavior Checklist (DBC).

### Organic Rule-Out Investigations
* **Genetics:** Chromosomal microarray (CMA) [染色體微陣列分析] (first-line), Fragile X DNA testing, Whole Exome Sequencing (WES).
* **Metabolic:** Thyroid function tests (TFTs), serum amino acids, urine organic acids, lead levels.
* **Neuroimaging:** MRI Brain (if focal neurological signs, micro/macrocephaly, or seizures).
* **Neurology:** EEG (if suspected subclinical seizures/epileptic encephalopathy).

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Behavioral Management
* **De-escalation:** Minimize sensory overload, establish predictable routines, use clear/simple communication.
* **Acute Pharmacotherapy:** For severe, immediate danger only (agitation/aggression) → Low-dose oral/IM atypical antipsychotics (e.g., Olanzapine, Risperidone) or short-acting benzodiazepines (e.g., Lorazepam). Caution: ↑ risk of paradoxical agitation with BZDs.
* **Medical Workup:** Rule out "diagnostic overshadowing" (e.g., acute distress caused by occult UTI, dental abscess, constipation).

### Risk Assessment
* **Vulnerability:** ↑↑ Risk of financial, physical, and sexual exploitation.
* **Self-Harm:** Severe stereotypic self-injurious behaviors (head-banging, eye-gouging).
* **Aggression:** Reactive aggression due to frustration/inability to communicate needs.

### Medico-Legal Considerations (HK Mental Health Ordinance Cap 136)
* **Mentally Incapacitated Persons (MIP) [精神上無行為能力人士]:** Governed under Part IVC of Cap 136.
* **Guardianship Board (GB) [監護委員會]:** Can appoint a legal guardian with powers to make accommodation and non-surgical/dental treatment decisions if the patient lacks capacity and it is in their best interest.
* **Consent to Medical/Psychiatric Treatment:** Section 59ZF allows treatment without consent if urgently necessary to save life, prevent serious deterioration, or alleviate severe pain. Routine treatment requires GB or family consensus depending on capacity.

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Pharmacotherapy Guidelines
* **Core Principle:** NO specific medication treats the core intellectual deficit. Meds ONLY for co-occurring psychiatric conditions.
* **Aggression/Irritability:** Risperidone [利培酮] or Aripiprazole (FDA-approved for irritability in autism/ID).
* **Depression/Anxiety:** SSRIs (start at low doses, titrate slowly).
* **ADHD Comorbidity:** Methylphenidate or Atomoxetine (monitor closely for ↑ irritability/tics).
* **Deprescribing:** Routine review to minimize polypharmacy and anticholinergic burden.

### Psychological & Behavioral Interventions
* **Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA):** Target specific skill deficits and reduce maladaptive behaviors.
* **Positive Behavioral Support (PBS):** Proactive modification of environment to prevent triggers.
* **Communication Augmentation:** Speech therapy, Picture Exchange Communication System (PECS).

### Social & Community Support in Hong Kong
* **Educational:** Special Educational Needs (SEN) support, Integrated Education (IE) in mainstream schools, or Special Schools.
* **Vocational:** Sheltered Workshops [庇護工場], Supported Employment via Social Welfare Department (SWD).
* **Residential & Day Care:** Day Activity Centres (DAC) [展能中心], Supported Hostels [輔助宿舍], Care and Attention Homes for Severely Disabled Persons (C&A/SD).

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Illness Trajectory
* **Chronicity:** Generally a lifelong condition, though adaptive functioning can improve significantly with sustained support and training.
* **Course:** Non-progressive, EXCEPT in specific neurodegenerative/metabolic disorders (e.g., Rett syndrome, mitochondrial diseases) where cognitive/motor decline occurs.

### Prognostic Indicators
* **Good Prognosis:** Mild ID severity, absence of severe comorbid medical/psychiatric conditions, early intensive behavioral intervention, stable/supportive family environment.
* **Poor Prognosis:** Profound ID, severe treatment-refractory epilepsy, significant sensory impairments, institutionalization, comorbid severe ASD.

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Medical Comorbidities
* **Psychiatric:** ↑ Vulnerability to all mental illnesses (rate is 3-4x higher than general population). Frequent depression, bipolar disorder, and early-onset dementia (especially Alzheimer's in Down Syndrome by age 40).
* **Medical:** High rates of epilepsy (up to 30% in severe ID), congenital heart disease, gastrointestinal issues (GERD, constipation), obesity.

### Social Complications
* **Caregiver Burden:** High risk of parental/caregiver burnout, financial strain, marital discord.
* **Systemic:** Long waitlists for SWD residential services in HK, institutionalization, high risk of bullying and marginalization.

### Psychotropic Drug Side Effects in ID
| Drug Class | Notable Complications in ID Population |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Antipsychotics** | ↑ Sensitivity to EPS, tardive dyskinesia, weight gain, metabolic syndrome, lowered seizure threshold. |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Paradoxical disinhibition/agitation, over-sedation, respiratory depression, falls. |
| **Stimulants** | Can exacerbate pre-existing stereotypies, induce tics, or worsen irritability/insomnia. |
| **Lithium** | Difficult to monitor toxicity due to inability to communicate thirst/nausea; high risk of dehydration. |

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is "diagnostic overshadowing" in the context of intellectual disability? Answer: The tendency for clinicians to incorrectly attribute a patient's new physical symptoms, behavioral changes, or psychiatric symptoms strictly to their baseline intellectual disability, thereby missing an acute, treatable underlying medical condition (e.g., UTI, dental pain).
* Trap: Assuming that a low IQ score alone is sufficient to diagnose Intellectual Disability. Pearl: A diagnosis of ID explicitly requires BOTH intellectual deficits (IQ < 70) AND significant deficits in adaptive functioning, both manifesting during the developmental period.
* Pitfall: Prescribing long-term antipsychotics for generic "challenging behaviors" without regular review. Always attempt environmental modifications, functional behavioral analyses, and active deprescribing to avoid long-term metabolic and extrapyramidal complications.
