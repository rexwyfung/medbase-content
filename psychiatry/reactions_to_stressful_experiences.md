# Reactions to Stressful Experiences (對壓力經歷的反應)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definitions of Stress-Related Disorders
* **Acute Stress Disorder [ASD] (急性壓力症):** Transient, severe psychological reaction to an exceptionally stressful event (threat of death, serious injury, or sexual violence) lasting 3 days to 1 month.
* **Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder [PTSD] (創傷後壓力症):** Maladaptive, prolonged reaction to extreme trauma lasting >1 month, characterized by intrusions, avoidance, negative cognitions, and hyperarousal.
* **Adjustment Disorder (適應障礙):** Emotional/behavioral symptoms in response to an identifiable psychosocial stressor (e.g., divorce, job loss) occurring within 3 months of stressor onset and resolving within 6 months post-stressor termination.

### Biopsychosocial Etiologies of Trauma Responses
* **Biological:** Genetic vulnerability (short allele of 5-HTTLPR gene) → ↑ amygdala reactivity; prior head trauma/TBI ↑ risk.
* **Psychological:** History of childhood abuse/adversity; neurotic personality traits; low resilience; external locus of control; pre-existing anxiety/depression.
* **Social:** Lack of post-trauma social support; lower socioeconomic status; ongoing occupational stress (e.g., first responders in HK); cultural stigma preventing early help-seeking.

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence:** Lifetime prevalence of PTSD in HK is ~3-5%, with peaks related to socio-political events and pandemic-related severe illnesses.
* **Gender Disparity:** ♀ > ♂ (approx. 2:1 ratio for PTSD and Adjustment Disorders).
* **Common Stressors (HK Context):** High-density living conflicts, extreme academic pressure (DSE), sudden medical diagnoses (e.g., cancer), and occupational burnout.

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurobiological Mechanisms in Trauma
* **Amygdala (杏仁核):** Hyperactive → exaggerated fear conditioning and threat detection.
* **Prefrontal Cortex [PFC] (前額葉皮層):** Hypoactive (ventromedial PFC) → ↓ top-down inhibition of amygdala → failure of fear extinction.
* **Hippocampus (海馬體):** ↓ Volume and hypofunction → impaired context processing and fragmented declarative memory encoding of trauma (re-experienced as flashbacks).
* **HPA Axis (下丘腦-垂體-腎上腺軸):** Dysregulated → chronic hypercortisolemia in acute stress, but paradoxical ↓ baseline cortisol with ↑ glucocorticoid receptor sensitivity in chronic PTSD.

### Core Psychopathology of Stress Reactions
* **Intrusions:** Flashbacks (dissociative reliving), nightmare looping, distress to trauma cues.
* **Avoidance:** Active suppression of trauma-related memories/thoughts; avoidance of external reminders (people, places).
* **Cognitive/Mood Alterations:** Dissociative amnesia (inability to recall key trauma details); persistent negative beliefs ("the world is completely dangerous"); anhedonia; detachment.
* **Hyperarousal:** Hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, insomnia, irritable outbursts.

### Neurotransmitter Pathways in Stress Responses
* **Noradrenergic System:** ↑ Norepinephrine (NE) tone in locus coeruleus → hyperarousal, startle, and encoding of traumatic memories.
* **Serotonergic (5-HT) System:** ↓ 5-HT → poor impulse control, mood dysregulation, and irritability.
* **Glutamatergic System:** ↑ Glutamate release during acute trauma → excitotoxicity and consolidation of maladaptive fear memory.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History Focusing on Trauma
* **History of Presenting Complaint (HPC):** Exact nature of trauma (direct exposure vs. witnessed vs. learned about); timeline of symptom onset (<1 month = ASD, >1 month = PTSD).
* **Symptom Review:** Elicit specific avoidance behaviors (e.g., refusing to take MTR after an accident); nature of flashbacks (sensory details vs. cognitive memory).
* **Past Psychiatric History:** Prior trauma exposure (cumulative trauma effect); previous coping mechanisms.
* **Personal/Social History:** Current psychosocial stressors compounding the trauma; availability of support network; substance use (self-medication).

### Mental State Examination [MSE] Findings in Trauma
* **Appearance & Behavior:** Hypervigilant scanning of the room, exaggerated startle to sudden noises, tearful, tense posture.
* **Speech:** Reluctant to speak about trauma (avoidance); may become pressured/agitated when trauma is triggered.
* **Mood & Affect:** Subjectively anxious/depressed; objectively restricted or blunted affect; mood congruent with trauma themes.
* **Thought Content:** Preoccupations with the traumatic event, survival guilt, themes of hopelessness. No formal thought disorder unless severe psychotic depression is comorbid.
* **Perception:** Dissociative flashbacks (pseudohallucinations vs. true hallucinations); derealization/depersonalization.
* **Cognition:** Impaired concentration/attention (due to hyperarousal); memory of trauma may be fragmented.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differential Diagnoses
* **Major Depressive Disorder [MDD] (重性抑鬱症):** Overlaps with anhedonia and negative mood, but lacks defining trauma intrusions/avoidance.
* **Generalized Anxiety Disorder [GAD] (廣泛性焦慮症):** Worry is generalized, not specifically linked to a past traumatic event.
* **Panic Disorder (恐慌症):** Panic attacks occur spontaneously, whereas PTSD panic is typically cue-triggered.
* **Borderline Personality Disorder [BPD] (邊緣性人格障礙):** Often involves childhood trauma, but characterized by unstable relationships, identity disturbance, and chronic emptiness rather than specific trauma re-experiencing.

### Organic & Medical Rule-Outs
* **Traumatic Brain Injury [TBI] (創傷性腦損傷):** Cognitive deficits, headache, dizziness post-head trauma. Can co-occur with PTSD.
* **Substance-Induced Disorders (物質誘發障礙):** Intoxication/withdrawal (e.g., alcohol, stimulants) mimicking hyperarousal and anxiety.
* **Endocrine Disorders:** Hyperthyroidism (mimics autonomic hyperarousal).
* **Neurological:** Temporal lobe epilepsy (can present with transient dissociative states/olfactory flashbacks).

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Summaries
| Disorder | Core DSM-5 Criteria Highlights | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Stress Disorder** | Exposure to actual/threatened death, injury, or sexual violation. ≥9 symptoms from 5 categories: Intrusion, Negative Mood, Dissociation, Avoidance, Arousal. | 3 days to 1 month post-trauma |
| **PTSD** | Exposure to trauma. Symptoms in 4 clusters: 1. Intrusion (≥1), 2. Avoidance (≥1), 3. Negative alterations in cognition/mood (≥2), 4. Alterations in arousal/reactivity (≥2). | > 1 month |
| **Adjustment Disorder** | Emotional/behavioral symptoms within 3 months of stressor onset. Distress is out of proportion to severity of stressor. Significant functional impairment. | Resolves within 6 months post-stressor |

### Standardized Rating Scales
* **PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5):** 20-item self-report measure to monitor symptom change during treatment.
* **Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale (CAPS-5):** Gold standard structured diagnostic interview.
* **Impact of Event Scale-Revised (IES-R):** 22-item self-report tracking subjective distress for specific life events.

### Organic Rule-Out Investigations
* **Neuroimaging (CT/MRI Brain):** If head trauma occurred (R/O intracranial hemorrhage, contusion, organic TBI).
* **Toxicology Screen:** Urine drug screen/blood alcohol level to R/O substance-induced hyperarousal.
* **Endocrine Panel:** TFTs (R/O hyperthyroidism).

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Pharmacological & Non-Pharmacological Management
* **Immediate Psychological First Aid (心理急救):** Ensure physical safety, provide practical support, normalize acute distress, connect with social supports. (Debriefing is NOT recommended as it may ↑ risk of PTSD).
* **Acute Pharmacotherapy:** 
  * Short-term Benzodiazepines (e.g., Lorazepam) for severe acute agitation/insomnia (use with extreme caution < 1-2 weeks due to risk of impeding fear extinction and dependence).
  * Z-drugs (Zopiclone) for severe transient insomnia.
* **Trauma-Focused CBT [TF-CBT]:** If ASD symptoms persist >2 weeks, early TF-CBT can prevent progression to PTSD.

### Acute Risk Assessment in Trauma Patients
* **Suicidality/Self-Harm:** High risk in acute trauma, especially with comorbid depression, substance abuse, or severe survival guilt.
* **Risk to Others:** Hyperarousal/irritability may lead to violent outbursts (especially in populations with pre-existing cluster B traits or substance intoxication).
* **Vulnerability:** Risk of re-victimization (e.g., domestic violence cases); mandatory reporting if child/elderly abuse is suspected in HK.

### Medico-Legal Considerations (HK Mental Health Ordinance - Cap 136)
* **Voluntary vs. Involuntary:** Most stress-related disorders are managed voluntarily. Involuntary admission (Section 31/32) is rarely justified strictly for PTSD unless severe comorbid psychotic depression or imminent suicidal risk is present.
* **Compensation & Litigation:** Post-trauma medico-legal claims (e.g., employee compensation for workplace injury, traffic accidents) are common. Malingering must be considered if clear secondary gain is present.

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Chronic Pharmacotherapy Strategies
* **First-Line:** SSRIs (Sertraline, Paroxetine, Fluoxetine) or SNRIs (Venlafaxine). Target mood, intrusive symptoms, and hyperarousal. Needs 8-12 weeks for optimal effect.
* **Adjunctive Therapy for Nightmares:** Prazosin (α1-adrenergic antagonist) ↓ trauma-related nightmares and sleep disruption.
* **Adjunctive Therapy for Hyperarousal:** Clonidine (α2-agonist) or atypical antipsychotics (Quetiapine, Risperidone) in severe, treatment-resistant cases with prominent agitation/dissociation.

### Evidence-Based Psychotherapy for Trauma
* **Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT):** Involves psychoeducation, relaxation training, and gradual exposure (imaginal and in-vivo) to trauma memories to facilitate fear extinction.
* **Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) (眼動身心重建法):** Patient focuses on traumatic memory while simultaneously engaging in bilateral sensory stimulation (e.g., eye movements). Facilitates adaptive information processing.
* **Prolonged Exposure Therapy (PE):** Intense focus on confronting trauma-related memories/triggers safely.

### Biopsychosocial Rehabilitation
* **Social Support:** Reintegration into community/work. Medical Social Worker (MSW) referral for housing/financial difficulties.
* **Substance Abuse Rehabilitation:** Dual diagnosis treatment if utilizing alcohol/drugs for self-medication.

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Natural History of Stress-Related Disorders
* **Acute Stress Disorder:** Roughly 50% of ASD cases progress to PTSD.
* **PTSD Course:** Fluctuating course. Approx. 30% recover completely without treatment, 40% experience mild residual symptoms, 30% develop chronic, unremitting PTSD.
* **Adjustment Disorder:** Generally excellent prognosis; self-limiting once the stressor is removed or the patient adapts.

### Prognostic Factors
* **Favorable (Good Prognosis):** Rapid onset of symptoms, brief duration of trauma, good premorbid functioning, strong social support, absence of comorbid psychiatric/substance disorders.
* **Unfavorable (Poor Prognosis):** Interpersonal/intentional trauma (e.g., rape, torture), prolonged/repeated trauma (CPTSD), childhood onset, comorbid MDD/substance abuse, prominent dissociative symptoms, delayed help-seeking.

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Social Complications
* **Comorbidities:** MDD (up to 50%), Substance Use Disorders, Anxiety Disorders (Panic, Agoraphobia).
* **Social/Occupational:** Job loss due to avoidance of workplace triggers, relationship breakdown/divorce due to emotional numbing and irritability.
* **Physical Health:** ↑ Risk of cardiovascular disease, chronic pain syndromes, and autoimmune conditions (related to chronic HPA axis dysregulation).

### Psychotropic Drug Side Effects in Trauma Management
* **SSRIs/SNRIs:** GI upset, initial ↑ in anxiety (start low, go slow), sexual dysfunction, sleep disturbances.
* **Prazosin:** Orthostatic hypotension, dizziness, reflex tachycardia (requires baseline and standing BP monitoring).
* **Atypical Antipsychotics:** Metabolic syndrome (weight gain, dyslipidemia, hyperglycemia), QTc prolongation.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How do you clinically differentiate Acute Stress Disorder from PTSD? Answer: Strictly by the duration of symptoms. ASD is diagnosed between 3 days and 1 month post-trauma; PTSD is diagnosed when symptoms persist beyond 1 month.
* Trap: Assuming routine psychological debriefing immediately post-trauma prevents PTSD. Pearl: Routine single-session debriefing (e.g., Critical Incident Stress Debriefing) is ineffective and may actually increase the risk of PTSD by disrupting natural coping and consolidating traumatic memories. Use Psychological First Aid instead.
* Pitfall: Prescribing benzodiazepines as first-line long-term treatment for PTSD anxiety. Benzodiazepines do not treat core PTSD symptoms, may impede the cognitive processing/fear extinction required in psychotherapy, and carry a high risk of dependence in a population prone to substance abuse.
