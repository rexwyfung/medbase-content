# Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (兒童及青少年精神科學)

## 1. Topic Definition, Scope & Relevance in Hong Kong

### Definition of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry
* **Child & Adolescent Psychiatry** (**兒童及青少年精神科學**) → specialized branch of psychiatry focusing on diagnosis, treatment & prevention of mental disorders in populations <18 years old.
* Encompasses neurodevelopmental, emotional & behavioural deviations from normal developmental trajectories.
* Heavily reliant on systemic context → family, school, peers.

### Epidemiological Scope & Relevance in Hong Kong
* ↑ prevalence of psychiatric morbidity in HK youth → ~16.4% of youths experience $\ge1$ mental disorder.
* **Hospital Authority (HA) Child and Adolescent Psychiatric Services** (**醫院管理局兒童及青少年精神科服務**) → primary provider in HK, managing ↑ waitlists.
* High academic stress in HK → ↑ rates of depression, anxiety & deliberate self-harm (DSH).

## 2. Core Theoretical Principles & Mechanisms (Psychological, Pharmacological, or Legal)

### Biopsychosocial Developmental Model
* **Biological** (**生理**) → genetic loading (e.g., ADHD heritability ~70-80%), pre/perinatal insults (hypoxia, maternal smoking), neuroplasticity.
* **Psychological** (**心理**) → attachment styles (Bowlby's theory), temperament, cognitive coping mechanisms.
* **Social** (**社會**) → family dynamics (expressed emotion), school environment, bullying (cyber & physical).

### Pharmacological Mechanisms in Developing Brains
* Hepatic metabolism often ↑ in children → may require higher mg/kg dosing vs adults.
* Developing neurotransmitter systems → DA/5-HT pathways maturing → ↑ susceptibility to adverse effects (e.g., SSRI-induced activation, ↑ suicidal ideation risk initially).

## 3. Key Classifications, Frameworks & Components

### Broad Diagnostic Classifications (DSM-5 / ICD-11)
| Category | Core Clinical Entities (English / Chinese) | Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Neurodevelopmental** | ADHD (專注力不足/過度活躍症), ASD (自閉症譜系障礙) | Early onset, steady course, CNS maturation deficits |
| **Emotional** | Depression (抑鬱症), Anxiety (焦慮症) | Internalizing symptoms, prominent somatic complaints |
| **Behavioural** | ODD (對立反抗症), Conduct Disorder (品行障礙) | Externalizing symptoms, rule-breaking, aggression |
| **Tic / Elimination** | Tourette's (妥瑞症), Enuresis (遺尿症) | Involuntary movements/vocalizations, sphincter control deficits |

### The Systemic Component Framework
* **Patient** (**病人**) → developmental stage, cognitive capacity.
* **Family System** (**家庭系統**) → parenting style, parental mental illness, socioeconomic status.
* **Educational System** (**教育系統**) → learning environment, special educational needs (SEN) support.

## 4. Clinical Indications & Standardized Assessment Tools

### Multi-Informant Clinical Assessment Strategy
* Absolute requirement for multi-source data → child, parents/guardians, teachers.
* Standardized forms (e.g., SNAP-IV for ADHD) must be collected from $\ge2$ settings (home + school) to verify pervasiveness.

### Common Standardized Assessment Tools in HK
* **SNAP-IV** → ADHD screening (parent/teacher).
* **Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL)** → broad internalizing/externalizing screening.
* **Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS-2)** → gold-standard ASD diagnostic observation.
* **WISC-V (HK)** → cognitive/IQ testing validated for local HK Chinese population.

## 5. Evidence-Base, Efficacy & Limitations

### Efficacy of Psychosocial Interventions
* **Parent Management Training (PMT)** → 1st-line for ODD/ADHD → highly effective, durable results.
* **Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)** (**認知行為治療**) → 1st-line for mild/moderate anxiety & depression.
* Limitations → requires high family engagement, limited availability of trained child psychologists in HK HA system.

### Efficacy of Pharmacotherapy
* **Stimulants** (Methylphenidate) → highly effective for ADHD core symptoms (response rate ~70%).
* **SSRIs** (Fluoxetine) → effective for moderate/severe depression & OCD, but requires close monitoring for behavioural activation.
* Limitations → off-label prescribing common in paediatrics, lack of long-term safety data for developing brains.

## 6. Practical Execution, Guidelines & Local Protocols

### Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) Approach in HKHA
* Assessment & treatment strictly executed via MDT → Psychiatrist, Clinical Psychologist, Medical Social Worker (MSW), Occupational Therapist (OT).
* Step-care model → mild cases managed in primary care/school social workers → severe/complex cases referred to HA specialized clinics.

### HA Guidelines for High-Risk Youth
* Imminent risk (suicidality, severe aggression) → prioritized via triage system for urgent appointment <1 week.
* Compulsory reporting protocols for suspected child abuse (Physical, Sexual, Emotional, Neglect) to MSW and Police.

## 7. Medico-Legal Considerations & HK Statutory Frameworks (e.g., Cap 136)

### Legal Frameworks Impacting Minors in HK
| Legal Framework | Key Provisions & Clinical Relevance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gillick Competence** | Minors <18 capable of consenting to treatment if they achieve sufficient understanding & intelligence. |
| **Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136) (精神健康條例)** | Form 1/2/3 can be applied to minors. Next-of-kin (parents) typically involved in voluntary admissions. |
| **Protection of Children and Juveniles Ordinance (Cap 213)** | Framework for Care or Protection Orders (CPO) if child is beyond control or in danger. |

### Consent & Confidentiality Dynamics
* Consent generally obtained from parent/legal guardian for those <18.
* Confidentiality owed to the minor, but can be breached to parents/authorities if ↑ risk of self-harm, harm to others, or abuse.

## 8. Special Populations (Lifespan, Intellectual Disability, Comorbidities)

### Intellectual Disability (ID) (智力障礙)
* High rates of psychiatric comorbidity (e.g., ASD + ID, ADHD + ID).
* Atypical presentations → distress often manifests as challenging behaviours, self-injury, or aggression.
* Requires modified assessment → simplified language, reliance on observational data & carer reports.

### Transition Age Youth (16-18 years)
* Critical period transferring from Child & Adolescent to Adult Psychiatric services.
* High drop-out rates during transition.
* Requires structured handover protocols within HA to ensure continuity of care.

## 9. Adverse Effects, Ethical Dilemmas & Systemic Challenges

### Pharmacological Adverse Effects
* **Stimulants** → ↓ appetite, ↓ growth velocity, insomnia, ↑ HR/BP.
* **Antipsychotics** (e.g., Risperidone for ASD irritability) → hyperprolactinemia, weight gain, metabolic syndrome.

### Ethical & Systemic Dilemmas
* **Systemic** → Prolonged waiting times for new case assessments in HA Child Psychiatry clinics (often >1 year for non-urgent cases).
* **Ethical** → Balancing child's autonomy vs parental authority (e.g., adolescent refuses antidepressants, parents demand them).
* **Diagnostic Labeling** → Stigma vs access to Special Educational Needs (SEN) funding in schools.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most critical difference between adult and child psychiatric assessments? Answer: The absolute necessity of multi-informant data (parents, teachers) and evaluating symptoms within the context of expected developmental milestones.
* Trap: Assuming a pre-pubertal child with severe mood lability and aggression has Bipolar Disorder. Pearl: Pre-pubertal onset of true Bipolar Disorder is extremely rare; severe chronic irritability is more likely indicative of Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder (DMDD), ODD, or severe ADHD.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing ADHD based solely on symptoms present in the clinic. Always obtain teacher reports (e.g., SNAP-IV), as DSM-5 requires pervasive impairment across two or more settings.
* Viva Question: When can a minor independently consent to psychiatric treatment in Hong Kong? Answer: When they meet the criteria for Gillick Competence, demonstrating sufficient maturity and intelligence to fully understand the proposed treatment and its implications.
* Trap: Promising absolute confidentiality to an adolescent patient during an assessment. Pearl: Always explicitly outline the limits of confidentiality upfront, specifically stating that you must inform guardians or authorities if there is a risk of harm to self, harm to others, or disclosure of abuse.
* Pitfall: Overlooking learning disorders or intellectual disability as the root cause of behavioural issues. Always review academic performance and consider psychometric (IQ) testing when school refusal or classroom disruption is the primary presenting complaint.
