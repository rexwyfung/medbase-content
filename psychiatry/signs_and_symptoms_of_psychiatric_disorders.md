# Signs and Symptoms of Psychiatric Disorders (精神科症狀與表徵)

## 1. Topic Definition, Scope & Relevance in Hong Kong
### Descriptive Psychopathology (描述性精神病理學)
* **Definition**: Objective description of abnormal mental states, conscious experiences, and observable behaviours, avoiding preconceived theoretical explanations.
* **Core Aim**: Elucidate essential qualities of morbid mental experiences to identify psychiatric syndromes (精神科綜合症) → crucial for HK Hospital Authority (HA) diagnostic and management pathways.
* **Key Exponent**: Karl Jaspers (Allgemeine Psychopathologie) → foundation for global and local MBBS clinical assessments.

### Experimental Psychopathology (實驗性精神病理學)
* **Definition**: Seeks to explain (rather than just describe) abnormal mental phenomena via cognitive, behavioural, or functional brain imaging methods.
* **Scope**: Explains causes of symptoms (e.g., cognitive models of delusions) within diagnosed syndromes.

## 2. Core Theoretical Principles & Mechanisms (Psychological, Pharmacological, or Legal)
### Form vs Content (形式與內容)
* **Form**: The structural nature of the symptom (e.g., Auditory Hallucination / 聽覺幻覺) → critical for diagnostic classification.
* **Content**: The thematic meaning (e.g., voices stating patient has AIDS) → guides risk assessment & clinical management.
* **Rule**: Form → Diagnosis; Content → Management/Risk.

### Subjective vs Objective (主觀與客觀)
* **Subjective**: Patient's internal complaint (e.g., low mood).
* **Objective**: Clinician's direct observation (e.g., psychomotor retardation / 精神運動性遲滯, crying).
* **Clinical Weight**: Objective signs usually carry greater diagnostic weight than subjective complaints alone.

### Primary vs Secondary (原發性與繼發性)
* **Temporal Meaning**: Which symptom occurred first.
* **Causal Meaning**: Primary arises directly from pathology; Secondary arises as a reaction to primary symptom (e.g., persecutory delusion 2° to accusatory AH).

### Jaspers' Bipartite Understanding (雅斯培的雙重理解)
* **Verstehen (Understanding / 理解)**: Empathic appreciation of subjective experience (what it feels like).
* **Erklären (Explanation / 解釋)**: Accounting for events via external/aetiological factors (e.g., ↓ mood explained by recent redundancy).

## 3. Key Classifications, Frameworks & Components
### Disturbances of Emotion & Mood (情緒與心境障礙)
* **Mood (心境)**: Pervasive, prolonged internal state (climate).
* **Affect (情感)**: Transitory, observable expression of emotion (weather).
* **Lability (情緒不穩)**: ↑ variation.
* **Blunting/Flattening (情感遲鈍/平淡)**: ↓ variation (characteristic of Schizophrenia / 精神分裂症).
* **Incongruous (情感不協調)**: Emotion inconsistent with thoughts/circumstances.

### Disturbances of Perception (知覺障礙)
| Phenomenon (現象) | Definition (定義) | Clinical Association (臨床關聯) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Illusion (錯覺)** | Misperception of a real external stimulus. | Delirium (譫妄), severe anxiety. |
| **Hallucination (幻覺)** | Percept experienced without external stimulus; indistinguishable from true perception; cannot be terminated at will. | Psychosis (精神病), Organic states. |
| **Pseudohallucination (假性幻覺)** | Percept lacks external projection (located in internal space) OR lacks subjective sense of reality. | Trauma, dissociation, personality disorders. |

### Types of Hallucinations (幻覺分類)
* **Auditory (聽覺)**: 2nd person (addressing pt) → often severe depression if derogatory; 3rd person (running commentary/discussing pt) → highly suggestive of Schizophrenia (精神分裂症).
* **Visual (視覺)**: Must always prompt organic rule-out (e.g., Delirium, Lewy Body Dementia).
* **Olfactory/Gustatory (嗅覺/味覺)**: Often unpleasant; linked to Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (顳葉癲癇) or severe depression.
* **Somatic/Tactile (軀體/觸覺)**: Bizarre → Schizophrenia; Formication (蟻走感) → Cocaine/Methamphetamine abuse.
* **Hypnagogic/Hypnopompic (入睡前/醒前)**: Occur while falling asleep/waking → often normal or narcolepsy.

### Disturbances of Thought: Delusions (妄想)
* **Definition**: Firmly held false belief, on inadequate grounds, impervious to rational counter-argument, out of keeping with cultural/religious background.
* **Primary (原發性)**: Arises suddenly without preceding mental events (e.g., Delusional Perception / 妄想性知覺).
* **Secondary (繼發性)**: Derived from preceding morbid experience (hallucinations, mood states).

| Delusion Type (妄想類型) | Characteristic Theme | Clinical Association |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Persecutory (被害)** | Belief of being harmed/harassed. | Schizophrenia, Severe Depression (if justified by guilt). |
| **Grandiose (夸大)** | Exaggerated self-importance/wealth. | Mania (躁狂症), Schizophrenia. |
| **Reference (牽連)** | Neutral events have personal meaning. | Schizophrenia. |
| **Nihilistic (虛無)** | Belief that self/world ceased to exist. | Severe Depression (Cotard's Syndrome / 科塔爾綜合症). |
| **Guilt (罪惡)** | Exaggerated responsibility for sins. | Severe Depression. |
| **Passivity (被動/控制)** | Actions/thoughts controlled externally. | Schizophrenia. |

### Thought Possession (思想擁有權障礙)
* **Thought Insertion (思想插入)**: Thoughts implanted by external agency.
* **Thought Withdrawal (思想被抽走)**: Thoughts extracted from mind (often accompanies thought block).
* **Thought Broadcasting (思想廣播)**: Unspoken thoughts known to others (via telepathy/radio).

### Disturbances of Thought Stream & Form (思想流與形式障礙)
* **Flight of Ideas (意念飛躍)**: Rapid shifts with logical/linguistic links (clang associations, puns) → Mania.
* **Thought Block (思想閉塞)**: Sudden, abrupt interruption of thought stream → Schizophrenia.
* **Loosening of Associations (聯想鬆弛)**: Loss of logical structure (derailment, knight's move) → Schizophrenia.
* **Perseveration (持續言語)**: Inappropriate repetition of the same response → Organic disorders (Dementia, Frontal lobe injury).
* **Neologisms (新造語)**: Patient-invented words → Chronic Schizophrenia.

## 4. Clinical Indications & Standardized Assessment Tools
### Psychiatric Interview (精神科面談)
* **Primary Purpose**: Elicit descriptive psychopathology directly to formulate a syndromal diagnosis.
* **MSE Integration**: Signs (objective) & Symptoms (subjective) systematically mapped onto the Mental State Examination (MSE / 精神狀態檢查).

### Distinguishing Obsessions from Delusions (強迫觀念與妄想的區分)
* **Obsessions (強迫觀念)**: Recurrent, intrusive, resisted, recognized as own, regarded as senseless/untrue.
* **Compulsions (強迫行為)**: Repetitive, stereotyped behaviors performed to ↓ anxiety from obsession.
* **Overvalued Ideas (超價觀念)**: Comprehensible idea pursued beyond reason (e.g., body shape in Anorexia Nervosa / 神經性厭食症), differs from delusion via slight residual insight/cultural acceptability.

## 5. Evidence-Base, Efficacy & Limitations
### Diagnostic Specificity of Psychopathology
* **Single Symptom Validity**: Almost no single symptom guarantees a diagnosis. Exception: unequivocal delusion usually indicates psychosis.
* **First Rank Symptoms (首發症狀)**: Certain hallucinations (3rd person, écho de la pensée) & delusions (thought alienation, delusional perception, somatic passivity) carry high predictive validity for Schizophrenia.
* **Cultural Limitations**: Must distinguish culturally sanctioned idioms of distress (e.g., curses, spells in specific subgroups) from true delusions.

## 6. Practical Execution, Guidelines & Local Protocols
### Eliciting Psychopathology in HK Settings
* **Direct but Tactful Questioning**: Never argue with a delusion. Use empathic probes: "Do you ever feel that others are trying to harm you?"
* **Testing Fixity**: Gently challenge evidence for the belief to test fixity without causing hostility.
* **Observation**: Motor symptoms (Catatonia / 緊張症) require active observation (e.g., waxy flexibility / 蠟樣屈曲, echopraxia / 模仿動作).

## 7. Medico-Legal Considerations & HK Statutory Frameworks (e.g., Cap 136)
### Thresholds for Compulsory Action
* **HK Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136 / 香港法例第136章)**: Severe psychopathology (e.g., command hallucinations for violence, profound nihilistic delusions causing food refusal) fulfills criteria for involuntary detention if patient poses danger to self/others.
* **Risk Assessment**: Irritability, anger, persecutory delusions, and active suicidal ideation must be documented as they legally justify statutory intervention.

## 8. Special Populations (Lifespan, Intellectual Disability, Comorbidities)
### Cultural & Demographic Variations
* **HK Chinese Population**: Depression frequently presents with somatic symptoms (軀體化症狀) rather than psychological complaints (e.g., chronic fatigue, GI distress).
* **Elderly**: ↑ frequency of hypochondriacal delusions (疑病妄想) and organic perceptual disturbances (Charles Bonnet Syndrome / 查爾斯·邦內綜合症).
* **Intellectual Disability**: Psychopathology often presents as behavioral changes rather than articulated complex delusions.

## 9. Adverse Effects, Ethical Dilemmas & Systemic Challenges
### Stigma & Misinterpretation (標籤效應與誤解)
* **Diagnostic Labeling**: Allocating patients to categories based on psychopathology can induce stigma (e.g., Schizophrenia label).
* **Systemic Challenge**: Risk of mislabeling political dissent or socially deviant behavior as "mental illness" (historical abuses).
* **Ethical Guardrail**: Psychopathology must be based on rigorous MSE and history, causing functional impairment, not merely social non-conformity.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls
* Viva Question: How do you differentiate a pseudohallucination from a true hallucination? Answer: True hallucinations are projected into external space and possess a definitive subjective sense of reality, whereas pseudohallucinations are perceived in subjective inner space or lack the full sense of objective reality.
* Trap: Assuming any false, fixed belief is a delusion. Pearl: Always verify the patient's cultural, religious, and educational background first; strongly held beliefs shared by a subculture (overvalued ideas or cultural norms) are not delusions.
* Pitfall: Mistaking severe obsessional thoughts for thought insertion. Obsessional patients know the intrusive thoughts are their own and resist them, whereas patients with thought insertion believe the thoughts originate from a distinct external agency.
* Viva Question: What is the defining feature of a primary (autochthonous) delusion? Answer: It arises suddenly, with full conviction, and has no comprehensible prior mental events or experiences leading up to it.
* Trap: Confusing flight of ideas with loosening of associations. Pearl: In flight of ideas, logical or linguistic links (puns, rhymes, environmental cues) are preserved between rapid shifts; in loosening of associations, the transitions lack any logical connection.
