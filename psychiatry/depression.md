# Depression (抑鬱症)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)
* **MDD (重性抑鬱症)**: Primary mood disorder characterized by pervasive ↓ mood, anhedonia (失去興趣), and somatic/cognitive symptoms lasting ≥2 weeks.
* **Core Feature**: Episodic course, causes clinically significant distress/impairment in social/occupational functioning.

### Biopsychosocial Etiological Framework
* **Biological**:
  * **Genetics**: ↑ risk if 1st-degree relative affected (heritability ~37%).
  * **Endocrine**: HPA axis hyperactivity → ↑ cortisol.
* **Psychological**:
  * **Cognitive Theory (Beck)**: Negative triad (self, world, future).
  * **Learned Helplessness**: Perceived lack of control over adverse events.
* **Social**:
  * **Precipitating Events**: Bereavement (喪親), job loss, marital breakdown.
  * **Perpetuating Factors**: Lack of social support, ongoing financial stress.

### Hong Kong Local Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Point prevalence ~2.9%, lifetime ~8.3% in HK (HKMMS data).
* **Demographics**: F:M ratio 2:1. Peak onset 20s-30s.
* **Cultural Nuance**: Asian patients often present initially with **somatic complaints (軀體症狀)** (e.g., insomnia, chronic pain, fatigue) rather than explicit ↓ mood.

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurotransmitter Monoamine Hypothesis
* **Serotonin (5-HT) (血清素)**: ↓ levels in synaptic cleft → mood/sleep/appetite dysregulation.
* **Norepinephrine (NE) (去甲腎上腺素)**: ↓ levels → fatigue, psychomotor retardation, ↓ concentration.
* **Dopamine (DA) (多巴胺)**: ↓ levels → anhedonia, ↓ motivation.

### Neuroanatomical Changes
* **Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)**: ↓ volume/activity → impaired executive function, poor emotional regulation.
* **Hippocampus**: ↓ volume (neurogenesis inhibited by chronic ↑ cortisol) → memory deficits.
* **Amygdala**: ↑ activation → ↑ processing of negative emotional stimuli.

### Core Psychopathology
* **Affective**: Pervasive sadness, anhedonia, emotional blunting.
* **Cognitive**: Guilt (內疚), worthlessness, hopelessness (絕望), suicidal ideation (自殺念頭).
* **Somatic (Vegetative)**: Insomnia/hypersomnia, anorexia/hyperphagia, ↓ libido.

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History Elements
* **HPC**: Onset, duration, severity of core/somatic/cognitive symptoms.
* **Past Psychiatric Hx**: Previous depressive episodes, **prior (hypo)manic episodes (critical to rule out bipolar)**, previous self-harm/suicide attempts.
* **Family Hx**: Mood disorders, suicide, substance abuse.

### MSE: Appearance, Behaviour & Speech
* **Appearance**: Unkempt, neglect of personal hygiene, weight loss.
* **Behaviour**: **Psychomotor retardation (精神運動性遲滯)** (sluggish movements, downward gaze) or **agitation** (wringing hands). Poor eye contact.
* **Speech**: ↓ volume, ↓ rate, delayed responses (latency), poverty of speech.

### MSE: Mood, Affect & Thought
* **Mood**: Subjectively "depressed", "empty", or "numb".
* **Affect**: Restricted/blunted, mood-congruent (depressed), lacking reactivity.
* **Thought Content**: Preoccupied with failure/guilt. **Nihilistic delusions (虛無妄想)** or **delusions of guilt** in severe psychotic depression.
* **Suicidality**: Must actively screen for thoughts, intent, and plans.

### MSE: Cognition & Insight
* **Cognition**: Subjective poor memory/concentration (**pseudodementia (假性痴呆)** in elderly).
* **Insight**: Usually intact, but may be impaired if psychotic features are present.

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Primary Psychiatric Differentials
* **Bipolar Disorder (躁鬱症)**: Rule out past manic/hypomanic episodes.
* **Schizoaffective Disorder**: Psychotic symptoms persist in the absence of prominent mood symptoms.
* **Adjustment Disorder with Depressed Mood**: Symptoms occur within 3 months of an identifiable stressor, do not meet full MDD criteria.
* **Normal Bereavement (正常哀傷)**: Normal grief reaction, self-esteem usually preserved, functional impairment mild/transient.

### Organic/Medical Rule-Outs
* **Endocrine**: Hypothyroidism (甲狀腺功能減退), Cushing's disease, Addison's disease.
* **Neurological**: Parkinson's disease, dementia, cerebrovascular events (post-stroke depression), MS.
* **Infectious**: HIV, neurosyphilis, chronic hepatitis.
* **Substance/Medication-Induced**: Alcohol, stimulants (withdrawal), corticosteroids, beta-blockers, interferon.

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria for MDD
| DSM-5 Criteria Requirement | Symptom Description (Must have ≥5 symptoms during same 2-week period; at least 1 must be Core) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Core Symptom 1** | Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day. |
| **Core Symptom 2** | Markedly diminished interest/pleasure (Anhedonia). |
| **Somatic Symptoms** | Significant weight loss/gain or appetite ↑/↓; Insomnia or hypersomnia; Fatigue or loss of energy. |
| **Psychomotor Symptoms** | Psychomotor agitation or retardation (observable by others). |
| **Cognitive Symptoms** | Feelings of worthlessness or excessive/inappropriate guilt; ↓ ability to think/concentrate, or indecisiveness. |
| **Suicidality** | Recurrent thoughts of death, recurrent suicidal ideation, or suicide attempt/plan. |
| **Exclusions** | Causes significant distress/impairment; Not due to substance/medical condition; No history of mania/hypomania. |

### Standardized Rating Scales
* **PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9)**: Self-rated, 9 items. Score ≥10 indicates moderate depression.
* **HAM-D (Hamilton Depression Rating Scale)**: Clinician-administered, measures severity and treatment response.
* **MADRS (Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale)**: Clinician-rated, highly sensitive to treatment changes.

### Organic Rule-Out Investigations
* **Bloods**: CBP, TFTs (TSH/free T4), LFTs, RFTs, fasting glucose/HbA1c, Vitamin B12/Folate, Calcium.
* **Imaging**: CT/MRI Brain (if atypical presentation, late-onset, or focal neurological signs).
* **Tox Screen**: Urine/blood toxicology if substance abuse suspected.

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Risk Assessment (Suicide & Self-Harm)
* **High-Risk Indicators**: Male, older age, living alone, past attempts, organized plan, access to lethal means, severe hopelessness, psychotic symptoms.
* **Acute Action**: If high risk → require urgent hospitalization, 1:1 constant observation (特別觀察).

### Acute Clinical Management
* **Setting**: Inpatient admission for high suicide risk, self-neglect, or severe psychotic depression.
* **Intervention**: Initiate rapid-acting symptomatic relief if severely agitated (e.g., short-term BZDs like Lorazepam). Start antidepressant therapy.
* **ECT Indication**: Life-threatening starvation/dehydration, severe suicidality, treatment-resistant depression, psychotic depression.

### Medico-Legal: HK Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136)
* **Form 1/2/3 (Section 31)**: Voluntary admission for assessment/treatment.
* **Form 4 (Section 31)**: Detention of voluntary patient for up to 48 hours to prevent leaving hospital (signed by registered medical practitioner).
* **Section 36**: Involuntary admission for observation (requires 1 AMHP/relative + 2 medical practitioners). Max 7 days.
* **Section 42**: Involuntary admission for treatment (extension of observation).

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Pharmacotherapy: First-Line Agents
* **SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors)**: Fluoxetine, Sertraline, Escitalopram. (First-line due to safety profile).
* **SNRIs (Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors)**: Venlafaxine, Duloxetine. (Used if SSRIs fail or severe somatic symptoms).
* **NaSSA (Noradrenergic and Specific Serotonergic Antidepressants)**: Mirtazapine (good for insomnia/weight loss).

### Psychotropic Drug Side Effects Profile
| Drug Class | Common Side Effects (副作用) | Severe/Dangerous Complications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SSRIs** | GI upset, sexual dysfunction, headache, insomnia. | **Serotonin Syndrome (血清素綜合症)**, ↑ bleeding risk. |
| **SNRIs** | ↑ Blood pressure, sweating, nausea, dizziness. | Serotonin Syndrome, severe withdrawal symptoms if stopped abruptly. |
| **Mirtazapine** | Weight gain, sedation, dry mouth. | Agranulocytosis (rare but serious). |
| **TCAs** | Anticholinergic (dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation). | **Fatal in overdose** (cardiotoxicity/arrhythmia). |

### Psychotherapy & Somatic Treatments
* **Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) (認知行為治療)**: Restructure negative automatic thoughts, behavioral activation. Equal efficacy to meds in mild/moderate MDD.
* **Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT) (腦電盪治療)**: Most effective treatment for severe/refractory MDD. Induces generalized seizure. Efficacy >80%.
* **Social Interventions**: MedSW referral for financial/housing support, occupational rehabilitation.

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Illness Trajectory
* **Untreated Episode**: Typically lasts 6-12 months.
* **Treated Episode**: Response generally seen in 4-8 weeks.
* **Recurrence Risk**: ~50% after 1st episode, ~70% after 2nd episode, ~90% after 3rd episode.

### Prognostic Factors
* **Good Prognosis**: Mild episode, absence of psychotic features, solid social support, rapid response to initial treatment.
* **Poor Prognosis**: Co-morbid substance abuse, personality disorders, severe childhood trauma, psychotic features, multiple prior episodes.

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Medical Complications
* **Suicide (自殺)**: Lifetime risk ~15% in severe untreated MDD.
* **Psychotic Features**: May develop in severe, untreated progression.
* **Medical Decline**: ↑ risk of cardiovascular disease, worsened outcomes of co-morbid chronic illnesses (e.g., diabetes) due to poor self-care/non-adherence.

### Social & Occupational Complications
* **Occupational**: Absenteeism, job loss, ↓ productivity.
* **Social**: Marital breakdown, social isolation, caregiver burnout.
* **Substance Abuse**: Secondary alcohol/drug dependence as maladaptive coping (self-medication).

### Treatment-Emergent Complications
* **Serotonin Syndrome**: Autonomic instability, altered mental status, neuromuscular hyperactivity (clonus, hyperreflexia).
* **Antidepressant Discontinuation Syndrome**: Dizziness, flu-like symptoms, electric shock sensations (brain zaps) upon abrupt cessation.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: How long must symptoms be present to diagnose MDD according to DSM-5? Answer: At least 2 weeks.
* Trap: Assuming memory loss in an elderly patient is definitely Alzheimer's Dementia. Pearl: Always screen for "Pseudodementia" (Depression); depressive cognitive impairment typically presents with "I don't know" answers and variable performance, whereas true dementia patients try to guess or confabulate.
* Pitfall: Prescribing TCAs to a severely suicidal patient. TCAs are highly lethal in overdose due to cardiac arrhythmias. Use SSRIs instead.
* Viva Question: What is the most critical question to ask a patient presenting with their first depressive episode before starting antidepressants? Answer: "Have you ever had periods of feeling excessively high, energetic, or needing less sleep?" (Must rule out Bipolar Disorder to prevent antidepressant-induced mania).
* Trap: Stopping antidepressants immediately once the patient feels better. Pearl: Antidepressants must be continued for at least 6-9 months after remission of a first episode to prevent early relapse.
