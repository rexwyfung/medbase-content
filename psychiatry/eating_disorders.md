# Eating Disorders (進食障礙)

## 1. Clinical Definition, Biopsychosocial Etiologies & Local Epidemiology

### Clinical Definition of Eating Disorders
* **Core Pathology** (核心病理): Severe disturbances in eating behavior + distorted body image/weight concerns
* **Major Subtypes**: 
  * **Anorexia Nervosa [AN]** (神經性厭食症): Deliberate weight loss, intense fear of fatness
  * **Bulimia Nervosa [BN]** (神經性暴食症): Binge eating + compensatory purging
  * **Binge Eating Disorder [BED]** (暴食症): Binge eating without compensatory behaviors
  * **Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder [ARFID]** (迴避/限制性攝食障礙): Restrictive eating without body image distortion

### Biopsychosocial Etiologies
* **Biological** (生物學): 
  * ↑ Genetic concordance in monozygotic twins (AN: 50-60%)
  * Female biological sex → ↑ risk during puberty (hormonal shifts)
* **Psychological** (心理學): 
  * Rigid perfectionism, low self-esteem, obsessive-compulsive traits
  * Need for control shifted to food intake (food = control)
* **Social/Environmental** (社會環境): 
  * Westernized media ideals of thinness
  * High-pressure environments (e.g., ballet, athletics, strict schooling)
  * Overprotective or enmeshed family dynamics (Minuchin's psychosomatic family model)

### Local Epidemiology in Hong Kong
* **Prevalence**: ~1-2% for AN in adolescent females; BN is 2-3x more common
* **Demographics**: 90% female; peak onset mid-adolescence (AN) to late adolescence/young adulthood (BN)
* **HK Context**: ↑ incidence in dense urban academic environments; often delayed presentation due to cultural stigma (mental illness hidden as physical somatic complaints)

## 2. Neurobiology, Psychopathology & Neurotransmitter Pathways

### Neurobiological Pathways
* **Serotonin (5-HT) Dysregulation**:
  * **AN**: ↓ 5-HT activity during active starvation → up-regulation of receptors → anxiety/obsessiveness. (Feeding ↑ 5-HT → dysphoria → restriction ↓ dysphoria)
  * **BN**: Blunted 5-HT response → impaired satiety → binge episodes
* **Dopamine (DA) Reward System**:
  * Altered striatal DA circuits → abnormal reward response to starvation (AN) or food cues (BN)
* **Neuropeptides**: ↓ Leptin (satiety), ↑ Ghrelin (hunger) in chronic starvation (body trying to stimulate appetite, but cognitively overridden)

### Psychopathology & Cognitive Distortions
* **Overvalued Idea** (超價觀念): Intense, culturally comprehensible but pathologically pursued belief (e.g., "I am fat") that dominates life
* **Body Dysmorphia**: Distorted perception of size/shape (feeling fat despite objective emaciation)
* **Alexithymia**: Inability to identify/express emotions → acting out distress via food restriction/bingeing
* **Ego-Syntonic vs. Ego-Dystonic**:
  * **AN**: Ego-syntonic (patient embraces the behavior, resists treatment)
  * **BN**: Ego-dystonic (patient feels guilt/shame regarding binges, hides behavior)

## 3. Clinical Assessment (Psychiatric History & Mental State Examination [MSE] ONLY)

### Psychiatric History Focus
* **Dietary Intake**: Exact 24-hour recall, fluid intake, caloric counting obsession
* **Weight History**: Highest/lowest lifetime weight, current trajectory, target weight
* **Compensatory Behaviors**: Vomiting (frequency), laxatives, diuretics, diet pills, excessive exercise
* **Menstrual History**: Primary/secondary amenorrhea (閉經)
* **Collateral History**: ESSENTIAL (patients conceal symptoms). Ask parents/partners about hidden food, bathroom trips after meals, prolonged exercise

### Mental State Examination (MSE) Findings
* **Appearance**: Emaciated, baggy clothes (to hide body), lanugo hair, Russell's sign (calluses on knuckles from induced vomiting), parotid enlargement (chipmunk facies)
* **Behavior**: Restless, pacing (to burn calories), poor eye contact, defensive
* **Mood/Affect**: Depressed, anxious, irritable, restricted/blunted affect
* **Thoughts (Content)**: Preoccupation with food, calories, weight, body shape. Check for suicidal ideation (↑ risk in AN/BN)
* **Thoughts (Form)**: Usually normal, but may have rigid/concrete thinking
* **Perception**: Distorted body image (not a true hallucination, but a perceptual distortion)
* **Insight**: Usually impaired in AN (denies illness), partially preserved in BN (acknowledges loss of control)

## 4. Differential Diagnoses (Psychiatric vs. Organic/Medical Rule-Outs)

### Psychiatric Differential Diagnoses
* **Severe Depressive Disorder** (重度抑鬱症): ↓ appetite and weight loss, but NO fear of fatness or distorted body image
* **Schizophrenia** (精神分裂症): Food refusal due to persecutory delusions (e.g., "food is poisoned"), not body image
* **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder [OCD]** (強迫症): Food rituals due to contamination fears, not weight concerns
* **Substance Use Disorder**: Stimulant abuse (cocaine, amphetamines) → ↓ appetite and weight loss

### Organic/Medical Rule-Outs
* **Endocrinopathies**: Hyperthyroidism (↑ metabolism), Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (weight loss despite eating), Addison's disease
* **Gastrointestinal**: Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD), Celiac disease, malabsorption syndromes
* **Malignancy**: Occult tumors (e.g., brain tumors) causing cachexia
* **Infectious**: Chronic infections (e.g., Tuberculosis, HIV) causing wasting

## 5. Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/ICD-11), Rating Scales & Organic Rule-Out Investigations

### DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria Comparison

| Feature | Anorexia Nervosa (AN) | Bulimia Nervosa (BN) | Binge Eating Disorder (BED) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Weight** | Significantly low (BMI < 18.5) | Normal or overweight | Overweight or obese |
| **Core Fear** | Intense fear of gaining weight/fat | Fear of weight gain | Distress regarding binges |
| **Body Image** | Distorted self-evaluation | Self-evaluation heavily influenced by shape/weight | No distortion required |
| **Bingeing** | Only in Binge-Eating/Purging subtype | Recurrent (≥1x/week for 3 mos) | Recurrent (≥1x/week for 3 mos) |
| **Compensatory**| Restricting or Purging | Recurrent inappropriate (vomiting, laxatives) | **None** |

### Standardized Rating Scales
* **Eating Disorder Examination Questionnaire (EDE-Q)**: Gold-standard self-report for psychopathology
* **SCOFF Questionnaire**: 5-question screener (Sick, Control, One stone, Fat, Food)

### Organic Rule-Out Investigations
* **Blood**: CBC (anemia/leukopenia), U&E (↓ K+, ↓ Cl- in vomiting), LFT (↑ transaminases in starvation), TFT (sick euthyroid), Glucose (hypoglycemia)
* **Cardiac**: ECG (bradycardia, prolonged QTc, arrhythmias)
* **Bone**: DEXA scan (osteopenia/osteoporosis in chronic AN)

## 6. Acute Management, Risk Assessment & Medico-Legal (HK Mental Health Ordinance)

### Acute Risk Assessment & Medical Stabilization
* **Admission Criteria**: BMI < 13-15, rapid weight loss, severe electrolyte imbalance (K+ < 2.5), HR < 40 bpm, prolonged QTc, syncope, high suicide risk
* **Refeeding Syndrome Risk**: Fatal shift in fluids/electrolytes upon feeding a starved patient. 
  * **Pathophysiology**: ↑ Insulin → intracellular shift of PO4, K+, Mg2+ → profound hypophosphatemia → cardiac arrest/arrhythmias
  * **Prevention**: Start low calories (e.g., 1000 kcal/day), advance slowly, prophylactic Thiamine (Vit B1), monitor PO4 daily

### Medico-Legal Considerations (HK Mental Health Ordinance - Cap 136)
* **Involuntary Admission**: AN patients often lack insight and refuse life-saving treatment. 
* **Criteria for MHO (Form 1/2/3)**: If patient has a "mental disorder" (AN qualifies), is a danger to themselves (starvation/suicide), and refuses voluntary admission. 
* **Compulsory Feeding**: Under common law (duty of care) or Cap 136, nasogastric (NG) feeding can be administered forcefully if the patient lacks capacity and is at imminent risk of death from starvation.

## 7. Chronic Biopsychosocial Management (Pharmacotherapy, ECT & Psychotherapy)

### Biological (Pharmacotherapy)
* **AN**: No specific medications treat core AN symptoms. SSRIs (Fluoxetine) ONLY effective for comorbid depression/OCD *after* weight restoration (ineffective in starvation due to lack of dietary tryptophan). Low-dose Olanzapine (奧氮平) may ↓ severe anxiety, obsessive rumination, and promote weight gain.
* **BN**: High-dose SSRI (Fluoxetine 60mg/day) is gold standard → ↓ binge/purge frequency independent of mood.
* **BED**: SSRIs, Lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse) FDA-approved to ↓ binge days.

### Psychological (Psychotherapy)
* **Family-Based Treatment [FBT / Maudsley Approach]**: First-line for adolescents with AN. Empowers parents to take control of refeeding; externalizes the illness from the child.
* **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Eating Disorders [CBT-E]**: First-line for adults (BN, BED, mild AN). Addresses cognitive distortions, establishes regular eating patterns, identifies binge triggers.

### Social Management
* **Supervised Meals**: Post-meal observation (1-2 hours) in inpatient/day hospital to prevent purging.
* **Dietetic Support**: Structured meal plans, psychoeducation on nutrition.

## 8. Prognosis & Course of Illness

### Prognostic Indicators
* **Good Prognostic Factors**: Early age of onset, short duration of illness, supportive family, early intervention
* **Poor Prognostic Factors**: Very low minimal weight, prolonged illness (> 5 years), comorbid personality disorder, severe bulimic symptoms, poor social support

### Course of Illness (AN)
* **Rule of Thirds**: 
  * 1/3 fully recover
  * 1/3 experience chronic relapsing course
  * 1/3 remain chronically ill (Severe Enduring Anorexia Nervosa - SEAN)
* **Mortality**: Highest mortality rate of any psychiatric disorder (~5-10% per decade). Causes: Cardiac arrhythmias (starvation/hypokalemia) and Suicide (up to 1/5 of deaths).

## 9. Complications (Psychiatric, Social & Psychotropic Drug Side Effects)

### Psychiatric & Social Complications
* **Psychiatric**: ↑ Risk of Major Depressive Disorder, OCD, Anxiety Disorders, Substance Use Disorder (esp. BN). ↑ Suicide risk.
* **Social**: Social isolation, academic/occupational failure, severe family distress, financial burden (cost of binge foods, dental bills).

### Medical Complications of Starvation (AN)
* **Cardiac**: Bradycardia, hypotension, mitral valve prolapse, pericardial effusion
* **Endocrine**: Amenorrhea, infertility, osteoporosis, sick euthyroid syndrome
* **GI**: Delayed gastric emptying, constipation
* **Heme**: Pancytopenia (gelatinous marrow transformation)

### Medical Complications of Purging (BN)
* **Metabolic**: Hypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis (from vomiting); metabolic acidosis (from laxative abuse)
* **Dental/ENT**: Dental enamel erosion (perimylolysis), parotid gland hypertrophy, Mallory-Weiss tears, esophageal rupture (Boerhaave syndrome)

### Psychotropic Drug Precautions
* **Bupropion (Wellbutrin)**: ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED in AN/BN due to ↑ seizure risk in electrolyte-imbalanced/starved states.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the most dangerous complication during the first week of treating severe anorexia nervosa? Answer: Refeeding syndrome, leading to fatal hypophosphatemia and cardiac arrhythmias.
* Trap: Prescribing an SSRI for a severely emaciated anorexia nervosa patient expecting it to treat their low mood. Pearl: SSRIs are largely ineffective in starvation due to the lack of dietary tryptophan (the precursor to serotonin); weight restoration must occur first.
* Pitfall: Assuming a patient with a normal BMI does not have a severe eating disorder. Avoid by explicitly asking about bingeing and purging behaviors, as patients with Bulimia Nervosa typically have a normal or slightly elevated BMI despite severe medical risks (e.g., hypokalemia).
* Viva Question: Which psychotropic medication is absolutely contraindicated in patients with eating disorders? Answer: Bupropion, due to a significantly increased risk of seizures.
* Trap: Diagnosing anorexia nervosa in a patient who has lost significant weight due to persecutory delusions about poisoned food. Pearl: Anorexia nervosa requires an intense fear of gaining weight and a distorted body image; food refusal due to paranoia is indicative of a psychotic disorder like schizophrenia.
