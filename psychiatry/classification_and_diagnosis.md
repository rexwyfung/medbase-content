# Classification and Diagnosis (分類與診斷)

## 1. Topic Definition, Scope & Relevance in Hong Kong

### Definition of Nosology and Psychiatric Classification
* **Nosology (疾病分類學)** is the systematic classification of diseases.
* **Psychiatric Disorders (精神疾病)** are defined as **syndromes (症候群)**: clinically significant clusters of symptoms, behaviours, and cognitive alterations.
* Mostly lack independent biological validation/biomarkers → rely on descriptive phenomenology.

### Core Purposes of Diagnostic Classification
* Formulates a universal language for clinicians.
* Maps clinical features to **prognosis (預後)**.
* Guides evidence-based **pharmacotherapy (藥物治療)** and psychotherapy.
* Enables structured epidemiological tracking and resource allocation within the healthcare system.

### Relevance to Hong Kong Medical Practice
* **Hospital Authority (HA)** relies universally on **ICD-11 (國際疾病分類第十一版)** for electronic patient records (CMS) and morbidity coding.
* **DSM-5 (精神疾病診斷與統計手冊第五版)** is heavily utilized in HKUMed academic research, clinical teaching, and standardized operational definitions.

## 2. Core Theoretical Principles & Mechanisms (Psychological, Pharmacological, or Legal)

### Categorical vs. Dimensional Models
* **Categorical (分類法)**: Binary (yes/no) presence of a disorder.
	* → *Advantage*: High clinical utility for acute tx decisions (e.g., to prescribe or not).
	* → *Disadvantage*: Creates artificial boundaries; poor fit for atypical presentations.
* **Dimensional (維度法)**: Continuums of severity (e.g., psychosis spectrum, personality traits).
	* → *Advantage*: Better mirrors genetic/neurobiological reality.
	* → *Disadvantage*: Cumbersome for acute emergency triage.

### The Organic vs. Functional Dichotomy
* **Organic (器質性)**: Demonstrable cerebral/systemic pathology (e.g., Delirium, Dementia).
* **Functional (功能性)**: Descriptive symptom clusters without gross macroscopic brain pathology (e.g., Schizophrenia).
* Conceptually flawed (enforces mind/body dualism) but retains deep-rooted clinical shorthand utility.

### Neurosis vs. Psychosis Construct
* **Psychosis (精神病)**: Severe condition; impaired reality testing; presence of delusions/hallucinations; typically lack of insight.
* **Neurosis (神經症)**: Milder conditions; insight preserved; driven by disproportionate anxiety/distress.
* No longer serve as organizing principles in DSM/ICD but heavily used in descriptive clinical communication.

### The Concept of Comorbidity (Dual Diagnosis)
* **Comorbidity (共病)**: Concurrent presence of $\ge$ 2 diagnostic entities.
* True comorbidity: Shared underlying aetiopathogenesis (e.g., anxiety + depression).
* Artefactual comorbidity: Byproduct of overly granular classification rules.
* Principle: Treat concurrent conditions synergistically rather than forcing an artificial diagnostic hierarchy.

## 3. Key Classifications, Frameworks & Components

### Comparison of Major Diagnostic Systems
| Feature | ICD-11 | DSM-5 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | WHO (Global) | APA (American Psychiatric Association) |
| **Primary Use** | Universal clinical practice, Primary Care | Research, Academic Psychiatry |
| **Format** | Clinical descriptors, alphanumeric (e.g., 6A20) | Strict operational criteria |
| **Multiaxial** | No | No (Abandoned post-DSM-IV) |
| **Structure** | Organized by shared aetiology/phenomenology | Organized developmentally/phenomenologically |

### Hierarchical Diagnostic Framework
* Implicit clinical rule out: **Organic → Psychotic → Mood → Anxiety/Personality**.
* E.g., Do not diagnose primary anxiety if it occurs exclusively during an acute psychotic episode.
* Shift in modern nosology: Flattening of hierarchy to encourage identification of **comorbidities (共病)**.

### Symptom vs. Sign vs. Syndrome
* **Symptom (症狀)**: Subjective patient experience (e.g., feeling low).
* **Sign (體徵)**: Objective clinician observation (e.g., psychomotor retardation).
* **Syndrome (症候群)**: Reproducible constellation of symptoms and signs.
* **Disease (疾病)**: Syndrome with a known, specific pathophysiology (rare in psychiatry).

## 4. Clinical Indications & Standardized Assessment Tools

### Standardized Diagnostic Schedules
* **PSE (Present State Examination)**: Archetypal clinician-administered interview.
* **SCAN (Schedules for Clinical Assessment in Neuropsychiatry)**: Broad diagnostic assessment utilizing PSE principles.
* **SCID-5 (Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5)**: Gold standard for research trial recruitment.
* **CIDI / MINI**: Designed for epidemiological surveys; usable by trained lay-interviewers.

### Symptom Severity Rating Scales
| Domain | Standardized Scale | Clinical Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Depression** | PHQ-9 (Self), MADRS (Clinician) | Outpatient screening & tx monitoring |
| **Anxiety** | GAD-7 (Self), HADS (Self/Med Wards) | Primary care & general hospital consults |
| **Cognition** | MMSE, MoCA | Organic workup, Dementia staging |
| **Global** | CGI (Clinical Global Impression) | Broad tx efficacy tracking |
| **Psychosis** | PANSS, BPRS | Schizophrenia severity & research |

### Needs Assessment & Functional Scales
* **WHODAS 2.0**: Measures 6 domains of disability/functioning.
* **HoNOS (Health of the Nation Outcome Scales)**: 12-item routine clinical outcome measure.

## 5. Evidence-Base, Efficacy & Limitations

### Diagnostic Reliability
* **Reliability (信度)**: Inter-rater agreement.
* Historically poor (1960s) due to vague descriptors.
* Massively improved by **operational criteria** (DSM-III onwards) specifying inclusion/exclusion rules and duration thresholds.

### Diagnostic Validity
* **Validity (效度)**: Does the diagnosis carve nature at its joints?
* **Face Validity**: Matches clinical consensus.
* **Predictive Validity**: High in psychiatry → reliably predicts prognosis and psychopharmacological response.
* **Construct Validity**: Low in psychiatry → weak direct linkage to specific neurobiological aetiology.

### Epistemological Limitations
* Extreme heterogeneity within single diagnostic categories.
* Massive overlap between categories (pleiotropy).
* Failure to accommodate sub-threshold distress gracefully.

## 6. Practical Execution, Guidelines & Local Protocols

### The Psychiatric Formulation Framework
* **Formulation (個案總結)** translates diagnostic labels into personalized narrative.
* **1. Summary**: Demographics + core presentation.
* **2. Differential Diagnosis (鑑別診斷)**: Ranked list with evidence FOR and AGAINST.
* **3. Aetiology**: Grid of Bio/Psycho/Social vs. Predisposing/Precipitating/Perpetuating.
* **4. Management**: Immediate (acute risk) → Short-term (meds/CBT) → Long-term (rehab).
* **5. Prognosis**: Expected trajectory based on epidemiology + personal protective factors.

### Systematic Risk Assessment Protocol
* Must accompany EVERY diagnostic evaluation.
* **Risk to Self**: Suicide, deliberate self-harm.
* **Risk to Others**: Violence, homicide (Previous violence = #1 predictor).
* **Risk of Neglect**: Severe self-neglect, vulnerable dependents.
* Dynamic mapping: Incorporate historical static factors + dynamic clinical factors (e.g., active command hallucinations) + situational triggers.

### Formulating the Treatment Plan
* Integrate the diagnostic label with the **Biopsychosocial Model (生理心理社會模型)**.
* Do not restrict tx to pharmacology; must map to identified perpetuating social factors (e.g., housing, isolation).

## 7. Medico-Legal Considerations & HK Statutory Frameworks (e.g., Cap 136)

### Application of the HK Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136)
* A formal psychiatric diagnosis is the fundamental prerequisite for utilizing **Involuntary Admission (強制入院)**.
* Disorder must meet the legal threshold of **"Mental Disorder" (精神紊亂)** under the Ordinance.
* Requires evidence that detention is necessary for the health/safety of the patient or protection of others.

### Diagnosis vs. Mental Capacity
* Diagnostic label $\neq$ **Incapacity (無行為能力)**.
* Capacity is decision-specific and time-specific.
* Core criteria: 1. Understand info. 2. Retain info. 3. Weigh info. 4. Communicate decision.
* E.g., A patient with Schizophrenia may lack capacity to refuse antipsychotics but retain capacity to consent to appendectomy.

### Confidentiality vs. Public Interest
* Absolute duty of **Confidentiality (保密原則)** applies to all diagnostic data.
* Override ONLY justified by: Explicit consent, valid court order, or immediate necessity to prevent serious harm/death to self/others (e.g., child safeguarding).

## 8. Special Populations (Lifespan, Intellectual Disability, Comorbidities)

### Intellectual Disability (Learning Disability)
* Diagnosis relies heavily on collateral informant history (parents/carers).
* Symptoms often present atypically as **behavioural equivalents** (e.g., aggression masking underlying depression/pain).
* Diagnostic severity dictated by IQ thresholds and adaptive functioning deficits.

### Cross-Cultural Diagnostic Challenges
* Distinguish pathological delusions from **Cultural Idioms of Distress (文化痛苦表達)** or normative religious beliefs.
* Assess via DSM-5 Cultural Formulation.
* Beware of interpreter bias/filtration during cross-cultural assessments.

### Pediatric and Geriatric Nuances
* **Children**: Diagnoses must be evaluated against developmental milestones. Heavy reliance on family systems.
* **Elderly**: Polypharmacy and high medical comorbidity. Must aggressively rule out delirium/dementia before diagnosing late-onset functional psychiatric disorders.

## 9. Adverse Effects, Ethical Dilemmas & Systemic Challenges

### The Stigma of Diagnostic Labels
* Psychiatric labels carry profound **Stigma (標籤效應)**.
* Leads to systemic discrimination (employment, housing) and internal **Self-Stigma (自我標籤)**.
* Clinicians must balance the necessity of a diagnosis for securing resources/treatment against the lifelong impact of the label.

### The Risk of Medicalizing Normality
* Lowering diagnostic thresholds risks pathologizing normal human distress (e.g., standard grief vs. Major Depressive Disorder).
* Driven by pharmaceutical pressures and societal demands for quick fixes.

### The Future: RDoC & Biomarkers
* **Research Domain Criteria (RDoC)**: Shifts focus away from ICD/DSM syndromal categories toward transdiagnostic neurobehavioural dimensions (e.g., negative valence systems).
* *Limitation*: High research value, zero current clinical utility for the ward doctor triaging an acute crisis.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What is the single strongest predictor of future violence in a psychiatric patient? Answer: A past history of violence.
* Trap: Assuming that a patient with a psychotic illness inherently lacks the capacity to consent to medical treatments. Pearl: Capacity is decision-specific; a patient with schizophrenia may lack capacity regarding psychotropics but retain capacity to consent to a surgical procedure.
* Pitfall: Eliciting a delusion by directly challenging the patient's belief early in the interview. This ruptures rapport and causes the patient to mask symptoms; approach with open, non-judgemental curiosity about their unusual experiences instead.
* Viva Question: How does a "primary" (autochthonous) delusion differ from a "secondary" delusion? Answer: A primary delusion arises fully formed without antecedent psychological processes, whereas a secondary delusion is a psychologically understandable derivation from a preceding hallucination or mood state.
* Trap: Confusing depersonalization with a psychotic delusion. Pearl: Depersonalization retains the "as if" quality (insight preserved), whereas a delusion is held with absolute, unshakeable conviction.
* Pitfall: Diagnosing a primary psychiatric disorder in an elderly patient presenting with sudden-onset behavioural disturbance. Always rule out organic causes (delirium) via physical examination and targeted investigations first.
