# Psychopharmacology (精神藥理學)

## 1. Topic Definition, Scope & Relevance in Hong Kong

### Definition of Psychopharmacology in Clinical Practice
* **Scope**: Study & clinical application of medications modifying behavior, mood, thought & perception (認知與情緒).
* **Core Aim**: Symptom remission, relapse prevention & restoration of functional baseline.
* **HK Context**: Guided by Hospital Authority (HA) Drug Formulary (醫管局藥物名冊). Rapidly evolving to prioritize SGAs (Second-Generation Antipsychotics) over FGAs (First-Generation Antipsychotics) to ↓ metabolic/motor morbidity.

### HA Formulary & Local Prescribing Landscape
* **General vs. Psychiatric Prescription**: Specialized drugs (e.g., Clozapine, Depot injections) strictly initiated by psychiatrists; maintenance often co-managed in HA General Outpatient Clinics (GOPCs).
* **Resource Allocation**: High patient volume in HK → ↑ reliance on pharmacotherapy (vs. psychotherapy) for acute stabilization.
* **Cultural Factors**: Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) concurrent use is common in HK → ↑ risk of CYP450 herb-drug interactions.

## 2. Core Theoretical Principles & Mechanisms (Psychological, Pharmacological, or Legal)

### Pharmacodynamics: Receptor Affinities & Neurotransmitter Modulation
* **Antidepressants**: ↑ synaptic Serotonin (5-HT) & Noradrenaline (NA) via reuptake inhibition or MAO blockade.
* **Antipsychotics**: Primary mechanism = D2 receptor antagonism (Mesolimbic pathway → ↓ +ve symptoms). SGAs add 5-HT2A antagonism (Mesocortical pathway → potentially targets -ve symptoms & ↓ EPS).
* **Benzodiazepines (BZD)**: Allosteric positive modulators of GABA-A receptors → ↑ Cl- channel opening frequency → hyperpolarization (CNS depression).

### Pharmacokinetics: ADME & Cytochrome P450 (CYP) Interactions
* **Absorption & Distribution**: Most psychotropics are highly lipophilic → rapid CNS penetration, large volume of distribution (Vd).
* **Metabolism (Hepatic)**: Heavily reliant on CYP enzymes (e.g., CYP1A2, CYP2D6, CYP3A4). 
* **Genetic Polymorphism**: ~10% Asians are CYP2C19 poor metabolizers → ↑ toxicity risk (e.g., Escitalopram).
* **Elimination**: Renal excretion (Lithium is strictly renally cleared, requires eGFR monitoring).

## 3. Key Classifications, Frameworks & Components

### Antidepressants (抗抑鬱藥): SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs & MAOIs
| Class | Core Examples | Mechanism | Key Clinical Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SSRIs** | Fluoxetine, Sertraline | Selective 5-HT reuptake inhibition | 1st-line for MDD/Anxiety. Safe in OD. |
| **SNRIs** | Venlafaxine, Duloxetine | 5-HT & NA reuptake inhibition | 2nd-line. Monitor BP (dose-dependent ↑). |
| **TCAs** | Amitriptyline, Clomipramine | Non-selective monoamine reuptake block | Lethal in OD (arrhythmias). Anticholinergic. |
| **MAOIs** | Moclobemide (RIMA) | Monoamine oxidase inhibition | Tyramine cheese reaction risk (hypertensive crisis) with irreversible MAOIs. |

### Antipsychotics (抗精神病藥): FGAs vs. SGAs & Depot Formulations
| Class | Core Examples | Receptor Profile | Key Clinical Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FGAs** | Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine | High D2 antagonism | High EPS risk, hyperprolactinemia. |
| **SGAs** | Risperidone, Olanzapine, Quetiapine | D2 + 5-HT2A antagonism | High Metabolic Syndrome risk (weight ↑, DM, dyslipidemia). |
| **Atypical** | Clozapine | Multi-receptor, low D2 | For Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia. Agranulocytosis risk. |

### Mood Stabilizers (情緒穩定劑) & Anxiolytics (抗焦慮藥)
* **Lithium (鋰鹽)**: Gold standard for Bipolar prophylaxis. Narrow therapeutic index (0.4-1.0 mmol/L).
* **Anticonvulsants**: Valproate (Depakote), Lamotrigine, Carbamazepine.
* **Anxiolytics/Z-Drugs**: Lorazepam, Diazepam, Zolpidem. Short-term use only (<2-4 weeks) to avoid tolerance/dependence.

## 4. Clinical Indications & Standardized Assessment Tools

### First-Line Indications per HKHA Guidelines
* **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)**: SSRI (e.g., Sertraline, Fluoxetine). Trial for 4-6 weeks before assessing efficacy.
* **Schizophrenia (精神分裂症)**: SGA (e.g., Risperidone, Aripiprazole). 
* **Bipolar Affective Disorder (BPAD)**: Lithium or Valproate for maintenance. SGAs for acute mania.

### Baseline Screening & Monitoring Tools
* **Metabolic Monitoring**: BMI, Fasting Glucose/HbA1c, Lipid panel at baseline, 3 months, then annually for all SGAs.
* **Cardiovascular**: ECG for QTc prolongation (esp. Haloperidol, Citalopram, TCAs). Baseline QTc > 450ms (males) / 470ms (females) = caution.
* **Rating Scales**: 
  * **EPS**: Simpson-Angus Scale (SAS).
  * **Tardive Dyskinesia**: Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS).
  * **Akathisia**: Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale (BARS).

## 5. Evidence-Base, Efficacy & Limitations

### Number Needed to Treat (NNT) & Relapse Prevention
* **Antidepressants**: NNT ~7-9 for acute MDD. Must continue for 6-9 months post-remission to prevent relapse (維持治療).
* **Antipsychotics**: NNT ~3 for acute psychosis. Non-adherence → 80% relapse rate at 1 year.
* **Limitations**: High placebo response rates in mild-to-moderate depression trials. Delay in therapeutic onset (2-4 weeks for antidepressants).

### Treatment-Resistant Paradigms & Augmentation Strategies
* **Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD)**: Failure of ≥2 distinct antidepressant trials. 
  * **Augmentation**: Lithium, Aripiprazole, Quetiapine, or Triiodothyronine (T3).
* **Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia (TRS)**: Failure of ≥2 antipsychotics (incl. 1 SGA) at adequate dose/duration.
  * **Gold Standard**: Clozapine (氯氮平). Requires strict absolute neutrophil count (ANC) monitoring.

## 6. Practical Execution, Guidelines & Local Protocols

### Safe Prescribing: Titration, Tapering & Cross-Titration
* **Initiation**: "Start low, go slow" principle, esp. in psychotropic-naïve patients.
* **Tapering**: Abrupt cessation of SSRIs/SNRIs (esp. Paroxetine, Venlafaxine) → Antidepressant Discontinuation Syndrome (FINISH: Flu-like, Insomnia, Nausea, Imbalance, Sensory disturbances, Hyperarousal).
* **Cross-Titration**: Gradually ↓ Drug A while ↑ Drug B to maintain symptom control & ↓ withdrawal.

### Rapid Tranquilization Protocol in HK ER/Wards
* **Indication**: Acute severe behavioral disturbance posing imminent risk.
* **1st Line (Oral)**: Haloperidol 5mg + Lorazepam 1-2mg PO.
* **2nd Line (IM)**: Haloperidol 5mg IM + Lorazepam 1-2mg IM (avoid Olanzapine IM + BZD IM simultaneously → fatal cardiorespiratory depression).
* **Monitoring**: Post-tranquilization NEWS (National Early Warning Score) monitoring every 15-30 mins.

## 7. Medico-Legal Considerations & HK Statutory Frameworks

### Mental Health Ordinance (Cap 136) & Involuntary Medication
* **Voluntary Patients**: Full right to refuse medication. Treatment requires valid informed consent.
* **Involuntary Patients (Section 1-3)**: 
  * **Urgent Treatment**: Allowed under common law necessity to save life / prevent serious harm.
  * **Form 4 / Form 3**: Authorizes compulsory psychiatric treatment. However, physical comorbidities *cannot* be compulsorily treated under Cap 136.

### Consent, Capacity & Advanced Directives
* **Capacity Assessment**: Patient must understand, retain, use/weigh info, and communicate decision regarding the specific medication.
* **Lacking Capacity**: Act in "Best Interests" (Part IVC of Cap 136). Consult family, but proxy consent for adults has no legal standing in HK without court appointment.

## 8. Special Populations (Lifespan, Intellectual Disability, Comorbidities)

### Psychopharmacology in Pregnancy & Lactation
* **Teratogenicity Risk**: 
  * **Valproate**: Neural tube defects, lower IQ. Absolutely contraindicated in women of childbearing potential without Pregnancy Prevention Programme.
  * **Lithium**: Ebstein's anomaly (1st trimester).
  * **Paroxetine**: Cardiac septal defects.
* **Lactation**: Sertraline & Olanzapine generally considered compatible. Avoid Lithium.

### Geriatric Prescribing: "Start Low, Go Slow"
* **Altered PK/PD**: ↓ renal clearance, ↓ hepatic metabolism, ↑ CNS sensitivity.
* **Risks**: Anticholinergic burden → delirium, falls, urinary retention. 
* **Dementia with Agitation**: SGAs (Risperidone) ↑ risk of stroke (CVA) and all-cause mortality. Use short-term only if non-pharmacological interventions fail.

### Hepatic & Renal Impairment Dose Adjustments
* **Renal Failure**: Lithium toxicity risk. Adjust dose or switch to hepatically cleared agent (e.g., Valproate).
* **Hepatic Failure**: Avoid Duloxetine, Valproate. Use BZDs metabolized by conjugation only (LOT: Lorazepam, Oxazepam, Temazepam).

## 9. Adverse Effects, Ethical Dilemmas & Systemic Challenges

### Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS) & Metabolic Syndrome (代謝綜合症)
| EPS Subtype | Onset | Clinical Feature | Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Dystonia** | Hours-Days | Sustained muscle contraction (torticollis, oculogyric crisis). | Anticholinergic (Benztropine IM). |
| **Akathisia** | Days-Weeks | Subjective inner restlessness, pacing. | Propranolol (Beta-blocker), ↓ dose. |
| **Parkinsonism** | Weeks-Months| Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia. | Anticholinergic, ↓ dose. |
| **Tardive Dyskinesia**| Months-Years| Orofacial choreoathetoid movements. Often irreversible. | Stop FGA, switch to Clozapine. VMAT2 inhibitors. |

### Life-Threatening Toxicities
* **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)**: Fever, severe "lead-pipe" rigidity, autonomic instability, ↑ CK. Rx: Stop antipsychotic, supportive care, Bromocriptine/Dantrolene.
* **Serotonin Syndrome**: Hyperreflexia, clonus, fever, diarrhea (often SSRI + MAOI interaction). Rx: Stop serotonergic agents, Cyproheptadine.
* **Lithium Toxicity**: >1.5 mmol/L (Tremor, ataxia, N/V). >2.0 mmol/L (Seizures, coma). Rx: Hydration, Hemodialysis if severe.

## 10. Examiner's Pearls

* Viva Question: What are the metabolic monitoring parameters for a patient started on Olanzapine? Answer: Baseline and serial monitoring of weight/BMI, fasting blood glucose or HbA1c, and fasting lipid profile, plus blood pressure.
* Trap: Assuming a patient on Valproate presenting with abdominal pain and vomiting just has gastroenteritis. Pearl: Check serum amylase/lipase; Valproate is strongly associated with acute hemorrhagic pancreatitis and hepatotoxicity.
* Pitfall: Prescribing IM Olanzapine and IM Lorazepam simultaneously for rapid tranquilization. Can lead to fatal cardiorespiratory depression; allow at least 1-2 hours between IM injections of these agents.
* Viva Question: A 25-year-old female with BPAD I is planning pregnancy; she is currently stable on Valproate. What is your management? Answer: Valproate is highly teratogenic (neural tube defects). Cross-titrate to a safer alternative like Lamotrigine or an SGA (e.g., Quetiapine) before conception, and ensure high-dose folic acid supplementation.
* Trap: Misdiagnosing Akathisia as worsening psychotic agitation and increasing the antipsychotic dose. Pearl: Increasing the antipsychotic will worsen akathisia; look for subjective inner restlessness and repetitive shifting of weight. Treat with Propranolol or dose reduction.
* Pitfall: Stopping a patient's SSRI abruptly due to mild side effects. Can trigger Antidepressant Discontinuation Syndrome; always taper gradually over weeks unless a life-threatening reaction (e.g., Serotonin Syndrome) dictates abrupt cessation.
